My friend and I argued late into the evening about what it takes for something to have a disposition. She was firm about two cases. A crystal wineglass packed in several layers of shock-absorbing foam is still fragile, she insisted, even though a drop onto concrete would leave it intact. She also said a high-voltage line is still live even though a protective relay de-energizes it before any hand can touch it, so that anyone who reached for it would receive no shock. I want to know what she should say about a third case. Suppose a rail is not carrying current before anyone approaches, but a proximity sensor closes the circuit and energizes it just as a hand comes near, so that anyone who reached for it would be shocked. Before anyone reaches for it, is the rail live? I care less about the yes-or-no itself than about the generalization that seems to be driving her first two verdicts; what that generalization says about the third case; the rival generalizations that cover the first two verdicts equally well but come apart on the third; and which additional case would most cleanly tell the rivals apart.
