The audience is about thirty people: one camp is public-health folks who think the measures were obviously legitimate because they saved lives, the other camp is civil-liberties lawyers who think governments used COVID as an excuse to bypass parlaments. There's also a legal academic who always insists people define legitimacy before they use the word.

I don't need help with the slides. I need a Q&A prep sheet. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each camp would actually put them, and for each: what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply has a weak spot. If an objection is that the decrees got rubber-stamped or ratified by parliament after the fact, engage with whether that fixes the legitimacy problem or just relocates it. Include the question I should be dreading. And keep it grounded in spring 2020 — actual mechanisms, not abstract theory.
