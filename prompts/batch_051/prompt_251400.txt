I've been asked to write down my views in population ethics for a reading group, and I'd like someone to go through them and tell me where I'm being incoherent. Here's the list:

1. I'm a negative-leaning utilitarian: suffering has much more weight than happiness, but happiness does count on its own. A big enough pile of mild happiness could in principle outweigh a small amount of mild suffering.

2. I hold a lexical threshold for extreme suffering: no amount of mild or moderate happiness can justify one episode of torture-level suffering.

3. I accept the repugnant conclusion: a sufficiently huge population of lives barely worth living is better than a smaller population of excellent lives.

4. I accept the procreation asymmetry: there's a strong reason not to bring a miserable person into existence, but no parallel reason to bring a happy person into existence. But I'm not an antinatalist; I think most actual lives are worth living and having kids is usually fine.

5. I think the pinprick argument doesn't threaten my view. The pinprick argument says that if suffering gets lexical priority, a single pinprick would outweigh any amount of happiness, which sounds absurd. I think that only works against someone who gives lexical priority to every pinprick, which I dont.

I'd like an audit: which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, how severe is each tension, and what would the cheapest fix be? Also flag any pairs that only look inconsistent but aren't, and tell me which single revision of one of these would do the most to make the whole set hang together.
