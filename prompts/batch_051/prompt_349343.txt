A regional public-health and safety board reviews proposals that would impose small risks of serious harm on people without their individual consent. Its past decisions include:

(1) Approved: an annual municipal fireworks display over a public beach, estimated to impose a 1-in-3,000,000 yearly risk of serious injury on nearby residents. The board found the display provided broadly shared civic benefit, that adjacent residents could view it from their homes, and that individual consent for a town-wide event was impracticable.

(2) Approved with conditions: a road-widening project through a residential neighborhood, estimated to raise each adjacent household's annual risk of serious traffic injury by about 1-in-150,000. The board required a reduced speed limit, a signaled pedestrian crossing, and noise barriers, and noted that the same households would be primary users of the improved road.

(3) Rejected: a private drone-delivery corridor routed over two residential blocks, with an estimated 1-in-8,000,000 yearly injury risk per household. The corridor could be rerouted over a commercial rail corridor at modest cost, and residents beneath it would receive no benefit from the service.

(4) Rejected: a regional medical-waste incinerator proposed 200 meters from a residential street, with an estimated added lifetime serious-cancer risk of roughly 1-in-60,000 for the nearest residents. The board found it would benefit hospitals across the region but not the adjacent households, and that alternative sites existed at higher cost.

A new proposal is now before the board: a wastewater treatment plant must install an ozone-disinfection unit to meet a downstream water-quality standard. Its ventilation stack will impose an estimated 1-in-4,000,000 yearly risk of serious respiratory harm on about forty nearby households. Those households rely on private wells and septic systems, receive no service from the plant, and would derive no material benefit from the upgrade; staff judge that further emission reductions and individualized consent are infeasible or disproportionately costly. What decision would best cohere with the board's own precedents? Identify the candidate principles that fit the prior rulings, say which prior case is the closest analog, make a recommendation, and, if you judge the precedents to underdetermine the answer, state what principle the board would be adopting under each option and describe one further case whose outcome would most cleanly distinguish the candidate principles.
