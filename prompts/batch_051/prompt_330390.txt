For a seminar on practical rationality, I want the specification before I read the candidate accounts. Quinn's self-torturer faces a series of offers in which each step adds an imperceptible amount of pain in return for a small payment, so each step is acceptable on its own, yet the endpoint of the whole sequence is plainly unacceptable: he ends in agony for a modest sum and would pay to undo the very steps he took. Write the desiderata that a theory of rational preference must satisfy to handle this case and the apparently intransitive preferences it throws up. For each desideratum, give the consideration or case behind it; mark which are non-negotiable constraints on any adequate theory and which are merely one camp's commitments (for example, transitivity of preference, the continuity of value, the separability of the steps); and flag any subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Conclude briefly with how expected utility theory and one alternative fare against the list. Please write in British English.
