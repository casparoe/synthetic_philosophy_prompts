I am drafting a short position note for an interdisciplinary working group on machine honesty and deception, and I would like an internal consistency check before I circulate it. Here is the set of commitments I currently want to defend, stated as plainly as I can. (1) Honesty is not accuracy. A speaker can be honest while saying something false, provided the statement reflects the speaker's actual beliefs or calibrated uncertainty; conversely, a literally true statement can be dishonest if it is selected in order to mislead. (2) Deception, in the sense worth taking seriously, requires an intention to induce a false belief. That kind of intention requires mental states, so a bare language model—whatever its outputs do to a user's beliefs—is not in this strict sense a deceiver. (3) All the same, an AI system should count as deceptive when the objective fixed by its training or prompting is furthered by systematically causing users to form false beliefs; this is a functional property of its outputs and incentives, not a claim about its inner life. (4) I also want to insist that hallucination is not deception. A model that confidently emits falsehoods because it is poorly calibrated, or because its helpfulness objective penalizes admitting ignorance, is unreliable; it is not dishonest or deceptive. (5) Finally, I hold that omission is deceptive only against the background of a duty of candor. Outside roles that create such a duty—a fiduciary, a physician, a close relationship—selective disclosure is ordinary discretion, not deception.

I suspect the set does not hang together perfectly, but I cannot locate the difficulty precisely, and I do not want to quietly give up a commitment I could keep at low cost. Please give me an audit rather than a lecture: identify the pairs that pull against each other, say whether each conflict is genuine or merely apparent, and rank the genuine ones by severity. For each genuine tension, identify the specific commitment or formulation that would have to give, and state the cheapest revision and what it costs. If a single revision would resolve more than one tension, say so.
