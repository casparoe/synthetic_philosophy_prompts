I am drafting a paper for an interdisciplinary workshop, and I want help preparing for the question period. The paper is about the ethics of risk imposition without consent, and my stated thesis is this: a liberal state may impose modest regulatory burdens on activities that impose small but nontrivial risks of serious harm on nonconsenting parties, even when the harm to any particular individual is too diffuse to ground an ordinary tort claim, but only if the regulation can be justified by a standing principle that does not reduce to the bare claim that the activity makes some future wrongdoing or accident more likely. I want to derive that principle mostly from John Stuart Mill, especially from his discussion near the end of Chapter 5 of On Liberty. There Mill grants that government may take precautions against crime and accident before they occur, but he warns that this preventive branch of state power is far more liable to be abused than the punitive branch, because nearly any legitimate freedom could be described as increasing the facilities for some form of delinquency. His example is the sale of poisons: he treats it as a test case for how far liberty may legitimately be invaded to prevent crime or accident, rather than to punish or directly stop harm. I am trying to turn that discussion into a test for when risk-imposition may be regulated without the consent of the risk-bearers.

My audience will be about fifteen people: political philosophers, legal theorists, and one or two tort lawyers. They are not all sympathetic to Mill. Some of the likely objectors are: a tort theorist who denies that a merely increased risk, without actual damage, is a harm at all; a Kantian who thinks using others as an involuntary risk pool is wrong regardless of whether any particular person ends up harmed; a committed Millian who suspects my test licences much more regulation than Mill himself would allow; and an administrative lawyer who doubts the test can be made judicially administrable. What I need is a Q&A prep sheet, not another draft of the paper. List the objections I am actually likely to get from this group, ordered roughly by how likely they are and how much damage they would do. Phrase each objection in the way the relevant person would actually put it. For each, give a two-minute reply I could make, and then a short note on where that reply is weakest or what I should concede. End with the one question I hope nobody asks, and explain why it would be hardest for me to answer.
