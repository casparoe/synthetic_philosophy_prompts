I'm a bioethics PhD and I keep going in circles on emergency triage. The protocols in a mass casualty event sort people by expected benefit: someone with a very low chance of survival gets tagged expectant and set aside, so the team can treat four or five other people with better odds. That practice seems to take it for granted that the numbers count — that saving four is just worth more than saving one. But ordinary hospital care seems to reject that; we don't let one patient die because three others would each get a small bump from the same resources, and we'd never even float the organ-harvesting version of that trade. So what does triage actually presuppose about whether numbers count? Does it strictly require a full consequentialist picture, or could a Taurek-ish view — that numbers alone don't give you a reason to save the many instead of the one — still make sense of it? And if that view was right, would triage have to change, or could it survive on some other basis? I'm after the implicit theory the practice is built on, not the official protocol.
