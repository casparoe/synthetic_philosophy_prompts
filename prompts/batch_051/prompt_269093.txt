I've been reading Locke's An Essay Concerning Human Understanding, and this bit from Book II Chapter XXIII stuck in my head:

"and so hardness, friability, and power to draw iron, we say, are qualities to be found in a loadstone."

I know it's not one of his famous lines, but he's saying that a thing's qualities include powers—things it can do even when it isn't doing them. That got me onto my own little theory, and I want someone to beat it up.

Call it the blank-room test. My idea is: you shouldn't say a thing lost a power just because the world got in the way. A magnet still has the power to draw iron even if you nail it to a table and it can't move, or even if it's wrapped in a thousand layers of foil. The power is still in the thing, just not getting expressed. My test is this: teleport the object, exactly as it is on the inside, into an empty room with nothing else interfering—no blockers, no extra helps either, just the thing and the thing it's supposed to act on. If it would do X in that room, then it has the power X here, no matter how many times it actually fails here.

But I can already feel something's off. For one, what counts as "interfering"? Is water on a match a blocker, or does a wet match just stop being a match in the relevant way? And is air a help for fire that I'm smuggling into the blank room without noticing? Also, if a thing is wired so that any attempt to trigger it destroys the trigger before the outcome happens, my test says it has the power, but it never has it in any real room. Is that still a power, or just a story about what could have been?

I think Locke's loadstone line shows this is a old problem: he calls power a quality, but it's a quality that only shows up sometimes. So is my blank-room test just restating that, or does it add anything? And where does it break—what would it count as a power that no sane person would call a power?
