The branch questions should be the claims that actually split the positions, including: whether the option she ends up picking can still be wrong if it's the best option available; whether the guilt or self-disgust she feels afterward is evidence that she did wrong or just a psychological cost of doing right; whether political office has its own permissions that ordinary morality doesn't; and whether, at the end, she should be punished, praised, or somehow both. Aim for six to eight questions. Make sure the leaves include at least: strict moral absolutism, straightforward consequentialism, dirty-hands realism where the act comes out right and wrong at once, role-based political realism, and an agent-regret view where feeling the weight of the harm is required but doesn't imply the act was wrong. Give each leaf a one-sentence note on the commitment it has the hardest time swallowing. Phrase every branch as a question a student can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and flag any leaves your tree can't really seperate, as well as any combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy.
