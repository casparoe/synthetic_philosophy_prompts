In The Varieties of Religious Experience, in the lectures on mysticism, William James gives four marks that characterize mystical states: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, and passivity. He says the state can't be described adequately, yet it feels like a state of knowledge; it doesn't last long; and the subject feels taken over rather than in control. I've been thinking about a non-theist's account of a long exhausting run: at some point the sense of self drops out, time becomes strange, and afterwards she says she can't put it into words but she now knows somthing about how things are—not a proposition, more like a felt certanty. She also says it felt effortless, like she was being carried. Is that a mystical state by James's criteria? Go through the four marks one by one, say where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and whether the case shows the criteria need changing. And if ineffability and noetic quality can coexist as James says, is there a way to know what cannot be said that isn't just mistaking a feeling for knowledge?
