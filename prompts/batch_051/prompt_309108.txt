I have been thinking through the implications of Nietzsche's genealogical method, and I would like your credence in a specific claim about moral desert. Suppose a compelling historical account established that the concept of moral desert originated and persists because it stabilized systems of punishment and cooperation, and that the psychological mechanism producing desert judgments was insensitive to whether anyone genuinely deserves anything. Conditional on that supposition, what is your credence that the discovery would undercut the justification of ordinary desert judgments—that is, make continued confidence unreasonable in the absence of independent support—rather than merely explain their origin? Decompose the estimate into the main sub-questions it depends on, including whether a truth-insensitive genealogy of a practice can undermine that practice's present justification, whether the genealogy accounts for the content of desert judgments and not only their motivational force, whether ordinary moral experience supplies independent evidence for desert that would survive the genealogy, and whether the practical indispensability of desert can justify retaining it. Assign a number and a one-line justification to each term, show how they combine, and identify which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would shift it.
