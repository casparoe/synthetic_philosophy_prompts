For a reading group, I've been asked to write down my philosophical profile on truth and knowledge so the others can test whether it hangs together. Here is the set of commitments I want to keep, though I can't yet see whether they are jointly stable. (1) Verificationist anti-realism: there are no truths that are in principle unknowable — for every true proposition, it is possible in principle that some knower at some time knows it. (2) There are many truths that are never in fact known — unrecorded details, secrets that die with their last bearer, facts no one ever thinks to check. (3) I accept the ordinary classical logic of conjunction and negation, and I accept that a proposition which entails a contradiction cannot be possible in principle. (4) I accept two minimal principles about knowledge: knowledge is factive (what is known is true), and knowledge distributes over conjunction (knowing a conjunction entails knowing each conjunct). I would rather not give any of these up without knowing the cost. Audit the set for tensions. Which tensions are genuine and which are merely apparent? Order the genuine tensions by severity, give the cheapest fix for each, and then say which single revision does the most to restore consistency at the lowest philosophical cost.
