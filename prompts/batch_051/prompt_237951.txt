I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and I have been trying to pin down exactly what locality requires of a hidden-variable theory of the Bell-type correlations. I have settled on four working criteria, and I would be grateful for a careful assessment of them against one case.

A hidden-variable theory counts as local only if it satisfies all of the following:

(1) No superluminal mechanism: the theory posits no causal influence from the measurement event on one side to the outcome on the other side that propagates faster than light.

(2) Parameter independence: conditional on the complete hidden state λ and the local setting, the outcome on one side is probabilistically independent of the setting chosen on the distant side.

(3) Outcome independence: conditional on λ and both settings, the outcome on one side is probabilistically independent of the outcome on the other side.

(4) Measurement independence: the probability distribution over λ is independent of the settings that will be chosen; the hidden state and the experimenters' choices are not pre-correlated. I realize this last condition is often called the no-conspiracy assumption, and treating it as a locality condition is itself contentious.

In the derivation of Bell's theorem that I find clearest, conditions (2) through (4) are the assumptions that force the observable correlations to satisfy a Bell inequality, which the quantum predictions violate.

Now consider a superdeterministic hidden-variable theory. On this picture, λ is the complete hidden state of a relevant past region of a deterministic universe, and this same state determines, or at least heavily constrains, both the disposition of the particle pair and the settings the experimenters will select. The theory reproduces the quantum correlations exactly, but it does so by violating condition (4): λ and the settings share a common cause in the deep past, so the distribution over λ depends on the settings. There is no superluminal influence, so condition (1) seems satisfied. Conditions (2) and (3) are where I get stuck: once we condition on λ, the distant setting may no longer be the sort of variable that could have been different while holding λ fixed, so the independence claims look either vacuous, ill-defined, or impossible to test.

My question is whether, on the criteria I just listed, the superdeterministic theory counts as local. Please work through the four conditions one by one, flag the places where they underdetermine or fail to engage the case, and then tell me whether the case shows that my criteria need revision. In particular, I would like you to address whether measurement independence is best classified as part of locality itself, as a separate assumption about experimental freedom, or as a precondition for the other statistical independence conditions to be meaningful at all. Is this theory a genuine counterexample to my criteria, or a sign that the criteria have been arranged in advance to rule it out?
