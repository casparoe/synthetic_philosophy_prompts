Here is an asymmetry in ordinary practice that bothers me. A company may emit carbon and then pay someone else to plant trees or install wind turbines, and many people will treat the company as having met its climate obligation. But if a person is sentenced to two hundred hours of roadside litter cleanup and pays a willing, well-supervised substitute to do those hours just as thoroughly, nearly everyone says the sentence was evaded rather than served. Both cases have the same outward shape: someone creates an obligation and then buys its fulfillment from a third party who played no part in the original wrong. Is there a principled reason for treating the first as payment and the second as evasion, or is one of the two reactions a mistake? If both are right, what general test tells us which obligations may be outsourced and which must be performed in person?
