Imagine that starting in 2027 a country adopts the following rule: the death penalty may be imposed only in murder cases where the killing was recorded from at least two independant tamper-proof camera angles, the defendant’s identity is confirmed by DNA plus a full biometric match, and a panel of three statisticians certifies that the probability of misidentification is below one in ten billion. Everything else about criminal procedure stays the same. Predict, with visible reasoning, how the death penalty debate and practice look in that country after twenty years. In particular: does the innocence argument disappear from abolitionist rhetoric, or just migrate? Do deterrence researchers finally get a clean natural experiment, and what would they likely find? How do retributivists and abolitionists respond to the fact that the state now kills only the certainly guilty? Does the death-eligible category stay narrow or creep outward, and through what mechanisms? Which links in your prediction are robust, which are guesses, and what assumptions would have to fail for your overall prediction to be wrong? End with a one-sentence summary of your overall prediction as the last line.
