The idea I keep running into is coherent extrapolated volition (CEV): roughly, instead of doing what someone currently asks for, a superintelligence should do what that person would want if they knew more, thought faster and better, were less biased, and were a more unified version of themselves. Here's what I want: a number. Conditional on an AGI genuinely trying to compute and implement that for all of humanity, what is your credence that the procedure lands on a single, fairly stable, genuinely good target, rather than a spread of incompatible preferences, a coherent-but-awful attractor, or no well-defined result at all? Put me at 0.35. And don't give me the it-depends-on-your-metaethics dodge: where metaethics matters, make it part of the decomposition. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion, in American English. Cover: (1) probability the idealization converges to a unique target instead of a scatter, (2) probability that phrases like knew more and more unified are determinate enough to do real work, (3) probability the converged target is good by our current lights rather than coherent but abhorrent, (4) probability the whole instruction is too underspecified to constrain an AGI much at all, and (5) your overall credence and which of items 1 through 4 would move your number most if you learned you were wrong about it.
