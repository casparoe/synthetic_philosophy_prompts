I work as a reinforcement learning engineer, and while my philosophy background is thin, I recently encountered a position I was told is called tranquilism. As I understand it, the claim is that the only thing that ultimately matters is the absence of craving and suffering; pleasure, achievement, understanding, and connection matter only derivatively, to the extent that they quiet craving or prevent suffering. On this view the positive content of a life is instrumentally useful stillness-seeking, never an independent source of value.

I would like you to restate tranquilism in the vocabulary I actually work in and trust: Markov decision processes, states and observations, reward signals, value functions, policies, exploration bonuses, reward prediction error, terminal states, discounting, and the distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic reward. Do this as systematically and honestly as you can, without quietly adding assumptions the view did not supply. Then give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to distort to make fit, and which ones have no counterpart in the RL picture at all. My working hunch is that tranquilism is just the claim that reward is zero everywhere except for the relief of aversive states, and I want to know whether that hunch survives an honest translation or whether the translation changes the view. In particular, can the claim that only the absence of negative reward matters even be stated in a framework organized around learning policies that maximize cumulative reward, or does stating it force you to smuggle in something like a reward function bounded above at zero? Is the untranslatable residue a limitation of the RL vocabulary, or is it exposing a place where tranquilism is less precise than it looks?
