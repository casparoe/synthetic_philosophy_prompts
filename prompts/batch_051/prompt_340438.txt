I have been trying to get clear on what accommodation actually does to a conversation, and I keep constructing an argument that makes me uneasy. Consider a plain sentence like "I have to pick up my sister from the airport." If I say it to someone who has no idea whether I have a sister, they will normally just accept that I have a sister and update the background rather than challenge me. On a scorekeeping picture of conversation, accommodation looks like a house rule that lets a speaker update the score simply by playing as though it had already been updated. That seems to hand speakers a remarkable power, and I cannot find the flaw in the following line of thought.

(1) An utterance that carries a presupposition is felicitous only if the presupposition is already part of the conversational common ground. (2) When a presupposition is missing, hearers often accommodate it, quietly adding it to the common ground. (3) Whatever is added in this way is thereafter part of the common ground. (4) So a speaker can insert a proposition into the common ground without ever obtaining the hearer's explicit agreement. (5) A proposition is part of the common ground only if the participants mutually accept it. (6) Therefore, merely by presupposing a proposition, a speaker can bring it about that the participants mutually accept it. (7) Consequently, there is a purely linguistic mechanism through which a speaker unilaterally changes what the conversation treats as settled, and the contrast between openly asserting something and quietly presupposing it is less a matter of principle than of style.

I half suspect the trouble lies in what "adding to the common ground" really amounts to, but I cannot say which premise that damages or what the corrected version of the relevant principle should be. Is the argument valid? If not, identify exactly which step fails and under what reading. If it is valid, say which premise is doing the questionable work. Either way, give the strongest defensible account of accommodation you can, and explain whether the conclusion survives once that account is in place.
