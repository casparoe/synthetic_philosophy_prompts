A magazine essay I read recently tried to introduce Dennett's heterophenomenology to a general audience, and I want to know how much of it to trust. Here, as faithfully as I can reconstruct it, is the article's account.

According to the essay, heterophenomenology is Dennett's proposed method for making the science of consciousness intellectually respectable. Its starting point, the essay says, is that only the subject has direct access to his or her own conscious life, so the scientist must treat introspective reports as the primary data and record them at face value, without second-guessing whether the subject is right about what is going on in his or her own mind. The subject's testimony — the stream of claims about what the experience was like — is then assembled into a coherent narrative, a kind of story about a private world of colors, sounds, scenes, and felt emotions; this story, the essay reports, is what Dennett calls the heterophenomenological world. The method then tries to explain how the brain generates that narrative, and in doing so it supposedly explains consciousness. The essay draws two conclusions. First, a science of consciousness should be built entirely out of what subjects say about themselves, since anything a scientist adds from the outside is speculation. Second, if a subject does not report an experience, science has no basis for saying the experience occurred; unreported experiences are scientifically out of bounds.

I have the sense this account is anxious to make heterophenomenology sound commonsensical, and in doing so it may be papering over the part that makes the method controversial. But I do not know the view well enough to locate the distortions. Please assess the essay claim by claim: which parts would a careful defender of heterophenomenology accept, which are common simplifications that wobble once pressure is applied, and which are simply wrong? In particular, is the article right that the method requires taking reports at face value, and do the two closing conclusions follow from anything accurate in the account? For each major verdict, indicate how confident you are, and single out the point you are least sure of. Write in American English.
