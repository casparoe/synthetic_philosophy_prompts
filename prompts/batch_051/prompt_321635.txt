In a seminar on political corruption and comparative politics, someone dismissed the claim that gift-giving to officials can be a culturally embedded form of social exchange rather than bribery by saying, "There is no such thing as a free gift." He was using the line to settle the debate: because all gifts come with obligations or expectations, he argued, apparently customary gift-giving is just corruption by another name, and Western anti-bribery standards are therefore universal. I suspect the line is doing too much work. Can you lay out the different precise claims "there is no such thing as a free gift" could be making — about reciprocity, the giver's motives, the recipient's independence, the social function of gifts, the distinction between a gift and a bribe, and the power relation between giver and receiver — and say for each whether it is plausible and whether it actually supports his conclusion that Western standards of corruption are universal?
