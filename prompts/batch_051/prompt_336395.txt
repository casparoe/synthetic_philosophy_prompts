A colleague claims that for an ordinary word such as 'game,' the many uses have no common essence: they are held together only by family resemblance, a network of overlapping similarities with no feature, and no fixed set of features, present in all and only the things so named. I cannot tell whether this is an empirical claim about the word's extension, about how competent speakers actually sort cases, or about the items themselves, and I suspect the claim's testability differs across those readings. Design the strongest test you can of the claim that a word's uses have family-resemblance structure rather than a hidden common core or a disjunctive set of criteria. Specify the data or elicitation task, the population and measures, the outcome that would favor each side, and exactly what even the cleanest outcome would still leave open.
