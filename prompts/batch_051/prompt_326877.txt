I'm trying to put together a "which account of Mary's room do you actually hold?" quiz for a philosophy of mind meetup, and I want it to sort people by how they diagnose the case rather than by which label they happen to like. So every question should be a concrete tweak on the standard setup (Mary knows all the physical facts about color vision but has never seen color, then she finally sees red even tho she knew all the physical stuff about it) and ask whether she'd learn or be able to do something, with answer options in plain language, not theory labels. Build the quiz out of the logical space of possible diagnoses: she gets a genuinely new fact not entailed by the physical facts; she gets no new fact but a new ability; she gets no new fact, just a new way of thinking about an old fact; she gets knowledge of a nonphysical property; the feeling that she gets anything new is a cognitive illusion; or some mix. I'd like 8-10 questions, each a small variant, like: Mary has had color vision in one eye all along; Mary has to predict what her own perfect physical duplicate would say on first seeing red; Mary is tested on red-green discrimination using only her physical knowledge; Mary hears a minute-by-minute verbal report from someone currently seeing red; Mary has the relevant visual area stimulated artificially; Mary gets a complete physical description plus all the indexical facts. Two or three answer options per question, nonjargony. Then give a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves: type-A physicalism (nothing is left out, so there's no gap), type-B physicalism (there's a genuine epistemic gap but nothing nonphysical), the ability hypothesis (knowing what it's like is know-how, not know-that), the phenomenal concept strategy (the new thing is just a distinct concept of a physical state), property dualism/qualia realism (the experience involves nonphysical properties), and illusionism about phenomenal consciousness (there are no phenomenal properties and the "gap" is a cognitive illusion). If a pattern fits no named position, say so and briefly diagnose whether that cell is empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag which leaves your quiz can't really seperate and what extra question would split them. The whole point is to sort readers by what they say about the variants, not by whether they'd call themselves physicalists.
