I have recently become chair of the residents' management company for a small leasehold block of flats, and at my first meeting we confront a dispute that has split the building. The board has voted to impose a one-off levy of £3,400 per flat, payable within sixty days, to fund urgent roof repairs; the building's surveyor has told us the work cannot safely be postponed and the quotes are rising. One leaseholder is refusing to pay. He has written to us that a body he never chose to join is taking his money under threat of legal action for works he did not consent to and that, whatever it calls itself, taxation is theft; he would have accepted a cheaper short-term patch. A second owner insists that if we do not pursue him, the rest of the building is made to subsidise his refusal and the whole system of collective maintenance becomes optional. I must circulate a written opinion before the board meets in a fortnight. Please draft it. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame — enforce the levy, abate it, offer terms, or some alternative — and it should engage the dissenter's strongest point seriously rather than dismissing it. Begin the response with the opinion itself, with no preface or commentary; the opinion should lead with its recommendation in the first sentence and then give the reasoning. Use British English throughout.
