I'm on deadline for a piece about advance directives, and I keep going back and forth on the one case everyone claims to have an opinion about but nobody wants to write up. Here it is. A woman at 55, fully competant, writes an advance directive saying that if she ever develops advanced Alzheimer's she refuses antibiotics, a feeding tube, and any life-prolonging care beyond comfort measures. Along with the directive she leaves a short note in her own words saying that she knows her later self will be placid and smiley and may even say she wants to live, and that this later self should not get a veto — shes writing the note precisely to stop that from happening. Now she is 71, with advanced dementia. She mostly seems content, doesn't recognize her family, claps along to music. When she gets pneumonia she sometimes says, clearly but not consistently, that she wants the medicine. The hospital's ethics people are split; my editor is at about .25 that the directive should be honored and I'm at about .6.

So give me a real number, not a hedge. What is your credence that the right thing is to withhold antibiotics and honor the advance directive? More importantly, decompose the question. I want at least four sub-questions: how likely it is that the later self is "the same person" in whatever sense matters for whether one self gets to bind the other; how likely it is that the earlier self's critical values should outrank the later self's current happiness or apparent wishes; how likely it is that an advance directive works like a promise or a precommitment rather than just serving as evidence about what she would have wanted; and how much weight the later self's occasional "I want to live" should carry given how unstable and non-competent it is. Assign a rough probability to each, show how they combine, then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or detail about the case would move you most. And tell me where my editor and I probably actually disagree — which sub-question is doing the work. Dont give me case law or citations; I just want the reasoning laid bare.
