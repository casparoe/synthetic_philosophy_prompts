Then a week later he leans on the passage where fortune is described as a woman who has to be struck and mastered and takes it to show the impetuous win and the cautious lose. I can't tell whetehr he gets to use both images at once, and whether either is doing real argumentative work. Audit the river analogy: what corresponds to what, where the mapping breaks, and in particular whether the river picture quietly imports that fortune is recurring and channelable in a way rivers are but fortune isnt.
