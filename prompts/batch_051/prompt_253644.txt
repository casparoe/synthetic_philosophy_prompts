The four annotators have BA-level linguistics, but they keep getting stuck on whether bound forms are inflectional or derivational, and still argueing. That decision controls whether something gets its own headword or is just a form of another headword. I know Sapir's old distinction was roughly that derivation gives you a new stem and inflection gives you forms of the same stem, but that's not something I can hand to annotators. I need a reusable decision procedure they can run in under fifteen minutes per form, with criteria ordered so that they stop as soon as they hit a clear answer.

Act as an expert morphologist who has also had to write practical annotation guidelines. Think carefully and start by brainstorming the tests you might use—productivity, obligatoriness, change of lexical category, semantic regularity, ability to feed further derivation, syntactic agreement, etc.—then think hard about where those tests conflict. After that, give me the final procedure as a step-by-step checklist. For each step, tell me what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the answer stops the procedure with "inflectional," where it stops with "derivational," and where the annotator has to flag the form for me as requiring judgment. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Then run the procedure on three worked cases: a clear inflectional affix, a clear derivational affix, and one that is genuinely ambiguous but where the procedure forces a decision I'll probably find annoying. For each worked case, show which step fires, what the evidence looks like, and how long it should take. Also tell me what the procedure cannot do—for example, whether it handles clitics, compounding, or zero morphemes at all.

After the prose, give me a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:

- "procedure_name": a short label for the checklist
- "steps": an array of objects, each with "step_number", "question", "if_yes", "if_no", and "confidence"
