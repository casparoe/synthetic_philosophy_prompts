I'm on the board of a small family foundation, mostly we fund things like a food bank, summer jobs for teenagers, nursing scholarships — stuff where you can see the payoff the same year. I was an electrician before I retired, so I'll admit most of the cosmology in this request goes over my head. We've got a proposal from two astrophysicists at the state university asking for $35,000 over two years to pay a graduate student to reanalyze data from a recent sky survey and check whether our galaxy sits inside a big under-dense region of space, what they call a void. Their pitch is that if you're sitting inside a void, the usual way of measuring how fast the universe is expanding gets thrown off, which would feed into the ongoing disagreement about that number, and it also tells you whether the early universe was as smooth as our models assume. They're upfront that there's no product, no treatment, no jobs beyond the one student — the payout, if any, is just that something we don't know becomes known.

So the board is split. Half says this is professors asking us to fund their hobby while people down the street need rental assistance, and the other half says private foundations exist precisely so we can pay for things nobody else will. I have to write a short memo to circulate before the vote, with an actual recommendation — fund it, reject it, or maybe fund with conditions — and I want it to treat the losing side as reasonable, not as the villain. I keep going back and forth because honestly both sides feel true to me: we exist to help people, and we'd never fund something like this if we only looked at next years receipts, but I also suspect a foundation that never funds useless questions will slowly turn into just another donor to already-obvious causes. Give me the memo I can send, under 900 words, in plain words.
