I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am trying to reason carefully about a claim I keep hearing in the AI-safety-adjacent part of my field. I want your actual probability estimate for that claim, with the reasoning decomposed rather than a bare number.

Let me define the terms as I understand them. The alignment tax is the extra cost—engineering time, compute, capability, or speed—that a developer pays to make a system robustly behave as intended instead of merely improving its raw performance. The competitiveness constraint is the pressure that arises when several roughly matched actors race toward the same technical goal: any actor that unilaterally pays the tax risks falling behind one that does not.

The claim I want a credence for is this: in a race between multiple serious developers toward transformative AI, the under-provision of safety and alignment work, relative to what those same actors would choose under a binding collective agreement, is best explained as a collective action problem in which the competitiveness constraint makes under-investment individually rational for each actor—not primarily as a consequence of individual incompetence, moral indifference, or ignorance of the risks.

Please give me a number for that claim, and then decompose it. Sub-questions I expect include: whether alignment spending has the structure of a public good, with costs internalized by the paying firm and benefits partly flowing to competitors; whether the race is sufficiently winner-take-all that a single lost cycle is decisive; whether reputation, regulation, procurement, or coordination could make safety pay off for an individual firm on a short enough timescale; and whether even a cautious monopolist would under-invest for unrelated reasons, which would pull against the claim. Assign each sub-question a rough probability, show how they combine into your final number, and say which sub-question your number is most sensitive to. Finally, tell me what observation or argument would move your number the most.
