For the last few months my metaphysics reading group has been stuck on the unity of the proposition, and I genuinely can't tell anymore whether we're arguing about the world or about what counts as an explanation. The setup as I understand it: take the proposition that a is F. What makes that a single, unified content rather than just a list with items a and F sitting next to each other? Bradley's old move is that if you answer by positing a relation of instantiation gluing a and F together, the regress starts: you then need another relation to glue the instantiation relation to a and to F, and another after that, forever. Russell's response is that the regress is just a symptom of a confused demand. Relations, on his picture, relate their terms; they don't need an extra bit of metaphysical glue to do it, and asking what ties the relation to its terms is like asking what makes a predicate apply to the thing it applies to. Armstrong tries a middle path: instantiation is a special kind of tie, not a normal relation, so supposedly the regress never gets going. But the Bradleyan in our group keeps saying every one of these moves stops the regress by fiat — it's just refusing to answer the question — and that the only honest stopping point is Bradley's own view that relations are appearances and the world isnt ultimately carved up into separate terms at all.

We've all read the same papers. Everyone agrees on what each author says. Nobody moves an inch. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't about what exists — it's about what a satisfying explanation of unity would even look like, and maybe about whether the original question "what makes the proposition one?" is well-posed in the first place. Is that right? Or is there a real metaphysical question underneath about how universals hook up to particulars? I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not another defense of Russell or Armstrong. And given your diagnosis: is there anything — a new distinction, a formal result, a differently framed question — that could actually move this, or should we expect the impasse to be permanent, and if so why?
