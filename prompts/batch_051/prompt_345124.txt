For my philosophy of science course we've been doing verisimilitude: Popper's idea that we want a notion of one false theory being closer to the truth than another, and the familar result that his truth-content/falsity-content way of defining it collapses. In my algorithms class we were just doing string edit distance (Levenshtein): the minimum number of insertions, deletions, and substitutions to turn one string into another. It struck me that verisimilitude is basically an attempt to define an edit distance on hypotheses, with the true theory as the target string and "add a claim", "drop a claim", "swap a claim for its negation" as the edit operations. Is this a deep structural analogy or just superficial pattern-matching? Concretely: what would the analogues of weighted edits and the triangle inequality be on the theory side, and what does the analogy predict that actually comes out false? I can already see one disanology: in string distance an insertion and a deletion have symmetric costs, but deleting a true claim seems much less bad than adding a false one—or is that exactly what the verisimilitude literature is trying to build in?
