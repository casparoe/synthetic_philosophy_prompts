So I got promoted into a role where for the first time I can actually tell people what to do and have it stick, and I've been trying to sort out what I think about obedience and authority. I keep catching myself wanting subordinates to just comply, which is making me a little uneasy. Here is the set of things I currently believe:

(a) Deference to legitimate authority is a genuine virtue, not just a sad necessity. It has an epistemic side (the person knows more or sees the whole board) and a practical side (coordination falls apart otherwise), and a good person is often someone who can accept another's say-so.

(b) Milgram's obedience studies basically got it right: normal people will keep delivering what they believe are dangerous shocks when an authority figure is running the situation. The deference machinery can be hijacked by anyone in a lab coat.

(c) Arendt's banality-of-evil idea is also basically right: most of the worst things get done by people being conscientious underlings, not by monsters.

(d) In a military or paramilitary chain of command, there really are obligations to obey lawful orders and respect the hierarchy. A subordinate who second-guesses every instruction isn't a model anything.

(e) The just-following-orders defense fails when the order is clearly wrong.

Do these actually hang together? I can feel something pulling but I cant locate it. Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and how bad are the real ones? If you had to rank the tensions and give me the cheapest repair, what would you cut or weaken?
