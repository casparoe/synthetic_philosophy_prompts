Me and a friend have been argueing about the Flint sit-down strike of 1936–37, the one where autoworkers occupied GM's Fisher Body plants and stayed inside for over six weeks until GM signed an agreement recognizing the UAW. We both know the broad outline: the Wagner Act was brand new and on paper said workers had a right to organize and act together, but nobody had settled whether sitting down in the plant counted as protected activity or was just trespassing; the workers stayed inside specifically so the line couldn't be run with strikebreakers; police tried to retake the plant; Governor Murphy called out the National Guard but refused to use them to clear the buildings, and instead pushed both sides into a deal. So far so good.

The disagreement is theoretical. Treat Flint as a test case for the view that the right to strike is just a corollary of freedom of association: you're free to associate with whomever you like, and if association is going to mean anything for workers it has to include the right to withdraw your labor together. Map the episode carefully through that view — who has what right at each stage, what GM's obligations were supposed to be, where the state was supposed to sit, and whether the occupation part of the story, as opposed to the quitting-together part, is actually covered by the theory or is something it has to wave away. Then be honest about whether this associational story can explain how the strike ended, or whether it has to smuggle in extra ingredients: a claim against retaliation rather than a mere liberty to walk out, a right to make the plant unusable, a right to be bargained with instead of just left alone. Use the liberty-right versus claim-right distinction if it helps. Last move: use Flint to say where the voluntarist, contract picture of unions — workers freely joining forces, employers free to refuse to deal — breaks down, and whether the right fix is to add a new right, reinterpret the old one, or admit that the right to organize is really a power struggle wearing rights language. Don't editorialize about whether the strike was a good thing; I want a disciplined mapping, and I want the theory to be the thing on trial.
