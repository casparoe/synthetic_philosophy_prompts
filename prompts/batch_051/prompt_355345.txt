I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I lead an infrastructure team, and several times a week I have to make a judgment call that another senior engineer I respect disputes: whether a database migration is safe to run during business hours, whether a performance regression should block a release, whether a proposed redesign will scale under load. The stakes range from a wasted afternoon to a multi-hour outage. I usually have about ten minutes to decide, and I need a procedure my non-philosophical colleagues can follow and sign off on.

I gather that philosophers debate peer disagreement, with some saying I should substantially lower my confidence whenever someone equally competent disagrees with me and others saying I can reasonably hold my ground. I want that debate turned into a decision procedure for my actual setting. Please give me an ordered checklist of questions to work through when another engineer disagrees with me, covering at least these factors: their track record on this specific kind of problem, how independent our reasoning paths were, whether we considered the same evidence, how much of my confidence rests on evidence they have not seen, and the stakes and reversibility of the decision. For each question, say what counts as a sufficient answer to move on, where the procedure says to abandon or substantially weaken my original view or escalate the decision, and where it says I may hold my position.

Then apply the procedure to three cases:

1. I am confident a migration is safe; a colleague with a slightly better migration track record than mine reviews the same runbook and disagrees, predicting an outage under load. I am under deadline pressure to complete the migration today.
2. A colleague with an excellent general track record but little storage-layer experience disagrees with my diagnosis of a performance regression; I have profiled the system and have traces they have not inspected.
3. A colleague and I roughly agree on the probability of a rare but severe edge case hitting production; we disagree about whether that probability is acceptable enough to ship.

Please flag explicitly which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and say plainly what the procedure cannot do. I especially want to know whether the procedure gives me an answer I will not like in case 1; do not soften it.
