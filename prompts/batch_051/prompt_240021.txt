I am a novelist at work on a book whose climax turns on a confrontation in which a reformer is told he has no standing to judge the environmental record of a company in which he once held shares, even though the facts he asserts are accurate. I need the underlying moral controversy resolved before I draft the scene. Write an essay on whether a person’s own past complicity or hypocrisy undermines his standing to blame others for the same wrong. The essay should separate the truth of the criticism from the standing of the critic, weigh the three leading ways of understanding that standing objection—as an epistemic defect in the critic, as a relational fact about the critic’s position toward the target and the wrong, and as a moral defect in the act of blaming itself—and reach and defend a definite conclusion about whether the objection is ever more than an ad hominem.
