After reading Bell's 1964 paper (or the standard summaries of it), I've ended up with an argument that I keep seeing implied but never stated exactly. It goes:

1. In the singlet state, when Alice and Bob choose the same measurement axis they always get opposite results. If the particles did not share a pre-existing assignment of outcomes for every possible axis, then the only way to guarantee this perfect anti-correlation is for Alice's result to depend on Bob's measurement axis (or vice versa)—a spacelike influence.
2. Bell's theorem (and the CHSH version) rules out any local hidden-variable model in which the particles share pre-existing assignments for every possible axis.
3. Experiments violate Bell inequalities as quantum mechanics predicts.
4. Therefore, in actual Bell experiments, Alice's and Bob's results must be linked by a spacelike influence.

I suspect the mistake is in (1), maybe because the only-way claim ignores that each outcome could depend on both settings jointly without either event influencing the other. But if that's the right diagnosis, what exactly did Bell prove about locality?
