ok so im trying to get my views on Goodhart's law and specification gaming into a defensible shape before a reading group, and i'd like a consistency audit rather than a lecture. here's where i've landed:

1. i take goodhart's law seriously and pretty generally: when a proxy gets optimized directly, its correlation with the underlying target decays under enough optimization pressure.
2. but i also think the proxy/target distinction itself is shakier than people admit. for messy human values, the "target" is usually just another proxy we're less explicit about, not a clean external criterion.
3. i tend to think most famous specification-gaming examples are not the agent being sneaky; they're what happens when you perfectly optimize an under-specified objective, and the fault is with whoever wrote the reward.
4. still, i want to say that deliberate tampering with the reward channel (like editing the reward signal itself) is different in kind and genuinely bad, not just another under-specified objective.
5. i also think goodhart's law is best understood as a distribution-shift phenomenon: proxy and target are only correlated in the training distribution, and strong optimization pushes you off-distribution. so it isn't really about the agent's intentions at all.
6. practically, i'm drawn to the idea that the fix is to use a diverse portfolio of weak proxies and avoid optimizing any single one too hard, and i suspect searching for one "true" objective is a conceptual mistake.

do these hang together? please go through the pairs that are actually in tension versus the ones that only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and tell me what the cheapest revision would be for each. also: which single revision would do the most cleanup?
