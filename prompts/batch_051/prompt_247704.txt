Adam Smith opens Book V, Chapter I, Part I of *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* with the sentence: "The first duty of the sovereign, that of protecting the society from the violence and invasion of other independent societies, can be performed only by means of a military force." He then distinguishes the military arrangements appropriate to different stages of society, and in due course compares a well-regulated standing army with a citizen militia, praising the former for its superiority in discipline and effectiveness while acknowledging the danger that such a force may become an instrument of domestic tyranny. Please work through the most important implications of Smith's claim about the first duty of the sovereign for thinking about the armed neutrality maintained by states such as Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland. In particular, does the principle imply that a state committed to neutrality has a stronger obligation to maintain a credible military force than a state embedded in a defensive alliance, or only that sovereignty requires the capacity to raise force in some form? And how should Smith's preference for professional standing armies over militias bear on our evaluation of the conscription-based, citizen-soldier models these neutral states have actually adopted? I am less interested in what Smith himself would have predicted about those states than in what follows from the principle as he states it and from the institutional analysis he builds upon it.
