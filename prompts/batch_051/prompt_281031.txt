I'm writing a piece on the old fight between Schopenhauer and Nietszche about pity, and I keep wondering whether a chunk of recent psychology splits Schopenhauer's argument right down the middle. Schopenhauer thought Mitleid — feeling another's suffering arise in your own consciousness — was the one genuinely moral motive, the thing that makes their pain directly motivating for you. Nietzsche thought that same capacity was a pathology: it spreads suffering, weakens the pitier, and smuggles in a dose of self-love. Now psychologists distinguish empathic distress from compassion: the first is literally catching someone else's pain, and it turns out to be biased toward the near and cute, and a recipe for burnout; the second is a cooler concern-with-a-motive-to-help that's trainable and less depleting. So here's my question: does Schopenhauer's argument survive that split? I read him as talking about the first thing — the felt transference of suffering — and if so the science undercuts exactly the mechanism he needed. But maybe a Schopenhauerian can swap in the modern compassion and keep the structure of the argument. Which premise does the research actually bear on, and can the argument be repaired, or is it dead?
