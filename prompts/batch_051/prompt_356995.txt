For a position paper I am drafting for my philosophy of mind seminar on self-knowledge, I have been trying to clarify what I think about self-examination, and I have arrived at the following five commitments. Before I write the paper, I would like a consistency audit.

(1) I accept a broadly Socratic view: a life that never subjects its central values and plans to critical self-examination is, to that extent, a deficient life.

(2) I think that, in ordinary cases, first-person inquiry into one's own attitudes — asking oneself what one believes and why, or what one is feeling and why — is a genuine route to self-knowledge: the answers reached reveal attitudes that were already there, and this kind of inquiry tends to make us less prone to bias and self-deception.

(3) I think that rumination, understood as repetitive, negatively focused, solutionless dwelling on questions like why one feels the way one does or what is wrong with oneself, is not a route to self-knowledge and often aggravates the very moods it purports to examine.

(4) I think there is also a class of cases, familiar from work on confabulation and on how people explain their own attitudes, in which answering a why-question about one's own attitude fabricates the answer rather than discovers it: the reported reason is a post hoc construction rather than a report of a pre-existing attitude.

(5) Finally, I think the value of self-examination is domain-sensitive: it is at its best when it scrutinizes deliberative commitments, such as what to value or whether to revise a plan, and it is at its least helpful when it is directed, in the moment, at skilled performance or at emotional processes such as grief.

My question is whether these five commitments hang together. Which of them are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision that would restore coherence, if one is needed? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating all of them as equally serious, and please be committed: take a definite position on each tension and recommend the specific revision you would make, with reasons, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
