I'm doing a philosophy of language PhD and I've been going back and forth with my supervisor about whether a performative can come apart from the speaker's occurent intention without simply failing. The picture I work with says an utterance that does something like marrying or promising goes through only if (i) there's a recognised conventional procedure with a conventional effect, and the right people perform it in the right circumstances; (ii) the procedure is carried out correctly and completely; and (iii) the speaker has the appropriate intentions, thoughts or feelings and intends to behave accordingly afterwards. That all sounds tidy until you consider cases where the words aren't fully chosen by the speaker.

Here's the case I keep coming back to. Anna has locked-in syndrome and communicates through a predictive brain-computer interface, which she's used for years as her main way of speaking. The system reads her neural signals but also runs a language model fine-tuned on her own past speech, so it sometimes completes her sentences before she has settled on the exact words. During her wedding ceremony over video link, the officiant asks whether she will take her partner as her spouse, and the interface outputs 'I will.' At that moment Anna's attitude is roughly this: she does want to marry her partner, but she was still worrying about whether something the machine says on her behalf really counts, and she wouldn't have chosen exactly those words had she been typing them out deliberately. Nobody else knows the system autocompleted the sentence. Later that evening she confirms via a slow eye-tracking letter board that she stands by the 'I will.' Was she married at the moment the interface spoke? Go through the three conditions one by one and say where the case gets hard. I'm especially interested in whether (iii) should be read as requiring a precise intention to perform that particular act, or whether a broader commitment plus later ratification is enough. And does the case show that the intention condition is doing too much work, or too little, or perhaps that it isn't a single condition at all?
