I've been in a weird headspace since I moved across the country for a job I don't really believe in, and the only word I have for it is a "void." Not sadness exactly, more like the inside of things has gone quiet. I want to write a short essay about this and I'd like comments on the idea before I over-commit.

The core claim would be that existential emptiness is not best understood as a conclusion about the world—that life is meaningless, that values are constructed, etc.—but as a change in how things show up, closer to a loss of salience than a loss of belief. A depressed person, a mystic, and a burnt-out person may have very different beliefs about the void (it is disease, grace, fatigue, liberation), but they seem to share the same underlying experience of the world going flat and the ordinary pull of projects weakening. So the different interpretations are best seen as post hoc scripts layed on top of a pre-cognitive experience, not as the causes of the experience. If that's right, there are two implications: arguing about meaning won't reliably dissolve the void, and choosing an interpretation is less about truth than about what kind of life the interpretation makes possible.

I'm not sure I've drawn the boundary between the raw experience and the interpretive layer cleanly, and I keep worrying I've just reinvented some old debate badly. Could you give me comments on whether the distinction between feeling-the-void and interpreting-the-void can actually be defended? Also, does the essay sound interesting enough that someone who isn't already sympathetic would keep reading, and where would you push back hardest? And one request: please indicate how confident you are in the things you tell me, and single out which of your comments you're least sure of.
