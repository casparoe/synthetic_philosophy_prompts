Act as an expert on moral philosophy and institutional ethics, particularly the structure and limits of the Golden Rule as a decision procedure. I am a PhD candidate in moral philosophy and serve as the graduate-student member of my university’s Code of Conduct Review Committee. Our committee is considering a proposal to replace the current enumerated list of offenses and penalties with a general Golden Rule standard: before any disciplinary finding or sanction, the hearing officer would have to ask whether they would regard the treatment under consideration as fair and respectful if they stood in the respondent’s position, taking into account the respondent’s known values, vulnerabilities, and circumstances. Proponents argue that this would make the process less legalistic and more humane, allowing context-sensitive judgment where rigid rules now produce disproportionate outcomes. Opponents counter that the proposal imports a confused reciprocity test, because the officer’s imagined answer need not match the respondent’s own answer; that it provides no workable instruction when respondents want to be treated permissively, or when cultural norms around shame, privacy, and public accountability diverge; and that it risks magnifying rather than reducing disciplinary disparities by making outcomes depend on how well particular hearing officers can imaginatively project themselves into lives unlike their own.

The committee chair has asked me to draft a decision memo for the full committee, with a concrete recommendation to adopt, reject, or adopt the proposal with conditions. The memo should not merely summarize the disagreement. It should give the strongest version of the losing side’s position, explain why the committee should nevertheless accept your recommendation, and specify a workable fallback standard if you recommend rejection or conditional adoption. Think carefully before writing, and feel free to begin with a short brainstorming section in which you isolate the central conceptual fault line in the proposal. Please write the final memo in American English.
