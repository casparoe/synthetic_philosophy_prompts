I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on modal metaphysics, and I've been going around in circles on the Barcan formulas. I can handle the model theory: with varying domains, the Barcan formula ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx is valid exactly when moving to an accessible world never adds new objects to the domain, while the Converse Barcan formula □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx is valid exactly when moving to an accessible world never loses objects, so the two together force constant domains. And I know the standard argument that the Converse Barcan formula, together with the seemingly trivial premise that necessarily everything exists (∀x∃y(y=x) holds at every world), yields ∀x□∃y(y=x) — each actually existing thing necessarily exists. That's full-blown necessitism: no object could have failed to exist. It feels wild that such an enormous metaphysical thesis falls out of what look like bookkeeping rules about how quantifiers and boxes commute.

What I can't get a feel for is where exactly the modal logic ends and the metaphysics begins. Is the argument genuinely sound, so that anyone who accepts the Converse Barcan formula is simply stuck with necessitism? Or does it only go through because the quantifiers are being read in a quietly actualist way — so that "everything exists" is automatically true at every world, and the principle then pumps that triviality into a substantive conclusion? My vague suspicion is that if you switch to a constant domain plus a separate existence predicate, the formulas constrain the domain but say nothing about existence, and necessitism drops out. But then I'm not sure what the formulas are even saying in that setting: if the domain is constant and existence is a predicate, what is left of the original controversy? I'd love an explanation of what, exactly, has to be assumed or smuggled in for the Barcan formulas to imply that everything exists necessarily, and what a contingentist can coherently say at each step. Also: is it really the Converse Barcan formula doing all the work, or does the plain Barcan formula earn its keep somewhere in the argument?
