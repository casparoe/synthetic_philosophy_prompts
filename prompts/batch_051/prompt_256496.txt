I'm designing a sorting instrument for a seminar on graded sentience and moral status. Please write a decision tree whose internal nodes are concrete yes/no questions about particular cases that a participant can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. The cases must include at least these six: a honeybee with a damaged antenna; a thermostat that slowly builds a predictive model of room temperature; a decapitated frog leg that withdraws from acid; a human in non-REM sleep; a nematode that reverses direction to avoid salt; and an AI system trained to report pain and avoid damaging inputs. Each branch should separate views by a claim on which positions genuinely come apart, such as whether suffering requires a unified self, whether valence can come apart from nociception, whether behavioral aversion is sufficient evidence, whether graded information processing yields graded sentience, and whether there is a hard threshold at subjective experience. The leaves should include at least: strong gradualism, thresholdism with multiple domain-specific thresholds, functionalist sentience, a biological-nociception-first view, and, if you think it is defensible, a panpsychist or micropsychist leaf. Mark any leaf that no one defends, and flag any pair of positions your tree cannot actually separate. The point is to sort readers by their judgments about cases, not by which label they like, so no question should simply ask which theory they accept.
