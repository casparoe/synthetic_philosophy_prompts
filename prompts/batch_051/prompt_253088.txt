Build me a thought experiment with two options, A and B, scored on two dimensions, richness and pleasantness, such that: the additive view ranks A over B, the multiplicative view ranks B over A, and a lexical view with a threshold on richness — call it 2: below 2 the option is disqualified, at 2 or above pleasantness decides — also ranks A over B. So additive and lexical end up on the same side, but for different reasons. Constraints: no zeros on either dimension, all scores positive integers from 1 to 10, a mundane setting (a medical decision, an NGO choosing between two programs, a career trade-off), no utility monsters, no perfect measuring instruments — just stipulate the scores as part of the story — and keep the scenario to one paragraph. Then show the arithmetic for all three views, say exactly which structural feature of the two pairs of numbers makes additive and multiplicative flip (I suspect it's something about one option having the bigger absolute advantage and the other having the bigger proportional advantage, but check it), and tell me what happens if I nudge one score by one point — does the whole pattern collapse, and why. Finish with the strongest objection a defender of the multiplicative view would make to the case, and whether it's an objection to the arithmetic or to the whole exercise.
