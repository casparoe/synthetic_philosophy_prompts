I am currently taking a course on the philosophy of language and misinformation, and we recently read Frankfurt's essay On Bullshit. I have adopted a policy for using large language models: I treat their outputs as bullshit in Frankfurt's sense rather than as lying, error, or testimony, because the model is not trying to report what it takes to be true—it only needs to produce text that is fluent and contextually appropriate. In practice, I never treat a model's saying that p as evidence for p, and I accept a claim from a model only if I have independent grounds for it. My worry is that this may be a category mistake, since a model has no intentions, whereas Frankfurt's bullshitter misrepresents what he is up to. Is this policy defensible? Can the category of bullshit apply to the outputs of a system without intentions, or would hallucination be better classified as ordinary error, or as lying in some extended sense? Would that reclassification change how I should treat the outputs? And is there a version of the rule that keeps the protection against fluent falsehoods without discarding the cases where the model happens to be right?
