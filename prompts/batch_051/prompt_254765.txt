At department lunch a couple weeks ago I sat and listened while two colleauges of mine, Marta (religion) and Dan (philosophy of science), went at each other about whether the number 40 in scripture is a real literary symbol or just pattern-hunting. Marta's claim was that it can't be a coincidence that the flood rains last forty days, Moses stays on the mountain forty days, Israel wanders forty years, and Jesus is in the wilderness forty days. Her view is that 40 gets used at moments of testing or transition often enough that it's a convention, and reading it as mere reportage misses what the text is doing.

Dan wouldn't have it. He said 40 is a low, round, culturally available number, and "a testing period" is a theme you can drape over half the biblical storyline after you've already noticed the number. He said pick any number under a hundred and a vague theme and he could make it look just as designed; he offered to do 23 and "moments of delay." Marta said that's a party trick, because 23 doesn't actually organize the narrative the way 40 does.

Dan then asked what observation would make her give up the reading. If every place 40 appears either fits the theme or gets ignored, he said, the theme isn't reading the text, it's wearing it. Marta said that's the wrong standard for an intentional text: you don't demand falsification from a literary interpretation, you ask whether it gives explanatory fit, coheres with other conventions, and beats the best rival explanation. Dan said the best rival explanation is the one he's already giving: a round number plus later narrative selection.

Then Marta shifted a little and said even if 40 started out as a vague round number, a tradition can start using it deliberately because the earlier uses had given it a semantic charge; from that point on it's a real convention, not a coincidence. Dan said that move just runs the same inference in a loop: first the number is meaningful because it keeps showing up, then the later cases are supposed to count as evidence because the number is already meaningful. He wanted to know what independent handle she had on the earlier uses before the convention existed.

I've been turning it over since and genuinely can't tell which moves should stick and which shouldn't. Can you adjudicate the exchange step by step, not just give an overall winner? At each turn, say who's on better ground and what they'd have to concede. And say whether there's something about the difference between detecting a pattern in a text and showing the pattern was intended that both of them are missing.
