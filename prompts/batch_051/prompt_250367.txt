so me and a mate just spent the whole evening arguing about whether programming languages can be a genuine langauge of thought, and she has this wierdly consistent set of verdicts i can't quite reverse-engineer. here they are:

- doing mental arithmetic by visualising arabic numerals: yes, thinking in a symbol system.
- a chess player calculating variations by visualising the board: yes, thinking in a spatial/iconic symbol system.
- planning your day in inner speech: yes, thinking in english.
- working through an algebra problem on paper: yes, thinking in algebra.
- a deaf person signing to themselves while solving a problem: yes, thinking in sign.
- a programmer who mentally steps through their code line by line to find a bug: no, she says that's thinking about the code, or simulating a machine, not thinking in python.
- a programmer who says the answer just arrives already code-shaped after years of practice: she says she genuinely doesn't know.
- an infant solving a problem before language: thought, but not in any language.

the new case we couldn't agree on: a developer who writes and rewrites code as their main way of working through a scheduling problem, not because they need the program but because the code is how they think the problem through. would she say the code is their language of thought? i'm less interested in the answer than in the rule she's running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which further case would best tell those rules apart. oh and one more: if you had to bet, would she say a language model that writes python to solve a problem is thinking in python? please write the whole answer in British English.
