I keep going back and forth with a friend about the extended mind thesis and we can't settle it, so I want to put this to a model with more patience than either of us. The classic version is the parity argument from the extended mind debates (the Clark and Chalmers one, with Otto and his notebook, and Inga and her biological memory). Otto writes everything down in a notebook he always carries, and when he needs to know where the museum is he just checks the notebook, same as Inga checks her memory. The argument says the notebook plays the same functional role Inga's memory does — the information is reliably there, easy to get at, and he automatically endorses it without second-guessing — so if Inga's memory counts as cognitive, Otto's notebook should too, in the relevant respect. So far so good.

Here's the wrinkle. Suppose Otto ditches the paper notebook for a "second brain" app with an LLM built into it. The app doesn't just store what Otto wrote. It tidies up entries, merges near-duplicates, autocompletes half-written thoughts, and sometimes quietly adds plausible-sounding details Otto never actually recorded. Half the time those details are right and half the time they're confidently wrong, and Otto can't tell which is which until he's already relied on them. The external "memory" is now partly authored by another system with its own statistical tendencies, not an inert store of Otto's past judgments. Does the parity argument survive this? My first thought is that the premise that breaks is automatic endorsement: the paper notebook was something Otto could just trust, but the app is more like a mildly unreliable junior assistent he has to audit. But I'm not convinced, because biological memory is also reconstructive and sometimes confabulates, and nobody says Inga's memory isn't memory for that reason. So maybe what actually breaks is an unstated assumption about authorship or ownership — maybe the stored content has to be, in some sense, Otto's. Or maybe the thesis never required the external store to be passive in the first place, and this just pushes it into stranger territory.

Which premise of the parity argument actually comes under pressure when Otto's notebook starts generating its own entries (wrong ones included), and what would the best repaired version say? Is there a defensible version where the app counts as part of Otto's mind only under some fidelty condition, or does the parity strategy fall apart once the external system has its own agenda?
