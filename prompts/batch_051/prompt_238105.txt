I have been trying to think through the proposal usually called AI safety via debate: rather than trusting a single AI's answer to a hard question, two AIs argue opposite sides before a human judge, and the judge decides which side is right. The load-bearing hope is that truth has an asymmetric advantage in such a setup. A false case must rely on some premise or inference that does not hold; the side defending the true answer only has to find that defect and exhibit it; and the judge, while unable to construct the true answer unaided, can verify a defect once it has been pointed out. The proposal thus concedes that the judge cannot generate the truth, but bets that adversarial pressure will make the truth legible to them anyway.

Now suppose we introduce a complication I will call obfuscated arguments. An obfuscated argument is a formally polished case for a false conclusion in which a genuine defect has been deliberately buried: a false lemma, a slide between two senses of a term, an invalid substitution, or an equivocation that only becomes visible after several intermediate steps, placed so that an opponent with bounded time must search through a combinatorially branching space of possible objections before it can produce the decisive counterexample. The defender of the false answer is free to generate such arguments, while the defender of the true answer and the human judge both operate under realistic limits on time and computational capacity.

Does the debate proposal survive this development as stated? I am not asking whether ordinary human judges are already easily snowed by long papers; the question is whether the proposal's central mechanism fails once the false side can systematically make the flaw costly to locate even for a highly capable opponent. Identify exactly which premise of the proposal the possibility of obfuscated arguments bears on, and give a reasoned verdict whether the proposal still goes through, needs repair, or is dead for this class of cases. If it can be repaired, say which added mechanism would do the work — limits on argument length, repeated cross-examination, debate about the debate itself, or something else entirely — and indicate what new failure mode that repair opens up or merely relocates. If you think it cannot be repaired in some respects, say what would have to be true about bounded reasoners for the original hope to survive.
