My reading group keeps going in circles on the original vs. derived intentionality thing, and one of us built the following argument on top of Dennett's two-bitser. It seems to show that nothing anywhere has original intentionality, and I hate the conclusion, but I can't find the step that breaks.

(1) In Panama, a quarter-balboa coin is close enough to a U.S. quarter in size, weight, and composition that a standard vending machine takes both. So no behavioral or mechanical test distinguishes a machine that detects quarters while merely tolerating balboas from one that detects balboas while merely tolerating quarters.

(2) So the same internal state of the machine supports two equally good content assignments, call them q-content and b-content. Since the wiring, the behavior, and the dispositions are identical under either description, the state itself doesn't fix which content it has.

(3) The only thing that picks out one description over the other is outside the machine: the designers' intention, the local payment convention, whose currency it's counted in. But that is exactly Searle's derived intentionality — content borrowed from minds or social practices outside the state.

(4) The same underdetermination hits brain states. Take a neural state that is supposed to mean that my keys are on the table. Any physical, behavioral, or physiological test you specify will be consistent with indefinitely many slightly different contents — that my keys are on the table, that my metal key-shaped objects are on the table, that my keys are on a table-ish surface, and so on. The brain state's causal profile cannot pick out the one true content any better than the vending machine's state can.

(5) Introspection can't break the tie, because introspecting the content is just more neural activity, and step (4) applies to that activity too.

(6) So the content of a brain state, when it has one, is fixed by something outside the brain: an interpreter's stance, a community's use, a selection history. Which means all intentionality is derived. Derivation all the way down.

(7) Therefore original intentionality — the kind Searle says thoughts have and the vending machine only gets on loan — exists nowhere. Brains are just two-bitsers with longer causal histories.

I take it the conclusion can't be right, since it seems obvious that my thought that my keys are on the table means that before anyone interprets it. So the argument has to go wrong somewhere. But I don't want a slogan; I want the step-by-step diagnosis, with the price of each repair.
