Here are the criteria I was taught for when an advance directive made by an earlier self should bind a later, unwilling self: (1) the earlier self made the directive voluntarily, with decision-making capacity and adequate information; (2) the situation that has now arisen is one the directive was intended to cover; (3) the later self's present refusal does not express a stable, informed change of values that would merit respect; and (4) honouring the directive would not independently wrong the later self.

Now consider the following borderline case. Ankita, a 38-year-old who carries a mutation for early-onset Alzheimer's disease, writes an advance directive. One part says that, should she become unable to recognise her family, she refuses artificial nutrition and hydration. Another part says that, in the same circumstances, she consents in advance to remain in a long-term observational study requiring quarterly lumbar punctures, even if she then objects, because the data may help her younger siblings make reproductive decisions. Seven years later she has moderate Alzheimer's. She cannot follow an explanation of the study and no longer recognises her siblings, but she is generally placid. She nevertheless becomes frightened when the procedure is due, tries to push the needle away, and has been consistent in this resistance for months. The question of artificial feeding has not arisen; the only live question is whether the researchers and her family should continue the lumbar punctures over her resistance.

Check the case against each of the four criteria, and say where each criterion gives a clear verdict and where it underdetermines the judgement. In particular, consider whether there is a morally relevant difference between allowing a future self to die under a past directive and actively performing a painful, invasive procedure on a resisting present self under the same kind of directive. Does this case qualify as one in which the earlier self's authority should hold? If your answer is no, say whether that is because the criteria need revising or because this case is different in kind from the treatment refusals for which the criteria were built, and explain how the criteria should be revised. Please answer in British English.
