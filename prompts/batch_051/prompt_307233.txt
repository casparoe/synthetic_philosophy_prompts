A county parks department posts signs along its trailheads that read, in plain lettering and without qualification: Mosquitoes transmit West Nile virus. The public health office that drafted the signs knows that most mosquitoes a visitor actually encounters do not carry the virus, and that only some of the mosquito species present in the county can transmit it. The office nonetheless regards the sign as accurate and as a sound basis for urging visitors to use repellent, and it does not accept that the sign is falsified by the observation that a particular mosquito on a visitor's sleeve almost certainly does not carry the virus. At the same public meeting, the office refuses to post a second sign reading: Hikers contract West Nile virus, on the ground that very few hikers ever contract the disease and the sentence would therefore mislead.

These are both generic sentences, of the form Fs are G, and ordinary speakers accept such sentences despite exceptions (ducks lay eggs, for example). Call a prevalence semantics the view that a generic sentence is true just in case most members of the kind have the property. Call a dispositional or kind-based semantics the view that a generic is true when the kind is disposed, in its normal operation, to produce the property or behavior, even if only a minority of members display it at any time.

What philosophical commitments about the meaning and truth conditions of generic sentences does this public-health signage practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit semantics that would best rationalize the asymmetry between the two signs; distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits; identify where the practice is underdetermined by the philosophy; and explain what the office would have to change if it adopted a strict prevalence semantics. In particular, make clear under each candidate semantics whether each sign comes out true, false, or indeterminate, and whether the asymmetry survives.

Respond in American English with a single JSON object and no surrounding prose, with exactly these fields:

- practice_restated: string, one to two sentences summarizing the practice and the asymmetry.
- generic_form: string, an explicit statement of what a generic is and how these sentences qualify.
- candidate_semantics: array of three objects, each with fields name, core_claim, mosquito_sign_truth, hiker_sign_truth, and asymmetry_explained. Use a prevalence semantics, a dispositional or kind-based semantics, and one further semantics you judge relevant.
- strict_presupposition: string, what the practice must be committed to, if anything, and why.
- mere_fit: string, what the practice is compatible with but does not require.
- underdetermination: array of strings, the main points on which the practice does not choose between rival theories.
- if_prevalence_only: string, what the signage or its justification would have to change under a strict prevalence semantics.
- overall_assessment: string, a brief verdict on whether the practice is coherent and what it implicitly assumes.
