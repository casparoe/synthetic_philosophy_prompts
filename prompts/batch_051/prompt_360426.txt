I've been drafting a statement of my views for an EA-adjacent reading group, and before I circulate it I want someone to check the set for internal coherence rather than take each claim in isolation. I've picked up the following commitments from years of reading LessWrong, the EA Forum, and the occasional academic paper, and they now function as a package, though I can no longer tell which parts of the package are doing the work:

(1) Strong longtermism, in roughly the standard sense: for a broad class of high-stakes decisions, the dominant consideration is the effect on the long-run future, and this makes reducing existential risk a pressing priority.

(2) A demanding form of expected-value reasoning, on which very small probabilities of astronomically large value differences can, and often should, dominate a decision.

(3) A deep cluelessness claim: for most interventions, including many non-extinction trajectory changes, we are in no position to know the sign of the long-run effect, let alone its magnitude.

(4) A commitment to normative uncertainty as a genuine constraint: we ought to hedge across a portfolio of plausible ethical theories rather than act on whichever first-order view seems most intuitive.

(5) A person-affecting intuition I have never fully shaken — that preventing the suffering of people who will exist anyway matters in a way that creating additional happy lives does not — even though I find it hard to square with the rest of the package.

(6) A concrete practical verdict: most altruistic resources are substantially better spent on AI safety than on near-term interventions such as global health.

(7) A companion claim: none of this makes present human welfare negligible, because present institutions, norms, and values are themselves inputs to the long-run trajectory.

I would like an audit rather than a defense. Which pairs among (1)–(7) are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say precisely which commitment would have to give and what the revision would cost.
