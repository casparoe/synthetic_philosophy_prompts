I keep noticing what looks like the same structure in two places that never get talked about together, and I can't decide if I'm onto something or just pattern-maching.

On one side there's Russell's theory of descriptions. His move, roughly, was that the sentence about the present king of France being bald isn't meaningless and isn't a presupposition failure; it's just false, because the definite description parses as: there exists exactly one present king of France, and he's bald. And famously the negation is ambiguous — wide scope (it's not the case that there's a unique such king who's bald, so the negation is true) versus narrow scope (there is a unique such king, and he's not bald).

On the other side there's how modern languages handle missing or null values, specifically optional chaining. In JavaScript, `king?.isBald` doesn't throw when `king` is null; it short-circuits and returns `undefined`, a real value you can negate or compare. In a language that throws on null dereference, the same expression is more like a presupposition failure: the computation aborts before it produces anything truth-evaluable. And the scope thing seems to reappear: `!king?.isBald` reads like wide-scope negation over the whole package (there is a king, and the king is bald), while the narrow-scope reading has no clean syntax because `undefined` just propagates.

So the analogy would be: definite description ≈ nullable reference; Russell's quantified paraphrase ≈ optional chaining that short-circuits into a first-class nothing; Strawson-style presupposition failure ≈ a null-pointer exception that poisons the following negation.

Is this a real identity of structure or just the shared idea of something being absent? If it's real, does anything transfer — for instance, does Russell's reason for preferring falsity over truth-value gaps (keeping classical logic tidy) become an argument that languages should return a bottom value instead of throwing? If it's not real, give me the cleanest place the mapping breaks. And either way, name one concrete prediction the parallel makes about, e.g., how negating an optional chain should behave, that turns out wrong.

Keep it under 400 words if you can, and don't be polite about it if the analogy is bad.
