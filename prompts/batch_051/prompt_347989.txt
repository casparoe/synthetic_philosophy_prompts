I just spent a month reconstructing why a project I was part of fell apart, and the more I write the more I catch myself cheating: I keep describing the warning signs as clear in retrospect, and the story bends toward the outcome like it had to happen. I've read enough about hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy to know thats exactly the trap, but I don't know what the non-trap version looks like. Before I write another word, give me the spec: what does an after-the-fact explanation of a past event have to get right to be honest about hindsight? I want the requirements list — the cases it has to handle (near-misses, choices that looked reasonable at the time, outcomes that were genuinely overdetermined, low-probability outcomes that actually happened), the distinctions it can't collapse (explanation vs. prediction, ex ante vs. ex post justification, necessity vs. narrative tidiness, a reason being available vs. being operative) — with the case behind each, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste. Then flag which pairs of requirements are hard to satisfy at once, and which single requirement does the most work to rule out the standard just-so story. I don't need the scorecard of existing theories; the criteria are the thing I'm after.
