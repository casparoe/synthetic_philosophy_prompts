Draft the joint statement that a committed Fullerian and a committed Razian could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the question whether the rule of law is an ideal with moral value. Take the two positions to be as follows. The Fullerian holds that the internal requirements of legality — that laws be general, public, prospective, clear, mutually consistent, stable, and applied by officials in accordance with what has been announced — form an inner morality of law: a system that satisfies these requirements necessarily treats citizens as responsible agents, and a ruler's failure to satisfy them, even in pursuit of otherwise good ends, is a distinct moral defect. The Razian holds that the rule of law is not an independent moral virtue but a device whose formal excellences are like the sharpness of a knife: they make the instrument more effective for whatever ends it serves, including wicked ones, so that any moral value the rule of law has is instrumental and conditional on those ends.

Produce the document such a collaboration would yield, structured as follows. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: the propositions about legality's formal features, about the central cases that motivate each side, and about what the dispute is not, that both parties can sign, with no slanted or vacuous wording. Second, a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated precisely, with the Fullerian formulation and the Razian formulation each written so that its own side would accept it. Third, for each disagreement, specify the crux on which it turns, the test, case, or argument that both sides agree would bear on that crux, and each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. Where the disagreement is partly verbal — for example, if the two sides mean different things by moral value or by calling something an ideal — identify that explicitly and state what, if anything, remains once it is set aside.
