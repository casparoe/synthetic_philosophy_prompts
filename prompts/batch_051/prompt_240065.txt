Here's one I keep going back and forth on. Suppose a freegan holds the following line: it's fine to consume an animal product when, and only when, the thing would otherwise go to waste and your consuming it doesn't create demand or prop up the industry that produced it. Let's sharpen that into four criteria for a consumption counting as "using what would otherwise be wasted":

(1) the item would be discarded or left unused if you didn't take it;
(2) your taking it sends no market signal that would lead to more production;
(3) nobody is made worse off, including the animal;
(4) your taking it doesn't express or normalize the treatment of animals as resources.

Now the borderline case. A friend rescued four ex-battery hens. They lay more eggs than she and her housemates want to eat, so she offers me the surplus. The hens are well looked after, there's no rooster, and the eggs are unfertilized. If I turn her down, she'll feed a few back to the hens for calcium and compost the rest.

Does eating those eggs qualify as "using what would otherwise be wasted," on the four criteria above? Go through criterion by criterion. Where the criteria underdetermine the answer, say so, and then say whether that means the criteria themselves need changing, or whether the case just shows the freegan line is messier in practice then it's usually made out to be.
