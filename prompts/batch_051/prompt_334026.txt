In a philosophy of mind seminar my professor summarized the definitional problem for physicalism this way: "Physicalists claim that everything is physical, but they cannot say what 'physical' means. If it means whatever current physics posits, then physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics is known to be incomplete and will probably be revised or replaced. If it instead means whatever some future, completed physics posits, then physicalism becomes empty: it is now just the thesis that everything is whatever a future physics will describe, with no present content, and it cannot rule out even souls, since a future physics might conceivably discover them. So physicalism is either probably false on the first horn or trivially true by definition on the second. This is Hempel's dilemma, and it shows that physicalism has no stable formulation." I suspect this is partly unfair, but I am not sure where. Please go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what is accurate, what is overstated or subtly wrong, how a careful physicalist would respond, and whether the closing claim follows from the accurate parts. If you think there is a defensible way for the physicalist to fix the definition of "the physical," spell it out and say what it costs.
