In debates about private cities, charter cities, and seasteading, a common move is to argue by analogy with a competitive market. A jurisdiction is like a firm; the bundle of laws, infrastructure, and public services it offers is the product; taxes and fees are the price; and residents are customers who can, in principle, take their business elsewhere. The analogy is asked to do real work: it underwrites the conclusion that with a credible right of exit to rival governance providers, governments would be disciplined the way firms are—poor governance would lose residents, and survival would track quality—so exit could stand in for, or greatly reduce the need for, political voice and democratic control, allowing people to opt out of bad governance the way they switch away from a bad company.

Please audit this analogy rather than argue the politics directly. First, give the correspondence table in full: what plays the role of the firm, the product, the price, the customer, the competitor, the market, and the contractual relationship. Then work through the disanalogies and separate those that are merely inconvenient from those that threaten the intended use. Consider at least the following: the fixed and largely non-portable assets residents must abandon to exit; employment, family, and identity ties to place; the way law and governance come bundled with land itself; public goods and free-riding within a jurisdiction; the difficulty of saying who enforces the rules of the market for governance; the danger that a provider becomes predatory after residents have sunk relocation costs; and incentives toward collusion, exclusion, or monopoly rather than competition. For each, say whether it looks like a design problem that a charter city or seastead could price in—through exit fees, arbitration, or posted collateral—or a structural feature of governance that the market analogy was quietly concealing. Finally, assess how much of the original conclusion survives once these disanalogies are on the table: under what conditions, if any, could exit genuinely replace voice? And say what a better analogy would be, if you think there is one.
