I'm in a jurisprudence seminar where we've been chewing on the old maxim that the king can do no wrong and how it survives in modern sovereign-immunity law, and our professor handed us a statute and a fact pattern that have been driving me in circles. The statutory text, which I'm supposed to treat as fictional but realistic:

"Section 11-402 of the State Tort Claims Act. No civil action for damages may be brought against the State, or any department, agency, board, officer, or employee of the State, arising out of an act or omission of an officer or employee of the State acting within the scope of the officer's or employee's office or employment, except to the extent the State has expressly consented to suit by statute."

The facts: In 2022 the state's health department put a chatbot called "Mica" on its Medicaid website to answer questions about covered benefits. The bot's underlying model was supplied by a private contractor, but the department's staff reviewed and approved the content library that the bot draws on; the bot assembles individual answers on the fly, and no human reviews any particular answer before a user sees it. Last year Mica told a resident, wrongly, that a certain infused cancer drug was covered. The resident relied on this, began treatment, and now owes six figures; she would have chosen a covered alternative had she known. The bot also gave her a link labelled "start your appeal" that led to an error page. It is undisputed that no department employee wrote the specific sentence the resident saw; the model generated it, and the content library contained nothing that stated the false claim so bluntly. The immunity provision predates the public internet; it was drafted in an era when state information reached people through printed pamphlets written by identifiable employees. The resident sued the state and the department, and the state moved to dismiss on sovereign immunity, arguing that the chatbot's answer was not an act or omission of an officer or employee. The department call the whole thing 'an unfortunate system error.'
