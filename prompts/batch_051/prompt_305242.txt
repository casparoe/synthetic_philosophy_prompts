Okay here's an asymmetry I keep tripping over. Suppose someone does something awful, and we find out his beliefs, values, and impulses trace back to a miserable childhood and a genetic dispostion he never chose. Do we let him off? Mostly no—we treat "that's just who he is" as part of what makes him blameworthy, not as an excuse. Now suppose instead we find out his behaviour traces to a brain tumor pressing on his frontal lobe, or a drug someone slipped him, or a hypnotic suggestion. Same formal situation: the action is fully caused by something upstream of his choosing, something he never controlled. But now we say it wasn't really him and he isn't responsible. The only difference seems to be that in the first case the causes run through his ordinary character, and in the second they bypass or distort it. Is there a real principle that justifies drawing the line exactly there, or is it just a feeling about which causes are "inside" him? Stress-test the distinction against the hard cases—addiction, a personality changed by a head injury, a compulsion the person fully endorses, a tumor that's been there since childhood shaping the personality everyone knows—and tell me whether the line should be kept, moved, or dropped.
