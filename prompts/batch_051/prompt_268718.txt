I've been turning this over on my own and I want a number rather than just a pile of arguments. If the mind is finite — finite neurons, finite possible states, or at least only finitely many states you can tell apart from the inside — does it follow that a human life contains only finitely many distinct experiences, the way a finite automaton can only pass through finitely many configurations? By a distinct experience I mean the felt character of a moment: not the situation, but what it's like to be in it.

The exact claim I want your credence on is this: for any one person, there's a sense in which, after enough experience, every further moment is qualitatively identicle to some moment they've already had — so their inner life could in principle be exhausted, and after that it's all just repitition.

Don't just give me one number. I want the question decomposed. Put a rough probability on each of the following and then say how you combine them:

- finite hardware to finite experience: does a finite brain actually force a finite number of possible felt qualities, or can you get unbounded combinations out of finite parts the way a finite alphabet gives infinitely many sentences?
- whether "same experience" is even well-defined across a lifetime, rather than a loose way of talking
- whether combining old ingredients can produce a genuinely new felt character, rather than just a new arrangement of familiar elements
- whether the act of noticing, remembering, or labelling an experience changes the experience enough that it counts as a new one
- whether context, mood, age, and the rest matter for identity of experience, and how much

Then give me your overall credence and tell me which sub-question is doing the most work: if you changed your mind on just that one, your overall number would move the farthest. Also, what evidence or thought experiment would move your overall credence the furthest in either direction?

For calibration: I keep flicking between roughly 0.7 and 0.2 depending on whether I'm thinking of my mind as a finite state machine or as something whose states only exist in response to an open-ended world. Where does the disagreement between those two versions of me most likely live?
