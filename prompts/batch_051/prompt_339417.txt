I'm the sole program officer at a regional foundation that gives out maybe 30 grants a year, mostly $20k–$150k, in one area: reducing poverty in a mid-sized metro. We've been around long enough that the board has drifted into saying they want "systems change, not band-aids," but every quarter we still get a pile of modest, crisp proposals — food pantry operations, a free tax-prep clinic, rental assistance for 40 families — that look like they do immediate good but don't change any structure. Meanwhile the actual systems-change proposals (a zoning-reform coalition, trying to shift how the transit agency does its budget, an eviction-court policy campaign) are hard to evaluate, have fuzzy outcomes, and can flame out after two years with nothing to show. We dont have an eval team; it's me and a program assistant, and the board wants a one-page recommendation from me per proposal within a week.

I need a decision procedure I can actually run on each proposal, not a set of platitudes. Something grounded in what's actually known about systems thinking vs. marginal interventions — feedback loops, leverage points, attribution, reversibility, the fact that pushing harder on a low-leverage point doesnt turn it into a high-leverage one. I want the distinctions that matter to survive in the procedure, and the ones that are just consultantspeak dropped.

Write me a reusable procedure for classifying and deciding on a proposal: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is about values or risk tolerance rather than facts. Then run it on three proposals:

- a $60k ask to run a free tax-prep clinic for one year in a few low-income neighborhoods, which our own back-of-envelope says returns about $1.4m to families in refunds and credits;
- a $120k ask from a coalition trying to get the city to let renters expunge old eviction filings, which they argue changes the rental market's screening rules but is extremely hard to evaluate and might not pass;
- a $90k ask to fund a community group's campaign to replace the transit agency's fare-enforcement policy with a one-time fare-subsidy pilot, something they're pitching as a systems win but that keeps reading to me as a service program.

Format: give me the procedure as a JSON object with named fields. Here's the schema I want, roughly:

- "procedure": an array of steps, each with "step_number", "question", "what_counts_as_a_sufficient_answer", "if_no_do_what", "judgment_required" (true/false), and "why_this_step_matters".
- "worked_applications": an array with one object per proposal, each with "case", "step_by_step_outcome" (array of short notes), "classification" (one of: fund-as-marginal, fund-as-systems, reject, escalate-to-board), "recommendation", and "unwelcome_result" where applicable.
- "limits": a short array of the cases where the procedure misfires or punts, and why.

I'd also like a two-or-three sentence prose summary after the JSON saying where you think the procedure will break first in practice.
