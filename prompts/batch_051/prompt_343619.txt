I have been unable to decide whether a distinction in programming language design and a distinction in the metaphysics of causation are really the same distinction, and I would like you to assess the parallel.

On the metaphysical side there is causation by omission. A gardener fails to water a plant, and the plant dies; ordinary causal talk says the omission caused the death. But no event of watering failed to happen: the omission is an absence. One family of views holds that absences can be genuine relata of causal relations. Another holds that omission-talk is true only derivatively, relative to a contrast class or a background of normal conditions and expectations. The underlying choice is whether to let an absence occupy the role of a cause.

On the computing side, language designers face a similar-sounding choice. Many languages use null as a special value that stands for the absence of a value; because null is treated as a value, it can travel silently through a computation and produce an error only later, far from where the absence originated. Some modern type systems avoid this with an option type such as Maybe or Option: a function that may fail returns an explicit wrapper rather than a bare value, and the caller is forced to handle the empty case before using the result. Absence is represented, but it is not allowed to masquerade as a value of the original type.

The analogy could go two ways. On one reading, the reificationist about omissions is like the nullable language: both let an absence occupy the position of a thing, with the gardener's failing to water treated as a cause and null treated as a value. On the reverse reading, the option type is the more reifying position, because the absence becomes a first-class object the caller must name, whereas the deflationist denies there is an entity to name at all. Part of what I want from your assessment is a decision between these readings.

- What would transfer (about 200 words): if the parallel is deep, say what lessons from language design would and would not transfer to the metaphysics of causation by omission.
