I write about science and ideas for a general audience, and I have been reporting on a museum conservation lab that restores ancient terracotta figures. What unsettles me is the way the institution uses the notion of sameness. A clay figurine arrives in fragments, is reassembled with modern adhesives, the gaps are filled with synthetic material, a missing arm is modeled from new clay and fired, and at the end the accession record continues to describe one object with one inventory number, now displayed as a single fifth-century BCE figurine that has been restored. The same museum, however, will describe a modern cast made from the original molds as a copy rather than the original, and will catalogue a Roman marble version of a lost Greek bronze as a separate work rather than the same work in a different material. So the institution lets an object survive the loss and replacement of much of its original matter, while refusing to let identity survive the reproduction of its form in new matter, however faithful.

What theory of identity or material constitution does this practice presuppose? Does it commit the museum to the view that the figurine and the particular clay it is made of are two things that coincide for a while, so that the figurine can persist through the gradual replacement of its matter even though the original clay does not? Or can a one-object view capture the practice by saying it is the same figurine but not the same material? Where is the practice genuinely underdetermined, in the sense that several philosophical accounts of constitution and identity could license it, and where does it quietly rule a rival account out? And what would have to change in conservation, labeling, or cataloguing if the institution actually adopted the view that an object's identity goes with its material, or alternatively the view that identity goes with form alone? I am less interested in the metaphysics in the abstract than in reconstructing the theory the practice already incurs, and in what follows if the practice means what it appears to mean.
