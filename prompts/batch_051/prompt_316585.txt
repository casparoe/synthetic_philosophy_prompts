Randomized controlled trials are permitted to enroll patients only while the expert medical community is in genuine, evidence-based disagreement about which arm is superior, a requirement known as clinical equipoise. Yet the oversight system that administers this requirement applies it unevenly. A clinician who has developed a firm, evidence-based hunch that one arm is better is treated as having an opinion that does not undermine the trial's ethical standing; randomization may continue because ethically relevant uncertainty is assessed at the level of the expert community rather than the individual physician. Second, a data and safety monitoring board viewing confidential interim data may stop the trial early once a prespecified statistical boundary is crossed, on the ground that equipoise no longer holds; but while the data fall short of that boundary, the board treats uncertainty as essentially intact even when the results are trending consistently toward one arm. Third, the same placebo-controlled trial can be judged ethical in one country and unethical in another solely because the local standard of care differs, even though the scientific question and the investigational drug are identical.

Reconstruct the philosophical commitments this practice presupposes. Does it presuppose that ethically significant uncertainty belongs to groups rather than to individual physicians, and if so, what theory of the physician–patient relationship would allow a patient to be randomized under those conditions? What epistemological assumption explains why below-threshold interim data are treated as compatible with equipoise while crossing the threshold is treated as destroying it, and is that assumption coherent? Does the practice strictly presuppose that patients may be exposed to a treatment some competent experts believe is inferior for the sake of generating knowledge for future patients, or can that conclusion be avoided while still licensing the arrangement? Finally, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and identify where several rival theories could each justify the same rules.
