okay so I'm in a metaethics seminar and we just spent two weeks on evolutionary debunking arguments, and I'm honestly a bit rattled. I went through my own moral judgments to see which ones I'd actually mistrust after taking the argument seriously, and I can't tell if there's a principled line behind my calls or if I'm just keeping whatever I like. Here's the list:

- Two adult siblings having consensual sex with reliable contraception: my gut says wrong, but I now think this judgment is probably debunked. When I push for reasons I get hand-wavy stuff about boundries and "it's just not done."
- Torturing a small child for fun: not giving this one up. I don't care how well natural selection explains the revulsion.
- Pushing the fat man off the footbridge to stop a trolley from killing five: I still think it's wrong, and I don't think debunking gets this one, even though I can't give a flawless theory of why.
- My sense that cheaters and free-riders deserve punishment even when punishing them costs me more than it helps anyone: I've become suspicious of this one. It smells like a group-living adaptation.
- Dumbfounding purity/ick stuff (drinking from a sterilized bedpan, wearing a laundered sweater once owned by someone evil): debunked, all of it, I don't miss it.
- My partiality toward my own kid over a stranger in equal need: I'm torn. I can't bring myself to treat them as equal, but I don't trust the strength of the feeling either.
- Judgments of the form "it's wrong because it's unnatural," apart from any harm: gone.

The closest I can get to a line is something like: distrust a judgment when the feeling stays after the reasons for it have been knocked out. But that doesn't explain why I'm suspicious of the cheater-punishment one, since I can rattle off fairness and desert justifications for punishing free-riders.

So here's what I want. Don't give me a survey—commit to the best principle you can defend, whether it's a repaired version of my line or something else entirely, something like "a moral judgment is debunked iff...," and use it to sort my list. Tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and which ones I have to give up. Then apply it to the case I keep flip-flopping on: whether it's wrong to break a promise made to someone who has since died, when nobody alive would ever find out.
