I'm the chair of the board at a small Christian high school, and our Bible department chair is up for contract renewal. Our statement of faith is Calvinist/Reformed — unconditional election, saving grace that can't finally be resisted, perseverance of the saints. The teacher has been teaching what half the board calls Arminian: that election is based on God's foreknowledge of who will believe, that God gives everyone enough prevenient grace for a genuinely free choice, and that a real believer can walk away and lose their salvation. He still holds total depravity and gets annoyed about being lumped with Pelagius. He keeps telling us he's actually closer to Augustine on free will than we realize, and he cites the early Augustine at us, which honestly goes over half our heads. I'm not a theologian; I run a hardware store. My gut says this is an in-house fight and we shouldn't lose a good teacher over it, but I can see the other side: if grace is resistible and election is conditional, maybe assurance and the whole shape of the gospel shift.

The board is split. Half say it's a salvation-level issue and renewing him means endorsing a different gospel. The other half say both positions sit inside historic Christianity and we shouldn't fire people over fine print. Our statement of faith doesn't spell out the contested points, just a few proof texts, and our accreditor hasn't weighed in.

I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. Make it a real decision memo: recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame (renew, don't renew, or renew with conditions), and engage the strongest point on the losing side instead of talking past it. Also, in the memo or in a note at the end, seperate what's actually settled here from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff, and don't present a contested reading (like what Augustine really meant) as if it's the consensus.
