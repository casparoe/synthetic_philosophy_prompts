I keep going back and forth about actual infinities. On paper I'm basically convinced by the Cantorian line: once I accept that two collections have the same size exactly when there's a one-to-one correspondence between them, it's hard to avoid the conclusions that the evens are as numerous as the naturals and that a full hotel can make room for a new guest without anyone leaving. The bijection criterion is clean, applies uniformly to finite and infinite cases, and gives a crisp answer where my pre-theoretic notion of size just shrugs its shoulders. But I also can't shake the intuition that a whole is strictly greater than any of its proper parts, and that a hotel with no unoccupied rooms is full, end of story. I know the standard move is to say the intuition is reliable for finite collections but simply doesn't extend, but that feels like dissolving the problem rather than answering it. Should I drop the part-whole intuition as a finite-only bias, amend or restrict the Cantorian criterion, go finitist about completed infinities, or is there a reading where both are tracking distinct notions of size? Give me the strongest versions of each side and tell me what would actually justify each move, not just which side you'd pick.
