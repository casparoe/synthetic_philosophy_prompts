Since a close relative's gambling debts forced our family to intervene, I have been following a proposal before our city council and want to understand how it would actually operate, not merely whether it sounds noble. The proposal creates a Contentment Authority, explicitly inspired by Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor and the claim that many people would trade the burden of freedom for the security of having their choices made for them. The Authority may prohibit a consumer good or leisure activity — gambling, short-form video, high-interest credit, alcohol, and similar products — but only under three rules: (i) the Authority's own five-year panel must find that heavy users of the activity report lower life satisfaction than matched non-users at every annual wave; (ii) a majority of current heavy users, when surveyed, must say they wish they had never begun; and (iii) every prohibition lapses after five years unless reauthorized by a referendum limited to residents of the neighborhoods where the activity's use is most concentrated. The Authority is barred from regulating speech, work, family life, and religious practice, and its board is appointed by the mayor. Analyze how this institution would behave: what it would likely prohibit first, what its staff would be incentivized to measure and how that measurement would be gamed, how firms would adapt, how the referendum would function in practice, what second-order effects it would have on the population's capacity for self-governance, and whether the failure modes could be corrected by modifying the three rules.
