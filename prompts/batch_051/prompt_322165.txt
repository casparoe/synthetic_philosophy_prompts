I've been working through a hypothetical counter-terrorism statute drafted in the late 1970s, and I keep returning to a case I cannot settle. The statute provides: "For the purposes of this Act, 'act of terrorism' means an act of violence against persons or property, or the credible threat of such an act, if it is committed with the intent to intimidate or coerce a civilian population or to compel a government or an international organization to do or to abstain from doing any act, and if it is not committed in the course of an armed conflict governed by the law of war."

The case is this. A non-state group opposed to a peacetime foreign military base in its country gains unauthorized access to the national emergency alert system and, at two in the morning, broadcasts to a major city a fabricated warning that a chemical plant upwind has ruptured and that residents must evacuate at once. The plant was never in danger. In the evacuation that follows, at least a dozen people are killed in traffic accidents and many more are injured. The group then issues a statement saying that it attacked no one, that the broadcast was a demonstration of the government's inability to protect its population, and that it will continue such disruptions until the base is withdrawn.

Does this statute treat the broadcast as an act of terrorism? Work through what the words of the clause say, what the purpose of the provision suggests, what the drafters would most plausibly have said if the case had been put to them in 1978, and what a reasonable person today would take the clause to mean. Say which approach should govern here, and whether the fact that people died changes the analysis. Please write in continuous prose, as you would in a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
