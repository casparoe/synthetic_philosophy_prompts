I'm giving a departmental colloquium next term drawn from my dissertation, and I want to be ready for a question period I expect to be politely hostile. The talk defends a claim the audience will resist: that counterpart theory for de re modal predication should be adopted even by actualists, and that the usual actualist reasons for retaining strict transworld identity are inherited from a picture of possible worlds those same people claim to have rejected. My argument runs roughly as follows. If possible worlds are abstract, maximal ways things could be rather than concrete universes, then a possible world does not literally contain you or me; it represents us. So the only real question about whether the Humphrey of some world is the same person as the actual Humphrey is whether the relation the semantics tracks must be identity or only something identity-like. I argue that a contextually governed counterpart relation does all the representational work with fewer commitments about essence, and that the standard objections—the Humphrey objection, the non-transitivity and non-symmetry of the counterpart relation, the worry that essential predication becomes unstable across contexts—depend on assumptions only a modal realist is forced to accept. The audience will be the department's metaphysicians and philosophers of language: mostly actualists who think of possible worlds in the abstract, ersatzist way, treat rigid designation and Kripke-style semantics as settled, and include two or three people working on two-dimensional semantics. None of them are Lewisians, and appeals to parsimony about concrete worlds will land flat with them.

Write me the Q&A prep sheet for this talk: the seven or eight objections I will actually get from this audience, not the objections I would get from modal realists and ordered by how likely and how damaging they are. For each, phrase the objection the way a member of this actualist, semantics-literate audience would put it, give a two-minute reply, and note the precise point at which the reply is soft and whether I should concede or hold. Include the question I should be most afraid of, phrased bluntly, with a short explanation of why it threatens the argument.
