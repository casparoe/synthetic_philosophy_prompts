I spent my career working in statistical physics, so I habitually think in terms of state spaces, symmetry constraints, maximization under uncertainty, and equilibria. Since retiring I have been reading political philosophy, and Rawls's veil of ignorance has begun to look to me like an argument I should be able to formalize: choose principles of justice behind an information constraint that makes your own social position unknown, and you end up with something like a maximin rule, which then yields the difference principle. I would like you to restate the original-position argument for the two principles of justice, and especially the argument for the difference principle, in the vocabulary of statistical decision theory and constrained optimization. Treat the veil as a specific ignorance condition, the interchangeability of persons as a symmetry or uniformization assumption, and the difference principle as a decision rule or objective function. Be faithful: do not smuggle normative premises into the translation and call them technical results. Then give me a residue report. Which parts of Rawls's position map cleanly onto that vocabulary, which parts map only with distortion, and which parts have no counterpart at all? Finally, tell me whether the residue indicates that my scientific vocabulary is too thin to carry the argument, or that some of Rawls's machinery is doing work that is not really formal.
