I would like your credence in a precise thesis about how partial beliefs should change in response to evidence, together with the reasoning that produces it. Please state each sub-credence as a number between 0 and 1 and give one or two sentences of justification for each; then give your combined number and explain how you combined the components.

Thesis. Jeffrey conditionalization is a genuine and correct norm of partial-belief revision. Let a partition of possibilities be a set of pairwise incompatible and jointly exhaustive propositions E₁,…,Eₙ. An uncertain-evidence update occurs when an observation leaves no proposition certain but leads the agent to adopt new probabilities q₁,…,qₙ over the cells of an evidential partition. Jeffrey conditionalization says the rational posterior is then fixed by

P_new(A) = Σᵢ qᵢ P(A | Eᵢ).

That is, each proposition's probability changes only because the partition cells are re-weighted, while every probability conditional on a cell Eᵢ stays exactly as it was (the rigidity condition). For example: a cloth may be blue, green, or blue-green; seen in dim light, your probability that it is one of the greenish colors rises without your becoming certain which it is. The target thesis says two things: (a) some such updates are genuinely irreducible — they cannot be represented, for every reasonable prior, as strict conditionalization on some proposition learned with certainty; and (b) whenever an uncertain-evidence update supplies a determinate input distribution over an evidential partition, the uniquely rational posterior is the one Jeffrey conditionalization gives.

Please decompose your credence in this thesis into at least the following components.

1. Existence. Your credence that there are rational updates of the uncertain-evidence kind described in (a): cases where an experience's evidential effect is best modeled as a shift in probabilities over a partition, not as learning a proposition with certainty.

2. Determinacy of the input. Your credence that, in at least the central cases of genuine uncertain evidence, the experience itself makes one privileged partition and one determinate distribution over it rationally appropriate — as opposed to delivering only a constraint such as an inequality, an interval, or a conditional-probability requirement, which would leave Jeffrey conditionalization without a well-defined application.

3. Rigidity. Conditional on a determinate input distribution over an evidential partition, your credence that the rational posterior must leave all credences conditional on each cell unchanged, so that the entire revision consists in re-weighting the cells.

4. Partition-boundedness. Your credence that re-weighting the cells of the input partition captures the full rational effect of the evidence. Consider especially cases where an experience intuitively bears only on one coarse partition but a rigid update seems to leave untouched finer structure that common sense would revise; give your credence that such cases are best handled by correcting the input partition or adding a separate element of evidence, rather than by abandoning the rule. Answer for the principle itself rather than for any particular example.

5. No successful rival. Your credence that every alternative general treatment of uncertain evidence — strict conditionalization on an experience proposition, minimizing an information-theoretic divergence such as cross-entropy, treating the evidence as a notional experiment with a known likelihood (virtual evidence), or some non-probabilistic revision rule — either fails or agrees with Jeffrey conditionalization in the cases where there is a determinate input distribution.

Finally, identify the component you are least confident about, and describe one concrete finding, argument, or case that would most shift your credence in that component.
