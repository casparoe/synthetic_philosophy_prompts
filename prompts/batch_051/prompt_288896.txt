I'm on my university's student-conduct panel, and we're stuck on a case that's got us arguing about what blackmail even is. An undergrad, Sam, got a D on a final paper from Professor Abrams and filed a grade appeal, but the appeal sat unreviewed for three weeks. While gathering materials for the appeal, Sam found (on a shared department printer) emails showing Abrams was in a consensual relationship with Lina, a PhD student Abrams supervises — which violates our consensual-relations policy. Sam then sent Abrams a note saying, roughly, that he didn't care about the professor's private life and just wanted the paper re-read against the rubric, but that he'd hate to have to submit an appeal packet with documents about "other things." Abrams re-graded the paper from D to B-minus three days later, then reported Sam for blackmail. Sam's line is: there was no explicit demand, and either act alone — telling the university about the relationship, or filing a grade appeal — would have been permitted, so combining them can't be misconduct.

We have to issue a decision memo this week, and I've been asked to draft it. Please write a short memo (700–900 words) recommending whether the panel should find Sam responsible for blackmail, and what sanction (if any) should follow. Put your recommendation up front. Treat the strongest consideration against your recommendation fairly: if you say responsible, deal seriously with the "two lawful acts can't become wrongful by being combined" point; if you say not responsible, deal with the worry that this lets students extract grade changes through barely-veiled threats. After the memo, give me a JSON object with this exact schema: "recommendation" (one of the strings "responsible", "not_responsible", or "responsible_no_penalty"), "rationale" (an array of exactly three strings, each one concise sentence), "strongest_argument_for_losing_side" (a string), and "sanction" (a string describing the penalty, or null if none). Just put the JSON right after the memo, not in a code block.
