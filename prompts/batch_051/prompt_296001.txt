I've spent way too much time in comment sections arguing about whether having kids is morally okay, and the two sides seem completely stuck in the same loop. The antinatalist side, as I understand it, says pain and pleasure are not symmetric: if a person is never born, the pains they avoid count as a good thing, but the pleasures they miss don't count as bad, because there's no one there to be deprived of them. If the person is born, their pains are bad and their pleasures are good. Since you can't ask the kid first, bringing someone into existence always leaves them net worse off than never having existed. The other side says this is basically wordplay: "avoiding pain" only means something when there's an actual person who is better off, and comparing a real life to the absence of a life is comparing something to nothing, so the whole ledger is meaningless.

What frustrates me is that nobody in these arguments seems to disagree about the ordinary facts. Both sides know kids can suffer and kids can be happy, both sides agree you can't get consent from someone who doesn't exist, and yet neither side moves an inch. My working theory is that the real disagreement isn't about pain or kids at all, but about whether "good for" and "bad for" need a subject to make sense, or whether nonexistence is even the kind of thing you can run a cost-benefit analysis on. I'd like a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a survey of positions. Is this a verbal dispute, a difference in starting intuitions, a hidden factual question, or are the two sides just answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually move the debate, or is it stuck for good? Take a definite position and commit to it with reasons, rather than laying out the options.
