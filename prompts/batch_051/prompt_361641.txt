I work in software, and one analogy keeps showing up in professional conversations about chip supply chains as much as in op-eds about trade: a country is like a person, so since self-reliance is a virtue for individuals, economic self-sufficiency—autarky—should be a goal, or at least a presumptive ideal, for a nation. I have seen this analogy used to defend onshoring semiconductor fabrication, food and energy independence, and import substitution, with the claimed payoffs being resilience, freedom from coercion, and something like national self-respect. Others reply that nations are simply not persons and that countries grow richer through specialization and trade.

I want an audit of the analogy, not a policy verdict. Please address the following in order:

(1) Make the mapping explicit: which features of the personal case are supposed to correspond to which features of the national case. A table of correspondences is fine, and it counts toward the limit.

(2) Separate the respects in which the mapping holds from the respects in which it breaks, and say which disanalogies matter specifically for the inference to autarky and which are irrelevant.

(3) Identify the features of personal self-reliance that the analogy quietly imports into the nation case without argument—for example, features involving agency, self-respect, vulnerability, or the availability of contracting out.

(4) State what the analogy can actually license once the disanalogies are on the table: full autarky as an ideal, only resilience through diversified dependencies, or something else.

(5) If a better analogue exists—a household, a firm, a body, a network—say which one and what it would license in place of the person–nation analogy.

One point I want handled explicitly: a person we praise as self-reliant usually remains deeply dependent on doctors, teachers, friends, and built infrastructure; the virtue concerns how they manage dependencies. Say whether, by the analogy's own lights, the nation-level target should be elimination of dependencies or diversification and redundancy of dependencies. Also flag whether a retreat from full autarky to the position that autarky is a regulative ideal rather than a literal policy is stable, or a slide between two claims.

Keep the entire answer to no more than 800 words; the correspondence table counts toward that.
