I have been reading Aristotle on nature and change alongside recent arguments about whether optimization and goal-directedness are real features of the world or only useful instrumental descriptions. The four causes keep entering my thinking, and I want to work out how they apply to a circumstance Aristotle could not have anticipated: a modern machine-learning training run. The obvious mapping is that the loss function plays the role of final cause, the optimizer with backpropagation plays the role of efficient cause, the network architecture is something like the material cause, and the trained weights constitute a new formal cause. But this mapping seems too quick. For Aristotle, an artifact's final cause exists in the intention of the craftsman, while a natural thing's final cause is internal to its form and its characteristic activity. A trained network seems caught between these categories: its objective is a scalar function that no one fully intends in detail, and the resulting behavior is often opaque to its designers, yet the system unmistakably acts in a directed way.

Suppose one could give Aristotle a high-level account of stochastic gradient descent, backpropagation, reward functions, and the way training generalizes only imperfectly outside the training distribution, without dragging him through the linear algebra. What would he say is responsible for the network's directed behavior? Would he identify a final cause, and if so, where would he locate it: in the objective function, in the researchers who selected that objective, in the dataset, or in the trained system as a new kind of composite being? Or would he argue that this is a case where the notion of cause is being stretched, because an arbitrarily chosen scalar reward is not a genuine end or good, only a measurement instrument? I would like the answer to pay close attention to Aristotle's distinction between natural and artifact causation, to mark the places where the machine-learning analogy does and does not fit, and to say what he would predict about the conceptual confusions that arise when modern vocabulary such as objective, optimizer, target, and goal is used as if it were teleology.
