My girlfriend has a very specific, almost suspiciously consistent set of verdicts about when someone actually owes gratitude, and since my stepfather died this has stopped being just an abstract hobby for me. The cases I've collected from her over the years:

- A rich uncle you barely know leaves you a chunk of money because he died before he got around to updating his will, and he never liked you. She says you got lucky; no gratitude owed.
- A stranger jumps into a river to save your kid. She says huge gratitude, the kind that basically never expires.
- A coworker puts in a good word for you mostly because she wants an ally at the new company, and it becomes your big break. She says you owe gratitude, but a middling amount, not the deep kind.
- A friend throws you a surprise party after you'd said repeatedly that you wanted a quiet night in, and the party is a disaster and you hate it. She says you still owe the friend real gratitude, maybe a bit less than if it had gone well.
- An ER doctor saves your life during an ordinary shift. She says no debt of gratitude; a sincere "thank you" is plenty.

The case I need her to rule on, but don't want to raise directly until I've thought it through: my stepfather paid for most of my schooling and left me money when he died. He genuinely worked hard and gave up comfort to provide it, and he did want me to have a decent life. But the gifts were basically control devices: he'd only pay for courses he approved, he reminded me constantly that I owed him, and he used money to keep me close and steer my choices. My own instinct is that I don't owe him much of anything, maybe not any gratitude at all, which people around me find crazy.

Predict what my girlfriend would say about the stepfather, and more importantly: what are the plausible rules she's actually running, which one best explains her record, and which of my five collected cases is the closest analog under that rule? Then give me a compact sixth case—a hypothetical I could describe to her over dinner—that would cleanly seperate the live candidate rules. Tell me too whether you think she's running one rule or two (say, one for strangers and one for close people), because I can't tell which would move her more: that he sacrificed for me, or that the whole thing came with strings.
