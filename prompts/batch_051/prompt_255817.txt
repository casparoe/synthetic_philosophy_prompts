Ok so in my epistemology class we've been doing Descartes through Hume, and we just spent a couple sessions on the problem of the criterion, Chisholm's way. I'm struck by how little the debate seems to have moved in fifty years: the particularist says look, I know there's a hand in front of me, so let's use that as a test case; the methodist says that's question-begging, because you can't pick a good test case without already having a criterion; and the skeptic says both of them are just assuming there starting points. Everyone seems to agree on the structure of the trap, and yet they all land in different places anyway. My working theory is that this isn't really a disagreement about knowledge at all — it's a disagreement about when it's okay to stop asking for justification, i.e., about what kind of starting point is acceptable in philosophy. Is that the right diagnosis? And if it is, does the debate simply stay stuck forever, or is there a move that would actually count as progress — and what would it look like? I don't want a survey of the literature, I want the structural diagnosis.
