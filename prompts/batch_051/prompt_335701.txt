I'd like a working definition of *complicity* — that is, the relation one stands in to a wrong when one is complicit in it, rather than its principal author or a mere witness. The definition should make explicit how much work is done by causal contribution, knowledge, and endorsement or intention. It should return the right verdict for these cases: (1) a getaway driver waiting outside a bank, knowing her passenger is robbing it; (2) a passerby near the same bank who notices nothing; (3) an investor holding one share in an index fund that includes a mining company known to be dumping waste, aware that selling that share would change nothing; (4) a file clerk at an immigration agency that, as a matter of policy, separates detained families, who knows what the policy does but only processes records that someone else would process if she resigned; (5) a customer who buys a phone made under abusive labor conditions, aware that her single purchase will not alter the supplier's behavior; (6) a citizen who pays taxes to a state fighting a war she votes against and publicly opposes, where nonpayment would be illegal and would not affect the war; (7) a bystander who watches a beating and does nothing, believing the attackers would ignore anything she said. Please run your definition against each case, revise it where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, defend any surprising verdict you keep, and then say how confident you are in the final definition and which single verdict or clause you are least sure of.

Give the deliverable as a single JSON object with this schema:

- "definition": the working definition, in one or two sentences.
- "verdicts": an array of seven objects, each with "case" (a short label), "verdict" (exactly one of "complicit", "not complicit", or "borderline"), and "rationale" (one or two sentences applying the definition).
- "revisions": an array of any cases where a draft definition gave the wrong answer or no answer, each with "case", "problem", and "fix"; leave empty if none.
- "confidence": an object with "overall" (one of "low", "medium", or "high") and "least_secure" (one sentence naming the single point you are least sure of).
