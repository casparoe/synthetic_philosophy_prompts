I rewatched Pretty Woman last week for the first time in maybe ten years, and I can't shake the feeling I'd been watching it wrong my whole life. I always filed it as just a 90s rom-com, but around the same time I went down an internet rabbit hole about how different countries handle sex work: the Nordic model where selling is basically legal but buying is a crime, Germany and the Netherlands where it's legal and regulated like a job, and New Zealand where they just decriminalized it all. Now the movie reads like it has an opinion on that whole debate, even though it's obviously also a fairy tale.

I'm supposed to lead a discussion at this little film group my library runs, and most people there aren't film people; a couple of them do street outreach with sex workers, so they'll call bullshit if I just do the usual Cinderella reading. Can you help me read Pretty Woman through that policy lens? Where does the movie treat what Vivian does as work, where does it treat it as degradation or as something she needs to be rescued out of, and where does it just refuse to look? I keep coming back to the opening pickup on Hollywood Boulevard, the Rodeo Drive shopping scenes, the polo match where her arrangement gets exposed, and the ending where she turns down the condo deal and holds out for the fairy-tale rescue. Does the film end up endorsing something like legalization, or the rescue logic people pin on the Nordic model, or is it too much of a fantasy to track any actual policy? And where should an honest reading admit the movie resists carrying that much weight?
