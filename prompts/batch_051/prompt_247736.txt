My argument in one paragraph: trust and trustworthiness feed each other, so high-trust societies build institutions that deserve trust while low-trust societies build defensive, box-ticking ones that underperform; high-trust citizens aren't credulous, they participate and demand transparency more, so their trust acts as a standing demand for accountability; and the costs of default suspicion show up in tax morale, compliance, and willingness to share data in a public health emergency. I also claim the American version is mostly a cultural prior rather than something responsive to evidence — it's distrust of the federal government across the board even where agencies are objectively doing fine — whereas the Scandinavian prior happens to be calibrated to institutions that are mostly trustworthy. Give me a Q&A prep sheet for after the talk: the objections in the order they'll actually come, phrased the way these people would put them, what I should concede on the spot, what I should hold, and the softest point in my paper that I should prepare to defend rather than hope nobody notices.
