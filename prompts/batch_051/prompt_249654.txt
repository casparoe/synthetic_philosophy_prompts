I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I keep coming across a pair of claims that are usually run together: Darwin inverted the commonsense view by showing that design-like competence can arise from a mindless process, and Turing inverted it in a different way by showing that a machine can do a task competently without grasping what the task is about. Before I read any more specific proposals, I want the full specification: what would a theory of competence without comprehension actually have to get right? Give me the requirements list, with the reason each is a requirement. It should cover at least the canonical cases the theory must get right or explain (evolution by natural selection, a calculator doing arithmetic or a machine-translation system producing fluent output, a person mechanically following an algorithm); the distinctions it cannot collapse (for example, between competence that merely does not involve comprehension and competence that excludes or competes with it, or between the competence of an evolved system and the competence of an engineered one); and any results from biology or computer science it cannot contradict. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of a particular camp, flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out sloppy versions of the idea. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivation rather than on summarising the authors who have defended the idea. Please answer in British English.
