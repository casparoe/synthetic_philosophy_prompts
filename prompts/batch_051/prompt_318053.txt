I am the fairness review officer for a county pretrial services agency. We evaluate vendor risk-assessment instruments every two years and decide whether to approve them, require modifications, or reject them. Our decisions bind judges and probation officers, but neither the county board nor the judges will read the technical literature; I have two weeks per vendor and no in-house machine-learning statistician. I need a reusable evaluation procedure that translates the impossibility results in algorithmic fairness into a concrete screen for vendor claims.

In their 2016 paper “Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores,” Kleinberg, Mullainathan, and Raghavan write:

“We formalize three fairness conditions that lie at the heart of these debates, and we prove that except in highly constrained special cases, there is no method that can satisfy these three conditions simultaneously.”

Build me a decision procedure for evaluating a vendor’s risk-assessment instrument from that result. It should be a checklist or flow in words, usable by staff without statistical training, that determines: which fairness conditions a vendor must demonstrate, under what conditions a vendor may claim all three, what evidence we should demand for each, and what the screen can and cannot settle. Include the ordinary cases in which the theorem forces us to choose among fairness criteria, and identify the exact point where the county board must make a policy choice rather than a technical one. Apply the procedure to two hypothetical vendors: one whose instrument is well calibrated and equalizes false positive rates across groups but fails to equalize false negative rates; and one whose instrument satisfies all three conditions because its scores are perfect predictors. For each, say what the procedure requires and whether the county board would like the outcome. Keep the procedure reusable across vendor reviews, not tailored to this year’s bids.
