I'm trying to settle how I should update my credences when I learn only a conditional whose antecedent I am not sure is true, and I want a probability estimate for a concrete case, with the reasoning decomposed.

Mara is lost at night in an underground complex. It has three sectors — North, East, and West — and each sector has an inner ring and an outer ring, so there are six possible zones: North-Inner, North-Outer, East-Inner, East-Outer, West-Inner, and West-Outer. Before any radio message, Mara's credence distribution over the six zones is as follows: North-Inner 0.20, North-Outer 0.10, East-Inner 0.15, East-Outer 0.25, West-Inner 0.05, West-Outer 0.25.

Base then sends a message she treats as fully reliable: "If you are in the North or East sector, you are in the inner ring." Let A be the proposition that she is in North or East, and B be the proposition that she is in the inner ring, so the message has the form A → B. On its face it rules out North-Outer and East-Outer and no other zone directly.

The dispute is whether anything else follows. One treatment reads the message as the ordinary proposition ¬A ∨ B — either she is not in North or East, or she is in the inner ring — and says she should conditionalize on the resulting elimination of North-Outer and East-Outer in the usual way. The other treatment reads the message as a constraint on the conditional probability P(B | A): it should move the probability of Inner given North-or-East to 1 while telling her nothing about whether she is in A at all, so the probability of A should stay at 0.70 and the probability of West should stay at 0.30.

I have the impression that the two treatments agree that North-Inner and East-Inner should stay in a 4:3 ratio and that West-Inner and West-Outer should stay in a 1:5 ratio, and that the entire dispute reduces to the 70/30 split between the A-block and West. Correct me if that diagnosis is wrong.

Please give me:

1. Your credence, from 0 to 1, that the second treatment — preserving P(A) at 0.70 — is the correct rule for updating on a conditional of this kind.
2. The posterior distribution over the six zones and over the three sectors that you recommend, under whichever rule you settle on.
3. A decomposition of that answer: the sub-questions or components your posterior depends on, with a rough probability or weight for each and a one-line justification. At minimum, address the prior distribution, the choice of update rule, how the probability mass within A should be divided between North-Inner and East-Inner, and whether the odds within West should remain 1 to 5.
4. Which single component or sub-question your recommended posterior is most sensitive to, and what additional message or evidence from base would do the most to shift it — for example, a later correction from base, a second conditional, or information about how the sensors work.

Then tell me how confident you are in the recommended posterior and its components overall, and single out the point you are least sure of.
