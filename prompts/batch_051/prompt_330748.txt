I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics. For the past year I have been convening a reading group in which an Everettian and a de Broglie–Bohm pilot-wave theorist have argued about quantum mechanics, and they have repeatedly talked past one another. Write the document they should have produced: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration. It should record what the two sides actually agree on, state their remaining disagreement in wording each side would accept, and identify the issues on which a settled answer would move at least one of them.

Set up the two positions as follows. The Everettian holds that the quantum state is the complete physical state of the universe; that the Schrödinger equation always applies; and that decoherence explains why components of the state stop interfering and why observers within a branch see definite records and relative frequencies. The Bohmian holds that the complete state is the universal wave function together with a definite configuration of point particles evolved by the guidance equation; that the wave function obeys the Schrödinger equation exactly; that particles always have determinate positions, which is what makes measurement outcomes definite; and that the Born rule describes ignorance about the initial configuration, which is distributed according to quantum equilibrium.

The document should have four sections. First, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, phrased so that neither side would regard the wording as already conceding its position. Include their shared commitments about the formalism, the empirical success of Born-rule statistics, the role of decoherence, and whatever both accept by way of rejecting an ad hoc collapse postulate or textbook Copenhagen as a fundamental solution. Second, a disagreement table stating the residual disagreements in neutral wording, with each side's own view formulated as that side would formulate it. Distinguish real disagreements from merely verbal ones: in particular, say how much separates the Bohmian claim that decohered but unoccupied wave-function components are real-but-empty branches from the Everettian claim that they are equally real worlds, and how much of that difference is substantive rather than verbal. Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one side to revise. I suspect the main cruxes concern the coherence and status of unoccupied wave-function components; whether Everettian probabilities, derived from self-locating uncertainty or rational-choice constraints, and Bohmian probabilities, derived from typicality over initial configurations, genuinely earn the Born weights rather than assuming them; and whether Bohmian particle positions explain records better than, or merely differently from, Everettian decoherent histories. Fourth, for each crux specify a concrete argument, thought experiment, or empirical case that both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side would predict or argue. If you invoke weak-measurement trajectory reconstructions or tests for quantum non-equilibrium, describe them in your own words rather than citing them.

Do not include citations, footnotes, reference lists, or attributions to named authors, and do not write a verdict about which interpretation is correct. The document should be something both participants could honestly sign, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than papered over.
