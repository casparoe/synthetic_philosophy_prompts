In my philosophy reading group we've been stuck on moral luck for months, and I've realised I can't even tell what kind of disagreement we're having, let alone who's right. Take two equally drunk drivers: one gets home fine, the other hits and kills a child. Or two people take a shot at someone: one misses, one kills. One side says the two agents are equally culpable, and harsher punishment for the unlucky one is just penalising luck. The other side says the outcome changes what there is to answer for; a moral psychology that treated killing and trying as the same wouldn't be one humans could actually live in. Every case and every argument has been aired, and no one has moved an inch. This debate has apparently been going round in the same circles since the 1970s, so it isn't just us.

So I want a diagnosis of the disagreement, not a verdict. Is it verbal — one side meaning 'blame' as a judgement of the agent's quality of will, the other meaning blame as a social response to the harm? Is there a hidden empirical claim about what ordinary punishers are actually tracking? Or is it just a bare clash between a control intuition (nothing unchosen should count against you) and a 'harms matter' intuition, with no common ground left? My hunch is that its partly verbal with a real difference about the point of blame underneath, but I'm not confident.

Given whatever diagnosis you settle on, tell me what would actually count as progress here — a distinction, a change of question, an experiment, some assumption being dropped — or whether the impasse just means the question is ill-posed and we should expect no progress, and why.
