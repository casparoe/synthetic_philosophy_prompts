Consider a research collective, a political faction, or an online forecasting community that must sometimes act on questions where the evidence is inadequate and delay is itself a decision. The community wants to preserve William James's idea that a genuine option — one that is living, forced, and momentous for the believer — may legitimately be settled on passional grounds, while still taking seriously the Clifford-style principle that one should not hold a belief on insufficient evidence. It therefore adopts an internal rule.

The flagged-conviction rule:

1. A member may avow a belief held on passional or practical grounds only when the question is a genuine option for that member, and only when the belief is explicitly tagged as passional in every communally visible context.
2. A tagged belief may not be used as a premise in binding collective decisions, such as policy votes, hiring, publication, or resource allocation. It may be used only to justify actions that are framed in advance as experiments, hedges, or reversible bets, and only when the person avowing it states what evidence would lead them to retract it.
3. Whenever a belief is tagged, the community must devote a fixed fraction of its common resources to testing it. If no feasible test has been proposed within a set interval, the tag lapses and the belief may no longer be avowed through community channels.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than a moral position, and walk through how it would actually operate. What does it push members to tag, and what does it push them to leave untagged? How would it be gamed — relabeling passional beliefs as experimental hypotheses; laundering a tagged belief into collective action through a chain of reversible bets; designing tests that cannot fail; capturing the testing budget with unfalsifiable causes; timing tags strategically to preempt rivals? What second-order effects does it produce for status inside the community, for which questions get taken up at all, and for the community's capacity to act quickly in forced and momentous cases? Where does the rule break down first, and which of those failure modes can be repaired by amending the rule rather than abandoning it? Throughout, say how confident you are in your major claims, and single out the point you are least sure of.
