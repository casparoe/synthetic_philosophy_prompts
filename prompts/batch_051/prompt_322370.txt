But at the same time, there's this long-standing thing called the absurdity doctrine, where a court will refuse to apply the plain wording of a statute when applying it would lead to a result that is, well, absurd—something the enacting legislature obviously couldn't have meant to bring about. So a judge who would never look at legislative history will nonetheless say: the text says X, but X is so crazy that no rational legislature could have wanted it, so we read it as not-quite-X.

I'm puzzled about what has to be true, philosophically, for that maneuver to be legitimate.
