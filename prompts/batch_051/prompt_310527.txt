I teach first grade, and I just had a long argument with a friend about whether a mark in one of my students' words was really an 'a' or a 'u'. The word was 'cat' and the kid left the top of the a open, so it looked like a u with a little tail. My friend said: no closed loop, no a, full stop. I said it's an a because the kid read it back as 'cat' and no one writes a u there. We went in circles for an hour, and I realized I've been grading this stuff for years with no explicit rule, just vibes. I want a decision procedure I can actually run while grading handwriting: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure says yes its an a, where it says no, and where it says the marks alone cant settle it and I need to ask the kid or look at context. It has to handle the hard cases — single-story vs double-story a, an a that looks like an o, a d with a short stem, letters that are only readable because of the surrounding word — and it should flag the steps that are really judgment calls dressed up as procedure, especially where the real question is whether I'm grading letter identity or just the style my school happens to teach. I grade dozens of papers in a sitting, so it can't take more than a minute or two per decision, and it has to be something I can explain to a parent who disagrees with a grade. Then run it on three samples: (1) 'cat' where the a is open at the top and looks like a u; (2) a single-story a written in isolation on an alphabet sheet, no word context; (3) 'dad' where the middle letter could be an a or a d depending on how you read the stem.
