Okay so I've been calling this idea 'archive gravity' and I want someone to actually tell me if its a real concept or just me renaming something obvious. The claim is: once something gets slotted into a record-keeping system, it becomes easier to record again, because the system already has a folder, a tag, a page, or a caption template for it. So an archive doesn't just sort the past — it steers the future, by pulling new recording toward whatever it already captured. Not on purpose, more like momentum.

At my school you can see it with the yearbook: basketball has had a two-page spread forever, so the committee assigns a photographer every season without even thinking about it, and they got a full spread this year in a losing season. The robotics club started two years ago and actually placed at regionals, but there was never a slot for it on the photo list, so it got one blurry hallway candid, and now kids talk about it like it barely exists. The early gap turned into what looks like an importance ranking.

I don't think this is just the thing about the winners writing history, because nobody is trying to erase robotics. The archive is just lazy, or it has inertia. I've heard of the Matthew effect and someone told me this is just path dependence, but I think archive gravity is narrower: it's about the filing system itself doing the pulling, not reputation or success. Maybe I'm wrong about that. Is 'archive gravity' a real mechanism or just a metaphor with a new paint job? Has someone already named it under an better name? And where does it break — can you give me a case where getting recorded early does NOT snowball, or where an archive fixes its own gaps? Please actually take a side and defend it, don't just lay out three options and leave me to pick.
