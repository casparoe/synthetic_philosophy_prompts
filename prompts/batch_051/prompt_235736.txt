I'm an evo bio PhD student trying to get my adaptationism straight by trusting my gut on cases. Here's where I land. The panda's 'thumb' (enlarged radial sesamoid): adaptation for stripping bamboo, even though its a clunky reuse of an existing bone. The human chin: not an adaptation—just a byproduct of facial reduction, and I've never bought the armor/ornament stories. The giraffe's recurrent laryngeal nerve looping all the way down the neck and back up: not an adaptation, just a developmental and historical leftover. Sickle-cell heterozygotes in malarial regions: adaptation; selection is obviously keeping the allele around despite its cost. The blind spot in our retinas: not an adaptation, just a side effect of the retina being wired 'backwards.' I can see I'm probably being inconsistent: I call one kludge (the panda's thumb) an adaptation and another kludge (the blind spot) not, and I can't say what the principled difference is. Then there's the case I can't settle: human menopause—sometimes if feels like an adaptation (grandmother hypothesis), sometimes like a byproduct of our long post-reproductive lifespan. Is there a principle that captures all my confident verdicts? Which of them does it force me to drop? And what does it predict about menopause?
