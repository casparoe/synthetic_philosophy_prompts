Okay so I’ve been stewing on this since last Thanksgiving. My cousin Marcus — sweet guy, adequate lawyer, no complaints — inherited a four-bedroom house from a great-aunt he’d met maybe three times. Totally legal, nobody contested it, and he’s been genuinely generous about it. But now he hosts every holiday and family gathering, since he’s the one with the guest rooms and the big kitchen, and I’ve noticed his preferences have quietly started winning: when we eat, what we do, who gets to stay over, what counts as a reasonable plan. Nobody voted for this. And I don’t want the house, I don’t even want to host. What bothers me is that the family’s internal structure of standing — whose say matters — shifted because of an inheritance, which is basically a lottery.

I’m trying to figure out whether what I feel is envy in the vicious, small-spirited sense, or what Rawls calls excusable envy: a reaction to an inequality that damages the social bases of self-respect, rather than just spite at someone having a nice thing.

Here’s my working criteria for excusable envy:

(1) the envied advantage is unearned — the person didn’t do anything that would make a nuetral observer say it tracks merit or effort;

(2) the advantage damaged the envier’s standing, not just their material position — it changed whose voice carries weight in some shared practice;

(3) the feeling is aimed at the inequality itself, not at the person — if the advantage stopped buying standing (say the house stopped being the default venue), the feeling would fade without needing Marcus to be knocked down a peg or lose the house;

(4) the envier would endorse a rule that kept that kind of advantage from conferring that kind of standing, even if they themselves never got the underlying good.

Run the Marcus case through this. Does it qualify as excusable envy? Try to be honest about where the criteria do real work and where they’re vague — e.g., is “standing inside a family” the kind of standing criterion 2 needs, or am I stretching Rawls? And if one or two of the criteria misfire on this case, say whether that means the case is a disguised clear case, or the criteria need changing.
