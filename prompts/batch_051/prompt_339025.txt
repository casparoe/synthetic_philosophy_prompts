I've been asked to write a short statement of where I stand in metaethics, and before I circulate it I'd like to know whether the package is internally coherent. Here are the commitments I'm thinking of including, in rough form:

(1) I'm a moral realist: there are moral facts, and their truth does not depend on what anyone or any culture happens to approve of. (2) I'm also a naturalist: moral facts are natural facts, the kind that could in principle figure in empirical explanations, and I'm sympathetic to the Cornell realist view that moral properties are natural kinds whose identity with descriptive properties is discovered a posteriori rather than by definition. (3) I think Moore's open question argument is decisive against analytic naturalism — the project of defining moral terms in purely descriptive vocabulary — but I don't think it threatens the synthetic naturalism in (2). (4) I think Mackie's argument from queerness is a serious problem for non-naturalist realism, but once you go naturalist it loses its force. (5) I think widespread moral disagreement is best explained by ignorance, bias, and distorting social pressures rather than by the absence of moral facts. (6) I think evolution has shaped our moral intuitions and that this debunks some moral beliefs — specifically, the ones where the evolutionary explanation of the belief does not go through the truth of the belief. (7) I accept the is-ought gap: no moral conclusion follows from purely descriptive premises alone.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each pair in tension, say how severe the tension is and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Also flag any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, so I can see where I don't actually have a problem. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on metaethics, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
