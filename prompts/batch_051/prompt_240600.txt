I keep noticing what looks like the same pair of options in two places that ought to have nothing to do with each other, and I want help deciding whether the parallel is real or just pattern-matching.

The first place is a debate in metaphysics about how ordinary objects persist through time. One view, which I've seen called endurantism, says that a thing is wholly present at every moment it exists: the mug on my desk now is numerically the same mug that was there this morning, and change is just that single thing gaining or losing properties. The rival view, perdurantism, says that what we call the mug is really a four-dimensional object spread out through time, and what exists at any one moment is only a temporal part of it, the way one frame is part of a movie rather than the whole movie.

The second place is something I noticed while learning to code. Some programs treat an object as one thing with a stable identity, and changing it overwrites its current state: there is still one object, now holding different data. Other programs — the kind common in functional programming, or in version control like Git — never overwrite anything. Each change makes a new version, and the file or the game is really the whole series of versions; any single version is only part of it. There even seems to be an analogue to the question of whether past and future moments are real or only the present is real: some software keeps all the old versions around, while other software discards the old state as soon as it is overwritten.

The mapping looks almost too clean: endurantism lines up with mutable objects that keep their identity through change, perdurantism lines up with a sequence of immutable versions, and the question of whether old versions are kept lines up with the question of whether past times exist.

Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is genuine, does anything actually transfer from software to metaphysics — for instance, do the practical reasons programmers give for preferring immutability (easier to reason about, no side effects, paid for in copying) line up with the arguments metaphysicians give for perdurantism? And where does the mapping break down in ways that show the two debates are not the same debate after all? I would also ask that you distinguish what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and not present a contested view as the consensus.
