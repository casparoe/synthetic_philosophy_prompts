Now that I'm retired I've been reading political philosophy, and one thing keeps bugging me. The same person will often say both of these: (1) a person has no moral obligation to obey a law just because it is a law, if the law is seriously unjust, and (2) someone who breaks such a law on purpose as a protest should accept the punishment, and the judge is not doing anything wrong by sentencing her. But then the state is punishing a person for an action we also say was morally permissible. Is there a principle that makes these two judgments cohere, or is one of them just a reflex we should drop? Give the strongest version of the principle you can find, and test it on two cases: a law that is clearly unjust but doesn't affect the disobedient personally, and a law that is only mildly unjust. If you can't make the principle work, tell me which judgment you'd give up and why.
