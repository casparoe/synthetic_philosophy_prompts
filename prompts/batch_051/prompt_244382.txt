I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology trying to choose a three-year dissertation project, and I keep circling around the gap between Aumann's agreement theorem and the epistemology of disagreement. The theorem says, roughly, that if two Bayesian agents start from a common prior and update on private information by conditionalization, then at any state where their posterior probabilities for a given proposition are common knowledge, those posteriors must be equal. That looks like a deep result about disagreement: it seems to show that rational agents cannot knowingly persist in differing beliefs, at least under ideal conditions. But I've never been able to see how much weight the result can actually bear, since real arguers don't share a common prior, don't have common knowledge of one another's exact credences, and don't update on well-defined partitions of a state space.

I'd like a defended, dependency-aware prioritization of the open questions in this vicinity, written for someone with my background: solid probability and epistemic logic, but only a passing acquaintance with game theory. The questions I'm weighing include whether the common-prior assumption is a substantive rationality constraint or just a modeling convenience; how much of the theorem survives if common knowledge is weakened to something like approximate, delayed, or finite-level mutual knowledge; whether the result's real upshot is a constraint on ideal agents or a norm for actual ones; what happens when the agents don't merely have different information but carve the space of possibilities up differently; and whether the formal result actually bears on the peer-disagreement debate in epistemology, or whether that debate is asking a question the theorem can't touch. For each question, I want to know why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is given my background, and what evidence over the next couple of years would move it up or down the ranking. I'm less interested in a survey of the literature than in a reasoned agenda: which question should I sink three years into, and what would have to be true for that to have been the wrong call?
