For months I have found myself returning to one piece of music that I can only describe as grief-stricken. The music does not make me cry; as far as I can tell, it does not make me sad at all. What it does is hold my attention in a way that feels almost like being in the presence of someone else's sorrow—the slow tempo, the drooping melodic lines, the way the harmony keeps sliding away from resolution. When the piece ends, I am usually not sad; sometimes I feel oddly invigorated by it. And yet calling the music sad feels exactly right to me, not merely metaphorical.

A friend who knows the piece dismisses this as confused. She says that when we call instrumental music sad, we are really just reporting that the music makes us feel sad, or that it belongs to the kind of thing that tends to make listeners sad. Since I have admitted that it does not make me sad, she concludes that either I am deceiving myself about my own feelings or I am using the word "sad" carelessly.
