I run a fiction workshop for about twelve adults, and we're reading Hobbes because my next novel turns on characters who hand someone else authority and then resent it. I want a ninety-minute session where participants actually feel the pull of the state of nature before anyone mentions the name. Design a game or simulation: they start with small stakes and a scarce resource, there's no real enforcement of promises, and the incentives are set up so that by the end they've stumbled into preemption, the fear that comes from rough equality, and a genuine desire for a sovereign on there own. The rules and payoffs should do all the work, no lecture up front. Then give me a debrief that maps each moment of the game onto the actual steps of Hobbes's argument, points out where a player could have resisted and what resisting there costs, and is honest about what the game cannot capture—for instance whether the move from mutual submission to an absolute, unaccountable sovereign is forced, or whether the state of nature needs to be a real historical exit rather than a fiction. What should the participants come away seeing, and what usually goes wrong when you run something like this?
