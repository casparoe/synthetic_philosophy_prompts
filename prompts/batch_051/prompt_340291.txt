Can you help me pin down what 'simpler' should mean when people say Occam's razor tells you to prefer the simpler explanation? I keep going in circles on this. I half-remember something about Kolmogorov complexity and Solomonoff induction, where you say the complexity of a thing is the length of the shortest program that produces it, and the prior probability of a description drops by half for every extra bit of program length, or something like that. I've also heard that changing the programming language only changes the lengths by a constant, and I can't tell whether that's a deep fix for the fact that simplicity seems to depend on what language you're speaking, or just a fudge.

What I want is a working definition of what makes one hypothesis simpler than another, the kind I could actually apply when two hypotheses both fit the data. Then run the definition through these cases and tell me where it breaks or gives a weird answer. First, a ten-thousand-bit string that just repeats 01 over and over, versus a ten-thousand-bit string from fair coin flips; i'm pretty confident the first is simpler. Second, a physical theory with two equations but ten free parameters, versus one with twenty equations but one free parameter; which one is simpler, and why? Third, a short slogan like 'things fall because they seek their natural place' versus Newton's three laws plus universal gravitation. By word count the slogan is simpler, but that feels like the wrong kind of simplicity. Fourth, a short program that runs for a billion years and prints out a long complicated-looking string; is the string simple because the program that generates it is short? Fifth, the first million bits of pi versus a random-looking million-bit string that has no short description. The pi bits look random but they're generated by a short prescription, so are they simple? Sixth, the old riddle where you compare 'all emeralds are green' with 'all emeralds are grue', where grue means green until the year 2100 and blue after that. Both fit all the emeralds anyone has seen so far, and 'green' feels like the simpler hypothesis, but I can't say what the simplicity amounts to.

My own views are a mess: the first case feels clear, the rest I genuinely don't know, and I suspect 'simple' is doing several different jobs at once. So propose the definition, run it through all six, say which verdict you are least sure about, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict.
