I spent my career in an ecology department, population genetics mostly, and since retiring I've gone down the philosophy-of-biology rabbit hole, especially the stuff around what Dennett calls competence without comprehension. A few months ago I joined a reading group that is mostly machine-learning people, and they keep running an argument I cant quite pin down. They say training a network with gradient descent just is Darwinian evolution in miniature: you start with a nearly random collection of weights, the loss function supplies the selection pressure, the stochasticity of batching and initialisation supplies the variation, and after enough generations you get a competent system that no one designed and no one can explain. A textbook case, they say, of Darwin's strange inversion of reasoning. From this they conclude that interpretability is close to a lost cause: expecting the internals of a trained model to be legible is like expecting a seabird's wing to be legible engineering, so the sane approach to safety is to manage models the way we manage wild populations — watch their behavior, adjust the selective environment, and give up on reading their minds.

I find this suspicious, but mostly because I know the biology too well to just nod along, and I have a feeling the analogy is carrying the conclusion rather than merely illustrating it. So I would like you to audit it. How far does natural selection as an analogy for gradient descent actually hold, when what it is being made to support is that anti-interpretability conclusion? Give me an explicit correspondence table — what plays the role of the genotype, the phenotype, the environment, fitness, variation, inheritance, population size, drift, speciation, and whatever else matters. Then say where the mapping breaks. I especially want the disanalogies sorted into the ones that matter for the interpretability conclusion and the ones that are just differences of bookkeeping, and then I want a verdict on whether the interpretability conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing the whole job. If it cant carry that conclusion, say what a better analogy would be, or tell me if no analogy will do and why.
