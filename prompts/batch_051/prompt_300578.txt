Someone I know keeps citing the gender-equality paradox — the pattern where more gender-egalitarian countries often show bigger, not smaller, sex differences in things like STEM enrollment or occupational preferences. His line is: once you take away legal and economic barriers, what's left is mostly innate difference, so stop trying to engineer parity. That seems way too fast to me. Could be wealth, could be the equality indexes are measuring the wrong thing, could be that richer countries just let people follow whatever stereotype they've internalized in comfort, could be STEM is defined differently across countries. I want something sharper than dueling anecdotes. How would you actually test the claim that the paradox reveals innate differences in interests rather than culturally produced ones? Walk me through a design, say what result would count for each side, and — this is the part I keep getting stuck on — say what a positive result would still not settle, including what the word innate is doing there and whether we should change hiring or education practices.
