I've been going round in circles on origin essentialism — whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg — because every time I settle on a rule one of my own cases breaks it. A friend raised IVF and identical twins and I realised my intuitions were all over the place, so I want to write them down and see whether there's a principle behind them. All the cases ask the same question: is the person described me?

(1) Fertilisation happens five minutes later than it actually did because the evening went differently; same sperm, same egg. I say: still me. (2) The same sperm and egg meet two years later, both gametes having been frozen and used in IVF. I say: still me, though I'm less sure. (3) Same egg, different sperm from my father. I say: not me, a sibling. (4) Same sperm, different egg from my mother. I say: not me, another sibling. (5) The zygote from my actual sperm and egg splits into monozygotic twins. I say: neither twin is me; I'd simply never have existed. (6) But if that same split occurs and one twin dies before birth while the other is born healthy, I'm inclined to say the survivor is me. (7) A clone is made from my DNA later on. I say: plainly not me. (8) The case I can't settle: the same zygote is transfered to a different woman and gestated by her; my gut says that child is still me, but I can't say why the womb should be irrelevant when the identity of the gametes matters so much.

The rough shape I keep reaching for is that what matters is being the same biological individual — the same organism — rather than the same genome or the circumstances of gestation. But (5) and (6) together make a mess of that, because the survivor in (6) only counts as me because the other twin died, and that's exactly the sort of extrinsic, relational fact my principle keeps trying to treat as irrelevant. So: is there one principle that captures most of these verdicts, which of them does it force me to revise, what does it say about the case I can't settle and cases I haven't thought of, and should I keep the twin pair at the cost of some epicycle or drop one of them?
