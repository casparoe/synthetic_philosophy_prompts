Got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether we owe it to people to be legible, and now I'm trying to pin my view down before I have to defend it in seminar. Heres what I think I think, roughly:

(a) There's a real interpersonal duty to be legible — to make your feelings, intentions, and practical reasons readable to at least some people — and I don't think it's just a special case of the duty not to lie, or the duty to answer truthfully when asked. You can be scrupulously truthful and still make yourself unreadable.

(b) I also hold that people have a right to opacity. No one is entitled to the contents of your inner life, and even an intimate doesn't get to demand an explanation of every state they'd like to understand.

(c) The ground of the legibility duty looks like reliance to me: once you've let a relationship develop so that the other person reasonably counts on being able to read you (for shared plans, for emotional coordination), you owe them readable signals. Where no one reasonably relies on reading you, opacity is fine.

(d) I want to say legibility only demands that the other person can tell where they stand and what you're asking of them — not that you disclose the underlying state. So a partner who says "I'm not okay right now, and I don't want to talk about why yet" is perfectly legible without surrendering opacity, while one who says "I'm fine" in a tone that clearly means otherwise has failed at legibility, not merely at honesty: the message doesn't let the other person know what to do.

(e) But I'm also drawn to the thought that obligations are about what an agent does and controls, not about how the recipient happens to take it. You can't owe someone that they'll understand you, since their understanding isn't yours to produce. At most you can owe them a good-faith effort at clarity.

Does this set hang together? Specifically, tell me whether (d) is doing illicit work by splitting "where I stand" from "why I feel this way" in a way that just quietly redefines the right to opacity, and whether (e) undermines the whole idea of a duty to be legible given that being legible is an uptake notion. Rank whatever tensions you find by severity, say which are merely apparent, and give me the least costly revision for the worst one.
