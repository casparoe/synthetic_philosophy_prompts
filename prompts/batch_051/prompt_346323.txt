I've been thinking about knowledge and skepticism on my own, and I keep running into Nozick's tracking theory. A popular philosophy book I was reading summarizes it like this: "Nozick says knowledge is true belief that tracks the truth. To track the truth, a belief has to be one you would hold if it were true and would not hold if it were false; in other words, your belief couldn't easily have been wrong. That is why Nozick says you can know you have hands without knowing you are not a brain in a vat. Your hands-belief is sensitive to how things are, but your not-a-vat-belief is not, since if you were envatted you would still believe you were not. Nozick therefore rejects the principle that knowledge is closed under deduction, and he says closure holds only when the ordinary use of the word 'know' demands it." I can't tell how fair this is. The last sentence especially feels wrong to me, but I don't have the background to say why. Could you go through the summary and tell me which parts a careful epistemologist would accept, which are harmless simplifications, and which parts actually misstate Nozick — in particular, whether the claim about closure follows from the accurate parts?
