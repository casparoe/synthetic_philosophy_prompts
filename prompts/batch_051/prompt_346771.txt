I train LLMs and RL agents, so I naturally think in terms of policies, reward functions, rollout trajectories, world-models, checkpoints, evals, and emergent behaviors. Lately I keep bumping into arguments about whether a sufficiently advanced digital mind could actually deserve moral concern — not just whether it could act like it suffers, but whether there could be something it is like to be it, whether it could have interests or welfare, and what would settle that from inside my field. I'd like you to translate the main positions in that debate into ML vocabulary as faithfully as you can, then report on what doesn't survive.

Take at least these views: substrate-independence (the hardware doesn't matter; only the right functional organization does), sentientism (only felt experience matters, so a system that behaves exactly like a sufferer but feels nothing has no welfare), and some version of the view that only biological systems can count — because they have evolutionary histories, homeostatic needs, or carbon-based feelings. Maybe add a middle position: a digital system could matter iff it had a real equivalent of pain, not pain-language that was just in the training distribution.

For each, say what translates cleanly into my terms — things like valenced reward signals, preferences over trajectories, self-models, mesa-optimizers, world-models as stand-ins for a point of view, and the difference between a model whose pain reports just track the training distribution and one with something closer to all-things-considered preferences. Then say what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression at all in my vocabularly. I don't care about the policy question of what we should do with current models; I care about where the translation breaks.

Finally, the residue report: once the translation is done, what is left that I literally cannot say in ML terms — and should I take that as evidence that my toolkit is missing something and needs extending, or that those leftover claims were doing less work than they looked like? Be brutal: I'd rather hear that my vocabulary is too behaviorist than watch you smuggle in raw feel as if it were a latent variable.
