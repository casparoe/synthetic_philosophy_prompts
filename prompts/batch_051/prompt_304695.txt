I've been reading through the literature on the value problem in epistemology — the Meno question, why knowledge is better than mere true belief — and I've hit the same wall I keep hitting: the debate has been running since Plato's Meno, the last few decades of epistemology have sharpened every move, and nobody moves, but nobody will say what kind of disagreement it actually is. The shape of it: Plato frames it as a puzzle about why knowledge is more valuable than correct opinion, Gettier then blows up the justified-true-belief analysis, and the value question survives as its own subfield. One camp says knowledge is valuable because it's an achievement, something you earn by exercising intellectual virtue, so its value doesn't reduce to the value of the true belief sitting inside it. The reliabilist, by contrast, says knowledge is just reliably produced true belief; the trouble is that once you already have the true belief, the reliability of the process that produced it seems not to add any further value — the swamping problem — so it's not obvious where the supposed extra value of knowledge comes from. A third camp says the whole thing is a pseudo-question manufactured by out standards of explanation: you define *knowledge* however you like and then ask whether your definition is valuable, which is just a question about the definition, not about anything in the world.

My working hypothesis, and I don't fully trust it: the impasse is an ambiguity in the word *better*. Some people are asking whether knowledge is *instrumentally* better (it gets you more true beliefs later), some whether it's *finally* or intrinsically better, and some whether it has a normative status that's neither of those. If that's right, the debate isn't substantive, it's three smaller debates sharing a word. But I can also tell a story where the disagreement is about something else: whether value has to be located in a state (the belief) or in a process (the knowing), or whether the real dispute is over philosophical method — conceptual analysis versus "what would an ideal cognitive agent care about." I don't want a survey of positions. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself: is it verbal, substantive, several questions under one heading, or ill-posed? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an experiment, a change of question — or should we just stop expecting this one to end?
