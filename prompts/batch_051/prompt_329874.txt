I got stuck on something a while back and I want your help thinking it through. I'm not an academic, never took a philosophy class, so I only know the rough shape of this from a debate I heard between Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso, plus my own late-night spinning. Sorry in advance if I mangle the labels.

The theory side keeps pulling me in. Every part of who I am — my temper, my self-control, the things I care about, even what I end up choosing — comes from causes I didn't choose: genes, parents, the neighborhood I grew up in, the luck of what situations I was put in, brain chemistry. If you trace any choice back, you eventually hit causes that were set before I existed. So the idea that a person could ever be the ultimate source of their own actions looks false. And if that's false, then the retributive idea — that a person deserves to suffer for what they did, over and above protecting people or deterring anyone — looks like it rests on a made-up self standing outside the chain of causes. The theory has this clean, hard-to-argue-with logic to it, and it says nobody ever truly deserves punishment.

But then I think about actual cases and the intuition comes back louder than the argument. Take someone who tortures a child over months and enjoys it. I can say all the right things about bad genes and bad luck and it dosen't touch the feeling that this person deserves what's coming to them — not because it deters, not because it keeps him off the street, not because it might fix him, just because he deserves it. That feeling doesn't feel like a leftover caveman impulse. It feels like noticing something real. The theory says the intuition is a useful social glue with no truth to it. But how do I tell whether that's a real debunking, or the theory deciding ahead of time that my intuition can't count as evidence?

I've also heard the compatibilist-sounding move that tries to have it both ways: we may not have ultimate desert, they say, but we still have everyday desert. People can still be held responsible because they're the kind of creatures who deliberate and control themselves, and that's enough to make a certain kind of "you earned this" true, the way the rules of a game are still real even though no one chose to be born into the game. I can't tell if that saves the intuition or just changes the subject to something more polite, a punishment that's really about teaching or protecting with the word "desert" kept around for comfort.

So here's what I want from you. Between the theory (nobody ever truly deserves punishment) and the intuition (some people genuinely do), which one should give? Or is there a reading where both can be true without cheating? I don't want you to tell me which side wins. I want to know what it would cost to drop each one, and what would actually justify dropping the intuition versus amending or abandoning the theory. If the compatibilist move works, say plainly what it preserves and what it quietly throws away.
