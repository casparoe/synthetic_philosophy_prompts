for my philosophy club we're doing consciousness and i got the user illusion view. the idea is that the whole rich inner world—the mind's eye, the inner voice, the sense of a self sitting in the theater—is more like a computer desktop: a simplified interface that hides the machinery underneath, and the user watching the screen is also just another icon on the screen, not an actual homunculus. i want to present a pantoum about it (the form where the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third of the next, so lines keep coming back in new positions). that looping structure seems perfect for the way the view keeps swallowing its own tail: you notice the screen, then you ask who's looking at the screen, then the theory says the looker is part of the display, and you're back where you started but one level down. could you write it so two of the recurring lines are roughly 'there is no viewer, only the view' and 'but someone has to be watching', and the form does the work of showing why the second line wont stay dead? i dont want it to just name drop qualia or phenomenal consciousness—someone who knows the user illusion view should recognize it, and someone who doesnt should still get a good poem. and the ending should leave the tension open rather than declaring the illusion real or the feeling proof of anything, since honestly the club could not agree either.
