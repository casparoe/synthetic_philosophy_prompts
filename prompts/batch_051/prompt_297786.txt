Im trying to work through Boethius’s solution to the foreknowledge problem in the Consolation, Book V, prose 6, and theres one passage I keep tripping over. My own rough rendering: Philosophy first defines eternity as the whole, simultaneous, and complete possession of an endless life, and says a temporal thing that lives through time can never grasp this, since it is always losing the past and waiting on the future. Then she says that God’s knowledge does not look at future things as future, because God’s eternal present comprehends all times at once. Later she distinguishes two kinds of necessity: one simple, like all humans being mortal, and one conditional, like if you see someone walking, then it is necessary that he is walking while you see him. She says Gods foreknowledge imposes only the second sort of necessity on future events. I don't see how the eternity definition and the conditional necessity discussion are supposed to connect. Is the idea that God sees my future act as present, so his knowledge has the same relation to it as my seeing someone walk has to his walking? But that still seems to leave a fact about what I will do fixed from eternity. So does the eternity point actually do any work, or does the whole solution rest on the conditional necessity point? And if the latter, isnt conditional necessity too weak to explain how certain divine knowledge is? Maybe I'm muddling epistemic certainty with causal necessity. Help me sort out exactly which claim is supposed to bear the weight.
