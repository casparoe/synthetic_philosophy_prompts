I've been fiddling with a variant of Parfit's repugnant-conclusion argument and I've gotten stuck: it seems to force a limit-case version of the conclusion, and I can't find the bad step. Here's the chain.

(1) Let A be a world with n people at welfare 100. Form A+ by adding m extra people at welfare 1 and leaving the original n people untouched. Since the added lives are worth living and nobody is made worse off, A+ isn't worse than A — so A+ is at least as good as A.

(2) Form B by taking the n+m people in A+ and redistributing the same total welfare equally among them. Same people, same total, more equal, so B is better than A+. Since better-than implies at-least-as-good-as, and at-least-as-good-as is transitive, B is at least as good as A.

(3) But for any ε > 0 you can choose m large enough that 99n/(n+m) < ε. Then everyone in B sits at welfare 1 + 99n/(n+m), which is between 1 and 1+ε. So for every ε > 0 there is a world where everyone is between 1 and 1+ε, and that world is at least as good as A.

(4) Finally, add a limit principle: if a sequence of worlds, each at least as good as A, converges to a limit world in the obvious sense that everyone's welfare level tends to a common value, then the limit world is at least as good as A. The equalized worlds have levels tending to 1, so this gives a limit world where everyone is at exactly 1, and it is at least as good as A.

(5) Since n and the 100 were arbitrary, the same reasoning runs with 1 replaced by any tiny positive welfare level q. So for any q > 0, some world where everyone is at exactly q is at least as good as A.

Where does this go wrong? I'd most like to keep the equalization step in (2) and the transitivity built into it, since I use versions of them elsewhere. That would force me to reject either the first mere-addition move in (1) or the limit principle in (4), and I can't tell which is cheaper. Is there a scope slip in moving from the fact that nobody is made worse off to the verdict that the world isn't worse, and would that let me reject (1) on the cheap? What exactly does each rejection cost, and is there any way out that doesn't just leave me with the for-every-ε version from (3), which still looks repugnant?
