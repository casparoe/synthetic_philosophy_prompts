i've been trying to sort out when an evolutionary-psychology story is a real explanation and when its a just-so story, and i think i've landed on a test i want checked. i call it the off-by-one cousin test. the idea is: for any trait T that someone claims is an adaptation, build the nearest alternative trait T-prime you can imagine, where T-prime differs from T in one small, evolutionarily accessible way — maybe the trigger is slightly different, or the preference is a bit broader, or the response is a little more or less intense. for the adaptation claim to count as more than a story, its proponent should have to say something about why selection would have built T rather than T-prime. if the exact same selective scenario they invoke would make T-prime adaptive too, or if they can't say anything at all about T-prime without inventing a whole new story on the spot, then i treat the original claim as not yet being a hypothesis about T. it's a story about a vague family of traits, and the precision of the actual trait is doing no work.

example. suppose someone says male sexual jealousy is an adaptation to paternity uncertainty. my off-by-one cousin might be: jealousy in males, same strength, but triggered by different cues — say emotional intimacy or resource-sharing rather than (or as much as) sexual infidelity, or triggered broadly by male-male competition instead of by specific rivals and specific sexual acts. can the person telling the story say why selection would have produced the actual trigger profile rather than the cousin, using the same paternity-uncertainty machinery? if not, i want to say the story hasn't yet distinguished itself from a false story.

i have three questions. first, does this already exist under a better name — it feels too obvious not to be in the literature somewhere, maybe a variant of falsifiability or contrast classes or something in the adaptationism-and-design literature? second, is the test any good, or does it overreach — e.g., does it demand more precision than early-stage adaptationist hypotheses can realistically give, or does it mistake we can't yet rule out the cousin for the hypothesis being false? third, where exactly does it break, and is there a fix that keeps the good part without throwing away legitimate hypothesis-formation? and please, as you answer, separate what is actually settled in the adaptationism debate from what is still genuinely contested among people who work on it, and don't present the contested side as if it were the consensus.
