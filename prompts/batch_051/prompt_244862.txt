People say a piece of music can be genuinely sad — the sadness is in the sound, not just in the listener. I suspect that's either trivially true or untestable, but I'd like to be shown otherwise. Design the strongest test you can of that claim. Be concrete: what you'd vary, who you'd test, and what pattern of results would count for "sadness is in the music" versus "we're projecting it." Then be honest about what even a positive result wouldn't settle — for instance, would a cross-cultural result or an acoustic analysis locate the sadness in the object, or only show that the code for sadness is universal and the code lives in us?
