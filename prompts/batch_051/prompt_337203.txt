Consider an artificial agent built to optimize a specified objective when that objective may be misspecified: the agent could score well on the objective while causing large, irreversible disruptions to things the designers value but failed to encode. The low-impact research program tries to constrain such agents by penalizing side effects without requiring a full specification of value. One influential approach, attainable utility preservation, measures an action's impact by how much it changes the agent's ability to achieve a broad set of auxiliary reward functions, relative to an inaction baseline in which the agent does almost nothing.

Let P be the following no-go claim.

P: No formal impact measure can do all three things at once. (i) Value-neutrality: the measure is defined only from the environment's transition dynamics and a specification of what the agent would do under inaction, with no substantive ranking of states, events, or variables and no assumption about which changes are bad. (ii) Goal-independence: the measure distinguishes intended effects from side effects without being given the agent's true goal or the designers' values. (iii) Extensional adequacy: across a broad range of environments, an agent constrained to keep the measure low would avoid the side effects a careful designer would flag, without the measure itself being tuned to those cases.
