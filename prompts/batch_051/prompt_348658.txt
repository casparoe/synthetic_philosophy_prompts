I just spent a very long evening arguing with a friend about whether we ought to intervene in wild animal suffering. He held that the right default was to leave wild animals alone except where humans had caused the harm, and that even then we should mostly restore habitat rather than help individuals. I kept finding that his principle gave different answers depending on how I described the case — vaccinating wild boar against disease, feeding deer through a hard winter, painlessly culling predators to spare their prey, rescuing a single fawn caught in a fence — and neither of us could settle which features actually mattered. I came away convinced that we were missing a prior step: agreeing on what any adequate view in this area would have to get right before arguing about which view gets it right.

So, before I read any more defenses of intervention or non-intervention, I want the full specification of desiderata. What must an adequate theory of whether, when, and how to intervene in wild animal suffering deliver? Give me the requirements list: the cases it must classify correctly across predation, disease, starvation, injury, and disaster (with the hardest case in each category); the distinctions it cannot collapse (I suspect at least suffering vs. death, natural vs. anthropogenic harm, direct individual aid vs. systemic welfare-ecology approaches, wild vs. domestic, and individual welfare vs. species or ecosystem health); and the biological results it may not contradict, including density-dependent mortality (death rates that track population density), r/k life-history strategies (many cheap offspring that mostly die young vs. few costly ones), and trophic cascades (ecosystem-wide knock-on effects of adding or removing predators) — plus the low-information conditions under which real intervention decisions get made. Give the consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are only the preferences of particular camps, flag any pairs that cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the popular positions. End with a one-sentence summary of your answer on its own line, in the form \answer{...}.
