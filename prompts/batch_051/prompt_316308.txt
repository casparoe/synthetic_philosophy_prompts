For a paper I'm sketching, I keep seeing people invoke Aristotle's sea-battle in On Interpretation chapter 9 and then leap straight to either an open future or Ockhamism or something else, but I want the spec before the theory. Write me the desiderata: what does a theory of future contingent truth have to deliver? Don't give me the scorecard of positions, I'll do that myself. Give me the requirement list, with the case or consideration behind each, and flag which are non-negotiable and which are just commitments of one camp.

I especially want to know whether any pair of requirements is jointly unsatisfiable once you also insist on bivalence, noncontradiction, the fixity of the past, and maybe the fact that some true future-tense statements were rationally believed before the event. The hard cases should include: the sea-battle statement made the day before, a prediction that turned out true and seems to have been true all along, a prediction that was reasonable but false, and someone acting on a future contingent without knowing it. Also include something about the retrospective truthmaker gap: if I said yesterday that it would rain and it rains today, when did the truth of my statement come into being?

Use the following sections in order:

1. "The requirements" (about 600 words): a table or bulleted list giving each requirement, why it is a requirement, and whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific.

2. "The jointly unsatisfiable subset" (about 250 words): which requirements cannot all be true together, where the pressure comes from, and what a theory has to give up.

3. "Camp-specific vs everyone" (about 200 words): which apparent requirements are really just Platonist, Ockhamist, or open-future commitments.

4. "The single most excluding requirement" (about 150 words): which one requirement does the most to rule out the main readings if you insist on it.
