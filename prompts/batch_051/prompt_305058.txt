I'm in a comparative bioethics seminar and we've spent the last two weeks on the split between Europe and the US over surrogacy and egg donation: a lot of European countries ban commercial surrogacy outright or allow only "altruistic" arrangements, while the American approach, at least in some states, is basically a market with brokers, price signals, and enforceable pre-birth orders. A friend in my study group floated what she called a third way for a country that wants to avoid both the shortage of a pure-altruism system and the commodification worries of the American model, and I can't tell if its clever or just a machine for generating loopholes.

Her proposal is a state-run single registry with two compensation tracks. For egg donors, payment is a flat amount per completed cycle (say 1,000 euros) plus documented medical expenses, and repeat donation is capped at six cycles. For surrogates, payment is expense reimbursement plus a fixed daily stipend capped at half the median wage, with nothing tied to a live birth except a small completion fee (say 200 euros) held by the registry and released after the surrogate signs away parental rights. The weird quirk is that the registry gives people who have previously donated eggs through the system a small priority if they later apply to become surrogates. Surrogates must have already given birth themselves, and there's a 21-day window after the birth in which they can rescind consent without repaying anything except the completion fee. Intended parents pay a fixed non-refundable fee to join plus all medical costs, and their identities are hidden from the surrogate until after matching.

Walk me through how this actually plays out. Who ends up donating eggs, who ends up becoming a surrogate, and who gets matched with whom? Where does it get gamed first — under-the-table top-ups hidden in expense receipts, intended parents recruiting outside the registry, women donating eggs just to jump the surrogacy queue, or something I'm not seeing? Does the egg/surrogacy compensation asymmetry do the ethical work she wants it to do, or does it just push the real payment into whichever track is more loosely audited? And can the failure modes be fixed without collapsing back into a ban on one side or the American market on the other?
