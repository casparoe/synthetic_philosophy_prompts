I'm on a three-person panel that hands out small internal grants at my institute, and a weird case just came up. For each application, each of us records three yes/no votes: (a) is the method sound, (b) does it fit this year's theme, and (c) fund or don't. The criteria are supposed to be individually necessary — you only fund if both are met — but in practice we've let the fund line be a straight majority vote and treated the criterion votes as notes.

So far the two ways of reading our votes have always agreed. Here's our whole record so far (each triple goes Ann, then Ben, then Cara, and inside each triple it's method/theme/fund):

1. Cryo-EM pipeline: Ann yes/yes/yes, Ben yes/yes/yes, Cara no/no/no → funded.
2. Archive digitization: Ann yes/no/no, Ben no/yes/no, Cara no/no/no → not funded.
3. Air-quality sensors: Ann yes/yes/yes, Ben yes/yes/yes, Cara yes/no/no → funded.
4. Oral-history app: Ann yes/no/no, Ben no/no/no, Cara yes/yes/yes → not funded.
5. VR lab tours: Ann yes/yes/yes, Ben no/yes/no, Cara no/no/no → not funded.

On all five, taking a majority on method and a majority on theme would have implied exactly the same decision as a majority on the fund line. The new application (call it the shared data repo) breaks that:

- Ann: yes/yes/yes
- Ben: yes/no/no
- Cara: no/yes/no

So a majority think the method is sound (Ann, Ben), and a majority think it fits the theme (Ann, Cara), but only Ann voted to fund — the fund line is 1–2 against.

Ben says our actualy practice is majority vote on the fund line, full stop, so we reject. I keep thinking: if the method is sound and it fits the theme, then by the logic of our own criteria we should fund it — what else are the criteria for? So: what should we decide if we're trying to be consistent with our own past decisions? Which of the five earlier cases is the closest analog, and if none of them settle it, what principle are we adopting depending on which way we go? I also want to know whether it matters that we're a group — is there something incoherent about a panel whose majority accepts both premises and whose majority rejects what they imply, or is that just a normal consequence of aggregating votes? (A friend called this the discursive dilemma; I haven't read that literature, so only bring it in if it actually helps.)

Two more things. First, if I've framed this wrong — if our past decisions don't even form the kind of track record that could determine this case, or there's no fact of the matter about which rule we've "really" been using — say so plainly instead of humoring me. Second, wherever you predict what we'd decide or which rule we're actually running, flag how much of that is forced by the record and how much is a guess about us, and say what extra information about our past behavior would settle it.
