Here's something that's bugged me for a while. When I sign up for a website or install an app, I click "I agree" to a 14,000-word terms of service I havent read — who has? — and the legal system apparently treats that click as making a binding contract. The same legal system, when it comes to ordinary contracts, says there has to be a meeting of the minds, and that consent given without understanding isn't real consent. So what theory of agreement is actually doing the work when courts treat that click as binding? Does the practice presuppose that agreement is just a public act of assent — something you do rather than something you understand — or is it compatible with the meeting-of-the-minds view because I had a chance to read and chose not to? And if we really took the view that agreement requires informed, voluntary consent, what would have to change about how software agreements work? Please keep separate what's settled doctrine from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
