I've been going back down the endurantism-v-perdurantism rabbit hole lately, and I'm half-convinced by a friend who says the whole thing is just verbal. The way I want to test that is to make the practical consequences vivid: I'd like you to write a deadpan terms-of-service agreement (about 600-700 words) for a fictional start-up called Worm, which offers "personal persistence as a service" on a perdurantist model, in the faux-friendly but menacing legalese of a tech company's sign-up flow. The comedy has to come from actually getting the metaphysics right rather than from vauge "lol you are just a time-slice" jokes: no temporal part of you is ever wholly present at more than one instant; the party to this agreement is the whole four-dimensional space-time worm and not the momentary stage currently reading it; temporary intrinsics (being drunk, being in love, being the one who clicked "I agree") are properties of stages rather than of the whole; and promising, guilt, and being blamed for what "you" did last year all need to be handled in ways that follow from that.

At some point the document should contrast itself with a rival endurantist product called Whole, which promises you are strictly numerically identical to the person who made that awful decision in 2019 and that no amount of temporal-parts talk dissolves the debt. I'd also like a section where the subscriber chooses between worm-theory and stage-theory billing, with slightly different liability clauses, and the stage-theorist is told in plausible corporate small print that their obligations transfer along a temporal counterpart relation to future stages. Keep it deadpan and legible throughout, and aim for a steelman: a metaphysician should recognise the view being satirised and think the parody is fair, not just a pile of time-slice puns.
