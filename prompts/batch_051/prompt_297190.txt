I'm a software engineer, so maybe I'm just pattern-matching to bugs, but I keep coming back to the classic example people use to argue that artifacts have politics: the parkway overpasses on Long Island were deliberately built low enough for cars but not buses—supposedly to keep bus riders away from the beaches. The usual takeaway is that the design itself was doing an exclusionary job; no single official had to keep choosing it. Now change the backstory. An archivist finds the original spec, and the clearance was never a deliberate choice: it started as a typo in a load table that got copied into every overpass order, and when a contractor flagged that buses wouldn't fit, the city ran the numbers and decided fixing it was to expensive. Does the standard artifacts-have-politics analysis still go through? Is the overpass still a political artifact, or does the politics now live entirely in the decision not to fix it once it was known—and does that difference actually matter?
