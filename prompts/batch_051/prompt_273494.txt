I live in a medium-sized city that recently experienced a high-profile act of violence in a public space, and I have been appointed to a citizens' advisory group charged with evaluating proposals for new security measures. In our meetings, the dominant assumption has been that more security and lower risk are always better. I am preparing a memorandum that collects the main arguments for why a society should sometimes tolerate a certain level of risk rather than attempting to eliminate it, so that our group can weigh the costs of the measures against their benefits. I have so far compiled the following arguments:

(1) Risk-risk tradeoffs: measures that reduce one risk often increase another, so the net effect on total risk can be negative.

(2) Opportunity costs: resources spent on safety are no longer available for other goods, including health and education, which may do more to save lives.

(3) Risk compensation: when people feel safer, they adjust their behavior and take on additional risks, so the actual reduction in harm is smaller than expected.

(4) The precautionary principle cannot settle the question: if we must avoid all actions whose consequences are uncertain, we must also avoid the precautionary measures themselves, because those measures introduce their own uncertainties.

(5) The distinction between voluntarily assumed and involuntarily imposed risks: risks people choose to take are morally different from risks imposed on them without consent, and not all imposed risks should be treated the same.

(6) Security theater: some measures increase perceived safety without reducing actual risk, and spending on them may crowd out measures that would reduce actual risk.

I am looking for one more argument of the same general kind — an argument that a society should tolerate some risk rather than attempt to eliminate it — that is not on my list and is not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of these six. If there is a standard argument I have omitted, I would like to know what it is. If my list already contains everything standard, I would like you to propose a genuinely new argument, and then say honestly whether it is any good. Either way, please explain how the new argument relates to the listed ones and whether it adds something they do not.
