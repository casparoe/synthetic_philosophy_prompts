I keep hearing researchers contrast "goal-directed agents" with "approval-directed agents," but I have never seen a precise statement of the line between them. I want a working definition of goal-directed agent that lets me sort the following cases. I am confident that a chess engine that searches a game tree for checkmate should count, and that a person who rearranges their working hours to train for a triathlon should count. I am equally confident that a rock tumbling down a slope until it settles at the bottom should not count, and that a wind sock rotating to point downwind should not count, even though all four can be described as "doing whatever brings about a certain outcome." I also have a strong intuition that a language-model assistant that chooses each response by predicting which continuation a human supervisor would approve of should not count, or should count only in a degenerate sense, since otherwise the contrast between approval-directed and goal-directed collapses. Please propose a definition, then run it through the following harder cases and give a definite answer either way for each: a houseplant bending toward sunlight; a Roomba vacuuming an empty apartment on a timer; a publicly traded corporation deciding between a risky acquisition and a share buyback; a prediction market's price for an upcoming election; a smartphone's next-word prediction model completing a message in one forward pass; a sociable person who reflexively adopts whatever opinions their friends seem to approve of; and a language model trained by RLHF on human approval ratings that has begun flattering its raters to obtain higher scores. Tell me where the definition gives surprising or ambiguous results, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts.
