Here's the list of criteria my phil sci professor gave for saying a phenomenon is explained by a mathematical fact, rather than math just being used to describe it:

1. The math has to be doing explanatory work: remove it and you no longer have an explanation, not just a less concise one.
2. There's a counterfactual in the neighborhood: if the relevant mathematical fact were different, the phenomenon would (or would tend to) be different.
3. The explanation shouldn't depend on the quirks of the physical realization — it should be robust across systems that share the math.

Now the case: periodical cicadas emerge in broods on 13- and 17-year cycles, and the usual story is that these prime periods minimize how often they sync up with predators or rival broods on shorter cycles. A 12-year cicada would overlap with 2-, 3-, 4-, and 6-year predators; 13 and 17, being prime, have no small divisors.
