I just ended a three hour argument with a friend about whether a finite mind could live forever without eventually running out of new experiences. He took the standard line: a finite mind only ever has access to a finite stock of elements, any new experience has to be some recombination of those elements, so after enough time every possible combination has happened and everything left is a rerun, which is what would make an endless life boring. I think the standard line cheats by treating the mind like a fixed library. My alternative view, which I've been calling attentional overflow, is that attending to any experience changes the mind doing the attending, so the next time you attend to the same content the material is the same but the attender is different, and that difference is itself part of the experience. That difference can then be attended to in turn, so each pass adds a new element to the library, including higher-order elements like the feeling of noticing how much you have changed since the last time. So the library never closes: raw material can be finite while the supply of new experiences is inexhaustible. My friend raised two objections: first, that this just smuggles in an infinite memory under a different name, and second, that even if the library stays open, the later entries get so thin and self-referential that they stop counting as experience in the normal sense. I'm not sure he's right, but I also dont trust myself here. Tell me in exactly five numbered items: where the view is strongest, where it actually breaks, whether it already exists under some other name, and whether my conclusion about immortality follows from it. No introduction or conclusion, just the five items, each a sentence or two.
