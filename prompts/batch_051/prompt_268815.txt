You are a tenured philosopher working at the intersection of value theory and the philosophy of mind, writing for a specialist workshop of colleagues. Your contribution must be a sustained piece of philosophical argument, not a survey, and it should not depend on citations. Reason carefully, and where a line of thought fails, say why before moving to a stronger line.

Please argue for the following position. The heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the objection that the pleasure of a warm bath, the pleasure of solving a proof, the pleasure of sudden relief, and the pleasure one takes in a rival's failure have nothing phenomenologically in common, so they cannot all be instances of a single thing — is a serious problem only for a specific, dispensable view of pleasure: the view that pleasure is a distinctive introspectible feeling. Once pleasure is understood instead as an intentional hedonic attitude, a way of being related to an object or content that presents that object or content as good, the heterogeneity of its objects and sensory accompaniments is no more damaging than the heterogeneity among the contents of beliefs is to the category of belief. Defend this position rather than merely asserting it. In particular:

(a) State precisely which version of hedonism, if any, is actually defeated by the heterogeneity problem, and identify the assumption the objection needs in order to get a footing.

(b) Distinguish heterogeneity of hedonic object or content from heterogeneity of accompanying sensation, and say why the critic needs to run these together.

(c) Work through three cases: the pleasure of sudden relief after a fright, the pleasure of solving a proof, and the pleasure a person takes in a rival's failure. What do they share on your account?

(d) Develop the analogy with belief far enough to show that it is doing real argumentative work, not rhetorical ornament.

(e) Articulate and rebut the strongest objection, which will presumably be either that the attitudinal account changes the subject because "pleasure" in ordinary speech refers to a felt quality, or that it is circular because the evaluative attitude it invokes already presupposes the notion of pleasure or goodness it was introduced to explain.

Begin by brainstorming the different things a critic might mean by saying that pleasures are "heterogeneous," and identify the interpretation on which the objection is strongest. Then build your argument step by step, and end with your reply to the strongest objection.
