Fernand Braudel argued that history moves on at least three different speeds. The longue durée is the near-motionless time of geography, climate, and the deep structures of material life: sea routes, harvest cycles, disease environments, the limits a landscape sets on what a society can do. Above that sits the conjoncture, the slow rhythm of economic and social cycles — prices, populations, trade patterns, the rise and decline of a dominant city — unfolding over decades. Finally there is the événement, the event: the treaty, the battle, the individual decision, which Braudel regarded as spectacular but shallow, intelligible only against the deeper motions beneath it. Braudel died in 1985. The Anthropocene — the proposal, popularized around 2000, that human activity has become a geological force, altering the carbon cycle, reshaping sedimentation, and driving a global mass extinction — was not a live category during his lifetime, though much of its evidence was accumulating.

Your task is to reconstruct, as plausibly and as specifically as you can, how Braudel would have thought about the Anthropocene had he encountered it late in his career. In particular: Would he classify the Anthropocene as a phenomenon of the longue durée, a conjoncture, an event, or some hybrid his three-level scheme cannot comfortably express? Would he see it as a vindication of his claim that the deepest history is geographical and almost immobile, or as a breakdown of that claim, since a geographically scaled change now appears to be produced by the accumulated choices of human societies? What, if anything, would he have had to revise about his low estimate of the historical importance of events and individuals? And is there any distinctively Braudelian way of narrating the Anthropocene — for example, starting not with the steam engine or a diplomatic summit but with port capacities, fertilizer flows, the caloric constraints on premodern agriculture, or the slow diffusion of fossil-fuel infrastructure through the Mediterranean — that alters the standard story? Where Braudel's framework would force him to resist the standard Anthropocene narrative, say so explicitly; where it would allow him to anticipate or reframe it, explain the mechanism by which it would do so.
