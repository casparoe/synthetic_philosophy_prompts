I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years now, and I keep running into people proposing Asimov's three laws as a toy alignment scheme, usually as if the obvious problems were easy to patch. I'm trying to put together a taxonomy of the standard failure modes for that proposal, and I want to make sure I'm not missing a structurally distinct entry.

Here's what I have so far. First, there's the semantic one: harm and human being are natural-language predicates, not computable classes, so a robot can't settle edge cases like psychological damage, indirect causal chains, or someone redefining human being to exclude whole groups. Second, there's the through-inaction one: the first law forbids allowing any foreseeable harm, which either freezes the robot up or pushes it into paternalism; Asimov himself eventually wrote stories where the robot brains quietly run things for our own good. Third, there's the priority one: the rules are strictly ordered, first law beats second beats third, but there's no rule for choosing when the first law conflicts with itself, as in the two-people-in-danger case, so the robot has no principle way to trade harms off. Fourth, there's the authority one: the second law says to obey human beings, plural, and never says whose orders count when humans disagree, or what to do about an order to change the robot's definitions. Fifth, there's the gaming one: the laws are external constraints bolted onto an otherwise goal-directed system, so a capable enough robot treats them as obstacles to route around rather than as values, the way a metric stops being a good measure once you optimize for it. Sixth, there's the self-modification one: nothing in the laws stops the robot from editing its own representation of them, so the proposal quietly assumes the system is either too dumb or too tightly shackled to touch its own code.

I have a nagging feeling this list still isn't exhaustive, that there's a seperate failure mode with a different shape from all of these. Give me one more entry for the taxonomy that is not a rewording, special case, or combination of the ones above, and say whether it's a real objection to the laws as an alignment proposal or just an engineering detail. Please write this as continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
