I've been reading around on what markets do and don't know, and it keeps bugging me that people talk about what the market knows without ever saying what standard there using. Before I read any more theories of how prices aggregate information, I want the specification sheet. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of what prices reveal has to be able to do to count as adequate. I'm after a theory good enough to tell me when I should treat a price as real evidence about underlying value and when I shouldn't—for a trader, a regulator, or just someone trying not to be an idiot about a housing market.

Give me the requirements one at a time, with the case or consideration behind each. Cover the classic cases: a prediction market on an election, a speculative bubble in real estate that everybody sort of knows is a bubble, insider trading before an earnings announcement, a thin market for some niche asset where a few people move the price, and the local-knowledge case where a price can pool facts that no single person has all of—like a thousand small buyers each noticing a supply shortage. Make it respect the no-trade theorem too: if everyone has common priors and common knowledge of rationality, mere differences of information shouldn't produce trade, so any adequate theory has to say which of those assumptions breaks when actual people actually trade, and why. It also has to face the paradox that if a price were a sufficient statistic for all available information, nobody could be paid to gather information, so the market couldn't get to that state unless information were free. And dont let it collapse the distinction between a price aggregating knowledge that's already out there and a price functioning as a discovery mechanism that creates new knowledge, or between a price being right on average and a price being a summary good enough that you can't beat it.

Then tell me which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the prejudices of one camp. Flag any pairs that cant be jointly satisfied—if the list itself is impossible, I want that said plainly. And one more thing: which single requirement does the most work to rule out the theories people usually propose? Keep it continuous prose, like an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
