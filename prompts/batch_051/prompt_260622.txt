I have been rereading Dennett’s essay “True Believers,” and a passage in it has been bothering me. In the passage, as I understand it, Dennett gives the recipe for the intentional stance: begin by treating the system whose behavior you want to predict as a rational agent; then attribute whatever beliefs and desires that agent ought to have, given its situation and goals; then predict that it will act rationally in light of those states. The essay is explicit that this works for chess-playing computers and that nothing more is required for a system to be, in its title phrase, a true believer.

If that is right, then something follows about systems much more complicated than chess programs. Here is an argument I cannot get around:

(1) A system is a true believer if and only if its behavior is reliably and voluminously predictable by taking the intentional stance toward it. (2) The text outputs of a contemporary large language model are reliably and voluminously predictable by taking the intentional stance toward it—for instance, by treating it as believing that certain continuations are statistically or semantically likely, and as wanting to produce a continuation that satisfies the prompt and the patterns in its training data. (3) Therefore a contemporary LLM is a true believer. (4) If something is a true believer, then it literally has beliefs and desires. (5) Therefore a contemporary LLM literally has beliefs and desires. But (6) this cannot be right: at inference, an LLM’s weights are fixed; it has no perception-action loop, no stable mental content across tokens, no preferences about outcomes in the world, and no obvious sense in which a token-level disposition is about something. If I accept the argument, I seem forced to say that ChatGPT wants to autocomplete.

I want to reject the conclusion but I can’t locate the bad step. I would most like to reject (2) or (4), but (2) looks empirically solid as a predictive strategy, and (4) is just what the phrase appears to mean. So tell me which premise or step fails, or whether the argument goes through. In your answer, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers working on the intentional stance and on AI, and do not present a contested interpretation of Dennett as if it were the consensus.
