I've been going in circles about what actually counts as a lie, and reading Augustine's absolutist stuff on it made it worse, not better. He thinks every lie is a sin, even the one you tell to save someone's life, which strikes me as a conclusion so harsh that the definition it rests on has to be doing a lot of work. So I want you to help me build a working definition of lying from scratch. I'm prepared to say that bluffing in poker is not lying in the ordinary moral sense, and that telling the murderer at the door that nobody is home is lying, even if its the right thing to do. I have no settled view about the in-between ones: a strictly literal truth said in a tone meant to mislead, the small polite lie about enjoying the dinner, an actor saying lines he knows are false, a joke, saying something false that you sincerely believe is true, and the person who doesn't care whether what he says is true or false at all. Propose a definition, run it through all of these, and tell me where it gives a comfortable answer, where it gives a wrong-looking one, and whether you end up revising it. I also want you to tell me what would change your mind about the definition you settle on, and which of my cases is doing the most work in revealing where the definition is least stable. Write this as continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
