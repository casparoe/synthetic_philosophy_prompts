I've been trying to work out what I actually think about believing other people, so I made myself a list of gut calls.

- A stranger at the bus stop says the 7 bus is rerouted tonight; I believe her and change my plans. I don't need her track record.
- A friend says his brother won a local chess tournament; I believe him.
- A coworker says the new café on Fifth is good; I'll go, no independent check.
- A doctor I just met says I have strep throat; I'll take the antibiotics, even though I know nothing about how accurate she is.
- A car salesman says the used Corolla had one careful owner; I want the service records before I believe a word.
- My cousin, who exaggerates, says he caught a fish the size of his arm; I don't take it at face value.
- Someone says the mayor is skimming city funds; I'd want real corroboration, not just their say-so.

The pair that bothers me is that if that same someone tells me it's going to rain, I'd believe them, but about the mayor I wouldn't. So maybe I believe people by default and only stop when the claim itself trips and alarm—not when the person lacks a track record. My tentative principle is: believe someone unless you have a specific reason not to. But "specific reason" is doing a lot of work. Does a car salesman's job count as a reason, before he's lied to me? Does my cousin's habit count, or do I need fresh evidence each time? The case I keep going back and forth on: my neighbor, who I've known for two weeks and who is clearly annoyed about my chickens, tells me one of them got into his garden and tore things up. The claim is mundane and I have no track record, but he has a motive to exaggerate. Do I believe him straight off, or go look first? I'd like someone to take my verdicts and my tentative principle, make it precise, or replace it with a better one that captures as many of my cases as possible. Please tell me which of my verdicts (if any) the principle forces me to drop, and how it comes out on the chicken case. Also, say how confident you are in the answer and which part you're least sure about.
