I'm drafting the introduction to a survey piece on the standoff between epistemicism and supervaluationism about vagueness, and I want to fix the standard of adequacy before I argue that either side is the default position. For orientation: epistemicism holds that vague predicates have sharp cutoff points whose locations we are in principle unable to know, and it preserves bivalence throughout; supervaluationism holds that borderline cases are neither true nor false, and that a sentence is true just in case it is true on every admissible precisification of the vague terms. Write the list of desiderata that an adequate semantics for vague natural-language predicates such as red, heap, and tall must meet: the puzzles it must solve rather than merely relocate, such as the sorites and higher-order vagueness; the inferential patterns and logical laws it must preserve or explain the failure of, such as bivalence, excluded middle, and modus ponens; the penumbral connections it must respect, such as the fact that a borderline patch that is red is not orange; and any data about competent speakers' judgments it cannot dismiss as mere performance error. For each desideratum, state the consideration or example that makes it a requirement. Then mark each as non-negotiable or as a commitment distinctive of one camp, and say explicitly whether any subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied in their straightforward form, and which side is hurt more by that forced choice. Present the answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
