I am a high school student, and I have been reading about Nietzsche's idea of the eternal recurrence. I came across the following summary in an online introduction to existentialism, and I want to know whether it is fair.

The summary says:

"Nietzsche's doctrine of the eternal recurrence says that time circles back on itself, so that every event in your life, down to the smallest detail, is fated to repeat forever in exactly the same order. He did not mean this as a prediction about physics, though, but as a test of how you are living. The test is this: if a demon appeared to you in your loneliest moment and told you that you would live this same life over again innumerable times, would you curse the demon or bless the news? For Nietzsche, the only adequate response is to welcome it, because to be crushed by the thought is to reveal that your life is not worth living just as it is. This welcoming acceptance is what he called amor fati, the love of fate, and it is the mark of the overman; those who recoil from the eternal recurrence are the last men, who want only comfort. The point of the idea, then, is to get you to treat each moment as though you were choosing it for all eternity, so that you become the author of your own life rather than someone who merely endures it."

The demon scenario matches what I had already read, but the way the summary turns it into a verdict on whether one's life is worth living, and then jumps to the overman and the last man, strikes me as possibly reading Nietzsche too much like a self-help formula. However, I do not know the texts well enough to tell whether that suspicion is fair or whether I am the one misunderstanding him.

Could you go through this summary and tell me which parts a careful reader of Nietzsche would accept, which parts are overstatements or conflations, and whether the final step, from affirming recurrence to becoming the author of one's own life, follows from anything Nietzsche actually held? I would also like to know whether my suspicion about the self-help flavor is fair.
