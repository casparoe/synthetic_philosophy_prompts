I'm a retired quantitative geneticist—spent my working life with livestock breeding and later with long-term ecological data—and I've recently gotten into political philosophy. The position I keep circling back to is luck egalitarianism: roughly, the claim that inequalities are only okay when they trace to people's choices rather than their circumstances, with the standard split between option luck, how a deliberate gamble turns out, and brute luck, the stuff that just falls on you. To my ear the whole thing is a variance decomposition with the labels stripped off. Brute luck sounds like environmental variance, or maybe the total phenotype minus whatever the agent controls; option luck sounds like the component you'd assign to the agent. Except in my old discipline, choices, temperament, self-control, and everything downstream of them are themselves partly genetic, partly environmental, and thoroughly gene-environment correlated, so the clean line philosophers want looks like its probably not sitting in the data. I know the genetics side cold and I'm still shaky on the philosophy, so correct me where I'm mangling the view, but your job isn't just to explain it back.

I want a systematic translation and a residue report. Restate the luck egalitarian position—especially the option luck/brute luck distinction and the idea that brute luck, being unchosen, is what justice ought to correct—in the vocabulary I actually use: total phenotypic variance, additive genetic variance, shared and nonshared environment, gene-environment correlation and interaction, heritability, the reaction norm, and the difference between a reaction norm and the realized phenotype. Go term by term and say which parts translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (and what gets bent), and which have no expression at all in my vocabulary. If part of the answer is that heritability is a population statistic and the philosophical distinction is about individuals, that's exactly the kind of residue I want to hear about. Then give me the verdict: is what doesn't survive a gap in my framework—a thing it was never built to say about agency—or a sign that the philosophers have been quietly presupposing an uncaused chooser that my vocabulary rightly decomposes away? After the translation, does option luck/brute luck still mark a real joint, or does it dissolve into a line we draw for other reasons?
