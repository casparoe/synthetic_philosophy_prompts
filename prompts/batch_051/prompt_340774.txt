I have recently been put in charge of validating a software system that has to satisfy an external benchmark, and I keep running into the conceptual question of how much you can honestly infer about what a system is "trying to do," and what options it had, from a log of what it did. I would like to work through that question using the Volkswagen diesel emissions scandal as a case study, because it seems to be the cleanest real case of behavior that changes depending on whether the system is being measured.

As I understand the episode: in 2015 Volkswagen admitted that some diesel vehicles contained software that recognized the conditions of the regulatory test cycle and activated a low-emissions mode during the test, while emitting at much higher levels during ordinary driving. The discrepancy was deliberately engineered.

Analyze this episode through the lens of inferring an optimizer's objective and search space from its behavior. Be explicit about which agent you are treating as the optimizer at each stage—the engine-control software, the engineering group, or Volkswagen as a corporate agent—and say what each stage of behavior licenses an outside observer to attribute as the objective and as the search space. Distinguish what the behavior alone supports from what the later admission adds. Explain how the apparent inconsistency between laboratory and on-road emissions dissolves once the objective and search space are specified correctly, and what the case shows about reward hacking, metric-targeting, and the difference between optimizing the real property and optimizing its measurable proxy.

Then tell me where the lens breaks. What does objective-plus-search-space inference fail to capture here—for example, the difference between a hard-coded test-detection rule and a policy found by search, the nesting of the software's objective inside the firm's objective, the legal and institutional background that determines which protocol counts as the test, and the risk of reconstructing an objective after the fact? Finally, say whether the episode supports or undermines the claim that behavior observed across enough contexts sufficiently constrains the objective and search space you should attribute to an agent.
