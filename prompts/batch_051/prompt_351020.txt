I am a novelist, and I have been rereading Tolstoy's What Is Art? because a character in the book I am drafting holds a version of its central claim. The passage that stopped me is not the formal definition itself but Tolstoy's later test for real versus counterfeit art: a work is art, he says, only if it infects its audience with the feeling the artist experienced; if no such infection occurs, then however learned, beautiful, or admired the object is, it is not art at all. I find this almost monstrous and almost true, and I want to stop oscillating by thinking about what a definition of art is actually for.

Before I read any more theories, I want the desiderata. Write the list of requirements that any adequate definition or theory of art must meet. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it: which artworks, practices, or distinctions it must capture, and what would be lost by a definition that failed it. The list should include, at minimum, what a definition of art must do with:

- ordinary uncontroversial cases such as a Cézanne landscape, a Bach fugue, and a passage from King Lear;
- hard or disputed cases such as Duchamp's Fountain, Cage's 4'33", non-Western objects made for ritual use, and a child's drawing;
- non-art that resembles art, such as a clever advertisement, a well-made tool, or a sports move;
- distinctions between art and craft, art and entertainment, art and propaganda, and between good art and bad art, if those distinctions belong inside the definition or are better treated as separate evaluations;
- psychological facts about expression and response, including ones that Tolstoy's infection model seems to deny or flatten.

Then flag which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of particular camps. After that, briefly test Tolstoy's infection criterion as stated in the passage against your list: which requirements does it satisfy, which does it fail, and which single requirement should worry me most if I let this character treat infection as the whole story. Do not turn this into an essay defending a definition; the deliverable is the list of criteria, with the scorecard after it.
