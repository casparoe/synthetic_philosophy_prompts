I’m a high school student trying to teach myself formal epistemology, and I’ve become attached to a particular view about how to update on a conditional. I want to know what would count against it—not just the obvious objections, but the full map of its exposure, ranked from fatal to merely awkward.

The case is sometimes called the Judy Benjamin problem. A soldier is dropped into a region partitioned in two ways: Red or Blue territory, and within each, Second Company or Headquarters areas. She begins with equal probability for each of the four combinations. She then receives a reliable radio message telling her that if she is in Red territory, she is in Second Company. Crucially, she knows the message is exactly that conditional, not the stronger claim that she is in Red territory, and she cannot directly observe which zone she is in.

The view I find myself defending is this: her updated probability should set the chance of being in Second Company *given* Red territory to 1, while leaving the probability of being in Red territory unchanged, and while also preserving, as far as possible, the relative probability of Second Company versus Headquarters *within* Blue territory. More generally, learning a bare conditional tells you something about the relationship between antecedent and consequent, but by itself gives you no reason to shift your probability for the antecedent.

I want to know what would count against that position. Which concrete cases, Dutch-book arguments, formal constraints, or symmetry considerations would show that the rule is wrong, underdetermined, or committed to consequences I should not accept? Which alternatives are the strongest, and what do they get right that my view gets wrong? Are there modified versions of my position that keep the core idea while avoiding the worst problems? And does the view risk anything at all, or has it been stated in a way that makes it too easy to protect?

If I have set the problem up in a confused way, or if the question as posed cannot be answered well, say so plainly rather than politely working around me. I would rather have the framing corrected than receive a smooth answer to a bad version of the task.
