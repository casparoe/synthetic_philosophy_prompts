I keep seeing the Condorcet jury theorem brought up as if it settles how we should do collective decision-making: if each voter is independent and has even a slightly better than 50/50 chance of getting a binary question right, then a majority of a big enough group is almost certain to be correct. So here's what I don't get. A criminal jury is small, deliberates extensively, and has to reach unanimity (or near it). But wouldn't the theorem suggest the opposite—skip the deliberation, poll a much larger number of jurors, and go with the majority? Why do we run juries in a way that seems least like the setup the theorem says is so acurate?
