You are a professional philosopher of science who specializes in Karl Popper’s work on the demarcation problem. I’m a high school student preparing a short presentation for a philosophy club, and I need help working through a borderline case.

In the opening sections of his essay Science: Conjectures and Refutations, Popper argues, as I understand it, that what separates science from non-science is falsifiability: a scientific theory must rule out certain observable states of affairs in advance, so that it risks being refuted. A nonscientific theory, by contrast, can be made compatible with any observation by adding auxiliary assumptions, reinterpreting its terms, or extending its scope after the fact.

I want to know whether the following case qualifies as scientific under Popper’s criterion. Imagine a popular online film-trend prediction service called Afterglow. Afterglow claims to be a scientific forecaster of which obscure movies will become cult classics. It publishes forecasts such as *Echoes of a Distant Shore will develop a cult following within five years*. But when a film flops and no cult forms, Afterglow does not treat this as a failed prediction. Instead it issues a clarification: the prediction was about the film’s inner cult potential, which could remain latent until a later rediscovery; or the audience was not authentically ready; or the prediction applied only if no external sociological disturbance intervened. When a film does become a cult hit, Afterglow lists it as a confirmed prediction and says its model was validated. Its defenders say the service is never wrong because it is constantly refining its concepts in response to the historical record.

Please begin by brainstorming the relevant features of Popper’s falsifiability criterion, keeping it distinct from actual falsification and from mere testability in principle. Then go criterion by criterion: does Afterglow make falsifiable predictions, does it rule out observable outcomes in advance, does it risk refutation, and does its practice follow the kind of methodological rules Popper would accept as scientific? Give a clear verdict on whether Afterglow should count as science, pseudoscience, or an intermediate case, and say whether the example exposes any weakness or ambiguity in Popper’s criterion itself.
