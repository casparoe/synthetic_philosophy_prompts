Please compare two ways of locating the unpredictability of deterministic chaotic systems. On one picture, often traced to Poincaré, the core issue is sensitive dependence on initial conditions: arbitrarily small differences in the starting state grow rapidly, so two states that are observationally indistinguishable become wildly different later. On another picture, associated with later work on algorithmic complexity, the core issue is that the sequence of states generated by a chaotic system is incompressible: there is no short rule from which the future sequence can be recovered, so predicting the next state is computationally hard in roughly the way that predicting a random sequence is hard. Are these genuinely different diagnoses, or is the second just a sharper way of stating the first? If they differ, I want to know what each account treats as a merely epistemic limitation and what it treats as a fact about the dynamics itself. And at what point, if any, should we stop saying the system is deterministic-but-unpredictable and start saying that the distinction between determinism and indeterminism has stopped doing useful work?
