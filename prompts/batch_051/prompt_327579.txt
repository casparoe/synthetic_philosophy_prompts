Give me a working definition of moral responsibility—specifically, what it is for some person or institution to be morally responsible for a harmful outcome—that gets the following cases right.

(1) A driver who is texting at forty miles per hour hits and kills a pedestrian in a crosswalk.

(2) A self-driving car strikes and kills a pedestrian after its camera misclassifies a plastic bag as harmless; the system passed millions of miles of testing, and no individual engineer chose the classification rule that failed.

(3) A hospital triage algorithm deprioritizes a patient who then dies; the attending clinician could not reconstruct the algorithm's opaque score in time and followed its recommendation.

(4) An autonomous drone fires on a group after its target-recognition system mistakes them for combatants; the commanding officer authorized the engagement but could not inspect the drone's real-time sensor data.

(5) A pretrial risk-assessment tool recommends detention on the basis of statistical patterns that neither the judge nor the vendor can fully explain; the judge, knowing the tool is imperfect, follows its recommendation.

(6) A pebble dislodged by wind rolls down a hill and scratches a parked car.

I take (1) to be a clear case of responsibility and (6) to be a clear case of no responsibility; for the middle four I keep changing my mind. I suspect the ordinary concept does two jobs—something like retrospective blameworthiness and forward-looking answerability—and that these cases pull those jobs apart. Please propose a definition, run it through all six cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. In doing so, say where, if anywhere, the definition leaves a responsibility gap: a harmful outcome for which no person or institution counts as responsible even though we are inclined to think that someone ought to be.
