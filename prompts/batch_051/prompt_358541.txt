I am applying for a one-semester fellowship in philosophy of science and need a two-page research proposal. My working idea is this: mechanistic accounts of explanation individuate mechanisms by their component entities, their activities, and their organization. But when a mechanistic model produces an anomaly—a false prediction in a new context—scientists usually respond by revising the model, adding, removing, or re-describing components, activities, or organizational links, while continuing to speak of the same mechanism. Nothing in the standard accounts settles when a revision preserves the mechanism and when it replaces one mechanism with another. I suspect the answer is neither read off the world nor purely conventional: it depends on which features of the model are doing explanatory work for the task at hand. Turn this into a proposal that states the question precisely, explains why existing mechanistic views leave it open, outlines a philosophical approach that could make progress, describes what a successful deliverable would look like and what would count as failure, and gives a realistic scope for six months. Do not include a bibliography; I will add citations myself.
