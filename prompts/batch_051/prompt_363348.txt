Imagine two board meetings at the same firm. In the first, the chief executive is told that a proposed restructuring will definitely pollute a downstream watershed; she answers that the watershed does not matter to her, only profit matters, and she approves the plan. In the second meeting, a different chief executive is told that an otherwise identical restructuring will definitely restore a downstream watershed; he gives the identical answer, announcing that the watershed does not matter to him, only profit matters, and he approves the plan. Survey subjects who read the first case overwhelmingly say the executive intentionally harmed the watershed; subjects who read the second overwhelmingly deny that the executive intentionally helped it. The side effect was foreseen and discounted in both cases, and the only thing that changes is the valence of the outcome. This valence asymmetry—the Knobe effect—appears robustly across many kinds of side effects, and it has become a central exhibit in debates between experimental philosophers and proponents of armchair conceptual analysis.

I am not asking for a summary of the empirical results. I am asking whether the asymmetry is justified. On one view, there is a principled reason the concept of intentional action tracks bad side effects differently from good ones: we hold agents more responsible for harms than for benefits, or bad outcomes trigger a search for agency that good outcomes do not. On the opposing view, the asymmetry is a contaminating moral bias—disapproval of the outcome leaking into what ought to be a descriptive judgment about what the agent did, and therefore an error. Provide the strongest available justification for keeping the asymmetry, testing it against nearby cases, and give an honest verdict about whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or given up. Whatever your verdict, say what it implies for experimental philosophy: can ordinary judgments like these serve as data about the concepts philosophers analyze, or do they mostly reveal how evaluation shapes description?
