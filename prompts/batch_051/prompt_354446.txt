I build machine learning models for a living, and I have been trying to work out how much of the standard case against evolutionary psychology is just an overfitting objection at bottom. The case, as I understand it, runs like this: for almost any trait or behavior one points to, an adaptationist can supply a fluent account of how it promoted reproduction in some ancestral environment, and these accounts are post hoc, unfalsifiable, easy to generate, and revised on the fly when contrary evidence appears. Before I draw any conclusion, I want that case translated into the conceptual scheme I naturally work in—model classes, capacity, regularization, training versus held-out data, leakage, and specification search—and I want the translation kept honest, not padded with machine learning assumptions presented as if they were already in the original argument. Then give me a residue report. For each component of the critique (the post hoc character, the unfalsifiability, the lack of independent evidence about ancestral environments, the ease of generating alternative adaptive stories, the ad hoc handling of exceptions), say whether it translates cleanly, translates only under distortion, or has no counterpart at all. Where something has no counterpart, tell me whether that exposes a gap in machine learning's conceptual scheme or a weakness in that part of the critique. The failures of the mapping are what I care about most, because that is where the actual content of the just-so-story charge must live.
