I recently found myself reading seriously about the Fermi paradox, and there is a pattern in the arguments that I keep noticing but cannot find a name for. Someone proposes that the origin of life was the improbable step; someone else proposes complex cells; someone else proposes tool-using intelligence; and each proposal is offered as though it alone makes the galactic silence intelligible. Yet a planet would have to clear every one of these hurdles in sequence, so each additional improbable step does two things at once: it makes our own existence harder to account for, and it makes the silence less informative, since once enough independent improbabilities have been stacked, an empty-looking galaxy is no longer surprising—it is exactly what the model predicts. The candidate filters all live off the same silence, but each one quietly drains evidential support from the others, and the discussion treats them as collaborators rather than competitors. Is there an established name for this specific situation, and how does it differ from the Great Filter as usually stated? I am less interested in a bare label than in the carving: what exactly this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
