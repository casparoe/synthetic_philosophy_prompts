After retiring from a research career in molecular biology, I have begun reading the philosophy of biology and have been struck by a disagreement about what it means to assign a function to a biological trait. On one view, sometimes called the etiological account, the function of a trait is the effect for which it was selected in its evolutionary history; on the other, often called the causal-role account, the function is the trait's present contribution to the organism's survival or reproduction, irrespective of its history. The two views appear to diverge in cases I find scientifically natural: a gene or structure that was selected for one effect but now contributes in a different way, or a trait that currently helps the organism but has no known selective history. I would like to know whether this is a factual disagreement that evidence could settle for a given trait, or merely a disagreement about how to use the word function. Please propose the strongest empirical test you can, real or hypothetical, that would separate the two accounts, state in advance what result would support each account, and then say clearly what even a clean result would fail to settle about the notion of function in biology.
