A funding consortia that backs replication studies wants to trial a "confidence stake" rule at a journal. When a lab submits a study, it can put a deposit of any size into escrow, and the published paper carries a "high-stakes" badge scaled to the amount. If a pre-registered replication of the central effect succeeds, the lab gets its stake back plus a 1.5x bonus paid out of a pool that every publishing lab contributes to. If the replication fails, the stake is forfeit: half to the lab that ran the failed replication, half to the pool. If no one attempts a replication within four years, the deposit is returned and the badge lapses. The stated logic is the handicap principle — only genuinely confident labs should be willing to put real money behind a claim. Walk through what this actually does. What sort of effects and effect sizes do labs start submitting, and how do they write the boundary conditions? Does the badge end up tracking confidence, cash reserves, risk tolerance, or something else? Where does the system get gamed first — by authors, by the replication labs, or by the consortium holding the escrow — and can any of the holes be patched without the whole thing turning into a tax on small labs?
