I'm in a reading group and we were working through David Hume's "Of Miracles" from *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*. The passage that set everything off was this:

"When anyone tells me, that he saw a dead man restored to life, I immediately consider with myself, whether it be more probable, that this person should either deceive or be deceived, or that the fact, which he relates, should really have happened. I weigh the one miracle against the other; and according to the superiority, which I discover, I pronounce my decision, and always reject the greater miracle. If the falsehood of his testimony would be more miraculous, than the event which he relates; then, and not till then, can he pretend to command my belief or opinion."

Two people in the group, Marcus and Dana, went back and forth. Marcus said this is basically a rigorous version of common sense. If someone tells you a guy came back from the dead, you have to compare two hypotheses: that the laws of nature actually broke down, or that the person is lying or mistaken. Since we have massive uniform experience of people being wrong or dishonest, but no verified case of a resurrection, the second will always be more likely. Dana said no, Hume's test is less neutral than it looks: to call the falsehood of the testimony a "miracle" at all, you have to treat human testimony as having a certain uniform reliability, but that is exactly the kind of regular experience the miracle claim itself would call into question. She said if you really held testimony to a strict uniform standard, a lot of surprising ordinary reports would fail too. Marcus came back with a friend-of-the-court move: he said we don't need to prove the falsehood is more miraculous in some cosmic sense; we only need to act on the balance of evidence we currently have, and that balance has never favored a resurrection. Dana said then the maxim is just a restatement of "believe the more probable," and the miracle language is doing no work. I couldn't tell if she'd scored a real point or just relabeled his position.

Can you adjudicate? Please answer in British English.
