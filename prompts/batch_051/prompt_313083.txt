I'm taking a global economic history course and the weeks on the Great Divergence kind of broke my brain. The debate seems totally stuck, and I cant tell whether people in the readings are even having the same argument. One side says Britain pulled ahead because of institutions and secure property rights; the other says similar conditions existed in the Yangzi Delta and the real difference was coal, colonies, or the pattern of trade. And then there's a separate fight about when the divergence actually happened — Song? Ming? 1800? — where each camp picks the date that fits its explanation. My working theory is that the whole question is badly posed from the start: asking why Britain and not China treats Britain as the default path and China as a negative case, so the question kind of presupposes what it's supposed to explain. Is that right? I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is — factual, verbal, methodological, a hidden dispute about what a historical explanation has to do — and what would actually count as progress, or whether the question should just be retired. Not after a survey of positions; we've read enough of those.
