I've been volunteering with elderly people in my neighborhood for the last year, and it's made me weirdly suspicious of my own compassion, which pushed me into a Nietzsche-versus-Schopenhauer rabbit hole. I've come down on the Nietzsche side: pity (Mitleid, suffering-with) is not the straightforward moral good Schopenhauer treats it as. My version of the view is more specific than "pity is bad." I think pity usually involves hidden self-regard — the person who pities gets to feel warmhearted and a little bit superior while the other person stays frozen in the position of the pitied. It often humiliates or infantilizes the recipient, and it increases the total amount of suffering in the world because now two people are suffering instead of one. It also has a weird conservative streak: pity needs an object, so it has an interest in keeping someone in a pitiable state. So I dont think pity can carry the weight Schopenhauer puts on it as the foundation of morality, and I find myself drawn to something like shared joy (Mitfreude) or a cooler kind of respect that helps without needing to feel the other person's pain.
