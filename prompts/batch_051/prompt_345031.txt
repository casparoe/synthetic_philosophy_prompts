A student of mine keeps pushing back on the socialist calculation debate, and I want to give her the strongest version of the objection rather than a straw man. In our course we have set Mises's contention that rational economic calculation is impossible without real market prices for the means of production against Hayek's deeper version of the problem, which holds that the decisive obstacle is not arithmetic but the dispersed, local, often tacit character of economic knowledge, and then against Lange's reply, which keeps public ownership while having a central planning board announce trial prices and adjust them in response to shortages and surpluses. The student's position is that contemporary machine learning quietly settles the question in favor of Lange, or at least against Hayek: Amazon, Walmart, and similar firms run what are effectively large internal command economies, using real-time demand forecasting, dynamic pricing, and algorithmic logistics to allocate resources across hundreds of thousands of products and thousands of suppliers, at a scale and speed no mid-century planner could have imagined, and she thinks the same tools could in principle be scaled to a national planning board. She grants that something still feels different between a firm and a whole economy but suspects the difference is one of data coverage and compute; I suspect the firm-level examples are quietly relying on the market prices they sit inside, but I want that tested rather than asserted.

I would like a diagnosis of where this development actually bites. Does massive algorithmic computation undercut Mises's original point about a common unit of account for heterogeneous capital goods, or Hayek's point about tacit and local knowledge, or an incentive and discovery point that neither Lange nor my student has fully articulated? Which parts of the socialist calculation argument survive the existence of machine-learning-driven supply chains, and what should a Hayekian now concede? I am asking about the fate of the argument itself, not the practical merits of any actual plan.
