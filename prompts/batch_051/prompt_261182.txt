Recently I've been drafting a rulebook for a hobby organization that has to specify, in plain English, which situations count as violations, and I keep encountering sentences that don't seem to divide cleanly into true and false: "the equipment was handled carelessly," "the field is playable," "the treasurer will repay the club by the end of March." A friend of mine, who has stronger logical convictions than I do, insists that any assertoric sentence that isn't simply meaningless must be either true or false even when we can't determine which; the appearance of a gap, he says, is just our ignorance or our unwillingness to sharpen our terms. I currently put my own credence in the universal principle at about 0.55; he is at roughly 0.9.

I'd like your credence in the following claim, with the reasoning decomposed rather than a bare number: for every assertoric declarative sentence that is not meaningless, exactly one of the sentence and its negation is true. In giving your number, please separate the main sources of doubt or support: (i) vague predicates, and whether borderline predications are false, true, or neither, and whether permissible sharpening removes the apparent gaps; (ii) future contingents, where the future event may not yet be settled; (iii) sentences whose own semantics generate pathology, such as liar-like constructions; (iv) presupposition failures and category mistakes, and whether these are genuine counterexamples or simply not assertoric in the relevant sense; and (v) the general thought that truth consists in correspondence with a determinate fact rather than something that can run out. Assign a rough probability to each sub-claim as it bears on the universal principle, with a line of justification for each, and say how they combine into your overall credence. Then identify which of these sub-questions your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, given the two of us sitting at about 0.55 and 0.9, say which sub-question most likely houses our disagreement, and tell me what evidence or argument would move your own number most.
