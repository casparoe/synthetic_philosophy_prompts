For a seminar on political ethics, I have been asked to set down, in my own words, the position I actually hold on the problem of dirty hands, so that the other members can examine it. I would be grateful for a consistency audit before I circulate the statement; I am not sure where, if anywhere, the weak points lie.

The position, as I would currently state it, is this:

(i) There are genuine cases of dirty hands in politics. A public official can be morally required, all things considered, to perform an act that is morally wrong in itself — for example, authorizing a lethal or deceptive operation that would clearly be wrong under ordinary circumstances — because doing so is the only available way to avert a much greater wrong.

(ii) The wrongness of such an act is not cancelled by the fact that the act was the best or required option. The act leaves a moral remainder; the official should not regard the matter as clean, and regret or remorse of an appropriate kind is called for even though the act was the right thing to do.

(iii) An official who resorts to dirty-handed measures repeatedly and without genuine reluctance, treating them as familiar instruments of office rather than as moral losses, is blameworthy for that pattern of conduct — even if each individual decision, considered on its own, was justified.

(iv) At least some justified dirty-handed acts must be concealed, or their details actively misrepresented to the public — in some cases for a very long time, perhaps permanently — because disclosure would defeat the purpose for which the act was done or would inflict grave damage on public institutions.

(v) In a democracy, citizens are entitled to hold public officials accountable for wrongdoing in office. Accountability here includes being able to identify the wrong, to see it publicly marked as wrong, to have those responsible sanctioned, and to bring about acknowledgment and repair for those harmed. A political system in which officials cannot be held accountable in this way for dirty-handed acts is, to that extent, deficient.

Please audit these five commitments as a set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and for each say what the least costly revision would be and what it would sacrifice. Finally, tell me which single revision would do the most to make the package coherent, and why.
