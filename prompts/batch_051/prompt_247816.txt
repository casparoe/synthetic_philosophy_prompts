I help run a small philosophy meetup, and for the last several months two of the regulars have been having the same argument in slightly different outfits. One of them—call her the Stoic-leaning camp—thinks the ideal of apatheia and ataraxia is basically right: grief, anger, exhuberant hope, and the whole noisy crew are ways of letting things outside your control run you, and a wise person dissolves them into something calmer and more uniform. The other—call him the Aristotelian-leaning camp—thinks that is a recipe for a flat life: grief is part of loving someone mortal, anger at cruelty is sometimes exactly the response the situation calls for, and a person who is never knocked sideways has probably stopped paying attention. After threatening to table the issue for another year, they have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document instead, and I said I would take a first pass at drafting it.

Please write the joint statement these two camps could both sign. I would like it to have (1) a numbered list of agreed claims—what each side means by disturbance, which states both agree are bad or irrational, where they grant each other a point, and when they agree a person can pursue calm in the wrong way; (2) a disagreements table where each row gives the neutral formulation of one live dispute and then the wording each side would accept for its own view; and (3) a crux section: for each crux, state the sub-question and provide a concrete case or type of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what it would show. I especially want a crux about whether deep grief and deep love can be pulled apart, and one about whether engaged social hope requires some susceptibility to anger or disappointment. Finally, flag any row of the table that you think is at bottom a verbal dispute, and say which term is doing the shifty work.
