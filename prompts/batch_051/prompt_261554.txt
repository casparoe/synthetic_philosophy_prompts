I work as a reliability engineer, and an incident review recently forced me to think hard about how we attribute fault to things that never happened. Our postmortem treated a monitoring check that was never written as a contributing cause of the outage, and I was struck by how similar that seems to a principle I later read about: in tort law, an omission counts as a cause of harm only against a background duty — the stranger who doesn't throw a rope to a drowning person isn't liable, but the lifeguard who doesn't is — while in software engineering, a missing check or absent failsafe counts as a defect only relative to a specification or a standard of reasonable design. In both cases, a negative condition becomes causal only because some norm supplies the missing act. Is this a deep structural parallel or a superficial verbal one? Spell out the mapping in detail, say what would have to be true on each side for legal results about omissions to transfer to incident attribution, and identify at least one specific place where the analogy predicts a result that is false on one side or the other.
