Give me your actual credence — a specific number between 0 and 1 — that the Lucas–Penrose family of arguments succeeds in refuting mechanism about mathematical thought. By mechanism I mean the thesis that the set of arithmetical truths a human mathematician could in principle be brought to recognize is the theorem set of some consistent formal system. By "succeeds" I mean: under the strongest fair formalization of the argument, an ideal reasoner who accepted its explicit premises would be rationally compelled to accept its conclusion. Pick the strongest fair formalization rather than dissolving into "it depends." Then decompose the question, assigning a rough probability and a one-line justification to each term, into at least these sub-questions: (a) human mathematical output can be idealized as consistent (or sound); (b) for any consistent formal system proposed as capturing our arithmetical competence, we can recognize the truth of its Gödel sentence; (c) that act of recognition is not itself the execution of some formal procedure outside the original system; (d) the argument establishes an impossibility rather than merely our present inability to specify the relevant system; and (e) any further term you regard as load-bearing. Show how the terms combine into your overall number, state which sub-question the final credence is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would shift it most.
