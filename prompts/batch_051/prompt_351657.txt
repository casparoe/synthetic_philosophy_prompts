I'm a retired physicist, and since I stopped doing real work I've been reading a lot of philosophy. I've caught myself applying a test I never questioned at the bench: before I'll treat an explanation as serious, I ask what symmetry it's built on and what that symmetry conserves. Noether hands you this in physics — time-translation invaraince gives energy conservation, gauge invariance gives charge, and so on — so it feels like a natural filter. But last month at a reading group someone offered an account of personal identity that just listed continuity conditions with no invariance worth naming, and I dismissed it as loose, maybe unfairly. Is my symmetry-filter a defensible general epistemic policy, or is it just physics envy dressed up as rigor?
