Me and my roommate were going back and forth about Maxwell's demon the other night, and we got stuck on a version I haven't seen addressed directly. The standard setup: a tiny demon sits at a trapdoor between two chambers of gas and lets fast molecules through one way and slow ones the other way, so one side heats up and the other cools down with no apparent work. The standard modern answer is that the demon has to measure the molecules and then erase its records to keep operating, and Landauer's principle says erasing one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of heat, so the second law is saved.

But then my roommate said: what if the demon just never erases? Give it an ever-longer tape (or an effectively infinite supply of blank cells), and every time it measures a molecule it writes the result in a fresh cell and never touches that cell again. Assume the measurement itself can be done reversibly, so the only place you could charge the demon is the record-keeping. In that setup does the second law actualy survive? Does it matter that the tape starts out blank, and should we count the tape as part of the thermodynamic system when we do the entropy accounting? And if the second law still holds, what exactly is the demon paying with, once erasure is out of the picture?
