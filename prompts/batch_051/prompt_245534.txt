Write the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over lying and misleading would produce. Call them the assurance camp and the outcome camp. The assurance camp holds that lying carries a wrongness beyond the false belief it induces: the liar invites the hearer to trust an assertion the liar believes false, and in doing so abuses the practice of assertion in a way that merely allowing a false belief to form does not. The outcome camp holds that the wrongness of deception is exhausted by the false belief, the harm, and the damage to the relationship; once those are held equal, a lie and a misleading statement are morally on a par, and any remaining felt difference traces to incidental features such as deniability, detection, or what the choice of method reveals about the speaker.

The statement both camps could sign should include: a numbered list of claims about lying, misleading, and deception that both accept, including a working characterisation of central cases (assertion, omission, implicature, half-truths) and the incidental differences that an honest comparison must control for; a disagreements table setting out, in wording each side would accept for its own position, exactly where they part ways, including whether the difference between the camps is intrinsic to the lie or merely evidential about the speaker; and the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both camps agree bears on it, and state what each side predicts it will show.

I suspect one crux is whether a matched pair of cases, a lie and a misleading true statement engineered to produce the same false belief, the same harm, and the same damage to the relationship, can nonetheless differ in wrongness. Another is whether a hearer's resentment can be warranted purely by the fact that the speaker asserted a falsehood rather than merely let a false belief form. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, for instance because one camp uses lying in a moralised sense and the other in a purely descriptive sense, or because they mean different things by misleading, say which part and why. Do not cite named authors or texts; the statement should stand on its own cases and arguments. Write in British English.
