Here's a pattern I keep tripping over. A lot of people who would never defend authoritarian government will casually praise Singapore as a well-run success story, or say that China's leadership at least delivers stability and growth. But ask those same people whether they'd accept a strongman at home — no real elections, courts under political control, jailed journalists — and they'd be appalled. So order and prosperity seem to count as a reason to accept authoritarian rule somewhere else, but not as a reason to accept it for oneself. Is there a principled basis for that difference — something about cultural fit, government competence, voluntary migration, or the distinction between soft and hard authoritarianism — or is it just a comfortable double standard? Give me the strongest defense you can build, and then test it on the harder case of a country that keeps order but only delivers mediocre growth. If your principle lets that one through, you've probably made performance legitimacy too strong.
