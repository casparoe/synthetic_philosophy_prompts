I'm the engineer who runs trust & safety tooling for a mid-size online platform where people mostly post about relationships, mental health, parenting, that kind of thing. We get around two thousand reports a day, mostly harassment and self-harm, and our moderators work off a flowchart: keyword match, any prior reports, then suspend / remove / escalate. Its fast and audit-friendly but everybody hates it, me included. Our old head of community keeps saying the flowchart "doesn't actually care about anyone," and our values page literally says we care for users, not just for rules. I have no philosophy background, but I read somewhere that there's a framework called care ethics that is about attention to particular people and relationships instead of abstract rules, and I think that is maybe the thing we're missing.

So what I want is a reusable decision procedure for our moderators, grounded in whatever care ethics actually says, that someone with no philosophy training can run on a report in about ten minutes. It should have the questions to ask in order, what counts as an acceptable answer at each step, where the procedure should stop and say no / escalate / leave the person alone, and where it has to hand the decision to a human because its a values call no checklist can settle. The procedure also has to produce decisions that can survive review by our policy lead and by a user appeal, so it can't just be "use your feelings."

Also be honest about the places where care ethics itself resists being turned into a checklist; I'd rather know that than get a flowchart that pretends. Then run the procedure on two cases for me: (1) a user posts "I can't do this anymore, I'm going to end it tonight," and the current flowchart says auto-suspend and message a hotline number; (2) a user reports their ex for harassing DMs but explicitly says "please don't ban them, I know they're in a bad place mentally, I just want it on record," and the current flowchart says ban the harasser. Tell me what your procedure would have us actually do in those two cases, and where it would force a choice my team will not like.
