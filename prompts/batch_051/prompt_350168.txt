So I rewatched Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind a couple weeks ago, right after a breakup, and I've also been doing this meditation thing where the teacher keeps going on about anatta, the Buddhist claim that there's no fixed self underneath the thoughts and memories, just a bundle of stuff (the five aggregates, I think). The movie seems perfect for this but I can't get it to line up. The Lacuna procedure wipes memories, and the film mostly talks as if Joel and Clementine are still the same people afterwards, just minus some data — wich is already a picture of what a person is. But then the film also shows Joel's feelings about her leaking through even as the memories go, like when he drags her into his humiliating childhood memories to hide her from the erasure, and after the wipe they still somehow find each other and click, like the attraction survived without the history. So one reading is the movie is smuggling in a soul, something in each of them that persists under the memories. Another is that it's actually showing anatta, because what's left after the wipe isn't a self, just dispositions and causes that rebuild a similar pattern — not the same Joel and Clementine, just conditions recombining. I'd like a reading of the film through anatta that takes both of those seriously, says which scenes support which, and where the film resists the Buddhist lens. Also, what do you do with the ending where they hear the tapes of each other's complaints and still choose to try again — is that a Buddhist point about starting over without the illusion of permanence, or is the film insisting on something no-self has to deny? And what does the movie get right about personal identity that the philosophy papers miss?
