I chair the Public Safety and Codes Committee of my city council. The committee is considering an ordinance that would make it a low-level criminal offense for any owner or operator of a commercial establishment to operate the establishment in a manner plainly out of keeping with the prevailing character of the surrounding residential or mixed-use block, as judged by a reasonable person familiar with the neighborhood. The measure grew out of complaints about one late-night bar on a formerly quiet block — loud patrons, 2 a.m. delivery trucks, harsh signage — but the draft applies citywide. A minority of the committee says the ordinance is void for vagueness: no owner can know in advance what the prevailing character of a block is or when conduct is plainly out of keeping with it, and the standard invites each code-enforcement officer to enforce his or her own taste. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before markup, no more than about 1,000 words. It should recommend one concrete course of action — advance as drafted, reject, or advance with a specific redraft — and defend it. In particular: (a) identify which feature of the drafting triggers the fair-notice concern and which triggers the arbitrary-enforcement concern, treating them as distinct failures rather than one generic complaint; (b) engage the strongest argument for advancing the draft as written, namely that the law routinely operates with evaluative standards like 'reasonable care' or 'unreasonable noise' without being void, so flexibility cannot itself be the defect; and (c) if you recommend a redraft, provide the replacement language and explain why it fixes the problem without collapsing into a rigid, loophole-ridden list. I do not need case names or citations; I need the reasoning, and I will have the city attorney verify the law separately. End with a short note to me, marked not for circulation, saying how confident you are in the constitutional bottom line and singling out the point in the argument you are least sure of.
