I'm a novelist, and I've been drafting a novel where a character builds a digital copy of his late wife and has to decide whether he's grieving a person or just tending a simulation. Functionalism is the view I keep coming back to, because it seems to get multiple realizability right: if something plays the pain-role — caused by damage, causing wincing and avoidance, hooked into the rest of the mind in the right pattern — then it is pain, whether it runs on neurons, silicon, or something else. That's exactly what would let me write the copy as genuinely conscious rather than a clever puppet. But I have a stubborn intuition that there's something its like that the functional story leaves out: if you swapped a person's neurons one by one for functionally identical chips, I can't shake the sense that the lights would go out at some point without any change in behaviour, and that the chips would be dark inside. I don't want to be told which side to pick. Walk me through the strongest version of the case for functionalism and the strongest version of the case for my intuition, then say what it would actually cost to debunk the intuition versus amend or abandon functionalism — or whether there's a reading on which both are compatible.
