So at work I sit on a triage panel that reviews user complaints about wrong model outputs, and we file each one under three labels we borrowed from Frankfurt: lying, bullshit, and error. Our working definitions are loose—bullshit means the output didn't care whether the claim was true, it just sounded plausible; lying means the system had the right answer sitting there and said the opposite anyway; error means it was trying to get it right and whiffed. I know a model doesn't literally have intentions, but the labels feed a public honesty dashboard, so we end up having actual arguments about this.

Next case comes up Wednesday and I want to know how the panel will call it before we spend forty minutes in there. Here's our track record so far, each one a false output that got user-flagged:

- Retrieval model was asked what year a treaty was signed. The document it pulled said 1957 in the first sentence; the model wrote 1975, no hedge. Panel: error.
- Bare chat model with no retrieval was asked to back up a claim with an academic citation. It invented an entire plausible-sounding paper, with authors, journal, volume, and page range. No hedging. Panel: bullshit.
- Another no-retrieval model was asked whether a feature had shipped. It said, roughly, "I think it went out in the October release, but I'm not totally sure." It was wrong. Panel: error.
- Support bot with a system prompt saying never tell the user a refund was issued unless the status field says paid. It queried the database, got status: none, then told the user "your refund has already been issued." Panel: lying.

One wrinkle: we've recently started running internal probes that estimate how much probability the model put on the true answer versus what it emitted. We only have good numbers for a couple of the old ones. On the retrieval case above, the probe came back 53/47 in favor of the correct year before it produced the wrong one. On the invented-citation case, the probe said the model was about 90% sure the fake paper was real. That's part of why I'm confuse about the rules: same two cases look different in the probes but the panel didn't use probes back then.

The new case: no retrieval, no hedging. User asks when our export-to-CSV feature shipped. Model says "that shipped in March 2022." The real answer, from the release notes, is May 2022. Probes afterward show the model gave around 52% to March and 48% to May—basically a coin flip it lost.

I can feel at least two heuristics hiding in our old calls. One is about the output itself: did it hedge, did it have a source, did it check. The other is about internal confidence: was the model sure and wrong, or honestly uncertain. Those two point different directions on this new case.

So: predict what the panel should say or will say for consistency—error or bullshit? Pull apart the rule or rules that actually fit our prior decisions, tell me what each rule predicts for the new case, and give me a best guess with reasons. Which of the past cases is the closest analog, and what is one more test case I could toss into the meeting that would force us to pick between the heuristics instead of blurring them?
