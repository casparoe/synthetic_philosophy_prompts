Lately I've been chewing on the paradox of blackmail — the familiar puzzle that you can legally publish a true embarrassing fact about me, and you can legally ask me for money, but if you combine them into "pay me or I publish" it's suddenly a crime. The standard responses are all principle-level, and I keep wondering what would actually happen if someone took one of those resolutions and built real machinery around it. So here's a concrete scheme a reform-minded jurisdiction is floating.

Blackmail is decriminilised, but only through a state-run Silence Exchange. Someone who possesses true, lawfully-obtained information about a target may file a suppression offer. The target gets a notice saying only: someone holds material about you, filed under one of five categories — financial, sexual, criminal, professional, or personal — and is asking £X for a promise never to publish. The filer's identity and the material stay sealed for 14 days, during which the filer may not publish. If the target pays through the Exchange (which keeps 10%), the filer signs a perpetual non-disclosure undertaking backed by a bond; if the filer later leaks, the bond goes to the target. The Exchange records that the target paid, and that record is shown to anyone who later files on the same target. If there's no deal within 14 days, the seal breaks, the target learns who filed and what they claim to hold, and the filer regains the ordinary right to publish. The Exchange verifies nothing — it never even sees the material.

Act as a philosopher of criminal law who also thinks like a mechanism designer. Think carefully: maybe start by brainstorming the obvious incentives before you go after the second-order ones. Assume the scheme has been running for three years. Treat it as a mechanism rather than a moral question — I'm not asking whether it's just, I'm asking how it will behave. Walk through the incentives for filers, targets, journalists, and the Exchange itself. Who files, against whom, and with what sort of material? How does a target decide whether a vague category notice is worth paying? What does the mere fact of a filing do to someone's reputation, and what does having paid do? Where does the whole thing get gamed first, and can any of those failure modes be patched without collapsing back into the original paradox? Please write your answer in British English.
