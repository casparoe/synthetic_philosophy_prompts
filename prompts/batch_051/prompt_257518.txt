ok so ive been turning the old blue bus / gatecrasher thing over and want a mechanism check on an idea before i take it to the reading group.

the rule: in civil cases, a plaintiff can bring a "statistical claim." the tribunal estimates p, the probability the defendant is liable given the base-rate evidence, and if p > 0.5 it awards p times the claimed damages instead of all-or-nothing. the leftover (1 - p) of the claim isnt dismissed; it goes on the defendant's books as an unresolved balance, which the defendant can clear at any time by paying (1 - p) times damages into a state fund. the fund pays out only to later claimants who show up with individualized evidence about the same underlying incident, and whatever is left reverts to the defendant after the limitations period. to keep gatecrasher suits from flooding the courts, a statistical plaintiff has to file an asserted p up front and post a bond equal to (1 - asserted p) times damages; if the tribunal finds a p more than 0.1 below the asserted one, the bond is forfeited to the defendant.

treat this as a mechanism, not a position statement: who sues whom under it, how do lawyers and experts game the p estimate and the asserted p, what happens to deterrence, insurance, and settlement, and where does the thing break first? and does having this civil outlet make the criminal refusal to convict on naked statistics even at high base rates more coherent, or less? you're an epistemologist who thinks like a mechanism designer; think carefully, brainstorm the main incentive effects before you settle on a verdict.
