For a panel in my high school game-design club, please write a short dialogue on the aesthetics of agency in games, specifically on whether a game's choices have artistic value mainly because the designer shapes them or because the player uses them for self-expression. One character, A, should defend the sculpted-choice view: choices matter when they are constrained, legible, and consequential, so that playing involves a kind of authored deliberation. The other, B, should defend the open-canvas view: choices matter when they let players set their own goals and express their own style, even when nothing in the game state is at stake. To force real engagement, both characters must discuss the same example at least once: a player swapping armor in a role-playing game purely to change appearance. The dialogue should consist of exactly five labeled exchanges, A and B each speaking once per exchange, with each speech one or two sentences. Include no introduction, conclusion, or stage directions, and make sure each character responds to the other's actual point rather than talking past it.
