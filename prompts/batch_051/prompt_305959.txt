Daniel Dennett's old line is that your self is a center of narrative gravity: not a real thing sitting somewhere in your brain, just a useful abstraction — like a center of gravity — that your own stories and other people's stories about you organize themselves around. The argument leaned hard on the fact that the only systems we knew that produced this kind of running self-narration were biological creatures with brains, bodies, continuity, and some kind of inner point of view, so its easy to read the abstraction as anchored in something real underneath.

Now we have LLMs that produce fluent, coherent, first-person-ish text and can keep up a self-narrative across an entire conversation, while having no continuity between sessions, no stable point of view, no feelings, and arguably no real center that anything is actually gravitating around. Does Dennett's view surivive this development? If it does, say why it doesn't commit a Dennettian to claiming the LLM has a self — or whether a Dennettian should just bite that bullet. If it doesn't, identify exactly which premise the LLM case breaks and give the strongest repaired version you can, probably with some added requirement the LLM fails, even if the result is weaker than the original.
