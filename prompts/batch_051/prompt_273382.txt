In the Meno, Socrates says to Meno early on: "For I literally do not know what virtue is, and much less whether it is acquired by teaching or not." Later, near the end of the dialogue, he argues that true opinion is just as reliable a guide to right action as knowledge, but that true opinions do not remain; they are not worth much until someone ties them down with an account of the reason why, and once tied down they become knowledge and stay.

Are these two moves in tension? On one reading, Socrates cannot claim to know what distinguishes knowledge from true opinion if he has just insisted he does not know what virtue is. On another reading, he is doing epistemology here, not ethics, so his ignorance about virtue is irrelevant. Lay out the best reconciliation of these two passages, and then say whether it is honest or special pleading. If it is special pleading, what does that do to the Meno problem about why knowledge is better than true belief?
