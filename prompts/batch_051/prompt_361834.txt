I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so I will state my understanding in rough terms and leave the careful work to the dialogue. In every team I have worked on, automatic code formatters (Black for Python, Prettier for JavaScript and TypeScript) are enforced in continuous integration; once a formatter is adopted, style arguments mostly disappear and the codebase settles into a uniform style. I recently read about David Lewis's account of convention, and my rough understanding is this: a convention is a regularity in a population's behavior that persists because nearly everyone conforms to it, expects nearly everyone else to conform to it, and prefers to conform conditional on those expectations, typically because the regularity solves a recurring coordination problem; it is also arbitrary, since some other regularity could have served about as well. I cannot tell whether an auto-formatted coding style is a convention in Lewis's sense, or whether the fact that the enforcement is delegated to a tool in CI changes something important.

Please write a dialogue between two equally able and charitable characters who disagree about exactly this. One of them—call him the purist—holds that auto-formatted style is not a full Lewisian convention, because the mutual expectations run mainly between each programmer and the tool, or because the regularity is not sustained by each person's conditional preference to conform to other programmers. The other—call her the extensionist—holds that formatter adoption is just a new way of making a mutually expected regularity common knowledge, and that the coordination problem Lewis cared about is still being solved, now with a machine as part of the mechanism. Neither should be a strawman, and each must respond to the other's actual arguments rather than talk past the other.

Have them get into the weeds about what actually turns on the disagreement, including at least these questions: Does it matter that the style could have been different if the formatter's defaults had been different? Is the object of mutual expectation other programmers' formatted output, or the tool's predictable behavior? What happens when a maintainer changes the formatter's configuration, or when two projects share a formatter but not a community? And how does the account extend to LLM-based coding assistants that absorb style norms from training corpora rather than from explicit coordination? I am less interested in a final verdict than in a precise statement of what each side would need to accept for the other to be right.
