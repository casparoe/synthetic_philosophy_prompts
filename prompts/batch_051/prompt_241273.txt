I've been trying to turn my scattered views on animal ethics into one blog post and I keep going round in circles, so I want a consistency check before I put it up. My current commitments are roughly: (a) suffering matters wherever it occurs, and the fact that wild animal suffering is 'natural' carries no moral weight by itself; (b) factory farming is one of the worst and most tractable sources of suffering, so avoiding animal products where reasonably possible matters; (c) if you are going to eat meat anyway, beef is better than chicken per meal, because one cow supplies many meals while a chicken supplies one or two, so beef kills fewer animals, holding welfare conditions equal; (d) crop cultivation kills field animals too (mice, insects, etc.) and these deaths count, so veganism isn't zero-harm, though it's still likely better than eating animals because farmed animals are themselves fed crops; (e) we nonetheless shouldn't try to reduce wild animal suffering through big ecosystem interventions like predator removal or habitat re-engineering, because we're too ignorant about the knock-on effects and the risk of making things worse is too high. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the least costly revision? I'd rather you ranked the tensions by severity than told me its all fine.
