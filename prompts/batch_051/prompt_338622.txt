I'll start. Your favorite claimed virtue is action-guidance: law must be capable of guiding conduct, and you present that as neutral between good and evil uses. But I dont see how a standard guides me if the only way to comply is to do something abhorrent — a rule that says 'inform on your neighbors' doesn't guide me, it threatens me. So your central formal virtue seems to rest on a floor, namely that the content isn't too wicked to follow. And a floor is a substantive moral condition, however thin. Haven't you let Fuller's inner morality of law in through the back door, and conceded the whole dispute in miniature?
