I work on AI safety, and I have come to think that most arguments about chain-of-thought monitoring get ahead of themselves. People defend or dismiss it by pointing to one demonstration where the displayed reasoning either tracked or failed to track the model's actual behavior, and then generalize. I want the specification before the verdict. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of chain-of-thought monitoring as a safety technique has to meet: the cases it must adjudicate (self-monitoring versus a separate monitor, hidden internal computation, training directly for monitor approval), the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the facts about how these models are trained that it must not contradict—with the case or consideration behind each requirement. In particular, work out whether the requirement that a chain of thought be faithful, the requirement that it be legible enough to actually monitor, and the fact that optimization creates pressure toward obfuscated or performative reasoning can be jointly satisfied, or whether that subset is inconsistent as written. Structure your response as follows. (1) Requirements, about two-thirds of the whole: each desideratum in a sentence or two, then the case that forces it. (2) Non-negotiable versus disputable: which requirements any account must keep and which are one camp's preference. (3) Conflicts: which subsets cannot be jointly satisfied as written, and what a reformulation would cost. (4) Most discriminating requirement: the one that rules out the most popular defenses of monitoring, and why. Finish with a one-sentence verdict on whether chain-of-thought monitoring survives this list.
