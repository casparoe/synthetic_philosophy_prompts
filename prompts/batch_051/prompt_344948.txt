I have recently started working with reinforcement learning in a setting where people seriously discuss whether the agents we train could suffer, and I keep noticing a move for which I have no word. Someone observes that an agent's reward signal is negative — it is being penalized each time it enters a certain region of its environment — and the conversation then proceeds as if the agent is in pain, or as if the only open question is how much suffering the training involves. The positive case goes the same way: from the fact that a policy maximizes expected reward, the discussion moves to what the agent wants, likes, or is better off for. The people making this move are not naïve about the system; they know the reward is just a scalar training signal, and they are not exactly arguing for the identification, just presupposing it. Is there an established name for this particular slide from reward signal to felt pleasure and pain? If there is no settled term, what would you call it, and how should it be distinguished from ordinary anthropomorphism of AI and from a deliberate philosophical thesis that scalar reward is what pleasure and pain consist in for such agents? I would prefer an answer in continuous prose, as in an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
