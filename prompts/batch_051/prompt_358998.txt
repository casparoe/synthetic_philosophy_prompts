Draft the joint statement that a committed endurantist and a committed perdurantist could each sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the metaphysics of persistence through time. Take endurantism to be the view that a persisting object is numerically one and the same thing wholly present at each time at which it exists; it has no temporal parts. Take perdurantism to be the view that a persisting object is extended through time and exists at a time by having a temporal part at that time, just as a road exists at a place by having a spatial part there.

Produce two main sections.

First, a numbered "Agreed Claims" section. It should state, in formulations both sides accept: the phenomena any theory of persistence must account for, including change, motion, survival across time, and ordinary counting of objects; the questions the debate is and is not about; and the logical space of views, with fair characterizations of nearby positions such as stage theory, understood as the view that only instantaneous stages exist and persistence is a matter of counterpart relations between stages. It should also record what each side concedes is not at issue: the semantics of tensed sentences, the existence of ordinary objects, or the usefulness of four-dimensional representations for certain modeling tasks.

Second, a "Disagreements" table. For each residual disagreement use these columns: (a) the question; (b) the endurantist's formulation, worded as endurantists would accept; (c) the perdurantist's formulation, worded as perdurantists would accept; (d) whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal and why; (e) the crux, that is, the sub-question whose settlement would move at least one side; (f) the argument, thought experiment, or empirical case both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance and each side saying what would count against its own view. Guard against formulations that build in conclusions: "wholly present" must not be defined in a way that simply asserts the endurantist's picture, and "temporal part" must not be defined in a way that simply asserts the perdurantist's.

The table must address at least these five stress points.

1. Temporary intrinsics. One and the same object is straight on Monday and bent on Tuesday. The endurantist must explain how the whole object can have incompatible intrinsic properties without contradiction; the perdurantist must explain why saying that different temporal parts have the shapes is not just restating the phenomenon. Is "being bent on Tuesday" an irreducible way to be, or a disguised relation to a time?

2. The terms of the debate. Can "wholly present" be defined without presupposing multilocation in time? Can "temporal part" be defined without presupposing that past, present, and future are equally real? State whether each side regards the other's core term as coherent, and whether their disagreement here is partly a disagreement about the logic of composition.

3. The relativistic challenge. In Minkowski spacetime there is no privileged global now. State exactly what each side must add or reject about time to accommodate this—for example, presentism (only the present exists) or a privileged foliation for the endurantist; eternalism (all times exist alike) or the B-theory (times are ordered by earlier/later without an objective moving now) for the perdurantist. Is the metaphysics of persistence hostage to the metaphysics of time, or can the debate be stated neutrally?

4. Fission and fusion. An amoeba divides into two; in a second case two streams merge into one. Each side should say how many objects existed before and after, and what it predicts about identity and counting. The endurantist should address whether fission forces non-transitive identity or relative identity; the perdurantist should address whether branching temporal parts misdescribe a visibly single object dividing.

5. Self-visitation in time travel. A time traveler meets their younger self at one external time. The perdurantist can describe this as two distinct temporal parts of one person colocated; the endurantist must say whether one person is wholly present twice, whether there are two persons, or something else, and whether this case tells against endurance, against the coherence of time travel, or neither.

End with a short "Residual Disagreement" paragraph. After cleaning up every verbal issue you can, state the single hardest remaining question in words both sides can sign. If you conclude that any part of the dispute is verbal, identify the term being used in two ways and give the neutral reformulation, then say what still divides the two views under that reformulation.
