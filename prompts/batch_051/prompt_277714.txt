in my philosophy club we keep going in circles on why blackmail is even illegal. like, if its legal for me to tell everyone something true and embarrassing about you (my information, my right to speak) and its also legal for me to ask you for money, why is "give me money or i tell everyone" a crime? the two halves are legal but the sandwich is illegal, and nobody could give me a satisfying answer.

so i want a working definition of blackmail, one that gives a clear yes/no or at least a principled maybe for each of these:
- "pay me $50 or i tell everyone you cheated on your girlfriend" (i say definitely yes)
- a parent saying "no dessert until your homework is done" (i say definitely no)
- a lawyer's demand letter: "settle for $10k or we'll see you in court"
- a store that catches a shoplifter and says "pay us $300 or we call the cops"
- "refund me or i leave a one-star review"
- one senator to another: "vote for my bill or i release the footage of your DUI"
- a journalist: "pay me and i won't run the story"

propose the definition, run it through all seven, and say where it surprises you. i think the first is blackmail and maybe the senat one too (tho that might just be political hardball), the parent one isnt, and i genuinely dont know about the lawyer, the store, and the review ones. also say whether the definition should track the ordinary word or the actual crime, since i think those come apart when something is legal but still feels grimy.
