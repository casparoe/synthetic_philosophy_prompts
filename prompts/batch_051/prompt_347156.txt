I'm in an upper-level aesthetics seminar and next week I present my term paper. My thesis is that the problem of aesthetic testimony is mostly overblown: there's no special epistemic barrier to learning aesthetic facts from other people. What looks like a principled problem is mostly social norms about not outsourcing your taste, plus the fact that aesthetic vocabulary is vague and low-information. I'm arguing aesthetic testimony can give you justified beliefs and even knowledge in basically the same way testimony about anything else does.

The audience is my seminar: the professor is a Kantian-leaning aesthetician who has defended something like the acquaintance principle (roughly, you can't properly judge an artwork without first-hand experience of it), two grad students work on social epistemology and testimony and will care whether I'm using "knowledge" precisely, and most of the undergrads just find deferring to others about art off, like its a matter of taste and identity.

Write me a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense: the objections I'll actually get in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way those people would put them, with which ones have real force, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and a short reply for each with a note on where it's soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

And if my framing is just off — e.g. I'm attacking a straw version of the acquaintance principle or my notion of "justified aesthetic belief" already begs the question — say so plainly instead of writing the prep sheet anyway.
