I'm putting together a short "where I stand" paper for a conference on secularism and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here are the views I've landed on over the years, more or less:

1. Liberal democracies should default to disestablishment — no official church, no constitutional privileges for one denomination over another.
2. That said, England's established church doesn't bother me much; it's mostly ceremonial now, and abolishing it would be a distraction rather than a priority.
3. The American "wall of seperation" talk overreaches. It's turned into a way of treating religion as something to be kept out of public life, rather than one normal voice in democratic argument.
4. Germany's church tax is actually defensible: it's opt-outable, it funds hospitals and social services, and it's less corrupting than American-style donor chasing.
5. French laïcité in its current form is too aggressive — headscarf bans in schools and the like are an illiberal imposition of one particular idea of what public space should look like.
6. Religious organizations should not get blanket exemptions from anti-discrimination law just becuase they're religious; at most they should get whatever a secular nonprofit would get.
7. And the through-line: secularism should mean state neutrality toward religion, not the privatization or marginalization of religion.

Before I present this as a package, I want to know which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. For each real tension, order them by severity, say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. If some of these are only apparently incompatible, say why they aren't. Don't just tell me everything is fine — I'm pretty sure something has to give, I just can't see where from the inside.
