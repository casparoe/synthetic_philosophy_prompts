I'm a philosophy PhD student working on moral uncertainty, and I also sit on my university's research ethics board (IRB). We meet weekly and review around a dozen protocols, and every meeting the same split happens: a study stresses people or uses deception, one camp says its fine because the expected benefits are big, another says you can't treat someone that way even with consent, and we vote our pet moral theories without any shared rule. I want to turn what I know about decision-making under moral uncertainty into a procedure the board can run in about twenty minutes per protocol — the idea that when reasonable moral views disagree you should weigh them somehow rather than just acting on whichever theory you happen to hold, like expected value but across moral theories. The procedure should be a sequence of questions with clear rules for each: what counts as a sufficient answer, where it stops with a no, where it hands the decision to the chair because the remaining disagreement is a values call rather than a factual one, and where it will misfire. Be honest about the point where you cant put different moral theories on a common scale, and say what to do then. Then run the procedure on two protocols: (1) a deception study that makes participants anxious during a task but debriefs them and probably causes no lasting harm, and (2) a study offering large payments for blood samples, recruiting mostly students with debt. Make at least one application come out in a way the board will not like, and don't hide that.
