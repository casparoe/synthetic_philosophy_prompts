I teach a philosophy of religion course and also sit on a local church's adult-ed commitee. We keep running into cases where someone says a well-established scientific finding is "against the Bible." I've read Galileo's Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, and this bit sticks with me: "They seemed to forget that the increase of known truths stimulates the investigation, establishment, and growth of the arts; not their diminution or destruction." And also his complaint about the academics who "made the grave mistake of sprinkling these with passages taken from places in the Bible which they had failed to understand properly, and which were ill-suited to their purposes."

I want a reusable procedure, not a one-time opinion. Suppose a pastor and an adult-ed teacher have an hour to decide whether a standard interpretation of a scripture passage should be reopened because of some scientific result. Give me a checklist they can run with no special science background, turning Galileo's principle into concrete steps. It should say what questions to ask about the science, the passage, and the interpreter. Then apply it to two cases: heliocentrism (Galileo's own case) and one modern case, say evolution. Be honest about where the procedure stops being Galileo and starts being your own advice, and where someone could rig it to get the answer they want. Also please separate what is settled in Galileo scholarship from what is genuinely contested, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
