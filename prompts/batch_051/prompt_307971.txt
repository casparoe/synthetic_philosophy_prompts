‘Leibniz’s whole moral theory is basically warmed-over Epicureanism. He says justice is the charity of the wise, but then he defines charity as a habit of loving, and love as taking pleasure in another’s happiness. And wisdom, for him, is just the science of happiness. So really justice is just doing whatever maximizes your own pleasure across the board: you love other people because it feels good to see them happy, and you’re wise only because you’re good at calculating which pleasures will last. All the talk about God’s will, eternal truths, and disinterested love is just window dressing—he needs to keep the theologians happy. His real view is that the good is whatever satisfies a rational agent, and since God made the world as good as it could be, Leibnizian ethics ends up telling us to be okay with things as they are.’
