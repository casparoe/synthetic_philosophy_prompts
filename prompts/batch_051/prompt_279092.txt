Here is an argument I have been turning over, and I would like an exact diagnosis of where, if anywhere, it fails. The conclusion is that the chain of thought a language model prints before its answer can never be evidence about how the model actually reached that answer.

(1) A written derivation is a faithful account of a system's reasoning only if the steps it states are the very steps that caused the system's answer.
(2) In a transformer language model, the answer is caused by the model's parameters together with whatever is input to the final token position.
(3) The chain of thought is just part of that input; it does not represent the parameters, and the parameters are what do the causing.
(4) So a chain of thought never states the actual causes of the model's answer.
(5) Therefore, no matter how plausible a chain of thought appears, it carries no information about the model's actual computation.

I find step (2) hard to dispute, and step (3) seems like a trivial architectural fact, but step (5) strikes me as far too strong. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails, give the strongest corrected version of that step, and check whether the corrected version still yields step (5).
