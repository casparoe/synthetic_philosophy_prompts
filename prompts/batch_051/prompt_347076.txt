You are a mechanism designer and a sociologist of contemporary religion. Work step by step: begin by brainstorming the relevant stakeholders and their incentives, then walk through the likely gaming strategies, then the longer-run equilibrium, and only then commit to a verdict.

A philanthropic foundation in a mid-sized city announces a network of "quiet chapels": small, secular storefront spaces, open every morning and evening, with one salaried curator each. The rules are explicit: no creed, no sacred text, no asserted supernatural claim from the front of the room; anyone may attend, and any attendee may propose a new ritual. Proposed rituals that meet basic safety and non-supernatural criteria enter a quarterly lottery among members, and the winners are run for one season. Curators are rotated every eighteen months, paid a fixed salary plus a retention bonus if their chapel finishes in the top quartile of the network. Funding comes from the foundation's endowment, member donations, and a city mental-health grant. The foundation ranks chapels by three metrics: attendance, two-year member retention, and a monthly self-report "existential meaningfulness" score; any chapel below threshold on all three metrics for a year is closed and reopened under a new curator.

The chapels' activities include guided "awe walks," seven-minute listening pairs, grief circles without afterlife framing, a monthly "gratitude audit" in which members state publicly what they are grateful for and to whom, mortality cafés, and silent open hours.

Analyze how this institution would actually behave, not whether it is a good idea in the abstract. Cover what it incentivizes among curators, regular attendees, ritual proposers, the foundation staff who set the metrics, and local religious and anti-religious groups. Show how each metric or rule gets gamed, what second-order effects appear over five to ten years, and which failure modes are fixable by changing the design without turning the chapels into either a new church or a wellness franchise. Then address the underlying question: does the mechanism show that spirituality without religion has its own stable content—practices and social forms that produce meaning, awe, and community without doctrine—or does it reveal that the religious element was silently doing most of the work? Reach and justify a definite verdict.

After your prose, provide a structured summary as a single table with these columns: (1) Stakeholder or rule; (2) Incentive created; (3) Likely gaming or distortion; (4) Longer-run second-order effect; (5) Fixable? If so, state the minimal design change.
