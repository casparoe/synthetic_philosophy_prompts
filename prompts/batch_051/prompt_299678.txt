I co-run an interdisciplinary working group on explanation in the social sciences, and for about a year two of its regulars have been arguing passed each other about whether social facts can explain anything in their own right. One is a methodological individualist: he says any explanation that doesn't bottom out in individuals and there beliefs, desires, and interactions is at best a placeholder until we find the micro-story. The other is a methodological holist: she says norms, institutions, and group-level rates have explanatory autonomy, and that decomposing them into individual-level stories often loses the phenomenon itself. It's been going in circles, so we've talked them into an adversarial collaboration: instead of another debate, the two of them have to produce one joint statement they'll both sign.

Write the document for them. It should have three parts: (1) a numbered list of claims about social explanation that both can genuinely accept, each stated strongly enough that it isn't empty; (2) a table of the remaining disagreements, with each disagreement phrased so that the side whose view is being described would say "yes, that's exactly what I mean"; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one of them would actually move at least one camp — with, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction recorded in advance.

My own guess is that one real crux is whether stable group-level regularities need microfoundations to be explanatory; the obvious test cases are spontaneous segregation (mildly homophilic preferences producing sharply sorted neighborhoods without anyone planning that) and stubbornly stable aggregate rates (a suicide rate that barely moves year to year even though entirely different people occupy the categories). Another likely crux is whether "the group" picks out anything over and above the individuals and their relations, or whether that's just shorthand. I also suspect a chunk of the fight is verbal — they use "reduce," "explain," and "individual" in different ways — so sort the verbal disagreements from the real ones rather than dressing the verbal ones up as if they were deep.

And if I've set this up badly — if this is a false dichotomy, or one of these positions is a strawman as I've described it, or no honest joint statement can be written between them — then don't dutifully hand me a clean document anyway. Tell me the premise is broken, and either draft the nearest honest thing or say what would have to change first.
