I run a small review site and I'm in a slightly panicked mood. Someone I gave a one-star review to is threatening to sue me for defamation, and they're also pointing at a post where I called their investment club "a Ponzi scheme in slow motion" and predicted it would collapse within a year. A law student freind sent me their read of the law to reassure me. Here it is, more or less verbatim:

"In US defamation law there's a firm opinion privilege: statements of opinion—reviews, ratings, forecasts—are constitutionally protected, because the First Amendment rests on the premise that there is no such thing as a false idea. The relevant test is whether the speaker meant the statement as opinion rather than fact. So adding the words 'in my opinion,' using a one-to-five star scale, or pitching something as a prediction all take a statement outside the defamation category. A one-star review that says the food was bad is safe because 'bad' is subjective, and a forecast that a business is a Ponzi scheme is safe because a prediction about the future can't be true or false in the way defamation law requires. The worst that can happen is a clearly meritless suit that gets dismissed."

I don't know how much of this to trust. Is this a fair picture of the law?
