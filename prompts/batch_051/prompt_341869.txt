Some Bayesians hold that the washing-out-of-priors results show that rational inquirers who begin with different prior probability distributions will eventually reach near-agreement as shared evidence accumulates, and that this underwrites the view that Bayesian updating is in an important sense objective or self-correcting. Please argue that this is too strong. In particular, argue that the standard convergence theorems establish at most that, within a fixed and shared hypothesis space, the influence of the prior on posterior probabilities over a given set of hypotheses diminishes in the limit; they do not establish that the agents are converging on the truth, or even on a good approximation to it. Explain why the latter conclusion depends on assumptions about the adequacy of the hypothesis space—for example, that it contains hypotheses sufficiently close to the true data-generating process and that it is not systematically misleading—that the theorems themselves do not supply. Where relevant, distinguish convergence of posterior opinion between agents from convergence of an agent's posterior to a true value.
