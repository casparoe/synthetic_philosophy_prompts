I keep bumping into something that looks like an asymmetry in how we treat physical and psychiatric diagnoses, and I can't tell whether it's justified. If a doctor tells me I have a benign tumor or a heart murmur, everyone treats the label as a neutral description of some tissue. The tumor does not care what we call it, and nobody worries that writing benign tumor in my chart will make the tumor worse or change who I am. But if a doctor tells me I have ADHD, or borderline personality disorder, or chronic depression, the label is treated as radioactive in a way the tumor label is not. The diagnosis might become my identity; I might start reinterpreting my whole biography through it; my friends and boss might start reading ordinary behavior through it; and clinicians themselves might look for evidence that confirms it. Because of this, we argue about psychiatric categories at an intensity we would never bring to cardiology categories, and people genuinely worry that adding a label to the DSM will create, or reinforce, the very thing it names. I think this is what Ian Hacking called a looping effect, but dont quote me.

What I want to know is whether there is actually a difference in kind between the tumor classification and the ADHD classification that justifies treating psychiatric labels as specially self-fulfilling or harmful, or whether that is just a comfortable line we have drawn. Maybe physical labels loop too — a cancer patient, an obese patient, a chronic pain patient — and we simply notice it less. Tell me whether the asymmetry should be kept, given up, or redrawn.
