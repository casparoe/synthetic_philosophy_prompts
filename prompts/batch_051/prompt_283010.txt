I keep noticing a split in how my own thinking happens, and I can't tell if the second half has a established name or whether I'd be making one up. Some of my thinking is just inner speech—full sentences in English running through my head, and the thought seems to be those sentences. But other times a whole thought shows up at once, like a counterexample while someone is mid-sentence, and it isn't in any language at all: I know exactly what I want to say, I can hold it still while I search for words, and the words that come out feel like a translation of something already complete. The clearest case is when I switch languages and the same thought comes out in English and then again in another language without the thought itself changing, as if it lives in neither language. I know inner speech is a term for the first kind, and I'm not asking about just that; this wordless thing feels like a different layer underneath. Is there a name for the view that this underneath-layer has a structure of its own—something with parts that combine, not just a vague feeling—and should that view also count inner speech as a case of it, or are there two different kinds of thinking here? I don't just want the label; I want the edges: where the thought stops and the words start, and what would count as evidence that the underneath-layer is real rather than just a feeling of knowing.
