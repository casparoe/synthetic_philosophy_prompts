In a seminar on human rights we keep going in circles about whether "human dignity" is actually doing any intellectual work. The Kantian side says dignity is the foundation: it's why you can't torture someone even when a ticking-bomb story makes that look useful, why consent doesn't fix everything, and why some treatment is wrong regardless of consequences. The skeptical side says dignity is a placeholder—anything it's supposed to explain is either autonomy, or respect for persons, or just the raw fact that something feels degrading, and calling it "dignity" mostly makes the argument sound grander.

I don't need you to settle that debate in the abstract. What I want to know is whether it could be settled, or at least moved, by evidence. Treat this as the target claim: dignity does no independent normative work; it's autonomy plus respect with better branding. Design the best empirical test of that claim you can. Be concrete about the vignettes or comparisons you'd use—say, cases where a person fully consents to being humiliated, or where someone's autonomy is protected but they're still treated as a prop—and say exactly what result would count as evidence that dignity pulls its weight, and what result would count as evidence that it's dispensable. Then tell me what the test would leave open even in principle. For instance, maybe you can show that people's judgments don't track a dignity/autonomy distinction, but that wouldn't show the concept is useless in justification; or maybe you can show the opposite and still leave open whether dignity is a primitive notion or just a summary term. I'm less interested in the actual results of some study than in the logic of the test itself, and its limits. There's a sense where its hard to tell if the question is empirical or just conceptual hygiene.
