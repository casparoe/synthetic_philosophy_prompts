I write about nationalism and collective memory for a living, and I can make the constructivist case in my sleep: most national traditions are 19th-century inventions, the supposedly timeless customs usually have traceable launch dates, and the sense that a people has always been here is the imagined community doing it's job. I believe the argument. And yet every Remembrance Sunday I stand in the crowd with my dad, and the swell of it doesn't feel recent or manufactured at all — it feels like it reaches back and claims me, even while I'm drafting the piece that debunks it. I want the strongest version of the case that this feeling is just the invented tradition working on me exactly as designed, and the strongest version of the case that it's tracking something real that the constructivist story leaves out. Then tell me which one should give way, and what it would actually take for the other to go.
