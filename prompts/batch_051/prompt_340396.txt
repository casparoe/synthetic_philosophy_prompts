Construct a thought experiment that satisfies the following specifications. The case is meant to isolate the difference between a Fregean and a Millian treatment of identity statements: a Fregean holds that a singular term contributes both a referent and a sense (a mode of presentation of that referent), while a Millian holds that it contributes only its referent. The case should force the two theories to agree about which object is designated and about every non-linguistic fact, while disagreeing about what the identity judgment says and about what a subject learns in accepting it. Anything that secures the disagreement by stipulating the theories' verdicts will count as a failure; the disagreement must be generated by the structure of the case.

The specifications are these.

(1) The case involves one ordinary object introduced under two distinct modes of presentation for a single subject. Neither mode of presentation is a proper name, an indexical or demonstrative, a quotational device, or a metalinguistic description (for example, a description that mentions the word or name by which the object is called).

(2) The subject has full competence with both modes of presentation and is in an otherwise ideal epistemic condition: fully attentive, no memory failures, no false background beliefs, and no motive for self-deception.

(3) Before the identity is recognized, the subject can rationally accept that the object presented in the first way is the object presented in the first way, while rationally doubting that the object presented in the first way is the object presented in the second way, and can treat coming to accept the latter as a genuine gain in knowledge.

(4) The scenario is confined to ordinary objects, persons, institutions, and events: no science-fiction elements, no exotic physics, and no psychology that an actual human being could not credibly exhibit.

(5) The informativeness of the identity judgment is not presupposed by the setup; it must emerge from the relationship between the two modes of presentation themselves.

Then do all of the following. First, state the scenario in at most two paragraphs and show explicitly that it meets each of (1)–(5). Second, give the Fregean account of the case: specify the two senses, describe what the subject knew before and after, and explain why the identity judgment is true yet informative. Third, give the strongest Millian or referentialist account of the same case: state what proposition the identity judgment expresses on that view, what the subject learns if anything, and how the Millian explains the felt informativeness without positing a semantic difference between the terms. Fourth, identify the single structural feature of the scenario that does the theoretical work, explain how a Millian would attack or redescribe that feature, and say whether removing it would collapse the case into a trivial tautology or leave a residual puzzle. Conclude with a precise statement of what the case does and does not establish about identity statements.
