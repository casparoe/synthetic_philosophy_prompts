I am a graduate student in environmental history, and I have been puzzling over an asymmetry in how the field handles non-human forces. We are comfortable writing that the Black Death reshaped European labor markets, that Little Ice Age climate stress contributed to harvest failures and unrest, and that the Tambora eruption chilled the Northern Hemisphere and ruined harvests. We call such things causes, and sometimes even historical actors in scare quotes, but we do not treat them as the same kind of explanatory element as a government, a social movement, or an individual: we would not say that a pathogen pursued a strategy, that a climate made a decision, or that a volcano bore responsibility. Yet the same field is quick to flag anything that looks like environmental determinism — explanation in which geography or climate simply dictates how a society develops, leaving no real room for institutions, culture, or contingency.

I want you to reconstruct the theory this practice implicitly presupposes. What account of the difference between a cause and an agent must an environmental historian be relying on, if any, to write in this way without contradiction? Does assigning major causal weight to disease or climate already commit one, in practice, to a determinism of the sort the field disavows, or is there a principled line between recognizing non-human causes and falling into environmental determinism? Spell out the distinctions the practice needs — for example between triggering, constraining, shaping, and deciding — and say which commitments are strictly required for the practice to be coherent, which are merely professional habits or anxieties, and where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several different theories of causation and agency would license the same writing. Finally, tell me what environmental history would have to change if the presupposed view were dropped: what would the field look like if it instead held that non-human systems can be genuinely agential, or that all explanation is causal and environmental determinism is only a term of abuse rather than a genuine fallacy? I care less about verdicts on particular episodes than about the structure of the implicit theory.
