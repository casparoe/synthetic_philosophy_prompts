A colleague of mine who works on the philosophy of contract keeps a set of imaginary cases for testing when standard-form terms actually bind someone. Over the years I have collected her verdicts. She says a customer who signs a printed apartment lease without reading it is bound by its standard early-termination penalty. She says a user who scrolls through but does not read a box of terms and then clicks "I agree" during an online purchase is bound. She says a user is not bound by terms accessible only through an obscure footer link that he never opened. She says a buyer is not bound by terms shown for the first time only after payment had already been taken. She says a user who checks a box stating that he has read and agreed to terms he has not read is bound even when the terms contain a one-sided arbitration clause, provided the checkbox and a link to the terms were plainly visible and competing vendors were readily available. She says a visually impaired user is not bound when no screen-reader-compatible version of the terms was offered and he clicked through without any realistic way to have the terms read to him.

The new case is this. An independent contractor must accept revised terms to continue working through the only platform that provides her income. The revised terms appear in a scroll-box and add a mandatory arbitration clause with a class-action waiver. She read the clause, understands it, and objects to it, but she clicks "I agree" because no realistic alternative exists: competing platforms use substantially identical terms, and leaving the platform would eliminate her income. There is no allegation that the platform threatened her or misrepresented the terms; the procedure was transparent.

What is your best prediction about how my colleague will judge this new case, and what rule or rules would make her earlier verdicts and this prediction cohere? In particular, does her earlier pattern commit her to a procedural notice-and-opportunity rule, or does it also require a meaningful alternative before she will call something agreement? If her past verdicts underdetermine the answer, say which candidate rules fit those verdicts, what each predicts for the new case, which earlier case is the closest analog, and specify a further case that would cleanly separate the candidate rules.
