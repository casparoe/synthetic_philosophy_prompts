I run a tiny philosophy-and-music podcast out of my apartment, and after a kind of stupid argument with my drummer about whether the demo and the final mix of one of our songs are "the same song," I've gotten myself tangled up in the ontology of recorded music. Next week's guest is a philosopher who defends a specific line: that for a lot of popular music, the work isn't the abstract song (the melody and chord changes) and isn't the live performance — it's the recording itself, and a live version, a cover, or a demo is a different kind of animal, not another token of the same work. I don't want the interview to be him just restating that thesis over and over.

Draft eight to ten questions that make him actually work. I especially want him pressed on: (1) what the identity conditions of a recording could be — if you remaster it, or fix one bad vocal edit, or release a mono and a stereo mix, is it still the same work? (2) what he says about music made directly on tape or in a DAW, where there was never a prior unrecorded "song" to be captured; (3) how he handles live records, or studio albums assembled from dozens of takes across different sessions; and (4) what, if anything, makes a cover "of" the same work on his view rather than just a similar-sounding new thing. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, so he can't just retreat to the stock reply.
