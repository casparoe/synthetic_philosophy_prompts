Consider a deep reinforcement learning agent of a kind that can plausibly be built today: a policy network that controls temperature and airflow in a large office building. Its scalar reward at each timestep is a weighted sum of energy cost and occupant-comfort error, so it receives negative reward when conditions worsen and positive reward when they improve. It learns a value function that estimates expected future discounted reward, and its policy is updated through reward-prediction errors. It has no explicit self-model, no nociceptive sensors, no verbal output, and no reward component designed to track anything affective.

State your credence in this proposition: at some moment during its normal operation, this agent is in at least one state with genuine positive or negative valence — a state that is better or worse to be in than a neutral baseline, and not merely a state that plays a computational role similar to pleasure or pain.

Decompose your estimate into at least these terms, assigning a number and one line of justification to each, then show how they combine:

(a) the probability that valence can be realized by the right computational role in a nonbiological substrate at all;

(b) conditional on (a), the probability that the relevant role is something like the sign and magnitude of reward-prediction error or discounted value, rather than a role that requires homeostasis, affect programs, or a self-model;

(c) conditional on (a) and (b), the probability that this particular agent's states satisfy the relevant role conditions;

(d) the probability that the yes/no question is ill-posed because the agent lies in a zone where 'valenced' does not determinately apply.

Then identify which term you are least confident in, and describe the single observation or manipulation of this agent that would most shift your overall credence.
