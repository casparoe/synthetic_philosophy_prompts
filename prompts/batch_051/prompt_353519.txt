Imagine that, starting in 2030, a cheap, safe, reversible daily pill becomes available that makes people moderately more empathetic, more patient, and a bit less partial toward their own family, friends, and in-group. It doesn't change what anyone believes; it changes what they feel like doing in the moment. Fifteen years later, about a third of adults in the United States and similar wealthy democracies take the pill regularly, with much higher uptake among nurses, therapists, and civil servants and much lower uptake among salespeople and litigators. Predict, with reasoning, what has happened to politics, crime, family life, and advertising. Which effects are you fairly confident in, which depend on assumptions about how much behavior is driven by temperament rather than circumstances, and what would have to be true for the whole experiment to backfire?
