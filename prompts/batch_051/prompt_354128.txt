Lately I've been working through a toy universe I came up with, and it has tied me in knots. Imagine a wide, flat tray filled with millions of tiny magnetized balls—call them simmballs—that slide around and click together under simple local rules. The balls are all there is to the toy. Left to themselves, they form steady clusters, and the clusters bump, merge, and break apart in regular ways. Eventually you can tell a higher-level story about the tray: some cluster-patterns act like messages or symbols, and a message reaching a cluster can change what that cluster does next. But I cannot decide whether the message is really pushing the balls around, or whether it is just shorthand for what the balls are doing anyway.

Here is the argument that keeps bothering me.

(1) At any instant, each simmball's next move is fully fixed by the pushes and pulls it receives from the other simmballs around it.
(2) So the complete layout of all the simmballs at one moment, together with those local rules, fixes the entire later layout.
(3) A message, sign, or symbol in the tray is not an extra thing over and above the balls; it just is a certain arrangement and sequence of balls.
(4) Therefore the message can add no shove beyond what the balls are already doing, which means the message itself is causally idle with respect to the balls.
(5) But the entire cluster-level story—and, by the same reasoning, any account of the tray pitched above the level of the balls—is idle in the same way.
(6) A thought or message in our own world is likewise only a pattern in some physical stuff, not an extra force, so by parallel reasoning no thought or message ever has any causal push.

I half-think this is just a clean lesson about levels of description, but it also seems to prove too much, and I cannot tell whether that is a surprising truth or a sign the argument slipped somewhere. Please take a definite position: if the argument is sound, say so and defend it; if it fails, tell me exactly which step fails, why it fails, and what the strongest corrected version of that step would be, and then tell me whether the corrected argument still reaches the conclusion in (6). Do not lay out the options and leave me to choose. Write in continuous prose, like a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
