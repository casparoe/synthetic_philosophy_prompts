I'm a philosophy grad student who did CS undergrad, and I can't get a certain analogy out of my head. The grounding literature says some facts hold in virtue of other facts: the table exists in virtue of the molecules arranged table-wise, and those facts hold in virtue of still others, until you bottom out in fundamental facts that dont depend on anything. Every time I read this I see a build graph. Fundamental facts are the source files, grounding is the dependency edge, a chain of grounds is a path through the graph, well-foundedness is the claim that the graph is acyclic enough that a topological sort exists and the build terminates, and the ban on circular grounding is a circular-dependency error. Asymmetry comes free, and transitivity is just walking the arrows.

The obvious objection is that make and its friends are temporal and causal — you build the prerequisite before the target — while grounding is supposed to be synchronic and non-causal. But I think I can dodge that by saying the analogy is to the static dependency graph, not to the build process. So: is this a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion? Where exactly does it come apart? Two things already bug me. First, circular imports are sometimes legal in real languages (Python lets you do it and just suffers at import time), which the analogy says should be impossible — but maybe that just maps onto the live debate about whether grounding has to be well-founded. Second, build dependencies are stipulated by the programmer and can be refactored away, while grounding is supposed to be mind-independent and hyperintensional; I can't tell whether that's a fatal disanalogy or just a sign that the graph is a model rather than the thing itself. What would have to be true on both sides for results about DAGs — topological sorts, strongly connected components, longest paths — to actually transfer to metaphysics? And does the parallel predict anything false about either side?
