Construct a thought experiment for use in an essay on whether the procreation asymmetry is best explained by a person-affecting premise or by an impersonal axiology with auxiliary principles. The asymmetry to be isolated is this: there is no reason grounded in the child's own welfare to bring into existence a child whose life would be worth living, but there is decisive reason grounded in the child's own welfare not to bring into existence a child whose life would not be worth living.

The experiment should consist of two choice situations facing the same prospective parents, identical in every respect except the sign of one welfare magnitude. In the positive situation, they may either create a single child whose life is projected to be slightly worth living or create no one; in the negative situation, they may either create a single child whose life is projected to be slightly not worth living or create no one. The identity of the possible child is fixed within each situation, with no alternative child and no option of a different child, so the nonidentity problem's multiple-option structure is screened off. No existing person is affected, there is no uncertainty about the welfare prediction, there is no resource scarcity, and the setting is an ordinary one rather than science-fictional.

For the deliverable, state the verdict each of the following theories gives in each situation, with the reasoning spelled out: (i) a strict person-affecting restriction, on which an outcome can be better or worse only if someone who exists, or would exist, is made better or worse off by it; and (ii) an impersonal total axiology, on which the value of an outcome is the sum of well-being levels across everyone who exists, with non-existence contributing nothing. Then identify the structural feature that does the isolating work. In particular, say whether the divergence survives when the positive life is pushed arbitrarily close to the zero point while remaining positive, and whether it collapses once the totalist is allowed a standing procreative-liberty constraint under which positive welfare reasons are optional but negative welfare reasons are mandatory. End by explaining how a defender of the total view would attack the case as rigged, and whether the person-affecting side can answer that attack without merely restating the asymmetry.
