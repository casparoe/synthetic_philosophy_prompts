Consider the following hypothesis from the physics of information, stated here as a testable claim rather than a slogan. A one-bit record of the state of a thermal system can function as a fuel: an engine that measures and then exploits which side of a box a particle occupies — a Szilard-style realization of Maxwell's demon — should in principle extract up to kBT ln 2 of work per cycle, while the measurement itself can be made thermodynamically almost free. The cost appears only when the demon's memory must be reset: erasing one bit requires dissipating at least kBT ln 2. On this picture, information enters the energy ledger in the same units as heat and work, and the second law holds only after the demon's memory is included in the accounting.

A colleague objects that this is a metaphor with equations attached: the demon, she says, is merely rectifying the particle's thermal fluctuations, and the apparent gain comes from not tracking the full entropy of the particle-plus-memory system.

Design the strongest experiment you can to resolve as much of this disagreement as an experiment can resolve. Specify the physical system, the measurement-feedback protocol, and the step that is supposed to convert information into work. State exactly what outcome you would count as support for the information-as-resource picture, what outcome would tell against it, and what a null result would show. Then explain what even a fully successful experiment would leave open: would it establish that information itself is physical, or only that a certain class of correlations can be converted into work under an austere entropy accounting? Which parts of the claim are empirical, and which parts are commitments about how to define a bit and how to individuate the demon's memory?
