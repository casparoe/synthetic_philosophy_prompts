I teach political philosophy and I'm on my university's benefits committee. We're supposed to revise our paid parental leave policy, and the committee is split. One proposal is a single transferable pool of leave that either parent can use; the other is an equal, non-transferable entitlement for each parent. People keep saying the transferable pool is more flexible, but I think that might let the same old default creep back in. I keep comming back to this passage from Mill's The Subjection of Women, chapter 2, where he describes the old common law of marriage:

"The two are called "one person in law," for the purpose of inferring that whatever is hers is his, but the parallel inference is never drawn that whatever is his is hers; the maxim is not applied against the man, except to make him responsible to third parties for her acts, as a master is for the acts of his slaves or of his cattle."

I know that's 1869 and about coverture, not parental leave. But it seems to me to get at how a rule can look formally neutral while actually letting one person's situation absorb the other's. I need to circulate a short memo before Thursday's meeting with a concrete recommendation. Please draft it. It should say which policy—or what hybrid—we should adopt, and it should take the Mill point seriously without pretending a leave policy is the same as coverture. Also engage the strongest argument on the losing side, because if my memo just ignores flexibility, I'll lose the committee.
