An instructor introduced Du Bois's "Criteria of Negro Art" and I've been turning it into an argument in my head, but it lands somewhere I don't want to accept. The bit that stuck with me is, roughly, the place where Du Bois says the objection that art shouldn't be propaganda is not evenhandedly applied: when art reinforces a dominant worldview, it gets treated as universal or disinterested, but when Black art pushes back, it gets branded as propaganda. From that I reasoned:

(1) Every artwork carries a political perspective, even when it presents itself as apolitical or purely aesthetic.
(2) If that's right, then "apolitical" or "disinterested" art is not a kind of art; it's just art whose politics aligns with the dominant view's self-image.
(3) So the distinction between art and propaganda is not a distinction among artworks, only among whose interests an artwork serves.
(4) So judging art for aesthetic value apart from politics is a category mistake.
(5) But that conclusion seems way too strong: it would make the difference between a subtle novel and a crude recruiting poster a matter of whose side you're on, not craft or honesty.

I want to keep (1), because it seems to be Du Bois's point, but I don't see how to reject (3) or (4) without buying into the asymmetry he was attacking. Which step do you reject, and what exactly would rejecting it cost? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, without preamble or restating the question. Also tell me what argument or consideration would most likely change your verdict.
