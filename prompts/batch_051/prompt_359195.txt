Some vegans are totally fine eating animal products they fish out of a dumpster or that a supermarket is about to toss — stuff that's still sealed and would've gone in the trash anyway — but they'd never pay for it. I'm trying to work out what that practice quietly commits them to. On the face of it they're locating the wrongness of animal products in something like demand: buying it keeps the system going, whereas eating trash food doesn't. But is the practice actually neutral, or does eating rescued cheese still do something, like treating animals as food or signalling to the people around you that eating them is fine? And would a vegan who thinks animals have rights, independent of any welfare calculation, even be able to touch dumpster meat, or does the practice only really make sense on a consequentialist picture? I want a reconstruction of the implicit theory behind dumpster-diving veganism, where its underdetermined (so more than one theory would license it), whether it's internally consistent, and what would have to change about the practice if you located the wrongness in something like defilement, disrespect, or a rights violation instead.
