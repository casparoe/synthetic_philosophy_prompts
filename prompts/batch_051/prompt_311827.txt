You are a professor of political philosophy with deep expertise in Hannah Arendt's work on truth, facticity, and lying in politics. I want a rigorous conceptual analysis, not a survey answer. Begin by briefly canvassing the ambiguities in the reconstruction below; then commit to the most defensible interpretation and apply it systematically.

Consider this reconstruction of Arendt's notion of the distinctively modern political lie, as opposed to the familiar, limited political falsehood. Four marks:

C1. Scope: it is an organized, sustained campaign rather than an isolated false statement.
C2. Aim: it seeks not to conceal one damaging fact but to install in the public a comprehensive, internally coherent substitute account of reality.
C3. Mechanism: it exploits the distinctive fragility of factual truth — that facts are contingent, depend on testimony and settled records, and can be rearranged once that record is destabilized.
C4. Effect on the liar: practitioners come to inhabit or at least prefer their constructed account, loosening their grip on the distinction between the fabricated image and the world.

Now assess the following case. A democratic government maintains a well-funded influence agency — call it the Bureau of Public Sense — that refuses to issue verifiably false statements. Its method is the mass algorithmic rearrangement of true materials: accurate statistics shown without their denominator, true but unrepresentative anecdotes amplified as typical, real events resequenced so that a false causal story emerges, and relentless targeted repetition until much of the public holds a picture of the economy, a foreign war, or a public-health program that is systematically false as a whole even though every discrete claim would individually pass a fact-check. Inside the Bureau, some analysts become convinced that their constructed picture is the only politically workable reality, while others remain clear-eyed.

Question: Does the Bureau's work instantiate the modern political lie in the Arendtian sense? Work through C1 through C4 one by one, saying explicitly which are satisfied, which fail, and which leave the verdict underdetermined. Then ask whether the case shows that the reconstruction must be revised: in particular, does an act of political lying require at least some literal falsehood, or can the systematic arrangement of truths, aimed at producing a false world-picture, count as the kind of lying Arendt regarded as politically dangerous? If you revise a criterion, state the revised version and test it against the case. Close by saying whether, on your best account, the Bureau is engaged in political lying in the sense that should concern a democratic public.
