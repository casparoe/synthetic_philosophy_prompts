So I got into this argument with a friend about whether Spinoza could make sense to a normal person without the whole system. I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, and I've only read parts of the Ethics in translation. The passage that grabbed me is from Part V, Proposition 17 of the Ethics — I don't have the exact wording, but he says something like: God is without passions and is not affected by any emotion of pleasure or pain. He also says somewhere around there that strictly speaking God loves no one. That hit me as weird because he also talks about the intellectual love of God, and from what I can tell he thinks we love God, and that God can be said to love himself, but not that God loves us back in any personal sense. Actually I think the exact line is in the Corollary to Proposition 17, something like "God is free from passions; strictly speaking, God loves no one," and before that he's arguing God cannot properly be said to love or hate anyone.

Now, my friend is a practising Catholic and pretty sharp, and the obvious move is to say: if God doesn't love me, why would I orient my whole life toward this intellectual love of God? What am I even loving? I kind of get that Spinoza means something different by "love" there — it's not an emotion that happens to you, it's more like understanding reality correctly and feeling joy that comes from that understanding. But I'm not confident I could teach it.

So here's the prompt I want. I have to run a one-hour discussion at a reading group of other software engineers, none of us with philosophy background. The topic is: how should I sequence the concepts so that the group actually understands Spinoza's intellectual love of God — and doesn't just hear it as "be rational and meditate on nature" or something? What has to be in place first? I'm thinking I need to explain his distinction between actions and passions (active vs passive emotions), and maybe his three kinds of knowledge, before the "love" part makes sense. But maybe there's a better order. I'd also like a carrying example that isn't from the Ethics, something from our actual lives as engineers, that makes the idea of a joy arising from understanding a necessary truth click. And what misconceptions should I expect? I already know one: people will read "love" and think it's a warm feeling, and they'll read "God" and think it's a person. Where in the sequence do I head those off? Also, what should I explicitly refuse to explain at that level, so it doesn't degenerate into us pretending we understand substance monism? I want a real teaching plan, not a summary of Spinoza, and I want a question I can ask at the end to check whether they got the distinction between loving God and being loved by God.
