I have been puzzling over self-binding and constitutions, and I have ended up with a view I nearly believe and an intuition I cannot make peace with.

The view is this: precommitment is not freedom given up but freedom distributed across time. When I promise, throw away the cigarettes, or accept rules that are deliberately hard to change, I am not letting an earlier self overrule a later one; I am one self governing its whole life against impulses it knows will arrive and knows it will be too weak to resist in the moment. A constitutional restriction adopted by a founding generation looks to me like the same kind of act at collective scale: not the past enslaving the present, but a political community holding itself to what it resolved on before panic, faction, or temptation arrived. The view has a single clean principle for both cases, and it explains why a rule I freely accepted yesterday can legitimately constrain me today without any appeal to mystery about the self.

The intuition is equally clear, and it will not dissolve. There feels like a real difference between Ulysses ordering the crew to bind him to the mast and a child born into a constitutional order she had no chance to join. When I have genuinely reconsidered something — not a craving, but a thought-out change of mind — yesterday's resolution stops feeling like my own voice and starts feeling like an outside constraint that happens to be stored inside my head. A constitutional restriction intensifies the problem: it is enforced against people who were not yet born, which feels like the dead hand of the past rather than a crew following standing orders. My own theory keeps telling me this difference is only one of degree, because my future self also never chose to be born into my earlier self. But I feel the two cases as different in kind, and I am not sure whether to trust the theory or the feeling.

I do not want a verdict that simply picks one side. I want the strongest case for each move: what it would take for this intuition to be the thing I abandon, perhaps by showing that it rests on a failure to identify with my own future or on a mistaken idea that birth, not participation, creates political obligation; and what it would take for the theory to be the thing I amend or abandon, perhaps by showing that it overgeneralizes from the narrow case of addictive cravings to considered changes of mind. Then tell me whether there is a reading on which both survive — for example, that precommitments are legitimate only when they bind against known transient distortions and only when those bound can in principle reauthorize or exit them — and whether that reading is a real reconciliation or just a hedge. I am less interested in what I should end up believing than in what it would cost me to give up each of these convictions.
