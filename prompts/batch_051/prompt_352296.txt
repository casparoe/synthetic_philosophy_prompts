A prominent reading of Kuhn holds that, across a scientific revolution, parts of the old and new paradigms' vocabularies are taxonomically incommensurable: terms such as mass or planet are embedded in different taxonomic structures, so there is no neutral language into which the statements of both sides can be translated without remainder. That claim is often treated as if it were straightforwardly an empirical thesis about the historical record, yet it is hard to see what observation could confirm it rather than merely showing that translation is hard, that scientists talk past each other for sociological reasons, or that later interpreters are missing relevant background knowledge.
