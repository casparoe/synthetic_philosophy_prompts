I'm running a reading group on nationalism and we've hit the same wall I've seen in the literature. The modernist side — Anderson, Hobsbawm, Gellner — thinks nations are modern constructions, assembled through print-capitalism, state-backed education, and invented traditions, so a question like "how old is France?" is basically a category mistake. The ethnosymbolist side, Anthony Smith especially, says that can't explain why some nationalist projects succeed and others don't, because successful ones usually attach themselves to pre-existing ethnic cores, myths, memories, and symbols that go back centuries. We've spent three sessions on this and nobody has conceeded anything. The empirical facts aren't really disputed; it seems like each side uses "nation" to pick out a different thing, then accuses the other side of not explaining the phenomenon they care about. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is: is it mostly verbal, is it two research programs asking different questions under one heading, is there a hidden political or normative stake, or is it a factual disagreement about history that could in principle be settled? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a sharper distinction, a particular kind of historical evidence, a formal model, or just agreeing the question was badly posed? Please separate what's settled among scholars of nationalism from what's genuinely contested, and don't present one school's view as the consensus. Also write in American English.
