I run a film-and-philosophy night and next month we're doing Inception alongside Hofstadter's idea that the self is a strange loop — an "I" that's just a self-referential symbol with no ground floor beneath it. The lazy move is to say the nested dreams are the strange loops, but I think that's wrong, and I want a reading that gets at why. My suspicion: the film keeps assuming there is a bottom level (Cobb's real world, the totem) and that the self is whatever can tell the levels apart — which is pretty much the opposite of a strange loop. So which is it: does the film dramatize the self as a strange loop, or a self that survives by being anchored outside the loop? Walk me through two or three scenes without recapping the whole plot, take the film and the philosophy seriously, and say where Inception resists the Hofstadter reading rather than papering over it.
