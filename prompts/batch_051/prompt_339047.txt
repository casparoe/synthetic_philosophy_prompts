Alright, I keep coming back to this and I can't tell whether I've found something real or I'm just pattern-matching. As a novelist—currently working on a branching narrative game, so this is on my mind a lot—I've started to think free indirect style and player-choice game design are the same trick seen from opposite ends. In free indirect style, the third-person narrator slides into a character's voice and limited view: the prose takes on the character's idiom, the reader sees only what the character sees, but the narrator is still the one fixing the facts, and the character never actually takes over. Nothing becomes true in the novel because I read it. In a branching game, the player steps into a character and makes choices, feels like the source of the story, but the game is a fixed set of pre-authored branches; nothing becomes true in the fiction unless the code already allowed it, and the designer set the space of possible facts in advance. Both look like a two-voice structure where an external author keeps final control while whoever is inside the fiction gets the felt experience of agency. Is this deep or superficial? My worry is the disanalogy is the whole point: a reader's attention doesn't causally change a novel, but a player's input does change the game state—so maybe free indirect style is just the novelist's way of faking an agency games give for real. But the player's 'real' agency is also confined inside a tree the designer drew. I'd like a clean statement of the mapping, what would have to be true of both sides for the comparison to carry weight, and at least one thing the parallel predicts that turns out false. Structural identity, loose analogy, or confusion?
