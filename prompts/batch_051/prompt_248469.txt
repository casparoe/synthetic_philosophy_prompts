I am a high school junior and the lead maintainer of my school's robotics team codebase. Our control software is distributed under the MIT License, but one vision module we ship depends on a library released under version 3 of the Free Software Foundation's GNU General Public License. While reading the license to figure out what we actually have to do, I focused on this sentence in Section 0, "Definitions," of the GPLv3:

To "modify" a work means to copy from or adapt all or part of the work in a fashion requiring copyright permission, other than the making of an exact copy. The resulting work is called a "modified version" of the earlier work or a work "based on" the earlier work.

The argument on the team concerns a case the people who drafted this language in 2007 did not have in mind. One of us produced a utility by using a large code-generation model that had been trained in part on GPL-licensed repositories, including the library we depend on. The tool emitted functions that do the same job as parts of the library, with a very similar structure and a few identical variable names, but our repository contains no verbatim copy of the library's source; the tool appears to have paraphrased, reordered, and recombined what it learned. The model provider must have copied the library to train the model, but our team only received the generated output. One teammate says our module cannot be a "work based on the Program" under this definition because no human on our team copied text from the library. Another teammate says the definition reaches our case anyway: by training on the library and emitting a close substitute, we are "adapt[ing] all or part of the work in a fashion requiring copyright permission," so our module would be covered by the GPL when we distribute it. I want to know what this definition actually requires in our situation. Please work through the different approaches to interpreting it—its plain words, the purpose of the GPL's copyleft requirement, what the Free Software Foundation's drafters would probably have said if they had been asked in 2007 about machine-learning-generated code, and what a reasonable reader of the license text would understand—and then say which approach should decide here, and why.
