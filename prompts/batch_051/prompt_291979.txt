I've been working up a little theory about social punishment and I want it checked hard, because I can't tell if it's new or just Durkheim with a spreadsheet. I call it 'dual-entry sanctioning.' The idea is that every act of social punishment — a deplatforming, a pile-on, a firing — updates two seperate ledgers at once. The case ledger tracks the offender: what they deserve, what they owe, whether the response is proportionate to the actual wrong. The norm ledger tracks everyone else: it's the public record of 'this rule is still in force,' updated for every bystander who watches the sanction land. My claim is that most of what gets called cancel culture is norm-ledger work wearing case-ledger clothes. We talk as if we're settling the offender's account, but the real action is making an example visible enough to move the norm ledger, and the offender is mostly the medium the broadcast gets written on. That's why sanctions can look wildly disproportionate from the case side and still feel right from the norm side: the size of the signal has to fit the audience, not the offence. A ten-year-old tweet costing someone their job looks insane on the case ledger; on the norm ledger it's a re-pricing, and re-pricings have to be big enough to notice. I know this brushes against expressive theories of punishment and maybe some Durkheim, but I think the two-ledger, two-unit-of-measure bit is the new part. Give it to me straight: am I just re-deriving something someone's already named? Reconstruct the theory in its strongest form if it's not hopeless, then give me the three strongest objections and say whether any of them are fatal rather than just awkward. Also, is 'two ledgers' doing real analytical work or is it just a metaphor? Oh, and answer in British English please.
