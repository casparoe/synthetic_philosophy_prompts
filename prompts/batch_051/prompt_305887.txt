- a salesperson lies that a second-hand phone has never been dropped, to get you to buy it — manipulative
- a friend gives you the actual price, battery life and storage of two phones and says it's up to you — not manipulative, just persuasion
- a charity volunteer tells you your neighbour already donated when they actually didn't — manipulative
- a coach says if you skip training you won't start on Saturday, and that's literally what the selection policy says — not manipulative
- an advert calls a trainer fast and shows a pro athlete sprinting, but makes no specific claim about what it'll do for you — not manipulative
- a website says you can cancel in one click, but it actually takes five menus and a phone call — manipulative

The way she talks about it, its mostly about whether the person still gets to make the choice themselves, or whether the other person is working on them to override that.
