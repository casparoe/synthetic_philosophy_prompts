I run a small philosophy blog aimed at non-academic readers, and I want to publish a sorting instrument: Which information environment are you actually in? It should function as a real diagnostic quiz, not a lightweight personality test.

Build the quiz around this theoretical distinction, which you should explain in the quiz's own words but not take for granted. An epistemic bubble is a structural feature of a person's information environment: relevant dissenting voices are simply absent from what they encounter, filtered out by platform algorithms, social homophily, or selective media habits, without any active campaign to discredit those voices. An echo chamber is different: relevant dissenting voices are not merely absent; the community or trusted mediators have already credentialed them as compromised or bad-faith, so when dissenting testimony does reach the person, it is processed as evidence of the source's unreliability rather than as evidence against the person's view. A bubble is a shortage of contrary input; a chamber is a trained distrust of contrary sources. The two can overlap, but they are not the same.

Please write the quiz to meet these specifications.

The quiz should have eight to ten multiple-choice questions. Each question should be a concrete, non-jargon scenario: a news story that cuts against a belief the respondent holds, a friend who has changed her mind, an outgroup expert making a careful argument on a podcast, a retraction issued by a major outlet, an in-group authority warning that certain sources are contaminated. Each question should offer at least three answer options. The questions should jointly probe two independent axes: exposure, meaning whether and how often decision-relevant dissenting information reaches the respondent from sources they could in principle take seriously; and credence-response, meaning whether, when dissenting information arrives, it can lower their confidence on the merits, or whether they have been primed to discount it because of its source. No question should ask respondents whether they think they are in an epistemic bubble or an echo chamber, and no answer option should ask them to choose among labels.

Then provide a scoring key. Build the classification from the logical space of the two axes rather than from the names: cross high and low exposure with high and low vulnerability to source-discrediting, and for each cell say what it corresponds to and diagnose the cell. The required leaves are: epistemic bubble without echo chamber; echo chamber without bubble; both together; neither, meaning open engagement with contrary evidence; and, if your questions can genuinely separate it from a bubble, a fifth leaf for deliberate, self-aware enclosure, a filter the person knowingly chose and can override. If any cell in the two-by-two space is empty, unstable, or represents a position nobody plausibly occupies, say so and explain briefly why rather than papering over the gap. In particular, keep the bubble and chamber cells distinct by asking what would happen to the respondent's confidence if a credible dissenting voice were introduced, not merely by whether such voices currently show up.

End with a short limitations section: what the instrument cannot distinguish on the basis of self-report, such as whether the respondent really sits inside a trust-manipulating community or is merely individually distrustful, whether the respondent remembers their media diet accurately, and whether low reported exposure reflects algorithms, habits, or the actual absence of dissent on the topic. Say which positions the quiz collapses and why.
