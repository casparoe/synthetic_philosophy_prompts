In a unit on medieval Islamic philosophy, we spent a lesson on Avicenna's flying-man thought experiment, and I am trying to figure out exactly how much it can prove. As our textbook paraphrased it: imagine a person created all at once, fully developed, blindfolded, suspended in the air with his limbs spread apart so they do not touch, and receiving no sensations from outside. Avicenna's suggestion is that this person would still know that he himself exists. The conclusion my teacher drew from this was much stronger than anything in the setup, and I want to know whether the argument really goes through.

Here is the reconstruction I wrote out:

(1) Such a person would have no awareness of anything bodily: no sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch, pressure, temperature, or sense of the position and movement of his limbs.
(2) That same person would nevertheless be directly aware of himself, and would affirm his own existence.
(3) If someone can be aware of himself while having no awareness of anything bodily, then the self he is aware of is not his body, or any part or state of his body.
(4) Therefore the self the flying man is aware of is not the body or any part or state of the body.
(5) Therefore each of us is not identical with his or her body; there is a self or soul over and above the body, and it can be grasped without the senses.

I keep going back and forth about where the mistake is. Premise (1) already looks questionable, since a person floating that way might still feel warm or cold, or sense the position of his limbs. But my teacher said the point was supposed to be against premise (3): just because I can know one thing under one description without knowing it under another, it does not follow that the two are distinct — a person can know water without knowing that water is H2O. Is that a fair objection to this argument? Is the argument valid as written? If not, which step fails first, and what is the strongest conclusion the flying man could still support once the defective step is corrected?

Please put your verdict up front (valid, or not valid and which step fails first), and keep the entire response under 450 words.
