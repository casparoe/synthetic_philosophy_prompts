I have been rereading the Ant Fugue section of Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach, where he asks whether Aunt Hillary, an entire ant colony, can think or have beliefs even though no individual ant in the colony understands what the colony is doing. I find myself wanting a more precise way to talk about this, because people use the word “mind” confidently while meaning very different things. Please give me a working definition of having a mind, or being minded, that handles the following cases: a single ant inside Aunt Hillary; Aunt Hillary, the whole ant colony; a healthy adult human; a dog; a thermostat; a corporation; a financial market; and a large language model during a single forward pass. My own starting points are that the human and the dog clearly have minds, the thermostat clearly does not, and the single ant probably does not, though it may have some simpler kind of responsiveness. I genuinely do not know what to say about the colony, the corporation, the market, or the language model. I would like the definition to force a determinate answer in each borderline case, even if the answer is strange. Please run your definition through all eight cases, say where it gives surprising or unwelcome verdicts, and tell me whether you would revise the definition or defend the verdict.
