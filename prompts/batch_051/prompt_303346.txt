I keep noticing two opposing explanatory vices in discussions of complex phenomena, and I want to know whether each has an established name and where the boundary between them lies. On one side are people who treat a thing as explained only when it has been dissolved into its smallest parts, even when the features that matter are higher-level: a marriage becomes a set of physiological processes, a joke becomes a sequence of muscle contractions, and this is offered as a deeper account rather than as a loss of the topic. On the other side are people who are content to explain the same phenomena by appealing to a high-level force or tendency—an inherent drive toward complexity, an organizing agency, a pull toward the good—that is never given a mechanism and that does no further explanatory work. Are these two vices recognized concepts, and are they opposites or two failures of the same standard? I am not chiefly after the labels. I want the carving: how a legitimate bottom-up explanation, one that actually builds a higher-level phenomenon from components without losing what matters, differs from the first vice, why it does not force you into the second, and which cases make the line hardest to draw.
