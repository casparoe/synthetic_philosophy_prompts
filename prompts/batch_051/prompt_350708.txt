Could you draft the joint statement that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between two people who have argued for years about whether self-deception is even possible? One side (the paradox camp) thinks strict self-deception is impossible: it would require a person to intentionally get herself to believe something she simultaneously knows is false, and that state is incoherent, so what gets called self-deception is really wishful thinking, changing your mind, or automatic bias. The other side (the bias camp) thinks self-deception is real and is best analyzed as motivated false belief: desires and fears bias how a person searches for, notices, and weighs evidence, so she ends up believing p even though the evidence in front of her, treated straight, supports not-p—with no need for a tidy moment of believing both at once.

Write the document they would both sign. Include a numbered list of claims both camps accept about actual cases—the husband who won't see the affair, the parent who won't accept a diagnosis, the student convinced he aced the exam, and a 'twisted' case like the jealous person who convinces herself of a betrayal she fears—and about what the psychological evidence does and doesn't show. Then a disagreements table: the exact points where they split, each formulated so the relevant side would accept the wording of its own position. Then the cruxes. I suspect the cruxes include at least: (a) whether the disavowed belief is stored and active simultaneously, or is gated or suppressed before it ever gets used, and (b) whether the biased processing is something the person orchestrates intentionally or something that just happens in the cognitive machinery. For each crux, give a concrete observation, experimental manipulation, or testable prediction both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also flag any place where you think there dispute is mostly verbal. Don't pick a winner—make the residual disagreement as exact as possible.
