I'm a PhD student in the philosophy of statistics, and I've somehow ended up on my university's seed-funding committee, which meets Thrusday to vote on a small pilot award. The proposal I've been assigned to write the memo on is from a CS postdoc who wants to build a general-purpose prediction system that makes, in his words, no domain-specific assumptions while matching or beating specialized methods on every nontrivial task; the key idea is a "truly objective universal prior" over hypotheses. Two committee members are ready to fund it. Another keeps muttering that the no-free-lunch theorems already rule this out, but he hasn't given us enough to vote on, and the rest of the committee is just confused. I need to circulate a one-to-two-page written opinion before the meeting with a clear recommendation: fund, decline, or approve a six-month pilot on the condition that the applicant revises the aims in a specified way. The memo should lay out what the no-free-lunch results do and don't imply for a proposal like this, how the proposal connects to the older problem of justifying induction, and whether a universal prior could genuinely get around either. It also has to engage the strongest reason to fund it anyway—probably that even if an assumption-free learner is impossible in principle, chasing one might still produce useful partial methods or clarity about what assumptions are doing—and then say whether that reason actually justifies the money. I'd rather it not just be a list of worries; we need a decision we can vote on.
