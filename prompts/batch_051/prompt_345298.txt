I've spent the last couple of months going round in circles trying to choose between staying in London for my partner's new job and moving back up north to help look after my mum. A friend sent me this argument and says it shows either choice is fine, so I can stop torturing myself. I want to believe him, but I cant shake the feeling there's a trick in it somewhere. The argument runs:

(1) A hard choice, in the interesting sense, is one where neither option is better than the other and they're not equally good either.
(2) If neither option is better and they're not equally good, then the options are incommensurable.
(3) If the options are incommensurable, then reason is silent about which one to choose.
(4) If reason is silent about which one to choose, then either choice is rationally permissible.
(5) So in any hard choice, whichever you choose is rationally permissible.

I'd love (5) to be true, because I'm exhausted by the thought that there's a right answer I'm missing. But the argument feels like it moves too fast. Is it valid? If not, tell me exactly which step fails, and what the corrected version of that step would have to say.
