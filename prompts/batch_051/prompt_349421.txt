I am taking a course on the philosophy of argumentation, and one position we have read defends competitive debate by leaning on a familiar analogy: argumentation is a sport, and making it more sport-like through formal rules, trained judges, rankings, and coaching would improve the quality of reasoning. I want that analogy audited rather than accepted. Treat structured argumentation of the sort found in competitive debate as the target and a formal sport such as tennis as the source. Return the audit as a JSON object with the following fields: correspondences, an array of objects each with sport_element (rules, officials, score, drills, ranked competition, spectators), argumentative_counterpart (format rules, judges, evaluation standards, training exercises, tournament rankings, audiences), and assessment, where assessment is one of strong, strained, or illusory; disanalogies_that_matter, an array of objects each with sport_feature, argumentative_case, and why_it_matters; disanalogies_that_do_not_matter, an array of strings; imported_features, an array of strings naming features the sport source quietly imports into argumentation, such as zero-sum scoring, a single determinate winner, rules that constitute the activity, or spectators who reward performance over correctness; verdict, an object with best_case_for_the_analogy, worst_case_for_the_analogy, and survives, where survives is one of yes, partly, or no; and better_analogy, a string or null naming a more apt comparison if one exists. The governing question: does the conclusion that making argumentation more sport-like would improve its quality survive once the correspondences and disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the conclusion?
