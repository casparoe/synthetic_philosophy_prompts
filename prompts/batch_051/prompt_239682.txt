The standard debunking move for "what are the odds?!" anecdotes is to point out that with enough people and enough interactions, mind-blowing coincidences are basically guaranteed — the birthday paradox point, that it would be weirder if none of them ever happened. But nowadays the anecdote you’re hearing usually isn't a random sample of what happened to people; a recommender system fished it out of millions of posted stories precisely because it's the most astonishing one and will keep people engaged. Does the debunking survive this, or does it need a new premise? I don't want a policy answer; I want to know exactly which step of the old argument the algorithmic selection bears on, and whether the repaired version ends up being just the old argument plus an explicit selection effect.
