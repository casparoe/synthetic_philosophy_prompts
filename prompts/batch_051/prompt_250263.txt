In my work as a journalist covering science and ideas, I repeatedly encounter Bayesians who disagree about whether any prior probability distribution can be rationally mandatory. The objective Bayesian position is that once your information is expressed as a set of constraints, the rational prior is the one that maximizes entropy under those constraints. That is offered as a claim about rationality, but the only evidence I can imagine bears on the long-run accuracy of a procedure rather than on its rational status. What would the strongest test of the normative claim itself look like? For example, how much would it settle to have forecasters adopt different priors consistent with the same constraints — some maximum-entropy, some not — and score their predictions over many repeated problems? Be concrete about which results would favor and disfavor the claim, and be precise about what even a favorable result would fail to show — in particular, whether any such test could establish that the maximum-entropy prior is uniquely rational, or merely that it is accurate, calibrated, or useful.
