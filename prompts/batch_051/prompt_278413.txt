You are an expert on the conceptual foundations of algorithmic fairness, able to move between precise mathematical formulations and the normative questions they raise. I want to avoid citations and scorecards; I care about the criteria themselves.

Please write the desiderata for an adequate theory of which fairness conditions to impose on a risk score or binary classifier when the decision procedure applies to two groups with different base rates. By a theory here I mean something that tells us which combinations of fairness criteria are acceptable, how to interpret conflicts among them, and what counts as respecting equality when the groups differ in relevant respects.

Background you should assume: several impossibility results show that popular fairness criteria cannot be jointly satisfied except in degenerate cases. The one I have in mind is the result that, when prevalence differs across groups, a risk score cannot simultaneously be calibrated within each group, have equal false-positive rates across groups, and have equal false-negative rates across groups unless the score is perfect or the base rates are equal. I want the desiderata to be responsive to results of this general kind, not written as if those conflicts did not exist.

Before writing your final list, begin by brainstorming at least eight candidate desiderata; then refine, merge, and discard them, explaining briefly why the ones you dropped are not genuine requirements.

For each desideratum in your final list, give the case or consideration that motivates treating it as a requirement, and say whether you regard it as non-negotiable or as a preference specific to one camp. Then flag any subsets of your desiderata that you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which single requirement does the most work in excluding widely used fairness criteria.

Throughout, separate what is mathematically settled from what is normatively contested, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus view. End with a short note on which part of your list a reasonable expert would most plausibly reject and why.
