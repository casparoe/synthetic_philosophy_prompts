(1) understanding is a matter of degree, not an on/off switch. (2) I'm fine saying a model partially understands a concept when it applies it correctly in cases it wasn't trained on, and I dont think it needs to have a first-person what-its-like for that. (3) I also dont buy "it's just predicting the next token" as an objection, because next-token prediction over that much data is exactly the kind of pressure that would make a system learn a compressed internal model of the world. (4) But I wouldnt call a giant lookup table that returned the right output for every input "understanding," even if it passed all my behavioral tests. (5) I'm also pretty convinced you can't tell from behavior alone whether a model has actually learned the rule for something like addition versus just interpolating between a mountain of memorized near-cases. (6) And I think the stochastic parrots line works best as a warning about how easily we over-attribute, not as a proof that models can't understand anything.
