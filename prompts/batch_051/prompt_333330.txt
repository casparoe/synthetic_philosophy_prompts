I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, writing a dissertation chapter on the computational conditions under which variation matures into creativity. My committee has asked me to submit a short statement of the working assumptions behind the project, so that they can be criticized as a package rather than one by one. I have tried to write them down honestly, and I genuinely do not know whether the set is jointly stable. Here is roughly where I have landed.

1. Creative work is variation on a theme. A creator explores a space of possibilities by adjusting the settings of a large number of locally independent features—knobs—of an existing material, style, schema, procedure, or problem framing. This is the knob-twiddling picture usually associated with Douglas Hofstadter.

2. Each step in a creative process modifies only the values of knobs already present for the creator at the immediately preceding step. There are no jumps to a region of possibility space that shares no dimensions with the creator's current position.

3. The cases we treat as breakthroughs—Schoenberg's departure from tonality, the shift from Newtonian to relativistic mechanics, the emergence of non-Euclidean geometry—are breakthroughs because they change which knobs exist, not merely where old knobs are set. Their significance lies in opening up new dimensions rather than in moving farther along old ones.

4. For any finished product one chooses, there will always be some retrospectively characterized space of parameters over which that product can be represented as the output of knob turning. Consequently, no individual artifact can, by itself, count as a counterexample to the model. I take the model's real content to lie in the specific and falsifiable generalizations it supports about how creators actually behave while working: early exploration of low-cost dimensions, narrowing of search under time pressure, deliberate re-widening after impasse, reuse of earlier partial solutions with mutation, and similar regularities.

5. Creativity is a property of the causal and representational process rather than of the finished artifact. One and the same object can be a mechanical permutation, a copy, a variation, or an original depending on the history that produced it; the object by itself underdetermines which it is.

6. A satisfying theory of creativity should be naturalistically respectable at both ends. It should not posit an unexplained faculty of genius, insight, or inspiration, and its claims should be assessable by ordinary empirical constraints on models of behavior rather than immunized against evidence by ad hoc extensions.

I would be grateful for an audit of this set rather than a defense of any individual item: which commitments are in genuine tension with one another, which apparent tensions dissolve under inspection, how the genuine tensions should be ranked by severity, and what the least costly revision would be in each case and what it would sacrifice. Please write your response in American English.
