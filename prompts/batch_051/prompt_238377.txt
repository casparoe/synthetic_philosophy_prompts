In his *Natural History of Religion*, David Hume does more than give a naturalistic account of religion’s origins. He also describes a recurring dynamic, sometimes called flux and reflux: polytheistic systems tend to elevate one deity above the rest, and this more theistic stage tends to relapse into practical polytheism because the same psychological tendencies that made people anthropomorphize and flatter the gods remain active. Let us call this the Hume oscillator.

Your task is to analyze the conceptual structure of the Hume oscillator. In particular:

Which reading does the text better support, and what kind of evidence would discriminate between them?

3. Identify the least obvious conceptual commitment of the mechanism. In particular, the oscillator assumes that theological beliefs and ritual practices are shaped by the same everyday social capacities that govern flattery and tribute to human superiors. If that assumption were false, where exactly would the mechanism break down, and what would the resulting religious systems look like instead?

The goal is not a historical summary. I want an answer that makes the mechanism precise enough to be testable, while clearly flagging the conceptual ambiguities that currently block such testing.
