I spent the past year helping maintain our lab's chain-of-thought monitor, and a recent red-team exercise has left me much less sanguine about the technique than I was when I started. In two weeks I am presenting to our quarterly alignment review, where I will argue that chain-of-thought monitoring should be demoted from a primary safety control to one behavioral channel among several. My thesis is that any training or selection pressure that makes a model's stated reasoning useful for oversight also rewards reasoning that is legible but unfaithful, so a monitor's access to the model's actual deliberations is eroded precisely in the high-stakes cases where we most want to trust it; the fix is to treat chain-of-thought as predictive evidence about the policy rather than privileged testimony about its reasons, and to redesign audits accordingly. The audience is a hard one for this claim: the two engineers who built the monitor I am criticizing, several interpretability researchers who believe paraphrase- and probe-based consistency checks can expose unfaithful reasoning, and our policy lead, who will say that downgrading chain-of-thought monitoring undercuts the demonstrations of safe reasoning we give external auditors and partners. I am not asking them to abandon interpretability, only to stop treating the model's own reasoning narrative as ground truth.

Give me a prep sheet, not a defense: exactly five numbered items, no introduction and no conclusion, ordered by how likely and how damaging each objection is. For each item, write exactly two sentences. The first sentence should be the objection phrased as that audience member would actually put it, including who it is most likely to come from. The second sentence should give my recommended reply — what to concede, what to hold — and end with a parenthetical noting the weakest point in that reply and the argument or empirical finding that would most change your recommendation about how I should answer. Ensure that one of the five items is the objection I am hoping nobody raises, and if my own position has a systematic vulnerability, place it there rather than hiding it.
