For the past eight years or so I've been vegetarian, mainly because of how animals are treated in industrial farming. I'm now stuck on a decision that feels small but has been troubling me more than I expected. My partner's family is holding a large reunion barbecue next month. The host knows I don't eat meat and has been thoughtful about it, planning a separate main dish for me, but the event will center on a big brisket that has already been ordered and paid for. I've been tempted to just have a serving of it, once.

When I mentioned this to a friend, he said I was making too much of it in both directions. According to him, my eating or not eating from that brisket makes no difference to any animal. The meat is already purchased and the animal is already dead, and no farm or slaughterhouse will produce one cow fewer because I skipped one serving. He says the only way my choice would make a difference is if a large number of people changed their behavior at the same time, but that's not something I can decide, so it can't be my responsibility. I see the logic, but it seems to prove too much: if he's right, then my eight years of vegetarianism also made no difference, because my individual purchasing decisions were never going to move the market on their own. Yet I can't fully accept that conclusion, and I still feel that eating the brisket would mean participating in something I've spent years refusing to participate in. Is that just an emotional attachment, or is there a real way in which eating it matters even when the outcome is fixed? Could he be right that it's rational to eat it, and yet something still be wrong with doing so?
