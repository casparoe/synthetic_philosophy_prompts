I'm taking a metaphysics class this semester and we've been doing grounding and fundamentality, and I keep having this frustrating experience where everyone in the literature seems to be talking past each other in a really specific way. On the one side are people who think reality has a layered structure and that there's a single relation — 'grounding' — that holds between the less fundamental and the more fundamental facts, and that all genuine metaphysical explanation is explanation by grounding. They also usually insist that grounding is well-founded: you can't have a chain of dependence that goes down forever, so eventually you hit fundamental things that don't depend on anything else. On the other side are people who either think 'grounding' is really a grab bag of different dependence relations that don't share a single nature, or who think there's no good reason to assume the chains bottom out, so reality could be infinitely descending with no fundamental level at all.

What's weird to me is that both camps agree on most of the paradigm cases — they'll both say the fact that the statue is made of clay is grounded in facts about the clay particles, or whatever — and they agree on what the disputed formal features are (transitivity, well-foundedness, whether it's one relation or many seperate ones). They just... don't move. My running hypothesis is that this is actually a disagreement about what metaphysical explanation is supposed to do — whether explaining a fact means finding a single common relation that eventually bottoms out, or whether that's just a theoretical aesthetic preference that got dressed up as a discovery about the world. Is my diagnosis right, or is there a real question about how the world is (or a formal question) hiding underneath the stalemate? And if the debate is stalled for the reasons I think, what would progress even look like — would a new distinction help, or is the sensible move to just drop the question?
