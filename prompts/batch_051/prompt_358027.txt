I am an undergraduate currently taking a seminar on the philosophy of art, and I also sit on my university's Student Art Prize Committee. Our next meeting is this Thursday, and I have been asked to circulate a short written opinion in advance. The committee is split, and I need a draft I can revise and send around.

This year's dispute concerns a series of six digital images submitted by a student who used a generative text-to-image model. The student did not simply enter prompts and submit whatever came out. Over several weeks, they wrote and revised prompts, generated hundreds of candidate images, selected compositions, blended elements from different outputs, corrected details by painting over them digitally, and fine-tuned the model on a small set of their own drawings. The prize rules require that an entry be an original work created by the submitting student. Some committee members argue that what the student did is continuous with accepted tools like Photoshop or darkroom printing, and that the exercise of selection, judgment, and revision is itself creative. Others argue that the model contributes the bulk of the pictorial content and was trained on the work of uncredited artists, so treating the result as the student's original work would stretch the rule until it is meaningless. The seminar I am taking has been discussing whether creativity lies in the originating intention, in the act of selecting and shaping, or in the audience's uptake, and the committee's disagreement seems to track those positions.

Please draft the opinion I can circulate before Thursday. It should recommend a concrete decision: admit the submission, reject it, or admit it only under revised conditions. It should spell out the view of creative authorship the committee would be committing itself to, apply that view to this case rather than merely restating intuitions, and take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side. It should also say how the eligibility rule ought to be interpreted in future cycles, so the committee is not forced to relitigate the same issue every year.
