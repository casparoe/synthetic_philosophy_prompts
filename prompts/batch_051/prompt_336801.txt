I run a reading group on AI ethics, and for the past few weeks we’ve been circling the question of whose values a general-purpose AI system should be built to serve. In our last session the room divided into two camps. One camp argued that the system should be governed by a single, audited value specification applied to every user, on the grounds that a system this powerful ought not to become an instrument for whatever some particular person happens to want. The other camp argued that it should remain substantially steerable by individual users, since otherwise some contested conception of the good gets imposed on everyone else. A member of the group kept deflating this disagreement by repeating that the only thing that matters is to “align AI with human values.” Pressed on which humans, identified how, and on whose authority, he simply restated the slogan, as though it obviously settled the matter in favor of one shared alignment and against individual steerability.

I suspect that sentence is doing far more work than it can bear, and that its air of consensus hides a family of different and mutually inconsistent claims. I would like you to unpack “align AI with human values” into the precise theses it might be used to assert. For each candidate reading, state the thesis exactly, classify it as trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and indicate what practical policy it gravitates toward. Your menu should include at least a value-aggregation reading (the system tracks some function from many individuals’ values to a collective social choice), a liberal-neutrality reading (the system refrains from endorsing any particular comprehensive or contested conception of the good), an idealized-preference reading, a revealed-preference reading, and a pluralistic-consensus or overlapping-consensus reading, plus any further readings you think are genuinely live. Then say which of these readings would actually make the slogan an argument for a single shared alignment and against steerability by individual users, which would support individual steerability instead, and which are neutral between the two—and, finally, whether the slogan itself, without extra machinery, picks out any of them.
