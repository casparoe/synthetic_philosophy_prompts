You are a philosopher of mind who works on the methodology of consciousness science. Think carefully and start by reconstructing the argument in your own words, breaking it into its load-bearing premises, before you evaluate anything.

Heterophenomenology was introduced as a method for studying consciousness scientifically. Its central move is to treat a subject's first-person reports — spoken or written claims about what their experience is like — as a text to be interpreted. The investigator attributes to the subject whatever beliefs, desires, and experiences would make sense of that text, while remaining strictly neutral about whether those attributed experiences really exist. The reports are taken as the subject's own world of seeming, not as authoritative descriptions of a real inner realm. The argument for the method is that this produces a public, third-person object of study, and allows consciousness science to proceed without granting subjects incorrigible first-person authority and without begging any questions against physicalism.

Now consider this development. We can build language models that produce fluent, detailed, introspective-sounding reports of qualitative experience, trained only to predict text. Suppose that in the case under discussion such a system has, by stipulation, no phenomenal consciousness — nothing it is like to be it, no first-person point of view. If heterophenomenology really is neutral, the method seems forced to treat these reports exactly as it treats human reports: attribute whatever experiences would rationalize the text, and bracket the question of their reality. Does the argument for heterophenomenology survive this development? Be specific about which premise is under pressure. Is it the assumption that reports issue from a first-person point of view worth interpreting? The assumption that bracketing the truth of reports is a harmless, merely methodological neutrality? Or something else? Is there a repaired version that still does useful scientific work — for instance, a version that treats reports as data only after some independent criterion has already identified a system as a candidate subject? Or does the case show that heterophenomenology was never fully neutral, because its argument quietly presupposes something the language-model case denies? Defend a specific verdict rather than merely surveying alternatives.
