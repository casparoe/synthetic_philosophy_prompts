i have a distiction I keep using and want someone to pressure-test it honestly. i call it 'local surprise' vs 'global surprise.' local surprise is when one specific expectation misses while the rest of the model stays usable — you reach for your keys, theyre not there, and you update to 'keys are on the table,' not to 'pockets are unreliable.' global surprise is when several independent expectations fail together, or one outcome undercuts the whole framework, and the model itself stops feeling usable for a while. my claim is that this is a difference in kind, not degree: local surprise makes you tighten attention and raise precision on the narrow thing that went wrong, while global surprise flips you into a low-precision, everything-uncertain mode where you re-open basically all priors.
