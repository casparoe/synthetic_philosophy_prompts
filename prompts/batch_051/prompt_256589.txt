I work in a transitional justice NGO and we're constantly being asked to turn the South African amnesty model into a reusable procedure for countries considering a truth commission. The Promotion of National Unity and Reconciliation Act says amnesty can be granted where there is "full disclosure of all the relevant facts" about acts "associated with a political objective." That sounds like a procedure, but nobody where I work can actually state the steps: what counts as full disclosure, what counts as a political objective, what you do when an applicant discloses some things but withholds others, and where the procedure stops being rule-like and becomes a judgment call by a commissioner.

Please write me a decision procedure a commission staffer could run on an individual amnesty application. The staffer is not a philosopher or a lawyer, has maybe an hour per file, and the procedure needs to say: the questions to ask in order, what a sufficient answer to each looks like, where the file gets transferred to a lawyer or a commissioner, and where the test is no longer mechanical and the staffer should be honest that a value decision is being made. Then run the procedure on three hypotheticals: a soldier who discloses his role in a bombing but not who gave the order because he fears retaliation; a local youth leader who burned a suspected collaborator's shop and claims it furthered the struggle but cannot tie it to any organisation's campaign; and a middle-ranking officer who gives a full, verifiable account of an abduction but denies the victim was tortured despite medical evidence. I want the answer to flag, at the end, what the South African model cannot deliver even when the procedure runs perfectly.
