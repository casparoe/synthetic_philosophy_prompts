I've come to accept a fairly strong version of the free energy principle — not just that perception is predictive coding, but that any system that persists in a changing environment can be described as minimizing variational free energy, and that this is the best general answer to why brains are built the way they are. Part of what sold me is the way this gets applied to psychosis: delusions and hallucinations described as the same prediction-error machinery with the precision weights set wrong. What would actually count against this package? Lay out the possible defeaters and rank them from decisive to merely awkward — formal results, empirical findings, arguments, and the things that have arguably already been shown. I also want an honest answer to whether the view has been insulated by being stated so generally that it doesn't risk anything, and if so whether that's a problem for a theory of this kind.
