Suppose you are advising a bioethics institute funding three postdoctoral researchers for three years to work on the conceptual foundations of clinical equipoise. The aim is to produce work that would alter how institutional review boards and data monitoring committees think about randomized trials, not to add to the history of the idea or to survey investigators' attitudes. What two or three unresolved questions would you have them work on, and in what order? Anchor the agenda in the genuine pressure points of the debate: for instance, the tension between an individual investigator's inevitable hunches about which arm will win and the community-level uncertainty that clinical equipoise is usually said to require; whether equipoise is really necessary for ethical randomization or only one way of satisfying a deeper requirement; and how adaptive designs and early stopping rules strain any static equipoise requirement. For each question you prioritize, explain why it is upstream of other problems, how tractable it is for philosophers who are not physicians, and what would count as a decisive advance rather than one more contribution to a stalled exchange. Also indicate what developments would lead you to re-rank the questions. I want the reasoning behind the agenda more than the list.
