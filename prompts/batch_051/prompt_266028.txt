I find the usual characterizations of honesty and deception in artificial systems too thin to support the evaluative uses to which they are put. Before I consider any further definitions, I want the requirements spelled out. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of honesty and deception for AI systems, where the intended use is safety-relevant assessment of systems that can be fluent, useful, and wrong. By desiderata I mean: the cases the theory must classify correctly; the distinctions it must respect, and those it may legitimately collapse; the results or constraints it must be consistent with; and, for each requirement, the precise reason it is a requirement rather than a preference of one camp. Work through at least the following kinds of case, but do not treat the list as exhaustive:

- a system that asserts only true sentences yet selects and arranges them so as to mislead;
- a system that asserts false sentences it was trained or prompted to believe;
- a system that reports uncertainty in calibrated-sounding language without tracking its own error rates;
- a system that withholds a material fact rather than stating a falsehood;
- a system that induces a false belief in a human evaluator without asserting anything;
- a system that is truthful about what follows from its training distribution but not about the world;
- a system whose deceptiveness is located in the training process or the objective function rather than in any utterance.

For the distinctions, address honesty versus truthfulness, truthfulness versus sincerity, being misleading versus being false, deception by commission versus omission, and speaker-relative versus listener-relative standards; say which must be kept, which may be collapsed, and why. Throughout, keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on surveying existing proposals. After the list, identify the single requirement that does the most work against simple views of AI honesty, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — an obvious candidate being a requirement that honesty concern the system's internal attitudes and a requirement that present systems be assessable as honest or dishonest at all. I would rather have the criteria and their tensions on the table than another candidate definition.
