Last night I spent three hours arguing with a friend about whether Gödel's modal version of the ontological argument works, and we ended up stuck between two diagnoses: either the logic breaks at a specific step, or the logic is fine and one of the premises is doing all the heavy lifting. I wrote out the reconstruction below after our argument, and I suspect I may have tacitly helped the argument in one of the premises while leaving another one undersupported. I would like you to go through it step by step.

Here is the argument as I reconstructed it.

(1) Some properties are "positive" in the sense of being pure perfections: roughly, it is intrinsically better to have such a property than to lack it. Assume for now that this notion is coherent enough to reason with, even if "positive" is not fully defined.

(2) Necessary existence — existing in every possible world — is a positive property. I will abbreviate this property as N.

(3) A being is God-like if and only if it has every positive property.

(4) Any positive property is at least possibly instantiated: there is a possible world in which something has it. A perfection that nothing could have would not be a perfection.

(5) Therefore, the property of being God-like is at least possibly instantiated: there is a possible world containing a God-like being.

(6) Suppose a God-like being exists in some possible world w. By (3) it has every positive property in w, and by (2) N is positive, so it has N in w.

(7) To have N in w is to exist in every possible world. I will also assume that whatever is God-like in one world is God-like in every world in which it exists; a God-like being could not have existed in a diminished or merely finite form.

(8) So, if a God-like being exists in any possible world, it exists in every possible world as God-like. Hence it is possible that it is necessary that a God-like being exists.

(9) By the modal axiom S5 — if it is possible that something is necessary, then it is necessary — it is necessary that a God-like being exists. Therefore a God-like being exists in the actual world.

My question is this. Is the argument valid as stated? If it is valid, or can be made valid by supplying suppressed premises, then state which premises you regard as weakest, describe the strongest repaired version of the argument, and say whether that repaired version succeeds on your view. I am less interested in a bare yes or no than in the precise location of the disagreement, so be explicit about the modal principles being used. Please answer in American English.
