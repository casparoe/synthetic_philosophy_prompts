I keep going back and forth on whether dreams are experiences. The Malcolm/Dennett line — that there's nothing it's like to dream while your asleep, and what we call the dream gets confabulated or backfilled at the moment of waking — makes a certain amount of sense, especially given how an alarm clock can get stitched into a dream as the climax of a story that supposedly had been building for minutes. But then lucid dreamers can move their eyes in a prearranged pattern to signal, from inside REM sleep, that they're dreaming right now, and that seems like real-time evidence that something experiential is going on. So I want you to actually design the test. Take the claim that dreams are experiences occurring during sleep rather than stories manufactured at waking, and operationalize it as hard as you can. Give me the exact setup, the result that would count for each side, and then be honest about what the test would and would not settle. In particular, if a sleeping subject executes a prearranged signal, does that show they're having an experience, or could it be a kind of consciousnessless execution of a trained pattern? And is there any observation that could rule out the cassette/backfill story for good, or does some version of it always survive no matter what the sleep-lab equipment shows?
