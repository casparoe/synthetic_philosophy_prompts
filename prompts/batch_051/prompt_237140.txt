In The English Constitution, Walter Bagehot writes:

“The best reason why Monarchy is a strong government is, that it is an intelligible government. The mass of mankind understand it, and they hardly anywhere in the world understand any other. It is often said that men are ruled by their imaginations; but it would be truer to say they are governed by the weakness of their imaginations. The nature of a constitution, the action of an assembly, the play of parties, the unseen formation of a guiding opinion, are complex facts, difficult to know and easy to mistake. But the action of a single will, the fiat of a single mind, are easy ideas: anybody can make them out, and no one can ever forget them.”

I have been developing an account I call the “attentional subsidy” theory of constitutional monarchy. It starts from Bagehot’s premise but turns it on its head: the Crown’s real contribution in a democracy is not to make the state intelligible as a whole; it is to be the part of the state that absorbs the public’s demand for a single, intelligible authority, so that Cabinet government can remain a dispersed, committee-like system without constantly failing the intelligibility test. On my view, the monarch acts as a standing simplification—emotional, visible, easy to imagine as the source of authority—while the efficient parts operate through coalitions, civil service bargaining, and trade-offs that could never satisfy the public’s longing for a single will. This differs from Bagehot because I hold that the monarchy’s function is not to explain the state but to divert an expectation the state cannot meet.

I would like an honest assessment. Is this merely a more cynical restatement of Bagehot’s argument, or does it identify a different mechanism? Does my theory imply something false—for example, that constitutional monarchies with a more remote or less personalized ceremonial style, such as Sweden’s or Japan’s, should be less effective at absorbing this expectation than Britain’s? And what is the strongest case against treating a constitutional monarch as a kind of deliberate cognitive scapegoat for the complexity of democratic government?
