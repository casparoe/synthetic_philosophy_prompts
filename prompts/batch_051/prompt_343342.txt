I've spent way too long reading rationalist blogs and arguing in forums about whether LLMs "really" have beliefs, and I've landed on a Dennett-flavored intentional stance view. I want you to map out what would count against it.

The view: when I say a model believes Paris is the capital of France, or that it "wants" to answer the question, I'm not saying there's any Cartesian theater, inner glow, or quale of believing inside the weights. But I'm also not saying it's just a metaphor or loose talk. The attribution is doing real predictive work: the system is predictively readable as an agent with that belief, and that's the same thing that makes it true that a human believes Paris is the capital of France. The difference between a thermostat "wanting" the room warm and a human wanting coffee is only a difference in the richness and robustness of the pattern, not a difference in kind. So belief-talk about AIs isn't a stopgap until some more mechanistic-honest vocabulary arrives; for high-level prediction, the intentional stance isn't a fake version of a real thing, it's the real thing operating at its proper level.

What would have to be true for me to be wrong? I want an honest map of the view's exposure, not a review essay. In particular:

- sort candidate defeaters by decisiveness: ones that would make me abandon the view entirely, ones that would force a patch or a narrowing, ones that are merely awkward;
- say what the view predicts that the main rivals don't (strong realism about internal LLM belief states, eliminativism about beliefs altogether, pure instrumentalism where attributions are fictional conveniences);
- include what defenders of the view would themselves concede counts against it, not just what critics say;
- wether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a bug or a feature here.

The thing I'd personally find most persuasive is a clean mechanistic story that fully replaces belief-talk for a system where it currently seems necessary, without just re-describing the same high-level pattern in lower-level vocabulary. But don't overweight my preference; I want the whole map.
