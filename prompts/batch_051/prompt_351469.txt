ok so I'm a high schooler and this might sound half-baked, but I keep seeing a move in arguments and I dont have a word for it. People will say something like 'what would you want if you knew everything' or 'what would the best version of you want.' At first I thought that was one trick, but now I think it's two tricks smushed together. One version keeps you as the same person with the same values, and just fills in all the facts and does the thinking perfectly. The other version imagines a wiser, more mature you whose values might be different, not just better informed. And these can point in opposite directions in a real case I'm living through. My grandpa has early dementia and years ago he signed a paper saying no feeding tube if he got bad. But now he seems content and says he wants to live. If you ask what he'd want if he were fully informed about his current condition, it feels like one answer; if you ask what the wiser long-run version of him would settle on after reflecting for a long time, it could be another. My mom does a version of the same thing to me too: she says 'you'll want to go to college when you're older,' but sometimes she means 'once you have more information' and sometimes 'once you've become a different, more responsible person,' and I cant tell if those are the same prediction or not. So my question is: does this distinction between adding facts and changing values in these what-you'd-really-want thought experiments have a name? And is there a name for the overall problem of which version of a person counts? If there's no standard name, what would you call the two moves? I want boundaries more than a label: where exactly does 'more information' turn into 'different person,' and can that line even be drawn cleanly.
