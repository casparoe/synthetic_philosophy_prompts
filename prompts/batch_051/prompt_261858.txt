I sit on the welfare subcomittee of an aquaculture certification body, and we're revising the crustacean chapter of our farm standard. The nastiest open item is eyestalk ablation in prawn hatcheries: crushing or slicing off one eyestalk of a female broodstock prawn to trigger ovarian maturation and spawning. It's cheap, reliable, and near-universal, and producers say there's no proven commercial alternative that works across all the species we cover. But if decapod crustaceans have even a low chance of feeling pain, we'd be certifying a fairly serious mutilation as "responsible" — and that's hard to square with the rest of the standard. We need to decide whether to ban the practice outright, phase it out by a fixed date, or keep it with conditions. Draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting: one clear recommendation with a timeline, a real description of what the conditions or phase-out would require on farms if you go that route, and a serious treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side rather than just restating the sentience debate.
