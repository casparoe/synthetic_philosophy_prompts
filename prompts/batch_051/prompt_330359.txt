I've been working through the demarcation problem, and I find myself stuck on what it implies for the study of history rather than natural science. As I understand it, Laudan and others have made a strong case that every proposed criterion separating science from pseudoscience either excludes some legitimate science or admits some established pseudoscience, so the search for a single general criterion looks hopeless. But if that is right, I don't see how scholars can continue to describe things like Fomenko's New Chronology, claims about a historically real Atlantis, or ancient-aliens accounts of prehistory as pseudohistory. Those classifications seem to lean on standards—internal coherence, consilience with the broader evidentiary record, openness to correction—that look like demarcation criteria under another name. Is the real lesson that there are no general standards here, and that we should simply assess each historical claim one at a time? Or does "pseudohistory" earn its keep in a way that "pseudoscience" cannot? If the question as posed rests on a false presupposition, please say so directly rather than trying to salvage my framing.
