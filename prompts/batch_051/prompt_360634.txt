Here's a thing I've been mulling over with a friend who just read L.A. Paul on transformative experience. Her claim, roughly, is that for some big choices — becoming a parent, moving permanently to another country, marrying, converting — you literally cannot know what you're deciding, because the experience changes the values you'd use to assess it. Not just "you don't have the information," but "the person who would have the information isn't around yet to do the choosing." Fine. Now the counterfactual.

Suppose that sometime in the next decade, a safe, cheap, one-night procedure — call it a preview — becomes available in wealthy countries. It lets a person fully live through the subjective experience of a candidate transformative state before deciding on the real thing. Eight hours of being a parent, or living in the new country, or being married, with the emotions, the boredom, the texture, all of it. When you wake up you remember everything, but you're otherwise unchanged: no obligations, no physical changes, and your pre-preview values snap back intact. So the epistemic gap Paul worries about can be closed for anything a person chooses to preview; what remains is only the permanence and the actual identity-change of doing the thing for real.

Predict, with real reasoning, how decision-making around parenthood, migration, conversion, surgery, transition, or whatever case you want to use would change if previews like this were widely available over the course of a generation. Would people make "better" choices, and by whose standard — the person before, the person after, or some third-person measure like regret? What would happen to regret, to commitment (if you know exactly what you're signing up for, does commitment get easier or does it loose something?), and to how institutions treat informed consent? Does the preview itself introduce new distortions — overconfidence, an experience you haven't actually built up to, changing what you want rather than revealing it? Be explicit about which predicted effects are robust and which are fragile guesses, and for each major prediction say what background assumption about identity, preference formation, or the role of imagination in deliberation it stands or falls with. And one last thing: where would you expect the preview to help least, despite closing the epistemic gap?
