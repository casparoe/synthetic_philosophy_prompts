Turing's proposal was to build a child machine—a system with a child's learning capacity—and educate it rather than program an adult mind all at once. A colleague claims the word education is not decorative there: an educated system differs from a merely trained one, and the difference should show up in behavior—for instance, in how it handles problems that share only abstract structure with anything in its curriculum, or in whether it can be brought to see a flaw in its own reasoning without being given yet more examples. I suspect education is just a warmer name for a well-chosen training schedule. How would one test whether a learning machine has been educated in anything like Turing's sense, rather than merely trained?
