I am a member of the amnesty committee of my country's truth commission, and we must rule this week on the application of a former intelligence officer who has acknowledged his role in a program of enforced disappearances. Under our governing statute, amnesty is available to perpetrators who make full disclosure of crimes committed with a political objective, and it says nothing about remorse. The applicant has provided a detailed, independently corroborated account of his own actions, but he concluded his testimony by stating that the disappearances were a justified response to an armed insurgency. Write the short memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete decision on the application, give the reasoning behind it, and address the strongest consideration on the losing side—in particular, whether an amnesty bargain like ours is best understood as a tool for extracting truth or as an act meant to advance reconciliation.
