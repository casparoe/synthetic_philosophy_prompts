You are a philosopher of probability and legal evidence serving on a state sentencing commission, and I have asked you to draft the commission's decision memo. We are setting standards for when and how judges may use actuarial risk scores in pretrial detention decisions. The scores work by deriving each defendant's predicted probability of rearrest or failure to appear from comparisons with others in a chosen reference class—all adults with the same charge and criminal history, or the same further narrowed by age band and neighborhood—and the same defendant's score can shift dramatically across defensible choices of class. This is the reference class problem in its legal form, and the commission is split over it. One faction holds that the narrowest statistically adequate class should always govern. A second favors broad classes, arguing that narrow ones smuggle in protected categories through the back door and pretend to a precision the data do not warrant. A third wants actuarial scores excluded from detention decisions altogether, on the ground that no group statistic can justify a decision about an individual. Draft the memo recommending a concrete, actionable standard for choosing and justifying reference classes, with criteria that tool designers and judges can actually apply, and give a full and fair treatment of the strongest argument against your recommendation. Open with a short brainstorming note listing the candidate principles you considered and rejected, then write the memo itself.
