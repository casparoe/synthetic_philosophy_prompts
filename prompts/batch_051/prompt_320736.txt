I have been working through C. S. Peirce's writings in a seminar on American pragmatism, and I have come to hold a fairly strong version of the pragmatic maxim. As I understand it, the maxim says that the whole meaning of a conception is exhausted by the conceivable practical consequences of its object: what would follow for deliberate conduct, expectation, or observation if the object conceived were real or the proposition believed were true. If two concepts or two claims would make exactly the same conceivable practical difference, then there is no further content to distinguish them, and any residual supposed difference is only a difference in words. I want to treat this not just as a device for dissolving idle disputes, but as a complete account of conceptual content.

I want to know what would count against this view, and I want the counter-considerations ranked rather than merely listed. The obvious pressure comes from concepts whose connection to practical consequences seems remote: pure mathematics, unobservable theoretical posits, dispositional properties that never manifest, aesthetic and moral terms, and concepts with indefinitely many or open-ended consequences. There are also structural objections worth considering: that the maxim must tacitly rely on a notion of conceivable consequence whose own meaning it cannot supply without circularity or regress; that it collapses into verificationism or operationalism and inherits their known failures; and that it cannot account for the difference between a consequence being conceivable and its actually occurring. I would also like you to address whether the maxim, as sophisticated defenders now qualify it, has been insulated to the point of unfalsifiability, and whether that is a serious defect or something a pragmatist can accept without embarrassment. I suspect I am most likely to be moved by cases where the maxim plainly misdescribes two concepts as identical or as meaninglessly different, rather than by purely abstract accusations of circularity.

Please begin with a one-sentence bottom line stating how exposed you judge the pragmatic maxim to be as a complete theory of conceptual content. Then work through the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing those a committed pragmatist would concede from those they would dismiss, and close by addressing the unfalsifiability worry directly. Keep the entire response under 700 words.
