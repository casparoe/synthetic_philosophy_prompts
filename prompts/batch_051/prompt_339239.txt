I keep noticing a structural likeness between a debate about machine translation and an episode in comparative psychology, and I want to know whether the likeness is deep or whether I am pattern-matching.

The first case is the debate over machine translation—roughly, whether systems that produce fluent and often adequate translations actually understand the source and target texts, or merely exploit distributional regularities absorbed from enormous corpora; this is a worry associated in particular with Douglas Hofstadter's writings about translation. The second case is Clever Hans, the horse who seemed to solve arithmetic problems by tapping a hoof until investigation showed that he was responding to subtle, largely involuntary cues from the people posing the problems—small shifts of posture and movement that told him when to stop—and that his performance collapsed when those present did not know the answer themselves.

In both cases, performance that is supposed to display a capacity turns out to be compatible with the absence of that capacity, because the performer can pass the test by tracking side-channel regularities in the testing situation rather than exercising the capacity itself. The mapping I have in mind is roughly this: Hans's hoof-tapping answers correspond to the MT system's output sentences; arithmetic competence corresponds to semantic understanding; the questioner's involuntary cues correspond to the distributional regularities of the training corpus; the human audience corresponds to the evaluator who judges fluency and adequacy; and the controlled testing that exposed Hans corresponds, perhaps, to adversarial or challenge translation sets that depart from the training distribution.

Please give the strongest, most precise version of this analogy you can, and then stress-test it. In particular: (i) make the mapping explicit, including what plays the role of the target capacity, the observable performance, the side-channel cue, and the experimental control on each side; (ii) say what would have to be true of both cases for a result in one to transfer to the other; (iii) identify at least one prediction the analogy makes about the machine translation case that is false or would badly mislead an evaluation of translation systems; and (iv) give a final verdict on whether treating statistical MT as a modern Clever Hans is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Along the way, address the most obvious disanalogy: Hans was an individual animal performing for an audience in a live interaction, while a translation system is a model trained on a static corpus and then run without an audience. Say whether that difference is essential or incidental.
