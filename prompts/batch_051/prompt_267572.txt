I cover science and ideas for a living, which usually lets me referee other people's disagreements from a comfortable distance. This one is closer to home. My sister and her husband are deciding whether to have a third child, and what began as a practical conversation has hardened into a philosophical standoff. He argues that the family's attention, money, and patience are finite; a third child would most likely be happy, but everyone else would be a little more tired, a little less closely parented, and a little less financially cushioned. On the whole, he says, the family would be worse off by the standard that actually matters: the average wellbeing of its members. Decisions like this, he insists, should be made for the people who already exist. She replies that he is keeping the wrong ledger. A family, she says, is not some fixed stock of wellbeing to be divided among however many people happen to be around; adding a child who will have a good life adds to the whole, and she finds it perverse to decline another loved and loving person just because the rest of them might have to settle for slightly less. Each can reduce the other to a position neither wants to own. He says her logic implies you should keep having children until everyone in the household is only just getting by. She says his logic implies that a small, unusually contented family is more valuable than a large, merely happy one, and that the happiness of a second, fifth, or tenth child would have to count for almost nothing.

I thought I could settle it from my own reporting, but the same structure keeps turning up there. When I write about housing, migration, or the long-term size of the human population, one side talks as if what matters is the average condition of the people already in a place, while the other talks as if what matters is the total number of people who get to live decently. Before I weigh in at the next family dinner, I want to know whether there is a principled answer here or just an ambiguity in what "better off" means. Are my sister and her husband making different moral claims, or are they actually talking past each other? And does it matter that the third child does not exist yet and cannot be consulted about the tradeoff?
