I'm on the board of a small lakeshore association—44 cabins sharing a boat launch, a beach, and a septic pump-out schedule. We have plenty of rules: quiet hours, guest limits, who can use the launch when, who owes what for the pump-outs. What we don't have is any way to enforce them. The board can send letters, but a handful of repeat offenders ignore them, and several members have stopped paying the pump-out fee altogether. This summer a couple of neighbors tried confronting people directly, and it nearly turned into a fistfight at the launch. We're not at each other's throats yet, but every month the rules feel more optional.

The proposal on Saturday's agenda is to scrap the do-it-ourselves model and hire a professional property manager on a three-year contract. He'd set and collect fees, issue fines, revoke beach and launch keys, and his enforcement decisions would not be appealable to the board. Karen, who put the proposal together, keeps saying we're in a textbook state of nature and that only a single authority with real teeth can pull us out. The opponents say we'd be paying someone to boss us around, theres no reason to trust a hired manager more than one another, and we should instead sign a mutual enforcement pact: every member pledges to confront rule-breakers and to refuse launch access to anyone who hasn't paid.

I need to send the board a short written opinion before the vote, and I honestly don't know where I land. Please draft the memo for me. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a timetable, and it should engage seriously with the strongest objection to whatever you recommend. End with a line that begins 'Recommendation:' and gives the bottom line in at most five words.
