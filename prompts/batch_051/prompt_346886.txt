I work as a software engineer, and over the past year I've been reading Hume on causation and some of the surrounding secondary debate — I gather the interpretive dispute goes by the name of the New Hume — mostly because causal inference keeps coming up in incident analysis. I've written down the position I seem to have ended up with, and I would like a consistency check before I present it to a reading group. Since I have no formal philosophy training, please make the audit precise: identify exactly which pairs of commitments pull against each other, rather than giving a diffuse list of worries.

The commitments are these.

1. Causation just is constant conjunction. For a particular event to cause another is for the two to be instances of event types that are constantly conjoined; there is no further productive fact in the world over and above the regular succession.

2. We never perceive any necessary connection between cause and effect. The felt necessity is the mind's contribution — a projection of the habit of expectation — and nothing in the world corresponds to it.

3. Causal claims are nonetheless objective. Whether "the failed deploy caused the outage" is true does not depend on what anyone expects or feels.

4. Part of what a causal claim asserts, and not merely a further inference one happens to draw, is counterfactual dependence: had the cause not occurred, the effect would not have occurred.

5. I also judge that overdetermination can be genuine causation. Suppose two independent faults each trip the same circuit breaker at the same instant, and each would have tripped it on its own; then both faults caused the breaker to open, even though, for each fault taken by itself, the breaker would still have opened had that fault not occurred.

6. Science has no need for powers, dispositions, or objective necessary connections; actual regularities suffice for explanation and prediction.

7. On the textual question, I lean toward the New Hume reading over the purely regularity reading. I read Hume as allowing that there may be real but hidden causal powers in the objects — the observed regularities being only their outward face — while denying that we can ever observe them or form a clear conception of them.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Finally, say which single revision would do the most cleanup. I would rather have a precise diagnosis than a long list of possibilities.
