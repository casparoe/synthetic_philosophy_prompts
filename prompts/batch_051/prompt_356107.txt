I am a third-year student in a law and technology course, and we have been discussing how territorial jurisdiction strains when speech travels across borders. As a thought experiment: suppose that starting tomorrow, every state gains the ability to enforce its own online content rules—defamation, hate speech, privacy, sedition, blasphemy, and the like—perfectly and costlessly against any platform, host, or individual whose content is viewable from its territory, regardless of where the speaker or the servers are located. Assume the enforcement is complete, so no platform can evade a state's law while that state's residents can still access the content. Predict, with reasoning, how the internet is governed and used ten years later. I want the mechanisms made explicit: which states' norms become the effective default for platforms; whether speech fragments along national lines or converges on a single standard; how small states and authoritarian states fare; and whether ordinary users end up with more or less room to speak. Where possible, say which predictions you are most confident in, which ones hinge on contestable assumptions, and what would have to be true for the more pessimistic predictions to fail.
