My political theory professor summarized Rawls on envy like this: “Rawls thinks envy is basically a vice—wanting what others have and being pained or hostile because they have it—but he allows that when a society’s inequalities get large enough, the envy of the worse-off is excusable. In fact, at that point envy functions as a signal that the inequalities are unjust, so it can legitimately figure in moral criticism of institutions. That’s also why he wants the difference principle: it keeps the gap between rich and poor small enough that people don’t fall into this destructive but justified envy.” Something about this feels too tidy, but the professor is a Rawls person and I assume its roughly right. I’ve heard Rawls actually separates envy from resentment pretty sharply, and that he takes resentment to be the moral sentiment while envy is not. If that’s right, does this summary still hold together? Go through it and tell me which claims are accurate, which are subtly off, and whether the part about the difference principle being there to manage envy is something Rawls would accept.
