I'm on the governing board of my kid's elementary school and we're about to vote on a discipline overhaul our new head of school is pushing, and I've got a feeling its either going to be really good or quietly blow up and I can't tell which. You're a moral psychologist who works on shame, guilt, and institutional design. Think hard before you answer, and start by brainstorming the different actors and incentives at play here.

The proposal: for any incident above "minor" (hitting, stealing, targeted cruelty), the student has to stand up at the all-school community meeting and read aloud a short account of what they did and who it hurt. The student's name plus a one-line description of the offense goes in the Friday newsletter that every family receives. Crucially, the mark isn't permanent: the student can "wash the slate" by making public amends agreed to by the harmed kid and at least two classmates, after which the record is destroyed and nobody, teachers included, is allowed to bring it up again. A kid who chooses not to wash has the record kept in their file, and after three unwashed offenses they get moved onto the old private "reflection and detention" track. Parents can opt their kid into the private track in advance, but that opt-out is announced in the newsletter too.

The head of school says this intentionally pushes the community from guilt-style discipline (private, internal, you know what you did) to shame-style discipline (public, reputational, the community sees what you did), and that the washing step avoids the usual objection that shame is a permanent stain. I doubt shame actually cooperates with wash steps that cleanly, and I suspect the kids will just treat the whole thing as a performance.

So: how does this actually play out? What do the kids, parents, and teachers do, who games it and how, what breaks first, and which failure modes can be fixed without turning it back into plain private detentions? And the thing I most want to know: what does the school look like in five years if this passes?
