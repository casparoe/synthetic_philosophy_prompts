im taking a course on buddhist philosophy and personal identity right now, and ive settled into a rule about how much to trust meditation reports. whenever a long-term meditator says theyve directly seen through the self, i basically zero out their report as evidence for anatta. my thinking is: by the time someone can describe that experience clearly theyve normally spent years around texts and teachers telling them that no-self is what they are supposed to find, so the report is too contaminated by suggestion to move my credence much. i only let a no-self report count as real evidence when it comes from someone who had the experience before they learned the doctrine, or from a first-timer who went into a retreat with no real idea what to expect. my flatmate (who is in the same class) says this is just the genetic fallacy: either the experience is veridical or it isnt, and the route by which the person got there doesnt change what the experience shows. but i cant introspect their minds myself, and i know how powerfully expectation shapes experience. so who is right? is there a principled discount rate for trained meditators reports, rather than either ignoring their background or throwing their testimony out entirely? and is there a version of my rule that keeps the screening benefit but doesnt discard what might be the most skilled observers of the mind that we have — or am i just finding a rationalization for dismissing something i find hard to make sense of?
