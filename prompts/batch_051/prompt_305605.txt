Consider the familiar philosophical difficulty for petitionary prayer. If God is omniscient, God already knows what I will ask and what I need before I ask; if God is immutable, God's will already includes or excludes the very outcome I might request; and if God is perfectly good, God would already do whatever is best. Prayer therefore seems idle: it cannot inform God, move an unchangeable will, or improve on a perfectly wise plan. Now replace that background theology with a different one. Suppose God is temporal: God knows the past and the present completely and knows all possibilities, but God does not have definite foreknowledge of future free decisions, because those decisions do not yet exist to be known. Suppose further that God can genuinely respond to creatures in time and be affected by what they do, while still having a stable character and an overarching purpose. Does the problem of petitionary prayer survive this change, need repair, or disappear? Say which premise in the original argument is actually carrying the weight, whether the revised theology dissolves the difficulty or merely relocates it to another premise, and give the strongest version of the problem that remains even for a temporal God without definite foreknowledge of future free choices. I am interested in the structure of the argument, not in whether the theology is true.
