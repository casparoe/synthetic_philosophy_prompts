Ok here's the situation. I'm on the label committee for a new permanent exhibit at a public history museum — working title "Hunger: Famines in the Modern World" — and it has three main cases: the Irish famine of the 1840s, the Bengal famine of 1943, and the Great Leap Forward famine in China at the end of the 1950s. We're down to the wire and the introductory wall panel is the sticking point.

The draft that came to us says, in effect, that famines are natural disasters — crop failure, blight, drought — made worse by bad luck. A faction on the committee (including the board chair) wants to keep that because they think its neutral and doesn't politicize the exhibit. Another faction wants the intro panel to say flatly that modern famines are political crimes. Their evidence: Ireland kept exporting food during the potato blight under British rule; Sen showed that Bengal in 1943 didn't have an unusual food-availability shortfall, and what pushed food out of reach was wartime inflation and hoarding; and the Great Leap Forward famine happened without any comparable crop catastrophe at all, driven by grain-procurement policy.

I've been asked to draft the committee's decision memo. It needs an actual bottom line: what should the intro panel say and how should the three cases be described (a sentence or two each is fine), plus the reasoning. I also need you to steelman the losing side — if we go with "political crime," the strongest case for keeping "natural disaster" language should be stated in a way my colleagues on the other side would recognize as fair, not a caricature. And one thread I want handled explicitly: does calling a famine a crime commit us to saying something about intent or identifiable villains in each case, or can something be political without a smoking gun? The Great Leap Forward fits the crime label much more cleanly than Ireland does, and I don't want the memo to paper over that.

Please structure it with these headed sections, in this order, roughly:

- Bottom line (100 words): what we should do, concretely, and on what timeline.
- Background (75 words): the dispute in two or three sentences.
- The case for keeping "natural disaster" framing (150 words).
- The case for "political crime" framing (150 words).
- Where I come down and why (250 words): settle the intent/blame thread here.
- Proposed label copy (150 words): the intro sentence plus the three case descriptions.
