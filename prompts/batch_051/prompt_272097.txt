I am a novelist planning a book about how lives unravel through individually reasonable choices. I'd like you to write a short story that dramatizes the self-torturer thought experiment in a realistic, non-electrical setting of your own invention. Preserve the strict structure. A protagonist faces a sequence of steps. At each step, the change from the previous step is too small to matter on its own — imperceptible, or nearly so — and accepting it brings some modest compensation or advance. But the steps are ordered so that, taken one at a time, they end in a condition the protagonist would have rejected outright if it had been offered to him as a single choice. The story must show, rather than argue, two readings: first, that the protagonist is a rational agent whose preferences happen to be structured intransitively — each adjacent state is genuinely preferred, yet the whole path is not; second, that he is self-deceiving, or weak, and that the real failure is his refusal to view the sequence as a single act. Let neither reading obviously win, and do not resolve the paradox; the discomfort should survive the last sentence.

Please write in two titled sections: Story (roughly 700–1,000 words), then Commentary on the choices made (roughly 250–350 words). In the commentary, identify which of the two readings the narration finally tilted toward, and explain which craft choice — point of view, tense, the size or nature of the increments, the compensation, or the moment the protagonist notices — did the most to create that tilt.
