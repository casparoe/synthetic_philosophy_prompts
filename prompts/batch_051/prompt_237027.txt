I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I have spent the term trying to get clear on what is actually at stake in the classic future-contingents debate and its familiar sea-battle case: whether the difficulty lies in the world's being open, or only in our talk about an open world. I find that my own view changes depending on how I frame the question, and I want to force my uncertainty into the open by assigning numbers to it.

Here is the thesis for which I want your credence. (T) Suppose that at noon on Monday someone utters, "There will be a sea battle tomorrow," and suppose that at noon on Monday the laws together with the complete state of the world leave both a Tuesday sea battle and no Tuesday sea battle open, in the sense that neither is already settled by the present facts. T says: the utterance already has a determinate truth-value at noon—it is already either true or false—and this by itself does not make the battle's occurrence or non-occurrence inevitable. Do not answer merely that it depends on one's semantics. I want your overall probability for T, stated as a single number. Then decompose that probability into the sub-questions your credence actually rides on, giving a rough probability and a sentence or two of justification for each. In particular, I expect you to address at least the following: (a) whether ordinary future-tensed assertions of this kind are bivalent; (b) whether, if they are, truth at a time requires some present fact that settles the matter; (c) whether the air of fatalism around the sea battle is best explained by the absence of a truth-value or by an illicit slide from truth to necessity; and (d) whether our forward-looking reluctance to say the sentence is true is a semantic fact or a pragmatic/epistemic fact about what we are in a position to assert. If you need to precisify T before assigning a number, state the precisification briefly and then proceed. Show how the sub-probabilities combine to yield your overall number, and tell me which single sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to and what argument or case would most move that sub-question. Finally, I place myself at around 0.3 and a colleague I respect at around 0.8; say which sub-question most likely accounts for the gap between us.
