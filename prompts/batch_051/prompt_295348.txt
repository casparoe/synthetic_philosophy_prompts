In Book V of the Consolation of Philosophy, Philosophy first states the prisoner's problem pretty starkly: God sees everything in advance and cannot be wrong, so whatever he foresees must happen, which seems to mean human choice isn't real. Later, after she introduces the idea that God's knowing isn't spread across past, present, and future but is a single simultanous present, she appears to take it back: future events aren't made necessary by God's knowing them, because God doesn't know them as future at all but as present. Which is it? Are these two passages in tension, and if so can they be reconciled? Give the best readings on which they don't conflict (different senses of necessity, knowledge vs. causation, the shift from our temporal standpoint to God's), and then say which reading is most honest to the text and which ones are special pleading. Don't just fall back on the claim that foreknowledge doesn't cause the event—say exactly what work the eternity doctrine is doing and whether it solves the problem or just relocates it.
