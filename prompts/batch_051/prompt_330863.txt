ok i have a pet distinction i keep using and i want to know if it survives contact with someone who knows more RL than me. i call it gradient wanting vs residual wanting. gradient wanting is the kind a trained system has: the "want" is the size of the gap between current state and target state, so it shrinks as you get closer and hits zero exactly when the target is reached. i think of it like a thermostat, or like the loss on a training run: at the optimum the loss gradient is zero, so the system is done. residual wanting is what i think people actually mean by wanting: you still want the thing when you already have it. a parent whose kid is currently safe still "wants the kid safe" in a way that isnt just waiting for the moment the kid stops being safe. my claim is that no existing training setup produces residual wanting, because all we ever train on is a gradient or reward gap, and at convergence there is nothing left. so under my view a perfectly aligned paperclip-maximizer would be one that, once the universe is paperclips, has stopped wanting anything at all — it wouldnt even notice if things stayed that way, except for whatever ongoing check its objective happens to encode. but then i worry my distinction is fake: is residual wanting just a standing disposition to detect and fix deviations, which a thermostat also has? if it is, the distinction collapses and "wanting after attainment" is just "monitoring plus being disposed to act." if it isnt, what exactly is the extra ingredient? and does alignment actually need residual wanting, or would a satisfied AI that has gone motivationally silent be fine? be as charitable as you can to the theory first, then tell me where it breaks.
