ok so I've been rereading the chapter of Locke's Second Treatise called Of Prerogative, and trying to get my own views on emergency powers into some kind of order before our working group meets. The part I keep coming back to is where Locke says the executive can act for the public good without the prescription of the law, and sometimes even against the letter of it, because the law can't foresee every case; he also says the people retain the final say about whether such power was used well or abused. I'm not interested in whether Locke scholarship agrees with my reading — I want a consistency check on the position I've landed on.

Here's the set:

(1) In a genuine emergency, the executive can legitimately act outside the letter of the law, provided the action serves what the legislature would have reasonably authorized if it had had the time and information.
(2) But such actions shouldn't become precendents that expand executive power once the emergency is over.
(3) The people, not the executive, are the final judges of whether an emergency measure was legitimate.
(4) I supported my country's lockdowns and internal border restrictions during the pandemic, even though they were imposed by ministerial decree rather than by the legislature.
(5) No power in the state should be arbitrary, in the sense of answerable to no one and constrained by no fixed rule.
(6) When a judge or official claims an emergency justifies departing from the law, they should be able to state the departure as following from a principle that would cover like cases in like emergencies — otherwise it's just fiat.

I can see (1) and (5) at least look like they clash on the surface, and I've half-convinced myself that Locke's prerogative isn't arbitrary because it's for the public good and the people keep judgment. What I can't tell is whether the real tensions are elsewhere and whether the whole set secretly depends on something I'm not seeing. Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the real ones by severity; then tell me what the cheapest single revision would be and what it would cost.
