In class we did Locke's memory criterion of personal identity and Reid's brave officer, and I cant stop thinking about it. Locke says you're the same person as some past person just in case your present consciousness reaches back and includes that person's thoughts and actions. Reid's counterexample is that an old general remembers being a brave officer, the officer remembers being the schoolboy who got flogged, but the general doesn't remember the boy — so if identity = memory, identity stops being transitive, and identity has to be transitive. Now suppose we can record, back up, edit, and copy episodic memories, uploading one person's memories into another brain or restoring forgotten ones. Does Locke's criterion survive that? You could fill in the brave-officer gaps and make the chain continuous, but if memories are copyable files then two people could each be the same past person. I want to know what happens to the argument itself: which premise gives way, and is there a repaired version that keeps memory central without making identity duplicable? I don't care whether the tech is realistic.
