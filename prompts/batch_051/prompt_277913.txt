I am a PhD student in philosophy of probability, working on anthropic reasoning, and a difficulty that recurs is whether the Doomsday Argument survives a development its original formulations did not anticipate. In the form associated with Carter and Leslie, the argument treats my rank among all humans who will ever have lived as a random draw. If 'doom soon' means total human births will number in the hundreds of billions while 'doom late' means many trillions, then my finding myself around the sixty-billionth birth is vastly more likely under doom soon, and I should update sharply toward expecting near-term extinction. The inference depends on a reference class of biological human births and on a future that contains many more instances of the same kind.

Suppose, as now seems plausible, that our civilization begins producing vast numbers of artificial observers — whole-brain emulations or digital minds — none of them biological births but many of them candidates for the same reference class, or for the class of observers whose total number fixes the sampling distribution. If such observers eventually number in the trillions or more, the argument behaves strangely. With a reference class of all observers, I am a wildly atypical early biological human under doom-late-with-machines, and the birth-rank reasoning no longer isolates biological extinction. With the reference class restricted to biological humans, the arithmetic survives, but 'doom' would then mean only the end of the biological era, which could coincide with a flourishing post-biological future rather than catastrophe.

I want to know exactly which premise the new development bears on: the self-sampling assumption itself, the choice of reference class, or the assumption that the future is a larger version of the past. Is there a repaired Doomsday Argument that says something weaker but still interesting, and if so, what does it predict that the old version did not? If the argument is dead rather than repairable, I would like it shown why the standard formulation cannot be salvaged. I am asking about the structure of the argument rather than for a forecast or a literature review.
