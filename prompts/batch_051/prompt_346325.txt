After a long argument with a friend about whether the mind is anything over and above the brain, I realized the disagreement rested on an asymmetry we never examined. We would both find it comical if a visitor to Oxford, having been shown the colleges, the libraries, and the administrative offices, asked to be shown the University itself; we would say there is no further thing, for the University just is those parts and their arrangement. Yet we both found it entirely natural to speak of the mind as something behind the workings of the brain, or of a person as a body plus something else that does the thinking. The two claims look structurally similar, but ordinary judgment treats the first as an obvious mistake and the second as common sense. Is anything actually justifying that asymmetry? I want to know whether the second claim is a category mistake of the same kind as the first, or whether there is a principled difference—some feature of mental life with no analogue in the case of an institution—that keeps them apart. Give me the strongest version of that difference, test it against the Oxford case, and say honestly whether the asymmetry survives or should be given up.
