I chair the Campus Transit Oversight Board, which must decide this week whether one of the university's two shuttle contractors is responsible for a serious pedestrian injury and, if so, what consequence follows. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Friday's meeting.

Facts: On a recent weekday evening at about 9 p.m., a student was struck from behind on the East Campus Loop by a university-branded shuttle that did not stop. The student cannot identify the specific vehicle; no camera captured the collision; and no witness recorded an operator name or vehicle number. The loop is served by two private contractors: Crimson Transit and Sagecoach. The university's route logs, whose accuracy no one disputes, show that during that hour Crimson Transit operates 82% of the shuttles on the loop and Sagecoach operates the remaining 18%. No other evidence has emerged that would tie the collision to one contractor rather than the other.

Our regulations provide that a contractor may be formally found responsible and subjected to contract penalties only if the Board is convinced on the evidence that it is more likely than not that the contractor's vehicle caused the harm. The injured student's family argues that the 82% figure plainly meets this standard: on the evidence, it is more than four times as likely that a Crimson shuttle hit the student than a Sagecoach shuttle, and refusing to find Crimson responsible would leave a known probable wrongdoer without consequences. Crimson responds that no testimony, telemetry, physical trace, or driver account connects its particular vehicles to the collision, and that punishing a specific contractor solely on the strength of a fleet-wide percentage would be an unacceptable use of purely statistical inference.

The Board is split. One faction would find Crimson responsible because the probability clearly exceeds the stated threshold; another would dismiss the claim because the evidence does not individuate any Crimson vehicle; and a third suggests a conditional response, such as requiring both contractors to contribute to a victim-compensation fund in proportion to their route share without making a formal finding of responsibility. Draft the written decision: recommend a concrete disposition and the reasoning for it, include an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the side you recommend against, and address the question of whether the Board should adopt a general rule about findings based solely on statistical route shares.
