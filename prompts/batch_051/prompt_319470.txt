ive been going back and forth with a friend about whether a system that just manipulates tokens could ever actually mean anything by them, and I wound up in Stevan Harnad's paper "The Symbol Grounding Problem" (Physica D, 1990). The bit that keeps nagging me is near the beginning, where he's separating real rule-following from rules an outside observer can project onto anything:

"It is not enough, for example, for a phenomenon to be interpretable as rule-governed, for just about anything can be interpreted as rule-governed. A thermostat may be interpreted as following the rule: Turn on the furnace if the temperature goes below 70 degrees and turn it off if it goes above 70 degrees, yet nowhere in the thermostat is that rule explicitly represented."

The way I read it, that's not just a point about thermostats: it's a warning that an account of grounding which helps itself to an external interpreter's gloss is solving the wrong problem. So before I read any more specific proposals (Harnad's own hybrid iconic/categorical story, sensorimotor or roboticist accounts, the usual Searle-flavored complaints), I want the full spec of what a theory of symbol grounding actually has to deliver. Write the desiderata: exactly five requirements that any adequate theory has to meet, with the case or consideration that forces each one, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied and why. Keep each item to a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion, and don't score existing theories against the list.
