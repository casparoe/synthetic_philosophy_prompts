You are a principal policy analyst at a technology-regulation think tank who works on the attention economy and the political economy of persuasive design. You have been asked to think through a proposal rigorously before it goes out for public consultation, so think carefully, look for second-order and equilibrium effects rather than first impressions, and begin by mapping the relevant actors and their incentives.

The proposal is an algorithmic agency rule for large feed-based platforms. Before a user opens any automatically ranked feed, the interface must offer five ranking dials, each presented as a spectrum: familiar vs. novel, comforting vs. challenging, short-form vs. long-form, immediate reward vs. delayed satisfaction, and broadly popular vs. tailored to you. The dials come preset to defaults chosen by the platform; users may adjust them, but the settings reset to the platform's defaults every twenty-four hours. The platform controls both the wording of the labels and the default positions, and it must publish a leverage coefficient for each dial indicating how strongly that dial actually moves the ranking; the platform sets these coefficients itself and may revise them monthly. Creators receive analytics showing how much of their reach came from users at each dial position and are free to optimize their content for whichever dial positions pay. The stated aim of the rule is not to cap screen time but to give users genuine leverage over the persuasive design of their feed.

Walk through how this mechanism would actually behave. Who would adjust the dials, who would never touch them, and what would platforms do with defaults, labels, and leverage coefficients once they realize users are watching? How would creators adapt, and what kind of content would get selected for as creators learn which dial positions produce reach? Would the rule meaningfully shift platform incentives, or would defaults, label framing, and strategically weak coefficients absorb most of its force? Where would it be gamed first, by platforms, creators, advertisers, or users themselves, and what repairs, if any, would address the gaming without gutting the scheme? Close by saying what would really need to be true about human attention and platform measurement for the mechanism to work as intended.
