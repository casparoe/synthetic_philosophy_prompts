I'm teaching a unit on Dennett's four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — and I want to use Kubrick's 2001 as a running example instead of just diagrams. Read the film as a ladder of minds: the man-apes at the start look mostly Skinnerian, learning by trial and error against the actual world; the bone-tool scene looks like a leap to something Gregorian, since the solution gets externalized into a tool and handed on; and HAL seems like a Popperian creature whose inner model of the mission has gone wrong in a way that gets people killed. The usual line that the monolith just upgrades them each time is too tidy. Where does the film actually fit Dennett's ladder, and where does it resist or flatten the distinctions? I'm hoping for a real critical reading that says which scenes do the philosophical work, which distinctions the film gets out of its depth on — e.g. is HAL really Popperian, or just a Skinnerian system with a lot of pre-wired competence — and what the starchild could even be on this taxonomy.
