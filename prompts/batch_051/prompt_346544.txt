Here's a conceptual claim I want to pressure-test. On a Strawsonian picture, moral responsibility is not something we first detect and then react to; rather, treating someone as responsible — resenting them, feeling indignation, being grateful — is what makes responsibility attribution possible, and the reactive attitudes are in some sense constitutive of holding responsible. The rival view says the attitudes are just the emotional echo of an independent judgment: you first judge that the person's action showed ill will (or whatever it is that makes them responsible) and then, downstream, you feel resentment.

How would one test which of these is closer to what people actually do? I want a concrete empirical design: what you'd manipulate, what you'd measure, and crucially how you'd dissociate the two things, because in normal cases the cues for responsbility — intentionality, ill will, control — and the reactive emotional response move together. What pattern of results would favor the constitutive reading, and what would favor the "attitudes as echo" reading? Then say what even a clean result would fail to settle about the conceptual question of what responsibility is, where the experiment runs out and the philosophical work still has to be done.
