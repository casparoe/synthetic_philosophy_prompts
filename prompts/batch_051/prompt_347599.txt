I'm giving a short toast at my older sister's wedding. She's an astrophysicist and her partner is a particle physicist; they met a summer school, and they both hate what they call "cosmic rhetoric"—talk of the stars aligning, the universe wanting this, or love outlasting the heat death of everything makes them cringe. The trouble is that a good half of the room is my very religious extended family, who'll be wanting something about blessing and eternity, and the couple will cringe if I hand them that.

I've been reading Frank Ramsey lately, the Cambridge mathematician and philosopher who argued—roughly—that the stars being big doesn't make them more important, because they can't think or love, and that a human life properly fills the foreground of the picture while the cosmos sits in the background. That's the register I want for the toast: honor the marriage without pretending the universe is on their side, and translate the family's "eternal" into something the two of them can actually mean, without embarrassing the relatives or turning it into a lecture on Ramsey. Please write the toast. Four or five minutes, warm but not saccharine, and please don't lean on direct quotations from him—paraphrase anything you borrow.
