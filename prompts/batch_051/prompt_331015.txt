In my upper-level epistemology seminar we've reached the regress problem, and several students have settled into a tidy two-option picture: foundationalism stops the regress, coherentism loops it, and infinitism is just a formal curiosity that no finite person could instantiate. I want a case that breaks that picture by making infinitism and coherentism agree while foundationalism is the theory left saying the belief is unjustified. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications.

The case should feature a single ordinary person who comes to hold a single ordinary proposition through an everyday practice of inquiry — a medical, engineering, financial, artistic, or similar judgment, nothing exotic, and no philosophical claims. The person's surrounding system of beliefs must be structured so that coherentism and infinitism both count the target belief as justified, while foundationalism does not. The person must remain finite: to satisfy infinitism, it is enough that for any reason offered there is a further, distinct, non-repeating reason available, so the chain is indefinitely extendable rather than actually traversed. To satisfy coherentism, the reasons need genuine weblike reinforcement — explanatory integration and mutual support — not a bare linear chain. And the case should not make foundationalism's failure cheap: do not simply have someone refuse to acknowledge an obvious basic belief; the design should be such that every candidate stopping point can itself reasonably be met with a further reason.

Walk me through the case as a designed object. State the target belief, sketch the first few links in the chain of reasons and at least one cross-support relation, then give each theory's verdict and the rationale it would give. Then identify the single structural feature doing the work and say briefly what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed. End with the strongest foundationalist objection to the case and a self-critical note: which part of your assignment of verdicts is most likely to fail, and what argument or amendment would lead you to change your mind about whether the case actually meets the specifications.
