I'm in a course on the Bible and its translations right now, and I've been trying to get my own position straight before the final paper. Here's roughly where I've landed and I want a consistency audit:

(1) Inspiration attaches to the original texts — the Hebrew/Aramaic of the OT and the Greek of the NT — not to later copies or to any translation.

(2) But I also think the Septuagint, which is a translation, really did function as Scripture for the early church, and the NT writers quote it even where it parts ways with the Hebrew; I don't think that made it defective or second-rate for them.

(3) I'm definately not a KJV-onlyist. No translation gets a special blessing that trumps accuracy, and the Textus Receptus is just one late, fairly messy textual stream.

(4) I hold to verbal inspiration: God's inspiration of the original texts extends to the very words, not just to the doctrine or the gist.

(5) I think a good modern translation (NRSV, ESV, whatever) can be authoritative for teaching and practice in the same way the originals are, because a translation that preserves the meaning carries the same message, and the exact words are just the vehicle for the meaning.

(6) But I also think that only the original-language texts are the final authority for settling doctrinal disagreements: where a translation and the Hebrew or Greek diverge, the original wins.

What I want to know is whether this set hangs together. I'm sure some pairs are in real tension and some just look like they are but dissolve once you clarify the difference. Go through them, separate the real clashes from the fake ones, rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me exactly what I'd have to give up or weaken to fix it. I'd rather get a definite ruling on the cleanest version of my view than a list of options I can choose between.
