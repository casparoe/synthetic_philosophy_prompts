I'm in a philosophy of space and time class and my professor keeps using this loaf-of-bread picture for spacetime. The whole loaf is the universe, every slice is a time, and all the slices exist at once — 'now' is just whichever one you happen to be cutting. Then he leans on relativity of simultaneity: different inertial frames disagree about which events are simultaneous, so events that are in my present are in someone else's past or future. Since no frame is privileged, he says, the future has to be just as real as the past, and the passing of time is basically a story we tell ourselves. He litearlly cites the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for this, and I can't tell whether the bread is doing too much of the work. I'd want an audit: what maps onto what (the slices, the crust, the long axis you cut along, the fact a loaf sits on a counter while you cut it), where the mapping breaks, what the loaf quietly imports that a 4D spacetime might not have, and whether the conclusion — the future is fixed and becoming is an illusion — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole thing. And if the bread is bad, what's a better image?
