I am a novelist, and I serve as the external lay member of the Meridian Foundation's Interdisciplinary Inquiry Fund panel. We are reviewing a funding application from a computational theorist and a new-media artist who propose to build a system that can represent, simulate, and score its own inference rules, and to present the work as an installation arguing that this self-referential capacity is what would turn a formal system into a self-aware mind. Their central claim is that Gödel's construction of a sentence that says of itself that it is not provable is a template for any mind: once a system can encode and evaluate claims about its own reasoning, the resulting loop closes and selfhood emerges. The applicants lean explicitly on the analogy made familiar by Douglas Hofstadter in Gödel, Escher, Bach.

The panel is split. Several members say the proposal confuses a proof-theoretic result with an empirical claim about cognition. They point out that the Gödel sentence depends on a carefully specified coding of syntax into arithmetic and a fixed proof predicate within a system strong enough to represent elementary arithmetic; the proposed software, by contrast, would sit atop neural networks whose inference rules are not statically specified, and would have no clear analogue of the arithmetization. They want to reject the application. Others reply that the panel should not penalize a project for working at the edge of a live metaphor, and that the history of cognitive science contains many cases where formal results seeded genuine empirical programs.

As the panel member responsible for the written recommendation, please draft a short policy memo that commits to a clear decision—fund, reject, or fund with conditions—and provides criteria the panel can actually apply, including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The memo should help us distinguish a productive analogy from a category error, and it should be legible to a novelist while remaining rigorous enough for the panel's formal-minded members.
