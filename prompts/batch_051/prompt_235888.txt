An economist colleague defends a strong monist thesis about value: the apparent multiplicity of human values—friendship, justice, aesthetic delight—is one underlying good wearing local costumes, and what we call conflicts of value are really conflicting beliefs about how to maximize a single currency such as expected well-being. On this view a fully informed agent would eventually see all such trade-offs as matters of calculation rather than contests between irreducibly different goods. I want to know whether this thesis is a testable empirical claim or a terminological choice. Design the best feasible test you can: give a concrete setup and instrumentation, the result that would favor the one-currency reading, the result that would favor genuine plurality, and a precise statement of what even a decisive result would fail to settle about whether our values are one or many. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
