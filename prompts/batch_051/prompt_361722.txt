I co-run a reading group on the philosophy of time, and the presentist and eternalist members have finally stopped talking passed each other enough that we want to write up what a year of argument actually established. Could you draft that document for us? By presentism I mean the view that only the present moment and its contents exist; by eternalism the view that past, present, and future times and their contents all exist equally, with "now" only picking out the speaker's temporal location, much as "here" picks out a spatial one. The two camps have agreed not to make physics do all the work; we know enough special relativity to be dangerous but not enough to be authoritative, so keep appeals to physics to claims both camps here would actually sign.

Structure the deliverable in exactly these sections:

(1) Agreed claims — a numbered list of 8–10 claims (roughly 450–600 words total) that both sides can sign without wincing, covering what the debate is not about, what tensed language does and doesn't commit one to, and what each side concedes to the other.

(2) Disagreements table — at least five rows, with columns: the issue as a neutral outsider would state it; the presentist's preferred exact wording; the eternalist's preferred exact wording. Both wordings should be ones the respective side would happily sign, not caricatures.

(3) Cruxes — the 3–5 sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one side to revise something. I have two candidate cruxes in mind: whether truths about the past (e.g., that the dinosaurs are gone) need truth-makers located in the past, and whether the felt passage of time is better explained by an objective moving spotlight or by a succession of perspectival nows. Feel free to replace or add cruxes if you think others are better.

(4) Crux tests — for each crux, a concrete thought experiment, case, or agreed datum that both sides accept bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance (roughly 400–500 words total).

(5) Verbal vs. substantive — a short note (about 150 words) on which remaining disputes look merely verbal and which are substantive, and why the verbal ones are verbal.
