I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two people who've spent months fighting about whether we're justified in trusting that our basic mathematical axioms are consistent. One is a 'Hilbertian optimist' who thinks PA (and probably ZFC) being consistent is about as certain as anything science gives us, even if Gödel showed there's no absolute proof. The other is an 'inconsistency skeptic' who thinks a century without a contradiction is nowhere near enough evidence, especially after naive set theory blew up with Russell's paradox, and that 'we use it and it works' is a weak reason to trust the foundations. Dont have them debate — produce the document they'd co-sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Structure it as: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, in neutral wording; (2) a disagreements table, with each side's view phrased so that side would own the wording; (3) the cruxes, the sub-questions that would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give at least one concrete item both agree bears on it — a proof, a future discovery, a formal result — and write down what each predicts about it. I suspect the cruxes are something like: (a) whether a century of failing to find a contradiction is strong inductive evidence; (b) whether Gentzen's proof that PA is consistent given induction up to epsilon-nought (ε₀), just beyond what PA itself can formalize, should raise confidence at all; and (c) whether a contradiction would likely be 'local' to the high-up ideal machinery and patchable, or would leak down and poison ordinary arithmetic about finite numbers. You can add or drop cruxes if that makes the statement more honest, but don't pad. Keep the whole document under 700 words, neutral-voiced, the kind of thing either party could circulate without being embarrassed.
