I'm a novelist, and for a book I'm sketching a language spoken on an island where nobody has words like I, you, here, now, this, or tomorrow—not just no pronouns, but no indexicals at all, nothing whose referent shifts with who is speaking, where, or when. People refer to themselves by name or by role ("your aunt says..."), and they locate events by fixed descriptions ("the summer the boats burned") rather than by anchoring them to the moment of speech. A linguist friend says I'm cheating. Her claim is that any language used by actual humans would force indexicals back in, because children would invent them and joint action would fall apart without them. I can't tell if that's a testable claim or just a strong intuition. How would you test wether a human language could really do without indexicals? Design the best test you can, say what result would convince you either way, and be honest about what the test could never settle even in principle.
