I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I work on the classification of falsehoods produced by large language models. The position I have been defending is that ordinary LLM hallucinations are neither lies nor mere errors but bullshit in the sense of Harry Frankfurt's On Bullshit. The passage that anchors my view is the one, toward the end of that essay, where Frankfurt distinguishes the liar from the bullshitter by their relation to truth: his point, as I read it, is that the liar must take account of the truth because he intends to guide his audience away from it, whereas the bullshitter's statements are not constrained by the truth even negatively — truth has no controlling authority over what the bullshitter produces. I want to use that as a diagnostic model for LLM outputs: a language model selects tokens for reasons that are indifferent to whether the resulting sentence is true, so a false output is not a failure of the system's aim, and it is not an attempt to mislead, but simply what the selection process yields when no truth-tracking constraint is operative.

What would count against this? I want a candid map of the position's exposure. Please distinguish considerations that would be fatal — for example, showing the analogy collapses into ordinary error or lying, or showing the Frankfurtian category is conceptually inapplicable to systems without intentions — from considerations that would be merely awkward, such as evidence that some components of a model do track truth locally or that fine-tuned models sometimes seem to weigh factual accuracy. What would a committed defender of the view concede counts against it, and what would they dismiss as attacking a straw version? I would also like you to assess whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability — for instance, by treating every apparent truth-tracking capacity as too marginal to matter — and, if it has, whether that is a problem for a thesis of this kind. I personally think I would be most moved by a demonstration that the training objective of current models instantiates a minimal allegiance to truth, sufficient to make false outputs failures of the system rather than by-products; but I want to know whether that would defeat my Frankfurtian diagnosis or only force me to qualify it. And if the task as posed rests on a category mistake or a confused premise, I would rather you say that directly than try to salvage it politely.
