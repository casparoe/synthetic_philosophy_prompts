ok so for a seminar I have to write a "where I stand on causation" one-pager and I'd rather not hand in something that falls apart under questioning. heres roughly what I believe, with varying confidence:

(1) causation is fundamentally about difference-making: c caused e just in case e wouldn't have happened if c hadn't. I'm in the counterfactual/Lewis ballpark.

(2) I think interventionist accounts (Woodward-style) get something right about how we test causal claims and what makes causal explanations useful: a cause is the sort of thing you could in principle wiggle to change the effect.

(3) preemption cases are real causation. standard case: assassin A shoots first and the victim dies; if A hadn't shot, assassin B would have. the death doesn't counterfactually depend on A's shot, but A's shot still caused it.

(4) omissions and double preventions are causes, not just loose talk. if my plant dies because I didn't water it, the omission caused the death; and in double prevention, the guy who removes the barrier that would have prevented the harm causes the harm.

(5) causation is fully out there in the world, mind-independent. what causes what doesn't depend on us, our interests, or what we find salient.

my worry is that some of these quietly conflict, and probably some of the ones that feel conflict-y are actually fine. could you audit the set: which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look that way, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix would be for each and which single revision would make the whole thing hang together? I mostly want to know exactly what I'd have to give up or complicate if someone pushes on this.
