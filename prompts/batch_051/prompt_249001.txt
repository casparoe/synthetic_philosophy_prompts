Two friends of mine got into an argument the other night and I got volunteered to settle it because I once said I "think about language for fun," which was a mistake. I'll call them Dan and Priya.

It started when Dan idly said "ducks lay eggs" and Priya said that's not really true. Dan asked what she meant. She said most ducks never lay an egg — males don't, ducklings don't, and plenty of females don't in any given season — so as a statement about ducks in general it fails. Dan said it doesn't mean every single duck; it means the species, like "humans have thirty-two teeth" even though some people don't. Priya said that comparison backfires, because "humans have thirty-two teeth" works only because it's true of most humans, while "ducks lay eggs" is not true of most ducks, since only the adult females do it. Dan said he wasn't appealing to a majority; he was saying the sentence is about what ducks as a kind do — the species reproduces by laying eggs. Priya said then "ducks are female" should be true by the same logic, since being female is part of how the species reproduces, but nobody accepts that sentence. Dan said no, laying eggs is something the kind does through its female members, while being female is a state, not a thing the kind does. Priya said that's a plausible-sounding distinction but he just invented it on the spot to keep his sentence true. Then she brought in "mosquitoes carry malaria," which everyone treats as true even though only a tiny fraction of mosquitoes carry it, only females, and only certain species. She said: so truth here can't be about how common the thing is — it has to be about how much it matters, which is exactly Dan's move, but that makes the rule way too permissive. Dan said it's not just that egg-laying matters; it's that laying eggs is how ducks continue as a kind, and malaria is something mosquitoes transmit, so these sentences describe a relation to the kind's lifecycle or to us, not a random property. Priya said that still doesn't explain why "ducks are female" is false, since being female is more central to the lifecycle than laying eggs is.

They asked me to referee and I couldnt do better than shrug. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right about what — not just who wins overall — and say whether "ducks lay eggs" is true, and if so, in what sense?
