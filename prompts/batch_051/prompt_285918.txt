A community organization I belong to — a neighborhood mutual-aid association of roughly 400 households — is debating a scheme, and I have been asked to think through how it would actually run rather than whether it is desirable in principle. The association would issue paper scrip, denominated in half-hours of care work (childcare, eldercare, tutoring, meal preparation), called care-notes. A care-note would come into existence only when the association's labor coordinator certifies that a member has completed one hour of approved care for another member; the worker would receive the note, stamped with its issue date and with an expiry date ninety days later. No one — not the association, not any enrolled business, not the holder — could redeem a care-note for national currency. The association would, however, accept care-notes, and only care-notes, in payment of monthly membership dues. Enrolled local businesses could accept care-notes at whatever rate they chose, but if they did, they could only pass them on to other businesses, to members, or to the association itself; they could not cash out.

What I want to understand is the mechanics of the thing. Walk me through the chain of expectations that would have to hold for a care-note to circulate: who has to believe what about whom, and how far does that chain of reciprocal expectations have to extend before the paper starts functioning as money rather than as a voucher or a private debt? What gives the notes a floor, and what happens to everyone's behavior as the ninety-day expiry approaches? Where does the system get gamed first — padded care logs, collusion with the labor coordinator, businesses that dump near-expiry notes on less sophisticated holders, or an unofficial exchange rate that drifts far from the face value — and are the likely failure modes fixable without destroying the conditions that made the notes circulate at all? Finally, what would the trajectory of this experiment reveal about the difference between a community's converging on something as money and an issuer's simply declaring that something is money?
