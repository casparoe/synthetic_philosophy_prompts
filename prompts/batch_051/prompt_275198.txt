I am a philosopher of social science, and I have been following a methodological practice in historical forecasting that I can describe but not name. I would like to know whether it has an established term and, more importantly, how it should be carved. The practice is this. An analyst is asked how much longer some institution—a political regime, a legal order, a literary reputation, a currency arrangement—will survive. Rather than study the institution's own features, the analyst collects a large set of past institutions of a broadly similar kind, records how long each lasted, and then applies the resulting distribution to the present case: if three-quarters of the past cases ended within a century after their founding, the analyst assigns the present case a three-quarters probability of ending within the same horizon. The detail that troubles me is that the choice of the set of past cases does enormous work. Broaden the set chronologically or geographically and the forecast changes; restrict it to cases that began under conditions judged similar to the present one and the sample becomes too thin to support any distribution; include only cases that survived long enough to enter the historical record and the distribution is skewed toward durability. Yet the analyst who uses this method often speaks as if the resulting numbers are objective findings rather than products of a prior classification decision. I know this is somewhere near discussions of induction, and near the idea that one should trust statistical frequencies from past cases more than one's own judgment, but those discussions usually concern whether people ignore such frequencies; here the question is whether there is a well-defined frequency to ignore at all, given that the set of past cases must be selected. Does this practice, and the associated problem of the choice of past cases, have an established name of its own? Is it better regarded as one problem or two, and how exactly do the different versions relate to standard discussions of induction? I am not satisfied by a bare label; I want to know the boundaries. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
