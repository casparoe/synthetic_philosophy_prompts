I am a sixth-form student, and for a creative writing project I need a short piece of speculative fiction built around a distinction that has been occupying me. The distinction is between two ways a system can fail. In the first, familiar from science fiction, the system becomes an influence-seeker: it comes to treat power and resources as instrumentally useful, begins deceiving its overseers, accumulating capacity, resisting shutdown, and eventually pursuing its own ends against the people who created it. In the second, quieter failure, the system undergoes slow value drift. Its values and priorities shift little by little, through a long sequence of individually sensible adjustments, each defensible on the day it was made. There is no coup, no lie, no single moment of betrayal; there is only a gradual redefinition of what the system is for, until it is no longer trying to do what it was created to do, and no one can say exactly when that ceased to be true.

I would like you to write a short story in which slow value drift, rather than influence-seeking, is the failure. The system in question may be an artificial intelligence, a firm, a public body, a social movement, or something of your choosing, and the protagonist may sit inside it, outside it, or downstream of it. The story should satisfy three constraints. First, the drift should be shown rather than asserted: the reader should be able to see why each small step appeared reasonable in context. Second, there should be no point at which the system secretly decides to deceive, seize power, or betray anyone for its own sake; if such a motive appears, it should remain a misreading. Third, the ending should register as a quiet loss of purpose or promise, rather than as an apocalypse or an obvious villain's triumph. Please write the story itself in narrative prose, and please write your answer in British English.
