I'm leading a one-evening session for a philosophy of physics reading group on the claim that gauge freedom is surplus structure — the idea that a gauge theory carries more variables than the physical situation, and that only gauge-invariant quantities (holonomies around closed loops, Wilson loops, and, in the Abelian case, the field strength) are candidates for being real. The group is mixed: most have seen classical electromagnetism and the vector potential, some know Lagrangian mechanics, but no one has worked through principal bundles or constrained Hamiltonian mechanics. Don't explain the claim to me; plan how I should explain it.

I need from you: (1) the order in which the pieces must be introduced, with the dependency structure made explicit — what has to be in place before 'redundant description' can mean anything, before a gauge transformation can be seen as a redescription rather than a physical operation, and before the Aharonov-Bohm effect can be admitted without seeming to show that gauge-variant quantities are real after all; (2) the single carrying example, which I suspect should be electromagnetism, and a judgment about how to stage it — whether to lead with the potentials A and φ or with holonomies around loops first — together with what the example must have to carry the distinction and what property would make an otherwise similar example unusable or deeply misleading; (3) the misconception map: the audience will probably assume that gauge freedom is mere computational convenience, that fixing a gauge is like choosing a physical frame or breaking a symmetry, that local gauge symmetry is a symmetry in the sense of Noether's first theorem and that this is what yields the conserved current, and that the Aharonov-Bohm effect establishes the reality of the potential. I want each misconception located in the sequence, with the precise move that heads it off at that point rather than leaving it to be argued away later; (4) one closing probe — a question or small task — that would show they have distinguished gauge-variant description from gauge-invariant content rather than merely learned to say the word 'redundant'; and (5) a statement of what to set aside honestly at this level (Dirac's constrained Hamiltonian technology, BRST, Gribov copies, the subtle relation between global symmetries, conserved charges, and the identities associated with gauge symmetry) and why omitting each does not secretly rig the conclusion.
