In our philosophy of psychiatry course we have been working through Ian Hacking on the looping effects of classifications: the claim that when psychiatry introduces a category, those classified respond in ways that can change the category itself. A classmate insists this is a substantive, empirically testable hypothesis about psychiatric kinds, while I suspect it is close to untestable, since we never observe the counterfactual in which the same people meet the same behavior without the label. What would a genuine test of a looping effect look like? For example, could the introduction or removal of a diagnostic category, or cross-cultural comparison between populations with different psychiatric vocabularies, show that a classification changes the people it names and is in turn changed by them, rather than merely tracking changes in diagnostic fashion? Design the strongest feasible test, and be explicit about what a positive or negative result would and would not settle.
