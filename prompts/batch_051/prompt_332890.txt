I sit on the ethics committee of a large academic medical center, writing in part because I have spent years reading rationalist forums and want a recommendation I can defend under cross-examination rather than a values statement everyone can nod along with. Our palliative-care service has asked to run a six-month pilot of a bereavement chatbot. A patient near the end of life may opt in and record voice notes and written reflections; after the patient dies, the family may choose to converse with a language model fine-tuned on those recordings, plus any correspondence the family supplies. The service would be presented as a grief aid rather than a substitute for the deceased, and the bot would be labeled as a simulation at the start of each session. The committee is split roughly down the middle. One camp argues this is continuous with ordinary mourning—talking to the dead at a graveside, rereading letters, listening to old voicemails—and may reduce the risk of prolonged grief. The other camp argues that a graveside monologue and a generated reply are crucially different: the system manufactures the experience of continuing a relationship, responds in ways the deceased never said or endorsed, and may delay the painful adjustment to a world without the person. There is also a quieter worry that even a fully consensual pilot trains families to treat the dead as an inexhaustible data source rather than as someone whose absence matters. I have been asked to circulate a short formal recommendation before next month's meeting: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It should state a concrete course of action with a time frame and safeguards, engage the strongest version of the opposing position rather than dismissing it, and close with a brief note saying which single argument or empirical result would most change the recommendation, and where the argument is least certain. Where a term like 'dignity' or 'authentic grief' is doing work, please spell out the mechanism rather than treating the word as self-explanatory.
