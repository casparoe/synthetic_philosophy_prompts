For a philosophy of mind handout I'm putting together, I'd like a decision tree that sorts people into positions on whether big higher-level structures really cause what their tiny parts do, using this setup as the shared case. A careenium is a sealed, frictionless arena full of little balls (call them simmballs) that click together when they meet the right way; the clusters can grow into spinning wheels and gears, and a big wheel can smash into a lone ball and send it flying, even though underneath it's all just ball-on-ball contact. The question the tree is supposed to sharpen is whether the wheel genuinely makes the lone ball move, or only the particular balls that happen to compose the wheel do, or whether even that much is misleading.

Build the tree from the causal question, not from mind-body labels. Make each branch node a concrete decision about the careenium (is the wheel real? if it is, does it cause anything over and above its balls hitting things? is the coarse-grained causal claim literally true or just useful shorthand?), phrased so a student can answer without knowing which position they'll end up at. The leaves should include eliminativism (there are only simmballs), epiphenomenalism about the macro structures, reductionism, a real-patterns view where the wheel is a real causal pattern but exerts no extra forces, and at least one view on which the wheel pushes the balls around in a way bare microphysics doesn't capture. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate, note which unoccupied combinations of answers are coherent but just unattractive, and give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Aim for six or seven leaves.
