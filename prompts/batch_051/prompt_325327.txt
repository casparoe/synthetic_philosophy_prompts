After a long argument with a friend about whether a mutual acquaintance really understands a position or has only learned to speak fluently around it, I noticed I was defending a policy I apparently hold: I do not count myself as understanding an idea until I can state it precisely enough to implement it, as working code where the idea is computational and otherwise as a formal specification. If I cannot make it executable or formal, I treat my view as provisional and decline to assert it. My friend says this is an engineer's prejudice that mistakes formalization for understanding and quietly rules most of ethics, aesthetics, and first-person experience out of court. Is this policy defensible as an epistemic rule? Where does implementability track understanding, and where do the two come apart? Is there a weaker version worth keeping—for example, treating code as a stress test for confusion rather than as a criterion of understanding? If the whole policy rests on a mistake, tell me that plainly instead of offering a repaired version.
