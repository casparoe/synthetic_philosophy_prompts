I'm compiling a list of loopholes through which a local hidden-variable theory could reproduce the observed violations of Bell inequalities without positing any nonlocal influence. So far I have four: the detection (or fair-sampling) loophole, in which undetected pairs might not be representative of the pairs actually emitted; the locality (or communication) loophole, in which the two measurement wings exchange a subluminal signal before both outcomes are recorded; the memory loophole, in which the hidden variable carries information from earlier trials into the present one; and the coincidence-time loophole, in which the window used to pair detections allows a biased subset of pairs to be counted. Give me one more loophole that is not a rewording, special case, or combination of these four, and that is neither a detection-failure variant, nor a timing or memory effect, nor a mere engineering fix. Say how it differs from the four listed loopholes, whether any experiment could in principle close it, and what it would mean for the interpretation of Bell tests if it cannot be closed.
