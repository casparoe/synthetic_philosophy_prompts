You are a professional philosopher of science with a command of the Popper–Kuhn–Lakatos–Laudan debate, and you are being asked for a number, not a survey. What is your actual credence in the following proposition: within the next half-century, the demarcation problem will come to be treated as dissolved rather than solved — because no criterion of science, including any sophisticated descendant of Popper's falsifiability, will have achieved wide acceptance among philosophers of science and historically informed scientists as a principled and historically adequate way to separate science from pseudoscience. Give one number. Then reconstruct the reasoning: list the sub-questions your estimate depends on, at minimum (i) whether 'science' picks out a natural kind or a shifting cluster concept, (ii) whether the historical counterexamples that undercut past criteria indict the whole demarcation project or only particular formulations of it, (iii) whether the boundary work is better delegated to local epistemic assessment of claims, and (iv) whether a future criterion could succeed where every existing proposal has failed. Assign each a rough probability, show how they combine, and identify the sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Close by stating the single argument or piece of evidence that would move that number the most.
