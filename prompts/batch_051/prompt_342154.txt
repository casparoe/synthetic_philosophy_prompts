I keep coming back to Lakatos's quasi-empirical picture of mathematics — the idea that math grows more like empirical science than like logic: conjectures get floated, proofs are messy and revisable, and counterexamples force people to stretch definitions or patch theorems, rather than knowledge just accumulating from settled axioms. Say I treat that as roughly right, not just as psychology of discovery but as a claim about how mathematical knowledge is actually structured. Give me the unflattering question: what would have to be true for this picture to be wrong or seriously overstated? What would a mathematics that isnt quasi-empirical look like — in how the community treats a counterexample, in the role of formal proof and automated checking, in whether progress is mostly consolidation vs concept-stretching — and which of those differences are live possibilities rather than merely logical?
