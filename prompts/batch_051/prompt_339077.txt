A survey article I had to read for a grad seminar in philosophy of science summarizes structural realism like this: "Structural realism is the view that science only ever discovers the abstract mathematical structure of the world, not the nature of the entities themselves. It was introduced to explain how theories can change radically while still making progress: the equations survive even when the objects they describe do not. This is why structural realists say the world is fundamentally relational, that objects like electrons are just placeholders in a web of relations, and that we should give up the idea that there is anything more to reality than structure." I cant tell whether this is a fair gloss or whether it's quietly sliding from one position into a much stronger one. I've also seen people object that structural realism can't even say what "structure" is without presupposing objects, which makes me think the survey is glossing over a real internal split. How fair is this summary? Go roughly sentence by sentence and say which parts a structural realist would sign off on, which parts run together the epistemic and ontic versions, and whether the last sentence actually follows from the theory-change motivation. What should I trust from this survey?
