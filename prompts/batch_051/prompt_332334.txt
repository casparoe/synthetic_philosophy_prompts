A friend and I spent the evening arguing about whether there is any coherent ethics of voting, and I came away with a pile of gut reactions but no principle that holds them together. He kept pressing a clean line: either a vote matters because it could change the outcome, or it is a purely expressive act, and you cannot have it both ways. I resisted that, but I could not say exactly why.

Here are the verdicts I actually hold. In a national election in a swing district, I think I am right to vote for the mainstream candidate whose platform I mostly oppose, because the alternative is far worse. In a safe district where my preferred minor party cannot win and the mainstream outcome is secure, I would vote for the minor party rather than the mainstream lesser evil, and I think that is not just permissible but better. I also think spoiling a ballot with a protest slogan in that safe district is fine, if a little self-indulgent. But if a friend in a swing district votes minor party knowing it could siphon votes from the acceptable mainstream candidate, I think they are being reckless, even though I would cheer the same vote in my own district. I think not voting at all in a local race because you know nothing about the candidates is fine, even admirable. But not voting at all in a national election in a swing district because you are disgusted with both candidates strikes me as wrong; that is exactly when you hold your nose. I would vote for a personally corrupt candidate with the right policies over an honest candidate with the wrong ones, though it feels dirty. And I think selling your vote is wrong even in an election where your vote is almost certainly not decisive, though I cannot say why if a vote's only value is its expected effect on the outcome.

The case I keep going back and forth on is this: I am in my safe district, but this year the race has tightened and my preferred minor party is still hopeless. Do I switch to the mainstream lesser evil, and if I do, was my minor-party vote last time a mistake? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Then state the principle precisely enough that I can see which clauses are doing which work, say which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and which it would overturn, and say what it predicts for a nearby case I have not listed.
