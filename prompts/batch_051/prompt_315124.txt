I'm on the board of a small housing co-op, and we're in a squabble about our work-share bylaw. It says:

"Each member household shall volunteer at least six hours per calendar month in cooperative work duties. Hours shall be recorded in the monthly work log at the time the work is performed, and each entry shall be initialed by a second member who was present during the shift."

This was written in 2011, when work-share mostly meant painting hallways, shoveling sidewalks, weeding, cleaning the shared kitchen. The problem is that since we moved the work log to a shared online sheet, one member has been logging what they call admin shifts at odd hours—updating the spreadsheet itself, reorganizing the pantry inventory into Notion, setting up an automatic dues reminder, summarizing old board meetings. They are almost always alone at their laptop in the middle of the night. Their partner, also a member but asleep upstairs, initials the entries afterward. They hit exactly 6.2 hours every month like clockwork, while the rest of us are scrubbing the stove and hauling compost. Some members feel this violates the purpose of the bylaw; others say they're doing more useful work than the people who stand around gossiping at a cleanup day.

We're trying to decide two things. First, as a matter of interpreting the rule: does "cooperative work duties" extend to remote administrative work that nobody else observes, and does an asleep partner count as "present during the shift"? Second, it feels like a Goodhart's-law/specification-gaming situation. The hours were meant as a rough proxy for shared physical labor and visible participation in the building, but once the log went digital the metric became the target, and now the easily logged laptop work is crowding out the actual work the bylaw was trying to produce. Is that diagnosis right? And if it is, what's the least stupid way to amend the bylaw so it doesn't just create another gameable metric?
