I am presenting a paper next month at a confessionally Reformed seminary's theology colloquium, defending the thesis that the doctrine of biblical inerrancy requires that the evangelists teach truly in what they affirm, but it does not require that every narrative detail across the four Gospels be reconciled into a single seamless chronological sequence. My key move is to draw on Augustine's Harmony of the Gospels, where he argues that the evangelists, though writing under one Spirit, each shaped their account according to a distinct scope and manner, so that apparent contradictions often arise from treating as an assertion of temporal sequence what the evangelist intended as a thematic or topical arrangement. My audience will be mostly conservative systematic theologians and biblical inerrantists who suspect that any concession about non-chronological arrangement, even if it is presented as a traditional Augustinian move, will weaken the historicity of the resurrection narratives and eventually cede ground to the kind of redaction criticism that treats the Gospels as freely composed fictions. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually get from this audience rather than from secular critics, each phrased the way a confessional theologian would put it, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is, with a two-minute reply to each and a candid note on where my reply is soft. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks, and close by telling me which single concession I should make up front to keep the discussion from turning into a debate about whether I believe the Bible contains errors. Please write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; the whole answer should read like a long email or letter.
