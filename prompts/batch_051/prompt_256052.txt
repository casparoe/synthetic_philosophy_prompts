I've been going around in circles with a friend in a cog-sci lab about the color phi effect, and I'd love a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement we're actually in rather than a verdict on who's right. Here's the setup. You flash a green dot on the left, then very shortly after flash a red dot slightly to the right; with the right timing, people report a single dot gliding across the gap and turning red about halfway along its path. The strange part is the midpoint color change: the later red flash somehow shapes the part of the experience that is reported as happening before the red flash was physically present. There are two standing ways people explain this. On the Orwellian story, your experience was initially of a green dot the whole way, and then the red flash arrives and your memory of the early frames is quietly revised, so you end up with an edited record where the dot changed color midway. On the Stalinesque story, there's no memory revision: your experience of the first flash is simply delayed long enough that the red flash is already in the system before anything becomes conscious, so what you consciously experience is a pre-stitched single dot that changes color midway. The subject's report comes out identical either way.

My hang-up is that I can't see what evidence could possibly separate the two, since any brain measure that seemed to show one could always be re-read by the other side as measuring the revision itself, or the pre-conscious stitching, rather than the experience. My working hypothesis is that the whole debate presupposes something false: that there is a determinate moment and determinate content of experience, which could be either edited after the fact (Orwellian) or staged before it reaches consciousness (Stalinesque). If you drop that presupposition, the two stories collapse into two descriptions of the same process from different angles. But I keep worrying thats just me changing the subject to avoid admitting there's a real empirical question. So diagnose the structure of this stalled debate: empirical, verbal, a disagreement about what experience has to mean, or something else? And be straight with me about what would count as progress — a new experiment, a distinction, a formal way of showing the hypotheses are equivalent, or an honest argument that no progress is possible and why.
