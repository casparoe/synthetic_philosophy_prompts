Here's a position I've settled into and want stress-tested. I'm pretty convinced the Orwellian-vs-Stalinesque debate about the color phi has no fact of the matter. The setup, in case you need it: a red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes a little way off, and people report seeing one dot that moves and switches from red to green mid-path. The Orwellian story says the early red experience got quietly rewritten in memory after the second flash; the Stalinesque story says consciousness was held back until the whole moving-dot episode was assembled. My view is that, once each story is allowed to tune its auxiliary assumptions about memory, timing, and the onset of awareness, they end up making identical predictions, so the question when exactly the moving, changing dot became conscious has no determinate answer. Tell me what would count against that. What experiment—real or merely possible—what independently motivated constraint on consciousness or memory, or what conceptual argument could actually seperate the two families of stories? And if your honest answer is that nothing can, say whether that's a stable result or just a failure of imagination, and whether that much insulation is a problem for a view of this sort. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and don't just hand me the critics' version: say what a principled defender of the no-fact view should admit puts real pressure on it.
