You are a professional philosopher with a solid working knowledge of social-scientific methods, including survey experiments, behavioral choice tasks, and observational inference. Think through the problem slowly, and begin by brainstorming several candidate operationalizations before settling on your strongest proposal.

A colleague defends what they call the self-standing value thesis: a natural language has value over and above the value it has for its users. They clarify the thesis with a benchmark case. Suppose a small language painlessly ceased to be used: every speaker voluntarily shifted to a neighboring language, no one experienced grief, lost identity, or lost access to anything they cared about, and no texts, recordings, or traditions were lost because none existed. My colleague says that even under these no-one-is-worse-off conditions, something of value would have vanished from the world. Moreover, they claim the thesis is not untestable metaphysics: it can be given empirical content.

Your task is to design the strongest test you can of the self-standing value thesis. Be concrete about the comparison, the measure, the intervention or observation, and which pattern of results would favor the self-standing value reading rather than the deflationary reading that the value is always just someone's valuing. Consider at least these candidate approaches, and improve on or reject them where necessary: (a) measuring how much outsiders are willing to pay, or otherwise sacrifice, to keep an undocumented and unloved language alive; (b) choice experiments involving last-speaker or painless-extinction scenarios; (c) comparing people's responses to a natural but obscure language with their responses to an invented language engineered to have the same structural and aesthetic properties but no historical community. Then say exactly what a positive result would and would not establish, and whether the residue no test can reach is a limitation of measurement or a sign that the original claim conflates two different things: the value a language has, and the valuing of a language by someone.
