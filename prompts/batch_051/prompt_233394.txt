I'm collecting objections to negative utilitarianism in its strong, lexical form — the view that no amount of happiness can outweigh any amount of suffering, so reducing or avoiding suffering always takes priority over creating or adding happiness, and only suffering (or its absence) is what ultimately matters. My list so far: (1) the world-exploder objection, that a consistent NU should be willing to painlessly annihilate all sentient life to prevent all future suffering, which can't be right; (2) the pinprick objection, that NU says no amount of happiness can justify causing even one extra pinprick of pain, which rules out trades almost everyone accepts; (3) the antinatalism objection, that since any life you create will contain some suffering, NU seems committed to saying it's wrong to create new lives at all; and (4) the empty-world objection, that NU can't regard a world full of flourishing, mostly happy beings as better than a world with no beings at all. Give me one more objection that is not on this list and not a restatement, special case, or combination of the ones I've got. It would particulary help if the extra objection attacked the underlying value theory or the asymmetry between pain and pleasure, rather than just piling up another bad implication — but if the strongest genuinely new objection is another implication, give me that and say so. Finally, be straight with me: is the extra objection actually any good? If I've mischaracterized one of my four, or if the remaining objections are all going to be variants of these, tell me that plainly instead of forcing a fifth.
