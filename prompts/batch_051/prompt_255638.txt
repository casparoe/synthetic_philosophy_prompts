Two people in my old probability study group spent an evening arguing about a puzzle one of them had read (she called it the Judy Benjamin problem, if that rings a bell), and I wrote down their moves because I want a verdict. A soldier is parachuted into a region divided into four quadrants — Red East, Red West, Blue East, Blue West — each initially equally likely. Her radio message from HQ says: if you are in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that you're in the Western sector. The question is what she should now believe about being in Red territory.

(1) Leo said the answer is boring. The message is a conditional: it only tells her how to redistribute probability inside the Red half. It says nothing about Red versus Blue, so P(Red) stays at 1/2. She should set P(Red West) = 3/8, P(Red East) = 1/8, and P(Blue West) = P(Blue East) = 1/4. Done.

(2) Mira said Leo's move is exactly the mistake. A rational update should change the whole distribution by the smallest amount that accommodates the new constraint — minimize relative entropy to the old uniform distribution. If you do that, the posterior still has P(West | Red) = 3/4, but P(Red) drops to a bit under half, around 0.467. So saying the message "only concerns Red" is wrong in practice: conditional information can shift the antecedent's probability.

(3) Leo came back that Mira had just helped herself to a disputable rule. If we only know that P(West | Red) has to become 3/4, that's one constraint on a four-cell distribution; it leaves almost everything else up for grabs. Keeping P(Red) fixed is one completion, minimizing relative entropy is another, and nothing in the radio message itself picks between them. So the problem is underdetermined.

(4) Mira answered that some principle of minimal change is unavoidable. Leo's "keep P(Red) fixed" is not neutral — it's just a different, unargued completion that treats the old value of P(Red) as sacred for no stated reason. And under the only well-worked-out revision rule we have for constraints that aren't ordinary propositions, P(Red) moves; that's a real discovery about updating on conditionals.
