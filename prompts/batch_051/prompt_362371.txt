My thesis is on the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism, but with a twist: I argue the problem is mislocated. The hard part isn't how the attitude an expressivist associates with a moral judgement composes under negation or the conditional — it's that everyone, including the truth-conditionalist, has to explain what is common between an asserted occurrence of a sentence and its unasserted occurrence in an embedding. I claim the expressivist can answer that question exactly as well as the truth-conditionalist, by treating moral predicates as contributing a constant inferential potential rather than a content, and that the standard embedding data is just what you'd predict once you do that.
