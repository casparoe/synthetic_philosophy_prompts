You are a philosopher who works at the intersection of ancient ethics and contemporary moral psychology, with a particular interest in the emotions and interpersonal normativity. Please think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the strongest objections to the position I am about to describe before settling on your argument; then write a precise, well-structured defense of it.

In his De Beneficiis (popularly known as On Benefits), in the Aubrey Stewart translation, Seneca writes:

"for a benefit is a loan, the repayment of which depends merely upon the good feeling of the debtor. To misuse a benefit like a spendthrift is most shameful, because we do not need our wealth but only our intention to set us free from the obligation of it; for a benefit is repaid by being acknowledged."

I want you to argue for the following position: the essential core of gratitude is not a debt-like liability to repay or reciprocate, but a distinct normative relation — a duty of accurate acknowledgement of the benefactor's good will — and the widespread habit of modeling gratitude on indebtedness systematically distorts what gratitude is. Take Seneca's passage seriously both as a source of insight and as a puzzle: he calls a benefit a "loan," yet he also says that what sets the recipient free is not a transfer of wealth back to the giver but the recipient's intention and acknowledgement. Show how those two thoughts can be held together, and why treating the loan language literally leads us astray; if you think they cannot finally be reconciled, say so and argue for the one that should win. In doing so, distinguish the material benefit from the benefactor's good will as possible objects of gratitude, and defend your view about which is primary. Address the backward-looking character of gratitude — why it answers to the past act rather than functioning simply as an incentive for future generosity — and test your account against two hard cases: the benefactor who helps us only by accident, and the benefactor who helps purely from self-interest. Conclude by stating what, on your view, a grateful person actually owes, and what would count as having discharged it.
