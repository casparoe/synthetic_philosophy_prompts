Write me a deadpan mock advance-directive form — call it a Self-Binding Order or something similar — around 600 to 650 words, in officious legal-medical boilerplate. It should satirise the idea that your earlier self gets to boss around your later self, and the piece should work as a straight-faced reductio: the clauses get steadily more absurd whilst staying inside the logic of precedent autonomy. Include things like a clause fining the later self for preference drift after the form is signed, a section where any present objection by the patient is recorded under 'symptoms' rather than testimony, and a waiver in which the signatory declares that any future version of them who disagrees is a different person and therefore has no standing to revoke. Nod to Ulysses contracts and the then-self/now-self problem, but don't turn it into an essay — the satire has to land through the form itself. The peice should be deadpan throughout, use British spelling, and stay legible enough that a reader who has never heard of advance directives still gets that the joke is about one self legislating for another it cannot know.
