I serve on the acquisitions committee of a mid-sized contemporary art museum. We have no written policy about AI-assisted submissions, but we have made a series of decisions and we want each new decision to be consistent with the principles implicit in the record we have built. The resolved cases are:

1. Studio. The applicant wrote her own generative image model, trained it exclusively on her own painted studies, generated several thousand candidates, selected forty, and made small painted corrections to each. We accepted it as an original work by the applicant.

2. Single Take. The applicant typed one offhand sentence into a commercial text-to-image service, took the first image it returned, and submitted it unmodified. We rejected it, on the ground that no deliberate contribution beyond issuing a command was apparent.

3. Palimpsest. The applicant began from a digital photograph he had taken of a disused cinema, then reworked it through roughly four hundred rounds of masked regeneration, controlling the composition region by region. We accepted it.

4. The Flood. The applicant wrote a program that generated a new composition every ninety seconds and exhibited a month's unedited stream, explicitly refusing to select among its outputs. We rejected it.

The case before us now is The Cull. The applicant used a publicly released image-generation model, produced one thousand images by varying its parameters, and then spent several weeks narrowing them to a final set of nine using a private written protocol; he altered not a single pixel of any selected image. The installation presents only the nine images, arranged in a deliberate sequence.

Should we accept The Cull as an original work by the applicant, if we are to remain consistent with our own record? Identify the closest earlier analog, set out the competing principles that would make us accept or reject, and say what each principle predicts for The Cull. If the record underdetermines the answer, say so explicitly, give your best prediction about the decision that best coheres with our practice, and state the further case that would most cleanly resolve the ambiguity.
