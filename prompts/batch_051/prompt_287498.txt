I've been reading philosophy since I retired, and one thing I keep tripping over is the claim that a lot of social norms are really conventions in Lewis's sense — arbitrary, stable regularities that persist because everyone expects everyone else to go along, and nobody wants to be the sole holdout. In my old life as an experimental scientist I'd have wanted to operationalize that. So take queuing: waiting your turn at a shop or a bus stop. It looks like it has both a fairness face and a coordination face. How would you actually test whether queuing is held up mostly by mutual expectations rather than by people thinking jumping the queue is wrong in itself and would enforce it even if nobody else did? Give me a concrete design (survey vignettes, field experiment, lab game, whatever), say what result would push each way, and be honest about what a clean positive result would still not settle — e.g. whether it shows the rule is arbitrary rather than just that expectations do some of the work.
