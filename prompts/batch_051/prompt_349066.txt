Here's roughly where I've landed in thinking about the architecture of the mind, and I'd like to know whether the set hangs together. (1) I think input systems — early vision, face perception, speech parsing — are modular roughly as Fodor described: domain-specific, fast, mandatory, and most importantly informationally encapsulated. (2) I also think central belief fixation cannot be modular in that sense, because which beliefs bear on which others is isotropic and Quinean; anything is in principle relevant to anything. (3) But I find the massive modularity picture quite persuasive: evolution likely produced a large collection of domain-specific reasoning adaptations, cheater detection being the one that's convinced me most. (4) And I'm committed to the idea that my beliefs remain revisable in light of evidence from any domain whatsoever — that revisability is part of what I mean by rational belief. Does this package cohere? Audit it for me: where are the real tensions, where are there apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and which commitment or commitments would have to change to make the rest consistent, and at what cost? I'd also like you to say where your diagnosis is least robust — what argument or consideration would most change your mind about which bit has to give. Please write in British English.
