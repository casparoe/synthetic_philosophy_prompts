I volunteer with a community group that runs a meals program and a little free legal-clinic help in a mid-size city. A handful of churches supply most of the volunteers and a big chunk of the money, and every few months the same argument comes back around in our group chat and then spills into the meetings. It's been going on for the three years I've been around, and neither side has moved an inch.

The pro-religion side says religious people consistently report giving more to charity and volunteering more hours, churches are the reason our group exists at all, and believing someone is watching (and will judge) keeps people honest in situations where they'd otherwise get away with it. The skeptic side says those surveys count giving to the church itself, it's self-report, and anyway when you zoom out the least religious countries (the Nordic ones, basically) have low corruption, high trust, and low violent crime, so you clearly don't need God to get a cooperative society. They also add that religion mostly redirects generosity toward people who already belong, and can make the us/them thing worse. At this point both sides accept more or less the same numbers, and the argument still doesn't budge.

I've started to think they're not actually answering the same question. One side means something like "are religious individuals more generous and rule-abiding on average," and the other means "is religion needed to produce the kind of society where strangers trust each other." Those could both be true and they'd still be stuck. But maybe I'm papering over a real disagreement, and I cant tell if that's the whole story or if I'm just tired of the argument. So I want a diagnosis, not a verdict: what kind of disagreement is this actually — a word thing, an evidence thing, different bedrock intuitions about human nature, or two different questions wearing one name? And once you've diagnosed it, is there anything that would unstick it, like a sharper distinction, a different kind of evidence, or just accepting that these are two separate questions and changing the topic accordingly? At the very end, give me a last line starting "Summary:" that says your diagnosis in one sentence.
