I serve on the ethics committee of a regional hospice and palliative-care organization. A technology company has proposed a six-month pilot in which bereaved family members would be given access to a chatbot that simulates a deceased loved one, built from texts, emails, videos, and voice recordings the person left behind. The deceased need not have consented; the company maintains that the only person in a position to be harmed is the grieving survivor, and that many people find that interacting with a likeness of the dead supports a continuing bond with them. One faction on the committee sees this as comfort for the living purchased through unconsented use of the deceased person's entire communicative life, and suspects the tool will be tuned to maximize engagement rather than support mourning. Another faction sees it as a natural extension of photographs and saved voicemails. After a long argument with a friend about where exactly the line falls between engaging with a representation of someone and engaging with the person themself, I find the disagreement is still unresolved. Draft the committee's decision memo, recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with reasons the vendor and the losing faction could actually respond to. Include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and write the memo in American English.
