So my friend and I just went back and forth for hours about wether it's rational to treat the consistency of PA—or whatever foundation you like—as having probability exactly one. His position was the usual induction-on-not-breaking: math has been used for centuries, planes fly, bridges stand, so the axioms are fine. My position was that Gödel already showed a system like PA can't certify its own consistency, so any consistency proof has to lean on something outside the system that you're just trusting anyway, and "nothing broke yet" is exactly the kind of evidence that can fool you—if a contradiction showed up, the community might just patch the axioms, quarantine the result, and move on as if no foundational crisis happened. The argument left me wanting to turn this into something more useful. Suppose a small foundation gave me three people for two years, with the goal of making ordinary mathematical practice more robust to the possibility that the basic axioms are inconsistent—not trying to prove or disprove consistency, just figuring out what practice should actually do differently if we took the possibility seriously. Which open questions or lines of work would you prioritize, and in what order? I want the reasoning more than the list: for each, why it's upstream of other work, how tractable it is in two years, and what evidence in that period would move it up or down. For instance, I keep wondering whether some axioms are more suspect than others—induction, replacement, powerset—and whether a contradiction somewhere high-powered would even matter for the finite math that actually gets used in applications, but I don't know how much of that question is well-posed. Last thing: I know parts of this terrain are genuinely contested, so please separate what's settled from what people who know the field still argue about, and flag wherever you're giving me a contested take as if it were the standard one.
