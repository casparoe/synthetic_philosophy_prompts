I write novels and I'm trying too get a definition of "collapse" right for a book where an empire falls apart slowly. The criteria I've been working with are: a big drop in population, loss of writing or record-keeping, a stop to major public building, and the disappearance of the central authority that ran things. Then I started reading about the Late Bronze Age and it doesn't sit cleanly with this. Around 1200 BCE the Mycenaean palaces burn and Linear B just stops, Hattusa is abandoned, Ugarit is destroyed, the long-distance trade network goes quiet. But huge numbers of people obviously survived: they farmed, made pottery, kept speaking an early form of Greek, just without the palaces and scribes; and Egypt and Assyria stagger on. So was that a collapse under my criteria, or are my criteria doing the thing where you mistake "the literate top fell" for "everybody fell"? Give me a committed answer: does the Late Bronze Age qualify as a collapse on those four criteria, and if not, which criterion needs to go or be tightened?
