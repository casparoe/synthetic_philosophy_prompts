Here it is: in a gauge theory, the only physical content is the gauge-invariant content — field strengths, Wilson loops, holonomies, and the like. For instance, people keep saying the Aharonov–Bohm effect shows the potential is physical, since the electron's phase shifts even though the field strength is zero along it's path. But I'd always thought that's just the holonomy, which is gauge-invariant, so it doesn't force me to reify the potential. Does that response hold up, or does the AB effect push harder than I think? Beyond AB, what else should worry me — formal results, other interpretations, or maybe the whole idea that 'real' can be read off gauge invariance at all? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me honestly whether I've built the view so that nothing could count against it, and whether that's a vice here or just what 'surplus structure' means.
