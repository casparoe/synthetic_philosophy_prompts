During revision for my school exams, a disagreement with my study partner has left me puzzled about what it means to know something. A few days ago I was convinced that I did not understand a mathematical derivation we had covered earlier in the year. I told my friend I was completely lost. But when she began asking me questions about it, I found that I could answer them correctly and eventually explain the steps back to her. It did not feel as though I was learning the material on the spot; it felt more as though the understanding had been there all along, but I had failed to notice it.

My friend insists that this cannot be right. She says that if I really understood the topic, I would have known that I understood it, because a person's own mind is the one thing they have direct access to. According to her, whenever someone knows something, it should be possible for them to tell, simply by reflecting, that they know it. I am not so sure. I can imagine someone knowing something without being able to confirm on their own that they know it — perhaps because they freeze under pressure, or because they have never been asked to put the knowledge into words. I also cannot rule out the possibility that answering my friend's questions created the understanding rather than revealed it.

We would be grateful for a careful answer. Is it possible to know something without knowing that you know it? Does my ability to answer correctly under questioning show that I knew the derivation all along, or only that I knew something weaker? Please explain the underlying issues and offer a reasoned judgement, in British English.
