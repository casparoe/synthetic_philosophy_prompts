Last spring I herniated a disc and had to quit the sport I'd poured years into, and I've been stuck on this question ever since: do people actually value effort for its own sake, or only when it produces results? Heres a scenario I want you to think through. Suppose that starting tomorrow there's a cheap wearable that measures a person's subjective exertion — how hard they're genuinely trying at whatever they're doing, calibrated to them, so their 8 out of 10 is comparable to anyone else's 8 out of 10. The readings are public by default and there's no way to game the sensor; it's treated as reliable as a photograph. Predict, with an actual chain of reasoning, how social life reorganizes over the next thirty years. Walk me through school and work, sports and fitness, dating and friendship, and how we treat disabled or chronically ill people. Tell me which effects are direct consequences of the mechanism, which ones are your speculation, and what would have to turn out false for your main predictions to be wrong. And say whether the world that results actually treats effort as valuable in itself, or just turns effort into another performance.
