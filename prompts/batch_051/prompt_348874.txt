I keep going back and forth on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — the claim that phenomenal consciousness, in the philosophical sense of what-it's-likeness, is an introspective illusion rather than something real. The problem I can't get past is that everything seems to hinge on what you mean by the term, and every defintion I try either builds in realism (so illusionism is false by stipulation, which is boring) or goes broadly functional (so illusionism stops contradicting the realists, also boring). I don't just mean access consciousness — being available for report and control — no one thinks that's an illusion.

I want a working definition of phenomenal consciousness: the thing in the vicinity of what it's like, subjective feel, qualia, that illusionists say is illusory. The definition should not beg the question against either side, and it should leave the debate substantive.

Then run your definition through these cases and give me a yes / no / unsure verdict for each:

1. The sting of a bee right now.
2. A thermostat registering that the room is 72 degrees.
3. Blindsight: a subject with V1 damage correctly guesses the orientation of a line far above chance but insists he sees nothing.
4. Peripheral vision: you feel as though you see the whole room in color and detail, but your ability to report color and letters drops off sharply just a few degrees from the center of gaze.
5. A vivid dream, remembered on waking as full of colors and sounds.
6. Tip-of-the-tongue: you're sure you know the word, it feels like it's right there, but you can't produce it.
7. An LLM that, when asked, says there is something it is like for it to see red and gives fluent descriptions of its supposed experience, though it was trained only by next-token prediction.
8. Change blindness: a large change in a scene goes unnoticed across a blank or an eye movement, even though the viewer would have sworn they saw the whole scene.

I'm confident 1 is yes and 2 is no. I lean 3 is no and 7 is yes-adjacent but unverifiable-ish. 5, 6, and 8 I genuinely don't know, and 4 is where I most feel the pull toward illusionism.

After you've run all eight, tell me which single verdict your definition handles least securely, and end with a line that just says:

Verdict: [case name]

with the case named, and nothing after that line.
