I keep noticing a structural parallel I can't adjudicate. The universalizability test—Kant's version, the everyday "what if everyone did that?" argument, and the later generalization arguments it inspired—works as a negative filter: a maxim that cannot be willed as a universal law without contradiction is ruled out, but a maxim that passes the test is not thereby shown to be right, and whether it passes depends heavily on how the maxim is described. Dimensional analysis in physics seems to have the same logical profile. You reject an equation if the units on the two sides do not match; matching units, however, says nothing about whether the equation is true, and which dimensions appear depends on what physical quantity you take a symbol to denote. Is this a genuine structural identity between two formal, negative, content-insensitive consistency checks, or only a loose analogy? If the parallel is genuine, something should transfer from one side to the other—for example, the dimensionless combinations that cancel their units and pass the dimensional check automatically might correspond, in ethics, to maxims that are universalizable under any description. If nothing transfers, say exactly which feature of universalizability has no analogue in dimensional analysis. Please write your answer in American English.
