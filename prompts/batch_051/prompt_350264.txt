The term dictator is worth pinning down because Arrow's usage is technical and easily confused with the ordinary political sense of the word, and much of the interpretive controversy around the theorem turns on how narrowly or broadly the non-dictatorship condition is read. Give a precise working definition of a dictator in the sense relevant to Arrow's impossibility theorem. Assume the standard Arrow framework as background: a social welfare function is a rule that maps any profile of complete, transitive individual preference orderings over three or more alternatives to a complete, transitive social ordering, against the background conditions of unrestricted domain, weak Pareto (if everyone strictly prefers one alternative to another, society must do the same), and independence of irrelevant alternatives (the social ranking of two alternatives may depend only on individual rankings of those two alternatives). Where one of the cases below fails to be a social welfare function in this sense, say so and explain how that affects the question whether it is dictatorial.

Then run your definition against each of the following cases, saying for each whether it counts as dictatorial and why:

(a) A rule that always adopts the strict ranking of one fixed voter, whatever everyone else submits.

(b) A rule that adopts the strict ranking of a fixed voter only in profiles where every other voter is indifferent between every pair of alternatives, and otherwise uses pairwise majority rule.

(c) A rule that, after all ballots are in, selects one voter by lottery and adopts that voter's ranking as the social ordering.

(d) A chair who never votes unless the other voters' preferences leave a tie, and then breaks the tie according to the chair's own ranking.

(e) Pairwise majority rule, applied to a profile in which the majority relation happens to be complete and transitive.

(f) A rule that ranks alternatives according to the preferences of the median voter on a single left–right dimension.

(g) A rule that ignores every voter's ranking below their top alternative and ranks alternatives solely by counting first-place votes.

(h) A rule that makes one fixed voter decisive over a single named pair of alternatives — their strict preference on that pair always becomes the social strict preference — while every other pair is settled by pairwise majority rule.

Where your definition gives a surprising or counterintuitive verdict, either defend that verdict or revise the definition and rerun the cases. Pay particular attention to three distinctions: dictatorial outcomes versus dictatorial rules, ex post versus ex ante dictatorship, and being decisive over one pair versus being decisive over all pairs.

Finally, state how confident you are in the definition you settle on, and single out the one classification you are least sure about, explaining briefly what would change your mind.
