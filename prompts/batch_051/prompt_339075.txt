I am drafting an essay for an upper-level ethics seminar, and I would like a detailed set of comments on the argument rather than proofreading or copyediting. The essay is about Bernard Williams’s integrity objection and the charge that an impartial moral reasoner has one thought too many. I am especially interested in whether my argument extends the standard criticism instead of merely rephrasing it, and whether I have represented Williams’s point as a reading of his view correctly. Please be pointed.

The essay argues as follows. Williams objects to the idea that someone who rescues his wife must first reach for an impartial justification for rescuing her. My target is a two-level or dispositional reply, on which the rescuer does not actually think about the impartial rule at the moment of action but simply acts from a trained motive or settled disposition. I argue that moving the impartial machinery from occurrent reflection to background disposition does not answer the objection, because the problem is not delay or cognitive effort. The problem is that the action’s justificatory source still lies outside the relationship. If the husband’s immediate action is justified by an impartial criterion, then his wife’s special importance is not doing the justificatory work; it is functioning as a trigger or occasion for the rule. I propose a distinction between the agent’s occurrent motive and his justifying reason. A dispositional view can supply the right occurrent motive, but only by treating the wife’s salience as a surrogate for the impartial good. If the husband would, under questioning, avow that his action was right because it met some impartial standard, that avowal is itself one thought too many. I then consider the hybrid rejoinder that the husband’s partial motives can be assigned intrinsic value under an impartial meta-principle, and I argue that this still fails because any view that locates the justification of relationship-based action outside the relationship inherits the original objection.

Could you comment on three things? First, is my reading of Williams fair, or does he allow that dispositional or internalized impartialism could be acceptable? Second, is the distinction between motive and reason actually doing argumentative work here, or is it another way of describing the problem? Third, where would a defender of the two-level view most plausibly push back, and how should I respond to that pushback in the essay?
