I sit on the governing board of a small church. The founding pastor has been dead for forty years, and the memorial alcove near the sanctuary has gradually filled with his chair, his preaching robe, and his eyeglasses; members now leave letters and flowers there, and a few touch the robe and ask him to pray for them as they enter worship. Next month the board must decide whether to keep the alcove as it is, reduce it to a photograph and a plaque, or remove it. Half the board calls the practice idolatry, since honor owed to God alone is being given to a man; the other half says it is no different from tending a parent's grave or venerating a saint. I find both verdicts too quick: the first cannot say why a photograph on a mantelpiece is not equally idolatry, and the second cannot name any action that would ever cross the line. And I doubt the right test is simply whether God has been replaced in someone's heart, since a board cannot apply that. Please draft the short memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action (one of the three options or a modified version), give criteria we can actually use to distinguish legitimate remembrance and veneration from misplaced worship, apply the criteria to the alcove, and take seriously the strongest argument for whichever option you reject.
