I teach an upper-level philosophy of language seminar on lexical meaning, and I am writing the final exam. Draft one essay question, answerable in ninety minutes, on how languages live with pervasive homonymy (English pitch or bank makes the point) and on whether its persistence is best understood as a tolerated byproduct of sound change or as evidence that ambiguity can be communicatively useful. The question should not be answerable by defining homonymy, polysemy, ambiguity, vagueness, and context-sensitivity; it must force candidates to state what would count as evidence for each side and to work through at least one diachronic mechanism and one pragmatic mechanism rather than cherry-picking a single example. Then provide a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, all in American English. The marking scheme should specify the moves that separate an excellent answer from a merely competent one and identify the characteristic errors that keep an answer out of the top band.
