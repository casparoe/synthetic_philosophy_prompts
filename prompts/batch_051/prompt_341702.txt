Suppose that in 2035 a probe sampling the subsurface ocean of Europa finds a single macroscopic entity — call it the Europa entity for now — that imports reduced compounds from the surrounding water, maintains a sharp internal pH gradient against its environment, grows by accretion, repairs damage to its membrane, and moves slowly along chemical gradients while its internal chemistry oscillates homeostatically. In five years of continuous observation it has never divided, budded, or shed anything resembling a propagule, and its nucleic-acid-like polymer appears to be copied only to replace damaged sections, never replicated wholesale. It therefore shows metabolism, homeostasis, growth, and responsiveness, but no reproduction, no heredity in the usual sense, no population, and no natural selection acting on variants.

Astrobiologists now face a version of the classic what-is-life question: is this thing alive, and why or why not? I want a reasoned prediction of how the debate actually unfolds over the next decade or so, not just a personal ruling from you. Which of the standard criteria — metabolism, homeostasis, growth, reproduction, evolvability, information-bearing heredity, maintenance of thermodynamic disequilibrium, and so on — end up carrying the most weight, and which get quietly demoted? Does the field converge on treating life as a property of individual systems here and now, or as something that only populations with histories can have? What experiments or observations would the rival camps propose to break the tie, and which possible result would push most people one way or the other? How does the episode change operational choices in astrobiology — mission design, biosignature criteria, or how narrowly scientists use the word life itself?

Where you can see mechanisms, make the reasoning explicit: institutional incentives, funding pressure, the sociology of a young field, the rhetorical pull of certain definitions, and the difference between a definition that does scientific work and one that just awards a label. For each major prediction, say how confident you are, which of my assumptions it depends on, and what fact about the entity or the scientific community would most change your mind. If the setup is confused — for example, if you think I have rigged the case by giving it metabolism but withholding reproduction, so that no observation could settle a fundamentally terminological dispute — say that plainly rather than complying politely.
