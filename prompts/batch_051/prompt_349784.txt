A colleague defends a version of assembly theory. On her measure, the complexity of an object is its assembly index: the length of the shortest sequence of joining operations that constructs the object from a small fixed set of elementary parts, where previously assembled substructures may be reused. She further claims that a high assembly index reliably marks objects produced by selection — life, technology, or any process that copies and propagates structure — rather than by chance. I am not sure how much of the second claim to believe: the index seems able to track how unlikely, or how heavily copied, something is under random assembly, and that is not obviously the same as complexity, still less a signature of selection. How could one test the claim concretely?
