In my European history class we read excerpts from Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert (1758), written after d'Alembert's Encyclopédie article argued that Geneva should establish a public theater. Rousseau replied that a theater would do the city moral and civic harm, and Geneva left the proposal unimplemented. I want to test his causal claims against a counterfactual.

Suppose that in 1760 the Genevan council reversed course and licensed a single public theater in the city center, run by a traveling French troupe, while everything else about Geneva stayed the same: its small size, Calvinist church discipline, watchmaking workshops, and neighborhood assemblies.

Using the mechanisms Rousseau actually gives rather than only his conclusion, predict the next thirty years of Genevan civic and moral life. The mechanisms I have in mind are these: (1) acting trains a person in self-alienation—putting on borrowed characters, appearing other than one is, living to be looked at—so the profession corrupts those who practice it and lowers the standing of everyone attached to it; (2) spectatorship is passive and solitary even in a full room, and the pity and fear the audience spends on staged suffering is pity and fear it does not spend on real neighbors; (3) a theater must please to survive, so it can only flatter the tastes a city already has and therefore strengthens rather than corrects the audience's existing vices; (4) a theater brings luxury, late nights, admission costs, and a permanent class of entertainers into a small, austere, self-governing republic, pulling people away from the civic activities that keep such a republic alive.

Trace which of these mechanisms would plausibly operate in mid-eighteenth-century Geneva, which would misfire or be swamped by other forces, and what observable signs you would look for after ten, twenty, and thirty years. Treat each mechanism as a hypothesis that could fail: for each one, say what would have to be true about Genevan institutions or about how people actually respond to fiction for that mechanism to work. Be explicit about where your prediction is robust and where it is a guess. Finally, tell me which single additional piece of evidence or argument would most change your overall prediction, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
