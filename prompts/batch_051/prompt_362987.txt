I got into it with my housemate because she keeps forwarding me Oxfordian articles arguing Shakespeare didn't write the plays, and I told her I know the mainstream picture is right so I dont have to read them. She said that's dogmatic; I said if I know p, then I know that any evidence against p is misleading, so why spend the evening on it? She said that can't be right, since it would let anyone ignore any criticism once they're sure. I tried to lay the argument out:

(1) If I know p, then p is true.
(2) If p is true, then any evidence against p is misleading (it points toward a falsehood).
(3) If I know p, then I can know that any evidence against p is misleading (I can see that p entails this, and knowledge is closed under known entailment).
(4) If I know that any evidence against p is misleading, then I'm rationally permitted to set counterevidence aside without examining it.
(5) So if I know p, I'm rationally permitted to set any counterevidence aside without examining it.
(6) That has to be wrong: it would make every known belief immune to revision, and a flat-earther could run the same argument on satellite photos.

Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Lead with the step you reject, then explain why it looked safe, and price the main alternatives. I'd most like to keep (1) and (2), but I can't see how to stop the slide to (6). Keep the answer under 350 words.
