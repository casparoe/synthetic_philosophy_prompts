A friend and I just had a long argument about Adam Smith's treatment of the colonial trade monopoly in Book IV, Chapter VII of The Wealth of Nations, and I came away more convinced than before that Smith's critique is correct: the monopoly of colonial trade is not simply exploitative of the colonies but self-defeating for the imperial power itself. The passage I keep coming back to is the one near the end of the chapter where Smith, if I may paraphrase closely, classifies the monopoly of the colony trade among the mercantile system's mean and malignant expedients, and says it depresses the industry of all other countries, principally that of the colonies, while not increasing but instead diminishing the industry of the country in whose favour it is established. I take him to be making an allocative argument: capital and labour diverted into the protected colonial trades earn monopoly profits only by being pulled from more productive domestic employments, so that the metropole's total output is on balance lower than it would have been under free trade, even after counting the gains of the monopoly merchants and the state revenue.

I want to know what would count against this position, not merely what Smith's mercantilist opponents said, but the strongest modern defeaters a sophisticated critic could marshal: evidence from economic history and the Atlantic economy, general equilibrium considerations about trade diversion and capital mobility, public finance arguments about the fiscal-military state, strategic and geopolitical rationales for exclusive colonial markets, and the political-economic dynamics that might make monopoly profits a tolerable price for securing broader imperial capacities. Which observations or models would genuinely embarrass the Smithian case, and which merely complicate it? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest assessment of whether the position, as I have stated it, has been qualified by its defenders into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation would itself be a problem for a thesis of this kind. Which of these would a committed Smithian concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss?
