So I got into this long argument last night with my friend who kept insisting that deterministic means predictable in principle: if you know the rule and the starting point, you can just run it forward, and any randomness in the forecast just reflects our ignorance, not anything real. I realized I knew the standard responses but I couldn't make him feel the point. I teach a 75-minute undergraduate philosophy of science seminar, 14 students, and I want to run an activity where they experience a deterministic rule becoming effectively unpredictable even when everyone is following the rule exactly. They should come away seeing that determinism at the level of the rule and predictability at the level of a forecaster can come apart, and that the gap is about precision, amplification, and the horizon of prediction, not about randomness. No lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the word "chaos" until the debrief.

Design a runnable activity around a simple deterministic recurrence, something like the logistic map x_t+1 = 4x_t(1−x_t). Include roles for forecasters and a "world" that follows the rule, a scoring scheme that doesn't use real money, and a concrete timeline for 75 minutes. The key mechanic should be that the forecasters are given the initial condition only to some number of decimal places, while the world is secretly run from a slightly different value inside that uncertainty range. The teams should discover that their forecasts get worse over time, but they shouldn't be able to fix it by being smarter, because the missing digits are simply not in the information they were given. Then give me the debrief: map what happened to determinism, sensitivity to initial conditions, measurement error, and the difference between unpredictability in principle and unpredictability in practice. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it—I suspect some students will want to treat the hidden digits as random noise, and I need a prepared answer for why that's not the right way to think about it. Be honest about a feature of the phenomenon the activity cannot reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself as the facilitator.
