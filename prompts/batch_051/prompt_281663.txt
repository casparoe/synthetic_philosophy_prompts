I'm in an IR reading group and one of the regulars, M, is a hardcore rationalist about failed cooperation: she thinks every war, arms race, or ruinous competition is explained by exactly one of private information, commitment problems, or indivisibility, and she always names just one. Her track record so far: she called the 2003 Iraq invasion a private-information case (the US couldn't tell whether Iraq still had WMDs, and Iraq had every reason to bluff, so the bargaining range looked empty when it wasn't); she called the Cold War nuclear arms race a commitment problem (both sides would have preferred a freeze, but neither could commit not to cheat, and cheating was easy to hide); and she called the space race an indivisibility case (only one side can be first, and you can't split that). Next week she has to diagnose the AI race between two leading labs: both say they'd accept a slowdown if the other verifiably slowed, but progress is secret, a defector who kept training could plausibly get a decisive lead, no agreement could be enforced, and the lead is probably winner-take-all. I don't especially care which mechanism is actually right about AI. I want to predict M: what rules could she be running that fit her track record, what does each rule say she'll name for the AI case, which one do you actually expect her to pick and why, and what extra case should I send her to cleanly seperate those rules?
