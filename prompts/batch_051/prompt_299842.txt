I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums forever, so I default to thinking about epistemology in terms of Bayesian updating, likelihoods, noisy channels, source reliability, calibration, and when you're allowed to throw out evidence because the process that produced it is suspect. Lately I've been poking at Nyāya epistemology, the classical Indian school, and something about it keeps bugging me. My rough picture is that Nyāya recognizes four pramāṇas—accredited sources of knowledge—namely pratyakṣa (perception), anumāna (inference), upamāna (comparison/analogy), and śabda (testimony), and that they spent a while fighting with Buddhists and Cārvākas over whether each one is genuinely irreducible. The stock example for upamāna is something like: you know what a cow is but you've never seen a gavaya, someone tells you a gavaya is kind of like a cow but bigger and darker, and then later, when you see one, you recognize it as a gavaya, and that recognition is supposed to count as knowledge. When I read this stuff it feels like it should all flatten into "evidence is evidence": perception is just your sensory channels, inference is just running the usual induction machinery, testimony is just another noisy channel you update on after calibrating the source, and upamāna is... something about similarity between representations that I can't quite locate in my usual toolkit, even though it's clearly doing *some* kind of work.

Restate the Nyāya theory of the pramāṇas as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I'm fluent in: Bayesian/probabilistic epistemology plus ML-ish ideas like feature representations, embeddings, learned priors, channels, source reliability, calibration, and maybe out-of-distribution generalization. Treat each pramāṇa separately and be explicit about how much of its epistemic role survives the translation. Then give me the residue report: which parts map cleanly, which parts I'd have to distort to fit, and which parts have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary. I genuinely want to know whether my "it all flattens to channels and updates" instinct is right, or whether it's like assuming pointers fully explain Frege's puzzle because both happen to involve something reference-like. I'd rather find out what I'm missing than have the Nyāya view flattered by a translation that quietly changes what it says.
