In my high school philosophy elective we have been discussing the relationship between free will and the kind of physics the universe runs on. The standard options seem unsatisfying: if determinism is true, my choices are fixed by events before I was born; if quantum indeterminism is true, decisions might be partly random, but a random twitch at the moment of choice feels no more mine than a caused twitch does. I keep coming back to a middle position I have been calling the Filter View of free will. On this view, a free action should be analyzed as a two-layer process. The first layer is option generation, where candidate actions, considerations, and imagined outcomes come to mind; the second layer is option selection, where my settled character—the comparatively stable package of beliefs, values, habits, and emotional dispositions that I identify as myself—assigns weights to those candidates and settles on one. My claim is that the two layers need opposite amounts of openness: the first should be genuinely indeterministic, and the second should be deterministic. Chance at the intake stage simply varies the menu without deciding what I order; chance at the selection stage would insert something that is not me into exactly the part of the process that is supposed to be mine.

An example: while deciding whether to report a friend for cheating, it may be partly a matter of chance which considerations surface first; but once honesty, loyalty, fear, and sympathy are all on the table, I want my ranking of those considerations to follow from who I am. The Filter View is meant to give me both openness and authorship without pretending that quantum randomness is itself a form of control. I realize this sounds close to existing two-stage models in the free will literature, where chance generates possibilities and choice selects among them, and it may just be compatibilism with quantum dressing. What I want is an honest assessment of whether the layer distinction does any real work, or merely relocates the luck problem: if the menu is partly produced by chance, then isn't whatever I finally do still partly a matter of luck? Please also say whether the boundary between the two layers can be drawn without making it arbitrary.

The five items should be: (1) your one-sentence reconstruction of the view, (2) the nearest existing position or positions and whether the Filter View is genuinely distinct or merely a restatement, (3) the strongest single objection, (4) the one thing the view gets right even if it collapses, and (5) your bottom line on whether it solves the original worry, survives with modification, or fails.
