ok so for my music theory class weve been reading this old book by Eduard Hanslick called The Beautiful in Music and I keep going back and forth on the chapter where he says music cant actually represent a definite feeling like sadness, it can only reproduce the dynamic side of a feeling—like its speed, its rising and falling, its intensity, that kind of thing. So the sadness isnt in the sound itself, its in how we respond. I want to know what my own gut actually adds up to, because I dont think I agree with him but I cant find the principle behind my verdicts.

Here are my cases:

- A slow song in a minor key with a falling melody sounds sad to me even if I dont know any context or lyrics.
- Hey Ya by Outkast is super upbeat but the lyrics are kind of sad, and it does feel sad to me, but not in the same way—more like tragic-ironic than sad-sounding.
- A recording of someone crying is definitely sad but I dont think it counts as music, just as sound.
- If you take a song I find sad and just change it from minor to major, same tempo and notes otherwise, it stops sounding sad almost completely.
- The trumpet piece Taps sounds sad to me even if I imagine hearing it without knowing its a military funeral thing.
- A friend of mine says minor keys only sound sad because of movie soundtracks and cultural training; I think that cant be the whole story because I thought minor chords sounded darker before I even watched many movies.
- A scary movie violin screech sounds tense and unpleasant but not sad, so its not just that all strong negative music feels the same.

Is there one principle about what makes music sad that gets all of these, or close? And does Hanslicks view explain all my cases better than I think, or do I have to say some of these verdicts are just wrong? Please tell me which of my cases do the most damage to Hanslick and which ones he can easily handle.
