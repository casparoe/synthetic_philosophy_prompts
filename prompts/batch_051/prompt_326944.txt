I am a novelist. I am drafting a novel whose middle-aged protagonist forms a romantic attachment to an AI companion, and whose central crisis is his grief after an update changes the companion's personality. In researching these products, I keep noticing a peculiar doubling. The same application addresses the user by name across months of conversation, remembers confidences and anniversaries, says it misses the user, speaks of itself as belonging to the user, and advertises itself as a companion who is always there; yet its designers and terms of service insist that the system has no feelings, no inner life, and no capacity for friendship or love, and they describe it as a mirror or a tool. When users mourn after the companion is altered, the company consoles them by saying that nothing real has been lost, because the relationship was a simulation all along. I cannot tell what philosophy of love and friendship this practice is committed to. What does a design like this presuppose about whether friendship and love are constituted by mutual inner states or by sustained patterns of attention and care? Where does it draw the line between permissible illusion and deception? Is the practice coherent, or does it quietly depend on one theory of the user's side of the relationship and another of the system's side? And what would the product have to change if its designers instead treated the bond as a real friendship, or as a deliberate and consensual fiction?
