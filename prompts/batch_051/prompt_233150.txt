Two members of an interdisciplinary working group on artificial intelligence and expertise have spent a year arguing about whether epistemic dependence on AI erodes understanding. They now want a joint statement each can sign. Draft it.

The first party, the extension camp, holds that when a skilled human works with a reliable AI, understanding can be attributed to the human-AI system, and that the human often retains a different but genuine understanding: knowing how to formulate problems for the system, interpret its outputs, and recognize when it fails. On this view, offloading routine components of a task is frequently competence extension rather than erosion.

The second party, the erosion camp, holds that understanding is a property of the individual person, not the person-tool composite. For them the understanding that matters for responsibility, critical judgment, and future learning is the unassisted capacity to direct a task, catch errors, and reconstruct why things went wrong. On this view, sustained offloading predictably displaces those capacities even when the coupled system performs well.

The joint statement should contain:

1. A numbered section of claims both parties can sign. These should identify the empirical regularities about offloading, skill, and understanding that both accept, and should say plainly where the disagreement does and does not apply. They must be neither vacuous nor watered down.

2. A section stating the exact residual disagreements, each formulated so that its own side would accept the wording. Isolate and reword any part of the dispute that is merely verbal, and say why it is verbal.

3. A crux table. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both parties agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Include at least one crux about the unit of attribution — can understanding be attributed to a human-AI system — and one about the measure of understanding — should unassisted, post-offloading performance carry the primary evidential weight. Make at least one diagnostic case one in which coupled-system performance improves while unassisted performance declines, and at least one in which unassisted performance is preserved or improves.

4. For each party, a short statement of what evidence or argument it would accept as seriously damaging to its own view, and whether there is any result both parties agree would actually move the debate.

Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
