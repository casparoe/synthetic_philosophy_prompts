I keep half-convincing myself of something, and I would like help deciding whether it is a real structural point or just an artifact of taking two courses in the same term. In my philosophy of mind course we have been working through Hofstadter's account of the self as a self-referential symbol. On that account, as I understand it, a symbol system rich enough to perceive, remember, and plan eventually builds a symbol for the organism itself and for its own ongoing symbolic activity. That symbol points back into the machinery that creates it, and by feeding into that machinery it helps stabilize the very activity it is a symbol of. The "I" is the strange loop this forms: a pattern at the level of mental contents that loops down into, and partly steers, the lower-level processes it abstracts over. The loop is graded rather than binary — it can be stronger or weaker — and it can extend outward into things a person has written, made, or identified with.

In my control systems course, we have been working through a basic result about regulation: if a controller is to hold a plant within its target range, its internal states must mirror the plant's states closely enough that it can pair each observed state with an appropriate correcting action, so the successful controller in effect contains a model of the plant. Now suppose the plant includes the controller itself, as a thermostat's output heats the same room it senses, or as an agent's choices alter the world its model must track. Then the model contained in the controller has to model the plant-as-including-the-controller, which means it has to model the controller's own contribution to the plant's dynamics, which in turn includes the model's own influence on what happens next. The result is a representation that refers to the very system of which it is a working part, and whose adequacy depends on getting that self-reference right.

The parallel that has been nagging me is this: Hofstadter's self-symbol just is what a regulator's internal model must become when the regulated system contains the regulator. A strange loop, that is, is an internally modeled control loop.

Please either deepen or dissolve this for me. I would rather know the exact stress points than be told that both sides involve feedback. Specifically:

1. State the mapping precisely. What in the control picture corresponds to the symbol, to the neural substrate it abstracts over, to the level-crossing, to the loop's effects on its own substrate, and to the graded or portable character Hofstadter attributes to the self?

2. Say what would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer. Does the control result need a fixed, exogenous performance measure and a tolerably known plant and target, while a self-symbol has no single objective and must generate much of its own target? Is that difference fatal, or only a matter of degree? Conversely, does the Hofstadterian loop require semantic content, narrative integration, or a capacity for self-perception that a thermostat or a robot controller need not have?

3. Name at least one point where the parallel predicts something false: a consequence the control theory would lead me to expect in a self but which selves do not display, or a feature of selves that a good regulator would not need.

My current suspicion is that the analogy captures reflexivity but misses why a self-referential symbol should be accompanied by experience from the inside; but I am worried that objection only restates the hard problem rather than testing the mapping. If that is what I am doing, say so and give me a sharper test.
