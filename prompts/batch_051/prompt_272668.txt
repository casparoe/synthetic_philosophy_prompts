I keep getting stuck on how Nietzsche’s eternal recurrence is supposed to work as an ethical test, especially in light of this passage from *Thus Spake Zarathustra*, “Reading and Writing” (Thomas Common translation):

“Ye look aloft when ye long for exaltation, and I look downward because I am exalted. Who among you can at the same time laugh and be exalted? He who climeth on the highest mountains, laugheth at all tragic plays and tragic realities.”

The standard reading of eternal recurrence is that the demon says you will live this same life again infinitely, and the test is whether you can want every detail of it. But this passage adds something else: being high means looking down and laughing at tragic realities, not just solemnly affirming them. So my question: does the eternal recurrence as an existential test survive if we replace its cyclical time background with a block universe / B-theory picture, where every moment already has permanent being and nothing literally recurs? In other words, does “this life will happen again and again” do something that “this life already is permanent, tenselessly” cannot, or does the block picture make the test starker? Which premise is doing the work, and is there a repaired version that keeps what matters without the cyclical cosmology?
