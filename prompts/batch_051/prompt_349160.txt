I am writing a paper for a course in the philosophy of psychiatry, and I have found myself stuck on a deceptively simple question: what does it mean to believe something? The reason it matters for my topic is that the two-factor theory of delusions says a delusion forms when an anomalous experience is combined with a second factor, which is usually described as a deficit in belief evaluation — a failure to reject, or properly weigh, a candidate belief. That way of stating the second factor already presumes some picture of what a belief is, and I suspect different pictures of belief return different verdicts on whether a given delusion is a belief at all. So I want to settle the definition first and then use it to evaluate the theory.

Give me a working definition of belief that gets the following six cases right. First, a commuter who checks a transit app, announces that the 7:15 train has been canceled, and takes an alternate route. Second, a patient who tells interviewers that the FBI is after him only because he has learned that endorsing the story earns him a transfer to a quieter ward, and who drops it entirely when no staff are present. Third, a person with Capgras syndrome who insists that her husband has been replaced by an impostor yet continues to live with him and does not treat him as a stranger. Fourth, a person with anosognosia who denies that his left arm is paralyzed, and when asked to touch his nose with that hand either makes no attempt or explains the failure as tiredness. Fifth, someone standing on a glass-bottomed walkway who sincerely says the glass is safe but cannot bring herself to step onto it. Sixth, a physicist who treats a model's simplifying assumptions as true for the purposes of a calculation without endorsing them as a description of the world. I am fairly confident that the first case is a belief and the second is not, and I do not know what to say about the middle four. Propose a definition, run it through all six cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts it yields, and close by saying whether your definition classifies ordinary delusions as beliefs and how the second factor of the two-factor theory should be described if your definition is right. Please write the response in American English.
