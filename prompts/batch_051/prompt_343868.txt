I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so bear with me. I'm stuck on a chapter and could use a political philosopher's brain. In the near-future novel I'm writing, a few hundred people have occupied an old offshore oil platform and turned it into a self-governing settlement. The chapter is their constitutional assembly, and the only thing on the table is who gets to vote in the platform's assembly. I want the debate to feel like a real clash of defensible positions, not a strawman parade, and right now the characters are mostly talking past each other.

The groups in the room:

- Permanent residents, who live on the platform year-round, maintain the thing, and have no other home. They say voting should be for permanent residents, full stop.

- Commuters, who spend about eight months a year working on the platform and the other four in a coastal town where there families live. They pay the same dues and do dangerous work, but they're not around for every decision.

- The founder-investor, who put up the money for the desalination plant and solar array, visits twice a year, and thinks her financial stake should buy her a weighted vote or a veto.

- A fishing town on the mainland, a few hours away by boat, whose waters are affected by the platform's waste discharge and whose economy now depends on trade with it. They want a seat.

- The teenagers who grew up on the platform, some of them sixteen or seventeen and working shifts, who say they're members as much as anyone.

My gut says the permanent residents are obviously right — it's their home and their risk, and everyone else is just a stakeholder. But my gut has a habit of flattening characters into right and wrong, so I want to stress-test it.

Act as a political philosopher / democratic theorist. Think carefully, maybe start by brainstorming the different principles that could draw the line here, and then give me: (1) the strongest version of each group's case, including the permanent residents' case for keeping the vote to themselves, and (2) your own honest judgment about where the line ought to go and why. Please dont give me reading lists or citations — I need to put these arguments in characters' mouths, so plain language works better.
