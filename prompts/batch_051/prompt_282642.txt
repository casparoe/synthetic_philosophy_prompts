Okay here's a position I've drifted into and I want you to stress-test it. I've come to think novelty is a basic component of well-being, not just an instrument to something else. Like, a life that keeps serving you genuinely new experiences is better for you than an otherwise identical life that doesn't, even after you subtract how novelty helps you learn, keeps you from getting bored, etc. I don't mean simple variety either — trying different flavors of ice cream. I mean the sense that the shape of your own future is open and can still surprise you. And I think the dread of a boring life is actual evidence for this: people fear a future they can fully predict even when everything in it is comfortable, and I read that dread as tracking something real rather than a mistake.

Some things I'd like to put on the table: I don't think novelty has constant marginal value; one genuinely new thing per week might matter a lot but I'm not claiming every extra novelty adds value. And I'm not saying a repetitive life can't be good overall — just that it would be better with the relevant kind of newness, other things equal. So the claim is deliberately a ceteris paribus one about final value.
