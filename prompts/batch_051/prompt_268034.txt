Take the standard Sleeping Beauty setup, but change the coin. On Sunday, Beauty is told that a machine first picks the coin's bias by a separate fair lottery: with probability 1/2 the coin will have objective chance 1/3 of heads, and with probability 1/2 it will have objective chance 2/3. She won't learn which bias was chosen until after the experiment is over. Everything else is vanilla: heads means exactly one waking (Monday), tails means two wakings (Monday and Tuesday) with the usual amnesia in between. When she wakes, she's asked (i) her credence that the chosen bias was 2/3, and (ii) her credence that the coin landed heads. Work out what she should say. Then say how a halfer and a thirder about the original fair-coin case would each handle the same numbers, and wether the Principal Principle—roughly, defer to the objective chance unless you have inadmissible information—actually settles it. In particular, does waking itself count as evidence about the bias, or not? Where exactly do the two views part ways? Show your calculation.
