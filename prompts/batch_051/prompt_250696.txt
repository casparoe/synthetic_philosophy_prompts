I run a tiny fansubbing group for a 1970s TV drama that was filmed in a small minority language with no proper dictionary. There's a word in it, 'brel', that we keep fighting about. We have every episode and the original scripts, and the word shows up just over fourty times. In each scene you could translate it as 'trust', 'duty', or 'gift'; all three fit if you nudge the surrounding lines a bit, but they imply very different relationships. Grammatically it acts like a noun but can show up in subject, object, or modifier position, so that doesn't settle it either. Native speakers are split too: one says it's closer to 'obligation', another to 'warmth'. The only person we found who worked on the show says the writer told actors to say it whenever 'there's a weight between the characters', which still doesn't tell us whether what's being talked about is a thing, a commitment, or a feeling. We have to lock one English subtitle track. Is there actually a right answer about what 'brel' means, or is the term genuinely indeterminate? What kind of evidence could settle it, and if none can, how should we decide? (Please answer in British English.)
