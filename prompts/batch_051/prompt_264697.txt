I am a philosopher of science whose working background is in operational weather forecasting, not in statistical theory, and I think most naturally in the vocabulary of forecast verification: calibration and reliability diagrams, sharpness, ensemble spread, hindcasting, data snooping, and skill scores. I want a translation exercise. Restate the current debate over the replication crisis — specifically, whether it indicts null-hypothesis significance testing itself or merely its abuse, and whether Bayesian methods would fix the problem — entirely in that forecasting vocabulary. Be honest about the translation: do not quietly let the forecasting framework decide the philosophical dispute. Then give a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which had to be distorted, and which have no counterpart in forecast verification at all, and say in each case whether the residue is a defect of the forecasting vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was less precise than it looked. Conclude by saying how confident you are in the translation and identifying the single point you are least sure of.
