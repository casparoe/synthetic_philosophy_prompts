I am a condensed-matter physicist. Restate the ancient Greek atomist picture of matter — according to which everything is made of atoms and the void, where atoms are indivisible, absolutely solid, contain no void internally, and cannot be penetrated, while the void is mere emptiness, the absence of body, offering no resistance — in the vocabulary I actually use: electrons and nuclei, quantum fields, the Pauli exclusion principle, the electromagnetic origin of contact forces, and the quantum vacuum. Be as faithful as you can, and then give a residue report. Which parts of the atomist doctrine translate cleanly into this vocabulary, which parts translate only under distortion, and which parts cannot be expressed at all? In particular, say whether anything in modern physics still does the work that solidity and impenetrability did for the atomists, and whether an atom/void dichotomy remains once the vacuum is treated as a structured quantum field. Where the translation fails, say whether that is because the modern vocabulary is missing something or because the ancient claim was less determinate than it appears. Write the response in American English.
