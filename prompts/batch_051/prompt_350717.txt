I am an undergraduate in a course on collective responsibility, and a classmate has presented an argument that seems to me to prove too much, but I cannot locate the mistake. The question occupying the course is whether a corporation can itself be the proper object of moral blame—blamed as a corporation, rather than merely blaming individual officers and employees. I want to say yes: when a firm runs a price-fixing conspiracy for a decade, or when a manufacturer's internal culture systematically hides a defect across several product generations, the corporation itself seems to have acted wrongfully, even though no single individual intended the whole outcome.

My classmate, who thinks corporate blame is shorthand at best, argues as follows:

(1) If a corporation is a moral agent that can be blamed for what it does, this must be in virtue of its internal decision structure: its formal procedures, differentiated roles, and policies, which allow it to reach corporate decisions and act on them.
(2) Those same features—procedures, roles, and policies that turn many individual moves into a single collective act—also exist, informally and in degrees, in other collectives. A market aggregates countless individual choices through prices; a mob coordinates many people around a shared aim; a social movement has implicit norms, informal leaders, and ways of reaching a common line.
(3) The differences between a corporation and these looser collectives are differences of degree—formalization, permanence, complexity—not differences in kind.
(4) So if a corporation's structure is enough to make it a moral agent, then a market's or a mob's structure is enough to make it a moral agent as well. Consistency requires treating the cases alike.
(5) But a market or a mob is plainly not a moral agent that can be blamed as a unit.
(6) Therefore, a corporation is not a moral agent either, and talk of blaming "the corporation" is at best a loose metaphor for blaming the people inside it.

I cannot accept (6). Yet when I try to say what a firm has that a mob lacks, each feature I name—formal rules, roles, ongoing identity—comes in degrees, which makes (3) uncomfortably plausible. Is the argument valid? If it is, which premise should I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost? If I reject (5) to save corporate blame, am I committed to saying that markets and mobs can be blamed as units, and would that be more defensible than it sounds? I want a full diagnosis of the reasoning, not just a vote between premises.
