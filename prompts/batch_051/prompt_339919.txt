Hey, I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about something I keep seeing in the more utopian corners of platform-governance conversations: a "reputation passport." The idea is that instead of being stuck with whatever karma you've earned on one site — Reddit karma, Stack Overflow rep, eBay feedback, Uber stars — you'd carry around a portable trust record, so you don't have to start from zero every time you join a new community. The critics say this is basically a category error: reputation is local, your 20k on Stack Overflow says nothing about whether you'll be a good moderator in a neighborhood listserv, and once a score becomes portable it stops meaning what it meant and turns into something people farm, fake, and maybe even buy (reputation laundering is the phrase I've seen going around). I'm not sure which side I buy, which is why the piece needs the argument laid out clean.

Could you write me a dialogue, around 1,000 words, between two equally good proponents of each view? The portability person should not dodge the gaming and context-collapse objections — maybe the passport isn't a raw score but more like a bundle of claims about your history that each destination community runs through thier own rules, or like a reference letter that gets re-read in a new context. The localist person should push hard on that: who builds the converters, what happens when they misread a migrant's reputation, and whether the whole thing just recreates a credit bureau for the internet with worse feedback loops. Give them real back-and-forth, where they respond to each other's actual points, and let them end somewhere a little different from where they started. And don't let one of them be a strawman — the localist especially should have some genuinely good warnings, not just conservatism.
