Ok, weird one. I've been reading Rawls on envy — specifically the distinction between envy proper, which he basically treats as a vice (you resent someone having something even though their having it doesn't take anything away from you), and excusable envy, where the gap is so large that it undermines the less advantaged person's self-respect and you can't reasonably expect them not to feel it. In seminar someone made a joke that a well-run social democratic state should actually try to elicit envy, because every new case of excusable envy is a free diagnostic for where inequality has stopped being defensible. I want to turn that into a satirical piece.

Write me a parody of a government intake form from something like the Office of Resentment Verification (come up with a better name if you like). The premise is that the state processes citizens' incidents of envy and resentment and classifies each one as either a validated signal of injustice — entered as evidence in the redistribution ledger — or mere envy proper, in which case the applicant is refered for moral correction. Do it deadpan and bureaucratic: checkboxes, conditional follow-ups, a field for "degree of contact with the primary goods of others," the diagnostic question about whether you'd be equally satisfied if the other person's advantage were destroyed rather than transferred to you, a section asking whether the inequality in question actually benefits the least advantaged under some strained reading of the difference principle, and a final determination stamp. The satire should target a specific style of discourse — the one where any felt comparative grievance gets retyped as evidence of structural injustice — but the form itself should never break character or wink; the clerk who designed it finds this all perfectly sensible. Around 650 words, formatted as numbered questions.
