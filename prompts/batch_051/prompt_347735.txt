Most arguments about reputation and karma systems are decided by whichever example is adduced last. One person objects that eBay-style mutual feedback makes buyers and sellers afraid to leave honest negative reviews; another says forum karma mostly measures agreement and sheer persistence rather than reliability; another says credit scores are opaque but at least they work. I suspect these are local symptoms of a smaller set of underlying requirements, and I want those requirements stated in full rather than assembled piecemeal from cases.

Write me the desiderata that an adequate theory of reputation and karma systems must meet. By "reputation or karma system" I mean any persistent, rule-governed aggregation of people's assessments or behavior into a standing number, label, or status that is then used to decide whom to trust, reward, display, filter, or transact with: marketplace seller ratings, forum karma, ride-share ratings, citation and contribution metrics, internal peer scores, credit scores, and the like.

For each desideratum, give the concrete case or failure mode that forces it. Include both distinctions the theory must not collapse — for example, between predicting someone's future conduct and rewarding their past conduct, or between someone's honesty in a transaction and whether others approve of their views — and structural or strategic constraints the design cannot ignore, such as coordinated fake accounts, retaliation for negative reviews, score inflation, feedback loops that entrench early winners, and the cold-start problem for newcomers. Do not leave each contested item open: for each requirement, say plainly whether it is non-negotiable for any adequate system under the purpose below, a preference of a particular camp, or eliminable, and defend the call.

Fix the purpose as follows: the system exists to improve decisions about whom to trust, cooperate with, or attend to in repeated interactions among a large population of partly pseudonymous agents; it is not being used as an instrument of state administration. A table for the requirements and their status is fine; use prose for the conflicts among them.

Then flag any pairs or subsets of your desiderata that you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and say whether each conflict amounts to a hard impossibility or only a trade-off. Finally, name the single requirement that does the most to exclude the reputation systems people actually deploy, and justify why. You may briefly score one or two real-world designs against the list (for example, eBay-style mutual feedback or Reddit-style karma), but keep that to a short paragraph: the desiderata and the conflicts among them are the deliverable. Where a requirement could be read more weakly or more strongly, say which reading you are committing to.
