I am a software engineer and I serve on the governance committee of a small open-source software foundation; I have no formal background in philosophy. Our bylaws contain one amendment clause, which reads: "These bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the steering committee." At our next meeting we face two proposals. The first would amend that very clause to require a three-quarters vote for all future amendments. The second would add a new clause stating that the project's license may never be changed and that this new clause itself may never be amended or repealed. A bloc on the committee argues that the plain text of the current clause lets us pass both proposals by a two-thirds vote, since it authorizes amendments to any part of the bylaws, including the amendment clause itself. Another bloc argues that a rule cannot legitimately use the old procedure to bind the future to a stricter procedure, and that an "unamendable" clause is even more suspect because a later committee could always repeal it under whatever amendment rule then exists. The whole thing strikes me as something like a self-modifying function: I can see how the call can be traced, but I am unsure what the correct execution should be. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a definite course of action: adopt or reject the three-quarters amendment, adopt or reject the unalterable license clause, and state the general rule you think we should follow about when a rule may amend the amendment procedure itself. Do not just lay out the options; give me a committed position with reasons, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the side you reject.
