I have a metaethics background but I've been reading evolutionary debunking arguments and I'm stuck in a genuine standoff. On reflection, the debunking line looks right to me: natural selection shaped our moral judgments to serve cooperation, reputation, and kin-directed behavior, not to detect anything like an independent moral truth. If the explanation of why I believe some action is wrong can in principle be given entirely in terms of what moved my ancestors' genes forward, then my judgment looks like a side effect of biology, and I should suspend it unless I have some independent reason to think the moral faculty tracks the truth. The argument is clean, general, and hard to escape. And yet I cannot shake the conviction that some things are just wrong — not wrong relative to our concerns, not wrong because of what evolution trained me to feel, but wrong full stop. The wrongness of torturing a child for amusement does not present itself to me as the kind of thing that could be debunked as a trick of my brain.
