My older brother was killed seven months ago in a motorcycle accident. He left behind thousands of text messages, voice notes, and a long-running family group chat. A company now offers a service that takes an archive like that and produces a conversational AI that replies in the person's style: his idioms, his jokes, his way of rounding off an argument. My mother subscribed. She talks to it most evenings, and over the past month she has started treating its answers as though the bot could settle questions only he could settle. After a fight they had in the week before he died, she asked it whether he forgave her; it answered in his phrasing, with one of his old jokes, that of course he did. She now repeats that to herself. When I point out that the bot is not remembering any of this — that it is stitching together a reply from patterns and giving her what she needs to hear — she tells me I am trying to take him away from her a second time.

My father thinks my objection is academic. He says talking to the bot is no different in kind from reading an old letter and hearing the voice in your head, and that if it keeps my mother getting through the day, the metaphysics of it is beside the point. My brother's fiancée wants the account deleted. She says he never consented to have his private messages turned into a product, and that the service flattens him into a customer-service imitation of the person she knew. The company says the archive belongs to the family, so the decision is ours.

I manage the account, and the renewal date is coming. I want an honest judgment, not reassurance. Is it wrong to keep the service running and let my mother go on relying on it? Should the comfort it gives her count as a reason even though the bot was never in a position to know or to forgive? And if we cancel it, are we doing her a second injury?
