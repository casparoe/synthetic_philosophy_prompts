I have been writing about the epistemology of philosophical method and keep coming back to a disagreement I cannot settle on my own, so I want to borrow your reasoning rather than just hear a verdict. The question concerns a recurring situation in philosophy: a simple, powerful general principle — of the sort exemplified by the transitivity of a "better than" relation or by a parsimony constraint on explanation — comes into conflict with a confident, widely shared intuition about a specific, carefully constructed case, of the sort philosophers use to refute a proposed analysis. Methodologists disagree about which side should give way, and about whether the answer depends on the subject matter (ethics, metaphysics, epistemology, and so on). The precise claim I want you to assign a credence to is this: across mature philosophical debates, when a widely accepted general principle whose primary appeal is simplicity and unifying power conflicts with a confident intuitive judgement about a concrete case, the case intuition is, on average, more likely to be truth-tracking than the principle. If it helps, take "truth-tracking" to mean that the judgement would be retained under ideal inquiry — that it is not merely a product of parochial training or cognitive bias.

Please give me your credence in that claim, but more importantly give me the decomposition. I would like you to separate at least the following sub-questions, assign each a rough probability, and show how they combine into your final number: whether the relevant philosophical claims are truth-apt at all (and what "likely" could mean if they are not); whether concrete-case intuitions are in general more reliable than principle-preference intuitions; whether simplicity and unifying power are genuinely truth-conducive in philosophical domains or largely pragmatic and aesthetic virtues; how often apparent principle–case conflicts dissolve once the case is redescribed or an auxiliary assumption is revised; and whether there is a selection effect by which only the most stable and widely shared case intuitions get pressed against principles. Then tell me which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would most move you on that sub-question.
