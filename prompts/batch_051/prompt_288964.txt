Russell's theory of descriptions says that a sentence like The present King of France is bald isn't really a subject-predicate sentence about a shadowy nonexistent king; its real logical form is quantificational—there is exactly one present King of France and he is bald—so the sentence is meaningful but false, and you don't have to put Meinongian nonexistent objects in your ontology. Part of the argument was that you need that hidden quantificational structure to explain how competent speakers can use descriptions that pick out nothing. Now we have LLMs that handle definite descriptions, including non-refering ones, about as well as people do, trained purely to predict text, with no quantifier variables or truth tables anywhere. Does Russell's argument survive this, need repair, or is it dead? I'm less interested in the verdict than in the dissection: which premise of the argument does the LLM development actually bear on, and what would a model have to do to count as real evidence against (or for) the theory, rather than just an implementation detail. Lead with the bottom line, then argue. Under 500 words.
