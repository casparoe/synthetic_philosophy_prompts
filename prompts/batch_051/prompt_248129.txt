I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for the last few months I've been hanging around the edges of a scene where dumpster-diving freegans and backyard animal sanctuaries overlap. They keep arguing about the same word — "waste" — and I've realized I can't define it well enough to write the piece fairly.

I need a working definition of "waste" in the food-and-animal context that actually sorts the cases people care about. Here are the ones I'm stuck on: a bag of bagels a bakery tosses at closing (obviously waste, right?); eggs laid by a rescued hen that nobody is planning to sell or eat; roadkill; fleece from a rescued sheep that has to be shorn for it's health anyway; a friend's leftover half-burger you'd otherwise bin; milk from a sanctuary cow produced beyond what her calf drinks; factory-farm manure that could be composted; and cells taken from a living cow to grow cultivated meat. My suspicion is that "waste" is doing two jobs — one descriptive (it won't get used unless somebody uses it) and one justificatory (because it would otherwise be wasted, it's permissible to use it). Give me the definition, or the two definitions, run them through all eight cases, and tell me which verdict you're least sure about.
