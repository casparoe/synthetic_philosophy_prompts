Im in a course on paternalism and behavioral policy, and I keep flip-flopping on the nudge stuff. You're a philosopher who works on autonomy and choice architecture — could you brainstorm the strongest version of each side before you commit, and be explicit about where you're uncertain?

Here's where I'm stuck. The case for what we've been calling means paternalism feels nearly airtight: if a policy makes me better off by my own lights — not the state's values, mine — and it doesn't remove any option, just reorders defaults or reframes how a choice is presented, then refusing it starts to look superstitious. People demonstrably have present bias and status quo bias; why not use those same biases, through the choice architecture, to steer them toward the choice they'd reflectively want? The view gives me a tidy decision procedure: ask whether the intervention serves my interests as I'd judge them after deliberation, and if yes, it's permitted.

But I have this stubborn intuition that when my university auto-enrolls me in something, or my employer defaults me into a plan, or a form is designed to exploit where my attention goes, something has been taken even if I'd endorse the outcome afterwards. And here's the weird part: the fact that I wouldn't have noticed the nudge makes it feel worst, not better. If someone argues with me openly and changes my mind, that's one thing; if someone rearranges the choice environment so my biased mind does the steering for them, that feels like being managed rather than respected. I reach for words like self-authorship or agency, but I don't fully trust them — they sound like the autonomy-talk the nudge side calls question-begging.

So this isnt just 'which side wins.' I want a negotiated answer: is the intuition tracking something the means-paternalism picture leaves out (awareness? contestability? who controls the defaults? being a co-author rather than just a beneficiary of my own choices?), or is it itself a kind of status quo bias I should debunk and drop? What would it take to justify each move — keeping the theory and giving up the intuition, amending the theory so it only permits a narrower class of nudges, or finding a reading on which both survive?
