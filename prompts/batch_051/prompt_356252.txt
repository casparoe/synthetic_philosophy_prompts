Last week in my environmental ethics seminar we got into the moral-hazard objection to solar geoengineering, and a friend of mine left the seminar convinced the whole thing proves too much. He wrote the argument out for me like this:

(1) The moral hazard in solar geoengineering is that it would lower the felt urgency of cutting emissions while leaving the CO2 that's actually causing the problem in the atmosphere.
(2) If a measure lowers the felt urgency of cutting emissions without removing the CO2, then it is objectionable in exactly the same way, and we should oppose it.
(3) But sea walls, heat-wave early-warning systems, and distributing drought-tolerant seed varieties also lower the felt urgency of cutting emissions without removing CO2.
(4) So by (2), we should oppose sea walls, early-warning systems, and drought-tolerant seeds too.
(5) That's absurd: building flood defenses in low-lying countries or rolling out heat warnings in a poor city is not some sort of climate procrastination we should refuse to do.
(6) Therefore (2) is false, and the moral-hazard objection to solar geoengineering depends on a principle we have to abandon — which would pretty much sink the objection itself.

I keep wanting to resist this, because it does feel like there's a real difference between dimming the planet and building a sea wall, but every time I try to state the difference I either smuggle in the conclusion or run back to termination shock. And termination shock (a sudden stop in deployment causing rapid catch-up warming) seems like a seperate objection altogether; it doesn't explain why the moral-hazard objection, specifically, is more than an empirical worry to be weighed. So which step do I reject, and what am I actually paying for it? If I give up (2), does the moral-hazard objection survive as something more modest, like a reason to worry rather than a knockdown? And if I keep (2) by drawing a line between solar geoengineering and adaptation, what does that line commit me to about other moral-hazard-flagged measures — flood insurance, seatbelts, needle exchanges?
