ok so here's roughly where i've landed after going down a rabbit hole on the gender-equality paradox and i'd like a consistency check before this becomes the spine of my paper. i hold all five of these:

(1) The big occupational splits by gender — women over in care and education, men over in STEM and trades — are mostly made by social forces: stereotypes, discrimination, gendered expectations from childhood, hiring networks, all of it. I'm not a hard blank-slate person, but I don't think innate interest differences are doing much of the explanatory work.

(2) I think gender equality is a live project, and I treat a society's level of occupational segregation as a reasonable index of how much gender inequality remains. If a country's women are still clustered in lower-paid, lower-status care work, then whatever the laws say, the job isn't done.

(3) I also think the gender-equality paradox is real and I won't hand-wave the data: more gender-egalitarian countries — the Nordics especially — often show higher occupational segregation, and fewer women in STEM, than a bunch of much less egalitarian countries. I don't think it's purely a measurement artifact.

(4) But I'm also committed to respecting individual choices. If a woman in Sweden freely picks nursing over engineering, I don't think that's a justice problem, and I'd be appaled if someone told me she's just internalizing oppression.

(5) And I have a boring methodological commitment: a good explanation should generalize. I don't want one causal story for Norway and a completely different one for India, or to claim the same pattern of persistent-or-worsening segregation has unrelated causes in different places.

I genuinely can't tell which of these are really in tension, which only seem to be, and what the cheapest repair is. Don't just lecture me on the paradox — audit the set. Rank the tensions by severity, say which apparent ones dissolve and why, and tell me which commitment (or small modification of a commitment) gives the best overall fix and what it costs me.
