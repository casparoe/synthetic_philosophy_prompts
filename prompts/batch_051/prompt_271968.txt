I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am writing a feature about a proposed consumer-finance rule that tries to institutionalize a concept from the theory of rational choice. The concept is the money pump: a sequence of individually acceptable trades that returns the agent to its starting position with less money, made possible because the agent's preferences are not stable across time. Some theorists argue that diachronically inconsistent preferences, present bias being the most familiar example, make real agents exploitable in fast, low-friction markets even when no single decision seems irrational at the moment.

The proposal is an opt-in rule for brokerage and investing platforms. Under the rule, the platform flags what it calls a completed reversal cycle: at least three trades by the same client within the same asset class, executed within a rolling ninety-day window, in which the client's position returns to materially the same state it had occupied earlier in the sequence while the client pays spreads, commissions, or comparable frictions on each leg. When an opted-in client attempts an order that would complete such a cycle, the platform must delay execution for one business day and display a ledger of the earlier legs, including prices, frictions paid so far, and any stated rationale the client recorded when opening the earlier positions. After the delay the client may proceed on the original terms; the platform may not block the order, may not charge for the delay, and may not identify the client when publishing aggregate statistics on how many flagged cycles are completed each quarter.

I would like you to treat this as a mechanism rather than as a moral position or a policy recommendation. Analyze what it would incentivize among individual clients, platforms, investment advisers, regulators, and the designers of trading interfaces; how it would be gamed; where it would fail or backfire; what second-order effects it would set in motion; and whether its failure modes could be fixed within the design or are intrinsic to it.
