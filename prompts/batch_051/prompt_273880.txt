Construct, for use in a paradoxes seminar, a hybrid paradox that combines the inferential engine of Curry's paradox with the non-self-referential structure of Yablo's liar, and then demonstrate that the construction satisfies each specification below. Take Curry's paradox to be the derivation of an arbitrary conclusion from a sentence whose content is a conditional with its own truth as antecedent; take Yablo's liar to be an infinite sequence of sentences, each making a claim about all later sentences, that issues in a contradiction even though no sentence refers to itself or to any earlier sentence. The goal is to isolate the role of the conditional from the role of the non-self-referential ordering, so a construction that merely embeds Curry's original self-referential sentence somewhere in the sequence does not satisfy the specifications.

Specifications:

- The construction must be a single infinite sequence S_1, S_2, S_3, ..., with no sentence referring to itself or to any earlier sentence; each sentence must refer only to later sentences, or to a totality consisting of later sentences.
- Each sentence must be built around a conditional, so that the conclusion is forced through the Curry-style behavior of the conditional rather than through the negation-and-falsity pattern of the original liar.
- The derivation must yield an arbitrary conclusion — for concreteness, the proposition that the Eiffel Tower is in Bologna — and must not presuppose that conclusion at any step of the construction.

Deliver the following in order:

1. The template. Give the general form of S_n, with the arbitrary conclusion and the quantification over later sentences made explicit.

2. The derivation. Show step by step that the arbitrary conclusion follows, and mark at each step which structural feature is doing the work: the conditional, the quantification over all later sentences, the unboundedness of the sequence, or the absence of self-reference.

3. The division of labor. State which part of the derivation is inherited from Curry's paradox and which part from Yablo's liar, and explain what each inheritance contributes that the other does not.

4. The load-bearing feature. Identify the single structural feature whose removal makes the construction collapse. Then verify the collapse in at least two ways: once by truncating the sequence to finitely many sentences, and once by replacing the conditional with conjunction. For each failed variant, give the truth assignment that blocks the arbitrary conclusion and explain why that assignment is no longer contradictory.

5. The objection. Someone who denies that Yablo's paradox is genuinely free of self-reference — because each sentence still quantifies over a totality of which it is a member, or because the construction as a whole has a self-referential fixed point — will press the corresponding objection against your hybrid. State the strongest version of that objection and give the best reply available to the defender of the construction.
