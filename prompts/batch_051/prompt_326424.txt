I teach a final-year undergraduate seminar in the philosophy of language, and I am writing the final exam. I want one essay question on Quine's indeterminacy of translation, to be answered in about 50 minutes for 20 marks. My students all know the slogan—rival translation manuals can be compatible with all the behavioral evidence and yet incompatible with one another—and they can all rehearse the point that 'gavagai' might be glossed as rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or rabbit stage. What they cannot do, in my experience, is explain why Quine takes this to be more than the familiar underdetermination of theory by evidence, and why he thinks it licenses the stronger conclusion that there is nothing to be right or wrong about. The question should therefore force them to work across that gap: from the claim that the behavioral data do not single out a unique manual to the claim that there is no fact of the matter which manual is correct. It should also build in the standard objection—that real field linguists do converge on translations, or that the example only shows ordinary underdetermination—so that a strong answer must meet the objection rather than sidestep it.

Please write the question, a model answer of around 600 words, and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should separate an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. In particular, it should distinguish the moves of separating indeterminacy of translation from inscrutability of reference, articulating the role of Quine's behaviorist constraint on what counts as linguistic evidence, and supplying the extra premise needed to get from empirical equivalence to the absence of any fact of the matter. It should also flag the characteristic B-answer misreadings: treating indeterminacy as nothing more than underdetermination of theory by evidence, treating it as a skeptical claim that translation is impossible, or recounting the gavagai example without explaining what philosophical work it is doing.
