I do VR and game rendering for a living — I live in meshes, shaders, draw calls, frame timing, head-mounted displays, all of it. Lately I've been poking at Thomas Metzinger's idea that the self is a phenomenal self-model, and that this self-model is transparent — and in his usage transparency does not mean you can look through it to some more real self behind it. It means the system never gets to see the representational act as a representational act; the model shows up as the thing itself, not as a picture of the thing. So on my reading, being me is the running of a self-simulation that I can never catch in the act of simulating.

Now my day job is arguably building a toy version of that: an avatar in a headset that gives enough presence that the player stops noticing the rendering. I want you to restate Metzinger's position as precisely as you can in the vocabulary I already have: render loop, frame, mesh, shader, texture, screen-space vs world-space, avatar, body ownership, presence, alpha transparency, and the fact that you never perceive the framebuffer, only the scene. Then give me the residue report. Which bits of the self-model story map cleanly onto that vocabulary, which bits you had to fudge to make them fit, and which bits of the philosophy have no counterpart in a rendering pipeline at all? I suspect the absolute absence of a homunculus — the fact that there is no one standing behind the headset watching the screen — is the one that doesn't survive, and I want to know if that is a bug in my vocabulary or a sign that the philosophical claim was murkier than it looked.

Two constraints on the answer. First, keep what is settled in the literature seperate from what people who work on this actually fight about; don't present a contested reading as the consensus. Second, format: exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction and no conclusion. Make each item a mapping point plus what breaks in that mapping, so the five add up to the full verdict.
