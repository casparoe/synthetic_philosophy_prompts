Here's an asymmetry I keep feeling and want to know if I can keep. If someone poisons the wasp nest on their porch because wasps keep coming in the kitchen, no one gives it a second thought. If the same person sets out glue traps that leave mice to die slowly, almost everyone calls it cruel. The obvious justification is that mice are much more likely to be sentient than wasps, so the mouse's expected suffering is orders of magnitude higher. But I'm not sure that justification survives the scale argument.

The scale argument goes like this: you don't need a high probability that an insect is sentient. Suppose you honestly put the probabilty that a wasp can suffer at 1%. For any single wasp that's a small expected harm. But once you multiply a small chance of sentience by enormous numbers — the whole nest, all the nests, the insects affected by ordinary agriculture at global scale — the expected amount of sentient suffering can come out enormous, maybe comparable to or bigger than what's at stake in the mouse case. So "wasps probably don't feel anything" may not be enough to make the asymmetry come out right.

I don't want the usual "be nice to insects" hand-wringing. I want to know whether there's a principle that lets a thoughtful person keep the wasp/mouse asymmetry while fully accepting the expected-value arithmetic, and whether that principle survives nearby cases.

Please structure it like this:
1. "The asymmetry, sharpened" — restate the two cases so the common judgment looks as reasonable as possible, and flag the feature doing the work (~150 words).
2. "The best case for keeping it" — the strongest candidate principle that generates the asymmetry (I can think of a few: differences in cognitive capacity, in the unity of a mind, in what a good life requires, in the kind of death involved). Defend the best one, not all of them (~250 words).
3. "Where it breaks" — test the principle against the scale argument and at least two nearby cases, e.g. a single wasp vs a single mouse, a million mayflies vs one dog, and wild insects dying in a drought vs crop pests we created (~300 words).
4. "My verdict" — say whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or given up, and state what empirical fact would change your mind (~150 words).
