I have been running a working group on vagueness, and the debate between supervaluationism and epistemicism has settled into a pattern I can predict before each session opens. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict on which side is right. The two accounts are engineered to match the same surface data: 'heap' applies clearly at one end, clearly fails at the other, and in the middle speakers refuse both assertion and denial; borderline predicates enter into penumbral connections; and no speaker can identify the grain at which the heap/non-heap boundary falls. Both save the classical consequence relation. The supervaluationist does so by holding that a sentence is true when true on every admissible sharpening and false when false on every one, so excluded middle survives while bivalence is surrendered in the borderline region. The epistemicist keeps bivalence and says there is one boundary, fixed by what the predicate means together with the non-semantic facts, and that we cannot know where it falls because knowledge requires a margin for error across cases that are alike in our dispositions. The recurring impasse is always the same. The supervaluationist argues that no fact about use could select one grain over the next, so the epistemicist has posited an unearned semantic boundary. The epistemicist argues that the supervaluationist's operator for the clear cases either draws precisely the sharp line the view was meant to avoid or launches an unending hierarchy of higher-order vagueness, and that truth-value gaps merely bury the boundary in the semantics instead of removing it. Each side treats its own invisible residue as an acceptable price and the other's as a reductio. My working hypothesis is that this is not a dispute about how speakers actually talk and not a merely verbal dispute about the word 'vague': it is a dispute about the order of explanatory debts, whether semantic content may outrun use, or whether an account of content must be built from use and dispositions before anything else is posited. But I suspect that hypothesis just restates the impasse. Is the disagreement better classified as metaphysical, epistemic, or methodological, or as two different projects sharing a heading? Is there a suppressed formal or empirical question doing the work? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result one side cannot produce, an experimental finding, a distinction that splits the question, or an argument that no movement should be expected?
