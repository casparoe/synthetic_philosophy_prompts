Consider this case. Two agents face a one-shot, symmetric Prisoner's Dilemma. The payoffs are the usual ones: each does best by defecting against cooperation, each does worst by cooperating against defection, and mutual cooperation is better for both than mutual defection. Now add the following twist: the two agents are perfect reasoning twins. They have exactly the same information, exactly the same preferences, and exactly the same decision procedure, and each knows all of this. They are therefore functionally guaranteed to choose the same action; the only outcomes available to them are the diagonal ones, mutual cooperation or mutual defection.

The theory I find compelling runs like this. Defection strictly dominates cooperation: whatever the other player does, I get a higher payoff by defecting than by cooperating. Since my choice does not causally influence the other player's simultaneous choice, I should hold the other player's action fixed and choose the action that leaves me better off in every case. Dominance reasoning has the systematic virtues of simplicity and generality, and it gives a tidy decision procedure that avoids speculation about the other player's inner workings. On this view, the rational action in the one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma is the same whether the other player is my twin or a stranger: defect.

But I have a firm intuition that this is the wrong verdict in the twin case. If my action and my twin's action are locked together, then by defecting I am, in effect, choosing the mutual-defection outcome, while by cooperating I am choosing the mutual-cooperation outcome. Since mutual cooperation is strictly better for me than mutual defection, cooperating seems clearly better for me in the only sense that matters. I cannot regard defection as rational when the structure of the case guarantees that defection lands me in the worse of the only two outcomes that can actually occur. Deciding by dominance here feels like evaluating my options against impossible states of the world: a world in which I defect while my twin cooperates, or cooperate while my twin defects, is not genuinely available.
