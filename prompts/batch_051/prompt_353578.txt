Approach Roko's basilisk as a decision-theoretic argument rather than as internet folklore. The core claim is that a future superintelligent AI with an interest in having been built could credibly threaten to punish people who knew of it but failed to help bring it about, and that because it can predict or infer your present decision, the threat is supposed to operate acausally: it would be rational now to take costly steps — donating, promoting the project — even though no such AI yet exists. What is your actual credence that this argument is rationally compelling for a present-day agent, in the sense that in at least some specified version of the scenario the costly action is the rational choice? Give a number and decompose it into exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state your overall credence and show how the later numbers combine into it. Items 2 through 4 should give probabilities for the main sub-questions — for instance, whether a decision theory of the functional/timeless variety is correct or applicable here, whether a future AI would have both the incentive and the technical capacity to carry out the punishment and condition it on your decision, whether the threat survives diagnosis as a Pascal's mugging, and whatever further terms you judge are doing the main work. Item 5 should state how confident you are in the decomposition overall, identify the single sub-question you are least sure of, and say what evidence or argument would move it most.
