Lately I've been defending a pretty spare view of well-being, mostly because nothing else gives me a tidy decison procedure. Roughly: a life goes well to the extent that it contains positive experiences or satisfied desires (I go back and forth between the two, but for this problem they have the same shape). Novelty only ever matters instrumentally: it offsets hedonic adaptation, so variety is valuable only because it keeps the running average up. The view is simple, it unifies a lot, and it gives a clean answer in hard cases — just look at the total. And yet I have a genuine, somewhat embarassing fear of living a boring life, even after I've done the arithmetic and the arithmetic says a boring life could be fine.

Here's the clash. Stipulate two lives with equal total pleasant experience, equal memory quality, no adaptation, no FOMO, no difference in narrative meaning — all the usual confounds removed. Life A is a single perfect day, repeated forever. Life B is a ceaseless run of different good days. I still find myself judging that B is better. Not because the repetition would eventually feel dull; I already stipulated that it doesn't. It feels like value is partly a property of the sequence, the temporally extended shape of a life, not just a sum over moments — the difference between a melody and the same note held for the same total duration. But if I'm being honest, that "shape" talk sounds exactly like the kind of mysterious structural good my spare view is supposed to avoid.

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want the strongest version of each move. What would it take to debunk the intuition — is it just an information-seeking adaptation firing inappropriately, a scope-insensitivity artifact, a heuristic for "diversify because the future is uncertain" that misleads in a stipulated case? What would it take to amend the theory instead — add a standing desire for novelty, make value a property of whole lives rather than moments, admit a structural good? And is there a reading where both survive: the theory keeps its arithmetic and the intuition is still tracking something real? Walk me through the costs of each, and tell me which one you'd actually give up, and what evidence would make you switch.
