Consider the discursive dilemma in its standard three-person form. A committee of three judges two premises, P and Q, and a conclusion R that is the conjunction P∧Q. Each member holds an internally consistent binary position. In the familiar profile — (P, Q, R), (P, ¬Q, ¬R), (¬P, Q, ¬R) — majority voting on each proposition separately accepts P and Q but rejects R, so the committee's aggregated position is logically inconsistent even though every member's own position is consistent.

Now change the case as follows. Instead of binary accept/reject judgments, each member reports a real-valued credence c(P), c(Q), c(R) in [0,1]. Each member is probabilistically coherent and treats the two premises as independent, so that c(R) = c(P)c(Q). The committee's collective credence for any proposition is the unweighted arithmetic mean of the three members' credences, and the committee accepts a proposition just in case that mean is strictly greater than 1/2.

Suppose the three members report:

Member 1: c(P) = 0.9, c(Q) = 0.9, c(R) = 0.81.
Member 2: c(P) = 0.9, c(Q) = 0.1, c(R) = 0.09.
Member 3: c(P) = 0.1, c(Q) = 0.9, c(R) = 0.09.

Answer the following.

(a) Compute the committee's mean credences for P, Q, and R and the resulting accept/reject verdicts. Does the discursive dilemma survive this variation?

(b) Is it still correct to describe the committee's collective state as inconsistent? Distinguish carefully between logical inconsistency of the accepted propositions, minimal probabilistic coherence of the three aggregate credences, and preservation of the independence rule each member used. What credence would the aggregate premises jointly support for R if the committee combined its mean premise credences in the way each member does?

(c) Identify the formal feature of arithmetic averaging that produces the gap between the aggregate conclusion credence and the conclusion credence implied by the aggregate premises, and say whether the acceptance threshold of 1/2 is doing essential work or merely making the gap visible.

Please write your answer in British English.
