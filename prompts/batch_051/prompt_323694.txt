After a long argument with a friend about whether you can justify sitting out a presidential election in a state that's obviously not going to be close — or casting a third-party protest vote in it — he kept coming back to the same analogy: voting is like recycling. One bottle doesn't make a measurable difference to the environment, and one vote almost never changes an election result, but if everyone reasoned that way nobody would recycle and nobody would vote, so the real question isn't weather your individual act tips anything, it's whether you're doing your part in a practice that only works when enough people take part. He said that makes my whole objection just a free-rider excuse. I pushed back that elections and recycling aggregate totally differently, that a vote doesn't have the same kind of causal footprint as a bottle, and that abstaining can itself mean something in a way not taking your can to the bin doesn't. We went in circles on whether those differences are fatal or beside the point. Can you run the analogy all the way down for us: what genuinely maps from recycling to voting, what the recycling picture quietly imports into voting that isn't there, where the mapping breaks, which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion he wants, and whether the conclusion itself — that you should vote anyway, and that staying home in a safe state is just free-riding — survives once the disanalogies are on the table? Give me a committed verdict, not a tour of the options: lead with your bottom line and then walk through the reasoning.
