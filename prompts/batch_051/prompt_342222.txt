Give me a working definition of famine that correctly classifies the following cases: (1) a crop failure in a well-governed country that causes hardship but little or no excess mortality because relief arrives promptly; (2) the Irish potato blight of 1845–49, in which the staple crop failed while grain and livestock continued to be exported under colonial policy; (3) the 1943 Bengal famine, in which per-capita food availability was not unusually low but wartime inflation and hoarding collapsed the purchasing power of rural laborers; (4) the Great Leap Forward mortality, driven less by harvest failure than by inflated procurement quotas and the disruption of collective agriculture; and (5) a siege or blockade that starves a city while food sits just outside it. I suspect the word is doing two different jobs, one about food supply and one about who can command food, and that the second is what separates a natural disaster from a political crime. Give the two definitions, run each through all five cases, and say which cases force the distinction and where your verdicts feel least secure.
