I maintain a moderately popular open-source library with three other engineers, and we keep hitting a recurring dispute that we have no settled procedure for. A user or a small company will have consumed the library heavily for months—sometimes building a commercial product on it—while never contributing code, documentation, bug reports, or funding, and yet will expect prompt support, influence over the roadmap, or special accommodation. One maintainer says this is only a real problem if the behavior would break the project were everyone to do it, and that until then the fairness complaint is just resentment. Another says the moral line turns on the difference between merely benefiting from a public good and actively accepting or relying on its benefits in a way that looks like taking on a reciprocal obligation, and that this distinction is standard in philosophical arguments about free riding but he cannot operationalize it for our monthly decisions. A third wants to reserve the word "parasitism" for cases where someone's consumption actively extracts something from the project—labor, attention, social capital—rather than merely failing to add to it, but he cannot say how to tell extraction from mere consumption in practice.

I want this turned into a reusable decision procedure for the four of us. The procedure should let us take a particular non-contributor, run through the steps in about twenty minutes, and reach both a classification (objectionable free riding, extraction, or acceptable use) and a proportionate response (leave them alone, ask privately for contributions, limit free support, or restrict access/revoke something). Constraints: none of us has formal philosophy training, the decision has to be defensible in a public issue thread, and two maintainers must sign off on any response beyond leaving the person alone. It should handle at least these hard cases: a startup that depends on the library commercially but is currently too small to contribute; a beginner who files noisy or low-quality issues but contributes no code; and another maintainer who regularly takes patches without attribution. Give me exactly seven numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Let items 1–4 be the steps of the procedure in order, items 5–6 be two worked applications (one of which must be a case where the procedure tells us to tolerate someone we would instinctively want to ban), and item 7 be the sharpest limitation of the procedure.
