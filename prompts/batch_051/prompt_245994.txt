I got into an argument with a friend about inheritance taxes last week, and we both kept circling Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain thought experiment: a million people freely pay twenty-five cents each to watch Wilt play, he ends up with a huge pile of money, and Nozick argues that any effort to redistribute it in line with some preferred pattern would require blocking or overturning at least one free transfer. My friend said the logic obviously extends to inheritence. If Wilt came by the money justly and can freely give it to whoever he wants, he can give it to his kids, and they can invest it and pass it on, so an inheritance tax would have to condemn a chain where every individual link looks as uncoerced as the original ticket sales. I could feel something changing once the example got stretched across generations, but I couldn't pin down where in the entitlement story it actually gives way.

Here's the version I want to work through. Suppose Wilt leaves his whole fortune to his daughter in a dynastic trust: each heir receives a generous fixed annual income from the trust, the principal is professionally managed and legally protected, and each heir is legally bound to pass the principal intact to their own children, generation after generation. No heir is coerced and no one can dissolve the trust. After six generations the Chamberlain descendants own a large share of the housing stock in the city where Wilt once played, have never had to work, and every rent check they receive traces back, link by link, to those original quarter admissions. Run Nozick's entitlement theory carefully on this case. Does it license the dynasty all the way down, since each step is an uncoerced transfer and the original acquisition was just? Or does one of the three principles — justice in acquisition, justice in transfer, rectification of past injustice — have to be revised once bequests can iterate indefinitely? And can an inheritance tax reject the dynasty without claiming that any single link in the chain was unjust? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
