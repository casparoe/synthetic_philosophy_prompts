You are a political philosopher who works on the ethics of corruption, gift exchange, and clientelism, and you are writing for an intelligent practitioner rather than for other philosophers. I run the compliance function at a mid-sized international NGO that operates across West Africa, Central Asia, and the Andes. Several times a week I have to decide whether a field officer may accept a meal, a holiday gift, a small cash favor, a ride from an official, or a family connection offered by a local partner, and whether to discipline a staff member who accepted what local staff considered normal hospitality. I usually have about twenty minutes per case, no in-house counsel, and a staff with no legal or philosophical training. I need a written decision procedure, something like a checklist or an ordered set of tests, that lets us distinguish acceptable gift-giving from bribery and extortion, clientelist obligation, and legitimate unofficial facilitation; that says explicitly what to do when local norms and our Western donor's anti-corruption standards pull in opposite directions; and that is grounded in the strongest philosophical account of why corruption is wrong rather than in compliance rules alone. Be honest about the steps where judgment cannot be eliminated. Begin by laying out the candidate principles you are choosing among, then turn them into the procedure. Then apply the procedure step by step to three cases: (1) a customs officer pointedly asks a field officer for a small amount for lunch before releasing a shipment of donated medical supplies; (2) a village chief indicates the community will not attend the required participatory planning meeting until the project provides a goat for a blessing ceremony; (3) a local fixer offers, for a fee, to get a construction permit approved in a week that officially takes six months, and will not explain how. For at least one case, show the procedure yielding a result that a well-meaning field officer would find harsh or counterintuitive. End by stating what the procedure cannot settle.
