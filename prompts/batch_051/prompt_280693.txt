I'm a reinforcement learning person — I work with POMDPs, stochastic policies, reward shaping, multi-agent model uncertainty, the usual toolbox — and I keep seeing deterrence theory describe nuclear brinkmanship, the credibility of threats, and the stability-instability paradox in terms like commitment, the balance of terror, and deliberately leaving things partly to chance. It looks to me like it should all restate in my vocabulary, but every time I try the translation, something slips.

Take these four claims: (1) the stability-instability paradox, that making the big war too catastrophic to fight can make smaller, lower-level fights more likely or more usable; (2) the credibility problem, that a rational actor may not actually execute a massive retaliation after the fact, so the threat has to be made credible before the fact; (3) the idea that a threat can be kept effective by deliberately leaving the final decision partly to chance, so not even the threatening side knows for sure whether they will respond; and (4) the claim that being able to manipulate risk — the probability that things escalate out of control — can substitute for genuinely being willing to fight.

Restate them one by one in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, the way I'd write them as POMDPs, policies, randomized action probabilities, commitment mechanisms, and uncertainty about the other player's reward. Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge to make it fit, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In particular, is deliberately leaving it to chance just a stochastic policy with a temperature parameter plus a commitment device, or is there something in that idea my framework cannot represent? And does the stability-instability paradox become precise in my terms, or does it start to look like an observation about a particular reward function rather than a result about the structure of the game? Don't paper over the gaps: I'd rather find out my vocabulary is missing something then be told it's all just RL.
