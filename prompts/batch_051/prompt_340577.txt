I'm a software engineer, and I keep coming back to a structural resemblance I can't decide is genuine or just a verbal coincidence.

In programming-language semantics there is a way to say two program fragments "behave the same" without a specification of what the program is supposed to do: contextual, or observational, equivalence. Two expressions are equivalent iff, inside any enclosing program, swapping one for the other never changes the program's observable behavior. Instead of consulting a single client, you quantify over all possible clients — the contexts. But the definition still has a free parameter: what counts as observable (termination? output? errors? timing?), and different choices yield different equivalence relations.

Recently I've been reading about formal AI alignment, particularly attempts to define "low impact" or "side effect avoidance" without a full account of human value. One family of proposals — I believe it's called attainable utility preservation, or something near that — doesn't measure harm against known values. It checks whether an agent's action preserves the world's ability to serve a broad distribution of possible goals: relative to some baseline (often inaction), the action is penalized to the extent that it shrinks how much value could still be obtained for those goals, compared with what the baseline would have allowed. That looks like the same move: replace the unknown "what matters" with a universal quantifier over possible clients (goals rather than contexts), and define the safe action as the one that looks like a no-op to all of them.

I can even fill out the mapping: possible goals ↔ enclosing contexts; retained ability to optimize a goal ↔ observable behavior; low impact ↔ equivalence to doing nothing. But being able to line up the boxes doesn't mean the structure underneath is the same.

So: is this a genuine identity of structure, or am I pattern-matching on the word "side effect"? What would have to be true on both sides for theorems to transfer? In particular, in PL semantics the observation criterion isn't intrinsic — changing it changes what counts as equivalent — and contextual equivalence has known pathologies (undecidability, full abstraction, distinctions visible only to exotic observers). Is the choice of a goal distribution in impact measures the same kind of free parameter, and do those pathologies have counterparts in the alignment setting? And is there a concrete result that the parallel would predict but that fails — something that would settle whether this is deep or superficial?
