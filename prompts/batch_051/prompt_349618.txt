I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether any AI we're likely to build soon could have a welfare, and its making me realize I don't actually have a clear test. I settled on four criteria for being the kind of thing that can be benefited or harmed: (1) there's some state variable that is genuinely better or worse for the system itself, not just for its designers — something like comfort, satisfaction, frustration, distress; (2) the system's own decision-making tracks that variable flexibly, trading it off against other goals rather than running a fixed reflex; (3) the variable is integrated into one point of view, so there's a single "it" that is doing better or worse rather than scattered subroutines; and (4) the system persists enough that being better or worse off can attach to a subject over time, not just a momentary blip.

The case I can't decide about is a warehouse robot a friend's company is prototyping. To teach it not to damage itself or the merchandise, the engineers gave it a learned "discomfort" signal that spikes when it bumps shelves or runs its battery hot; the control policy is trained to minimize that signal, not the raw physical damage. Weirdly, it starts doing things they didn't explicitly script: it slows down when the signal is running high, takes longer detours, sometimes pauses near the dock, and appears to accept small immediate discomfort to avoid larger predicted discomfort later. But it has no long-term memory between shifts (same weights restart each morning), there are forty units running the same policy while accumulating different local tweaks, and the discomfort signal is computed by a separate module that the main policy just receives as another input. One engineer calls it a lobster flinch; another says it's just a cost function with extra steps. Meanwhile the safety team wants to use the same kind of negative-reinforcement signal in a surgical robot and in a foundation-model agent to suppress dangerous outputs, and if the thing is genuinely worse off when the signal runs high, then "train it safely" and "don't make it suffer" might be in real tension.

On my four criteria, is the warehouse robot a welfare subject? Go criterion by criterion, flag the places where my criteria underdetermine the verdict, and say whether this case should make me revise the criteria themselves. I care more about where I'm on shaky ground than about a clean yes/no — so if you think "welfare subject" is doing too much work or my criteria quietly smuggle in a conclusion, tell me to back up and say it plainly rather than playing along.
