Ok so I'm not an economist or anything, but I've been going down a rabbit hole about whether a planned economy could work, and I think people keep talking past eachother because theyre actually turning different dials. I came up with a way to seperate the whole debate, I call it the four dials of economic calculation.

Here are the four dials:

1. The possibility dial. Whether a planner could even in principle get the numbers right without real market prices. Like, if you gave the planner infinite time and a perfect computer, is there still something about prices that just can't be computed from the center at all?

2. The speed dial. Whether the planner can change prices fast enough when a factory breaks or a new fad hits, instead of always being a few months behind what people would have actually paid.

3. The taste dial. Whether the planner can know what people really want and which local stuff matters (like which worker is lying about how fast a machine runs) without those people being able to put their money where their mouth is.

4. The blame dial. Whether you can have a working economy if nobody personally eats the cost of a bad guess. In a market, if I overestimate demand for purple sneakers, I lose my savings. In a planned economy, the planning board just writes the shortage off as a learning experience.

My actual theory is that the first three dials are kind of a distraction and the blame dial is the whole game. Even if a planner had god-level knowledge of everyone's real wants and every factory's real limits, the prices it announced would still be flimsy, because there is no one whose own money is on the line when theyre wrong. A price is only honest when someone bleeds for it. So the calculation problem isnt really an information problem, it's a who-gets-punished problem, and all the stuff Hayek said about little bits of local knowledge is just one symptom of that deeper thing.

I know there are existing positions roughly like Mises saying it's impossible in principle, Lange saying the central board can just trial-and-error its way to real prices, and Hayek being the information guy. But I think my four dials thing does something those dont, because it says the debate was never really about calculation, it was about loss.

Is this coherent? Honestly: do any of my dials collapse into each other? Is the blame dial just some incentive stuff that economists already know about but I've dressed up in a new metaphor? Am I being unfair to Hayek or Mises? And where does the theory actually break if you push on it?
