So I keep seeing hedonism get dismissed in rationalist-adjacent comment sections with the claim that "pleasure" isn't one thing at all — that the pleasure of scratching a bad itch, solving a crossword clue, the warm rush after a run, relief when a migraine breaks, and the weird faint glow of schadenfreude don't have any single felt quality in common. The anti-hedonist line is usually that hedonism needs a single hedonic currency and the phenomenology just refuses to be monist. I want to know whether that's actually an empirical question rather than just an armchair intuition.

Suppose someone made the specific claim: there is a common felt component — call it positive hedonic tone — present in all and only pleasures, the thing that makes them count as pleasures. How would you actually test that? I mean concretely: what would you measure, in whom, with what controls? For instance, could you do similarity-rating studies where people rate how alike the pleasure of a foot rub is to the pleasure of being right about a hunch, and see whether those fall into distinct clusters vs. all sharing some factor? Could you look for a shared neural signature (e.g., a common hedonic hotspot) and would finding one show anything about felt quality rather than just a shared output channel? Does the wanting/liking dissociation in habit and addiction already tell against "liking" being the common core, or is that orthogonal? And where does relief from pain fit, since it feels pretty different from reward?

I want a proposed study or battery of studies, with enough detail that I could picture preregistering them, then an honest breakdown: what result would count for the common-tone view, what would count against, and what residue no amount of data could settle (like whether hedonism as an ethical theory stands or falls on it, or whether a deflationary view where "pleasure" is just a family-resemblance term is really a rival empirical hypothesis at all).

Also: keep the settled vs. contested line seperate. I know there's real psych literature behind parts of this, so don't present some niche disputed interpretation as "the" consensus position; tell me what's fairly robust, what's one reading among several, and what you're just inferring.
