I am a novelist, not a philosopher, and I have spent years carrying around a working definition of art that I use privately to explain how some made things allow one to speak truthfully by inventing everything. I call it the counterfactual-capture view. The claim is this: something is a work of art just in case it is framed — either by its maker or by its later placement — so that its primary invitation is to enter an as-if in the first person, to inhabit a possibility while knowing the terms are staged, and to let that excursion reorganize some part of one's actual seeing, feeling, or judging. A novel is the easy case: the printed book and the label "fiction" do not hide the falsehood; they license it, converting invention into a simulation of a life one will not live. A song can be art even when no world is described, because it lets one rehearse an emotion for which one has not yet had cause. A proof does not count: it insists on its conclusion and cannot be shrugged off as optional, however beautiful it is. A bridge does not count: it carries its load whether or not one enters its fiction. A landscape may produce a similar inner shift, but no one framed it as a possibility, so on this view it remains aesthetic rather than artistic.

The hard cases are where I most want correction. An abstract canvas or a piece of pure instrumental music seems to lack the "possibility" the view requires, and my patch has been to say that what is simulated there is not a world but a manner of attending — a shape that feeling could take, tried on for its own sake. I also waver on found objects: a piece of driftwood set on a plinth appears to become art by a change of address rather than by any act of making, which would mean the frame can be retroactive and perhaps not the artist's own. I would like an honest assessment rather than encouragement: is counterfactual capture a real distinction or a metaphor doing all the work; is it already abroad under another description I should know; and where does it break — what does it count as art that it should not, or exclude that it should not?

I ask, too, that you mark your own confidence and say what would change your mind: which example, objection, or competing account would most likely force you to revise your verdict, and where your reasoning is least secure.
