I work as a control systems engineer, and the concepts I trust most are drawn from stability analysis, feedback, disturbance rejection, observability, and the certification of safety-critical systems. I have spent some time reading arguments that frontier AI development should be paused or sharply decelerated, and I keep sensing that beneath the distinctive language of extinction risk, recursive self-improvement, race dynamics among labs, and alignment failures there is a case that ought to be expressible in my own idiom: we are being asked to operate and scale a plant whose dynamics are poorly identified, under incentive feedback that may be destabilizing, with evaluation regimes that give us little observability into the relevant internal states, and without an accepted way of certifying stability before deployment. I would like you to attempt a systematic, honest translation of the strongest version of the pause argument into the vocabulary of control theory and safety engineering. Take each major component—the concern about capability growth and recursive self-improvement, the difficulty of alignment and interpretability, the pressure of competitive deployment, the limits of pre-deployment evaluation, and the alleged irreversibility of the relevant failures—and say for each whether it translates cleanly, translates only with distortion, or fails to translate at all. By honest I mean that I do not want loose metaphor-matching, and I do not want the assumptions of control theory smuggled in and then presented as the argument itself.

Then give me the residue report. After this translation, which elements of the pause argument have no genuine counterpart in a control-theoretic picture? Is that because my framework is too narrow to represent them—because it has no natural place for them—or because those elements turn out to be doing less conceptual work once the engineering gloss is removed? I am not asking for your verdict on whether a pause or slowdown is in fact justified; I am asking which premises carry weight when the argument is restated in terms I can audit, and whether the untranslatable remainder should make me more suspicious of the argument or of my own vocabulary.
