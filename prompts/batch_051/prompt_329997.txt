I am drafting a short position statement on the ethics of work and leisure for a workshop, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit of my commitments rather than a lecture on the literature. My stated commitments are these:

(1) The Protestant work ethic, once detached from its original theological scaffolding, still captures something true: there is a distinctive intrinsic good in disciplined work within a calling, so that work is not merely an instrumental cost borne for the sake of consumption, and its value is not exhausted by its output.

(2) A society that cannot offer meaningful work to those willing and able to do it has failed its members in a way that income support alone does not remedy.

(3) Karoshi is not the full flowering of a healthy work ethic but a pathology of it: a culture in which people work themselves to death has a structurally defective culture of work, not merely an accident of long hours.

(4) Legally mandated reduction of working time, of which the French 35-hour week is the familiar model, is on balance sound policy.

(5) Leisure is not best understood as recovery from, or preparation for, work. A life in which leisure had only recuperative value would be impoverished, because play, contemplation, friendship, and aesthetic activity are intrinsic goods.

(6) The state ought not to legislate a particular conception of the good life, including a particular ideal of the work-leisure balance; it should remain roughly neutral among reasonable conceptions of how to live.

Please go through these six commitments pairwise and tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is, ranking the genuine tensions by severity. Pay particular attention to whether (1), on its own, gives me the resources to distinguish a calling worth pursuing from the overwork culture that produces karoshi, and whether (6) can be squared with the diagnosis in (3) and the policy in (4). For each genuine tension, say what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to restore consistency with the least collateral damage.

When you answer, please also state how confident you are in each part of the audit, and identify the claim in your audit that you are least sure of.
