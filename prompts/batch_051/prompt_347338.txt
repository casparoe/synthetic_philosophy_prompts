I've been trying to work out the real lesson of Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise. As I understand it, the Tortoise shows that stating modus ponens as an additional premise does nothing, because someone who accepts the premises can still refuse to draw the conclusion and can demand yet another premise to license that move, generating an infinite regress. But if that is the correct reading, doesn't it follow that deduction can never be rationally justified to a person who does not already go along with it—and that logic, at bottom, rests on something non-rational such as habit, convention, or faith? If so, what becomes of the idea that a valid argument gives anyone a reason to accept its conclusion?
