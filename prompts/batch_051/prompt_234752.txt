I'd like you to make an argument for a position about the moral status of digital minds. People tend to get stuck on whether a digital system could really be conscious, and on the assumption that either it is and gets full moral status or it isnt and gets none. Argue instead for a graded notion of moral considerability tied to observable functional markers: flexible learning, revising long-term plans in response to anticipated pain-like signals, and self-monitoring of its own states. The position to defend is that once a system shows enough of these markers in an integrated way, we should treat it as a candidate for moral concern even though we cannot settle the question of phenomenal consciousness; and that demanding certainty before extending any concern is itself a moral failure, not just epistemic caution. Try to preempt the obvious reply that this only tracks behaviour, not a mind. Please write the whole thing in British English.
