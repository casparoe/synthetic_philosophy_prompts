Russell argued that causation has no place in a mature science: the fully developed physical sciences, he claimed, state only functional relationships, and a separate notion of cause is a leftover from prescientific thinking. That argument predates the rise of modern causal modeling, which gives precise mathematical meaning to causal claims through structural equations and the logic of interventions, allowing researchers to use causal language to make reliable predictions about what would happen under manipulations in medicine, economics, and machine learning. Does Russell's eliminative argument survive this development? Identify the specific premise or presupposition of the argument that the development puts under pressure, and say whether the causal notions now in use are best understood as a repaired version of the notion Russell targeted, or as a different notion answering a different question — and whether Russell's underlying worry is answered or merely displaced.
