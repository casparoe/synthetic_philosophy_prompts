Lewis's Principal Principle says that a rational agent who knows the objective chance of A at time t to be x, and has no inadmissible information, should assign credence x to A. The principle appears to require that there is a single objective chance to defer to. Now suppose the fundamental dynamics is deterministic, and probabilities enter only through coarse-grained statistical-mechanical descriptions of the world; since coarse-graining is not unique, two equally respectable descriptions can assign different probabilities to the same event. Does the Principal Principle survive in this setting? Say which premise the development puts under pressure, whether the principle can be repaired in a weaker form that still constrains credence, or whether deference to chance should be replaced by something else entirely.
