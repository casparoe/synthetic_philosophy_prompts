I sit on the education committee of a Holocaust museum, and we're deadlocked about whether to approve a proposed temporary exhibition that would put the Holocaust next to the Armenian genocide and the Rwandan genocide in one narrative built around modern, bureaucratic mass killing. The case for it is that it leans on the Bauman-style point we already make in our permanent galleries: that the Holocaust wasn't a relapse into premodern barbarism but something made possible by modern administration, record-keeping, division of labour, and the distance between killers and victims. On that view, comparison isn't "all genocides are the same" — it's the very thing that lets you state what was specificaly modern about the Holocaust. The case against is a mix of board members who think any side-by-side display, however carefully framed, hands the relativizers a gift ("everyone has their genocide, nothing special about Germany"), and members who point out that Rwanda actually weakens the bureaucratic-modernity thesis, since an awful lot of that killing was done up close with machetes by neighbours who knew their victims. We meet Thursday, and I need to circulate a short written opinion (~1,200 words) that makes a concrete recommendation: approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a review date. It should take the strongest version of the losing position seriously rather than knock down a straw man, and it should give the committee something it can actually vote on, with a time frame. At the end, add a short paragraph saying which single consideration, if it turned out differently, would most likely flip the recommendation, and where the memo's reasoning is shakiest.
