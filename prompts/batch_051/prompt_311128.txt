I'm in an upper-level democratic theory course, and we've been doing epistocracy vs. democracy. I'm trying to organize the objections to epistocracy, but I keep feeling like their all circling the same two or three ideas. Here's what I've collected so far: (1) the demographic objection — any competence test will end up excluding poor, less educated, and minority citizens, so it cant be neutral; (2) the who-chooses-the-choosers problem — voters who can't pick good policies also can't be trusted to pick good epistocrats; (3) the equal-standing objection — being denied an equal vote is an expressive injury independently of outcomes; (4) the expert-disagreement objection — on most contested political questions there's no single expert consensus to defer to; (5) the historical track-record objection — literacy tests and similar restrictions were already used to disenfranchise people, so these institutions get captured; (6) the values-vs-facts objection — most of politics is about interests and values rather than technical competence, so there's no relevant expertise; (7) the aggregation objection — individually ignorant voters can track the public interest through shortcuts and aggregation, so claims about democratic incompetence are overstated.

Most of these are fairness objections (1, 3, 5) or feasibility objections (2, 4, 7), and (6) just denies that there's political expertise in the first place. What I want is an objection that attacks the competence principle itself — the claim that it's okay in principle to weight votes by political knowledge — on its own terms. To be clear, the objection should grant for the sake of argument that the demographic problem could be solved, that there are relevant political facts, and that some citizens know far more of them than others, and still say the principle is wrong.

Pretend you're a political philosopher who works on democratic theory. Think it through carefully: brainstorm a few candidate objections, discard anything that's just a variant of (1) through (7), and then give me exactly five objections I'm missing, numbered 1 to 5, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion. For each, say in a few words what makes it different from everything on my list and whether it's actually any good.
