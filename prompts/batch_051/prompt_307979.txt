I read somewhere about the old dilemma for defining "physical," and I can't tell whether it's a real problem or a verbal trick. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) Physicalism says that everything is physical.
(2) The word "physical" has to mean either "described by current physics" or "described by an ideal, completed future physics" — there are no other options on the table.
(3) If it means current physics, physicalism is almost certainly false, since current physics is incomplete and its posits keep getting revised.
(4) If it means ideal future physics, physicalism is empty: nobody can say what that physics will include, so "everything is physical" stops being a claim that could turn out false.
(5) Therefore physicalism is either false or empty.

I suspect the sleight of hand is in (2), but I can't make it stick. Is the argument valid? Are the premises true?
