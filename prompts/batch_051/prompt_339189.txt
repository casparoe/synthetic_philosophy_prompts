I teach a sixteen-student upper-level seminar on decision theory, with an eighty-minute session set aside for the absent-minded driver problem. The puzzle became personally vivid for me recently when I drove home from a late flight on an unfamiliar toll road and genuinely could not remember whether I had already passed the first of the two exits before mine; the plan that had been obvious in advance suddenly looked different from the inside. I want an activity that gives students that same experience before I ever name the problem. Please design a classroom game around the standard case: a driver reaches each of two exits without remembering which one it is; exiting at the first exit pays 0, exiting at the second pays 4, and continuing past both pays 1. Students should play both the planner choosing a strategy in advance and the driver deciding at an exit, across several rounds, so that they discover for themselves the tension between ex ante reasoning and the reasoning available at the moment of choice. Include rules and roles, a round-by-round timeline, a payoff scheme requiring no real money, and a debrief that maps the rounds onto ex ante versus interim reasoning and the driver's beliefs about which exit he is at. Please also say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the paradox the game cannot reproduce on its own.
