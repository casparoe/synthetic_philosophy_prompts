In my school's philosophy club we did the dust speck thing: would you rather one person be tortured for a year, or a basically infinite number of people each get one speck of dust in their eye for a second. Everyone, me included, says obviously the dust specks—it feels like no amount of little annoyances can add up to one real torture. But then I changed the small harm from a dust speck to a paper cut, then a wasp sting, then a bad migraine, and the same kids start flipping. With migraines, alot of them say yeah, if there's enough of them, that probably is worse than one torture, and they start doing the utilitarian math. So there's some line between 'too small to ever add up' and 'big enough that it can add up,' and nobody can say where it is. I definately don't think that line can be just, like, vibes. Is there a principle that actually puts a paper cut, a wasp sting, and a migraine on the right side of it? Or is the dust speck intuition just a failure to keep multiplying, and we should give it up?
