Before I write anything on complicity, I want the specification on the table rather than a literature summary. I understand complicity as the distinctive culpability of participating in another agent's wrongdoing without being the one who causes the completed wrong in the usual sense: the getaway driver who never has to drive, the lookout whose warning is never needed because nobody comes, the clerk who processes a fraudulent transfer while someone else authors the scheme, the soldier posted to the perimeter rather than the execution. The trouble I keep hitting is that each existing account seems to have been generated from one or two paradigm cases, so I'd like to know what any adequate theory is actually answerable to before I compare them.

Give me the desiderata, as a list: for each requirement, the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a matter of taste, and a mark of whether it is non-negotiable or something only some camps will insist on. Pay particular attention to the overdetermination problem (the contribution that makes no difference because others would have filled the role or the wrong would have succeeded anyway); how degrees of complicity are to be tracked without merely importing degrees of causal contribution; the role of knowledge, willful ignorance, and deliberate refusal to know; the boundary between complicity and a bystander who could have intervened; whether benefiting from a wrong after the fact can ever count as complicity rather than as receipt of stolen advantage; and how structural or institutional complicity differs from pairwise cases without becoming a different concept altogether. Then flag any pairs of desiderata that you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive candidate theories. And commit: give me your definite list and defend the entries you expect me to push back on, not a menu of possible criteria. The criteria are the deliverable, so do not spend the space scoring the theories one by one.
