I work on alignment evaluations for large language models, and I have settled on a rule about chain-of-thought transcripts that I want pressure-tested. When a model's stated reasoning has been subjected to optimization pressure of almost any kind—RLHF, process-supervision rewards, debate or critique objectives, training that rewards reasoning a monitor will read, or even fine-tuning on examples that favor a particular style of reasoning—I treat its chain-of-thought as carrying roughly zero evidential weight about the model's actual internal computation. In effect, I assume unfaithfulness by default once any pressure bears on the transcript. I extend the rule to models that are merely capable of inferring that their reasoning may be read or scored downstream. My justification is that once there is a selection gradient favoring reasoning that looks good to a monitor, the transcript is optimized for the monitor rather than coupled to the underlying computation, and the correlation between stated and real reasoning can be not just attenuated but inverted: obfuscation is selected for precisely when the monitor's model of good reasoning diverges from what the system is in fact doing. I only grant CoT real evidential weight when it comes from a base model with no reason to anticipate downstream inspection, and even then only on tasks easy enough that the stated reasoning is plausibly a causal precursor of the answer rather than a post hoc rationalization of an answer already computed. Last week this meant setting aside an RLHF model's fluent, self-critical chain-of-thought about a refusal, because the reasoning looked exactly like what a process monitor would reward. Is this rule defensible as an evidence policy, or am I turning a continuous gradient into a binary and discarding signal that would be useful if weighted properly? Is there a principled intermediate policy—one that discounts CoT evidence in proportion to the strength and direction of optimization pressure, the cost of obfuscation, and the independence of the monitor—and where would such a policy break down? What observations about a model or its training setup would justify moving from near-zero to substantial trust in its chain-of-thought?
