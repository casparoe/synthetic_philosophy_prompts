The thing I keep coming back to is Harnad's symbol grounding problem. As I understand it, the classic argument is this: if a system's symbols get their meaning only from other symbols, you can never break out of the circle — it's like trying to learn Chinese from a Chinese-Chinese dictionary when you don't know a word of Chinese. A purely symbolic AI, no matter how good it's internal rulebook, would be stuck with syntax but no semantics; the symbols would be meaningless to the system itself.

Here's the development I can't shake. A lot of today's large models are not purely symbolic in that old sense. Some are trained from the start on paired images and language, or on video, audio, and text together, so the symbols are learned jointly with perceptual patterns. A model like CLIP doesn't just get the word 'red' defined by other words; it has a mapping between the token and actual visual redness. So the grounding relation Harnad said was missing looks like it is at least partly there — or is it?

Here's what I'd like from you. First brainstorm about which premise of Harnad's argument this development actually bears on, and whether that premise can be repaired. Then give me your honest verdict: does the symbol grounding argument survive multimodal models, does it need repair, or is it just dead for that case? And if there's a residual problem, say precisely where it lives — is passively learned correlation with pixels enough, or does grounding require something more like doing things in the world, having goals, or being able to check the mapping for yourself? I care less about the technology than about whether the philosophical argument still has teeth.
