I chair the credentials committee for my denomination, and we're hung up on an ordination candidate. His ministerial paper argues that God suffers with the oppressed. His actual line is more careful than that sounds: he says impassibility, properly understood, rules out only involuntary suffering—being acted upon, overwhelmed, or changed against God's will—but that God can freely take creaturely pain into the divine life and suffer out of love, the way you might choose to share a friend's pain. Half the committee thinks he's found a way to keep both the tradition and God's solidarity with the suffering. The other half thinks he's just doing a bait-and-switch on the word impassibility, and that our confessional language about God having no passions pretty plainly rules out what he's describing. We owe the full presbytery a written decision memo: recommend proceeding with ordination, proceeding only with conditions (e.g., he revises or clarifies the paper), or denying. Write that memo. It needs an actionable bottom line, criteria the presbytery can actually apply, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the loosing side.
