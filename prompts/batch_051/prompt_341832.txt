My younger sister was hospitalized this spring for severe depression, and the psychiatrist kept telling our family that her condition is as physical as a broken leg. I realized I could not say what that sentence asserts, what would make it false, or how it differs from the promise that future physiology will eventually explain her symptoms. Since then I have been trying to get clear on what 'physical' could mean in claims of this kind, and I have run into the familiar impasse: if 'physical' means whatever current physics posits, physicalism is almost certainly false; if it means whatever a completed future physics posits, physicalism threatens to become trivial. Before I read any further, I want the specification of what an adequate account of 'the physical' would have to get right.

Write the desiderata: what any account of 'the physical' must deliver for the thesis that everything is physical (the mental included) to be a substantive claim rather than either a hostage to current physics or an empty promissory note. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. I especially want the tension made explicit: which requirements pull against one another, and is the core pair—non-triviality and tolerance of future physics—jointly satisfiable at all, or is that the whole burden of Hempel's dilemma? Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of particular camps. Then briefly indicate which single desideratum does the most to eliminate the standard strategies (definitions via current physics, via completed physics, via the negative route of 'not fundamentally mental', and structuralist accounts), and how each fares against the list.

Among the cases I want the account to classify correctly are these: a depressed person's mood and volition; the neurotransmitter concentrations and neural activity that covary with them; numbers and fictional characters; a Cartesian soul if one existed; and a hypothetical world physically identical to ours but without consciousness, if such a world is genuinely conceivable. I am not asking for a survey of positions or a bibliography, and I do not want the supervenience formulation; the supervenience formulation already contains the word 'physical' and is exactly what needs discharging.
