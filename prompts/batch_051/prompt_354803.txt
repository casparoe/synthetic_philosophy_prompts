In a reading group I attend, the same argument recurs so regularly that I suspect it is a named fault line in the causal inference literature, but I have never found a label for it. Two members of the group routinely agree on every empirical particular — the data, the model, the estimated effects, even the diagram of dependencies — and yet they split, week after week, over whether some factor may properly be said to cause an outcome. One will allow a factor into causal claims only if there is a well-defined operation by which one could, at least in principle, alter that factor while leaving the rest of the system fixed; for him, a person's ancestry, sex assigned at birth, early neighborhood, or a country's colonial history are not the right kind of thing to appear on the left-hand side of a causal sentence, because no such operation is available. The other regards that restriction as mistaking our limitations for facts about the world: he holds that the causal structure is settled by how the outcome would differ under differences in the factor, whether or not any agent could produce those differences, and he is comfortable saying that culture, historical trauma, or demographic categories cause later outcomes. What puzzles me is that neither man disputes any particular conditional; they agree about what would happen under the various changes they can describe. They disagree only when one of them must commit to an actual sentence of the form "X causes Y."

I am looking for the established term, if one exists, for this exact disagreement. I keep trying to describe it as a quarrel about manipulability, but I am not sure whether that is right, and I would like to know how it sits in relation to the inventories of causal theories I half-remember: counterfactual dependence views, interventionist or manipulationist views, agency views, and the more ordinary contrastive way in which people pick out one cause among many jointly necessary conditions. Is this a single distinction, or is it two or three distinctions that tend to travel together — one about the metaphysics of causation, one about what counts as a legitimate variable in a causal model, and one about the pragmatics of when a causal claim is worth asserting? And are there cases on which the two researchers, despite their agreement in principle on what would happen under described changes, would nevertheless be led to draw different inferences about what to do next? If no exact label fits, tell me how you would name it and where its boundaries are.
