Two people in my philosophy reading group got stuck on vagueness last week and I honestly couldn't sort them out afterward. It started when Dan said the whole "no sharp cutoff" picture is a dodge: take a heap of 10,000 grains and pull grains off one at a time. At the start it's clearly a heap, at the end it clearly isnt, so if you accept ordinary two-valued logic there has to be at least one grain such that removing it flips the predicate. We can't say which one, but that just means we're ignorant, not that there's no fact. Maria said that's a decree, not an argument: nothing in how we use "heap" picks out any such grain, so he's positing a fact with nothing that makes it true, which is worse than the disease. Dan sidestepped the "what makes it true" worry and came back with the witness problem: on Maria's supervaluationist view, "there is a cutoff" comes out true, because every admissible sharpening of "heap" draws one somewhere, but every particular answer to "where is the cutoff?" is neither true nor false, since any given spot is the cutoff on some sharpenings and not on others. So you get an existential that's true with no true instance, which Dan thinks is quietly incoherent. Maria said it's only incoherent if you treat "neither true nor false" as a third truth value. It isn't: it's the sentence getting opposite verdicts on different legitimate sharpenings, and the classical tautologies still come out true, so classical logic is mostly intact. Dan's last move was higher-order vagueness: Maria can't say where "borderline" ends and "clearly heap" begins either, so her view re-grows the same fuzz one level up, while for the epistemicist higher-order vagueness is just more ignorance, which at least isnt internally awkward. Maria said so what: she never promised a sharp border between the clear cases and the borderline ones, and a vague "admissible" is only embarrassing if you demanded sharp borders somewhere, which she doesn't. At that point we had to end. Walk me through this move by move: which specific claims are right, where each of them is overreaching, what neither of them is seeing, and who you'd side with overall. Keep it under 700 words.
