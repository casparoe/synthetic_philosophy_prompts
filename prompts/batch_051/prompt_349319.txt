As a philosopher of social science I have long been immersed in Karl Popper's argument against historicism, the doctrine that the social sciences should seek laws of historical development and use them for long-range prediction. I have only moderate fluency, however, in a different vocabulary: that of nonlinear dynamical systems and forecasting. By this I mean state spaces, trajectories, attractors and basins of attraction, sensitivity to initial conditions, structural stability, time-varying parameters, model error, overfitting, and out-of-sample prediction.

Please restate Popper's anti-historicist position in that vocabulary, systematically and without smuggling in commitments he did not have. Be sure to translate at least these four components: (1) his distinction between laws and trends; (2) his claim that human history is a unique, non-repeatable process and so cannot furnish the repeated instances a scientific law requires; (3) his argument that the growth of knowledge is unpredictable and that this unpredictability propagates into any long-run social prediction; and (4) the Oedipus effect, the way a prediction can alter the very process it predicts.

Then give me a residue report. Which of Popper's claims map cleanly onto the dynamical-systems idiom? Which can be made to map only by distorting them, and where exactly does the distortion enter? Which have no counterpart at all, no sensible translation, and why? Finally, tell me whether the untranslatable residue is a limitation of my target vocabulary, or evidence that some part of Popper's argument was doing something other than precise theoretical work. I do not need a summary of Popper or a tutorial on dynamics; I need the mapping and an honest accounting of what does not survive it.
