In the reading group I help organize, one of the engineers has a habit of cutting off discussion of corrigibility (systems that cooperate with being corrected or shut down), interpretability, and reward learning by saying something like: "Alignment is just a problem of specifying the right objective function. If you had the right objective, the rest would follow." I can tell this slogan is doing genuine argumentative work for him, but I also suspect that the phrase "objective function" is sliding between several different meanings. I want to go back to the group with a careful disambiguation. Treat the slogan as the object of analysis. What distinct claims could it be used to express, from the nearly trivial to the highly ambitious? For each reading, say explicitly what "objective function" would have to refer to, and give a verdict: is that reading true, false, open, or only vacuously true? Then tell me which of those readings would actually support using the slogan to dismiss corrigibility, interpretability, and reward learning as less fundamental, and whether the slogan, on those readings, actually establishes that conclusion.
