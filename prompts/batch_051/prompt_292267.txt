I serve on the charter revision commission of a city of roughly two hundred thousand residents. A ballot measure proposes to replace the elected city council's authority over long-term land-use, capital investment, and utility rates with a standing citizens' assembly: two hundred adult residents chosen by lot, serving single one-year terms with paid leave, staff support, and binding votes; the mayor would implement but not veto assembly decisions. Proponents argue that this would reduce capture by developers and genuinely give all affected members of the city equal political power. Opponents argue that ordinary residents lack the specialized knowledge needed for complicated infrastructure decisions and that such an assembly would be unstable under the pressure of momentary majorities. Draft the commission's decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or a modified design. In the reasoning, treat Spinoza's political account of democracy as a live framework: he argues that democracy is the most natural form of commonwealth because the sovereign body remains the whole multitude rather than a smaller governing class, and that the power and stability of a commonwealth are best expressed when many people act from shared purposes rather than when a few rule through fear. Do not turn the memo into an exposition of Spinoza; use that framework to weigh the proposal. Give a concrete, actionable recommendation, and state the strongest argument on the side you do not take.
