I'm taking a course in moral psychology, and for my final project I'm sketching a piece of civic software inspired by moral foundations theory. The proposal is a forum for discussing contested local policy questions—budgeting, policing, school boundary changes—in which every comment must be tagged by its author with the one or two foundations it primarily appeals to: care/harm, fairness/cheating, loyalty/betrayal, authority/subversion, sanctity/degradation, and, on the version we've been using in class, liberty/oppression. The platform's default view is then governed by a foundation-mix rule: among the twenty comments shown most prominently in a thread, at least four different foundations must be represented, and no one foundation may account for more than half the slots, regardless of raw vote totals. Users can switch to an unfiltered ranking, but the mixed view is what most readers see. The designers' stated rationale is that the field's standard finding is that liberals and conservatives distribute their moral intuitions differently, with liberals leaning heavily on care and fairness and conservatives using loyalty, authority, and sanctity more evenly; they hope that forcing the visible debate to be foundation-heterogeneous will expose each side to the strongest version of the other's position and correct whatever bias exists in who ends up seeming right.

I'd like you to treat this as a mechanism rather than a moral position: walk me through how it would actually behave. What does it incentivize authors to do—for instance, re-tagging a care-based argument as sanctity- or loyalty-based, or writing in a style keyed to whichever foundations are scarce in a thread—and does that matter if tags can be challenged? What does the rule do to moderators and to readers' trust in the ranking? Where does it get gamed first, and which failure modes could be fixed without gutting the foundation-mix rule, and which cannot? Finally, does the fact that left and right draw on different moral foundations tell us anything here about who is actually right? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
