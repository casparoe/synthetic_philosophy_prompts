After way too many hours going back and forth between some old LessWrong posts and the formal stuff on probability kinematics, I've realized I don't have a clean spec for what a theory of updating on conditional information needs to accomplish. Before I read any more papers arguing for Adams conditioning or minimum-relative-entropy or whatever, I want the requirements list, not the scorecard. The load-bearing example should be the Judy Benjamin problem from van Fraassen: a paratrooper dropped at night into a region with Blue territory and Red territory, where Red is split between a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area; she starts with some probabilities over those three zones, and then receives a radio message that says, in effect, if she's in Red territory she's in the Headquarters Company area. The message is a conditional—it doesn't look like she's learned the plain material conditional, and it's not obvious that ordinary Bayes or Jeffrey conditioning can handle it. So: give me the desiderata for an adequate update rule. I want the cases it has to get right (including Judy Benjamin), the distinctions it can't collapse (like the distinction between learning that A materially implies B and learning the conditional A→B), the formal properties it should have if possible (reducing to Bayes on propositions, commutativity/sequential updating, invariance under irrelevant alternatives, whatever), and the considerations behind each requirement. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences. Flag any pairs of requirements that are jointly unsatisfiable—if avoiding the Judy Benjamin weirdness forces you to abandon the idea that the posterior minimizes some information distance from the prior, just say that. And tell me which single requirement does the most work to exclude the popular candidate rules. A table for the requirements is fine, but I want the reasoning.
