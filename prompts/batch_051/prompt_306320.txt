The board relitigates the same fight in different clothes: should we be celebrating local founders and "great citizens" (monumental history), painstakingly preserving old houses, artifacts, and photos for their own sake (antiquarian), or exposing the ugly past, like our redlining maps and the mob violence that emptied the old Black neighborhood (critical)? Right now the loudest faction wins, and I want something better than that.

Can you turn the monumental/antiquarian/critical triad into a reusable decision procedure my staff could actually run on a proposal before it reaches the board? I want a checklist or flowchart in words: questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the process stops with a no, where it hands off to a person because the question is values rather than facts. For each mode, build in a check for its characteristic excess — monumental history becoming fake, mythic celebration that buries real possibilities; antiquarian history mummifying everything old and treating age as its own justification; critical history souring into self-righteous demolition — but in language my board can use, not seminar language. Then run the procedure on three proposals: (a) restoring the mansion of a local industrialist who, we recently learned, used company guards to break strikes in 1911; (b) a permanent exhibit on housing discrimination with recorded testimony from surviving families; (c) a bicentennial mural series of the city's "twelve greatest citizens." Make sure at least one case comes out in a way I won't like. And flag the places where Nietzsche's text resists operationalization, where the procedure is really my values wearing a flowchart.
