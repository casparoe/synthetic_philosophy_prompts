The argument runs as follows:

(1) A plan is, at bottom, a commitment formed in advance to perform some action later.
(2) A rational agent revises such a commitment whenever, at the time of action, her current best judgment recommends doing something other than what she planned; to do otherwise is to let her past self overrule her present self for no good reason.
(3) If a rational agent always follows her current best judgment at the time of action, then the earlier plan does no genuine work: it merely records a past guess about what her later judgment would recommend.
(4) Someone who acts on her current best judgment in the unfolding situation, rather than on a prior commitment, is improvising.
(5) Therefore, for a perfectly rational agent, planning and improvising coincide, and the distinctive value normally attributed to planning is an illusion.

Is the argument valid? If it is not, say exactly which step fails or where the inference breaks down. If it is valid, say which premise I should reject and what the strongest corrected version of that premise would be.
