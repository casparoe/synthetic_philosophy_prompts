Okay so i keep going back and forth on this and i want someone to actually do both sides justice instead of just picking one. On one hand, the classical picture of God as actus purus, outside time, not composed of parts, incapable of being acted on by anything outside himself—that package seems basically forced on me once i accept that God is ultimate in the relevant sense. And from it, it follows pretty directly that God cannot suffer. Suffering is something that happens to you; it's passive. A being with no potentiality, no passivity, can't undergo grief or pain or disappointment, period. I get the elegance of it: immutability, simplicity, aseity, impassibility all fall out of the same move, and the move is basically just "God is not one of the things in the world that gets pushed around."

But then i read the crucifixion accounts, or honestly just try to pray, and i can't fully shake the intuition that a God who cannot suffer cannot love in the way that matters. A love that can't be wounded, that doesn't grieve with the beloved, that risks nothing in the loving—that seems less like perfect love and more like a kind of emotional invulnerability that in a human we'd diagnose as a defect. If i found out my father was constitutionally incapable of being hurt by my suffering, i wouldn't think his love was purer; i'd think something was missing. And the cross is supposed to be the center of the whole religion—if God was not really in that agony, then what exactly is being revealed there, a demonstration?

So here is what i actually want. Don't tell me it's a mystery. I want to know what it would cost, bit by bit, to let God suffer: do i have to give up immutability, simplicity, atemporality, or can some of those be reformulated in a way that lets God be affected by creaturely pain without making him just another thing in the world? And what it would cost to instead keep the whole classical package and say my intuition is just me anthropomorphizing, projecting creaturely psychology onto a being who can't have that kind of psychology. Is there a reading of "God cannot suffer" on which God is still genuinely present to and moved by creaturely pain—maybe a distinction between being involuntarily overwhelmed by a passion and something else—or is that just trying to have it both ways? Which side should yield, and on what grounds would i know?
