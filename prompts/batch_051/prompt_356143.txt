At my department's legal philosophy reading group, two of us got into it over contracts of adhesion — specifically whether clicking 'I agree' on a terms-of-service popup is genuine agreement. Dan said it can't be: a contract of adhesion isn't a meeting of minds, you can't agree to terms you don't know, and nobody actual reads those things. Priya said the law can take the act at face value — you're shown the terms (or a working link), then you click a button that literally says 'I agree.' Dan replied that the choice is a sham: it's take-it-or-leave-it, and leaving means exiting the platform everyone in your life is on, so there's no real voluntariness. Priya said consent doesn't require attractive alternatives; we consent to plenty of stuff we haven't read, and the opportunity to read is all a legal system can reasonably demand. Dan said that gets it backwards — the company drafts unreadable forty-page terms precisely because it profits from you not reading, so treating the click as consent lets them pass off unilateral rules as something you chose. Priya said fine, but if clicks aren't agreement then most of the online economy is resting on a fiction, and the question is whether there's any better line than the click. Neither moved. They asked me to referee and I couldn't. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: say which specific claims are right, where each person goes wrong, and what a decent account of agreement should say about clickwrap.
