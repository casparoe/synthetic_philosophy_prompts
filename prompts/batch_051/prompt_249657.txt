I keep using the word 'community' as if it obviously names something, but I've realised I can't actually say what makes a group a community, which is a bit awkward given how much weight people put on the idea that communities dissolving is a loss. I'd like a working defintion of community, not an encyclopedia entry, tested against some cases I've collected.

Please count these as communities: a close rural village where people look after each other's kids, a synagogue or mosque congregation that meets weekly, and a pub quiz team that's met every Thursday for years. Please don't count these: the people who get the same 7:40 bus every morning, and everyone who's read the same bestselling book. Then give a definite yes or no for each of these, and don't be afraid to defend a slightly surprising verdict: an online subreddit for people with a chronic illness, where regulars know each other's usernames and histories; a workplace where people get along during shifts but never meet outside and most leave within two years; the fan base of a football club that sings together in the stands but otherwise has no contact; neighbours who only interact through a WhatsApp group about parking and bin collections; a friendship group that drifted apart a decade ago but still call themselves the lads at weddings; and an entire country during a World Cup. I keep going back and forth on the subreddit and the World Cup one, so tell me if you vote against my current hunch, which is that the subreddit counts and the country doesn't.

Two constraints. The definition shouldn't be a moralised one: it can't make mutual flourishing, equality, or people's interests being served part of the test, because a cult or a tight-knit criminal gang seems like a community even though it's bad, and I'd rather keep that awkward verdict than define the term as something cosy. And don't define it as simply a shared feeling of belonging, because that makes the World Cup crowd a community at kick-off and not at full-time, which seems to track mood rather than the group. The definition should be usable in the debate about what, if anything, is lost when communities dissolve, so it shouldn't settle that question by stipulation.

Write it as continuous prose, like an email or letter — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables. Propose the definition, run it through all the cases in the order I gave them, say which ones it gets wrong or where it surprises you, and then revise it or defend the surprise. End by saying what your definition implies is lost when a community dissolves, if anything.
