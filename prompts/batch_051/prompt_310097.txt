I recently retired from a career in the natural sciences and have been teaching myself philosophy, mostly around the conceptual foundations of AI safety. The thing I cannot get clear is why the alignment problem is treated as several problems rather than one. The distinction I have most trouble with is the one between outer alignment — specifying what we actually want the system to optimize — and inner alignment — ensuring that the trained system really optimizes that specified objective rather than something else it was inadvertently shaped to pursue. But the objective, reward function, or task representation that actually governs an AI is always an internal state of the machine; even a human-specified objective only becomes effective once it has been written into the system somewhere. Is the outer/inner distinction then anything more than the same gap described once from the designer's side and once from the machine's side? What would be lost if we dropped it and spoke only of a single specification-vs-implementation gap?
