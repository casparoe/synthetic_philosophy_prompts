I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a feature about whether the therapy/enhancement distinction can bear the weight that sports regulators, insurers, and clinicians now place on it. I'd like you to help me reason through a concrete case.

The medical code of a major sporting federation, adopted in 2004, contains this clause:

"A prohibited enhancement is any substance or method whose purpose or effect is to raise an athlete's physiological or psychological capacity above the athlete's normal healthy level. Nothing in this provision shall prohibit the treatment of a diagnosed medical condition, where the treatment is intended to restore the athlete to, but not above, their normal healthy level."

The case is this. A track cyclist was diagnosed at fourteen with a progressive muscle-wasting disease. She received an experimental gene therapy that halted the decline and left her muscle tissue regenerating more efficiently than it otherwise would. She is now twenty-four and competing internationally. Her doctors' best modelling indicates that her present physiological capacity is above the level she would have reached had she never been ill: the therapy overshot restoration. The code was drafted with insulin, cortisone, and similar ongoing treatments in mind, and its drafters plainly assumed that a treated athlete had a known prior healthy baseline to be restored to. They did not contemplate a one-time intervention that alters an adolescent's developmental trajectory before any adult "normal healthy level" has ever existed for that person.

Has she breached the clause? Please argue the case separately from (a) the ordinary meaning of the words, (b) the purpose of the clause, (c) what the drafters would most likely have said if asked in 2004, and (d) what a reasonable reader of the code would understand it to require. Then tell me which of these approaches should govern here, and why.

Finally, consider a strengthened version of the case: suppose the disease so shaped her development that there is no well-defined counterfactual "healthy adult cyclist" for her at all, so the clause's own reference point cannot be pinned down. Does the clause still apply, and what should an adjudicator do?

Please write the answer in British English.
