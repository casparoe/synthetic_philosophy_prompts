As a novelist I keep tripping over a parallel I can't decide about, between how we argue in workshops and the split in biology over what makes something a function. The way I understand it: an etiological account says the heart's function is pumping because pumping is what hearts got selected for, while a causal-role account says the function is whatever the heart contributes to the organism's current machinery. In a novel the same two answers show up. A scene is 'for' what the author was steering toward across drafts, the thing that made it survive revision; or a scene is 'for' what it actually does to a reader in the finished book. I've seen chapters that do nothing in the final text still get called functional because of their role in an earlier draft, and the reverse as well. Is this one real structure or just the word 'function' in two rooms? If it's real, does anything from the biology side transfer — vestigial organs, spandrels, malfunction — and where does the analogy snap? I can't figure out what a literary version of malfunction would even be, and I cant tell whether that's the deep part or the part where the whole thing falls apart.
