I keep seeing a parallel I can't decide about between Lakatos's Proofs and Refutations and the way a codebase grows under a property-based test suite. In the dialogue a counterexample to V - E + F = 2 (the picture frame, the star-polyhedron) doesn't kill the conjecture: the class either monster-bars it as not a real polyhedron or stretches the definition, until the counterexample is absorbed as an explicit condition — the theorem now says: for simple polyhedra, V - E + F = 2, with the hidden lemma built into the statement. In property-based testing a failing property also doesn't kill the program: you either fix the implementation or tighten the precondition/generator, and the once-failing case becomes a regression test, which looks exactly like lemma-incorporation. Is this a deep identity of structure — both treat refutation as something to absorb, so maybe mathematics really is quasi-empirical in a non-fluffy sense — or does it only catch the trivial fact that both have claims and counterexamples? The obvious break is that a mathematical counterexample is decisive while a test samples finitely many inputs, and that a software spec can change for commercial reasons with no Lakatos counterpart; but I can't tell wether that's the whole story. What would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and what's the clearest thing the analogy predicts that turns out false?
