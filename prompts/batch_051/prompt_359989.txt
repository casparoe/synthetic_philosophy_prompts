I was reading Zorn’s 1935 paper where he proves the maximal principle, and it reminded me of something in moral epistemology. Zorn’s lemma says: in a partially ordered set, if every chain has an upper bound, then the set contains at least one maximal element. Reflective equilibrium (the Rawlsian kind) also seems to assume something chain-complete: you move from one coherentist position to another by revising the weakest part whenever two commitments conflict, and you only stop when no local revision improves coherence. The parrallel looks like the same structure: chains of revisions correspond to chains in a poset, the endpoint you can’t improve corresponds to a maximal element, and the completeness condition corresponds to the hope that any chain of revisions has some coherent limit. But in Zorn’s lemma the ordering is fixed before you start, whereas in reflective equilibrium the ordering ‘more justified than’ is partly what the revisions are revising. Is this a deep point — e.g., does it explain why reflective equilibrium risks stopping at a local, path-dependent maximum — or is it a superficial pattern-match? If it’s deep, what would a theorem here look like?
