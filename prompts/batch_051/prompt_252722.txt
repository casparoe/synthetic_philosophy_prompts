The standard reading of the Löbian obstacle says that a deductive agent faces a principled barrier to self-trust: a consistent proof system strong enough to encode its own provability predicate cannot prove that its own provability implies truth, so a proof-based agent cannot soundly derive that its conclusions about its own actions are reliable. Now change a central assumption of that argument. The agent is not a proof-search system but a trained neural policy whose self-model is empirical and probabilistic: it assigns credences to conditionals of the form if it takes action A in state S, then outcome O follows, on the basis of its own track record and observations of similar agents, and it updates those credences as evidence arrives. Does the Löbian obstacle still bite against this agent? Isolate the premise the obstacle needs, say whether the probabilistic agent still satisfies it, and then say whether any analogous barrier to self-trust remains, and in what form.
