Its a weird feature of the last couple years that more and more testimony-shaped stuff reaches me without any testifier at all: a chatbot answers the question, a model writes the summary, autocomplete finishes the sentence. It's made me wonder whether the old anti-reductionist argument about testimony just quietly dies once that becomes a big part of our epistemic environment. The argument said accepting what people tell you by default has to be a basic source of justification, because you could never bootstrap language or history if every report had to be checked first; reductionists undersell how much we take on trust. But it was built around a person who intends to inform you. Does the shift to generated text with no assurer actually break the argument, or does it just shrink its domain and push the real epistemic work somewhere unreconstructed? Which premise takes the pressure, and is there a repaired version that keeps the anti-reductionist point without pretending a chatbot is an informant? I'm asking about the fate of the argument, not about AI policy.
