Bertrand Russell maintained that most conventional sexual morality was superstition, that marriage should be easy to enter and leave, that the state's legitimate interest in sexual relationships runs chiefly through the welfare of any children they produce, and that jealousy is largely a socially produced attitude rather than an innate drive. He died in 1970, so he never saw app-mediated dating, the destigmatisation of consensual non-monogamy, same-sex marriage, or the long decline in marriage rates. Write an essay predicting, with arguments, how Russell would probably assess these developments. Would his views commit him to welcoming the decline of marriage and the normalisation of non-monogamy, or would the evidence of the last half-century plausibly have led him to revise the psychological claim about jealousy? Engage fairly with the strongest consideration against your conclusion, and write your response in British English.
