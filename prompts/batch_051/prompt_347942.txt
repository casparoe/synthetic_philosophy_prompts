I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about Quine's gavagai example, and I came away suspecting that we had been running together two doctrines that Quine keeps distinct: indeterminacy of translation and inscrutability of reference. My friend's position is that the field linguist's situation is simply an extreme case of underdetermination of theory by evidence—several translation manuals fit the observed behavior, but there is still a fact of the matter about what the speaker means, and a manual is only a practical device for coping with that evidence. My position is that Quine means something stronger: in translation there is no fact of the matter for a uniquely correct manual to capture, because the relevant facts about meaning are exhausted by dispositions to verbal behavior. We also disagreed about the rabbit case itself. My friend said the real issue is at the level of whole sentences and how to map them onto observation sentences, while I thought the lesson depends on the term-sized alternatives—rabbit, undetached rabbit parts, rabbit stages, rabbithood—and the idea that the native term's reference is not uniquely fixed by all possible behavioral evidence.

Compare indeterminacy of translation with inscrutability of reference as they operate in the gavagai case. In particular, explain which of the two doctrines the rabbit example is primarily meant to establish, and whether the example can carry the stronger conclusion that there is no fact of the matter about correct translation rather than the weaker conclusion that the evidence merely underdetermines a choice between rival manuals. Does the linguist's background language, with its own ways of dividing the world into rabbits and parts and temporal stages, do illicit work in making the stronger conclusion seem plausible? Tell me whether my friend is right that the case collapses into ordinary underdetermination, whether I am right that it does not, or whether the most defensible reading splits the difference—and say what that reading would be.
