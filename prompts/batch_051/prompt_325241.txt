So I'm presenting a paper next month at an AI governance workshop, and I'd like a Q&A prep sheet rather than a fuller defense. The paper applies Fearon's rationalist explanations for war to AI development races. My thesis is that the usual story people in that space tell — racing to build AGI is a collective action problem or tragedy of the commons, where everyone would be better off if they could just coordinate but defection is too tempting — is the wrong frame. In the cases that actually scare me, the parties are talking and can reach a bargain in principle; the problem is that committments not to race, not to sabotage, and not to use a lead once you have it are not credible, because the payoff to a decisive lead is enormous and hard to verify away. So I argue the binding constraint is mostly Fearon's commitment problem, with private information and incentives to misrepresent as the secondary mechanism. I'm not claiming anyone is about to start a shooting war; I'm claiming the same structure that makes rational states fight explains why AI races stay dangerous even with communication between the actors. The audience is mostly AI policy people and lab-governance types: they know the Moloch framing and the prisoner's dilemma, a few have read some international relations but it isn't their home field, and the median person is skeptical that war analogies transfer to firms. What are the seven or eight objections I'll actually get from this exact audience, phrased the way they would phrase them, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are? For each, give me a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And if one of their objections genuinely sinks the paper, tell me that rather than giving me spin.
