I'm a novelist, so language is kind of my whole trade. The people building large language models train the things on nothing but text—trillions of words scraped from books and the web—and then they decide whether a model "understands" language by seeing how well it predicts held-out words or answers questions. That whole setup is a practice: a way of building a language-using thing and a standard for calling it understanding. What view of meaning does that practice presuppose? Does it quietly commit them to the idea that a word's meaning just is it's pattern of co-occurrence with other words, with no hook to anything outside language? Or is the practice actually compatible with the view that grounding matters, and word prediction is just a rough proxy for something deeper? And if someone worries that symbols need to be grounded in the non-linguistic world, where exactly should they say the setup goes wrong—the training diet, the training goal, or the evaluation?
