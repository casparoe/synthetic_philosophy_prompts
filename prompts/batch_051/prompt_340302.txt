In my physics class this year I started keeping a list of the answers I get wrong, because they feel weirdly organized. Like, they aren't random — some of them seem to come from the same wrong idea wearing different costumes, and some are just sloppiness. I've been trying to sort them, and I want to know if there's actually a principle that matches my sorting, or if I'm just imagining that some of my mistakes are secretly a little theory.

Here are the cases and my gut call on each one.

1. A hockey puck sliding on ice. My first instinct is that the puck keeps moving because the hit left some force in it, and that to keep moving at a constant speed it needs a constant push. I know it's wrong when I stop and think, but it's still my default. This one feels like a full system: it predicts that a harder hit means the puck carries force longer, and that a steady push is what keeps it at steady speed. I'd call that a theory.

2. A heavy ball and a light ball dropped from the same height. My reflex is the heavy one lands first, even though I can say out loud that they land together. But this feels different from the puck thing — its only about falling, it doesn't spread to other topics, and I can sort of turn it off by picturing that video of a feather and a hammer being dropped on the Moon. Verdict: more like a reflex than a theory.

3. A series circuit with two bulbs. I keep drawing it as if the first bulb uses up some of the current, so the second bulb gets less and looks dimmer. This one feels theory-ish again: it predicts that with three bulbs the last one is dimmest and the first is brightest, and those predictions still feel right to me even after we measured it in a lab and saw otherwise. Verdict: a theory.

4. Converting km/h to m/s. I divide by 3.6 half the time and multiply by 3.6 the other half, and I can't find any pattern in which one I do. Verdict: not a theory, just carelessness.

5. Seasons. I used to think summer happens because Earth is closer to the sun. That idea did make predictions — like that Australia should have summer at the same time as us — but the predictions are wrong and I dropped the whole idea the day a teacher showed me the tilt diagram. So this one messes up my rule: it generated predictions (so theory-like?) but it died instantly (so not theory-like?).

My tentative rule is something like: a wrong answer counts as a theory if I can use it to answer questions I've never seen before, and it's just a slip or a reflex if it's isolated and doesnt generate anything.

But I can tell that rule has problems with case 5, and maybe with case 2 as well, since the heavy-falls-first idea is famous enough that maybe it should count as a theory no matter how it feels to me. So: is there a principle that actually captures my sorting, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, and what does it say about a case I haven't decided yet — I also think a winter coat keeps you warm by generating heat, rather than just slowing down heat leaving your body. Is that a theory or not?
