I've been reading Dōgen's fascicle Uji on the side and I can't shake a parallel I noticed with contemporary philosophy of time, and I'd like a sanity check on it. My understanding of Dōgen is roughly this: time isn't a container that beings move through; being and time are the same thing, so there's no distinction between something and its own time. He uses the firewood/ash example to deny continuity through time: firewood is fully firewood, ash is fully ash, and though ash comes after firewood, there's no one thing that was firewood and then became ash. Each moment is complete and self-contained, and pasing from one moment to the next isn't a matter of some persisting thing traversing time.

What caught me is that this sounds awfully close to a B-theory/eternalist picture — all times equally real, no objective passage, the "now" as just an indexical perspective — and also to 4-dimensionalism, where an object just is its whole temporal career, and maybe even to stage theory, where each temporal stage is the whole object at that time and nothing persists across stages. But I can also see how Dōgen's emphasis on impermanence might make it the opposite: a kind of radical presentism where only the current moment exists but it doesn't flow. Is the parallel with eternalism/stage theory deep or just verbal? If it's real, what does Dōgen's view inherit from it — for example, do B-theory's problems about explaining our sense of passage transfer over? And where does the mapping clearly break? I keep going back and forth, so I'd like to know which parts of this are the genuine structure and which parts are me pattern-matching.
