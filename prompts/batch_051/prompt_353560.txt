I help run a programming languages reading group, and we've spent the last few weeks on mutable state. The thing everyone keeps losing their footing on is aliasing: two syntactically different names that denote the same cell, so an assignment through one name changes what you see through the other. It's the fact that `x == y` can be true on one line and false a few lines later without either binding being touched by name, like math has quietly stopped being the law of the land. We want to close the semester with a poem that renders this instead of just describing it.

So: write a villanelle, the usual nineteen lines (five tercets plus a final quatrain), about two names that share a mutable cell. Here's the constraint that matters to us: the two refrains should start out identical by design — the way you'd see if two variables pointed at the same object — and then one refrain should start changing, line by line, as the shared cell gets assigned new contents while the other refrain keeps its original phrasing. A real villanelle wants its refrains to repeat verbatim, and this one should fail at that on purpose; the drift is the philosophical point, not becuase the poem got lazy. By the time the two refrains meet in the final couplet, a reader should be unsure which line is the variable and which is the value, or whether the equality between them still holds now. Someone who works on state and identity in PL should be able to point at the exact line where the mutation happened and the exact line where substitutivity broke; someone who doesn't should still get a poem about sharing something that changes underneath both parties.

After the poem, give me a JSON object summarizing the structural side, with these fields:

- `"initial_refrains"`: the text of both refrains when they first appear, in order.
- `"first_mutation"`: an object with `"line_number"` and `"new_text"` saying which refrain changes first and what it becomes.
- `"subsequent_mutations"`: a list of objects like the above for each later change to a refrain.
- `"alias_variable"`: a short string saying which refrain plays the role of the name/binding and which plays the current value at the cell.
- `"final_equality"`: the string `"true"` or `"false"` relative to the end of the poem, plus a one-sentence justification for what the two final lines now denote by the poem's own lights.
- `"specialist_reading"`: two or three sentences explaining, in programming-language terms, where the poem tracks aliasing and how the broken refrain rule corresponds to losing referential transparency.
