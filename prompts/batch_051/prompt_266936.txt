I teach a 300-level political philosophy seminar — 16 students, 80-minute session — and I want a classroom activity on Locke on property: the labour theory of acquisition and the Lockean proviso. I don't want any lecture up front. I want them to run into the proviso as a problem inside the rules, and only hear the labels afterwards.

Here's the thing I want to get right, which most textbook treatments blur. There are really two different constraints bundled in the Lockean proviso: a spoilage/waste constraint (you can't claim more than you can make use of before it spoils) and a leave-enough-for-others constraint, which isn't about the quantity you take but about the situation you leave other people in. And there's a further question whether the constraint binds only the original act of taking or also later transfers — a chain of individually fair deals can still leave everyone in a situation that violates the spirit of the original constraint. I'd love the game to have a second phase where claims can be traded or inherited, so the class feels that distinction: does the proviso apply at each step, or only at the first moment of mixing labor?
