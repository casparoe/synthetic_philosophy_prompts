I've been chewing on a pattern in ordinary English that I can't pin down, the kind of thing I'd normally take to a rationalist forum and get three different taxonomies back. Start with "ducks lay eggs." It's true, but only some ducks—mostly adult females—ever lay eggs; males, ducklings, and plenty of others never do. "Lions have manes" is true in the same way, though it's only adult males, and "mosquitoes carry malaria" is true even though only a tiny fraction of mosquitoes ever carry the relevant parasite. But the same surface form is clearly false with other predicates: "ducks are female" is false, "lions are male" is false, and I have a strong feeling that "mosquitoes carry [some harmless bacterium they almost all actually have]" would also be false. So the rule isn't "most members have the property," and it doesn't seem to be "the property is part of the kind's essence" either. Egg-laying is tied to the female reproductive role, manes are a male secondary sex trait, and malaria-carrying matters to us because it's dangerous, not because it's biologically central to being a mosquito.

What I'm after is the concept (or concepts) for this. Is there an established name for these sentences and for the hidden standard that makes "ducks lay eggs" true and "ducks are female" false? More specifically, I want to know whether the egg-laying/mane cases and the malaria case are treated as one phenomenon or seperate ones. The first two look like they track something about the kind's characteristic biology, even though they don't cover most members; the third looks like it tracks salience or danger to humans, since "mosquitoes carry malaria" would stay true even if fewer than one in a thousand mosquitoes transmitted it. Are those two mechanisms or one? And where's the boundary—if we stipulate that 80% of mosquitoes carry some harmless bacterium, does "mosquitoes carry that bacterium" automatically become true, or does the sentence still need something more? I'm less interested in a bare label than in knowing what the nearest concepts are, how well each fits what I'm describing, and where the edges break.
