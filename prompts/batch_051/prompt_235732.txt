I am making a decision tree for a meeting of my school's philosophy and biology discussion group, on the question of what makes something alive. I would like the tree to sort readers by their judgments about borderline cases rather than by which definition they already prefer, so no question in the tree should mention the position it leads to, and no question should simply ask whether the reader accepts a definition.

Please construct the tree from the logical space of answers, not from a list of labels: choose the branch questions first, then locate the named positions among the combinations of answers those questions allow. Each branch node should be a concrete yes-or-no question about an entity or scenario that a classmate could answer without specialized vocabulary. The audience is high school students with no background beyond a standard biology course. The leaves should include the metabolic view, the replication-first (Darwinian) view, the self-production (autopoietic) view, the thermodynamic (dissipative-structure) view, the informational view, and the deflationary cluster view. For each leaf, give the name, a one-sentence statement of the view in your own words, and one sentence on its most awkward commitment: the case it has to classify in a way that conflicts with ordinary judgment. If a combination of answers corresponds to a coherent position nobody defends, make that leaf unnamed and state what the position would be.

The tree should work through at least these cases: a virus, fire, a mule, a prion, a bacterial endospore, a hypothetical droplet that maintains an internal compartment separated from its environment and consumes fuel but cannot copy itself, and a computer program that copies itself with random errors and evolves under selection. You may add one or two further cases if they help separate positions, but do not depend on species facts this audience would not know.

After the tree, list any combinations of answers that no named position occupies, and give a one-sentence diagnosis of each as incoherent, coherent but unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate using the cases above, and either specify one additional case that would separate them or say clearly that no case would.
