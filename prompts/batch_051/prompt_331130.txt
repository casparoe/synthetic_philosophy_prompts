In my philosophy of action course we have been using the following account of akrasia: an agent acts akratically only if, at the moment of action, (1) she acts intentionally and freely and could have done otherwise, (2) she holds an all-things-considered judgment that some available alternative is better, and (3) she acts against that judgment. Here is a case I want to test against it. On Sunday night a doctoral student settles on the considered judgment that she should spend Monday evening revising a chapter rather than going to a friend's party. By Monday afternoon the party is hours away and the benefits of revising remain distant; nothing about the merits has changed, but because her temporal discounting is hyperbolic, she now sincerely judges, all things considered, that going to the party is the better thing to do. She goes, enjoys the party, and late that night tells a friend that this was exactly the reversal she had anticipated in advance. Apply the three conditions and say whether her action counts as akrasia. Where the conditions appear to force an answer that conflicts with your own sense of the case, say whether the better response is to revise the account's insistence that the better judgment be held at the moment of action, or to regard hyperbolic preference reversal as a change of mind rather than weakness of will.
