So I live in a housing co-op and I somehow ended up on the committee that approves or rejects any new gadget someone wants to bolt onto the building. I mostly joined because my mom got locked outside for twenty minutes in February: our intercom calls an app on my phone, I missed it, and she doesn't have a smartphone anyway. So this is not abstract for me right now.

Our past decisons, as best I can summarize from the minutes:

1. A resident wanted to mount a wifi doorbell camera in the third-floor hallway after some package thefts. We rejected it. The minutes say he wasn't the problem; the problem was that a hallway camera isn't something you can take back once it's wired in — after that, what gets done with it stops being ours to decide on a Tuesday evening. Some members also just hated the idea of everyone's comings and goings turning into footage at all.

2. We approved a keypad for the shared basement workshop. The code gets changed whenever someone moves out, nothing is logged, and the committee can change it at any meeting. The reasoning was basically that a code is a policy we can revise; it's not a standing arrangement that revises us.

3. We rejected an otherwise nice proposal to move the laundry booking to an app only. Several older members don't use smartphones, and we said a shared resource can't be available only through a tool some residents don't have. Efficiency wasn't the tiebreaker; who gets left out was.

4. We approved an automatic opener for the heavy front door after a member who uses a wheelchair pointed out that the old door quietly assumed everyone could pull against its spring. The committee's reasoning was that keeping the old door was itself a choice, and it said something about who the building was built for, so we voted to build in the opposite assumption.

Now the live case. I'm actually the one proposing it, though I'd have to recuse myself. A vendor wants to install a fob entry system: every unit gets physical fobs, no smartphone required, so my mom's problem disappears. Guests get buzzed in through a call box that rings whatever phone number a resident lists. The committee can deactivate any fob instantly through an online portal, and the reader itself can be uninstalled in an afternoon if we hate it. But the system logs every single entry — which fob, which door, what time — on the vendor's servers for three years. We can query the logs but not delete them, the software is closed, the contract auto-renews, and there's language about the vendor being allowed to share "anonymized" data if the company is sold.

If we want to be consistent with our own record, how should we vote, and which past decision is the closest analog? And if the record could reasonably support either outcome, tell me what rule we'd be adopting with each choice and what one further fact about the proposal — or one further hypothetical case — would do the most to tell those rules apart.
