After forty years in physical chemistry I've been spending retirement on something I never had time for: little one-dimensional cellular automata on my laptop. Heres a thing I keep running into, and I bet it has a name but I dont know it. You take a row of cells, each on or off, and a rule that decides a cell's next state from itself and its two neighbors. The rule can be written in one line. Some one-line rules are easy: after watching them a while you see stripes or nested triangles, and you can write down a formula that jumps straight to step ten thousand without grinding through the intermediate steps. Other rules, just as simple, produce something that looks like noise, and no formula I can find gets me to step ten thousand; the only way I know is to actually run all ten thousand steps. And what bothers me is not just that I can't find a shortcut. It is that the system appears to be doing a kind of minimal work to produce its own future, and no shortcut could do less, because the thing itself is the cheapest way to find out where it goes. I've seen the same shadow in a few reaction-diffusion systems and toy models of turbulence. Does this property have a name in the philosophy of science or complexity theory? I know about chaos and sensitive dependence on initial conditions, and I know about halting-problem undecidability, but this seems different: the system is finite, perfectly deterministic, and each step is trivial, so it is not undecidable in the usual sense, and it is not really about nearby trajectories flying apart. I've also picked up the impression that this is connected to a stronger claim, something like all systems above a certain threshold can emulate one another, so the differences between them are differences of setup rather than of underlying power. Are those two different claims, and is either one the kind of thing that could be tested, or are they more like philosophical attitudes? Don't just give me a label. I want the boundaries: where this property ends and ordinary chaos or algorithmic undecidability begins, which of the two claims actually follows from the first, and what you would call the pair of them if there is no standard name. Please write back in continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you'd write a letter.
