I am a novelist planning a book whose plot turns on how much authority the imagination has over what is genuinely possible, and I want a document that will keep my invented worlds honest. Two philosophers in my reading group have argued for a year. One — the conceivability optimist — holds that conceivability, when it is positive, coherent, and sharpened by ideal reflection, is strong if defeasible evidence for metaphysical possibility. The other — the modal skeptic — holds that conceivability reveals the limits of our concepts and cognitive powers rather than the structure of possibility itself, so that a scenario's being vividly imaginable tells us little about whether it could obtain. Draft the adversarial-collaboration statement they should sign. It should open with a numbered list of claims both accept: about the difference between positive and negative conceivability and between prima facie and ideal conceiving, about the standard cases in which conceiving has misled us, and about what the dispute does not concern. It should then state their remaining disagreement precisely, with each side's formulation worded so that side would accept it, and identify the cruxes whose settlement would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or test both would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Include at least these two test cases: a world in which gold has an atomic number different from its actual one, and my plot device, a world in which pain is a publicly inspectable state of the body rather than a private feeling. Format the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
