Imagine that Theodor W. Adorno and Max Horkheimer could be shown a representative slice of contemporary media culture: the algorithmic feed of a short-form video platform, a music streaming service that assembles playlists from listeners' behavioural data, a user-generated review and discussion platform, and a generative model that produces images, songs, and prose on demand. Their account of the culture industry predates all of this. Your task is to predict, with as much argumentative rigour as you can, the assessment they would give of these developments.

Take as your starting point the following minimal version of their position. Cultural production, they argued, had been subordinated to the logic of the commodity: works are manufactured for sale, standardised in their underlying structure, and differentiated only at the surface, so that an appearance of novelty — pseudo-individualisation — conceals an underlying sameness. The result is that art loses its capacity to negate or stand at a critical distance from the existing order; instead it reconciles audiences to that order, turning them into consumers of whatever is offered and closing off the space in which a genuinely critical subjectivity might form.

With that apparatus in place, address each of the following. (1) Algorithmic personalisation appears, on its face, to be the opposite of standardisation: every user receives a different stream. Would Adorno and Horkheimer treat this as a refutation of their account, as its perfected form, or as something between the two? Reconstruct the argument they would most plausibly make, and consider whether the move that saves their account — that the underlying units and the selection logic remain standardised even when the sequence is not — survives the objection that the variability is more than superficial. (2) User-generated content blurs the distinction between producer and consumer. Would they see this as a democratisation of cultural production, or as the culture industry extending itself into the audience's own activity? Say which of their concepts would carry the analysis, and why. (3) Generative AI reduces the cost of producing a competent pastiche in any style to nearly zero. Which of their commitments would be strained by this development, and which would remain intact? (4) Finally, identify one point at which their framework would probably misdescribe or underestimate something genuinely new in these developments, and one point at which it would capture something a purely technological account misses. Throughout, distinguish what Adorno and Horkheimer themselves would most likely say from what a careless follower of theirs would say.
