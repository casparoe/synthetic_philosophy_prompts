I run HR at a mid-size company (about 400 people) and I want a reusable decision procedure my junior HR staff can follow for deciding whether an employee request counts as a religious accommodation. We get two or three of these a month — time off, dresscode, food, scheduling around rituals. Too loose and people game it and morale sours; too strict and we're looking at a discrimination complaint; and employees compare notes, so consistency matters. I'm not after legal advice and I don't want a reading list. I want the underlying concept thought through, because the cases that actually reach me are the edge cases. One employee does zazen and wants to leave early on certain days but insists Zen isn't a religion. Another wants a day off for an annual ancestor-veneration rite but says it's cultural, not religious. Another wants to wear a small colander as a religious head-covering, citing Pastafarianism. Another wants Sundays off to attend a 'secular congregation' with no creed or deity, and insists its a religion for the same reasons others get Sundays off. Give me a step-by-step checklist, or a flowchart in words, that a non-specialist can run in about 30 minutes per case; mark the points where judgment can't be squeezed out. Apply the procedure to three of the four cases, and make at least one application come out somewhere I won't like. My junior staff do the sorting and I sign off on denials. Then tell me what the procedure will systematically fail to handle.
