I have four particular judgments about when small harms add up to more than a large one, and I want to know what principle, if any, captures all four. (1) One person being tortured for an hour is worse than any number of people each receiving a single dust speck in the eye. (2) A public health campaign is justified if it will predictably cause five thousand people to endure a day of severe nausea but will prevent fifty deaths. (3) A safety regulation is justified if it imposes a one-in-ten-thousand chance of a permanently disabling injury on each of ten thousand people but will certainly prevent one other person from suffering a permanently disabling injury. (4) It would be wrong to give one hundred thousand people each a mild headache in order to prevent one death. Is there one principle that delivers all four of these verdicts? If not, which verdict should I revise, what principle best fits the remaining ones, and what does that principle imply about a policy that would give five thousand people one day of moderate nausea to prevent one death? Please respond in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
