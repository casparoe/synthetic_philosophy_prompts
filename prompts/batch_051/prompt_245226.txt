Ok so the standard story about Maxwell's demon goes like this. A little being runs a shutter between two boxes of gas and only lets fast molecules through one way and slow molecules the other, so one side heats up and the other cools down with no work input. That looks like free energy out of a uniform bath, which would violate the second law. The usual fix is that the demon has to measure each molecule and note whether it's fast or slow, and eventually its memory fills up, so it has to erase those notes. And erasing one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of heat dumped into the surroundings. So the entropy decrease from sorting gets paid back, with interest, when the demon deletes its records, and the second law survives.

Here's what I keep getting stuck on. People in reversible computing point out that erasure is only dissipative when it's logically irreversible, like overwriting a bit. If the demon is a reversible computer and just never erases—keeps writing each measurement to a fresh blank cell on an ever-longer tape—then that kT ln 2 bill never comes due. The demon just accumulates a longer and longer record. I can see to possibilities: maybe the growing tape itself counts as entropy after all, even though each bit is 'known' information; or maybe the real resource was never erasure but the supply of blank cells, which is a kind of fuel; or maybe the second-law argument only applies to a demon that returns to its initial state each cycle, and a never-erasing demon just isn't that.

So my question is: does the erasure-based resolution of Maxwell's demon survive if we take reversible computing seriously and let the demon never erase? Which premise of the resolution is actually doing the work, and what does a repaired version say? If the resolution dies in that case, what's the right way to save the second law—and if it doesn't, show exactly where the entropy cost sneaks back in.
