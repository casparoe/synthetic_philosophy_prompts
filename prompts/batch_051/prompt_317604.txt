An argument I keep encountering runs as follows. Once deepfakes are mature, audiovisual recordings lose their standing as an epistemic backstop for contested public events, and from this it is then inferred that factual disagreement about public events becomes rationally unresolvable. I suspect one of the steps is doing more work than the others, but I cannot make the diagnosis precise. Here is the argument in numbered form.

(1) In the pre-deepfake setting, audiovisual recordings could function as an epistemic backstop: when testimony and a recording conflicted, the recording was treated as presumptively authoritative, because fabricating a convincing recording was costly, rare, and usually detectable.

(2) A kind of evidence can serve as an epistemic backstop only if fabricating it is hard enough, in cost, skill, or detectability, that a rational disputant does not in ordinary circumstances need to take the fabrication hypothesis seriously.

(3) A mature deepfake technology would let a forger produce, at trivial cost, an audiovisual recording that is by ordinary inspection indistinguishable from a genuine recording of an event that never occurred.

(4) Therefore, for any particular recording offered as evidence of a disputed event, the hypothesis that it is a deepfake is cheap, live, and not decidable by ordinary inspection; so by (2), no particular recording can retain the backstop role.

(5) What might still serve as a backstop is not a single recording but the surrounding provenance: chain of custody, metadata, multiple independent recordings, institutional attestation, and the coherence of the whole record. But each component of that provenance is itself either a digital artifact of the same forgeable kind, or an institutional assertion that is fallible and can be faked or pressured.

(6) If each component can be forged in a way that is indistinguishable from the genuine component, then a sufficiently well-coordinated forger can produce an entire evidential record for a fabricated event that is indistinguishable from a genuine one; hence the composite record cannot be a backstop either.

(7) Therefore, in a mature-deepfake world, no audiovisual evidence, whether single or composite, can settle contested public events. The backstop has collapsed, and a rational person may in principle reject any audiovisual evidence whatsoever, no matter how extensive.

Is this argument valid? If it is not, say exactly which step fails, and why that step rather than the one before or after it. In particular, address whether (6) commits a fallacy of composition, whether the argument slides from the possibility of a forgery to the rational rejectability of evidence, and whether (2) is setting the bar too high for something to count as a backstop. If some corrected version of the argument survives, state it and its conclusion; if the conclusion is stronger than the premises allow, say what weaker conclusion the argument actually supports.
