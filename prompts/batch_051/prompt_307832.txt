I'm doing a philosophy of language module this term and we spent exactly one lecture on two-dimensional semantics, which mostly left me with the feeling that I'd been shown a diagram and not told what it was a diagram of. So I turned to a blog post by a science writer, and now I have a summary of the view stuck in my head that I dont fully trust. Its version, as best I can reconstruct it, is this. Two-dimensional semantics holds that every expression has two intensions rather than one. The primary intension is the meaning you grasp before you learn which world is actual: it is a rule that, for any way the world might turn out, tells you what the expression refers to there. The secondary intension is what you get once the actual world is fixed, and it does the ordinary work of determining reference across possible worlds. So 'water' has a primary intension that picks out the watery stuff in every possible world, and a secondary intension that picks out H2O in every possible world. This is supposed to explain the necessary a posteriori: 'water is H2O' is necessary because the secondary intension of 'water' is H2O everywhere, but a posteriori because you only find out which primary intension is realised by inspecting the actual world. The post adds that the apparatus generalises: names like 'Hesperus' have a primary intension (the bright star visible in such-and-such a position) and a secondary intension that picks out Venus. And it claims that two-dimensional semantics is what allows Chalmers to argue that if zombies are conceivable, then they are possible, which in turn shows physicalism is false.

I cant shake the sense that this is all a bit too tidy, but I don't know enough to say where. I'm especially unsure whether Chalmers, Jackson, and Kaplan are all really in the same business here, or whether the post is running together two or three different two-dimensional frameworks under one heading. Could you go through the summary and audit it: which parts would a careful defender accept, which are harmless popular-level simplifications, and which are actual mistakes or conflations? I'm inclined to think the Hesperus bit is dodgy, but I might just be picking up on a standard Kripkean worry. And does the advertised jump from conceivability to conclusions about consciousness survive the corrections, or is that where the post is doing most of its work?
