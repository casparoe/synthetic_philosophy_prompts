Working on a novel has made me weirdly aware of a thing about taste that I keep stubbing my toe against, and I’m hoping there’s a word for it — or at least a way of carving the territory that I can steal for how characters talk about each other’s judgement.

Three versions of it:

a) A friend can talk for an hour about the greatness of a novel she’s never read, because she’s absorbed the reviews, interviews, podcasts. When I finally read the book, her take is often sharper than mine. But the moment I find out she hasn’t actually read it, I discount the whole thing. Same with film: the person who’s seen it gets default authority over the person who’s only read the criticism, even when the second person’s judgement is plainly better.

b) If a trusted friend tells me the 4:10 train to Albany is cancelled, I don’t feel I need to stand on the platform before I’m entitled to say the train is cancelled. But if the same friend tells me a novella is devastating, I feel faintly fraudulent saying it’s devastating until I’ve read it. I’ll say “she says it’s devastating” — I report the testimony — but I won’t assert the property, the way I will with the train.

c) Stranger still: I’ll happilly pass along secondhand that a book is “structurally ingenious” or that a film “does something technically bold.” Those feel like claims testimony can settle. But secondhand that it’s “beautiful” or “moving” or “profound” feels different — as if the fact can’t be transferred, only the report of the fact.

So: does this cluster of intuitions have a name? And more to the point, is it one phenomenon or several? I know there’s a debate somewhere about whether taste can be trusted secondhand — but the versions I’ve come across treat it as one big divide, and I’m not sure my cases all sit on the same side of it. If the nearest established concept exists, tell me its name and then tell me where its edges are: is the relevant line between aesthetic and non-aesthetic claims, between descriptive and evaluative claims, between what can be settled by a rule and what has to be “tried on”? What about the case where someone’s taste is a known quantity — you’ve calibrated on a hundred books together — and she tells you a new one is excellent: have you learned something about the book even if you haven’t felt it? I’m not after a bare label; I want to know how the territory is carved, and whether the thing I keep noticing is even the thing the label picks out.
