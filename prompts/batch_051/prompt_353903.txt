Imagine an introductory physics textbook whose mechanics chapter says Newton's laws are time-reversal invariant: if a sequence of motions is physically allowed, then the exact reverse sequence, with every velocity flipped, is equally allowed. The same textbook's thermodynamics chapter says the entropy of an isolated system never decreases over time. Taken at face value those two claims clash, because any ordinary entropy-increasing process, run backwards, would be a mechanically legal entropy-decreasing process. Can the two be reconciled?
