I've been trying to figure out whether the etiological account of biological function survives de novo protein design, and I want help thinking through the argument rather than a literature survey. The etiological account says a trait's function is the effect it was selected for: something is a heart not just because it pumps blood, but because pumping blood is what hearts were selected for. The causal-role account says function is just what the thing does in the current system, no history needed. Now suppose a lab designs a protein from scratch at the sequence level to cleave some industrial plastic, and sticks it in a bacterium. There is no selection history in nature; the gene didn't get shaped over generations, someone wrote it down and synthesized it. But it seems wrong to say the protein has no function, and its hard to say its function isn't plastic-cleaving. Does the etiological account survive this? Is the designed protein actually a counterexample, or does it have an etiological function through the designer's intentions or the lab's screening procedures? And if the account has to stretch 'selected for' to cover human design, is that a repair of does it quietly give up the naturalistic payoff of the view? I'm interested in exactly which premise gives way.
