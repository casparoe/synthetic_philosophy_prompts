Take the old dust-specks-vs-torture setup: either one person is tortured for fifty years, or an astronomically huge number of people each get a single dust speck in the eye, where a speck is just a momentary sting and leaves no damage. Now vary it this way: keep the total number of speck-in-the-eye events fixed, but bunch them up, so one-thousandth as many people get a thousand specks apiece spread over an ordinary day and everyone else gets none. Does the additive utilitarian's answer change? More importantly, if bunching the harms like that makes someone change their mind about which option is worse, what does the flip show about whether their original judgment was tracking total harm, or instead tracking how harms are distributed across persons as opposed to within one person? Then work out what a threshold view and a lexical view would each say about the bunched version.
