A friend of mine has a distinction between people she calls "real" and people she calls "NPCs," of the sort one picks up in rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums, and I have been trying for months to reverse-engineer it. She swears she is not a solipsist: she will say, without hesitation, that every human in the cases below is conscious, and she waffles only about the pigeon. Yet she insists that "conscious" and "real" come apart for her, and that calling someone an NPC is not a claim that there is nothing it is like to be them.

I have kept a list of her verdicts:

A commuter who passes her without acknowledgment — NPC.
A barista who runs the standard greeting-and-order script — NPC.
A barista who stops after taking her order and warns her that her usual bus is rerouted because of construction — real.
A support agent who pastes policy paragraphs into the chat — NPC.
A panelist who gives the same answer he has given at four other events — NPC.
A panelist who, when she asks a question, says he doesn't know and works it through aloud in front of her — real.
Her partner mid-argument — real.
A seven-year-old who interrupts her reading to explain an invented species of lizard — real.
A street performer doing a rehearsed trick — NPC.
A character in a dream she is having — NPC.
A fluent LLM — NPC.
A pigeon that recognizes her and returns to her windowsill every morning — real.

I am about to ask her about three new cases, and I want to know what she will say before she says it:

A podcaster she listens to daily, who has no idea she exists, but whose style is exactly that of the second panelist — he keeps stopping, admitting he doesn't know, and thinking aloud on air.
A phone-bank volunteer who is visibly working from a script, but the script has been personalized with her name, her neighborhood, and the amount of her last donation.
A patient in a coma, unresponsive, whom she visits.

I care less about the bare labels than about the rule she appears to be running, at least one other rule that fits her previous verdicts equally well, what each candidate rule predicts for the three new cases, and your best guess for each with reasons. Then tell me which of the new cases, if any, settles which rule she is actually using, and what further case you would put to her if none of them does.

One more suspicion I want tested explicitly: I think her real/NPC distinction is not the solipsism question at all. It may be tracking something like whether the other person is co-authoring a live scene with her, rather than merely appearing in that scene as a character. Tell me whether her verdicts support that reading, whether a simpler rule does the job, and whether the coma case is the one that finally tells us whether she means anything about consciousness by "real."
