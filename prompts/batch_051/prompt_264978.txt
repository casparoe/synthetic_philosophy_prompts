A friend of mine works on mechanistic explanation and says a mechanism is really just entities and activities organized so that the activities produce the phenomenon, from setup through intermediates to termination. I keep finding cases where that's either too strict or too loose. Give me your own working definition of "mechanism" as it's used when a biologist says they've explained something mechanistically, and run it through these cases. (a) The neuron-firing story: voltage-gated channels open, sodium flows in, the membrane depolarizes, which opens more channels. I'm confident this is a mechanism. (b) A study showing that stress correlates with heart attacks but with no intervening steps specified: I'm confident this is not a mechanism. (c) Natural selection as an explanation of an adaptation, spelled out as heritable variation, differential survival, and inheritance. (d) A predator-prey model made of two coupled differential equations that reproduces the population oscillations but says nothing about what the organisms are doing step by step. (e) A single enzyme changing shape to bind a substrate. (f) A pathway diagram that just shows A goes to B goes to C with no account of how each step is brought about. Tell me which case force the biggest revision to your first pass, and where you land.
