I'm writing something about moral psychology and I've been going back and forth on the difference between envy and resentment, because people use them interchangably and I have a hunch there's a real distinction hiding under the sloppiness. I'd like a working definition—maybe a pair of definitions, one for each term—that actually does the sorting work, not just a one-line gloss like envy is wanting what someone else has. Here are the cases I want your account to handle, and I'd like a determinate verdict on each one, even if the verdict is "this is a borderline case because the definition is doing real work here."

First, a friend got a fellowship I also applied for, and I think she genuinely earned it, but I still feel a little twinge when she brings it up. Is that envy, or something milder? Second, a coworker got promoted because he's the boss's nephew. I'm angry about that, but the anger feels aimed at the process and the unfairness, not at him having the job. Is that resentment rather than envy, or are they the same emotion with different beliefs about desert attached? Third, there's the familiar talking point about the politics of envy: someone on welfare who is furious that billionaires exist while he can't feed his kids. Is that envy, resentment, righteous indignation, or some overlap? Fourth, Rawls's idea of excusable envy—the thought that envy felt by the worse-off toward the better-off stops being a personal vice and becomes something we shouldn't blame them for when the inequalities are too large. Whatever the merits of that idea, how would your definition classify it? Is excusable envy still envy, or has it become something else? Fifth, a person who would rather the good be destroyed than that you keep it, since he can't have it. Is that a species of envy, or a related but distinct attitude?

Please propose a definition (or pair of definitions), run each case through it, and if a case forces you to revise or split the definition, say so and defend the revision. Also, please write your answer in American English.
