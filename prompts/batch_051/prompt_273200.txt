I have been caught on a parallel between two distinctions and want help deciding whether it is a structural identity or a loose analogy. In Book V of the *Nicomachean Ethics*, Aristotle distinguishes the broad sense of justice from a narrower distributive sense. He introduces the broad sense as follows:

"Justice, then, in this sense of the word, is complete virtue, with the addition that it is displayed towards others."

He later says that this broad justice is about lawfulness and differs only in being exercised toward others; particular justice, by contrast, is about the fair distribution of goods and burdens, and Aristotle analyzes it as a kind of equality between persons according to a proportion, not as simple equality in the goods themselves.

The modern distinction I see echoing this is between moral equality and distributive equality in egalitarian political philosophy. Moral equality—the requirement that institutions treat each person with equal concern and respect—functions as a background constraint that is not about any particular distributable good; it is supposed to be prior to and encompassing any specific allocation. The "equality of what?" debate then asks which metric should be equalized or prioritized—welfare, resources, capabilities, opportunity for welfare—when we are doing first-order distribution. Modern theorists often treat the first question as settled while fighting over the second.

My question: is the modern split between moral equality and distributive equality the same structural move as Aristotle's split between justice as complete virtue and particular justice, or is the analogy misleading? If the mapping is genuine, then positions in the currency debate should be reconstructible as rival accounts of the proportionate equality Aristotle describes, and the reasons for rejecting one currency should mirror reasons for rejecting a proposed ratio. If not, where exactly does it come apart? Please state the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and identify at least one prediction the parallel makes that looks false.
