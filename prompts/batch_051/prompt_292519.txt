I'm a software engineer with basically no formal philosophy background, so apolagies if I mangle the terms here. I got into an argument after re-watching Margin Call (the 2011 movie about a Lehman-like investment bank that discovers overnight that its mortgage book is toxic and decides to dump the assets on its clients before the market catches on). My friend kept saying the bank is morally to blame, full stop. I kept saying that doesn't make sense: a company doesn't have a mind, doesn't decide anything, it's just a bunch of people in a hierarchy, so blaming "the firm" is lazy — you should have to point at which humans did the bad thing and say how much each one is on the hook. But the movie sort of punishes my view, because the decision never seems to live in any single character. The junior analyst finds the numbers, the senior analyst is horrified, the trading head pressures everyone to keep it quiet, the CEO makes the call, and the senior partners nod along. Each of them can offer a halfway-decent story about why the real fault lies somewhere else, and yet the firm does the thing anyway. So now I'm not sure the reductionist line does'nt actually survive contact with the movie, or at least that the movie is doing something to my intuition that my own argument isn't.

I'd like a careful reading of Margin Call through the corporate moral agency debate: can a company itself be a proper target of blame, or is blame always and only about individual people?
