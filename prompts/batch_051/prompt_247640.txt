i've been turning the four-colour theorem over as a way into the question of what a proof even is, and i want a poem that does some of that work. Write a sonnet on the Appel–Haken proof. The octave should build the naive picture: a sweet, concrete problem (any map, four colours, no two neighbouring regions matching), and the elegant reduction of infinitely many planar maps to a finite unavoidable set of configurations a computer then checks. The volta should be the moment the reader realises the finiteness is a trap: it was still something like 1,936 configurations, checked over more than a thousand hours of machine time, and the full argument is too long for any single human reader to actually survey.

Then the sestet should get at the philosophical stakes without moralising: a proof nobody can survey is either a new kind of proof or a new kind of trust, and it matters which. Make the closing couplet say exactly what has to give way—i'd put my money on surveyability, not certainty—but leave enough edge that a philosopher of mathematics wouldnt think youve strawmanned the camp that says it is still a proof, just a long one. Accuracy matters more to me here than beauty, though both would be nice.
