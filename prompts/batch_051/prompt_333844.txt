I'm a programming-language theorist and I work every day in types, terms, kinds, type constructors, subtyping, intersection and union types, inheritance, interfaces, and the difference between a value and its type. Here is my working summary of the positions, in my own words. Correct any label if I have it wrong, but don't turn it into a history lecture.

1. The two-nature (Chalcedonian) settlement: one subsistent person—the eternal Word or Son—is the single subject and agent throughout; that one person has two complete natures, a divine nature and a human nature, united without the natures being mixed, changed, divided, or separated. Each nature keeps its own properties and operations, but whatever belongs to either nature can be attributed to the one person, in either direction (the communicatio idiomatum). So the claim that God was born of Mary is true not because the divine nature came to exist in time, but because the very person who is God is also the person who has, and acts through, a human nature.

2. The two-subject view (what later polemic called Nestorianism): two natures and also two subjects or persons—the Word and the man Jesus—joined in a closeness of will and honor rather than being one subsistent subject. Mary may be called mother of Christ but not Mother of God.

3. The one-nature view (what the councils called Eutychianism, and the broader monophysite position): after the union there is one nature. The human nature is absorbed or swallowed up, or the two blend into a single new nature, so the result is no longer fully consubstantial with created humanity.

4. The Apollinarian view: the Word takes a human body and an animal soul, and the place of the human rational mind is occupied by the divine Word itself. The result is one subject and one controlling principle, but the humanity is incomplete.

5. The later refinement (monothelite versus dyothelite): whether the one person has a single will and single principle of operation—the divine one—or two wills and two operations corresponding to the two natures.
