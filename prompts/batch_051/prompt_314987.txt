Analyze South Africa's transition from the interim Constitution of 1993 to the final Constitution of 1996 as a case study in the paradoxes of legal self-reference — specifically, the difficulties that arise when a legal order purports to govern the conditions of its own amendment and replacement. The salient facts are these: the interim constitution, adopted as part of the negotiated end of apartheid, established a Constitutional Assembly and required it to draft a final constitution consistent with a set of enumerated constitutional principles drawn from the negotiation; it also created a Constitutional Court and required that the final text be certified by that court as complying with those principles before it could come into force. The court initially declined to certify the draft, and the text was amended and resubmitted before certification was granted.

Using the concepts of self-amendment, self-entrenchment, and the limits of a legal order's authority over its own successor, work through the following. First, specify who the relevant actors are, what options the interim legal order formally gave them, and what kind of authority each was purporting to exercise. Second, spell out what the formal-legal and self-reference-based analysis predicts: can a constitution validly bind the body and the procedure that must replace it, and can a court constituted under the interim order validly pronounce on whether the new order conforms to the constraints under which it was drafted? Third, identify which features of the South African case this formal picture explains well and which it misdescribes or leaves unexplained — consider, for instance, where the constitutional principles actually derived their authority from, the role of the multilaterally negotiated pact, and the political legitimacy of a transition in which the legal forms of one order were used to bring about another. Finally, state what the episode should lead us to conclude about the paradox itself: is it a genuine constraint internal to law, a merely formal puzzle that dissolves once power and legitimacy are brought in, or a political question dressed in legal form? Throughout, separate what is settled in constitutional theory from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
