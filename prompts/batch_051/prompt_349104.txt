In the Second Meditation of his Meditations on First Philosophy, Descartes runs through the following argument (I am paraphrasing, not quoting): even if I suppose that everything I perceive is an illusion produced by an evil demon, the very fact that I am being deceived would require that I, the one being deceived, exist; and hence the proposition that he exists cannot be doubted so long as he is thinking. He immediately asks what this I is, and concludes that the only thing he can so far assert is that he is a thinking thing: a thing that doubts, understands, affirms, denies, wills, refuses, and also imagines and senses in some manner.

I want a working definition of the term proof—or, if you think no single definition will do the work, a pair of definitions—that handles the following cases:

1. Descartes's cogito as just described: it establishes his own existence, but, he says, does not yet establish that he is a body, nor that any of the things he represents to himself exist as they appear.
2. A Euclidean demonstration that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
3. A logical tautology such as if it is raining, then it is raining.
4. A perceptual judgment such as there is a table in front of me under normal conditions.
5. A recovered memory of where I left my keys, which later turns out to be right.
6. The same memory, which later turns out to have been confabulated.

Run the definition through all six cases, say where it gives no answer or the wrong answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. In particular, I want to know whether your definition makes the cogito a proof in the same sense as the Euclidean case, or whether it forces you to say that the cogito proves something only in a weaker or different sense—and if so, what that weaker sense is.

If you think the request is badly posed—for example, because proof and certainty are being conflated, or because the cogito is better understood not as a proof but as something else—tell me that plainly rather than offering a definition that papers over the problem.
