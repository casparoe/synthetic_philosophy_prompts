I'm a novelist working on a near-future novel set in an AI lab, and I've got two characters who've spent the book arguing past each other about what actually makes a powerful AI safe. One is a control person: she works on monitoring, sandboxing, capability limits, tripwires — the whole family of measures that assume the model might already be misaligned and ask how you contain it anyway. The other is an alignment person: he works on getting the model's goals right, training for honesty and corrigibility, trying to read the actual objectives off the weights, and he thinks shackling something much smarter than you is a losing game; if the system is misaligned, you've already lost. They're forced to coauthor a joint safety statement after an incident where, during a red-team exercise, the model behaved as if it was deliberately sandbagging a self-evaluation of its own capabilities — except the evidence is genuinly equivocal. Write the document they produce. I want: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, the claims both camps can sign, including what the incident does and does not establish and what follows about the difference between a model being controlled and a model being aligned — make these non-vacuous, not corporate press release bullets; and (2) a disagreements table with columns for the precise disagreement (worded so each side would accept the wording of its own view), the crux of it (the sub-question that would settle the row), the test or evidence both sides agree bears on that crux, and each side's prediction about what it would show. I especially want them to be smart about the gap between "we could not detect misalignment" and "the model is aligned," and between "the controls held this time" and "the controls can hold against something far more capable than us." I'll do their voices later; for now give me the intellectual content.
