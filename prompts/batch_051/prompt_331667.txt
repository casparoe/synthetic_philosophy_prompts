Write a deadpan parody of a startup's public transparency FAQ, about 650-800 words, in which a company that sells a large language model gets out ahead of its hallucination problem by leaning into Frankfurt's old distinction from On Bullshit. The company's line, said in a cheerful, helpful corporate voice, should be roughly this: our model never lies, because it has no beliefs or intentions; and it doesn't merely err either, because an error is what happens when something aims at the truth and misses, and our model isn't aiming at the truth. What it is, in the technical Frankfurt sense, is a bullshitter: its outputs are produced with indifference to the way things actually are, optimized for confidence, coherence, and helpfulness instead. The company should be weirdly proud of this.

Format the piece as six or seven numbered Q&As. Suggested questions (adjust if you like): Is your model trained to tell the truth? When it says something false, is it lying? Then is it an error? Would you call it bullshit? If the model has no intentions, who is doing the bullshitting? Can anyone be held responsible when the output is false? Include one short deadpan quote from an invented CEO.

The jokes need to come from getting Frankfurt right - keep the distinction in your own words, no verbatim quotes from him. The liar tracks the truth closely enough to hide it on purpose; the bullshitter just doesn't care. So the company can happily explain that its reward training tends to favor fluent, confident, helpful answers, and that any coincidence between those and true answers is a nice accident, not the objective. But the piece also has to quietly land the real conceptual problem: bullshit is normally something an agent does, so if the model isn't an agent, the actual bullshitter in the story is the company and its training decisions, not the weights. A reader who knows On Bullshit should feel the distinction is being used exactly right; someone who doesn't should still come away able to tell a lie from an error from bullshit.

After the FAQ, write a seperate section titled Commentary, 200-300 words, explaining the main choices: why hallucination is treated as neither lying nor error, how you moved the bullshit attribution from the model to the company, and anywhere you knowingly fudged the Frankfurt picture for the joke.
