Here's a proposed rule for a big private foundation — call it the One Ledger Foundation. They're sick of board fights where one trustee argues for malaria nets, another for legal aid, another for arts funding, and every meeting re-runs the same argument about what "doing good" even means. So they adopt the following. The foundation commissions a single 0-to-100 "flourishing index" meant to combine health, income, education, dignity, belonging, and beauty. The weights are set once a year by a random sample of five thousand citizens, who have to allocate 100 points across the six components in a budgeting app; the weights are then frozen for the year. Every applicant has to state its expected impact in flourishing points per dollar, using a common measurement manual. The board is allowed to compare proposals only by projected points per dollar; no one may argue that a value matters but is hard to measure, and anything that can't be scored is ineligible. An audit team reviews a random tenth of completed grants against their predicted scores, and grantees whose projections miss by more than twenty points are banned for three years.

Walk me through how this actually plays out. What gets funded in year one, and what has quietly disappeared by year five? How do applicants re-engineer their programs and their write-ups, what do they stop doing, and where does the first gaming show up — and can the audit team catch it, or does the audit just create its own layer of distortion? What happens to the citizen panel as they realize their weights are steering billions, and to the foundation's own sense of purpose? Then tell me whether the failure modes can be patched without reopening the original values fight — and what this little experiment shows about whether our values are really many, or really one thing we're just very bad at measuring.
