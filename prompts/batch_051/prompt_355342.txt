I'm a retired chemist (physical chemistry, if it matters), and over the last couple of years I've been working through formal epistemology on my own. One idea I keep coming back to is van Fraassen's Reflection principle, or at least the informal version of it: roughly, my current degree of belief in a proposition should equal what I now expect my degree of belief in it will be later in life. As a scientist this sounds backwards to me, because I spent a career treating my future self as someone who might know more than I do, but only if there was a specific reason to think so — new data, a better model, a sharper instrument. Then I realised I don't actually follow the principle at all, and I've turned my resistance into a policy.

My policy is: I don't defer to predicted future-me. If I can see, from here, that five years from now I'll probably have a lower credence in some claim simply because I'll have forgotten details, lost track of the litterature, or gotten tired of defending it, I don't lower my current credence now. I treat current-me as the anchor and future-me as a source of information only when I can, right now, identify the content of that information. So predicted belief change, by itself, carries no weight for me. I've even gone further: on questions where I have a settled expert opinion, I leave notes telling my future self not to revise without writing down the new evidence, because I expect the revision to be memory loss rather than learning. I know the standard exceptions to the principle are cases where you expect your future self to be irrational, drugged, or forgetful, and my worry is that getting old is a slow-motion version of all three. Some friends in a philosophy reading group say this is just old-person stubbornness dressed up to look principled, but it seems to me exaclty what a scientist should do with an instrument (my own future head) that they know is degrading.

Is my policy defensible as an epistemic rule, or is it a bias? Under what conditions should predicted future belief move current belief, if ever? And am I entitled to act on my predictions of my own cognitive decline, or is that a separate question from whether the policy itself is rational? Please answer in British English by the way.
