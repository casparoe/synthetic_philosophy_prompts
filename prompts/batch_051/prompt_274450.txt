In my philosophy of perception seminar, I keep coming back to a pair of observations about the boundary between visual experience and the use of visual information, and I would like to know whether either has an established name and how the two are related. In the first sort of case, a subject briefly views a grid of letters and is subjectively confident that she saw all twelve in visual detail, yet when asked to report them she can produce only three or four and the remainder are no better than guesses; the impression of a rich, detailed experience persists even though it cannot be retrieved. In the second sort of case, a subject reports no awareness of a stimulus, yet her behavior shows that the stimulus was processed far enough to prime related words, guide motor responses, or shift attention. So experience appears to outstrip reportability in one case, while reportable and behavior-guiding information appears to outstrip experience in the other. Is there an established term for the first mismatch, and is there an overarching way of describing this two-way dissociation? I care less about the label than about the carving: what should a global workspace theory say about each case, and does the first case show that a state can be conscious without being globally broadcast? If the nearest established concept applies to only one of the two cases, what exactly distinguishes the case it fits from the case it does not?
