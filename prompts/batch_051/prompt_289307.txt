I am an undergraduate in a course on Nietzsche and the body, and I recently read a popular intellectual-history piece that summarizes a passage from the first section of Ecce Homo, Why I Am So Clever. The passage, as described in the piece, claims roughly this: Nietzsche says that the proper question of nutrition is at bottom a question of the stomach, that the German spirit is a kind of indigestion that never finishes anything, and that prejudices can be traced back to the intestines. I want to use your response to check the piece before I rely on it for a short seminar paper.

Here is the piece’s summary: Nietzsche’s discussion of diet in Ecce Homo shows that he was a medical materialist avant la lettre. On this view, he thinks that ideals, values, and apparently abstract philosophical systems are downstream of physiological condition. Because he cured his own migraine and gastric problems by adopting a plain, self-disciplined diet, avoiding alcohol and stimulants, and treating climate as part of health, he concluded that Christianity, German idealism, and the morality of self-denial are best explained as bad digestion dressed up as metaphysics. The article claims this is why Nietzsche’s critique of Christianity cannot be answered with theological arguments: it is really a dietary diagnosis, not a philosophical position.
