I'm a PhD student in linguistics, and one of my office-mates does language documentation. His go-to analogy, which he treats as basically argument-ending, is that languages are like species. His standard version runs like this: a language is a unique historical lineage shaped by drift and selection, like a species; its grammar and lexicon are the accumulated adaptations of a community to its social and physical environment, like a genome is the accumulated adaptation of a lineage to a niche; when the last speaker dies that's an extinction event, full stop, not just a shame — and unlike a species, it leaves no fossils you can go back and study intact; and linguistic diversity is functional in the way biodiversity is, a pool of variation that keeps cultural evolution resilient, the way genetic diversity buffers ecosystems. From this he concludes language preservation shouldn't be treated as a heritage hobby but as a conservation problem, with the same moral seriousness as the sixth mass extinction, maybe more, because when a language dies we lose the only record of how to be human in that particular place. I agree with the conclusion, but the analogy gives me a rash.

Give me an audit, not a pep talk. First lay out the correspondence table: what in the language case is meant to be the genotype, the phenotype, the niche, mutation, selection, speciation, extinction, the ecosystem, the invasive species? Then the disanalogies — I can already see a few (speakers aren't genes, languages borrow and creolise, Hebrew) but I want to know wich ones actually matter for the argument he's making and which are just decoration, and what the species frame is quietly importing into the language case that hasn't been earned. Does the moral conclusion survive if the analogy is doing mostly rhetorical work, or does it start to look unsupported once the mapping breaks? And if you had to swap in a better source domain — crop varieties, musical traditions, whatever — what would it be and what would it fix? Answer in British English by the way. And no need for references, I want the argument, not a reading list.
