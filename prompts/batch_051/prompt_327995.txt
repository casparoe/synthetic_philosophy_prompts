Roughly, an ideal intervention on X with respect to Y in Woodward's picture meets four conditions: it changes X, it doesn't affect Y except through X, it breaks X's usual dependence on other causes of it, and it's independent of other causes of Y that run to Y without going through X. Now take a central bank that hikes nominal interest rates hard to bring down inflation. The hike is itself set in response to the same macro conditions that drive inflation; the announcement shifts inflation expectations before any borrower actually faces higher rates; and whatever effect there is runs through housing, the exchange rate, and expectations all at once. Is the rate hike an intervention on interest rates with respect to inflation in anything like Woodward's sense? Work through it criterion by criterion, and where it fails say whether that's because the criteria are to strict, because monetary policy is just a bad fit, or because 'intervention' isn't doing real causal work here. Also say whether this case makes the circularity worry better or worse (the worry that interventions are themselves defined in causal terms, so the account moves in a circle).
