i've never taken a philosophy class but I've been chewing on this for years and I want someone to actually stress test it instead of being polite. I call it the conservation of guilt. The core idea is that guilt in a group behaves like a conserved quantity: it never really gets destroyed, only moved around. The old scapegoat ritual is the cleanest example. A community piles up accumulated wrongdoing, loads it all onto one goat (or one person), and when the goat is driven out or killed everyone feels clean again. My claim is the guilt didnt vanish, it got transferred. If the goat is driven off, the guilt literally leaves with it; if it's killed, it soaks into the ground or gets handed over to the god. You see the same swap in the office scapegoat, the political fall guy, the kid in the family who always catches the blame. The relief people report is real because the guilt really did move somewhere, not because it's gone.

I can already see one case where my rule looks broken: when someone confesses to the person they harmed and gets forgiven, the guilt seems genuinely gone, not relocated. Unless forgiveness is just another transfer, where the victim volunteers to carry it? That thought bugs me because it makes forgiveness sound a little creepy.

First tell me what I'm actually getting at in your own words, then tell me where it falls apart. Is this a real concept or just a metaphor stretched till it sounds like physics? Does the total amount stay fixed, or does moving guilt around usually create more of it, the way retelling a rumor grows the rumor? Give me the strongest objections and be honest about whether the idea is worth keeping or should be dropped.
