I'm stuck on a decision and a friend's attempt to help made me more confused. I have to choose between moving back to my hometown to take care of my dad and taking a research job across the country. When I try to think it through, I notice the two options pull on me in ways that don't share an obvious scale: the job appeals to curiosity, ambition, and a shot at doing something lasting; staying appeals to loyalty, gratitude, and a sense that I'd be missing time with my dad that I can't get back later. They don't feel like different amounts of one thing — they feel like different kinds of reasons.

My friend says I'm wrong. He says all the reasons are secretly one thing, like expected wellbeing or the choice I'd endorse after thinking it through with unlimited time and help. On his view, the experience of distinct values is a heuristic my brain uses becuase it can't run the full comparison; underneath it's all one currency. I can't tell if I find that implausible or just unpleasant. And even if he's right, I'm not sure it changes how I should choose: if the common currency is real but I can't actually read it off, maybe I should keep treating the values as separate anyway.

So, two questions: is my friend's view right, or at least more plausible than my instinct says? And if it were true, does it matter in practice, or is the reducing step sort of irrelevant to how I should decide?
