At the end of a seminar on the ontology of music, a sonicist and a contextualist about musical works agree to produce an adversarial-collaboration statement. The sonicist holds that a musical work is a repeatable sound-structure: anything that presents that structure — live performance, studio recording, MIDI realization, independent duplicate — is an instance of the same work, regardless of instrument or causal origin. The contextualist holds that a musical work is an indicated sound-structure whose identity includes the composer's specified performance means and creative context, so that changing the instrumentation or the causal history can change the work even when the resulting sound is indistinguishable. Draft the document they should co-sign. It should contain: (i) a numbered agreed-claims section on scores, live performances, studio recordings, transposition, and minor mistakes, written so the claims are substantive rather than vacuous; (ii) a two-column disagreements table stating the residual dispute in wording each side would accept for its own column; (iii) a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side; and (iv) for each crux, a concrete case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The cases should include at least: a Bach cello suite played on marimba; a note-for-note sonic duplicate of a Chopin mazurka produced by a non-intentional machine process; a Beatles song whose canonical version is a studio recording, performed live by a band with different instrumentation but the same melody, harmony, and words; and a fixed-media electronic work that exists only as a finished audio file. If any part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, add a short separate section saying which part and why.
