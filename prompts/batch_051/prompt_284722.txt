I run a small philosophy of mind discussion group that just spent three weeks on Malcolm and Dennett on whether dreams are experiences, and it ended in exactly the kind of mess I love: half the room now says they hold Malcolm's view but then describes dreams as things they watch from the inside, and the self-described Dennett fans keep talking as if he denies we dream at all, which I'm pretty sure is not the point. So I want to make a short 'which theory of dreaming do you actually hold?' quiz for the next session, and I'd like your help designing it.

The quiz should have eight to ten multiple-choice questions, each one a little concrete case or scenario, like: a lucid dreamer who signals with prearranged eye movements during REM; a long, elaborate dream that must have fit into a couple of minutes of REM; an alarm clock beep getting woven into the dream; someone who claims they never dream but their partner reports twitching and mumbling; whether a dream feels more like watching a film or like daydreaming; whether you can really be in pain in a dream or only seem to be; being woken mid-dream versus sleeping through and never reporting; and a report of dreamlike mental activity from non-REM sleep. Use whichever of these actually discriminate, and add others if needed. The questions and the answer options have to be phrased so someone can answer without knowing which position their voting for, so no item may ask anything like 'do you think dreams are experiences?'

I'd like the scoring key to be transparent: each answer option should contribute weights toward the leaves, and the result is the highest-scoring leaf, with one or two tie-breaker questions if you can manage. The leaves I care about are: the ordinary real-time experience view; the imagination model, on which dreaming is sleeping imagining rather than quasi-perception; Malcolm's no-experience view, on which the dream report is the criterion rather than evidence of some earlier private episode; Dennett's cassette-style view, on which the narrative is loaded into memory at or around waking; and a lucid-dreaming realism that treats real-time sleep signals as decisive. But please don't start from the names and force the quiz to match them. Start from the logical space of answer patterns, especially the split between 'the dream ran in real time during sleep', 'it was inserted or edited at waking', and 'it was an imaginative state all along', then locate each position in that space, and say which answer combinations nobody occupies because the view would be incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Be explicit about any two positions the quiz can't pull apart, and whether that's because the views genuinely collapse on the cases you chose or because the cases need to be sharpened.

The group has read both papers but nobody is a professional philosopher, so keep the writing casual and the philosophy honest.
