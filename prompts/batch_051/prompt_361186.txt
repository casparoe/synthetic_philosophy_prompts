I teach a philosophy-and-film course and we screen The Matrix next week. I want the discussion to center on disjunctivism and its reply to the argument from illusion, not the usual how-do-you-know-you're-not-a-brain-in-a-vat shuffle. The film seems to assume that if an experience inside the Matrix is indistinguishable from a real one, it is the same kind of experience, just with a different cause. Disjunctivism denies exactly that: a veridical perception and an indistinguishable hallucination are two different kinds of state, not one shared kind of state with two kinds of cause. Read the film against that view. Where does the plot actually need the indistinguishable-means-same premise, where does it quietly insist on a real/simulated difference anyway, and where does the movie run together not being able to tell from the inside with there being no difference that matters? I don't want my students to come away thinking it's just skepticism with kung fu.
