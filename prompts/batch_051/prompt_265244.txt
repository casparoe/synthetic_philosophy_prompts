I am a backend engineer on a mobile product team, with no formal background in philosophy. Recently we debated whether to move our app to a 64-bit-only build. The change would improve performance and simplify maintenance for the large majority of active users, but it would leave a small minority on older 32-bit phones without further updates or with a badly degraded experience. A senior engineer closed the debate by saying, in effect, that this obviously does "the greatest good for the greatest number," so shipping is the only sensible call. I have been unable to shake the feeling that the sentence is doing too much work, but I cannot separate its possible meanings myself.

Please treat that slogan as your object and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making. For each reading, specify what counts as "good," whose good counts over what time horizon, how the "greatest number" enters (largest majority, summed welfare, average welfare, or some other aggregation rule), and whether the slogan is being offered as a decision procedure or merely as a vague ideal. Then give a verdict on each reading: trivial, substantively defensible but contestable, or false. Say which of the readings would actually support overriding the minority in our case, and which would not. I am especially interested in which readings treat each user as a separate person whose loss cannot be canceled by being added to someone else's gain, and which readings quietly treat the whole user base as a single pool of value that can be summed. I have since heard this discomfort described as a worry about the separateness of persons, and that seems right to me, but I do not know how to cash it out against this particular slogan. I want a menu of readings I can bring back to the team, not a single favored interpretation, and I want it to be clear which reading the senior engineer's argument actually needs.
