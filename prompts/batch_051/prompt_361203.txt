I am a high school student moderating a Q&A for my school's philosophy and economics club. Our guest is a labor economist who argues that leisure is a basic right, not a reward for productivity; she therefore supports a statutory 35-hour work week and a right to disconnect, and she treats the Protestant work ethic as the main cultural obstacle to both. She tends to deflect objections about output, wages, and competitiveness by saying, in effect, that each treats leisure as a cost rather than a right. Write a sequence of interview questions that makes this deflection progressively harder to sustain. Press the strongest versions of the objections, anticipate her standard replies, and include the follow-ups. At least one question should challenge her on whether the Protestant work ethic is a rival account of work's value rather than merely a cultural error, and at least one should ask how she handles Japan's karoshi (death from overwork) cases, where overwork seems to provide identity and status. End with a question about what evidence, such as data from countries that have adopted shorter work weeks or workers' own revealed preferences, could count against her view. Please write the answer in American English.
