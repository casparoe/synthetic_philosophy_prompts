So I'm a novelist, and I've spent the last few years banging my head against a book set in the 1640s that follows five characters at roughly the same moment: a silver refiner in Potosí, a spice trader in the Moluccas, a scribe in Istanbul, a weaver in London, and a tax clerk in Hangzhou. They're connected by the silver-for-goods circuits of the period, but I don't want any of them to be the main character. The problem is that no matter how I arrange the chapters, some location quietly becomes the "home base" the reader returns to, and it's almost always the European one. If I try to fix it by giving everyone equal space, the book stops feeling like it has many centers and starts feeling like it has none—which isn't the same thing at all.

I'm applying for a six-month fellowship at a foundation that funds projects by artists who also have a conceptual question, and they want a one-page research proposal. The question I'd work on is whether a single narrative past can be told from everywhere at once—or whether the form of a novel inevitably forces a center, and if it does, what a genuinely non-Eurocentric, polycentric history could even be. So here's what I need from you: write that proposal. State the question more precisely than I just did, explain why the existing positions—connected history, world-systems approaches, microhistory, the usual debates about Eurocentrism—leave it unsettled, and lay out how a philosophical rather than empirical project would make progress, specifically by getting clear on what a narrative "center" even is and whether polycentric form avoids it or just relocates it. Then say what a useful deliverable looks like: something I could actually use while drafting, maybe a set of constraints or tests for whether a chapter arrangement is sneaking in a center, plus what would count as failure. And include a short answer to the objection the board will definitely raise, namely why a foundation should fund a novelist to do philosophy about narrative perspective instead of just going off and writing the damn novel. Dont bother with a bibliography or citations; I'll fill those in myself. Keep the scope realistic for six months, and keep it to about a page.
