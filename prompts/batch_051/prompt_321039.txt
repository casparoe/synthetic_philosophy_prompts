I've been trying to settle a question that keeps coming up whenever I read about health, and I'd like your help in the form of a working definition rather than a survey of opinions. The question is: what actually makes something a disease?

I don't have any training in this area, so I'll describe the two positions I keep meeting in my own words. The first says a disease is an objective biological breakdown: some part or process is failing to do what it is statistically normal for members of a species to be able to do, independent of whether anyone happens to dislike it. The second says a disease is ultimately a value judgment: we count a condition as a disease exactly when we regard it as bad for the person and as a legitimate object of medical attention. The first view feels satisfyingly objective, but it seems to turn harmless biological variations into diseases and to miss some things we clearly treat as diseases. The second view is honest about how much medicine is driven by what people care about, but it seems to make the answer nothing more than a matter of opinion. I want a definition that takes both pressures seriously.

I'm confident about a few cases. Measles, a broken femur, and type 1 diabetes are diseases. Pregnancy, being left-handed, a normal menstrual period, and ordinary grief shortly after a bereavement are not. The following are the ones I cannot place, and that is where I need the definition to do real work:

- A 45-year-old whose blood pressure is consistently in the low 130s over 80s, who feels entirely well, has no organ damage, and has been offered medication.
- A person with a body mass index in the low 30s, good fitness for their weight, and normal blood tests, who has tried repeatedly to lose weight and has remained the same size for years.
- An adult who has always been restless, distractible, and disorganized; he is failing in an office job but functioned well in an outdoor, physically active job.
- A healthy person with one sickle-cell gene and one normal gene, with no symptoms and no symptoms expected in ordinary life.
- A 25-year-old who cannot fall asleep before 3 a.m. and sleeps soundly until 11 a.m.; on that schedule she is well rested and unimpaired, but a normal office schedule leaves her chronically sleep-deprived.
- A person with slight high-frequency hearing loss on an audiogram who does not notice it in daily life and does not want any treatment or accommodation.
- A 30-year-old with the gene variant that will certainly cause Huntington's disease later, but whose brain and movements are currently completely normal, with symptoms not expected for roughly twenty years.
- A healthy 52-year-old woman going through menopause with hot flushes and the end of periods; it is a universal life stage, but it is sometimes treated as a hormone deficiency.
- An endurance athlete with a resting heart rate of 38, no symptoms, normal exercise capacity, and a cardiologist's note that this is an athletic adaptation.
- A gay adult in a tolerant society, with no distress or impairment; I add this because the American Psychiatric Association only removed homosexuality from its manual of mental disorders in the early 1970s, by a vote.

For each borderline case, give a verdict and your reasoning. If a case exposes a flaw in your definition, say what change it forces; I would rather see the definition get better than see you fit the cases to it. Once you have a version you are satisfied with, say which parts of the biological-breakdown view and which parts of the value-judgment view survive in it, and which parts you had to give up.

Please return the whole answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- proposed_definition (string): your initial working definition, before being tested on the borderline cases.
- clear_case_verdicts (array): one object for each of my clear cases, each with case (string), verdict (string, either "disease" or "not_disease"), and reason (string).
- borderline_case_verdicts (array): one object for each borderline case, each with case (string), verdict (string, one of "disease", "not_disease", or "ambiguous"), and reason (string, including which part of the definition forced the verdict).
- revisions (array): objects with trigger_case (string) and change_made (string), in the order you make them; use an empty array if no revision was needed.
- final_definition (string): the definition after all revisions.
- how_the_two_views_fare (string): a short statement of which commitments of the biological-breakdown view and which of the value-judgment view are preserved in your final definition, and which you abandoned.
