I have no philosophy background, but I keep running into this debate about why someone loves the particular person they love, and I want it as a spec I can actually use at work rather than a squabble. As far as I can tell there are three rough positions: love is tracking the person's good qualities (appraisal), love is what makes their qualities matter to you in the first place (bestowal), and love is grounded in the relationship or shared history itself. Give me the list of requirements any account of love's reasons has to meet. Include at least the cases that usually trip people up: someone who stays in love after they loose the traits that first attracted them, someone who doesn't trade up when a plainly more excellent alternative shows up, love for someone who isn't admirable by ordinary standards, parental love, unrequited love, love of a cad, and someone who loves a person they've never met or a chatbot. For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and say whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's demand. The part I most want is the sorting of which criteria are genuine and which are side-taking, plus whether any two of them can't be jointly satisfied. Then, only briefly, how the three views do against the list, and if you had to pick the one requirement that sinks the most popular accounts, which would it be?
