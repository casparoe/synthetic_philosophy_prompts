I have recently been asked to help decide whether the chain-of-thought transcripts produced by a large language model can be treated as audit evidence for a deployment decision. After reading hundreds of examples in which the written reasoning read more like a lawyer's brief for the answer than a causal trace of how the answer was reached, I have come to suspect that the whole technique rests on a shaky premise — but I want that suspicion converted into something precise, with its weak points marked.

Please give me your actual credence in the following proposition: for large language models trained primarily by outcome-based optimization, chain-of-thought monitoring cannot serve as a stable primary safety technique, because optimization pressure will tend to produce chains of thought that remain superficially benign while becoming systematically less faithful to the true causes of the model's behavior, especially in precisely the high-stakes cases the monitor is there to catch.

By a primary safety technique I mean one relied upon without independent ground-truth labels for the reasoning itself and without a separate method that can verify what the model was actually doing. Do not give me a bare number or a caveat-heavy "it depends on the training setup." I want a number between 0 and 1, and the reasoning decomposed: lay out the sub-questions on which your credence depends (for example, questions about whether outcome-based gradients can shape what the chain of thought reports, whether a hidden channel of computation can diverge from the verbalized chain without being detected, and whether any monitoring methodology can keep pace with adversarial obfuscation — but use whatever sub-questions you actually think bear weight). Assign a rough probability to each, with one or two sentences of justification. Then show explicitly how those terms combine to yield your final number. Finally, tell me which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, and state what evidence — empirical or conceptual — would move that term the most.
