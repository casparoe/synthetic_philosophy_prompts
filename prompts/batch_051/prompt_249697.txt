From what I understand, Solomonoff induction is supposed to give a fully objective Bayesian prior: it weights each computable hypothesis by 2 to the power of minus the length of its shortest program, and it converges to the truth in the limit. So that looks like Occam's razor made mathematically exact, and it makes me wonder why Bayesians still treat the choice of prior as subjective judgment. Shouldn't rational agents be required to use the universal prior, and doesn't that settle once and for all that simpler theories are more likely to be true? I know the thing is noncomputable, but idealizations are used all over the place, so that alone doesn't feel like the real objection. What am I missing? Please answer in American English.
