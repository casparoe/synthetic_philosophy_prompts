An article I read on the history of penal reform summarizes Bentham's views on sexual morality as follows: 'Bentham held that a law is justified only by the pain it prevents, and since sex between consenting adults in private harms no one, it could never properly be made a crime. On his account, the public's sincere disgust at such acts is mere irrational antipathy, not a real cost to be weighed, and therefore no consensual sexual act could ever be punished consistently with his principles. What the law called sodomy was, he thought, religious prejudice wearing legal dress.' The article concludes that Bentham should be counted an early defender of the harm principle, and that any legal system which still punishes private adult consensual conduct is irrational by his lights. This seems to me too tidy, as if Bentham is being turned into a modern libertarian a century early, but I do not know enough to say where the distortion lies. Please go through the summary and tell me which parts a careful reader of Bentham would accept, which parts they would call subtle distortions, and whether the article's final conclusion follows from what is accurate in the summary.
