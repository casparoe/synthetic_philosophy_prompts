So here's the assignment: build me a thought experiment where the simple conditional analysis comes apart from both of its usual rivals in the nonstandard direction. Let O be some ordinary object, S a stimulus, M a manifestation, and engineer it so that:

- Simple conditional analysis: O is disposed to M when S, because the counterfactual "if S were to occur, O would M" comes out true.
- The intrinsic-causal-basis fix (the Lewis-ish one, where O needs some internal property that would produce M absent interference): O is NOT disposed to M.
- A primitivist about dispositions (someone who thinks fragility and friends are real, non-analyzable properties, and conditionals are just unreliable symptoms) also says NOT disposed, but for a reasons distinct from the causal-basis person's.

The "yes" verdict should come from a mimic — the manifestation would fire reliably, but not because O has the disposition in question. Two no's, one yes, with the two no's not being the same argument. Constraints: mundane objects, kitchen-or-garage tech at most, no sorcerers, no just stipulating that the stimulus produces the manifestation, and the scenario should fit in one paragraph. After the scenario, walk through the reasoning each of the three positions gives and pin down the structural feature doing the work (I'd bet on the manifestation's source being causally outside O, but show the work). Then tell me: if you delete that feature, does the case collapse into a standard mask or fink case, or something even more boring? And how would a defender of the simple conditional analysis push back — are they forced to say the disposition belongs to a larger composite (O plus whatever gizmo), or is there a better move? Finish by briefly contrasting the mimic with a fink and with a mask so it's clear why the mimic is the one that makes the causal-basis fix and the primitivist converge.
