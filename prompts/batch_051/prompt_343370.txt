I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep running into a conceptual obstacle I cannot name. I work on autonomous agents, and the design requirement sounds simple: the agent should accomplish a narrowly specified task while changing the rest of its environment as little as possible. The trouble begins as soon as I try to implement that clause. First, I must define a baseline world, the world as it would have been if the agent had not acted, but every way of drawing that baseline already commits me to a view about which differences count as change. A robot that nudges a cup one centimeter is indistinguishable from baseline under one description, and catastrophic under another, if the baseline has the cup resting on a pressure switch. Second, even with a baseline fixed, I need a measure of distance between worlds, and the units of that measure seem to express what I care about rather than anything measurable in the physics. I know this is somewhere near the frame problem and near ordinary talk of side effects, but those discussions seem mostly to concern how an agent knows what persists after an action, not how to decide which changes are bad. I want to know whether the thing I am describing has an established name of its own, whether it is really one problem or two, and, more importantly, how to carve the boundary between the representational difficulty and the evaluative difficulty. A bare label would not satisfy me.
