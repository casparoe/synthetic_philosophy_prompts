I've got two friends who have argued for years about whether "qualia" names a real property, and they've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration instead of talking past each other. One is a qualia realist: she says there's just a brute fact about what it's like to taste pineapple, and introspection is mostly authoritative about it. The other thinks the posit is incoherent — he always reaches for the Chase and Sanborn case, where two coffee tasters both stop liking the coffee, Chase says the taste itself changed, Sanborn says the taste is identical and only his standards changed, and introspection can't settle who's right. Write the document they should produce. It should have a numbered section of claims both can actually sign, then a disagreements table where each side's view is stated in wording that side would accept rather than a strawman. After that, identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would genuinely move at least one of the two — and for each crux give a concrete thought experiment, case, or argument that both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Flag any part of the dispute you think is merely verbal, and say what would have to change for the substantive parts to converge. Don't just report their slogans; do the drafting work of finding formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted.
