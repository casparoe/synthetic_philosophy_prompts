Consider the classic color phi display. A red dot is flashed briefly at one location, and then, after a short interval, a green dot is flashed at a nearby location. At the right spacing and timing, subjects do not report two separate flashes; they report a single dot moving along the path between the two positions and changing from red to green abruptly in the middle of that path, before the green dot has actually appeared. The puzzle is how the experienced color change can be caused by a stimulus that has not yet occurred.

Two standard accounts have been offered. On the Orwellian account, the subject's experience at the midpoint is initially unrevised: the moving dot looks red, and only after the second dot appears does the brain retroactively alter the subject's memory so that they later recall the dot having turned green midway. On the Stalinesque account, conscious experience is delayed long enough for later information to be folded in before the experience is assembled at all, so the subject never consciously sees the dot as red at the midpoint; the show they become aware of already has the green midpoint built in.

Now vary the setup. Replace the retrospective verbal report with an online task. The subject is trained and paid to press a left button if the dot looks red at the midpoint and a right button if it looks green at the midpoint, pressing at the moment the midpoint color is seen. The order of the two colors is randomized trial by trial, so from the subject's vantage point at the midpoint the eventual second color is unpredictable. Stipulate, as a thought-experimental idealization, that apparent motion persists while the path is made long enough that a practiced button press at the midpoint can be time-stamped before the second dot is flashed.

What does each account predict about which button is pressed, about when the press occurs relative to the second flash, and about the subject's later memory of their own response? Do the two accounts now make genuinely different, time-stamped predictions, or does each have a redescription that brings them back into alignment? And does the standard deflationary diagnosis, associated with Dennett, that the choice between these accounts presupposes a single time and place at which consciousness occurs and therefore has no fact of the matter, survive this variation, or does the variation finally give the distinction a determinate content? Work through the dialectic in detail rather than choosing a side in advance.
