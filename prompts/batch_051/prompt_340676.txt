I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about moral compromise between people who have very different values, so here are my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases.

Two roommates, one vegan and one not. The vegan does all the dishes, including the meaty pans, in exchange for the other never cooking bacon while the vegan is home. Fine.

Two donors with very different priorities—one thinks global health is the best use of a marginal dollar and farm animal welfare is maybe a third as good, the other has the reverse estimate—agree to each give this year's donation to the other's preferred charity. Each thinks the other's cause is worthwhile but worse than their own. Fine, honestly kind of clever.

A colleague will back my proposal on a city policy I think matters if I back hers on a policy I think is pointless but harmless. Fine.

I could vote for a bill that funds something I care about deeply, but the version in front of me also includes a provision I'm confident will wrongfully make a bunch of people worse off, and I calculate the whole package is on balance good. I still wouldn't vote for it. Not fine.

A documentary filmmaker cuts the segment exposing a mining company's abuses because the company will fund the rest of a genuinely valuable film. Not fine, even though she gets substantially more of what she values.

A local donor will fully fund a housing campaign I run, but only if I appear at the launch of the donor's flagship project, which I think is deeply racist. Obviously not fine, even if no one else will match the money and the campaign would help hundreds of families.

The one I keep going back and forth on: my colleague is broadly anti-vax. She'll volunteer for the school science program I run if I let her add a "do your own research" line to the parent handout. I said no, but I can't give a principled reason, because I'd happily trade favors with her on almost anything else and even she admits the line is small. Some days it feels like the harmless city policy and some days like the mining company deal.

Is there one principle that captures these verdicts, or close to it? If some of them have to go, which ones, and what does the repaired principle say about the handout case? Also test whatever you come up with on a fresh case of your own so I can see how it generalizes.
