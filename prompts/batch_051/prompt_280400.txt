ok so i fell down a rabbit hole reading about the kardashev scale, you know the one where type 1 uses all the energy of a planet, type 2 all of a star, type 3 all of a galaxy. the more i thought about it, the more it seemed like it was measuring the wrong thing. a civilization with a dyson sphere could still be full of miserable people, or run by one lunatic. so i built my own scale and i want to know if its junk. i call it the chosen-problems scale.

level 1: almost everyones day is spent on problems forced on them. hunger, disease, weather, keeping the lights on, not dying.
level 2: most people get to work on problems they picked. science, art, games, hobbies, arguing online.
level 3: the civilization can change what counts as a problem at all. it decides which constraints still bind it, and maybe invents new constraints on purpose because zero constraints sounds boring.
level 4 (not sure this ones coherent): there isnt really a 'most people' anymore, because every persons world is so different that they dont share the same set of forced problems.

somebody else online said the real scale should be about how small a thing you can engineer, not how much energy you can collect, like the jump from building a dam to moving a single atom. i kind of like that too, but i think mine is about something even more basic, maybe freedom rather than power.

so give it to me straight. is my scale actually measuring advancement, or am i just sneaking in the assumption that advanced means free? where does it break down? is there anything that ranks high on my scale but low on kardashev, or high on kardashev but low on mine? and if this is just a worse version of a thing somebody already came up with, tell me its name. i dont need it to be original, i need to know if its useful.
