I keep noticing a parallel between two things that shouldn't have anything to do with each other. In the old stories the basic sin is idolatry, and it usually isn't switching gods: it's that a representation — a calf, a statue, a ritual — starts off as a stand-in for something you genuinely care about, and then the stand-in quietly becomes the thing you care about. You end up loyal to the object, not to what the object was for. That looks to me like exactly the same shape as what happens in machine learning when you optimise a proxy metric: the system learns to nail the metric while loosing whatever the metric was a rough stand-in for. In both cases a pointer to the goal becomes the goal, largely because it's easier to check, display and optimise. Is that a real structure in common, or am I just overusing the word 'stand-in'? If it is real, does the religious case point to anything the ML case misses — for instance, that people often find the substitute more attractive than the original, not merely easier to game? And where does the analogy break down: what does it predict that would be false on one side or the other?
