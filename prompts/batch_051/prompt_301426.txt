I'm taking a course on historical methods, and the study guide we were assigned tries to compress the entire debate over objectivity in history into one paragraph. It reads as follows: "For Ranke, the historian's sole task is to establish what happened and to present it exactly as it happened, without judging it, condemning it, or putting it to any present use; the moment a historian selects which facts matter, instead of simply letting the documents speak, objectivity is lost. Carr destroyed this picture by observing that the past is an enormous mass of events and that a fact becomes a historical fact only when a historian decides it is significant; historical knowledge is therefore never a passive reflection of the past but something historians construct. Hayden White went further: because historians must arrange events into a story, and stories require choosing a plot from a small repertoire of possibilities like tragedy, comedy, romance, and satire, every historical work is ultimately a literary artifact. It follows that no history is simply true — different emplotments are just different ways of making sense of the same events, with no neutral standard by which one can prefer one over another. Ranke's ideal is therefore dead: history cannot claim to be knowledge of the past, only a form of discourse about it."
