The vegan argument I've always heard is roughly this: it's wrong to use animal products because buying or eating them pays for animals to be exploited, creates demand for more animals to be bred and kept, and treats a sentient animal as a resource rather than an individual. That logic seems solid to me when it's about supermarket eggs or cheap wool.

But I've started helping at a small sanctuary that takes in ex-battery hens and a couple of sheep that were going to be culled. The hens lay eggs all the time anyway — nobody's making them, the eggs aren't fertilised, nothing would ever hatch. Some of the older hens eat their own eggs to claw back calcium, but there's still a surplus most weeks, and it either gets binned or fed to the pigs. The sheep need shearing or they overheat and get skin problems; the wool gets sold to pay vet bills, or composted when it's too rough to sell. I've since found out some people call using stuff that would otherwise go to waste like this freeganism.

Here's what I keep going back and forth on. Suppose I take some of those leftover eggs home and eat them, or use a bit of the wool that would have been composted. No extra animal gets bred, bought, killed, caged or deprived of anything because of it — the eggs and wool turn up either way. So does the usual vegan argument actually survive that situation, or was it secretly resting on the demand part the whole time? If it still applies, which part of it is doing the work once demand is gone, and can it be restated so it doesn't overreach? If it doesn't survive, then what should we call the unease that's still there — is it a real moral point or just a feeling about symbolic cleanliness?

My friend says it's still wrong because every time you use an egg or some wool you're rehearsing the idea that those bodies make things for you, so you're still treating the animals as little factories in your head, and other people see it and think it's fine. I sort of get it, but by that point the argument seems to have stopped being about the animals and started being about my private attitudes. Is that move actually doing the real work, or is it just patching the original argument after the demand part fell away? Could you steelman both sides honestly, because I keep flip-flopping and I want to know which side is actualy stronger once the demand link is broken.
