I am a novelist at work on a section of a book in which the protagonist, a hospice chaplain who has quietly lost her faith, tries to write down what she means when she says she feels a void. I would like you to write the poem she produces.

Please write a sonnet of fourteen lines, in loose iambic pentameter, with an unmistakable volta. The octave should render the first interpretation of the void: it is a privation, a hollow where belief or a particular person used to be, aching and demanding to be filled, experienced by the character as a defect in herself. The sestet should render the second interpretation with equal seriousness: the same void re-seen as a clearing rather than a wound, an openness or capacity for attention that her former certainties had crowded out, yet not a cheap consolation, because this openness is as vertiginous as it is free. The volta should be the moment of re-seeing, and the closing couplet should leave the two readings side by side rather than settle the matter, since the character herself cannot. The poem should name no doctrine; a reader who knows the via negativa, Buddhist accounts of emptiness, and the existentialist notion of nothingness should recognize their shape in it, while a reader who knows none of them should still find it exact about the experience.

After the poem, add a note of no more than two hundred words saying which of the two readings the poem finally leans on, where it takes liberties with those traditions or risks collapsing them into one another, how confident you are in that assessment, and which point you are least sure about.
