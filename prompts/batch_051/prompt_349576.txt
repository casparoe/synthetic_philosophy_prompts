These two passages keep bugging me. Exodus 20:5 has God saying he's "a jealous God, punishing children for the iniquity of parents to the third and the fourth generation of those who reject me." But Ezekiel 18:20 says "A child shall not suffer for the iniquity of a parent nor a parent suffer for the iniquity of a child; the righteousness of the righteous shall be their own, and the wickedness of the wicked shall be their own." That looks like a flat contradiction about inherited guilt, and both get used as prooftexts in debates over original sin. I dont want a lecture on different authors; I want to know whether one tradition can actually hold both. Lay out the candidate reconciliations — covenant-level versus individual punishment, historical consequences versus final judgment, hyperbole, a later correction — and tell me which ones do genuine work and which are special pleading. Then give me a verdict: can they be reconciled, and what does that do to the idea of inherited guilt?
