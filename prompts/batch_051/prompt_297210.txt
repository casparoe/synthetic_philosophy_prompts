I don't have any formal background in philosophy or physics — I'm a self-taught backend developer, and when I try to think about almost anything abstract I fall back on how I model data: entities, primary and foreign keys, join tables, pointers, references, mutable state, whether something is a real object or just a row that exists while a relation holds. I keep chewing on the old Newton's bucket debate, which I mostly know from pop-science videos. As I understand it, the fight is about whether space is a real thing that exists on its own, or just the set of distances and relations between actual objects and nothing over and above that. I've seen the labels substantivalism and relationism attached to these two sides, but that's about my whole exposure to the literature. The bucket move goes: you hang a bucket of water from a twisted rope and let it spin; while the bucket is moving relative to the water the surface stays flat, but once the water catches up and they're at rest relative to each other, the surface is curved. So the water is doing something that can't be explained by its relation to the bucket, and the claim is that it must be rotating relative to space itself. The other side says motion only makes sense relative to something, so the curve has to be a response to motion relative to the rest of the universe's mass or something like that.

What I'd like is for you to translate this whole debate into the vocabulary I actually think in. Take each side's claims — empty space as a real thing, space as just relations, acceleration being absolute vs. relative, the water 'knowing' it's spinning — and restate them in terms of schemas, entities, foreign keys, whether an entity can exist with no rows pointing to it, where something like 'absolute acceleration' would even live as a property, and so on. For each claim, tell me which ones port over cleanly, which ones have to be bent or lose something in the move, and which ones I just can't express no matter how I set up the schema. Then the part I actually care about: take whatever doesn't survive the translation and tell me wether that failure is because my vocabulary is too thin, or because the physics claim was doing less work than it looked like. If the residue points to something a relational way of thinking simply cannot say, I want to know whether that's a real feature of the world or just a blind spot I should be suspicious of.
