My housemate has a proof that "complicity" is either an empty notion or a universal one, and I can't find the fault. It goes:

(1) You're complicit in a wrong only if your contribution made a difference to whether that wrong occurred — if it would have happened anyway without you, you can't be on the hook for helping it along.

(2) In nearly all of the wrongs we actually care about — the factory using forced labour, the bank laundering money, the industry dumping waste — any single person's contribution is superflous. The line runs without you, the loan gets processed regardless, the waste goes in the river whether or not you sign that form.

(3) So, by (1), no individual in these cases is complicit. The manager, the courier, the shopper: each is off the hook individually.

(4) But that would shrink complicity to the small cases where one person's act is necessary — essentially conspiracy or direct causation. That can't be the concept's point; we reach for "complicit" exactly for the big diffuse cases.

(5) So (1) must go: complicity can't be about making a difference.

(6) But if it isn't about making a difference, it seems it can only be about a non-causal connection — being inside the system, benefiting, knowing, not opting out.

(7) And then everyone inside the system is complicit: the customer, the employee, the pension fund, the taxpayer. The term starts denoting everyone and condemning no one.

(8) So either complicity is nearly empty or nearly universal. Either way it's useless as a tool of serious moral criticism, and we should stop using it.

I want to keep the claim that the shop-floor worker and the ordinary consumer aren't in the same moral category as the owner who sets the policy, but I also don't want to concede that participants in diffuse wrongs get off scot free. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does rejecting it force me to say? Explain why that step looked fine, and price the main alternatives — difference-making purism, shared-intention or participation views, benefit-based views — against each other. Please write in British English.
