I'm in a philosophy of language class right now and we're doing Frege, and I've managed to tie myself in knots over one paragraph of "On Sense and Reference." The passage is:

"The regular connexion between a sign, its sense, and its reference is of such a kind that to the sign there corresponds a definite sense and to that in turn a definite reference, while to a given reference (an object) there does not belong only a single sign."

I tried to turn it into an argument, and I got something that seems to contradict Frege's own morning star/evening star example, but I can't figure out where the mistake is. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) According to the passage, a sign has a definite sense, and a sense in turn has a definite reference.
(2) 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' are both signs.
(3) 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' have the same reference, namely Venus.
(4) If a sense has a definite reference, then two signs with the same reference must have the same sense. Otherwise the same reference would be the "definite reference" of two different senses, which sounds like it breaks the regular connexion Frege is describing.
(5) So 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' have the same sense.
(6) If two signs have the same sense, then 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' and 'Hesperus is Hesperus' should be equally informative.
(7) But they aren't: the first is a genuine astronomical discovery and the second is trivial.
(8) So either Frege's sense/reference distinction can't actually explain why identity statements are informative, or I've messed up somewhere.

I keep thinking step (4) is doing the sneaky work, but when I try to deny it I feel like I'm just ignoring what "definite reference" means. The thing is, Frege says earlier in the same paper that the reference of 'evening star' would be the same as that of 'morning star', but not the sense. So he obviously thinks one reference can go with two different senses. Is my step (4) a misreading of "definite," or is there a real tension here? Tell me which step fails on the most charitable reading, why exactly it fails, and whether the argument can be repaired.
