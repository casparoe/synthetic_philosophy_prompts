Take the discovery of Neptune in 1846 as a test case for the Humean vs. anti-Humean debate about laws of nature. Quick recap in case you need it: Uranus kept drifting a bit off where Newtonian mechanics said it should be; Le Verrier (and Adams, roughly independantly) worked backward from the deviations and predicted an unseen planet, and Galle found the thing almost exactly where the math pointed. I don't need the history repeated, I want the philosophy. The Humean says a law is just a compact summary of the regularities in the total mosaic of local facts; the anti-Humean says laws are something over and above the facts, with real modal force, which is why the calculation could say there must be a planet out there rather than just that the data would look neater if there were. Map this episode carefully: what does each side say was actually going on when Le Verrier made the prediction, does a successful prediction to an unobserved object count as evidence for anti-Humeanism (or can a best-system Humean story absorb it equally well), and are there places where the old debate is just silent about what actually mattered in the episode.
