Take the old evidence problem but vary one parameter. The stock case is Mercury's perihelion: the anomaly was already known (P(E)=1) before general relativity, so on the strict Bayesian account P(H|E)=P(H) and the old evidence can't confirm the theory. Now suppose the historical record is just a little shaky, so an agent is only 0.95 sure of the anomaly, not certain; assume the theory H entails E, so P(E|H)=1. Run the math: what is P(H|E) as a multiple of P(H)? It comes out positive but tiny, which doesn't match the intuition that Mercury gave GR massive support. So is the old evidence problem just a knife-edge artifact of setting P(E) exactly 1, or does the 0.95 case show the weirdness survives the idealization? And if it survives, does this variation push you toward a fix where the confirmatory work is done by learning the logical relation (H entails E) rather than E itself, or toward a counterfactual probability of E with your knowledge of E deleted, or does it push against both?
