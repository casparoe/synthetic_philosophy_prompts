Just had a long argument with a friend about Germany shutting its last nuclear plants while France gets most of its electricity from them and is planning new ones. He kept saying the German exit is simply irrational—especially since Germany still burns coal and imports some power from France—but it strikes me the two countries are makeing different bets about uncertainty: Germany treats a rare but catastrophic accident as something you just cannot accept, while France treats it as a risk to price and manage. What does each policy actually presuppose about how to handle low-probability, high-consequence risks? Reconstruct the implicit theory of risk or precaution behind each side, say whether the policies strictly need those theories or are compatible with a plain cost–benefit view, and tell me what would have to change in each country if it adopted the other's underlying attitude toward risk.
