I'm retired now—spent most of my career in zoology, the sort of life-history evolution work where you keep little model organisms in the basement—and I've lately been reading Popper straight through instead of getting him second hand. His Darwinism material has unsettled me more than I expected. Early on he seems to have argued that natural selection is not a testable scientific theory but something closer to a metaphysical research programme: the slogan about the fittest surviving is worryingly close to a tautology if fitness just mean who actually left the most descendants, and evolutionary history is a one-off process you cannot rerun to falsify the general claim. Later, I believe in the late 1970s, he softened or recanted and said natural selection is testable after all. My old colleague—a geneticist—keeps emailing me that Popper's early view was obviously right: real biologists test little local models (peppered moths, beak sizes, pesticide resistance, bacteria evolving in a flask), while the grand claim that adaptation is explained by differential survival and reproduction of heritable variants never itself gets tested; it just sits there as a framework. I am less sure.

Decompose the number into whatever terms you think belong, but at least address these: (1) whether fitness can be characterized independently of actual survival—say, as expected reproductive success for a specified phenotype in a specified environment—well enough to break the tautology; (2) whether a claim that mostly unifies and retrodicts historical cases rather than issuing novel predictions still counts as testable in the right way; (3) whether an unfalsifiable core that generates lots of falsifiable submodels is a defect at all, rather than just how research programmes work; (4) how much weight to put on Popper's recantation itself, given that he may have been being sociable rather than changing his mind for philosophical reasons; and (5) the flexibility worry that any trait can be given some adaptive story, so failures of specific models never get to count against the general principle.
