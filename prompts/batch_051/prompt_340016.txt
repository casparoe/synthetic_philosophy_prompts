I'm a software engineer, not a philosophy person, so I'm probably mangling this, but I was reading through an old Buddhist text online — the Milindapañha, or The Questions of King Milinda — and I got stuck on the whole no-self, rebirth, what-keeps-going question. The monk in it basically tries to show the king there's no abiding self. First he does the chariot thing: if you take it apart, you won't find a chariot in the pieces; chariot is just a name for parts arranged in a certain way. Then he does a flame thing: a flame lights a second lamp, and the second flame is not the same flame but not totally other either. I can see that as a description of causal continuity, but I can't get it to line up with my gut reactions to a bunch of cases.

Here are my gut calls. (a) A Star Trek teleporter destroys my body and rebuilds a perfect copy of me on Mars: I'd step in, and the person who wakes up there is me. (b) Same machine, but it builds the copy without destroying the original: the copy is not me; it's a new person with my memories. I realize a and b are in tension, since the only added thing in b is that the original survives, and I can't say why destruction should matter. (c) My neurons get replaced over several years, one at a time, by chips that perform the same function while my memory and behavior stay continuous: still me throughout. (d) After that replacement, someone uses saved scans of my original biological brain to build a second silicon brain: the second one is not me, but I don't know why, because it has the same continuity of causal history as the first, just with a later off-ramp. (e) A person suffers total amnesia, same body, no memory of his old life: I'd say same person, different personality. (f) The rebirth case from the text: a new baby, no memories, no same body, no same brain, only a karmic causal chain: I don't think the baby is the same person as the dead one, even though I can grant it's part of the same process. Is there a principle that gets most of these, or close? If not, which verdict should I drop, and what would the resulting principle say about a case I haven't thought of? Please answer in American English.
