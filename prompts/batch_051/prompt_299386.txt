In the standard trolley case the lever trades five humans for one. Now swap the side-track person for a stretch of marsh: pulling the lever sends the trolley through a dense vole colony, crushing some on contact and wrecking enough burrows and vegetation that most of the rest die in the following weeks. Assume you're a consequentialist who counts nonhuman suffering, and assume the wild-animal-welfare claim that an average wild animal's remaining life is net-negative, so that preventing those lives counts as a gain and not a loss. Does that flip your decision about pulling the lever? Walk through what the analysis should do with the pain of the crush itself, the prevented future births, the predators who just lost their food supply, and whether it even makes sense to assign a single welfare score to the marsh. Where exactly does the usual five-vs-one arithmetic stop being able to answer the question, and what one extra fact about the marsh would you most want before deciding?
