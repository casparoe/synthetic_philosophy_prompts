I keep seeing pieces about wild animal suffering that jump straight to a verdict — either "nature is fine, hands off" or "we should be out there rewilding and predator-eliminating everything" — and each side just picks the one case that makes the other look daft. Before I read another one, I want the actual spec. Give me the requirements list for a theory of when we may or must intervene in the lives of wild animals to reduce their suffering. For each requirement give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it's non-negotiable or just a camp preference.

Specifically, I want the list to handle at least these:

- the difference between one-off rescue (a bird with a broken wing in my yard) and systematic intervention (suppressing predation, contraception programs, parasite control), and how the two are related, without collapsing them into each other or treating them as totaly unrelated;
- the distinction between suffering and death — a quick death in a wolf's jaws vs. months of disease — and whether an animal can be harmed by being deprived of a life it would have enjoyed, because that determines whether "less predation" automatically means "better";
- the baseline problem: there is no untouched baseline anymore, since humans have already reshaped most habitats, so is "not intervening" a real option or just a choice to keep the current distribution of suffering, and does the wild/domestic line even do moral work once we're already feeding, vaccinating, and culling the animals around us;
- whether the theory has to stay answerable to population dynamics, since a less lethal habitat often just means more animals starving or dying of disease instead;
- whether it respects the difference between causing a harm and failing to prevent one, i.e. does it say anything about whether we have positive duties here or only negative ones;
- whether respecting something like the sovereignty of ecosystems or naturalness is a genuine requirement, or just a prejudice I can drop.

Then tell me two things: are there any two requirements on this list that plausibly can't be satisfied together? And which single requirement does the most work in killing off the standard positions, both the "let nature be" view and the "we should engineer ecosystems to minimize suffering" view? Don't score all the main views in detail — I care about the criteria themselves, not the rankings.
