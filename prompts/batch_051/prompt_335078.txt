Heres something that's been bugging me. The government can force you to sell your house to make way for a highway, as long as it pay's you fair market value, and we mostly accept this. But nobody thinks it can force you to donate a kidney to save someone who will otherwise die, even if it offers to pay you well. Both are cases of the state taking something from a person for a public benefit, with compensation on the table. What theory of rights does this pair of practices presuppose? Does it commit us to saying property rights are weaker than bodily rights, or that rights are just interests that can be bought out at a price (but then why not organs), or that the word "rights" isn't really doing any unified work at all? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the practices strictly presuppose it or merely fit it, and tell me what would have to change if we took rights to be absolute trumps, or instead pure instruments to be traded off whenever total welfare wins.
