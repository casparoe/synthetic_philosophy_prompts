I keep going back and forth on a view about moral status that, on paper, looks undeniable to me, and that I also can’t accept in its full form. The view is that moral status is a matter of degree all the way down. Whatever it is that makes a creature matter morally—the capacity to suffer, or to have any kind of welfare, or to integrate information into a point of view—comes in gradations, so moral status should track those gradations continuously. A dog matters more than a mouse, a mouse more than a bee, a bee more than a roundworm, and so on. What I like about this is that any hard cutoff looks arbitrary: wherever you draw the line between “has moral status” and “doesn’t,” there are beings just on either side that don’t differ enough to justify the split. So the tidy view is that there is no cuttoff at all; the gradient just keeps fading down through insects and on into extremely simple systems.

At the same time, I can’t shake the intuition that the gradient does end somewhere. It seems plainly true to me that a thermostat has no moral status—not a tiny amount. I don’t mean we should treat objects carelessly or be cruel to them; I mean that saying “a thermostat has 0.001 units of moral considerability” sounds like a category mistake, not a false but respectable claim. And if thermostats have zero, then somewhere between a bee and a thermostat the “more or less” story has to turn into a “something or nothing” story, and we’re back to needing a line after all.

Please don’t just tell me which side wins. I want to know which thing should give, and what each concession would actually cost. Is the intuition just me failing to imagine very small amounts of the relevant property—a scope-sensitivity artifact, or something like that? Is the theory smuggling in a threshold by calling everything “some degree” of sentience when at the bottom it isn’t sentience in the same sense anymore? Or is the right move a two-level view: gradations above some minimal threshold, a qualitative difference below it, plus an account of what makes that threshold non-arbitrary? Give me the strongest version of each side, then tell me which concession is less costly and why. And please answer in American English.
