so here's where i've landed: du bois was basically right that all art is propaganda — i'm paraphrasing, since i can't quote it exactly — that every artwork takes a political stance whether the artist admits it or not, and that black artists in particular should judge their work by how well it serves racial uplift and the fight against white supremacy. i think i buy both parts: the descriptive claim that even art trying to be apolitical is itself making a political move, and the normative claim that for black art, usefullness to the struggle isn't a distortion of aesthetic value but a legitimate and even central criterion. what i want is a map of what would count against this. not the lazy art-for-art's-sake line, but the strongest version of the case that art has aims other than political ones, and that grading art by its usefulness to a cause collapses something important. which observations, cases, or arguments should actually move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? which ones would a committed du boisian themselves concede count against the view, versus ones they'd dismiss as refusing to see that trying to be neutral is itself a political stance? and be honest about whether the descriptive claim has been insulated into unfalsifiability — if every artwork, including the apolitical ones, gets read as propaganda for something, does anything really count against it anymore? and if it has been insulated, is that a fatal problem for a view like this or just what a view about the politics of culture looks like?
