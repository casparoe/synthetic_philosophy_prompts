I am putting together a proposal for a one-year postdoctoral project and would like you to turn a half-formed thought into a sharply framed proposal of about 1,200 words. The half-formed idea is as follows. The debate over whether religious experience can serve as evidence for God's existence is almost always conducted at a single level of generality, as though religious experience were one kind of mental state. I suspect that is a mistake. We should at least distinguish an experience as of a specific, personal presence that seems to address or encounter the subject from a diffuse sense of sacredness, gratitude, or the sheer fittingness of things, which has no definite object. My suspicion is that the familiar objections to religious experience as evidence, especially the problem of diversity and the charge that such experiences are entirely the product of prior interpretation, tell rather strongly against the diffuse kind while leaving the specific-presence kind largely untouched. I would like the proposal to ask whether this distinction allows one to defend a modest evidential role for specific-presence experiences even while conceding that the diffuse kind is evidentially inert.

To keep the project tractable, please define evidential role in terms of an experience's capacity to confer prima facie justification on a belief in its apparent object in the absence of a specific defeater, in roughly the way perceptual experience is standardly taken to do. The proposal should state the central question precisely; explain why the existing global framing leaves that question open rather than settled; identify the distinctions and cases that would need to be drawn; justify why proceeding by separating these two kinds of experience is a better strategy than the obvious alternatives, such as assessing the argument from religious experience wholesale or simply assimilating religious experience to ordinary perception; and say what a successful outcome would look like, what would count as the project's failing, and what can realistically be accomplished in one year. Please write the proposal in British English throughout, and do not include citations or a reading list, as I will add the scholarly apparatus myself.
