So in a Discord server I'm in there's an argument that's been going in circles for about a year, and it never moves. A podcaster we all used to listen to got dropped by her network after someone dug up screenshots of jokes she made in a private group chat like eight years ago. Everyone agrees on the facts: the jokes were mean and bigoted, she was in her early twenties, she's apologized a bunch since and pretty clearly isn't that person anymore. Everyone also agrees that the people hurt by those jokes don't owe her forgiveness. And still half the server thinks the network did the right thing, and the other half thinks it was a cowardly overreaction, and nobody has changed position the whole time.

The weird part is when we try to state the principle, both sides end up saying almost the same thing: people should face consequences, but consequences should be proportional, but some harms can't be measured. So I don't think it's a hidden factual disagreement, and I don't think either side is secretly fine with the jokes. Part of the fight is even about whether the network "punished" her at all, since its just a private company choosing not to work with someone. My working theory is that the two sides are really answering different questions under the same words: one side is asking what the network owed its listeners and the people the jokes targeted, the other side is asking what one person deserves for things she did years ago and already changed from. Maybe the real disagreement is about what a social sanction is even for: retribution, deterrence, protection, sending a message. But I not sure.

What kind of disagreement is this actually? Is there a genuine substantive dispute under the surface, or is it mostly a verbal and definitional one about words like accountability, consequences, and cancel culture? And if it is substantive, is there anything that would actually move the two sides, or is this the kind of thing where no new fact or distinction would change anyone's mind? I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict on whether the network made the right call.
