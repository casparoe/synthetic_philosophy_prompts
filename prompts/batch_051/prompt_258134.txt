I remeber reading about a study once where they took a story about a CEO who starts a new program just to make money. The program also has a side effect on the environment. In one version the side effect was good, in the other it was bad, but in both versions the CEO was told about it beforehand and said he didn't care, he just wanted the money. People mostly said he intentionally harmed the environment in the bad version, and mostly said he did not intentionally help it in the good version. Same state of mind, same kind of side effect, only the good/bad flipped. I've been turning that over ever since.

I want a working definition of "intentionally" (or "doing something on purpose") that gets the following cases right. I'm not a philosopher, so give me something I can actually apply without a pile of footnotes. Here are my cases, and I'll be upfront about where I stand:

1. A man stabs someone in the chest during a fight, trying to kill him. I'm sure this is intentional.
2. A man sneezes while carrying a tray of drinks and drops all of them. I'm sure this is not intentional.
3. A chef makes a dish with peanuts because it tastes better. The host already told him one guest is allergic, and the chef knows the guest could wind up in the hospital, but he doesn't care. The guest eats it and gets sick. Did the chef intentionally make the guest sick? My gut says yes.
4. Same chef, same dish, same reason. This time the host told him peanuts are this guest's favorite food, and the chef knows the guest will be delighted, but he doesn't care either way. The guest is delighted. Did the chef intentionally delight the guest? My gut says no, and I can't explain why 3 and 4 come apart.
5. A burglar breaks a window to get into a house. He doesn't care about the window at all, he just wants to get inside. Did he intentionally break the window? I keep saying yes, but then I can't figure out what that yes has that the yes in 3 doesn't.
6. A doctor gives a dying patient a large dose of morphine only to relieve pain, knowing it may also make the patient die a little sooner. The patient does die sooner. Did the doctor intentionally kill the patient? I lean no, but again can't say why this feels different from 3.

Propose a definition, run it through all six, and say where it gives an answer that surprises you or where you'd need to revise it. If it can't capture the difference between 3 and 4 without just stipulating that bad side effects count and good ones don't, say so and tell me what that says about the word.

Whatever you end with, make the last line a one-sentence summary of the definition you settle on, and tell me wether that summary is genuinely the definition or just the best you could do.
