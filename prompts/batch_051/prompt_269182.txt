ok so I've been trying to pin down my views on teleology and Aristotle's four causes for a term paper, and I want a consistency check before I write it up. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Aristotle's four causes aren't four separate things that can each be 'the cause' of an outcome in the modern sense; they're four kinds of answer to the question 'why?' — matter answers what it's made of, form answers what it is, the efficient cause answers what brought it about, and the final cause answers what it's for.

(2) Modern science hasn't actually disproven final causality; it just quietly demoted it, treating 'what is it for?' as a legitimate question only about artifacts and intentional actions, never about natural substances.

(3) Biological function talk — 'the heart is for pumping blood' — is fine and irreducible to mere physical description, but it's really shorthand for a selection history: the heart was selected because pumping blood helped ancestors reproduce.

(4) If we discovered a species of organism that had no evolutionary history at all, say it spontaneously arose in a one-off event, it would still be wrong to say its heart-like part is 'for' pumping, even if the part pumps blood and the organism depends on it.

(5) Artifacts have final causes in a straightforward way: a chair is for sitting because someone designed it to be sat on.

(6) The reason natural teleology made sense for Aristotle but not for us is that he thought nature itself has a kind of internal order aimed at an end, and we don't need that anymore.

(7) That said, I also want to hold that when a living thing heals a wound or maintains its temperature, there's a genuine sense in which it's 'directed at' its own continued existence, and that isn't just a projection of our own purposes.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? I can't tell whether I'm smuggling in an Aristotelian nature while pretending it's gone, or whether 3 through 5 together make 7 impossible. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
