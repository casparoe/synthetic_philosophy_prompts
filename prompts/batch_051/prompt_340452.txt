Design a single, concrete scenario — a future discovery that physics or neuroscience could in principle announce — that forces Hempel's dilemma about the meaning of "the physical" into the open. The dilemma is this. Ask a physicalist what "the physical" means. If she says it means whatever current physics describes, or can in principle be derived from current physics, then physicalism has real content but looks almost certainly false, because the history of physics repeatedly introduced entities that earlier physics could not countenance. If instead she says it means whatever an ideal, completed future physics would describe, then physicalism becomes nearly impossible to falsify: any new law-governed phenomenon we succeed in studying can be welcomed as physical by definition, and the thesis threatens to collapse into a tautology.

Constraints on the scenario: no miracles, no supernatural or stipulated non-physical substance, and no exotic physics in the science-fiction sense; the discovery should be something a sober scientist could imagine announcing at a press conference. The scenario must be specific enough that you can state (i) exactly what is discovered, (ii) the experimental signature or observation that forces the discovery, and (iii) why that thing is not already part of current physics.

After presenting the scenario, run the checks. First, say what the physicalist who defines the physical by current physics would conclude about the discovery, and identify the precise feature of the scenario that forces that conclusion. Second, say what the physicalist who defines the physical by ideal future physics would conclude, and explain why her opposite conclusion nevertheless leaves the doctrine looking empty or unfalsifiable. Third, isolate the single feature of the scenario that is doing the real work, and explain what happens to the dilemma if you remove or replace only that feature. Then give the best defensive move available to each physicalist: what the current-physics physicalist should say so that her view is not hostage to future surprises, and what the ideal-physics physicalist should say to show that her definition is not merely verbal. Close by stating which horn of Hempel's dilemma your scenario sharpens, and why it sharpens that horn rather than merely illustrating it.
