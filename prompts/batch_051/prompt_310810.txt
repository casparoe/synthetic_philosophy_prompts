In a logic course my professor summed up Lewis Carroll's tortoise exchange like this: 'Carroll shows you can't compel someone to accept a conclusion by giving them premises they accept, because applying modus ponens would require the further premise that if the premises are true, the conclusion follows—and the tortoise can just refuse to accept that premise too. So deduction has no non-circular justification, and accepting it is ultimately a matter of faith.' This sounds mostly right to me, but the jump to 'faith' feels like it overreads, and I'm not sure whether a rule of inference really should be counted as an extra premise. Which parts of his summary are fair to what Carroll actually shows, which parts are subtle misreadings, and does the skeptical conclusion follow from the accurate parts?
