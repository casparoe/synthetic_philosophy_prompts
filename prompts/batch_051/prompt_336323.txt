I've been reading about alignment proposals built on debate, in which two AI systems argue opposite sides of a question before a human judge who is too limited to evaluate the question directly, and about the obfuscated-argument worry that a dishonest debater can repackage a flawed argument so that a bounded judge cannot find the flaw. It struck me that this is structurally very close to the theory of the common-law adversary system: two partisan advocates argue before an impartial but bounded judge or jury, each may cross-examine the other, and the system assumes that partisan advocacy before an impartial decider is the most reliable route to the truth. Both settings depend on an asymmetry between defending the truth and defending an error, and both can fail when one side produces superficially persuasive arguments that hide their flaws from a limited decision-maker.
