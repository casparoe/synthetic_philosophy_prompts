I've somehow found myself on the selection committee for a small community arts fund, and it's made me weirdly anxious about how we're choosing. We get around a dozen proposals each round, and we've been comparing them two at a time because a scored rubric felt to mechanical. The problem is our 'better than' judgments keep going in circles. A looks clearly stronger than B — more local partnerships, a more realistic budget — and B looks clearly stronger than C — cheaper to run, much easier to deliver. But then when C sits next to A, C feels bolder and A just feels safe, so C wins that comparison. At first I assumed people were getting tired or sloppy, so I made everyone write out their criteria in advance and score each criterion 1-5. That didn't fix it; the circle just moved somewhere else. One member now says 'better project' isn't a real ranking at all and we should just screen out the weak ones and then draw lots. Another says there's one correct ranking and we're simply not deliberating hard enough until the cycle disappears.

I'm the person who has to present our method at the next meeting, and I honestly don't know wether to defend pairwise comparison, propose we switch to something else (ranked ballot? total points?), or stand up and say the problem isn't the procedure — it's that 'better than' doesn't have to behave like we assumed. Which is it? What's actually happening when a group's pairwise comparisons cycle like this, and what should a small committee do about it in practice, given that we can't deliberate forever?

Also, I want to know how much to lean on whichever story you tell: please say what would change your mind about it — which argument or consideration would push you to a different verdict — and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
