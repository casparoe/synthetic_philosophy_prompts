The just-so story criticism, which is associated mainly with Gould and Lewontin, says that evolutionary explanations of behavior are unfalsifiable narratives that can always be adjusted to fit whatever is observed. Take the hypothesis that men prefer younger romantic partners because youth is a cue of female fertility. Evidence for this hypothesis is that the preference appears across many cultures and is stronger in men than in women. Evidence against it would be if mens preferences tracked their own age or social status instead of the partners age. The criticism is not decisive, because the hypothesis makes falsifiable predictions. For instance, the preference should show up in cultures without mass media, and it should be stronger in men than in women, and both predictions have been supported in studies. A lot of the resistance to this kind of work seems to be about not liking the implications rather than a genuine methodological problem.
