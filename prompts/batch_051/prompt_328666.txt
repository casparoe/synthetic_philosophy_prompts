Construct a thought experiment in which a luck-egalitarian principle of equality of opportunity mandates a 100% inheritance tax, while the difference principle forbids that same tax. The purpose of the case is to isolate the point at which these two principles diverge: a transfer that destroys value rather than merely moving it. It would therefore be cheating to get the divergence by making the government corrupt, wasteful, or unable to enforce its laws.

Constraints. Use one realistic small-town family-owned firm—not an aristocracy, a multinational, or an economy-wide model. The state is competent and enforcement is perfect: no offshore trusts, no gifts made in anticipation of death, no evasion, no capital flight. People's preferences, talents, and effort are held fixed. Give the difference principle full fiscal flexibility: you may assume the government spends whatever revenue the tax raises in the way most beneficial to the least advantaged, and the least advantaged must still end up worse off than under a lower or zero tax. The harm must arise from the character of the inherited asset itself, not from administrative failure or from anyone's deliberate response to the tax.

Take these as the relevant principles, and make them precise as you apply them. The luck-egalitarian principle of equal opportunity holds that an heir's starting position should reflect only what she has chosen or earned; inherited wealth is a paradigm unchosen advantage, so inequalities of inheritance should be taxed away to equalize starting places. The difference principle holds that departures from equality are justified only if they are part of the arrangement that leaves the least advantaged as well off as possible; a tax is therefore condemned when it worsens the least advantaged's absolute position relative to a feasible alternative.

After setting out the scenario in one paragraph, do the following. (1) State the luck-egalitarian principle precisely and show why it requires the 100% tax here. (2) State the difference principle and trace the causal chain by which the tax leaves the least advantaged worse off. (3) Identify the single feature of the case that does the work. (4) Say whether the divergence collapses if the inherited asset is replaced with a diversified portfolio of marketable securities, and why. (5) Give the strongest attack a luck egalitarian could mount against the case, and note what could be added to the scenario to block that reply.
