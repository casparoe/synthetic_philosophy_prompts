In a reading group I attend, the subjectivists about probability have a stock analogy, adapted from the early betting tradition associated with Ramsey and de Finetti but now used as a self-contained argument. A degree of belief, they say, is like a price. A price is not something an object has in itself; it is a rate at which someone is willing to trade, made visible only in a transaction. Likewise, a probability is not something a hypothesis or an event has in the world; it is a rate at which a thinker would buy or sell bets on that hypothesis or event. And the rational constraints are meant to match: just as coherent price lists are policed by arbitrageurs, coherent credence assignments are policed by Dutch books. From this they conclude that asking for the objective probability of a one-off event is as confused as asking what a sandwich truly costs independently of any buyer, and that disagreements about probability are ultimately disagreements in what one is willing to stake.

I want to know how much of this conclusion the analogy can actually carry. Audit it thoroughly. First, give the explicit correspondence table: what, on the credence side, plays the role of the commodity, the buyer and seller, the offer, the transaction, the arbitrageur, and the market? Second, identify the places where the mapping strains or breaks — where a credence is not like a price, or a believer is not like a trader or a market. Third, sort those disanalogies into the ones that matter for the subjectivist conclusion and the ones that are merely cosmetic. Fourth, say what survives once the serious disanalogies are on the table: does the analogy establish that objective, single-case probability is a confusion, or does it mostly establish coherence constraints while staying silent on calibration to the world? In particular, say where this leaves frequentist and propensity interpretations — does the analogy refute them, or do they expose a load-bearing disanalogy? If a better analogy would do the subjectivists' work more honestly, provide it and say what it adds.
