I'm writing an essay on welfare conditionality and a case keeps tripping me up. My lecturer gave us five criteria that people apparently use when deciding whether a claimaint is deserving of support, and I'll paraphrase them here because I want to apply them to the case.

1. Control: did the person bring the need on themselves through their own choices?
2. Need: how bad is their situation, taking the full picture into account?
3. Reciprocity: have they contributed in the past, or are they at least willing to contribute now in some realistic way?
4. Attitude: are they compliant and grateful, or do they act like the system owes them?
5. Identity: are they "one of us" — and the lecturer admitted this one does a lot of quiet work even when people say it shouldn't.

The case is R. R is 41 and worked full-time in a warehouse for fourteen years, paying tax throughout. Then a back injury stopped him doing the heavy lifting; his employer moved him into a stock-control desk job, and then the company folded eighteen months later. He's been on unemployment benifit since. About a year ago he started helping at his brother's takeaway three evenings a week, mostly taking phone orders and cashing up the till. He says it wasn't a job and he never got a wage: he got a free meal on shift, and his brother would slip him twenty pounds for petrol now and then. A compliance officer visited after a tip-off, saw him behind the counter, and now he's been hit with a fraud penalty. His benefit was stopped, he's liable to repay, and the fraud marker makes employers reject him at the background-check stage. He can physically manage a part-time desk job, but nobody will take him on. He's now applying for a hardship payment while he appeals. No savings, no dependants. His line is that he made a mistake about the rules rather than trying to game the system, and he's pretty bitter about being treated like a scammer after fourteen years of paying in.

So my question is: by those five criteria, would you say R is deserving of the hardship payment? Go through them one by one and say which way each cuts, because I find they don't all point the same way. Then tell me whether this is a genuinely borderline case or whether the criteria themselves are doing the work badly — do attitude and identity deserve to be doing any work in a benefits system at all? And when control, need and reciprocity conflict, what tie-breaker would you use and what does your choice reveal?
