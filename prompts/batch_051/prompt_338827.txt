Several communities that run workshops on rationality have for years described their project in martial-arts terms: the workshop is a dojo, the skill-building exercises are katas, adversarial practice is sparring, and the promised payoff is that a practitioner will act well in the cognitive equivalent of a street fight. The analogy is not decorative; it is being used to license a particular conclusion: that there exists a domain-general skill of good reasoning which can be deliberately trained in contrived, low-stakes exercises and will then transfer to the messy, high-stakes settings of real life, much as a dojo-trained fighter can act under the stress of a street assault.

I want an audit of this analogy, not a promotional restatement of it and not a dismissal of it as mere branding. Begin by laying out the correspondence table explicitly: what maps to the novice, the instructor, the kata, the sparring partner, the tournament or belt test, the dojo, and the street fight? Be concrete about what the rationality-training versions of these things are (for example, calibration drills, Fermi estimates, and pre-mortems as katas; red-teaming or adversarial collaboration as sparring; a genuine high-stakes decision under time pressure and motivated reasoning as the street fight).

Then identify where the mapping strains or breaks, and be systematic about it. Distinguish the features of martial arts that get quietly imported into the rationality version even though they may not hold: a repertoire of techniques refined over generations by cumulative competitive feedback; an opponent who cooperates within agreed rules; injuries and progress that are observable on short timescales; the fact that a practiced kata resembles the live fight more closely than abstract drills resemble real decisions. State which of these disanalogies matter for the intended transfer claim and which are harmless.

Finally, adjudicate the conclusion the analogy was recruited to support. Does the workshop-to-real-life transfer claim survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or has the analogy been carrying a burden that the evidence would not carry on its own? If the martial-arts analogy is doing illicit work, propose a better analogy for what rationality training can realistically accomplish and state what that replacement analogy would license instead.
