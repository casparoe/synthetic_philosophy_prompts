Kant's essay on the supposed right to lie (the short one where he responds to Benjamin Constant) has this weird bit after the murderer-at-the-door case that i keep chewing on. He argues, roughly, that if you lie to the murderer and your friend happens to have slipped out anyway, so the murderer finds him and kills him, you can be held legally accountable for the death; but if you answer truthfully, whatever follows is not your responsibility. The idea seems to be that a lie, unlike a true statement, makes you responsible for all the downstream consequences even when the causal chain runs through the murderer's own choice. Write an essay on whether that specific argument can be made coherent.
