I coach a high school debate team and after a rough tournament I've started thinking rationality is less like a body of knowledge and more like a martial art: you can lecture kids on fallacies and base rates forever and it doesnt change how they argue when theyre down two points in a round. I want a training program I can actually run with them, not a reading list.

Design me a weekly 20-minute practice routine for improving real-time epistemic habits, using the martial-arts frame of drills, forms/katas, controlled sparring, pressure testing, and transfer. It should be grounded in what's actually known about how skills transfer (or fail to transfer)—near and far transfer, automaticity, retrieval practice, interference—but translated into concrete exercises a debate coach can run without any background in the theory.

The routine has to be something I can implement on a Tuesday with fifteen teenagers and a whiteboard. List the exercises in order, say how long each takes, what good form looks like, when to make it harder, and what observable behavior tells me it's working instead of just looking good in drills. Pay special attention to the classic problem where the same kid who spots a biased sample in a drill falls for the first vivid anecdote in a real round. Flag the points where I as the coach have to make a judgment call rather than just follow steps.

Then apply the program to three students:
1. Maria can recite every fallacy but in rounds she just accumulates evidence for her side and never asks what would change her mind.
2. Jay over-updates dramatically from one vivid example and won't let base rates count unless I force him.
3. Lena is great at spotting other people's motivated reasoning but noticably worse when she's losing.

For at least one of these three, the program should probably recommend something I'm not going to like as a coach (for example pulling a kid from competition practice for a month, or watching them lose winnable rounds while they do boring drills). Tell me straight. And end by telling me what the routine cannot fix.
