Robin Hanson's core argument for futarchy is that prediction markets aggregate dispersed private information into a price, and that this lets a polity select policies: the electorate fixes a welfare metric, markets trade assets whose payoffs depend on that metric conditional on each candidate policy's being enacted, and the policy with the highest conditional market estimate is adopted. The argument's force comes from a specific premise: traders hold different, partly independent information, and betting corrects individual error into collective accuracy. Now suppose the marginal traders in these markets are LLM-based forecasters trained on heavily overlapping text, sharing similar priors, similar blind spots, and strongly correlated errors, and that these traders dominate trading volume. Does the core argument for futarchy survive this change? If not, isolate the exact premise under pressure and state the strongest repaired version of the argument, saying what it would have to assume in order for correlated algorithmic traders not to undermine it.
