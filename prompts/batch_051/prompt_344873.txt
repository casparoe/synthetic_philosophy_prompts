I keep going back and forth on whether elegance in math or physics is actual evidence that a theory is on the right track, or just a vibe we project onto whatever survives. Two stories I keep hearing:

(a) The aesthetic-induction story. Historically, theories that struck people as elegant or simple—say Dirac's equation or special relativity—ended up doing much better empirically that a naive guess would predict. So elegance works as a weak, defeasible kind of evidence: not a guarantee, but a decent prior.

(b) The filter story. We only see the survivors. For every clean elegant theory there are a bunch of equally elegant ones that died quietly, and plenty of ugly, patched-up theories stuck around because they fit the data. What we call "beautiful" is mostly just what got cleaned up after surviving contact with experiment. On this view beauty is mostly a side effect of selection, not an independent guide.

Compare these two. Are they genuinely rivals, or can both be true without contradiction? Which do you think better explains why elegance feels informative even when it might not be? And if someone wanted to actually test which story is closer to the truth, what kind of historical or present-day evidence would you tell them to look for?
