I've been arguing with a friend about Machiavelli. Her line is that when a leader comes through a crisis well you can tell, at least in principle, whether it was virtù and not just fortuna, because virtù has a signature: it flexes when the situation changes, it doesn't mistake early luck for a plan, it builds the embankments before the river floods. I think that's just post-hoc storytelling. So how would you actually test the claim in one real case — say, a prime minister who barely survived a confidence vote and then won a war, or pick your own? Design the best test you can: what data or comparison would you collect, what result would push you toward the virtù reading, what result toward the fortuna reading, and what even the cleanest result leaves open. I especially want to know whether your actually measuring virtù itself or just redefining it as whatever the winners turn out to have had. And does Machiavelli's own idea that virtù is only visible when fortune gives it an occasion mean the two can't be cleanly separated at all?
