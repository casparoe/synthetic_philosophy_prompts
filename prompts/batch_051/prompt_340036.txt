I'm preparing a philosophy-of-mind unit on collective minds and want to use the Borg from Star Trek as a case study. Read the Borg through Hofstadter's ant-colony thought experiment in Gödel, Escher, Bach, where Aunt Hillary argues that a mind can belong to a whole colony even though no individual ant has one: the self is emergent, not located in any component. The Borg look like a natural dramatization — many bodies, a single collective voice, assimilation that dissolves individual identity — but two details pull the other way: the Borg Queen suggests a top-down controller rather than an emergent whole, and Picard or Seven of Nine recover a selfhood that reads as suppressed rather than destroyed. Work through whether the Borg would have to be a mind at the level of the whole collective for the analogy to hold, where the show actually dramatizes that versus quietly undercutting it, and which scenes an honest critic would cite on both sides. End with what the Borg, taken at its strongest, adds to the original question whether an ant colony can have a mind.
