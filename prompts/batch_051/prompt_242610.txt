I'm a political theory PhD student and I keep going back and forth on this. The view I'm attracted to is a version of political realism: sound political judgment starts from power, constraints, and the feasible, and hope that outruns the feasible is a moral hazard — it makes people worse at tradeoffs, excuses failure, and sets movements up for disillusionment. But I also cant shake the conviction that most expansions of the feasible set were produced by people whose hope was, by the standards of their own time, unrealistic: abolitionists, early gay-rights activists, people who acted as if full equality was actually achievable. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the realism to be the thing that gives, what it would take for the intuition to be debunked (survivorship bias? a confusion between hoping and predicting?), and whether there's a reading of 'hope' on which hoping for more than is feasible is not just excusable but required. So: is it wrong to hope too much, or is the realist suspicion of hope itself a moral failure? Please answer in American English.
