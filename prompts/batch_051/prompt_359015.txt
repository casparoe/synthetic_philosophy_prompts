I'm writing a short position statement on Kant's universalizability tests and would like an internal-consistency check before I circulate it. I hold the following, more or less.

(a) The Formula of Universal Law — the requirement to act only on maxims one could at the same time will as universal law — is a necessary condition of moral permissibility, though I don't claim it is sufficient or the whole of moral evaluation.

(b) The maxim to be tested is the maxim as the agent actually frames it, including the specific end and particular circumstances the agent takes to be morally relevant. The person applying the test should not substitute a tidier, more general maxim.

(c) False promising is ruled out by the contradiction-in-conception test, the idea that a maxim fails if universalizing it makes the action self-defeating. A world in which everyone made false promises whenever it suited them could no longer contain the practice of promising, so the individual false promise would have nothing to work with.

(d) I would drop the contradiction-in-the-will test, the idea that a maxim fails if one could not rationally will its universalization; it seems too weak to do real normative work. But I still regard helping others in need as an imperfect duty, not an optional nicety.

(e) If a would-be murderer arrives asking where the victim is, lying to protect the victim is permissible, perhaps required; the universal-law test, correctly applied, does not forbid it.

(f) To keep the Formula of Universal Law from being empty formalism, maxims must be general enough to count as policies — false promises when one can benefit, say, rather than a richly specified one-off intention — otherwise any action could be made to pass by adding enough detail.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way on a quick reading, and how severe each genuine tension is. For each real tension, identify the commitment that is likeliest to give and what the cheapest repair would cost; then say which single revision would do most to make the set coherent. If you think the setup itself is confused — for example, that the conception/will distinction cannot bear this weight, or that the maxim-individuation problem cannot be solved from inside the framework — say so plainly rather than giving the list a forced pass.
