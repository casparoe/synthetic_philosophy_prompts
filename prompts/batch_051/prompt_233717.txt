So I just way overpaid for a used lamp in an online auction — it's worth maybe ninety, I paid one forty, and I'm the one who "won." I know about the dollar auction, and the standard lesson everyone gives is to ignore sunk costs. But that isn't what my head was doing. Here's my theory, which I've been calling the spite pivot: at some point in an escalation the thing you're fighting over stops being the object and becomes a device for moving the loss onto the other person. After the pivot you aren't asking whether the lamp is worth $140; you're asking whether $140 is a fair price to make the other bidder eat a $135 overpayment. That feels like a different decision rule, not just a failure to ignore sunk costs. I want an honest assessment. Is this just the sunk cost fallacy wearing a hat? Is there really a separate criterion after the pivot? And can you give me an escalation where my account says keep going but the rational move is to stop — or the reverse? I might have invented the whole thing just to feel less stupid about the lamp, so be as harsh as you need to.
