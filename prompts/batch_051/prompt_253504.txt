Question: Let f(x) = x³ − 2 over Q. Label the three roots α, αω, αω², with α the real cube root and ω a primitive cube root of unity. Explain the sense in which the labeling of the roots is arbitrary, and spell out the difference between the claim 'the labeling is irrelevant' and the claim 'there is a canonical labeling.' Use the Galois group of the splitting field where it helps.

Student's answer: It doesn't matter which root you call α, since the fields Q(α), Q(αω), and Q(αω²) are all isomorphic, and any construction that doesn't privledge an embedding gives the same result up to isomorphism. The Galois group of x³ − 2 is cyclic of order 3, generated by multiplying a root by ω, and it acts transitively on the roots. So there is no way to pick out the real root using the field operations alone, and no labeling can be canonical. The distinction between the two claims is just well-definedness versus canonicity: a well-defined construction is one that gives the same answer however you relabel the roots, while a canonical labeling would be one fixed by every automorphism. Well-definedness holds here and canonicity doesn't.
