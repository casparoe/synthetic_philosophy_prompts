I've been rereading Frankfurt's On Bullshit for the fifth or sixth time, and there's a passage I keep coming back to: the one where he says the liar actually has to know the truth in order to hide it, so lying is responsive to truth, while the bullshitter doesn't care whether what he says is true or false, only whether it suits his purpose. That's a paraphrase, not the exact wording, but it's the argument in the middle of the Raritan essay. Anyway, I've been trying to apply this to LLM hallucination, and I've landed on a term I've been calling "precedent-bound bullshit." The idea is that a hallucination isn't lying, because the model has no beliefs or intentions to deceive; it isn't ordinary error either, because error presupposes a belief that fails to match the world, and there's no belief; but it also isn't Frankfurt bullshit, because the human bullshitter has a purpose and a knowing disregard for truth. An LLM is more like a system that has never had a relation to truth at all—it just produces the token string that the training distribution makes most probable given the prompt. I've been saying this is a new category: the model is bullshitting not because it doesn't care about truth, but because the concept of caring about truth doesn't apply to it in the first place.

But then I catch myself and think: maybe this is just Frankfurt's bullshit generalized to non-agents, or maybe it's just error with a different causal history. I keep trying to steelman it and then it slips away. Give me a committed assessment: reconstruct my distinction in its strongest form, say whether it's actually coherent, whether it collapses into Frankfurt's bullshit or into ordinary error, and tell me where it breaks.
