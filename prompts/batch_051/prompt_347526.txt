My brother and I got into it over a mug last week. He bought a limited-run mug for $12 at a street fair, and now he won't sell it for $40. He admits that if he saw the same mug in a shop today he wouldn't pay more then $15 for it. I told him that makes no sense: the mug is worth the same either way, and if he wouldn't pay $40 for it he should be happy to sell it for $40 and go spend the money on something he actually wants. He says I'm missing something, because the mug is now part of his kitchen, he reaches for it every Sunday, and selling it would feel like losing a routine rather than gaining $40. Then I noticed I do the same thing. I wouldn't pay $60 to see a band I only mildly like, but if someone handed me a ticket I wouldn't resell it for $80, even though I don't care enough to have paid $60. I don't think that's just laziness about reselling.

I keep going back and forth on whether we're both making a mistake or whether ownership actually changes what it makes sense to want. Is there a real difference between the "would you buy it?" question and the "would you sell it?" question, and can the gap be defended as a legitimate preference about attachments and routines rather than a bias? If it's defensible, what would make it defensible — for example, does it matter that the thing has memories or a stable place in a daily routine? And if it isn't defensible, what should a person do differently to keep the attachment without overvaluing the object?
