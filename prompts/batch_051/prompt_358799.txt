Reconstruct the underlying argument in Pascal's treatment of justice and force. He appears to endorse three claims at once: that right without force is ineffectual, that force not joined to something presented as right is experienced as tyranny, and that reason left to itself cannot settle what is just, so what is treated as just is chiefly what custom has established. Show how these three claims form one account of authority rather than a set of separate skepticisms: in particular, explain why force on this view does not merely enforce justice but helps constitute what comes to be treated as just, and why the impression that some laws are naturally just is better explained as the effect of habituation. Then press the internal difficulty: if justice is convention all the way down, Pascal's own condemnation of purely unadorned force seems to appeal to a standard his account has already dissolved. Explain the best answer available to him, and say whether it actually rescues the position or only relocates the problem.
