Section 7 of our republic's founding peace charter reads: "No state shall maintain a standing army, for such a force is in itself a standing threat of war and makes peace impossible." The drafters explicitly described themselves as applying Kant's argument in Perpetual Peace against standing armies. Today the republic has no conventional standing army: it relies on a small, rotating citizen militia for territorial defense. But it does maintain a permanent Cyber Defense and Deterrence Directorate, staffed by full-time civilians who are trained to infiltrate and, if ordered, disrupt another state's electrical grids, financial networks, and military communications. The Directorate is not called an army, and its members are not soldiers; it is authorized to act only in response to a cyberattack or on direct legislative authorization. Does the Directorate violate the peace charter? How would the text, the purpose the drafters took from Kant, and the question "what would the drafters have said if asked about civilian cyber operators" each apply? Where do these approaches diverge, and which should govern here?
