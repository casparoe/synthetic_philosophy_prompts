Please argue that the nineteenth-century arithmetization of the continuum — defining the real numbers via Dedekind cuts or Cauchy-sequence completion — is best understood as a discovery of a structure the real line already possessed, rather than as a stipulative definition that replaced a vague geometric notion. Address head-on the objection that the two constructions are distinct as definitions and that we standardly treat any complete ordered field as the real numbers only after identifying isomorphic copies; then explain why that fact does not reduce the achievement to the invention of a set-theoretic artifact. A strong answer should say what the construction manages to latch onto, and should avoid simply equating the discovery reading with full-blown Platonism about sets.
