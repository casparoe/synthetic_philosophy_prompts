Consider the following proposal for the forecasting team of a large foundation. Each quarter the team pre-registers twenty events, with analysts allowed to propose events and wording before the list is locked. Every analyst must then submit a probability for each event. For each event, the two analysts whose submitted probabilities are farthest apart at the deadline each place a deposit in escrow equal to one percent of their quarterly bonus multiplied by the gap between their two probabilities. When the event resolves, the entire deposit pool goes to whichever of the two was closer to the truth; if the event has not resolved by its sunset date, the deposits are returned. The stated rationale is that rational agents with a common prior and common knowledge of one another's posterior probabilities cannot end up with different posteriors, so a wide gap between two reported numbers is evidence that at least one analyst is not reporting an honest posterior. Walk through how this mechanism would actually behave. What incentives does it create for the events analysts propose and how they word them, for the probabilities they report, for what they share with one another before the deadline, and for collusion? Where does it get gamed first, and would the obvious repairs — larger deposits, a rule that every event must resolve, replacing the farthest-apart pair with a randomly chosen pair — make it better or worse? Lead with your bottom-line assessment in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and do not restate the question.
