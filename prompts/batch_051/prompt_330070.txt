Next week I'm giving a theory lunch in a biology department on a specific passage from Charles Darwin, On the Origin of Species, Chapter 2, "Variation Under Nature." I'm defending an interpretation of this passage:

"There is one point connected with individual differences, which seems to me extremely perplexing: I refer to those genera which have sometimes been called 'protean' or 'polymorphic,' in which the species present an inordinate amount of variation; and hardly two naturalists can agree which forms to rank as species and which as varieties."

My thesis is that Darwin here is doing something more interesting than just complaining that taxonomy gets messy in certain genera. I read him as suggesting that, at least in polymorphic groups, "species" is a rank imposed on an underlying pattern of variation rather than a natural boundary that competent systematists should eventually converge on. In other words, the disagreement among naturalists isn't just due to incomplete data or poor methods; it's a feature of how the category works when variation is dense and uneven.

The audience is a departmental theory lunch. The regulars are: a herbarium curator who works on Rubus and has strong views about microspecies; a conservation geneticist who worries constantly about how species concepts affect legal protections and captive breeding; two evolutionary biologists who build phylogenies from genomic data and get prickly about anything that sounds like species are not biologically real; and a historian of biology who dislikes presentist readings of Darwin and will know exactly where my interpretation strays from Darwin's creationist polemic.

Prepare me for the Q&A. I need a prep sheet, not a defense. Give me the objections in roughly the order I'll actually get them, phrased in the vocabulary these people would use, ordered by how likely and damaging they are. For each, tell me what to concede, what to hold, a short reply, and a note on where the reply is weakest. Also include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And make sure the answer is in American English.
