Here's the question I set:

"Augustine opens Book XI of the Confessions with a puzzle: he knows what time is until someone asks him to explain it. Reconstruct the puzzle about the being of time that he develops (why the past, present, and future each seem not to exist). Then present his positive account: what does he mean by calling time a distentio animi, and what role do memory, attention, and expectation play? Finally, address this: does Augustine hold that time is something objective, in the world, or something subjective, in the mind? Justify your reading with his own argument."

And here's one student's answer:

"Augustine's puzzle is that the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist, while the present has no extension — if it had any duration at all it would already be split into past and future. So none of the three times seem to be. But we still say things like 'that speech was long' and we measure spans of time, which looks impossible if we're measuring what doesn't exist. Augustine's answer is to relocate the three times in the mind: the past is present as memory, the present is present as attention (contuitus), and the future is present as expectation. When we measure something like a psalm being recited, we aren't measuring the syllables themselves, which pass away as soon as they sound; we measure the impression (affectio) they leave in memory, which stays. So time is a distentio animi, a stretching-out of the mind that holds past, present, and future together. This means Augustine is a kind of idealist about time: he denies that there is any time in the physical world, and he would say that a world without minds would have events but no time at all. Past and future are, in the end, just images we project from the present. So the measurement of time and the existence of time both turn out to be mind-dependent."
