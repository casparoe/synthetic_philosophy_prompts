I clerk for a trial judge whose rulings on statistical evidence have been unusually consistent. Her published opinions include four cases. (1) Blue bus: a pedestrian hit by an unidentified bus sued the company that operated 80% of the buses on that street at that hour; the 80% figure was the only evidence linking the company to the injury, and she entered judgment for the defense. (2) Gatecrasher: a rodeo sued a spectator for the admission price after evidence showed that 90% of the crowd that day entered without paying; the spectator's admitted presence and the 90% figure were the only evidence against him, and she entered judgment for the defense. (3) Cold-hit DNA: in a rape prosecution, the only inculpatory evidence was a database match with a random-match probability of one in 500 million; she held the evidence sufficient to prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. (4) Prison yard: a guard was assaulted by one of eight inmates in a yard, and the prosecution could show only that the defendant was one of the eight; she directed a verdict of acquittal.

We need a recommendation on a new civil case. A passenger was injured by a taxi that then left the scene. The only evidence tying the defendant cab company to the injury is a dispatch log showing that 99.8% of pickups at that stand during that half-hour came from its fleet; no witness saw the company's livery, and the passenger cannot identify the driver.

What would the judge most likely do if she wants to remain consistent with her own precedents, and which of her prior cases is the closest analog? Spell out the rival rules that are consistent with all four precedents, what each rule predicts in the taxi case, and which rule the judge is probably running. Then identify a further case that would cleanly separate the rival rules, and say what principle the judge would be adopting either way if she follows one rule rather than the other in the taxi case.
