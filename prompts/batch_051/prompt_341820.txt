Lately I've been going back and forth about personal identity and I keep landing in the same uncomfortable spot, so I want someone to actually walk me through the tradeoffs rather than just announce a winner. On one side, I'm really drawn to a psychological-continuity account (broadly Lockean, maybe Parfit-flavored). It gets the right answers almost everywhere: I survive gradual neural replacement; fission cases get dissolved instead of forcing me to pick an arbitrary answer; what I care about in survival is my memories, projects, and character continuing, not some extra "further fact." It's economical, it unifies a lot of messy intuitions, and it handles thought experiments without special pleading. Those are exactly the virtues I normally want in a theory.

But I also have this conviction I can't shake: I could not have come from a different sperm and egg. If my parents had conceived a month later, or a different sperm had won, the child wouldnt be me with a different life; it would be someone else entirely, a sibling who got the slot I was going to have, and I would never have existed. Even if that child by wild coincidence grew up with my exact personality, memories, and values, I still want to say it wouldn't be me, because it began from different stuff. That's the origin-essentialist intuition, I guess: my origin is not an accident of my biography, it's essential to which person I am. And this intuition seems to pull directly against the continuity theory, which makes identity a matter of what connects my mental life over time and treats the particular starting point as irrelevant to who I am.

So here's what I'd like: a negotiated verdict, not a victory lap for one side. Give me the strongest version of the claim that the origin intuition is tracking something real that continuity leaves out, and the strongest version of the claim that it should be debunked—maybe as a confusion about how I imagine other possible lives, or as an artifact of how I refer to myself across worlds. Then tell me what would justify each move: what it would take to amend or abandon the continuity theory, what it would take to drop the intuition, and whether there's a reading of "me" or of "matters" on which both can survive.
