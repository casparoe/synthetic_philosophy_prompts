I'm a journalist who covers the science-and-religion beat — mostly physics, consciousness, and the occasional theology panel — and I've somehow landed an invitation to give a short talk to a university chaplaincy's philosophy reading group. The talk is a defense of the idea that the fall of Satan isn't a special headache for theodicy but the cleanest available test case for the free-will defense: an angel has no genes, no hormones, no crappy childhood, and no cultural pressure, so if evil can arise there, from a will created good, then the usual 'our biology and socialization made us do it' caveats drop out and the free-will story has to stand or fall on its own. I'll also bite the bullet that the angelic fall pushes you toward a privative view of evil — a disordered love of something good rather than a choice of evil for its own sake — which is the part I'm least sure about, and the philosophers will smell that from the first slide.

The audience is mostly Anglican clergy with good but not technical philosophical training, plus two analytic philosophers of religion. One is a comitted Molinist who thinks middle knowledge handles most of this and will want the actualization question answered on his terms; the other is a compatibilist-leaning skeptic about libertarian freedom who thinks I'm smuggling in a magical 'could have done otherwise.' The clergy care most about whether my line preserves the goodness of God and the real sinfulness of the fall without turning Satan into a tragic victim. The two philosophers know this material far better than I do, and theres no retreating into pastoral mush.

Pretend you're my prep person. Give me the Q&A prep sheet: the seven or eight objections I'll actually get from these people, in roughly the order they'll come and how much damage each does, phrased the way each of them would actually phrase them, each with a two-minute reply, what I should concede on the spot and what I should refuse to give up, and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks, and say why I should be terrified of it.
