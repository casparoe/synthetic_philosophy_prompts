I keep noticing a situation I have no word for. Two people will give me the same probability, say seventy percent, and yet I treat their claims quite differently. One person's number comes from a long record of similar cases, and I expect it to stay near seventy percent even if some new facts come in. The other person's number is a judgment built from a single vivid example and a loose analogy, and I expect it to move a great deal as soon as any real evidence appears. The number is the same, but something other than the number is doing all the work in my confidence. I have come across the saying that risk is different from uncertainty, but that distinction seems to be about whether a probability can be assigned at all, whereas my cases have probabilities, and I am asking what differs between two with the same value. Does the thing that varies between my two cases have an established name, is it the same as that risk-and-uncertainty distinction or something finer, and if no single term fits exactly, what would you call it and where do its boundaries lie?
