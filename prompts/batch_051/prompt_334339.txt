Im a novelist, and I keep running into a problem when I try to write characters who feel bad about themselves. Everyone says shame is about the self and guilt is about the act, but that formula falls apart the moment I actually test it against scenes I'm trying to write. I'd like a working definition of shame (and, as the contrast, guilt) that gets the following cases right. Here's my list:

1. A woman cheats on her husband, tells no one, and feels terrible, but the terrible feeling is mostly a sense that shes no longer the person she thought she was. Is that shame even though theres no exposure?
2. A man feels bad because he grew up poor and now can't afford the kind of clothes his friends wear, though he hasn't done anything wrong. Is that shame, or just social anxiety?
3. A teenager is bullied for stuttering. He isnt ashamed of the stutter itself; hes ashamed that he cares what the bullies think.
4. A bank teller skims cash, never gets caught, and the feeling that troubles him isn't "I did something wrong" but "if people knew what I am they'd hate me." Guilt or shame?
5. A soldier freezes under fire and someone else gets hurt. He later says "I did something unforgivable." His therapist says it was impossible to act differently. Is the label guilt or shame doing the work here?
6. A character cries when shes praised, because she feels like a fraud. Not for any specific deception, just a general sense of being a fake. Is that shame?

I know some of these might be both, or neither. I want the definition to discriminate, not just say "its a mix" for each. Run the definition through all six, tell me which verdict is least secure, and then be self-critical: what would change your mind about the definition, or which case would most likely break it if a reader disagreed with you? Also, since I'm a novelist worrying about cultural variation: could a whole culture lean on one of these emotions more than the other, and would your definition survive translation into a setting where people dont have the same self/act distinction?
