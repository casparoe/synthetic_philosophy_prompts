Over the past three years I have learned to think about stories almost entirely through the categories used in fan fiction communities: canon and canon-compliance, canon divergence, alternate universes, out-of-character characterization, headcanon versus fanon, fix-it fics, and real-person fiction. Recently I have been following arguments about how far biopics and historical novels may rework the lives of real, deceased people before they become dishonest or disrespectful — whether the filmmaker owes the dead person accuracy, whether inventing scenes and dialogue is wrong in a way that differs from ordinary fiction, and whether living descendants have any standing to complain. I want to see how much of that debate my fandom vocabulary can state precisely, and how much it cannot.

Please restate the debate about what we owe the real dead when we dramatize their lives in the vocabulary I just listed, as faithfully as you can. Give the clean mappings — I expect, for example, that complaints about factual invention will partly map onto canon divergence, and complaints about flattening a person's character onto out-of-character writing — but flag every point where the fit is forced or misleading rather than natural. Then give me a residue report: which claims in the debate translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression in the fandom framework at all. I suspect the hardest to house will be the moral standing of the real person, the question of whether one can harm the dead, and the special position of descendants and other living people. Finally, commit to a view: is the residue a failure of my vocabulary, or does it reveal that some of the philosophical debate is itself less clear than it looks? I would rather have a definite, argued position than a menu of possibilities.
