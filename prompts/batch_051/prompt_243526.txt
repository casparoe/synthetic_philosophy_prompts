We treat the people who first said women, colonized peoples, or animals belong inside the circle of moral concern as visionaries, and we call the expansions they won moral progress. But let someone today argue that the circle should now take in insects, or digital minds, or the interests of people who won't be born for centuries, and the same sort of people rolls their eyes and calls it crankish. Is there a principle that seperates the expansions we've canonized from the ones we resist, or is the only difference that the first batch already happened and got normalized? I want the strongest candidate principle you can find, tested against one case from each side of the asymmetry, and an honest verdict on whether it justifies the asymmetry or just explains it, and whether we should keep the asymmetry or give it up. If you keep it, tell me what you'd say to a 19th-century abolitionist who was told exactly what we now tell the insect advocate.
