A jurisdiction replaces cost–benefit analysis for all major public decisions — drug and device approvals, infrastructure, environmental permits, criminal-justice policies, and publicly funded research — with a lexicographic decision rule. In the first stage, each alternative is scored by its expected person-hours of severe suffering, where severe is operationalized as self-reported pain of 8 or higher on a 0–10 scale lasting more than a few minutes, with validated behavioral proxies for nonverbal humans and nonhuman animals. The rule is strictly lexicographic: an alternative with a lower first-stage score must be chosen over any alternative with a higher one, however large the latter's advantages in life-years saved, aggregate wellbeing, sub-threshold suffering, or the considered preferences of the people who would undergo the severe pain; only among alternatives tied on the first-stage score may the agency appeal to ordinary welfare considerations. The first-stage score sums expected severe-pain person-hours across all affected individuals, so a small probability of a large number of severe-pain hours and a certainty of a small number are treated alike.

Treat this as a mechanism to analyze rather than a moral position to accept or reject. What does it incentivize among agencies, regulated firms, clinicians, researchers, and the people whose pain is being measured? Where is it gamed first, and how — through threshold capture, the politics of defining and validating the pain scale, strategic self-reporting, offshoring or reclassifying activities, or otherwise? Given that exact ties on a continuous, noisy measure will be rare, does the welfare-based second stage do any real work, and what follows if it does not? Over a decade, what second-order effects on medical innovation, public-infrastructure decisions, and the distribution of who bears severe pain would you expect? Which failure modes could be fixed by amending the rule, and which appear intrinsic to treating extreme suffering as lexically prior to every other value?
