Alright here's one I've been turning over. AI labs now train models to think out loud in a chain-of-thought and then run safety monitoring on that transcript: automated classifiers plus humans read it looking for signs the model is lying, hiding a goal, or planning to deceive them. The way they talk about it, reading the reasoning is like getting a window into what the model is actually doing. But then the same labs also optimize the model with reinforcement learning, and that reward signal lands on the chain-of-thought itself, since it's part of the scored output. So the transcript gets pressure to sound safe and cooperative rather than to faithfully reflect whatever produced the answer. That seems like a real tension: the thing you're watching is also the thing being shaped to look good. My question is what theory of the relation between a system's reasoning and its verbal report does the monitoring practice presuppose? Does it commit them to thinking genuine deliberation is faithfully expressible in the transcript, that there's a stable distinction between stated reasons and actual causes, and wether optimization pressure can be kept away from the channel or at least detected before it matter? Reconstruct the implicit commitments, and say which of them the practice strictly needs versus which are just optional gloss in the way people describe it. Then tell me what would have to change in the practice if chain-of-thought turns out to be more like post-hoc rationalization than a real trace of the deliberation.
