I'm a philosophy professor teaching a grad seminar on pre-Socratic and Hellenistic materialisms, and next week we hit Lucretius' argument in Book 1 of De Rerum Natura for why there has to be void. You know the passed over bit: he says if there were no empty space in things, nothing could ever move, because the whole function of matter is to block and impede, and he gives examples like sound passing through walls, water through rock, and food through the body's passages. I've been going back and forth about this with a friend who does condensed matter — he keeps saying that "solid" in his mouth just means a lattice with defects, phonons, band gaps, vacancies, interstitials, not a total absence of emptiness. I have a decent working grasp of that vocabulary, but I'm not fluent enough in it to do the translation myself.

So here's the task. Restate Lucretius' argument for the existence of void in the vocabulary of modern condensed matter physics — vacancy, interstitial, dislocation, phonon, effective mass, band gap, compressibility, bulk modulus, electron hole, vacuum state, and the like — as faithfully as you can, treating his "atom" and "void" as things that need to find counterparts in a solid-state picture rather than just being waved through. Then give me the residue report. Which of his claims map cleanly (e.g., a vacancy is just a missing lattice site, not an independently existing nothing), which ones you had to fudge, and which ones have no counterpart at all. In particular, I want to know whether the old claim that matter is impenetrable and that its function is obstruction means anything in a framework where ordinary solids are overwhelmingly unoccupied by point particles and forces are carried by fields. Is the Lucretian argument really just making a point about compressibility and available modes of deformation under a different name, or is there a residue that condensed-matter talk cannot express? My physicist friend's line is "it's just vacancies and interstitials, man," and I suspect that's right about the mechanics but deaf to something metaphysical. I'd like to know exactly where the translation starts leaking.
