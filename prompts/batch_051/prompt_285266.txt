So my two closest friends, Marcus and Priya, have been stuck on the same argument for about two years now. Marcus thinks that when you make a serious romantic commitment, there is a real moral obligation to be sexually exclusive, and that breaking it is its own kind of wrong — not just breaking a promise, but a betrayal, even if your partner never finds out, and even if your partner had said in advance they don't mind. Priya thinks exclusivity is just one relationship style among others, a convention that different couples and cultures handle differently. For her, the only things that are actually wrong are lying, breaking an agreement you did make, and ignoring consent; there is no hidden default obligation baked into the word "commitment." What finally got them to try something constructive was a mutual friend whose partner agreed to an open relationship on paper but then fell apart when it actually happened. Marcus says that gut reaction was tracking a real wrong; Priya says it was just a socially installed script. Neither of them (or me) have any philosophy background, so don't write it like a term paper.

They've now agreed to do a good-faith "adversarial collaboration" — I heard that phrase on a podcast — and produce a joint statement. Can you write what they'd actually be able to put their names to? Start with a numbered list of claims both can sign without wincing: about what commitment does and doesn't involve, what honesty and consent require, how jealousy and the feeling of betrayal work, and so on. Then give the exact remaining disagreements as a table, phrased so that each person would accept the wording of their own side. Then the cruxes — the smaller questions where, if one of them were settled, at least one of them would actually change their view. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence they'd both agree bears on it, and say what Marcus predicts and what Priya predicts. If part of the dispute turns out to be mostly about words, say what part and why. Keep it specific enough that a person could argue with any single claim in it.
