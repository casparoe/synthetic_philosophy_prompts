I spent much of last night in a lengthy argument with a friend about infinite regresses — specifically, whether the epistemic regress of justification is genuinely vicious, or whether the usual objection to infinitism quietly assumes that a reasoner would have to traverse the whole chain. The discussion left me wanting to be unusually precise about the distinction between vicious and benign regresses. By coincidence, I now have to grade a set of undergraduate exam answers on exactly this topic, and I would appreciate help with one of them. I am asking for a grade and for the point-by-point comments that justify it, so that the assessment could be handed to the student for revision.

Exam question:

Explain the distinction between a vicious and a benign infinite regress, and discuss whether the regress of justification in epistemology is vicious. Include at least one example of a regress generally thought to be benign, and explain what makes it different from a vicious one. Finally, state what the epistemic regress does and does not show about the choice among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism.

Student answer:

An infinite regress is a series in which each item stands in the same kind of relation to the next and the series has no final member. The series is vicious when each item has some status only in virtue of the next item's having it, because then the status is postponed forever and the first item never actually acquires it. It is benign when each later item is generated by, or follows from, an earlier item that already has the relevant status; continuing the series adds nothing and subtracts nothing. A familiar benign regress is the truth regress: if the sentence "snow is white" is true, then the sentence saying that it is true is also true, and so on without end. Each later truth is a consequence of the earlier one rather than a condition of it, so the lack of a last member is harmless.

The distinction therefore turns on the direction of dependence. When the later members are supposed to ground the earlier ones, the regress is vicious; when the later members merely unpack an earlier item that is already secured, the regress is benign.

The regress of justification is the chain generated by the demand that every justified belief be justified by another belief that serves as its reason. Here the justifying work is postponed at every step: B1 is justified only if B2 is justified, B2 only if B3 is, and no member has its justification in itself. So the regress is plainly vicious. Moreover, because a finite mind cannot actually traverse or complete an infinite chain of reasons, a real believer could never have the justification that an infinite chain is supposed to provide. The only live option is therefore to stop the chain at basic beliefs that justify themselves. Foundationalism is forced, and both infinitism and coherentism fail: infinitism simply keeps the vicious chain going, while coherentism replaces a straight chain with a circle.
