I'm on my university's research policy committee, and we're finally facing a problem I've been muttering about for years, so I want to circulate a short memo before Thursday's meeting. Here's the situation. Our rules currently let any department chair sign off unilaterally on "low-risk, exempt-from-full-IRB" behavioral studies that draw on university-wide pools: the alumni email list, the student subject pool, campus messaging channels. There's no central registry, so nobody sees the whole queue. Next term, six different units (psych, econ, a business-school lab, two policy centers, ed school) have faculty planning their own field experiments, each sending "light touch" persuasive messages to overlapping slices of the same alumni and student lists. Individually each study is harmless — a little noise in the data, whatever — but in aggregate these people are going to get six versions of a nudge in a month, which will poison response rates for future studies and leave the impression that the university is quietly experimenting on people without asking. The catch is exactly the unilateralist's curse: each chair, looking only at their own faculty member's proposal, correctly judges that "this one study is fine," and each of them is right about that ... but the aggregate is not fine, and no single chair has the authority or the incentive to be the one who says no.

The committee wants to decide Thursday weather to (a) keep decentralized chair sign-off as is, (b) require a central registry and committee approval for any study touching a shared pool, or (c) something in between. I've been asked to draft the written opinion that will frame the discussion.

Could you write that memo for me? I want it to lead with a concrete recommendation — the actual policy we should adopt, stated as a bottom line in the first paragraph — and then argue for it. Use these sections, in this order:

1. **Recommendation** (~100 words): state exactly what we should adopt Thursday and the key mechanism (a registry, a cap, a coordinating body — whatever you think).
2. **Why this is a unilateralist's curse** (~150 words): explain, concretely and without jargon, why decentralized sign-off predictably produces an aggregate no chair would individually endorse.
3. **The case for keeping decentralized approval** (~150 words): steelman the chairs who'll fight this — local knowledge, speed, not bureaucratizing small studies.
4. **The case for coordination** (~150 words): the positive argument for the fix, and what it would require in practice (who logs what, who decides when the pool is saturated).
5. **Objections and replies** (~100 words): the strongest objection to your recommendation and your answer to it.
6. **Bottom line restated** (~50 words).

Keep the whole thing under 700 words; it should be something I could literally send around, not a philosophy paper. Do the reasoning honestly — don't pretend the decentralized side is stupid.
