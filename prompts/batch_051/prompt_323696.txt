Spent way too long last night arguing with a friend about presupposition accommodation. My line was that when I say "even Sam is coming to the party" and nobody objects, the group genuinely updates a shared conversational scoreboard: 'Sam was unlikely to come' gets added to the common ground even though nobody ever asserted it or explicitly agreed to it. His line is that the scoreboard is just a metaphor, and all that really happens is each listener infers something about my beliefs and uses that to interpret what I say next; 'accommodation' is just the name we give after the fact. I'd love a experiment that could pull these apart. How would you test whether accommodation is a real change to a shared record, as opposed to just per-person inference? Give me the design, what outcome would favor each reading, and what a positive result still wouldn't settle.
