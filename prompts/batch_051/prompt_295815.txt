I've been arguing with my seminar about a term I made up, "crane-washing," and I want somebody to tell me straight whether it's a concept or just a vibe.

Setup: Dennett's cranes vs. skyhooks. Skyhooks are unexplained explainers lowered in from above; cranes are actual bottom-up mechanisms that build the next level; greedy reductionism is the mistake of pretending only the bottom level is real. I think there's a matching failure that nobody flags, and I've been calling it crane-washing. A theory gets credit for being reductionist because it uses low-level-sounding vocabulary — circuits, mechanisms, algorithms, selection, information, "the brain does X" — but the actual explanatory work is still being done by a skyhook that got relabled at the last minute. The low-level nouns launder the miracle into respectability.

Examples: "altruism evolved because groups with altruistic genes outcompeted groups without them" sounds like a crane, but "altruistic genes" is the very thing to be explained, wearing a lab coat. Or "the brain recognizes faces via a face-recognition mechanism" — the mechanism is the skyhook, nouned until it sounds mechanical. My suspicion is that crane-washing is worse than plain skyhookery because it can pass peer review without anyone noticing that the mystery just moved house.

Is this actually a distinct failure mode, or have I just rederived Dennett's point (or Molière's dormitive virtue, or something in the explanation literature)? What's the strongest version of the idea, and where does it break — like, when is leaning on a not-yet-explained mechanism legitimate good science and when is it laundering? Keep it under 700 words, and be mean if I've just reinvented a wheel.
