I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep doing something with conference registrations that my friend, a philosopher, insists is a distinctive kind of irrationality rather than just bad planning. Earlier this year I paid an early-bird, non-refundable fee for a cosmology meeting in Italy. A month later I reread the program I already had and decided it was too broad, so I ate the cancellation fee and registered for a smaller astrobiology meeting. Two weeks after that, still with no new information, I compared the same to programs and decided I'd been overrating the astrobiology schedule, so I paid to switch to a data-journalism bootcamp. Then, again with no new facts, I reread everything and concluded the Italy meeting was actually the best fit for my work after all, so I paid another fee plus the late rate to switch back. Each individual swap genuinely felt like trading a worse option for a better one. My friend says the pattern shows my preferences run in a circle, and that someone with the right menu of three options could deliberately bleed me dry forever. He adds that the deeper problem is that I let whichever me is deciding at 11pm outvote the earlier me who made a commitment. I want to write a column about this. Is he right that a loop of locally sensible pairwise choices can amount to irrationality? What exactly has to be broken in my comparisons for the sequence to work? And is the real fix to stop paying switch fees, or to change how I weigh my own earlier commitments?
