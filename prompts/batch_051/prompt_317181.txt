A friend of mine spent an evening trying to convince me that a white horse is not a horse, on grounds he says come from the School of Names. He laid it out like this:

(1) If a white horse were a horse, then "bring me a horse" and "bring me a white horse" would be the same request — same success conditions, same thing asked for.
(2) But a yellow horse satisfies the first and does not satisfy the second.
(3) So the two requests are different.
(4) So "white horse" is not just "horse" with an extra harmless detail; the two phrases pick out different things.
(5) Therefore a white horse is not a horse.

I cant accept (5), and I'd like to keep (2) because its just a fact about how the words work. But if I reject (5) while keeping (2), the break has to be in (1), (3), or (4), and none of them look like the kind of claim you can just deny. Besides, if the roles were reversed and I said a white horse is a horse, he asks me why a yellow horse will do for "horse" but not for "white horse" — and I don't have a good answer. Where does the argument go wrong, if it does, and whichever premise I give up, what else do I have to pay for it?
