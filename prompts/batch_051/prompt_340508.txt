Peter Suber, in The Paradox of Self-Amendment: A Study of Law, Logic, Omnipotence, and Change (1990), reconstructs Alf Ross's version of the paradox of self-amendment. The passage that anchors the assignment is this:

"Suppose a constitution has an amending clause, cited as AC.1, which provides that an amendment to the constitution will be valid law if and only if condition K.1 is met. K.1 specifies a procedure such as ratification by three-fourths of the states within seven years. We want to amend AC.1. Our proposed amendment, call it AC.2, provides that an amendment to the constitution will be valid law if and only if condition K.2 is met. We would not want to amend AC.1 if K.1 and K.2 were the same. Hence, suppose that K.1 and K.2 make valid amendment turn on different conditions. Suppose further that they are inconsistent in the sense that each permits something that the other forbids and forbids something that the other permits. [...] That is the paradox. Whenever the amended AC will contradict the original AC, which will almost always be the case in self-amendment, then the inference that models the act of self-amendment will be invalid."

Taking that passage as the test case, suppose someone claims to have a general theory of self-amendment: an account of how a system of rules can validly change the very rules that govern change — constitutional amendment clauses, Nomic's mutable rule set, a legislature's standing orders, contract clauses governing their own modification. Write the list of requirements such a theory must meet to count as adequate, with the consideration or case behind each, and mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp (for example, the camp that models amendment as deductive inference, the camp that treats rules as temporally indexed, a Hart-style acceptance model, or a formal logic of self-reference). Pay particular attention to three questions. Which widely demanded features are genuine desiderata and which are artifacts of one modeling approach? Is any subset of your requirements jointly unsatisfiable — in particular, does the passage show that deductive-inference validity, self-amendment, and consistency cannot be held together, or can that appearance be dissolved? And which single requirement does the most to exclude otherwise plausible candidate theories? Skip scoring the existing theories against the list; the criteria are the part I want.
