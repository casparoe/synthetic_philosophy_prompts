I'm in a philosophy of religion course and we just did the problem of hell, and I keep noticing that the three main positions—eternal conscious torment, annihilationism, universalism—have all been around forever, use a lot of the same proof texts and the same philosophical moves about proportionality, and yet nobody actually changes sides. The standard framing is whether a finite sin can deserve an infinite punishment, but I'm starting to think that framing is what's stuck: one side hears it as a question about retributive desert (and answers that wronging an infinite God has infinite gravity), another hears it as a question about what punishment is even for, and annihilationists seem to be answering a different question about what destruction amounts to. My hypothesis is that its not really a textual dispute or even a dispute about proportionality, but a disagreement about the point of punishment/justice, and everything else is downstream. I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is—verbal, substantive, different questions under one heading, starting intuitions neither side can argue for—rather than a defense of one view, with reasons not a survey, and an honest answer about whether anything (a distinction, a moral argument, a reframe) could count as progress. And tell me what would change your mind about the diagnosis, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
