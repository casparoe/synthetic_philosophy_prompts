i've been rereading the early bits of Hofstadter's GEB—the part where he teases apart frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages—and it keeps bugging me that the distinction is easier to state than to actually keep straight. so: write a pantoum, five or six stanzas, where the same phrase gets reused so that at one point it's working as the frame message (the poem announcing itself as a thing to be decoded), at another as the outer message (telling the reader how to decode it), and at another as the inner message (just the content). the pantoum's way of recontextualizing its lines seems built for this, since the whole point is that the same words change their role depending on what level they're operating at. don't name-drop the terms in the poem itself; the structure should do the vertigo of asking where the meaning actually lives. and if the last stanza loops back to the first so the frame/inner distinction starts to wobble, even better.
