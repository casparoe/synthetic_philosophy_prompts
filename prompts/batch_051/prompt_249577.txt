I've been going round in circles on something a mate said. Suppose someone has been blind from birth and can tell a cube from a sphere by touch, no bother. Then they get an operation that gives them sight. The question is: the very first time they see a cube and a sphere, without touching either, could they point to the one they'd just been holding? I don't mean knowing the words 'cube' and 'sphere' — just matching by eye. My gut says obviously yes, since they've already learnt the shapes, just through their fingers. My mate says no, that seeing and touching are like two different languages and you have to learn the translation before either can tell you about the other. At first, he says, the shapes would just look a jumble. I said you can't test it, because you can't give a person sight. He reckons it has been tested, but I'm not sure a test could even get at the interesting bit. So: what would the best test actually look like? What result would count each way, and what would it settle and not settle — does it tell us something about how concepts work, or only about how eyes and hands get wired together?
