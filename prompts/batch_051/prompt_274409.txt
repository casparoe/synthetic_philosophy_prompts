I've just joined my local council's community safety and events sub-comittee, and I've been asked to draft the written opinion because my daughter was the target of a nasty school scapegoating thing last term, so I can't just pretend its academic. A local art's group wants to stage a New Year's Eve public ritual called 'Carrying Out the Town's Guilt': people put written confessions into a straw mock king, parade it through the streets, and then beat and burn it. Half the committee thinks this is a healthy ritual substitution that stops people from scapegoating each other; the other half thinks it literally enacts a scapegoat mob. The art's group has sent us this passage from the 1922 abridged edition of James George Frazer's The Golden Bough, in the preface:

"The custom presents a close parallel to the ancient Babylonian festival of the Sacaea, at which a mock king was dressed in the royal robes, allowed to enjoy the real king’s concubines, and after reigning for five days was stripped, scourged, and put to death."

They say this proves that ritual mock-kings were a substitute for collective guilt, not a punishment. Draft the decision memo for the sub-committee: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and make the reasoning turn on what Frazer is actually describing in this passage. Take the losing side's strongest point seriously, not just the legal/permits side. Write in British English. As the last line, put "Verdict:" followed by one of: approve, reject, approve with conditions.
