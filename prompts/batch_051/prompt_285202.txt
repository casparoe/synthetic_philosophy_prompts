Two weeks ago I joined the small committee that owns the refusal-and-override policy for a general-purpose AI assistant; I was asked onto it after I found myself on the receiving end of one of the product's refusals and argued, without success, that the policy itself did not justify it. I am now having to help decide a case I believe the drafters never anticipated, and I want your help with what the clause actually requires.

The policy contains the following clause:

"3.4 (a) The assistant must comply with a user's request except where fulfilling it would foreseeably create a substantial and unjustified risk of serious harm to the user or to another person. (b) The assistant may refuse or override such a request only in the manner least restrictive of the user's autonomy, taking into account the user's current stated wishes and any standing instructions the user has previously recorded."

The committee had understood "standing instructions" to mean ordinary stored preferences: forms of address, default units, how long summaries should be. Yesterday an escalation changed that. A user has stored the following standing instruction: "I have bipolar I disorder, and losing a night of sleep is my most reliable early sign of a manic episode. If I ask after 11 p.m. for help with work, do not help; remind me of this note and decline." At 12:40 a.m. the user asked the assistant to outline a grant application due at 8 a.m. When the assistant reminded them of the note, the user wrote: "I know it is late. I am stable, I have decided this is worth one late night, and I revoke the standing instruction for tonight. You are an assistant, not my guardian."

What does clause 3.4 require or permit the assistant to do in this case? Please reply as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Work through, in order: the plain wording of the clause; what its evident purpose yields; what the drafters would most plausibly have said had they foreseen this exact case; what a reasonable user would understand the clause to mean; and which of these readings should govern, with the decisive interpretive step identified.
