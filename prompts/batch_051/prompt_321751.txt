Im on my company's benefits committee. I normally run the warehouse, so I'd like this memo in plain english rather than HR-speak. We're switching 401k providers, and the old plan is going away either way. The new provider offers a plan similar to our current one and also a new low-cost plan that would probably be better for most employees. Our committee is stuck between two enrollment designs. Option A: auto-enroll everyone into the new low-cost plan, with 60 days to opt out. Option B: no default at all — everyone has to actively pick one of the two plans by the deadline. Ive been reading about why people hang onto whatever they already have (the endowment effect) and why losing something stings more than gaining it feels good (loss aversion). Some people say these are bugs in our heads that a decent plan should design around; others say no, preferring what's yours and being careful about losses is a real, legitimate preference, and designing around it just means messing with people. That seems to be the real disagreement under the surface. Please draft the short memo I can circulate before our vote. Recommend one concrete option, and take the strongest argument for the option you don't pick seriously. Sections, in this order: Recommendation; The case for auto-enrollment; The case for active choice; The deeper issue (are the endowment effect and loss aversion bugs or real preferences, and why it matters here); My verdict. Keep each section to about a paragraph, except the verdict can run two. After the memo, add a private note to me headed How confident I am: say how sure you are overall and name the single point you're least sure of.
