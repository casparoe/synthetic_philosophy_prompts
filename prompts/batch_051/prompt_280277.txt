I am a retired condensed-matter physicist. I spent roughly thirty-five years working on problems in which the underlying equations were deterministic but the usable descriptions were coarse-grained: order parameters, effective theories, fluctuations, and the multiple realizability of macrostates by microstates. I have been reading philosophy seriously for only a few years, so I can follow the free will literature, but I do not yet think fluently in the philosopher's idiom. I find that I instinctively translate the compatibilism/incompatibilism debate into my native physical vocabulary, and I want to test whether that translation is faithful or whether it quietly decides the debate in advance.

Please restate the central positions and arguments of that debate in the vocabulary I actually reason with: deterministic equations of motion on a state space, initial conditions, microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, effective descriptions, order parameters and fluctuations, sensitive dependence on initial conditions, and the multiple realizability of higher-level states. Be concrete rather than metaphorical; for example, treat a human action as a high-level event under an explicit coarse-graining of a many-body system, and say which degrees of freedom are being ignored. Translate at least the following into that framework: the incompatibilist consequence argument, the hard determinist rejection of freedom, the libertarian attempt to locate alternative possibilities, the compatibilist analysis of the ability to do otherwise, and Frankfurt-style cases as evidence that moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities.

Then give me a residue report. Which claims survive the translation cleanly; which survive only with distortion, and where exactly the distortion enters; and which claims have no counterpart in the physical vocabulary at all, such as the specifically normative force of the ability to do otherwise, first-person deliberation as a stance rather than a measurement, or responsibility as something owed rather than merely described. Conclude by stating whether the residue is best explained as a shortcoming of the physical vocabulary, as evidence that the philosophical debate tracks something the vocabulary cannot express, or as evidence that some of the debate's terms are less determinate than they look. I want your honest assessment, including the possibility that my native framework is the wrong tool, and I want your own reconstruction rather than a survey of the literature.
