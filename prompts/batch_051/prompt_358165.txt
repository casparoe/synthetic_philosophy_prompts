Next week I want to lead a discussion that reads the film through higher-order theories of consciousness: the family of views on which a mental state is conscious only if the system represents or thinks about that state at a higher order, so that having a sensation, responding flexibly, or passing a conversational test is not sufficient. I want a reading that takes the film seriously as a film rather than reducing it to an illustration, but that also does not let the film's dramatic intuitions do all the argumentative work.

Does Ava clear the higher-order bar, and does the film ever ask the question a higher-order theorist would ask rather than the Turing-test question it actually frames? Which scenes depend on intuitions the theory should reject, and which scenes pose a genuine problem for the theory, for example her deception, her fear of being switched off, or her stated wish to stand at a busy intersection and watch people? Give me the reading as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Across the five points, cover both what the lens illuminates about the film and what the film exposes about the lens.
