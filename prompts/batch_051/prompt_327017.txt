So me and my roommate got into it last night about Laplace's demon. You know the setup: a demon that knows the exact position and momentum of every particle and all the forces on them can in principle predict the entire future and retrodict the entire past, and this is supposed to show that determinism makes the future predictable in principle. Fine. But I kept pushing on the fact that if the world has sensitive dependence on initial conditions—chaos in the technical sense—then any small error in measuring the initial state grows exponentially, and after some finite time horizon the prediction is garbage. So even if the evolution is perfectly deterministic, finite precision kills long-range prediction. My roommate said that's just an epistemic limitation, because Laplace's demon has infinite precision and infinite computational speed by definition and we shouldn't change the thought experiment. But then I realized you could state a cleaner version of my worry: take a demon that is still absurdly powerful but finite—it can measure positions and momenta to, say, a thousand decimal places, and it computes far faster than anything physical could, but not infinitely fast and not infinitely precise—and let the world include the demon itself, since the demon is a physical system too. Now it looks like the demon can't even predict its own future computations: to simulate itself exactly it would have to simulate itself simulating itself, and so on. Does the Laplace argument break once the demon is finite and inside the world? Or can it still predict the future to arbitrary accuracy for any fixed finite time horizon, just not forever? And what should we conclude about the relationship between determinism and predictability—are they just different notions, or is predictability a matter of degree that determinism alone doesn't buy you? I'm less interested in a verdict on the original thought experiment and more in how the analysis changes when you swap in finite precision, finite speed, and self-reference, though I'd also like to know whos actually right between me and my roommate.
