Take the standard sorites argument about heaps: one grain isn't a heap, and if n grains aren't a heap then n+1 grains also aren't, so no finite pile is ever a heap. Now run the same argument on generations instead of grains. Suppose someone born on January 1, 1965 is squarely Gen X and someone born on January 1, 1997 is squarely Gen Z (or Millennial under someone else's cuttoff, doesn't matter). The tolerance step would be: if a person born on day d is Gen X, then a person born on day d+1 is also Gen X, because one day can't switch your generation. Chain that across 32 years and you get the 1997 baby being Gen X, which everyone wants to reject. But here's what I'm actually asking: does the sorites argument even transfer to a category like a generation, or does it only work when there's an underlying continuous property in the world? If you're a defender of the idea that Millennials and Gen Z are real kinds, which move should you make—is the right response that generational boundaries are vague the way heap is vague, or is it that the tolerance step is false because generation membership gets fixed by an explicit convention and not by closeness along a continuum? And if you take the second route, what does that do to the realism claim? In other words, does the sorites problem land any actual damage on the ontology of cohorts, or does it presuppose that a generation is heap-like before it can get its induction going?
