I'm a software engineer on a platform team whose services pass data to one another through a tangled dependency graph, and we've been discussing whether to adopt ideas from Pearl's causal hierarchy and the do-calculus for reasoning about experiments and incidents. One senior engineer keeps arguing that we already have the relevant concepts under different names. In his rendering, a structural causal model is just a module dependency graph with the labels changed: each observed variable corresponds to a function or service in the codebase, each arrow corresponds to a data dependency or call path, exogenous noise corresponds to hidden inputs or internal nondeterminism, a confounder corresponds to shared state or an unseen common dependency, and the do-operator corresponds to monkey-patching a node to a fixed value regardless of what its upstream callers would ordinarily compute. On that basis he concludes that interventional questions are just controlled A/B tests, counterfactual questions are just what-if replays of a single function call under a different input, and causal inference therefore adds no conceptual machinery we don't already use as engineers; we only need better dependency analysis and more careful experiments. His version sounds suspiciously neat to me, but I don't have a formal philosophy background, so I'd like the analogy audited rather than just arbitrated.

Could you lay out the correspondences explicitly, then walk through where the mapping is faithful and where it breaks? In particular, I want to know whether monkey-patching genuinely has the semantics of a do-intervention — the rest of the system held invariant while the incoming arrows are cut — or whether it quietly imports assumptions (a fully specified graph, no hidden common causes, replaceable functions whose downstream effects stay put) that structural causal models treat as substantive rather than free. Then address whether the analogy survives the move from doing-questions to counterfactual questions about a particular observed run, where, as I understand it, the standard account first updates the hidden noise terms in light of what was actually observed, then applies the intervention, then recomputes the downstream values. Does replaying a function call with a changed input cover that, or is there something in the counterfactual step with no clean code-equivalent? Finally, tell me which parts of the senior engineer's conclusion survive the audit and which parts turn out to have been carried by the analogy itself.
