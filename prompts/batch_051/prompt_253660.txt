I'm a PhD student in the history and philosophy of science, and my advisor keeps a running set of assessments of how quickly different fields make reliable progress. Her entries include the following. Astrology: no reliable progress, since its claims are vague enough to fit any outcome. Weather forecasting: rapid and steady progress, because forecasts are issued daily and scored against observations. Nineteenth-century clinical medicine: stagnant despite enormous activity, because outcomes were observed but heavily confounded. Post-1950 medicine: rapid progress, credited to randomized controlled trials. Software engineering: rapid progress, because tests and users return immediate error signals. String theory: nearly stagnant across four decades, because its proposals have not met empirical test.

She has now been asked to predict whether long-term climate modeling will make reliable progress over the next twenty years. The brief she was given notes that climate models issue quantitative projections on decadal timescales, so a given projection cannot be directly verified for decades; however, the models are continuously tested against historical and recent observations through hindcasting, and individual model runs are cheap.

What is she likely to predict? State the principle about feedback and progress she seems to be running, give the other principles that fit her track record about equally well, say how each bears on the climate modeling case, identify which earlier case she would treat as the closest analog, and name a further case that would best settle which principle she actually holds.
