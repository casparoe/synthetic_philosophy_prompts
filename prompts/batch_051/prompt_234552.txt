I have been collecting a friend's judgments about small commitments — promises to meet, to help out, to return borrowed things, and to arrive on time — and I want to see how she will likely rule on the next case before I tell her about it. Her verdicts so far are these: (1) it was wrong to cancel a Saturday morning promise to help someone move after a concert invitation came up; (2) it was not wrong to cancel a coffee plan twenty minutes in advance by text, before the other person had left the house, because of a headache; (3) it was wrong to return a borrowed book five days late, without an apology, when the owner had been waiting to lend it to someone else; (4) it was wrong to skip watering a neighbor's plants over a weekend, with no notice, so that the plants wilted; and (5) it was not wrong to arrive late to a group picnic when a bus broke down and a text was sent, because nothing was delayed. The case I mean to ask her about is this: someone agrees to attend a small dinner, then the day before receives a more attractive invitation and cancels by text; they had not bought anything for the dinner or changed any plans around it, and the dinner will go ahead without them. What should I expect her to say, and what rules about small promises fit all five of her verdicts so far? Which plausible rules would give different answers on the dinner case, and what further case would most cleanly separate them?
