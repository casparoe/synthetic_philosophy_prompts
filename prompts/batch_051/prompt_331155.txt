I have been trying to turn a recurring intuition into a position, and I would like it stress-tested. By 'recognition' I mean the attitudinal side of respect: being treated as someone whose word, judgment, and standing count in a conversation or institution, as opposed to merely being protected from overt insult or procedural exclusion. The descriptive idea I keep coming back to is that recognition failures are self-compounding in a way that one-off acts of disrespect are not. Suppose a junior colleague's suggestions are repeatedly ignored or attributed to others. The natural response is for her to offer fewer suggestions; her subsequent reticence is then read as evidence that she has little to contribute, which further lowers the recognition she receives. Her capacity to claim recognition — to protest, to insist on being heard — is itself one of the things the deficit erodes. I have been calling this a 'recognition deficit,' by analogy to a balance sheet, not because I think respect is literally a currency, but because the compounding structure seems real.

From this I draw a normative claim I am much less sure about. If deficits compound, then a duty to give everyone equal recognition — the same baseline of being taken seriously — will reproduce existing deficits rather than correct them. So I am tempted by what I call 'recognition triage': those running the largest deficits have the strongest claim on our attitudinal effort, such as sustained interpretive charity, patience in debate, and being taken at their word, and this is owed as a matter of attitude rather than mere conduct. The distribution is deliberately unequal, aimed at equal standing over time rather than equal treatment at a moment. I can see the obvious objections: it seems to license moral scorekeeping of inner attitudes, to grade people by a rough metric of oppression, and to make respect a scarce resource that must be rationed. What I want to know is whether the 'recognition deficit' concept is coherent enough to carry this normative weight or whether the balance-sheet metaphor is doing illicit work; whether the deficit and triage claims have already been worked out under existing names; and where precisely the position breaks — what does it over-moralize, and is there a defensible core left after the objections?
