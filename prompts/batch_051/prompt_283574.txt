I am looking for a working definition of 'denialism' as applied to twentieth-century mass atrocities — one that separates denialism from legitimate historical revisionism and from ordinary factual disagreement about details. Propose a definition, run it through the following cases, and say where it gives a verdict you would defend and where it gets into trouble.

The cases, with my own leanings in parentheses:
1. A pamphleteer claims the Holocaust was fabricated for political and financial gain, backing the claim with forged documents. (Clearly denialism.)
2. A professional historian argues in a peer-reviewed venue that the commonly cited death toll for the Nanjing Massacre is inflated by several tens of thousands, while affirming that the massacre occurred and was brutal. (Clearly revisionism, not denialism.)
3. A scholar argues, using genuine but selectively quoted Ottoman documents, that the Armenian deaths were the unplanned consequence of war, famine, and deportation rather than a campaign of extermination. (I lean denialism, but I am unsure whether the decisive problem is the selectivity, the conclusion, or something else.)
4. A head of state acknowledges that large numbers of Armenians died but declines to call the events genocide, saying the matter is for historians. (I have no settled view.)
5. A historian shows that one widely repeated atrocity story from Nanjing is poorly sourced and probably propagandistic, while fully accepting the massacre itself. (Not denialism — yet it shares a surface feature with case 3, and I cannot say precisely what the difference is.)
6. Someone accepts the Holocaust but insists the number of dead was roughly 300,000 and that most died from typhus and the ordinary privations of war. (I want to call this denialism even though it admits some of the facts.)

I suspect the term is doing two jobs at once, one about a writer's method and good faith and one about the political use of what is said, and that cases 3 and 5 expose the strain. Give the definition, apply it to all six, and tell me which of my labels, if any, you would overturn. Please also keep explicit what is settled history and what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
