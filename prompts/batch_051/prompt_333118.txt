I've been working through Mill's late essays on socialism and trying to figure out what he'd say about a proposal that's floating around where I live right now. As best I can reconstruct, here's how he came down on the big proposals of his own day, judged as a list:

- Worker-run cooperatives: the one thing he's most enthusiastic about. He thought they could eventually crowd out wage labor, but he kept insisting the transition has to be voluntary, through small experiments and profit-sharing, because workers have to learn to manage capital first.
- Profit-sharing between a firm and its employees: fully in favor, mostly as a training ground for full worker ownership later.
- Planned communities of the Fourier type: he respected the design and said they might work somewhere down the road, but he didn't think people were ready, and he didn't want the state coercing anyone into them.
- Inheritance: he wanted steep estate taxes and an upper limit on how much any one person could receive, though he stopped short of abolishing inheritance outright.
- Land and industry: he was all for a special tax on unearned increases in land value, but he resisted having the state expropriate land or run industries itself, which he thought would be clumsy and politically dangerous.

Now the new one. There's a bill that would require every firm above a certain size to become at least 51% worker-owned within thirty years, with the shares held in an employee trust so no individual worker has to by in. There's no state ownership involved, and the firms keep competing in normal markets.

What do you think Mill would say about that bill? Don't just give me a yes/no: I want to know what rule you're reading off his earlier verdicts, whether a different rule fits those same verdicts just as well, what each rule predicts about the bill, which reading you'd put your money on and why, and what further proposal you'd test him on to seperate the two readings once and for all.
