I keep having this argument with a coworker about whether "the company" can be blamed for stuff as a company, not just as a pile of employees. He thinks companies set incentives, have a culture, and make decisions that none of the individuals would've made alone, so when our org ships something harmful the company itself is the right target of moral blame. I'm on the only-people-can-be-blamed side: blame has to land on particular humans, and "blame the company" is either sloppy shorthand or just a confused way of talking. We've gone back and forth long enough that we want to write one of those adversarial-collaboration writeups — the thing where you lay out what you both sign, exactly where you split, and what would actually move one of you.

Draft that document for us, and don't resolve the debate or point me at readings — I want the joint statement itself. Structure it like this:

1. Agreed claims — what we both accept about how company decisions work, the difference between legal liability and moral blame, and what "blameless postmortems" do and don't show (around 250–350 words).
2. Disagreements, as a table — each row is one sub-disagreement, with his formulation and my formulation each written so its own side would accept the wording (around 300–400 words).
3. Cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would genuinely change at least one of our minds (around 150–200 words).
4. Cases and predictions — for each crux, a concrete scenario we'd both agree bears on it, plus what each of us predicts the right verdict in that scenario is (around 300–450 words).
5. What's probably verbal — the part of the dispute, if any, that's really just about words (around 100–150 words).

Make the examples feel like the kind of thing that actually happens at a tech company — understaffed safety reviews, a toxic culture nobody individually chose, cost targets that make bad outcomes predictable — not abstract trolley-variants.
