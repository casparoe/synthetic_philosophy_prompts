I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether harms done inside games and virtual worlds are real harms, going by my gut on cases. A stranger joins my friend's Minecraft world and burns down the base she spent 300 hours building: clear yes, real harm, I'd be livid. Someone cons a guildmate in an MMO out of a rare mount that he could have sold for maybe $80 on a gray-market site: also real, but somehow the money part isn't what bothers me. A griefer on a shared survival server spends every evening wrecking the same group's builds using mechanics the server allows: yes, real harm, mostly because of the persistence and intent. I load a mod that lets me torture an NPC with pain animations, but no one else is involved: not a harm to anyone, though it makes me a little queasy. A game company shuts down an always-online game and everyone's stuff vanishes: that's a real loss, but it doesn't feel like the same kind of wrong as the griefing. Is there a principle that gets these, or close? And what does it say about the one I keep going back and forth on — in a fit of anger I delete a seperate shared family save file that has my sibling's builds in it, not just mine?
