I run a weekly reading group for second-year graduate students in mathematics and computer science who are philosophically curious but have never taken a philosophy course. Next session is on the rule-following paradox as Kripke reconstructs it from Wittgenstein, and I have ninety minutes to make it land. I do not want a transcription of the lecture; I want a plan for it. The throughline I am trying to establish is this: the difficulty is not that we cannot predict what someone will do next, but that nothing about a person's finite past usage, her present sense of certainty, her brain states, or her behavioral dispositions seems to single out one continuation as the one she meant; the subsequent skeptical solution then changes the subject from truth-conditions to assertability conditions and to agreement among speakers in shared practices. Students should leave able to state why the problem is said to threaten content itself rather than merely future behavior, why the obvious naturalistic fixes fail, and what the skeptical solution does and does not claim.

Please lay out the order in which the components must be introduced, and what each later step presupposes from the earlier ones, so that the skeptical conclusion seems unavoidable rather than a trick. Then tell me the single running example around which the session should be built — I assume it will be an arithmetic rule such as plus and its divergent variant, but explain what that example must have and what it must not be loaded with. I also want a misconception map: the predictable things this audience will reach for — treating the paradox as Humean induction, proposing that dispositions fix meaning, reading the skeptical solution as ordinary social constructionism, or taking "rule" in the everyday sense of explicit instructions — and for each, the point in the sequence where it should be headed off and how, without breaking the arc. End with a check for understanding that would show the main idea has actually landed, and tell me what I should openly leave out for this audience and how to mark it as a boundary of the session rather than a silent omission.
