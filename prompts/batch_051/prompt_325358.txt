I am a novelist at work on a book in which a character learns that, by a few hours, he might have been conceived under different circumstances. I keep returning to the philosophical claim that he could not have existed at all if a different sperm had fertilized the egg: that a person's origin in a particular pair of gametes is essential to being that person. I want to know whether this is a claim that can genuinely be tested, or whether it is only a way of talking. Please set aside exegesis of the literature and design the strongest test you can — an experiment, a comparison across cases, or a structured set of observations — that could distinguish an origin-essentialist reading from the view that the claim is false or a harmless redescription. Specify which result would favor each side. Then say exactly what the test would and would not settle: in particular, whether it could separate psychological facts about how we track identity from metaphysical facts about identity itself, and whether a positive result would be built into the operationalization rather than discovered. Finally, tell me where your reasoning is most likely to fail and what argument or evidence would change your verdict.
