One of my friends has gotten really into the meaning-of-life stuff lately and she has a bunch of verdicts about which imaginary lives count as meaningful. I've been trying to reverse-engineer what rule she's actually running, because her calls seem to hang together but I can't quite state it. Here's what she's said so far:

- The Sisyphus Camus describes, rolling a boulder up a hill forever with nothing to show for it: not meaningful.
- Sisyphus but with the twist that the gods make him genuinely love the boulder-rolling, so he wakes up thrilled to push the rock: she still says not meaningful.
- Someone plugged into an experience machine who has the felt experience of a rich, accomplished, deeply satisfying life, though none of it is real: not meaningful.
- A women who raises her kids well and finds it deeply fulfilling: meaningful.
- A painter who produces genuinely admired work and loves painting: meaningful.
- A man who spends his life counting blades of grass and finds it absorbing and pleasant: not meaningful.

Next week she's going to be asked about a scientist who develops a cheap cure for a disease that kills millions of people, but who is miserable the whole time, gets no satisfaction from the work, and says the whole thing feels pointless. What will she say about whether the scientist's life is meaningful? I'm less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which further case would best tell them apart.
