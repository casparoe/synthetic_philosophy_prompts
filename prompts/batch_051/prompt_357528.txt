I'm reading this piece on the history of the concept of matter, and the author keeps leaning on the old atomist line that "nothing exists except atoms and the void" to settle a bunch of questions about what matter is—specifically, that matter is essentially impenetrable and solid, and that empty space is real without being a body. Fair enough, but the longer I stare at the slogan the less clear it is what its actually claiming. It could be an ontology (two kinds of thing: body and space), or a claim that all there is is solid bits and the gaps between them, or a reductive claim that every change—and maybe every quality—is just bits rearranging through the void. Then there's the weirdness that the atomists also treat the void as what is not, so does the slogan even say the void exists? There's even a fragment where Democritus reportedly said that thing is no more than nothing, which sounds like it either guts the slogan or is a bit of wordplay on his technical terms for atom and void. Give me the full menu: what are the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making, which of them are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, which are false or anachronistic baggage from later corpuscularianism, and which precise reading is my author actually leaning on when he uses the line to argue that matter is essentially impenetrable?
