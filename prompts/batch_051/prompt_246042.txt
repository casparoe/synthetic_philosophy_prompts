I'm putting together a satirical handout for a philosophy of science course, for the week we do the perennial physicists-versus-philosophers spat, and I'd like something I can actually make the students read. Could you write a Swiftian modest proposal as a deadpan, buerocratic memo from a university physics department to the provost, arguing that the philosophy of science program should be defunded and replaced by a Division of Unintentional Philosophy housed inside the physics department. The memo should take the familiar scientists' line—Hawking announcing the discipline is dead because it hasn't kept up with modern physics, the quip usually attributed to Feynman comparing philosophy of science to ornithology for birds, Weinberg complaining that philosophy of science has not significantly helped the advance of physics—and push it one step further to its logical conclusion: if philosophers are useless but physicists keep doing philosophy by accident anyway, the efficient move is to give the philosophers' budget to the physicists and let them do the philosophy as an unfunded hobby, with no training and no peer review. Around 700 words, numbered like an actual memo, and include at least one quote from the fictional department chair, who is sincerly convinced he is doing philosophy a favor.

The comedy has to come from getting the actual philosophy-of-science content right, not from strawmen: underdetermination, realism versus instrumentalism, the measurement problem, the demarcation problem, the difference between explaining and merely predicting. The memo should reason in ways that are obviously mistaken to someone who knows the field but in a way a smart physicist might really reason—for instance, treating realism about unobservables as settled by how confident the chair feels when he looks at a Feynman diagram, or declaring the measurement problem closed because the shut-up-and-calculate attitude has never wasted anyone's time. It should stay deadpan throughout, so that the provost—and the student—can tell exactly what is being ironized without the memo ever winking.
