Something I've never managed to get straight is the claim that sola scriptura carries a built in regress problem. As I understand it, the worry is that if the text alone is the final authority, then every doctrinal dispute turns into a dispute about what the text means, and the moment you appeal to an interpreter, a tradition, a confession, or the text itself to settle that, you've either smuggled in a second authority or pushed the same question back one step. I can't tell whether this is a genuine infinite regress or just the shallow point that reading is fallible. Please explain in ordinary prose what the regress actually is, and then work through the main ways people try to stop it: saying the text is clear on the essentials, saying the individual reader is directly guided, adopting a shared confession, or giving an institutional body the final say. For each, say whether it actually blocks the regress and what it costs. No headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; continuous prose only, the way youd write an email.
