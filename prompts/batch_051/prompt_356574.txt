Okay so in my early modern philosophy class weve spent a lot of time on Descartes's cogito argument — the move where he doubts everything and then concludes that the very fact he is thinking proves he exists. A later line of criticism we touched on says this overstates things: maybe the most the cogito can show is that thinking is occurring, not that there is an I standing behind the thought doing the thinking. It could be some kind of subjectless stream of thoughts. My question is a bit counterfactual: if Descartes had been able to see this later objection, how do you think he would respond? Would he accept that the cogito only gets you to the bare fact of thinking, and then try to rebuild the I from something else, or would he insist that the I is directly present in the very act of doubting? And if he insists on the I, does that I have to be a full mental substance, or could he get away with something thinner? Im not asking what the right answer is — I want to know what Descartes would have said, given his wider commitments.
