Ok so I just got home from a four-hour argument with a friend about whole-brain emulation and wether the upload would be *you*. He kept saying an emulation is obviously just a copy, I kept saying that depends on what continuity actually amounts to, and we never managed to pin down what we were even disagreeing about. Can you write me a short story that does the work we couldn't? Make it about someone who has to actually decide whether to be scanned, and don't let the story settle it by fiat. Make it weirder than the standard teleporter horror: maybe the scan isn't destructive and there are two of them afterwards, maybe the emulation gets restored from an old backup after a glitch, maybe they have to run the scan three times because the first two are only 97% faithful, or the person stays awake during the scan and feels a strange gap while everyone else insists nothing happened. The story should force the reader to notice what they're actually tracking when they call something the same mind — fidelity, temporal overlap, substrate, causal continuity, whatever it is — but through the plot, not a lecture. Ideally it should make one specific answer to the identity question feel viscerally right, even if it's not the answer the character starts with. And please skip the lazy ending where the original dies in the scanner while the copy lives on happily.
