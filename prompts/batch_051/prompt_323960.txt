You're a political epistemologist who moonlights as a market designer. Start by brainstorming the failure modes, and only then settle on the list — I want you to think carefully here, not pattern-match. Before I read another futarchy proposal, write me the full desiderata a system of governance by prediction markets would have to meet to count as a serious rival to an ordinary parliamentary system in a mid-sized country, not just a clever blog post. For each requirement, give the concrete failure scenario that justifies it. I especially want the awkward ones handled head-on: thin markets and low liquidity, manipulation by insiders and by the government whose policies are being bet on, the choice of the welfare metric the markets are supposed to maximize, the need to operationalize that metric in advance, the transition problem (who gets to switch the country to futarchy, and what stops them switching back), and the bored public that stops trading after year three. Don't stop at those. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste (e.g. wether the metric should be GDP-ish or broader, continuous markets vs. periodic market-based votes, real money vs. play money). Flag any pairs of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I care most about. And tell me which single requirement most candidate designs actually fail on. No bibliography or reading list; I want the criteria themselves.
