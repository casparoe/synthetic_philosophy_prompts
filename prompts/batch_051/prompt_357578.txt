For my school's philosophy club, I have to write a one-page "belief profile" on consciousness and submit it for a consistency audit. I currently accept all of the following:

1. Phenomenal consciousness exists and cannot be fully captured in purely functional or behavioral terms.
2. Consciousness is epiphenomenal: brain processes cause conscious states, but conscious states have no effects on the physical world.
3. When I form a belief that I am in pain, the pain itself does not cause my belief; the neural processes that cause the pain also cause the belief.
4. Introspection is a generally reliable way for me to know my own conscious states.
5. A belief is not justified if the process that produces it would have produced that same belief even if the state it purports to report had not occurred.
6. The explanatory gap between objective descriptions and subjective experience is real, but its existence is not by itself good evidence against physicalism.
7. Zombies are conceivable, but conceivability of zombies does not show that consciousness is nonphysical.

I would like you to go through these and tell me whether the set hangs together. Please rank any tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix would be for each, and also identify at least one apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection. Keep the response to at most 800 words.
