I've been reading Strawson's essay on the reactive attitudes and moral responsibility, and I keep feeling like I'm being pulled in two directions at once. The view I'm drawn to says responsibility isn't a metaphysical fact waiting out there to be settled by whether determinism is true. It's just the whole practice of resentment, gratitude, indignation, and the demands we make on one another. On this picture, even a convinced determinist still gets angry when a friend deliberately breaks a promise, and she doesn't experience that anger as a mistake she should correct. The objective stance we take toward, say, someone in a psychotic episode or a small child is a local exception, not a genuine option for all our relationships at once. I find that unifying and honest about what were actually like.

But I also can't shake the intuition that when I resent someone I'm claiming, at least implicitly, that they could have done otherwise. And if determinism is true, then in some sense nobody ever could have done otherwise. So doesn't that make the entire practice of holding people responsible a systematic error, even if it's an error we're built to carry on making? I don't want a referee's verdict. I want to know what it would take for the could-have-done-otherwise intuition to be the thing that goes, say as a confused demand for a kind of freedom that was never coherent; what it would take for the reactive-attitudes view to be amended so it keeps the practice but gives up on desert; and whether there's a reading of could have done otherwise that's local and dispositional enough that both sides survive. Give me the strongest version of each move and what would justify it.
