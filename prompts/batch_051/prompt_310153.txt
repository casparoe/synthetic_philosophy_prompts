Heres a parallel I cant decide about. In reinforcement learning, most of the learning is local credit assignment: you compute a TD error — basically a prediction error about how good that specific choice was in that specific situation — and you nudge just that value a little. You rarely send a system-level signal saying the whole value function is badly miscalibrated and should be scrapped. Now the guilt/shame distinction in moral psychology: guilt is supposed to target a bad act (I did a wrong thing and should repair it), while shame targets a bad self (I'm the kind of person who does that). So the parallel would be: guilt is to action-level TD error as shame is to model-level error, and maybe the reason people usually find guilt the more productive moral emotion is just the same reason learning works better with lots of small local corrections than with throwing away the model every time it gets something wrong.

But I'm suspicious of my own analogy. A self isnt literally a value function, and shame does social work — appeasement, signalling lowered status, preempting rejection — that a model-level error signal doesn't do. Also, someone could defend shame as tracking real facts about character that no number of action-level repairs can reach, which would make the parallel less of an analogy and more of a point about when local versus global correction is appropriate. On the other side, if the parallel were deep, it would predict that shame is useful exactly when your errors are correlated across situations — when you're not just unlucky in isolated cases but systematically misjudging — and I don't think that's quite how shame actually operates.

So is this a deep structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion? What would have to be true on both sides for the parallel to carry real weight, and what does the parallel predict that turns out false?
