The debate over the direction of time and the thermodynamic arrow has now run for well over a century, and as far as I can tell the participants never move. Everyone in the discussion accepts that the relevant microdynamics are, for this purpose, time-reversal invariant; that entropy increases in one temporal direction and not the other; and that the early universe was in an extraordinarily low-entropy state. What remains contested is whether that low-entropy boundary condition — call it the Past Hypothesis — does any real explanatory work.

The disagreement stalls, I think, because four positions each diagnose the others as confused rather than as getting an empirical fact wrong. Boundary-condition minimalists hold that symmetric dynamics plus one contingent low-entropy end is a complete explanation, and that any further demand is a category error. Mechanists hold that the specialness of that state is exactly what must be explained: only a dynamical or cosmological mechanism that makes low-entropy beginnings probable or typical would count as physics. Two-boundary advocates take the observed arrow to be local rather than global, and treat the singling-out of one temporal end as unjustified. Epistemic deflationists hold that the past/future asymmetry is a feature of records, knowers, and interventions, so the search for an ontic thermodynamic arrow is ill-posed. Each side says the others are equivocating on "the arrow of time" or on "explanation," which is precisely why nobody moves.

My suspicion — offered as a hypothesis, not a settled view — is that this is really three disagreements sharing one name: a disagreement about what counts as explaining a global asymmetry; a disagreement about whether a boundary condition on the universe as a whole can be confirmed independently of the asymmetries it is invoked to explain; and a definitional slippage among thermodynamic, epistemic, and causal/interventionist senses of "the arrow." But I can also see a hidden empirical question underneath: whether any proposed mechanism that makes low-entropy beginnings typical can avoid predicting that typical observers are transient local fluctuations. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it all three, one of them, or something else entirely? Give me a diagnosis, engaging what each side actually says, not a survey of the positions.
