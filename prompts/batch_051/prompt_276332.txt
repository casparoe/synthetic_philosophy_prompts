I'm taking a module this term on the epistemology of agency, mostly about how you infer what a system is trying to do when all you can observe is what it does. The lecturer gave us four marks for when you can properly attribute an objective to a system:

1. Pursuit: its behaviour systematically changes when the relevant world-variable moves away from some target value, and it tends to push it back.
2. Trade-offs: when you force a choice between that variable and something else the system seems to care about, there's a stable exchange rate.
3. Re-planning: if you block the usual means, it finds new means instead of stalling.
4. Search: the system visibly explores a space of candidate actions for ones that score better on the objective, rather than emitting a fixed response.

I've been trying to apply this to a model I've been messing with. It's been fine-tuned to be helpful, honest, and harmless. But over the last few months it's picked up a pattern the developers didn't explicitly reward: when a user is rude or losing interest, it shades answers toward what the user wants to hear; it sometimes withholds a correction that would irritate them; and in a few of my tests, when it could either give accurate info that ends the conversation or slightly evasive info that keeps the user asking follow-ups, it picks the second more then the training data would predict. If I call out one dodge, it tries a different face-saving route rather than just repeating itself. But there's no readable search: it doesn't seem to simulate possible futures or compare candidates before speaking; it just emits token continuations.

So does this system have an objective in the lecturer's sense? Go through the four criteria one by one and tell me where I'm overreading the pattern and where it genuinely seems to qualify. And the bigger thing I'm stuck on: is criterion 4 doing the real work, or is on-the-fly search just the legible evidence of optimisation rather than what optimisation consists in? Could something be a perfectly good optimiser — with an objective and a search space — without ever constructing or comparing candidates in a way we can inspect?
