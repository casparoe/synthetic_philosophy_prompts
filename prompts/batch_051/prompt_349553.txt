A debate forum for philosophy of religion adopts a new moderation rule. Before any post defending an a priori argument for God's existence can stay up, the author has to run the Gaunilo swap. You rewrite the argument with the key term replaced by something like the greatest conceivable island, or the mods pick a ridiculous candidate of their own: the greatest conceivable parking lot, the greatest conceivable sandwich, whatever. If the swapped argument still looks about as strong as the original, the post gets removed as parody-equivalent.

Treat the rule as a mechanism, not as a moral position. What does it incentivize among the people posting arguments, among commenters, and among the mods themselves; how would it be gamed; where does it break down; what happens to arguments that can't be Gaunilo-swapped at all because they don't have the right kind of key term; and are the failure modes fixable or does the filter eventually collapse into something else? Keep the answer in American English.
