Im a cog sci grad student who came in from backend engineering, and every time I read the modularity-of-mind stuff I catch myself thinking "this is just software architecture with a bit of philosophy bolted on." So here's the exercise I want. Restate Fodor's modularity thesis in the vocabulary I actually build in: services, APIs, contracts, encapsulation, shared state, message passing, orchestration, monolith vs microservices. I'm fluent in the engineering, not in the philosophy, so be precise about the mapping rather than cute. Cover at least: domain specificity, informational encapsulation, mandatory and fast operation, shallow outputs, and the central systems that do whatever modules cant. Then do the same for massive modularity — the view that the mind is all modules, a Swiss-army knife of evolved special-purpose systems and no general-purpose central processor to speak of. For each view, say what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what has no expression at all in the architecture vocabulary. I especially want to know what happens to Fodor's worries about isotropy, the frame problem, and homuncular regress when you try to say them in architecture terms: are those real constraints my vocabulary hides from me, or are they artifacts of a framework that assumed there must be a central orchestrator? Finish with a residue report that says which residue is a defect of the target vocabulary and which residue is evidence that parts of this debate are less substantive than they look.
