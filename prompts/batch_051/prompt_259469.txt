I keep going back and forth on whether LLMs can make genuine intuitive leaps or just remix their training data. A coworker swears they can't, so I wrote out his argument to see where I'd push back. (1) Anything an LLM outputs is, sampling quirks aside, a function of its weights plus the prompt. (2) The weights are what gradient descent settled on from a finite training distribution of human text and code. (3) A function fit to a finite distribution can't reach outside what that distribution determines; it can only interpolate between, or recombine, things already in it. (4) A genuinely novel leap of intuition is, by definition, outside the training distribution. (5) So LLMs can't make genuinely novel leaps. I keep feeling the sleight of hand is in (3), but then I confuse myself with a counterexample: fit a degree-2 polynomial to three points and it spits out values at x=100, which is nowhere in the training data. Is that outside the distribution, or does the argument fail at (4) because 'novel' is being used in two different senses? Pin down exactly which step or steps fail and under what reading, or tell me the argument is valid and the conclusion just feels wrong. And please don't just say step (1) is false because sampling makes things stochastic—I'm looking for the philosophical weak point.
