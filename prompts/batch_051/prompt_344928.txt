The instructor's guide pushes this harder: because these misconceptions apparently make predictions, organise experience, and shrug off a single disconfirming observation, it says teaching should work the way scientific revolutions supposedly do, by staging conceptual change. You confront the student with an anomaly, let them struggle with it, and let the bad theory die. I'm due to build a lesson around this and I want to know how far I can trust the analogy.

I know the education literature splits here — some people describe these as full framework theories, while others say they're a loosely organised grab-bag of p-prims, small context-specific intuitions that fire in particular situations and only look theory-like because the kinds of problems we set happen to trigger the same ones — but I've never known whether that dispute actually matters for teaching.
