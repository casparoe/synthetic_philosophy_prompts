Our textbook gives a compressed summary of Paul Churchland's view that I keep rereading and can't quite tell if it's fair. It says something like: folk psychology is a theory whose main explanatory posits are beliefs and desires; because it hasn't made real progress in thousands of years and because neuroscientists don't find belief-like or desire-like structures in the brain, we should conclude that beliefs and desires don't exist. It follows, the book says, that psychologists should already be dropping belief and desire explanations and describing people only in neural terms.

Something here strikes me as too strong, but I'm not sure how much of that is just me not wanting the conclusion to be true. The last "it follows" step looks like it needs an extra premise, and I don't see why being a stagnant theory or not yet being found in the brain is enough to proof that a posit doesn't exist. My roommate says the textbook is basically right and I'm overthinking; my TA said something cryptic about it being a fair gloss but with a couple of important slips. I'm trying to figure out which parts to trust. Could you walk through the summary and say what's accurate, what's a common simplification, and what a defender of eliminative materialism would actually object to?
