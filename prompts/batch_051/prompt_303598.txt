I've been going round in circles on the old 'should AI be utilitarian or deontological' question, and my current view is that the interesting issue is not which we should pick but whether we could ever tell them apart from the outside. Suppose a lab says it has put a genuine deontological constraint in a model: the model treats some actions as ruled out even when the expected consequences clearly favour doing them. But every refusal I have seen it make is something a smart utilitarian would also refuse, because of knock-on effects or bad precedent or reputational damage. How would you test whether a system is applying a side-constraint rather than just assigning a very large negative value to some outcomes? Design the strongest experiment or battery of prompts you can, with cases where a deontologist and a sufficiently far-sighted utilitarian genuinely come apart. Say which results would favour each reading, and then say what no behavioural test could settle. I know about the move where any constraint can be rewritten as a utility function with a huge negative term for violations, so I half expect the answer to be 'nothing', but I want to know if the question has more content than I think.
