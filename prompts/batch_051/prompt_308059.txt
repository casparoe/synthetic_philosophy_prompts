Last month on a long car ride, a friend tried to get me to take the simulation argument seriously, and I realized I kept shutting the conversation down in a way I couldn't fully justify. My rule, as best I can reconstruct it, is this: I only let a skeptical scenario get a grip on me — brain in a vat, the world created five minutes ago with all my memories in place, a seamless simulation — if I can be told what I would experience, expect, or do differntly if that scenario were true. If the scenario is designed so that every observation and every sensible choice comes out exactly as it would on the everyday assumption that there is an external world, then I treat it as a story with no doxastic weight and go back to believing I have hands, my friends exist, and the car we are riding in is real. My friend says this is just Moore's proof of an external world being smuggled in as an ordinary rule: point at your hands, insist the everyday belief is more certain than any premise of the skeptical argument, and then pretend you've answered the question rather than begged it. He also says I'm confusing the fact that a scenario doesn't change what I would do with the claim that it couldn't be true, or that it gives me no reason to lower my confidence. I see some force in that, but I also can't shake the sense that a hypothesis stipulated to be invisible and behaviorally inert shouldn't be allowed to move my beliefs at all, since belief is supposed to answer to evidence and evidence has to be the sort of thing that could in principle differ.

So what I want from you is a verdict on the rule itself. Please answer as an epistemologist, and think carefully before you commit. Work through the strongest version of my rule and the strongest version of my friend's objection, then give me a definite position: is my policy defensible, is it a verificationist shortcut dressed up as epistemic hygiene, or is there a repaired version you would endorse? Be explicit about where the rule fails — can an undetectable skeptical hypothesis still be a live rational possibility that should lower my confidence, and if so, what is the difference between taking it seriously in theory and letting it change my beliefs in practice? Commit to an answer and give your reasons rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me.
