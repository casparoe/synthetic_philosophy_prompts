I help run a small research initiative that builds early-warning indicators of democratic decline, with a focus on Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States. We can fund roughly three workstreams over the next two years, and I need an agenda, not a survey. I am not asking for a reading list; I am asking which intellectual work we should do first and why.

Our internal disagreements keep returning to a few questions. One is conceptual: should backsliding be modeled as a continuous quality-of-democracy score, as crossing a threshold, or as a repertoire of specific anti-democratic moves? Another is weighting: should we weigh formal institutional capture, such as changes to courts, electoral administration, and media regulators, more heavily than the erosion of informal norms like mutual toleration, institutional forbearance, and accepting electoral loss? A third is whether backsliding names one syndrome at all, or rather a family of distinct trajectories, such as executive aggrandizement, partisan capture of referee institutions, sustained delegitimation of the opposition, and manipulation of the information environment, which may call for different measurement strategies. A fourth is more uncomfortable: several partners suspect that backsliding is just a partisan label that whoever is out of power applies to whoever is in power, so I need the concept to be specified in a way that gives that charge a refutable meaning rather than an ideological one.

Please rank the lines of work that would most repay our two years. For the ones you recommend, tell me what makes them upstream: do they have to be settled before other questions become tractable, or before the index's raw scores become interpretable? Do they favor data we can actually collect across our four cases, rather than concepts that only fit rich democracies with stable party systems? And please say which prominent candidate you would deprioritize because resolving it would not change much downstream. For your top two or three, tell me which finding or argument would most plausibly change your ranking, and where you think your reasoning is weakest.
