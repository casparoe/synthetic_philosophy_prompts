I run a small physics-and-philosophy reading group and last week we got completely stuck on Maxwell's demon, in the good way. The problem is everyone knows the one line answer — Landauer, erasure cost, second law safe — but when I pushed people on whether the demon's *measurement* of a molecule is free or not, or what happens if you let the demon have an unbounded memory so it never has to erase, it turned out we didn't actually share a view. I'd like a handout for next meeting that works as a decision tree: it should sort a reader by what they actually think about the demon, not by which slogan they can repeat.

Please build the tree from the logical forks rather than from a list of names. The root should be whether acquiring information about a molecule is thermodynamically free. If it isn't, split the "measurement costs" branch into Szilard's view (the cost is in the logic of measurement itself, as in the one-molecule engine) and Brillouin's view (the cost is physical, roughly the photons you'd need to see the molecule). If information is free, the next forks should cover: (a) whether the demon's own sorting mechanism — trapdoor, ratchet, shutter — is itself subject to thermal noise, which gives you the Smoluchowski–Feynman fluctuation line; (b) if the mechanism is noiseless, whether the demon's memory is finite and must eventually be reset, which gives you Landauer–Bennett; and (c) if memory is unbounded, whether the second law even needs to be saved or is just a statistical regularity that small fluctuations don't threaten, which gives you the Earman–Norton style "the demon is a non-problem" position. Please also include, as a final leaf, the lonely combination where information is free, the mechanism is noiseless, memory is infinite, and the second law is supposed to hold strictly — that leaf should be labeled as an actual violation of the second law, and you should say outright that almost nobody defends it because it just is the position that the second law fails.

Give me exactly six numbered leaves, no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences: a short label for the position, then one sentence saying what it commits to, with the path of question-answers that leads to it in parentheses. Flag right inside the relevant items any two leaves your tree can't cleanly separate.
