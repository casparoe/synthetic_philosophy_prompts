I've been noticing a weird move in arguments and negotiations, and I want to know if it has a name — and if not, I want you to coin one and defend it. Im not looking for a literature review, and I don't want a menu of possible terms. Take a position.

The pattern is this: two parties disagree, and instead of just arguing the merits, each side tries to be the first to make its own position impossible to walk back. The point of locking yourself in is to force the other party to be the one who caves. The best example I have is from work. Two lead engineers were fighting about whether to keep patching our old payments service or rebuild it. One of them announced a deprecation date to customers, which meant he couldn't back down without publicly eating crow. The other merged a big chunk of the new service behind a flag and let half the org start depending on it. Neither of them wanted the company to end up running two overlapping payment systems — each was trying to make the other guy be the flexible one. They both pulled it off, and now we have two overlapping systems and nobody will delete either one.

I've seen a version of the same shape between companies, where two vendors publicly committed to incompatible "standards" at the same time, each clearly expecting the other to blink. It looks like chicken, but the key move is to remove your own steering wheel first, before the other side removes theirs. Its not really sunk-cost escalation either, because the lock-in is deliberate and upfront, not something you slide into after investing.

So the thing I'm after is specifically this: a race to become the party that cannot yield, used as a bargaining tactic, where if both sides finish the race the outcome is worse for everyone. Is there an established concept for exactly that? If you think it's just a special case of some broader idea, say which one and mark the boundaries — what separates this from ordinary brinksmanship, a standoff, escalation of commitment, and a plain game of chicken. If nothing fits, give the thing a name and a definition, then give me one borderline case that sits just outside it. The carving matters more than the label. Keep it under 400 words.
