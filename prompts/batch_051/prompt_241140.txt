I'm starting a reading group with two people who've been arguing past each other for six months about whether history gets locked in by accidents. One is a path-dependence person: small contingent events matter, increasing returns and network effects lock things in, and rerunning the tape would give you genuinely different outcomes. The other is a structuralist: the broad outcome was overdetermined by deep incentives and constraints, so you'd get something in the same class anyway even if the names and dates differ. They've finally agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration statement instead of just arguing. Write it for them.

Anchor the statement in the usual QWERTY-keyboard example (don't quote anyone, just use the case) and in at least one case that pulls the other way, like the near-simultaneous discovery of natural selection or calculus.

Lead with the bottom line: the two or three substantive claims both sides will sign without hedging, then a numbered agreed-claims section, then a table of the genuine disagreements phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view, then the cruxes. For each crux, give what each side predicts in advance and one test, case, or argument both would accept as bearing on it. Separate the verbal disagreements (especially what counts as 'the same outcome' and what 'locked in' means) from substantive ones, and don't pretend they agree where they dont. Cap the whole thing at 700 words.
