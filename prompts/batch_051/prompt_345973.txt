Amateur gardener here, and I keep noticing a pattern in how people (myself included) draw the line around killing, and I don't have a word for it—or rather, I don't know if its got a proper one. I would never put rat poison in the shed without a lot of agonising, and if a neighbour told me they'd poured boiling water on a mouse nest I'd think they were cruel. But I regularly spray a systemic insecticide on my roses, killing every aphid, and nobody so much as blinks. My father-in-law once spent a whole afternoon relocating a hedgehog but happily uses an electric bug zapper that incinerates moths by the hundred. When I ask people why the insects don't count, the reasons are odd: they 'don't really suffer', they're 'too simple', there's 'too many of them', 'it's what farmers do'. But some of the same people will swat a wasp for stinging them, which only makes sense if you think the wasp can be hurt, or at least punished. And if I ask whether one aphid on a leaf matters, people say no; a million aphids is just a million nothings, which can't be right as arithmetic, but it feels right to us.

I'm not after the question of whether killing insects is actually wrong. I'm after the specific name, if there is one, for the pattern itself: we withdraw moral weight from a living thing not because we have good evidence about its capacities, but because it is small, numerous, unfamiliar, and unable to protest. It doesn't feel like ordinary selfishness, and it doesn't feel like a simple 'humans first' bias either, because the same person will carefully rescue a butterfly for a child and crush a caterpillar between two stones later the same afternoon. If this has an established name I'd like to know it, and if the nearest named concept only half covers it I'd want to know exactly where the described thing comes apart from that concept. If there is no name, what would you call it, and where do the edges fall—do plankton count, or the mites living on our faces, or a wasp that can sting back? Please keep it under 400 words; I want the carving, not a survey.
