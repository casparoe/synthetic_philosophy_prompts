Two friends of mine got into it at a dinner thing last week over Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, and I ended up stuck refereeing, so I'm outsourcing it. (1) Marcus said the book actually solves the problem of Abraham: what looks like attempted murder is a teleological suspension of the ethical — the universal ban on killing isn't discarded, it's temporarily suspended for a higher end, the absolute duty to God, and that's a genuinely different category from ordinary moral failure. (2) Priya said that's just a fancy label laundering private revelation: once a higher end can suspend the ethical, there's no public test distinguishing Abraham from a deluded man who hears voices telling him to kill his son, so the category is empty. (3) Marcus came back that the lack of a public test is exactly the point — the knight of faith is indistinguishable from the outside, can't explain himself in the language of the universal, and so has no justification to hand anyone; Kierkegaard isn't writing Abraham a permission slip, he's marking how terrifyingly opaque faith is. (4) Priya replied that if you can't tell it from murder and can't justify it to anyone, then saying it's suspended rather than broken is doing hidden normative work: either Abraham is a murderer by the only standards we have, or right before God is just the move every fanatic makes. (5) Marcus said a fanatic can also say they love their child while obeying a higher call, but Abraham still forces the question whether there could be a duty that isn't expressible in the universal, and ruling that question out from inside the ethical just begs it. That's where they stalled.
