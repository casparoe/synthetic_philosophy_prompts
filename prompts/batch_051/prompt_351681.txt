I'm in a graduate seminar on population ethics, and for my project I want to reconstruct the document that an advocate and an opponent of the procreation asymmetry would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The advocate holds that, in the comparison between creating a person and refraining, the absence of a life full of suffering is good in a way that the absence of a flourishing life is not bad; the opponent denies that contrast and takes the value of possible lives to behave symmetrically. Draft the statement both could sign. It should include a numbered list of claims they agree on, a disagreements table stating the residual difference in wording each side would accept as fair to itself, and an identification of the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether the fact that no one is deprived by nonexistence can carry the moral weight the advocate assigns to it, and another is how the asymmetry behaves in choices between sets of possible lives of different sizes. For each crux, give a concrete case both sides would accept as relevant, and record each side's prediction in advance.
