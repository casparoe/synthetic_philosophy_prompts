You know the three-part test people use for whether a biblical command binds all Christians or just the original audience? A command is transcultural if (1) it's grounded in creation or God's nature, (2) it shows up in both testaments, and (3) it isn't tied to a specific local custom. Ok, now take Paul telling the women in Corinth to cover there heads when they pray or prophesy. He argues from the order of creation and throws in a cryptic remark about the angels, so he plainly doesn't think it's just culture; but a few verses later he seems to wave the whole thing off by saying none of the churches have such a custom, and most Christians today file it under culture anyway. Go through the test criterion by criterion and say whether the head covering command comes out transcultural. Then tell me whether the case shows that one of the three criteria is doing all the real work, or that the test is basically just rationalizing the answer people already wanted.
