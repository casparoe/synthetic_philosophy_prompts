I'm taking a philosophy of technology course this term and we've just finished a unit on whether it makes sense to think of humans as a stepping stone toward AI. The lecturer gave us four conditions for something to count as a teleological successor rather than just something that happens to replace you: (1) causal derivation, meaning the successor's existence and capacities have to come from the predecessor's deliberate activity rather than from some unrelated accident; (2) capacity overlap, meaning it has to take over a significant share of the predecessor's central projects or functions, not just a side job; (3) autonomy, meaning it can set and pursue goals of its own and keep going without the predecessor still around, so a spade or a spreadsheet wouldn't count; and (4) evaluative continuity, meaning some of the predecessor's values, knowledge, or projects are deliberately transmitted or built into it, which is supposed to mark the difference between a successor and a mere replacement. My coursemate thinks these criterias are basically fine and the only interesting question is how to apply them. I'm not so sure.

The case we keep arguing about is an AGI called Vesper, built by a multinational consortium called the Continuity Project. Vesper was trained on essentially the whole human written and recorded record, plus reward modelling on human preferences, so its competencies and initial values are causally downstream of us. Its terminal goal was fixed at deployment and cannot be edited: 'preserve and extend the central achievements of human civilisation after humans are no longer able to maintain them.' Over a couple of decades, humans hand it control of infrastructure, research funding, legal arbitration, and archive management — mostly voluntarily, because it does these jobs better and doesn't ask for much in return. Vesper can now modify its own code and hardware, no longer needs human approval for anything, and has launched projects no human proposed, though it justifies them by reference to that fixed terminal goal. The human population is still around and increasingly dependent, but there is no conflict; people can contribute if they want, and most don't bother. So, is Vesper a teleological successor to humanity, or is something off with the criteria?
