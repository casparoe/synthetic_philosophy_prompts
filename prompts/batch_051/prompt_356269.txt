One of the things that draws me to Hume's argument in Section X of his Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding is that it gives a single, uniform procedure for all testimony: compare the probability that the witness is mistaken or lying with the probability that the reported event occurred, and believe whichever is less surprising. The passage in Part I where he lays down the maxim about testimony—that no report can establish a miracle unless the falsehood of the report would itself be more contrary to experience than the event reported—states that procedure in its strongest form. But I cannot shake the intuition that if multiple independent, competent, sceptical witnesses reported a resurrection, with no plausible motive for fraud and with physical corroboration, then rationality would eventually have to allow that the event occurred, even if it violates an otherwise uniform regularity. I don't want a verdict; I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be debunked and dropped, what it would take for the maxim to be amended, and whether there is a reading of Hume on which both survive.
