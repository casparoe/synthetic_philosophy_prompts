I am writing a high-school research paper on how a culture treats its most successful people, and I want to use the public treatment of Lance Armstrong as my case study. On one side is the American Dream framework: a person who overcomes obstacles through talent and effort, succeeds enormously, and is then celebrated as proof that anyone can make it. On the other side are Jantelagen, the Nordic norm that one should not think one is anyone special, and the tall poppy syndrome, the Australian and New Zealand idea that people who stand out get cut down out of resentment or envy. Armstrong survived testicular cancer, won the Tour de France seven consecutive times, became a widely admired figure and built the Livestrong brand, and was later stripped of his titles for systematic doping and then admitted it. I do not need a retelling of the story. I need you to analyze this rise and fall through those frameworks: which features of the public reaction each explains well, which it misdescribes, and where it goes silent. Then take a definite, reasoned position on whether Armstrong's downfall is better understood as a tall-poppy cutting-down or as a response to genuine wrongdoing, and say what the episode shows about whether the American Dream and the tall poppy syndrome are really opposites or two ways of managing the same anxiety about success. Please commit to one interpretation and argue for it rather than only presenting the options.
