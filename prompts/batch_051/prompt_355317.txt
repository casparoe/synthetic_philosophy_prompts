I am an undergraduate in a seminar titled Authority and Worship in Early Modern Christianity, and this week's reading covered the regulative principle of worship: roughly, the position that in public worship whatever Scripture does not command is forbidden, in contrast to the normative principle that whatever Scripture does not forbid is permitted. We also discussed the standard qualification that mere circumstances of worship, such as time, place, seating, and building maintenance, may be arranged by the congregation, whereas the elements of worship require positive biblical warrant. I want to write a short satirical piece for a class portfolio, and I would like it to be a parody of church governance rather than a direct argument.

Please draft the official minutes of a single meeting of the worship committee of an invented congregation that has just adopted the strict regulative principle for the first time. The piece should run about 700–900 words and be formatted as numbered agenda items in the deadpan voice of a church clerk, with motions, seconds, and votes; the irony should be sustained and never announced.

The humor should come from the actual casuistical pressure points of the position rather than caricature: the element/circumstance distinction, the line between public and private worship, the status of instrumental music and of hymns that are not metrical psalms (sung texts drawn from elsewhere in Scripture or composed afresh), and the way a committee can elevate its own aesthetic preferences by reclassifying them as commanded while delegating anything inconvenient to the category of circumstance. Include at least one motion that is theologically absurd but follows plausibly from the committee's stated principles, and at least one item that is tabled indefinitely.

The target should be the style of hair-splitting that the principle can generate, not the principle itself or its sincere adherents: a sympathetic defender of the regulative principle should recognize the logic and find it fair, while a reader who has never encountered the debate should still find the minutes funny.
