At a policy workshop, the discussion was whether to require third-party safety audits before any lab can deploy a model above a specified compute threshold. One participant dismissed the design question with: "This is all beside the point — we should pause frontier AI." I want to know what that slogan actually says. Distinguish the precise claims it could be making along each of these dimensions: the object of the pause (new training runs, deployment, commercial access, or the open release of weights), its subjects (all developers, only the leading labs, or states), its duration and exit conditions (a permanent halt, an indefinite moratorium, or a pause until specified safety bars are met), and its justification (precaution, cost-benefit analysis, or something else). For each major reading, say whether it is platitudinous, substantive and defensible, or implausible; then say which reading the participant needed for the slogan to actually settle or displace the audit question, and whether that reading does settle it.
