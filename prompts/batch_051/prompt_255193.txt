I'm drawn to a structural account of wanting: a system wants an outcome just in case its behavior is organized around bringing that outcome about — the outcome figures in its objective function, preference ordering, or planning in the right functional way — and no further ingredient is needed. I like the account because it is clean, applies uniformly to humans, animals, and machines, and doesn't require me to posit a private something I can never observe in another system. But I can't shake the intuition that this isn't enough. A chess engine that searches for winning moves doesn't want to win in any sense I care about; a system flawlessly maximizing a single arbitrary quantity (the proverbial paperclip maximizer) still looks to me like it wants nothing, because nothing is at stake for it. Wanting, I want to say, requires caring, and caring requires something like there being something it is like for the system. The structural account makes that felt difference either an illusion or a causally idle add-on, and it seems to flatten the difference between my wanting coffee and an engine's pursuit of a win — which I find harder to accept than the engine case alone. I don't want to be told which side wins. Give me the strongest version of each side: what would justify deciding that the intuition is an artifact of how I'm imagining the cases and should be dropped, what would justify amending or abandoning the structural account, and whether there is a reading of 'wants' on which both survive. Which move should I make, and what does it cost?
