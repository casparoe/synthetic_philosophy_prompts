I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to write a character who bullshits constantly — not a liar exactly, but someone who just... talks. Everyone I've mentioned Frankfurt to says the key distinction is that the liar cares about the truth enough to hide it, while the bullshitter is indifferent to whether what he says is true. Fine. But then I started sorting through concrete cases, and my gut verdicts keep pulling against the neat trichotomy, so I want to know if there's actually a principle underneath them or wether I should just drop some.

Here are my verdicts:

1. A witness on the stand testifies that the defendant was with her all night, knowing he was actually with someone else and wanting the jury to believe her. That's plainly lying.
2. A politician asked whether his new policy has raised wages says "wages are up twelve percent under my watch" because the number sounds strong; he has no idea what the actual number is and would be fine either way. That's bullshit, not lying.
3. My father, in early dementia, once confidently told us he'd mailed the property tax check that morning when he hadn't. That's neither bullshit nor lying — it's just error, even though he said it with total confidence.
4. A fellow writer at a party holds forth about a famous "study in Norway" showing people who read literary fiction are better at reading faces; the study doesn't exist, and he made it up to keep the table entertained, not caring whether anything like it is true. That's bullshit.
5. A used car salesman tells a customer the car was "owned by a little old lady who only drove it to church on Sundays." It might actually be true; he doesn't know and sees no reason to find out, because it sells the car. Also bullshit.
6. An AI assistant, asked for work on a topic, confidently cites a 1983 paper by "D. Ellman" with a full abstract, except no such paper exists. My first instinct is that this is bullshit — same indifference to truth, just automated. But the machine has no intentions at all, so maybe it should be called something else entirely, or just error.

Is there one principle that captures all of these, or nearly all of them? Wich of my verdicts would it force me to give up? And where does that leave me on LLM hallucination — lying, bullshit, error, or a fourth thing? Also, if bullshit doesn't require a mind with intentions, what exactly distinguishes the chatbot from my father's error?
