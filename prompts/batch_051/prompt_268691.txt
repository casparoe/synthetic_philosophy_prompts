I've been teaching a seminar called Language and Representation, and every year I get into the same argument with the students: whether those vector embeddings -- the things where king minus man plus woman lands near queen -- are actually doing semantics or just shuffling co-occurence statistics. So this year I tried to settle it by putting a list of criteria on the board. Here they are, about as I wrote them.

For a vector space over a vocabulary to count as a semantic representation of the language, I said it has to:

1. Put expressions with similar meanings close together and expressions with clearly different meanings far apart.
2. Do more than compress lexical similarity: it should separate relations that raw proximity conflates, especially synonymy, antonymy, and mere relatedness. "Good" and "bad" should not end up looking interchangeable.
3. Compose. Give me vectors for the parts and a combination rule, and the vector of a phrase should track the meaning of the parts and their structure, not just the bag of words.
4. Respect entailment at the sentence level. If one sentence entails another, the representation should mark that in some recoverable way, rather than making both of them look like equally good continuations.
5. Be in some minimal sense about the world. It should distinguish a sentence's being true under a model from its just being a fluent string.

Now take the embedding space of a workhorse bidirectional language model, one of the standard ones fine-tuned on a large entailment corpus. Its word vectors reproduce human similarity ratings and do well on analogy tasks; its sentence vectors are good on paraphrase and decent on entailment probes; but antonyms are notoriously close neighbors, and nobody has managed to read anything like truth conditions off the geometry. Question: does this embedding space count as a semantic representation of the language under my five criteria? I want a criterion-by-criterion call. Then tell me whether the real problem is that the model isn't semantic, or that one or two of my criteria were rigged from the start.
