I work on camera software, and I keep trying to reverse-engineer my friend's gut calls about when you genuinely see something through an image, because her track record is wierdly consistent. Mirror: yes, you literally see yourself. Jupiter through a telescope: yes. A faded printed photograph of her grandmother: yes — she insists she's seeing her grandmother, not just a picture of her. A live camera feed of the parking lot on her phone: yes. An oil portrait of her grandmother: no. A photorealistic digital painting made from a photo of her grandmother: no. So now I'm trying to predict her on three cases I actually care about. An X-ray of her wrist — does she see her own bones, or only an image of them? A phone's night-mode photo, where the software merges several short exposures into one final image — still seeing through it? And a 3D model reconstructed from a few hundred ordinary photos, where you can swing the view around to angles no camera actually occupied — transparent or not? I'm less interested in her exact yeses and nos than in the rule she seems to be running, whether some other rule fits her track record just as well, and which of those three cases would best seperate them.
