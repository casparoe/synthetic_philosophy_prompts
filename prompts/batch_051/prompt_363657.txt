The sleep-lab people I work with keep talking past each other on a deceptively simple question: is a dream an experience—a temporally extended stream of conscious episodes actually unfolding during sleep—or is it a story the waking brain confabulates at, or just after, the moment of recall? The skeptical position, in the spirit of Malcolm though I would not pin it to any particular text, says a dream report is the only datum there is, and a report can never be checked against a private occurrence, so there is no fact of the matter about whether the dream took place during sleep; asking someone whether they dreamed may itself elicit the narrative. A stronger position, in the spirit of Dennett's cassette metaphor, grants that sleep involves memory formation but denies that anything was played through in experience: the whole narrative could be loaded into memory at the instant of waking, complete with apparent duration and detail, producing the illusion that a temporally extended experience occurred when it did not.

I want a test—the best one that can actually be run—of the claim specifically that a dream is an experience occurring in time during sleep, rather than a waking reconstruction that never existed as a stream of experience. Design it. You may use only realistic apparatus: polysomnography, electrooculography, stimuli delivered during confirmed REM sleep, and trained lucid dreamers who can prearrange to perform a distinctive sequence of eye movements, or otherwise signal, from inside the dream. State exactly what pattern of results would favor the experience reading and what pattern would favor the cassette/confabulation reading. Then give the skeptic's strongest replies at each step and say whether the design rules them out. Finally, describe the residue: even a fully positive result leaves what untouched? Is that residual gap a limitation of the experiment, or a sign that the two views are not fully separated by any observation—and if so, what exactly does "experience" do in the claim that no operationalization of it can capture? The part I care about most is your account of where the test stops settling anything, and why.
