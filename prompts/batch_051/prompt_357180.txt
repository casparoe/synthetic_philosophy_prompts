I serve on the language-access and patient-safety committee of a hospital system that treats patients in eight languages. The administration has proposed replacing most human medical interpreters with a state-of-the-art neural machine translation system, beginning with discharge instructions and consent for routine procedures. The vendor's evaluation reports near-human fluency and strong scores on back-translation fidelity tests. One faction on the committee is ready to approve a pilot; a second argues that these metrics cannot detect fluent, confident, and substantively incorrect translations, and that the system lacks the kind of understanding even a mediocre human interpreter brings; a third would approve only if every translated output is reviewed by a qualified human interpreter on site. Draft the committee's decision memo: recommend one of these courses of action, or a variant you specify, give reasons the hospital administration could act on, and address the strongest consideration on the losing side.
