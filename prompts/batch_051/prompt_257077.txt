I have been reading Hume's discussion of causation in his Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, and I keep running up against what looks like a contradiction. Hume argues that when we observe any particular case of cause and effect, we never actually observe a necessary connection between the two events; we observe only that one regularly follows the other. He then says that repeated experience produces a habit or custom in the mind, and that our idea of necessary connection arises from the feeling of that customary transition, not from anything we perceive in the objects themselves. This sounds as though necessity is something the mind contributes rather than something in the world. Yet Hume also constantly speaks of the hidden powers and ultimate springs of nature, which he says lie beyond the reach of the senses and of natural philosophy. I do not see how to fit these two things together. If the idea of necessary connection is produced entirely by custom, and if we never discover any power in objects, does it not follow that, for Hume, there are no real powers or necessary connections in nature, and that talk of hidden powers would be empty by his own lights? And if so, why would he keep appealing to those hidden powers as if they were real?
