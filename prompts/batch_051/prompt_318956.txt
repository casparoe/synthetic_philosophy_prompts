I keep noticing a pattern in cases about trust, promises, and self-control, and I can't tell whether it has a settled name or whether it's just a tangle of neighbouring things.

Case one. You're stuck somewhere remote, and a stranger with a truck offers you a ride, but only if you promise to pay him later. He's a good judge of character, so he'll only accept a sincere promise. You know, and he knows, that once you're home you won't have any reason to pay: he can't find you, there's no enforcement, and the money will be more usefull to you than to him. So you can't sincerely promise. But you also see that if you were the sort of person who pays no matter what, he'd take you along. You'd be better off being that sort of person. Could you rationally take a drug that would make you that sort of person, knowing that later, when you pay, you'll be doing something that, all things considered, you'd rather not do at that moment? Is there a conflict between the rationality of taking the drug and the irrationality of the later act?

Case two. A friend has a smaller version. She's calm by nature, and people around her know it, so they dump extra work on her, knowing she'll absorb it. She says she'd be better off if she could make herself genuinely quick to anger about being taken advantage of. The anger would be unpleasant, and it would sometimes make her act against her own interests, like snapping at a client. But the standing disposition would make the exploiters back off. She calls it "buying a temper."

These don't feel like ordinary cases of binding yourself. Ulysses ties himself to the mast to stop his future self from doing something the future self will want to do. Here, the person is trying to install a trait that will cause the future self to do something the future self will *not* want to do, and it's only worth installing because other people can see the trait in advance. That seems like making yourself less rational on purpose, and it looks both smart and insane.

Does this configuration have a name of its own? If it's a recognised thing, say what exactly it is and how it differs from plain commitment devices, precommitment, and simply becoming a different person with different values. If there's no settled name, say so and coin one, with a real definition and edge cases: does it matter who observes the trait, and does it matter whether the later act is truly against your interests, or just against your earlier preferences? I'd rather you take a definite position than give me a list of candidates; the carving is what I'm after, not just a label.
