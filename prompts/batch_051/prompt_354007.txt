Question. In his methodological writings on economics, Milton Friedman defended a position sometimes called the F-twist: the claim that unrealistic assumptions in a scientific model are not, by themselves, a defect of the model. Explain Friedman's reasons for this position. Then present the strongest version of the objection that a model with unrealistic assumptions fails to represent the real causal structure of the phenomena it models, and assess whether Friedman's position can answer that objection. Your assessment should distinguish clearly between evaluating a model's assumptions and evaluating its predictions.

Student answer. Friedman's view is that the realism of a model's assumptions is irrelevant to its scientific value, because what a model must do is yield accurate predictions about the class of phenomena it is intended to explain. A good model covers many cases with few postulates, which means leaving out most of the details in which real cases differ; so the more significant a model is, the more unrealistic its assumptions will tend to be. This is not a criticism, because false assumptions can still function as a reliable predictive instrument. Friedman's illustration is that we can predict how an expert billiards player will strike the ball by treating the player as if she were solving the relevant physical equations, even though no such calculation occurs. The strongest objection is that a model with unrealistic assumptions can fit existing data merely by accident or because it was fitted to that data; such a model may then fail on new data, and even when it predicts, it may be only a black box that identifies no genuine causal mechanism. Friedman can answer this objection decisively. Since the model's predictions are derived from its assumptions, a model that predicts correctly and robustly across new phenomena must have assumptions that are at least approximately true in the respects relevant to those phenomena. Otherwise its predictive success would be an unexplained miracle. Therefore, once a model has earned its predictive record, no further objection from unrealistic assumptions remains, and the objection fails.
