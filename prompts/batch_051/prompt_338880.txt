I am a condensed matter physicist, and I have recently been forced to think seriously about reduction and emergence because I am co-writing a review article on emergent phenomena in strongly correlated systems, and my co-author, a philosopher of physics, keeps using distinctions I cannot locate in my own mathematical practice. I am fluent in renormalization-group and effective-field-theory language: Wilsonian coarse-graining, fixed points and flows in theory space, relevant, irrelevant, and marginal operators, universality classes, decoupling of high-energy degrees of freedom, quasiparticles, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and emergent gauge structure. I want a systematic translation, not an explainer, and not an analogy.

Restate the main positions in the philosophical debate over reduction and emergence in science in that vocabulary. Cover at least these four positions or arguments:

1. The classical intertheoretic-reduction idea, on which a higher-level theory, law, or kind is reduced to a lower-level one when it can be derived from the lower-level theory together with bridge principles linking the two vocabularies.
2. The multiple-realizability objection, with its conclusion that higher-level kinds cannot be identified with lower-level kinds and therefore retain some autonomy.
3. The distinction between weak and strong emergence: roughly, weak emergence says higher-level behavior is surprising and best captured in higher-level terms but is in principle derivative from the lower-level dynamics, while strong emergence says the higher level has its own causal powers and is not derivable even in principle.
4. The causal-exclusion worry, on which the lower-level causes of an effect threaten to make any higher-level cause of that same effect redundant, so the genuineness of special-science or higher-level causation is called into question.

For each one, tell me what translates cleanly into the renormalization-group and effective-field-theory picture, what must be distorted or silently amended in order to fit, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Be explicit about the distortions: do not translate each position into its nearest physics result and then declare the question settled or dissolved. Then give me the residue report: take everything that failed to translate and say, for each item, whether it is most plausibly (a) a limitation of my physics vocabulary, (b) a sign that the philosophical claim was less clear or less consequential than its formulation suggests, or (c) a genuine point of disagreement that the physics vocabulary can state but cannot by itself settle.

The reason I want this so precise is partly practical. I model low-energy physics that looks like a gauge theory even though the microscopic Hamiltonian contains no such fields. When I say that this structure emerges, I used to mean only that it appears after coarse-graining and is not visible at the level of the bare fields. I now suspect philosophers mean something stronger by emergence, and I want to know whether the gap between what I mean and what they mean is merely terminological or marks a real disagreement I should have a view about. Please write the answer in American English.
