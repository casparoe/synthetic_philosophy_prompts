My colleague Miriam is famous in the office for being completely unbuyable. She won't accept so much as a coffee from a supplier, she declares every tin of biscuits that turns up at christmas, and she once let a rep walk back to the station in the pouring rain rather than accept a lift, because she said it might count as a gift. Fair enough, maybe. But last month she turned down the use of a client's guest flat for a weekend, even though the client said it was just because the local hotels are grim and the flat would sit empty anyway. It would of almost certainly closed a deal we'd been chasing for a year; instead the client now thinks we're cold and the deal's dead. Everyone says this is just what being principled looks like, and that someone who can't be bought is always admirable. I can't shake the feeling that something's gone wrong, but I can't say what. Is being unbuyable always a good thing? Can it tip over into a flaw — and if so, what's the flaw and where's the line?
