Suppose that beginning in 2026, a wealthy Anglophone country of roughly fifty million people establishes a national Competitive Reasoning League. Two-person teams play twenty-minute matches before a three-judge panel. Scoring follows a fixed rubric: points for valid deductive or probabilistic inference; a mandatory steelman of the opponent’s strongest argument before any reply; irrevocable concessions when a premise cannot be rebutted; penalties for audience-directed rhetoric; and bonus points for deriving a falsifiable consequence of the opposing view and showing that it fails. Matches are televised, and a team’s league ranking feeds into university admissions, academic hiring, and grant review. Predict, with the mechanisms, incentives, and constraints behind each step made explicit, how the country’s broader argumentative culture changes over thirty years: which norms migrate from the league into ordinary discussion, which stay contained inside the game, and where the design most plausibly backfires.

Write in American English. Give exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should state one major predicted effect and the mechanism producing it, and should say whether you regard that prediction as robust or as a guess. In the fifth item, state the consideration about competitive institutions—or about how people actually argue—that would most likely change your overall verdict on whether the league improves or degrades public reasoning.
