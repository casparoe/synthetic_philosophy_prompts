I direct a small policy-research group, and I have always tried to be the kind of supervisor who gives frequent, specific, and warmly expressed praise. My assumption was that this keeps junior researchers motivated and gives them a usable sense of when their work is on track. A friend of mine who works on AI alignment recently pressed me on this in a way I have not been able to shake. He argued that once your approval becomes conspicuous enough to predict, people begin to treat it as the target rather than as evidence of being on course: an analyst will select framings, analyses, and even conclusions in part because they are the sort of thing that has reliably produced my approval, rather than because she has independent reason to think they track the underlying policy question. He recommended that I decouple warmth from evaluation, praise the work rather than the person, and reserve evaluative praise for those features of the process that are genuinely diagnostic, while staying noticeably neutral about outputs whose quality I cannot yet verify.

The immediate difficulty is that my team's current project is exactly the kind of work in which the actual goal is not checkable for years: we are building the analytical foundation for a multi-year policy evaluation, and whether our recommendations are any good will only become clear long after the junior people on the team have internalized whatever they internalize from me now. They are still learning to distinguish an analysis that will hold up from one that will merely get through review. If I follow my friend's advice too strictly, I worry I will become withholding and signal-poor precisely when they need calibration; if I ignore it, I worry I will be quietly training them toward approval-shaped rather than truth-shaped work in an environment where the truth will not arrive in time to correct them. I would like concrete advice rather than a resolution through abstraction: is this a real dilemma or a false opposition? What rule for praise and criticism would you actually adopt in my position, and what would it still permit you to say to someone whose draft you genuinely admire?
