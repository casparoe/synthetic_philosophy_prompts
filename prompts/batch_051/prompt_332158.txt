They part ways over what the second factor produces. The doxasticist holds that it produces a genuine belief, a full but badly evaluated belief, because belief-evaluation goes wrong; the anti-doxasticist holds that the result is better modeled as a non-belief, an imagining, an avowal, or a state the patient reports without endorsing, citing the way patients often fail to act on the delusion or otherwise keep it compartmentalized. The agreed claims should cover what both accept about the clinical phenomena, the first factor, and the explanatory job the second factor must do.
