I've been turning over the old idea that the good life is one of being emotionally undisturbed—equanimity, apatheia, whatever you want to call it—and I've got a pile of gut verdicts on cases that I can't get to cohere. Here they are.

Getting worked up about a news story I can't affect: I think that's mostly pointless, and I'd genuinely rather not feel it; the calm of ignoring it feels like a gain. Grieving my partner's death slowly and deeply: I don't think that's a disturbance to be cured, and I'd think less of someone who could just switch it off. Anger when a friend is treated unfairly at work: the anger feels right even though it wrecks my sleep, and a version of me who felt nothing would be worse. Anxiety in the week before my kid's operation: I'd nearly say the anxiety is part of loving him, and it's absence would quietly disturb me more. Taking a beta blocker before a talk to kill the nerves: completely fine, no loss at all. A hypothetical pill that would make me placid and content no matter what happens: I wouldn't take it, even though it promises the most undisturbed life there could be. Meditation that quietens my rumination and lets me drop grudges: good, and I do it. And then there's the monk so imperturbable that he doesn't grieve at his own child's funeral: I find that monstrous, not admirable.

Is there one principle about the value of being emotionally undisturbed that gets all of these, or close? If not, which of my verdicts does your best principle overturn, and why is that the one to drop? Also: what would the principle say about the case I keep wobbling on—grief that has long since turned into years of depression? Is continuing to feel it still a form of love, or has it become exactly the kind of disturbance a good life is better off without? Please be explicit about whether your principle is ultimately about fittingness, authenticity, love, or something else altogether. And write the answer in British English, say how confident you are in the principle, and single out the point you're least sure of.
