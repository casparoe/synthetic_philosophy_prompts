I'm an engineer at a startup building a shared-space AI assistant — it handles little daily conflicts in apartments, offices, and co-living houses: the common-room TV, the thermostat, the group grocery list, stuff like that. We keep a informal log of how our de facto values person, Priya (a senior colleague, no philosophy background), has ruled when two sets of residents want different things, and now the CEO wants us to turn her rulings into a policy for the model's default behavior so we don't have to bug her on every new case. The log so far: (1) common-room music, four housemates want loud pop, one has a migraine, she said drop the volume to a level the migraine person can tolerate; (2) dinner planner, three want a steakhouse, one is vegan, she said pick somewhere the vegan can actually eat even if the meat-eaters find it mid; (3) office thermostat, six want 68°F, one gets headaches below 74°F, she set it to 74°F; (4) shared garden, four want a flowering plant, one is allergic to it, she vetoed it and went with a species everyone can physically be around. I honestly can't tell whether she's running a rule like never make one person pay for the group's enjoyment, or minimize the single worst outcome, or just do whatever is inside everyone's constraints.

Next week we have a case that breaks the pattern. The model controls the lounge TV in a hostel. Around 40 guests want to watch the World Cup final and are genuinely hyped about it; one guest wants the lounge quiet so they can read, and they find the noise genuinely aversive. There's no second common room. If the model plays the game, the 40 fans get a great evening and the one guest is annoyed but can go to their room; if the model keeps the lounge quiet, the one guest gets what they want and the 40 fans either miss it or huddle around one phone. If we want to stay consistent with Priya's track record, what should we decide, which earlier case is the closest precedent, and what principle would we be signing up for under each option? Also: give me the single future case I should bring to Priya to test whether she's actually an absolutist about never overruling a single person, or whether there's a threshold where a big enough aggregate win flips her.
