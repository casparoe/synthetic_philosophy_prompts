You are a philosopher of physics with a specialty in the foundations of quantum mechanics and decision theory, and also a careful reader of experimental fiction. I would like you to think slowly before answering, and to work through the comparison step by step rather than giving me a quick verdict.

I am a novelist who writes branching narratives: interactive books in which every choice forks into a fully written continuation, so that the finished work contains all the paths. No path is the actual one; they are all equally real as text. And yet I find that I do not treat them equally. One path receives thirty pages and the thematic resolution; the path in which the same character makes the other choice receives three pages and reads like a counterfactual aside. Some choices I expect most readers to take; others are there mainly to be glimpsed. The book's meaning seems to concentrate in certain branches even though every branch is present on the page. There is, in short, a kind of weighting over branches that floats above their equal actuality.

This reminded me of the problem of probability in the many-worlds interpretation. As I understand it, every outcome of a measurement is realized in some branch, all branches are equally real, and yet the Born rule attaches weights to branches through the squared amplitudes, and a rational observer is supposed to act as though those weights were probabilities. I cannot tell whether my authorial weighting is the same kind of thing as the Born weights, whether it is closer to a reader's uncertainty about which path they will follow, or whether the analogy is misleading because a reader chooses a path from outside the work while an Everettian observer is already inside one branch.

Please do three things. First, set out the mapping as exactly as you can: what on the fiction side corresponds to a branch, an observer, a weight, and the Born rule. Second, say what would have to be true on both sides for the parallel to be structural rather than merely suggestive, and identify at least one thing the parallel seems to predict that is false on one side or the other. Third, tell me whether anything transfers: does the fiction side genuinely illuminate the Born rule problem, does the physics side illuminate the craft of branching narrative, or is the comparison mostly an evocative dead end? I care less about the final label than about the carving — where the structures match, where they diverge, and why.
