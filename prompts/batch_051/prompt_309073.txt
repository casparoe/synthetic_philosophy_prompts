I've been reading a bunch of Greek myths this year and something keeps nagging at me. Like with Medusa: in one telling she's a monster from birth, one of three Gorgon sisters, and in another she's a regular beautiful girl who gets transformed by Athena as a punishment. For a long time I just assumed that for any myth there has to be an original, "real" version somewhere, and all the other versions are later mistakes or embellishments. So the job is to figure out which one's oldest or best supported, and that's the actual myth. I like that view because it makes the whole thing feel solvable and gives a kind of procedure. But I also have a really strong gut feeling that a myth just is all its different versions, and asking which Medusa is the real one is like asking which ending of a joke is the real ending. There was probably never a single official version; each telling is the real one for the people telling and hearing it. I keep fliping back and forth. Should I give up the intuition, give up the "original version" theory, or is there a way to keep both? I want the strongest case for each side and then your own negotiated verdict on which should yield. Please write it as continuous prose like an email: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
