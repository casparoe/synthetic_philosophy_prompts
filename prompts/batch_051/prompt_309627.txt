Over the last year I've been trying to think through something pretty local and unglamorous: when is it actually okay to kill insects and other small animals we file under "pests," and when are we just being lazy or squeamish or speciesist. I keep ending up in arguments where someone leans on a single case — head lice, bedbugs, a wasp nest by the back door, aphids on the tomatoes, mosquitoes that vector malaria, pantry moths, termites in the beams, a cluster fly in the bedroom — and treats it as if it settles everything. I don't want to do cases one at a time anymore. I want the spec first. Write me the desiderata: what does an adequate theory of permissible pest control have to get right? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are realy just one camp's preferences (for example, the idea that only disease-vectoring pests count as a serious problem, or the idea that whatever sentience these animals may have is irrelevant to what we may do to them).

A few constraints so it doesn't come out generic. First, treat "pest" as a suspicious category: a honeybee is a protected pollinator in one context and a nuisance when it swarms in a wall, and an awful lot of what counts as a pest is downstream of decisions we already made about land use, monoculture, housing, and imports. I want your desiderata to say whether an adequate theory has to explain how the organism became a pest in the first place, or whether it can take pesthood as given and just regulate our response to it. Second, address the unit question: can a theory just aggregate over individual insects, or does it have to treat colonies, populations, species, and even ecosystems as morally relevant units? Third, say whether an adequate theory must distinguish killing from exclusion and habitat-removal, or whether those are just slower ways of doing the same thing. Then tell me which requirement does the most work in ruling out popular views, and whether any pair of requirements can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want to see. Keep the scorecard of utilitarianism, rights views, virtue ethics, and so on short; I'm after the criteria, not a survey.
