One of the background questions I keep chewing on is whether there's actually a duty to make yourself legible to other people, or whether that's just a rationalist-adjacent norm that never got challenged. Before I can even have that argument I need a working definition of 'legible' — the thing we supposedly owe each other. It should count these as clear cases: wearing a nametag at a meetup; stating, in plain words, exactly what you want from someone with no hedging or hints. It should count these as clear non-cases: being unfailingly polite and predictable while never disclosing what you actually want; keeping a 40-page values document that none of the people you interact with has ever read or could summarize. Then run the borderline ones: someone with flat affect who announces their emotional state in plain sentences ('I'm angry with you right now') but shows no anger; someone whose behavior is highly predictable to people who know them, but whose stated reasons for the behavior are all post-hoc and keep changing; wearing a pronoun pin. Give the definition, run every case through it, and tell me which verdict your least confident about and where the definition might be doing two jobs.
