I've been reading the ontological argument discourse on and off for years—on forums, in the comments of old philosophy blogs, in the occasional rationalist-adjacent thread—and it has the same shape every time. Nobody moves. One side offers Anselm (God is the greatest conceivable being; a being that exists only in the understanding is less great than one that exists in reality too; so God must exist), the other side offers Gaunilo (same logic gives you a perfect island), the first side says islands don't have an intrinsic maximum but God does, the second side says that's ad hoc, then someone brings up Kant's existence-isn't-a-predicate point, someone else says Plantinga's modal version sidesteps that, and then everyone reverts to their prior. I've seen this loop run for nearly a millenium and I still can't tell whether it's a real philosophical dispute or two groups doing different things with the word God.

My current hypothesis is that the real disagreement is about the epistemology of possibility. In the Plantinga-style version the load-bearing premise is something like: it is possible that a maximally great being exists. Theists tend to treat that premise as rationally permissible or obvious; critics treat it as smuggling in the conclusion. So maybe one side is asking "can a reasonable person accept this premise without special evidence?" and the other is asking "does this premise force a neutral person to the conclusion?"—and those are different questions. But I'm not sure. It could also be that people just disagree about whether existence and necessity are genuine perfections, and that disagreement is bedrock intuition rather than something either side can argue for. Or it could be a verbal dispute about the word "greater." I've tried to steelman both sides, and the real crux never seems to get located.

I want a diagnosis, not a survey. Commit to a view: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, an argument over starting intuitions, an argument about what the argument is even for, or something else? Support it with what each side actually says, even the forum versions I just sketched, and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis. If the honest answer is "nothing," commit to that and explain why. Actually pick, don't lay out four live options and leave me to choose.
