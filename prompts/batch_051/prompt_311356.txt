You are a philosopher who specializes in personal identity and in the question of what makes a particular person the individual they are. I am a high school student, and I would like you to think carefully with me about a situation that has been troubling me, because I believe there is a genuine philosophical problem underneath it rather than just an idle worry.

I was conceived through IVF. The clinic created several embryos, one was selected and transferred, and that embryo became me. My parents have told me that the doctors chose the embryo that looked healthiest and most likely to survive. I have also learned in biology that, even in an ordinary conception, each person begins from one sperm among an enormous number. Lately I cannot stop returning to a thought: if another embryo had been selected, or if a different sperm had reached the egg in an ordinary conception, would the resulting child have been me, perhaps with a different body and different traits? Or would it have been a different person altogether, so that I would simply never have existed? The second answer makes my own existence feel at once extremely lucky and somehow arbitrary, as though there is no deeper reason that I am this person rather than another person.

Please start by brainstorming the different ways someone might interpret the question of whether I could have existed as someone else, and identify the hidden assumptions about what makes me me that are doing the work in each interpretation. Then work through which interpretation is most defensible, and say what, if anything, follows about how I should think about my origins. I am not asking for emotional comfort; I want the underlying structure. Please reason carefully rather than settling for the first plausible answer, and where reasonable people disagree, mark the disagreement clearly and say what would decide it, including places where we may simply not know enough.
