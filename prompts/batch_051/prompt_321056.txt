I have recently returned to the practice of petitionary prayer after a long absence, and it has forced me to confront a puzzle I once treated as merely academic. I believe that God is both omniscient and strictly immutable: nothing I do can add information to the divine mind or alter a divine plan that is already complete. Yet I find myself asking God for determinate outcomes—a relative's recovery, a friend's safety, the resolution of a family conflict—rather than limiting myself to a general act of submission. A friend has suggested that I think of this as a case in which an infallible predictor has already fixed the outcome on the basis of what I will do, so that my prayer is at best evidence of the outcome rather than a cause of it. I find that suggestion unsatisfying, but I want a rigorous alternative rather than a retreat to mere edification.

Please argue that petitionary prayer for specific outcomes can be rationally coherent and even genuinely efficacious while holding fixed both divine omniscience and divine immutability. In doing so, clarify the sense in which a prayer whose answer was willed from eternity can nonetheless make a difference to whether the outcome occurs, explain why the predictor analogy is inapt or at best partial, and defend the position against the charge that such prayer is pointless, manipulative, or a disguised attempt to change a being one believes cannot change.
