Last night my friend and I got into a long argument about whether you can mean something without saying it, and once I pushed back he launched into a whole lecture on Grice that was meant to settle it. His summary went roughly like this. Grice's central move is to claim that communication is at bottom the exchange of intentions: when a speaker says something, she means whatever she intends the hearer to believe, and communication succeeds when the hearer recovers that intention. Grice starts by separating natural meaning (smoke means fire) from non-natural meaning — meaning in the sense that someone meant something — and then introduces the famous machinery. Conversation is a cooperative activity, so by default we are all following four maxims: Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner. When a speaker obviously breaks one of these maxims, the hearer assumes she is still being cooperative and works out an implicature. His example was: if you ask 'Did you like the film?' and I answer 'The popcorn was good,' I break the Relation maxim, and you infer as a conversational implicature that I didn't like the film. And an inference only counts as an implicature if the speaker intended it — a hearer cannot read into my words something I didn't mean and call that an implicature. From all this he concluded that Grice's picture makes sentence meaning secondary: the real work in communication is done by speaker meaning, which is basicly a matter of the speaker's private mental states.

I'm suspicious of a couple of the moves — especially the bit about meaning being 'just intentions' and the claim that conversation is cooperative in the way he describes — but I don't know the material well enough to pin down where his summary is fair and where it starts to fudge. Could you give me an accuracy audit? In particular: which parts would a careful Gricean stand behind, where does my friend's version run together distinctions Grice himself kept apart, and do the conclusions he draws (abstract sentence meaning is secondary, meaning is basically private mental states) actually follow from the accurate parts? The more exact you can be about the fudges — for example, which traditional distinctions are collapsed — the better. Could you also write your answer in British English?
