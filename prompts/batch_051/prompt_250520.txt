I got elected to our town council last year on a fix-the-potholes platform, and now I've landed on the finance subcommittee and have to vote on something that feels way over my head. The town is settling a lawsuit from a former water-department employee who says she was forced out after she complained that the department's reports to the state about lead levels were being quietly edited to look better. Our solicitor is recommending we settle for $165,000 with a non-disclosure clause barring both sides from discussing the allegations or the amount; she says going to trial could cost three times that even if we win, and the NDA would protect the town from copycat claims and bad press. The local paper has already put in a public-records request for the settlement terms, and one councilor argues that a government settling a whistleblower case in secret is basically buying the public's silence with it's own money. I can see both sides: we have a fiduciary duty not to waste taxpayer dollars on a trial, but it also feels wrong that the people who drank the water would never find out what their own department was accused of.

Write the decision memo I'd bring to the full council: approve the settlement with the NDA, approve it without the NDA (which will probably cost more), or reject the settlement and go to trial. Give the strongest case for the losing side and an honest bottom line, not a hedge. At the end, add a short section on what evidence or argument would flip your recommendation, and where your reasoning feels shakiest.
