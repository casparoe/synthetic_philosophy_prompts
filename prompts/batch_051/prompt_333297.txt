Take the current U.S.–China competition in advanced AI, together with the narrower race among frontier AI labs, and analyze it through James Fearon's rationalist explanations for war. Fearon's framework begins from the observation that war is ex post inefficient, so rational actors who could strike a bargain should prefer to settle short of fighting; he explains actual war through mechanisms such as private information combined with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibilities. Apply this to the AI race: who are the relevant actors, what would count as the "war," what would a negotiated settlement or arms control agreement amount to, and which of Fearon's mechanisms, if any, best explains why no stable settlement has been reached? Run the analysis in both directions—what the framework illuminates about the AI race, and what the AI race exposes about the framework's limits. I want a committed response: take a definite position on whether the AI race is best treated as a war in Fearon's sense, defend that position, and say explicitly where the framework is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
