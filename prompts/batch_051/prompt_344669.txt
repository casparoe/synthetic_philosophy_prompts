I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am writing a feature on whether beauty, elegance, and simplicity in mathematics and physics should count as evidence that a theory or proof is on the right track. For that piece, I need you to draft the document that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between two camps. Call them the elegance realist and the elegance skeptic. The elegance realist holds that aesthetic judgments—when made by trained practitioners—are fallible but genuinely epistemic signs that a structure or theory tracks something real. The elegance skeptic holds that such judgments are heuristics shaped by training, cognitive biases, sociological fashion, and local convenience, with no independent evidential force.

Use as the shared touchstone Poincaré's chapter on mathematical discovery in *Science and Method*, where he assigns the aesthetic sensibility a filtering role in mathematical invention and denies that this role can be reduced to practical utility. The document should contain: a numbered section of claims both sides can jointly sign about what Poincaré actually asserts, about historical cases in which elegant theories turned out to be empirically or mathematically successful only later, and about cases in which elegance demonstrably misled; a disagreements table in which each side's view is formulated in wording that side would themselves accept; and a list of cruxes on which the dispute genuinely turns—for example, whether the two sides could agree on a formal criterion of beauty whose track record could then be tested, whether derivation from deeper constraints can explain the apparent success of elegance, or whether cases such as Ptolemaic epicycles or the stagnation of string theory are diagnostic. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that case or argument would show. The final document should be constructive and precise, something both parties could sign, with whatever remains of the disagreement made exact rather than rhetorical.
