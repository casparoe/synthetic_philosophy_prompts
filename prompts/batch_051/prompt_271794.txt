In a recent policy seminar, a colleague claimed that inherited wealth is the main channel through which advantage passes across generations, and that since that advantage is unearned, taxing inheritances as income to the recipient is more consistent with equality of opportunity than taxing earned income. I suspect the normative conclusion is doing more work than the causal claim can support. Design the strongest feasible test of the causal core: that large inheritances received early in adult life, rather than the parenting, networks, schooling, and neighborhood effects that accompany them, are what reproduce advantage. Specify the comparison or natural experiment you would examine, the result that would favor the inheritance account and the result that would favor the family-environment account, and be explicit about which part of the fairness claim would remain untouched no matter how the test comes out. Finally, say which result you would expect and how it should alter someone's willingness to tax inheritances as income at the point of receipt.
