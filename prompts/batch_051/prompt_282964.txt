I'm a PhD student and I've been stuck on an argument about middle knowledge that feels like it proves too much, and I cant tell whether that should make me happy or worried. Here's the argument as I've been running it.

(1) If Molinism is true, God has middle knowledge: before creating or decreeing anything, God knows the truth of every counterfactual of creaturely freedom — propositions like 'if S were in circumstances C, S would freely choose A.'
(2) So for any actual creature S and any actual circumstance C, there is already a true proposition about what S would freely choose in C, and it is true before S exists and before S is in C.
(3) If such a proposition is already true, then when S actually lands in C, it isn't open to S to do otherwise; to do otherwise, a proposition that is in fact true would have to be false, and no one can make a true proposition false.
(4) So S cannot do otherwise in C.
(5) But being able to do otherwise is part of what creaturely freedom means, or at least part of what the Molinist is trying to protect.
(6) So S does not freely choose A.
(7) The same applies to every creaturely choice, so middle knowledge entails that no creature ever acts freely.

The conclusion is obviously unacceptable to a Molinist, but I don't know which step to blame.
