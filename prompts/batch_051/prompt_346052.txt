I'm in a population ethics course right now and I've ended up defending a roughly totalist view about population size: a world with more lives that are worth living is better than one with fewer, other things equal, and I don't much care that average welfare goes down. I've made my peace with the Repugnant Conclusion — I'll bite that bullet — and I don't think the non-identity problem lands against me either.

But I've started to worry the view is too easy to insulate. Whenever someone gives me a case I don't like, I just say "yeah but total welfare is higher, so I'll take it." I want you to actually attack it. What would have to be true for my view to be wrong? Give me the defeaters sorted from "would make me abandon it by Friday" to "merely awkward, I can absorb the cost." I don't just want what average utilitarians say; I want an honest map of where totalism is genuinely exposed, including which objections defenders of my view would themselves concede count against it and which they can reasonably wave off.

Personally, the thing I'd find most damaging is the person-affecting intuition: if actions can only be good or bad for actual individuals, then a lot of my view — especially the part where creating happy people is good and extinction is really bad — seems to break. How much of totalism survives if I had to accept a person-affecting restriction? And there's a concrete case I keep coming back to: a world with 100 billion people at +90 versus 200 billion at +70. My stated view says the second is clearly better, but I notice I'd vote against a policy that traded +20 per person for a population doubling, and I cant tell whether that's just scope insensitivity or evidence against the view. Also, if the intuition of neutrality is right — that adding a life barely worth living doesn't make the world better — is that a decisive problem for totalism or just a reason to set a threshold? Be honest about whether the view risks anything at all, and whether its unfalsifiability (if that's what it is) is a problem for a view like this.
