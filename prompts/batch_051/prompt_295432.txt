I am a software engineer with no formal philosophical training. Out of professional habit, I reconstruct arguments as numbered steps before trusting them. I have been reading summaries of the Korean Four–Seven Debate between Yi Hwang (Toegye) and Gi Dae-seung (Gobong), and I tried to formalize Toegye's claim that the Four Beginnings and the Seven Emotions have distinct sources. I would like an exact diagnosis of whether my formalization goes through.

Some setup. The Four Beginnings are commiseration, shame and dislike, modesty and yielding, and the sense of right and wrong; they are the moral feelings Mencius treated as sprouts of virtue, and the tradition takes them to be good whenever they occur. The Seven Emotions are joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, and desire; these are ordinary passions and may be good or bad depending on the case. In the debate, Toegye's mature position was that the Four issue from principle (li) with material force (gi) following, whereas the Seven issue from material force with principle riding on it. Gobong's position was that the Four are just the good instances of the Seven and that both have a single source.

The argument I extracted for the distinct-source claim runs as follows.

(1) If two kinds of emotion have the same source, then they have the same profile of possible moral outcomes.
(2) The Four Beginnings have the profile of being good in every instance.
(3) The Seven Emotions have a wider profile: some of their instances are bad or morally mixed.
(4) Therefore, the Four Beginnings and the Seven Emotions do not have the same source.
(5) The only candidate sources on offer in this scheme are li—principle, which is wholly good—and gi—material force, which varies in clarity and turbidity.
(6) Therefore, the Four Beginnings issue from li, and the Seven issue from gi.

The move from (1) through (4) looks mechanically valid to me, but I suspect (1) is smuggling in something too strong—as if one were assuming that a single function whose outputs depend on its arguments could never return values of two different kinds. My question is: is the argument valid, and if not, at which step does the inference fail? If it is valid, state which premise is false or ambiguous, give the strongest reading of that premise on which it is true, and say whether the argument still works on that reading. Also, which step would Gobong reject, and what replacement for it would he sign? Please aim at the specific step and the exact reading under which it fails rather than the debate as a whole.
