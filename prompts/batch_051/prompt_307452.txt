I'm a retired biologist who got into philosophy late, and Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility has really got under my skin. As I understand it: you act from the way you are, so to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for the way you are; to be responsible for the way you are you'd have to have chosen to become that way, which needs an earlier self doing the choosing with a nature it didn't choose, and so on back without end. Nothing can be the cause of itself, so nobody is ever ultimately responsible for anything — and without ultimate responsibility, the desert-and-blame kind of moral responsibility looks impossible too. I'm fairly convinced the argument is sound. What I want is a map of what would count against it. I don't just mean the standard compatibilist line that it only attacks a godlike notion of responsibility nobody needs; I want the premises that are actually load-bearing, what would have to be true for each of them to fail, which of those escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible, and how much damage each would do (fatal or just awkward). Also tell me honestly wether the argument has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a serious problem for an argument of this kind.
