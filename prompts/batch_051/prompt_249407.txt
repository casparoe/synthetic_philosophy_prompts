I run a small show where a scholar answers in character as a historical thinker, and the next episode is Plato, talking about drunkenness with the Symposium and the Laws as the main touchstones. I want eight to ten interview questions that go past the "your ideal city regulates wine strictly, why?" level and force him to sit with the tensions inside his own treatment of drink.

The questions should include, at minimum: one on the distance between the Symposium, where an evening of careful drinking frames real philosophical work and Socrates sails through it untouched, and the Laws, where drunkenness as such is banned for the young, for soldiers, and for officeholders; one on the claim in the Laws that wine can function as a kind of controlled test, a drug that stirs up the very feelings—shamelessness, overconfidence, pleasure—that self-control has to handle; one on the special permission for older citizens to drink as a sort of medicine against the sourness and stiffnes of age; and one on what the various exclusions tell us about which virtue the institution is actually training, since the official line says moderation but the appeal to testing sounds a lot like courage. Each of these should carry a follow-up keyed to the answer he's likely to give, not a generic "could you say more?"

And two more things. I want a question about Alcibiades' drunken speech in the Symposium, because it's somehow the rawest, most truth-telling speech in the dialogue even though he's the drunk one, and I want the guest pressed on whether Plato actually treats drunkenness as an aid to truth there. Finally, end with a question about Socrates himself: if the point of the drinking is to lose control in a controlled setting, Socrates never does, so is he a poster boy for the Laws' account or a problem for it? Include the follow-up that anticipates the obvious reply—that Socrates is exactly the proof the training works—and that pushes on what the wine is testing in someone who is already immune.
