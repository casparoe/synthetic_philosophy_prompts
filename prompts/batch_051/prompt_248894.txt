I manage the publication-standards team at a health-information website that publishes short, automatically generated summaries of clinical-research abstracts, several thousand of them every day. Human reviewers audit a sample and log every false or unsupported claim they find into one of three categories: 'error', 'bullshit', or 'lie'. The categories drive different remedies. An error usually goes back to the prompt engineers; a claim logged as bullshit means the summary is withdrawn or relabelled as machine-generated rather than verified; and a claim logged as a lie is escalated, because it may expose us to liability for knowingly publishing false medical claims attributed to a source.

The reviewers use the three words interchangeably, and we have no shared criteria. Write me a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Harry Frankfurt's distinction between lying and bullshit, that a reviewer with no philosophical training can follow. Take the distinction, roughly, to be this: the liar deliberately misrepresents what he takes to be true, while the bullshitter is indifferent to whether what he says is true and says whatever suits his purposes. Address the obvious difficulty directly: a language model has no beliefs, purposes, or intentions in the way Frankfurt presumes. Decide what the nearest workable analogues are in this setting, and make a case for whether 'lie' should be applied to the model itself, to the system that produced the output, or only to the people who configured it.

The procedure should include the following. A step-by-step checklist or decision tree that a reviewer can work through in under five minutes using only what is in the audit record: the generated sentence, the source abstract, and the prompt or system instructions, but no model internals. Three worked examples, one of which should be a case where the procedure returns a classification the team will resist. And an explicit statement of where judgement still cannot be eliminated, with those cases marked for escalation to me.

Do not lay out alternative procedures and leave the choice to me: make the cuts, defend them, and indicate how the procedure behaves at the boundaries. Write in British English.
