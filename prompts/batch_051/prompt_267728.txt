Had a huge long argument with a friend last night about whether emotions are part of the good life or just noise. He is basically a stoic: he says grief, fear, even joy are really judgments with some bodily churn attached, and the judgment is the only part that can be true or false or reason-responsive; the felt heat is just a side effect, no more part of the good life than a stomache ache. I'm more on the romantic side: I kept saying the felt quality itself is part of what makes a life go well, and a correct judgment can't substitute for actually feeling something. Thing is, we agreed on the physiology, we agreed about which judgments are involved in his examples, and we still didn't move each other an inch. I don't want a verdict on who's right. I want to know what kind of disagreement we're actually having. It feels like we might be arguing about two things under one heading: what an emotion is (is it the judgment, the feeling, the bundle, the perception?) and what makes life go well (right cognition, or the texture of experience?). Or is the first question just a verbal scuffle and all the real weight sits in the second? Diagnose the structure of this debate for me, using what each side has to be saying, and tell me what would count as progress given the diagnosis—a distinction, a better definition, an experiment I can't imagine yet, or maybe just admitting it's a temperament clash dressed up as philosophy.
