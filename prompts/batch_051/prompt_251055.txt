ok so ive been kicking around a concept i call cognitive escrow and i want a professional epistemologist—ideally someone who works on social epistemology or the epistemology of technology—to pressure-test it. think carefully, maybe brainstorm a little before you answer.

the idea is this: when you outsource a cognitive task to an AI, your understanding doesnt get deleted. it goes into escrow. you still have a kind of title to it—if the AI vanished tomorrow you could recover the skill or knowledge much faster than someone who never had it—but you dont have present-tense access to it. and like real escrow, the account has a maintenance fee nobody reads the fine print about: the escrowed understanding stops getting updated. the world moves, the AI gets retrained, the database schema changes, and the understanding you have a claim on quietly drifts into something that is no longer the understanding you need.

my pet example: a lab switches from hand-rolled stats to an AI pipeline. six months in, the grad student could still reconstruct what the old analysis did, given a weekend and some old scripts. so the knowledge wasnt lost. but meanwhile nobody in the lab can say whether the new pipeline's assumptions still match the experimental design, because the checking itself got escrowed. so what erodes isnt the skill, its the maintenance of the skill.

i know about extended mind stuff and transactive memory and the google effect and deskilling, but i keep feeling they each describe only half of it. the extended mind people say the AI is part of my cognitive system; the deskilling people say ive lost something. i want to say: both, and the missing piece is that what ive lost is the updating process, not the content.

so, honest assessment time. is this a real distinction or just 'use it or lose it' with nicer branding? has someone already worked it out under another name (id be relieved if they had)? and where does it break—what case does the escrow model get wrong, maybe where outsourcing really does delete the thing rather than parking it? also please keep the answer calibrated: separate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this area, and dont present a contested view as the consensus.
