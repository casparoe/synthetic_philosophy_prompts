I keep going back and forth with my brother about this idea he calls the "hinge of history." His claim is roughly that our lifetimes overlap with the most important stretch of time there will ever be, because we're standing right before the point where humanity either figures out how to spread across the universe or wrecks itself permanently, so what people do now matters more than anything anyone does later. I liked the sound of it at first, but now I suspect it might just be the kind of thing every generation tells itself so it feels special. He challenged me to name what would actually settle are argument, and I couldn't come up with anything better than "wait and see," which he says is dodging.

So here's what I want to know: if someone claims we live at the hinge of history -- not just that our time feels important to us, but that it will turn out to be the single most consequential period, the one all later history branches away from -- how would you actually go about testing that? What data or observations could a person gather right now that would count for or against the claim, and how much should each one move a reasonable person? And then I want you to be straight with me about what part of the claim no test could ever reach, even in principle, no matter how much evidence you had. My brother thinks it's a factual claim about the world, not just a mood, so if it's not testable at all I'd like to know that too.
