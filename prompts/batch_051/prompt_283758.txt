I have been thinking about a simple visual illusion and what it forces me to say about when my experiences occur. I have ended up with a set of commitments that each seem right on their own, but I suspect they cannot all be true together, and I would like someone to work through exactly where the friction is.

The illusion is this. A red dot flashes briefly at one position, and a fraction of a second later a green dot flashes at a second position a short distance away, with a blank interval between them. When the timing is right, one sees a single dot move smoothly from the first position to the second, shifting from red to green roughly halfway along the path. The halfway point occurs before the green dot has actually flashed. I will call this the moving-dot report.

Here is what I find myself wanting to affirm:

(1) The moving dot is part of the experience itself. I am not seeing two separate static flashes and then, when I report what happened, unwittingly smoothing them over in memory. If it looked like a single dot gliding and changing color, then my visual experience at that moment had gliding-and-changing content.

(2) Experience is not a delayed broadcast. If the green flash really arrives a fifth of a second after the red one, I want to say that I saw the red flash before the green flash happened in the world. I do not want to accept that everything I am aware of is already slightly in the past, a reconstruction running a beat behind the events.

(3) The brain cannot assemble the moving-dot experience until the green flash has arrived and been processed, because the green flash supplies two things the moving dot depends on: where the dot is going and what color it turns into. The earliest the moving-dot experience could be complete is therefore after the second flash.

(4) I am more prepared to distrust my sense of when something was in my experience than my sense of what was in it. I can accept that my timing may be reconstructed and sometimes wrong, but I do not think my conviction that I saw a moving dot could be that kind of error.

(5) There may be a third option that keeps everything above. The moving-dot experience could be assembled only after the green flash, but carry an earlier time stamp, so that it seems to have occurred before the green flash existed. This would be backdating the experience, not editing a memory after the fact. I find the idea unattractive but hard to rule out.

I cannot tell which of these are load-bearing conflicts and which only look like conflicts. Please go through (1) through (5) and give me the following: a ranking of the real tensions by severity; an explanation, for each real tension, of what would have to be given up to remove it; a note on which apparent tensions dissolve once you look closely; and a judgment about whether the backdating move in (5) is truly a third option or merely one of the other positions disguised in new words. Finally, tell me which single revision among (1) through (5) would do the most to make the set consistent, and what that revision would cost.
