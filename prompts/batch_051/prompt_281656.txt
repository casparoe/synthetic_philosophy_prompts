Write a villanelle about the axiom of choice. The two refrains should be the constructive and the nonconstructive faces of the axiom, something like "there is a function that picks" and "but no rule says which one." The form should carry the argument: the refrains should alternate and finally meet in the closing couplet the way existence-in-principle and definability keep talking past each other, with the axiom's strength being that it supplies all the choices at once without ever exhibiting a selector for any of them. Include, accurately but lightly, at least one standard equivalent — the well-ordering theorem, Zorn's lemma, or Tychonoff's theorem — and the fact that the axiom is independent of ZF; a set theorist should recognize the view being rendered, not a caricature. It should read as a real poem, not a mnemonic or joke, and it should end with the tension between unlimited choice and the absence of any rule left intact. Write in continuous prose except for the poem's own line breaks, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
