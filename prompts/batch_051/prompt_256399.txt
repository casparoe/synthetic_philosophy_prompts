Me and my reading group have spent months arguing about whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real divisions in European history or just later inventions, so write the adversarial-collaboration document we should have produced. One of us is a period realist: he thinks the labels pick out genuine, fairly sharp discontinuities in institutions, self-understanding, and intellectual practice. The other is a period constructivist: she thinks they're retrospective impositions that flatten more than they reveal, and that the continuities (say, between late medieval kinematics and seventeenth-century mechanics, or between 'medieval' and 'Renaissance' religious culture) does more explanatory work than the breaks. Structure it as: 1) Agreed claims (about 300 words) — the chronology, what changed and what didn't, and what both accept about how the labels themselves arose. 2) Disagreements table (about 250 words) — the residual claims, each worded so its own side would accept it. 3) Cruxes (about 250 words) — the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of them, each with a case or kind of evidence both agree bears on it and each side's prediction. 4) A short note (about 100 words) on which part of the dispute, if any, turned out to be just verbal.
