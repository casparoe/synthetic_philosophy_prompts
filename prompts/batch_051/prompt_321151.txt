I have come to accept a broadly Kantian view that human dignity is the foundation of rights. In the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, Kant distinguishes two kinds of worth: whatever has a price can be replaced by something else as an equivalent, but what is above all price and admits of no equivalent has dignity. I take this to mean that persons cannot be used as mere means, and that rights protect this irreplaceable worth. I would like to know what would count against this position. Please map out its exposure: which arguments, cases, or implications would show that dignity is false, empty, or doing no independent work, and which would be merely awkward for a defender. Distinguish what a committed Kantian would concede as genuine counterevidence from what they would dismiss as misunderstanding. Also address the charge, associated with Ruth Macklin, that dignity adds nothing to autonomy and is too vague to guide moral reasoning. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
