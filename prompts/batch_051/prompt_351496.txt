I help run an informal philosophy reading group, and for several sessions we have been going back and forth about whether the Golden Rule can be repaired. By the Golden Rule I mean the familiar reciprocal injunction to treat others as you would want them to treat you, taken as a serious general moral principle rather than as a loose heuristic. We agree that the standard objections carry weight: the masochist case, where a person sincerely wants to be harmed, so a literal reading of the rule seems to license harming him; the conflicting-preferences case, where what I want done to me is not what you want done to you, so the rule seems to issue contradictory instructions unless we silently replace stated preferences with some conception of interests; and the paternalism worry, where treating others as I would want to be treated in their position can merely project my own values onto them. What divides the group is whether there is any formulation that fixes all three problems while remaining recognizably the Golden Rule — that is, a rule of imaginative reciprocation or standpoint-shifting — rather than a disguised version of Kantian universalizability, preference utilitarianism, or a full theory of value.

I would like your credence in the following proposition, with the reasoning decomposed. The proposition: there is at least one formulation of the Golden Rule such that (i) it is non-vacuous, in that it rules out some ways of treating others; (ii) it yields plausible verdicts in the three kinds of case just listed; (iii) it remains distinct from the categorical imperative's universalizability test, from utilitarianism, and from any independent theory of interests or value; and (iv) it is still recognizably a rule of reciprocity or standpoint-shifting rather than a stand-in for do the right thing or promote well-being. By yielding plausible verdicts I mean that an otherwise competent user applying the formulation without importing an unstated moral theory reaches the answer most reflective people would accept in those cases.
