I've been trying to understand why faithful interpretation is still considered an open problem, and I keep coming back to a simple recursive scheme. Suppose we have a large model A whose internals we want to understand. We train a smaller model B to imitate A's internal activations and to output explanations of A's computations. B is smaller and therefore somewhat easier to study, but it remains too opaque to audit directly, so we train a still smaller model C to explain B, and continue in this way. Since each model in the chain is optimized specifically to explain its predecessor, fidelity seems to be built in at every link.

Eventually the chain reaches a model small enough for a human to inspect directly, perhaps a short symbolic program or a shallow decision tree. At that point, couldn't we compose the explanations down the chain to obtain a human-checkable account of A's computation? Wouldn't the only remaining difficulty be the engineering problem of training adequate interpreters, rather than any in-principle barrier? I can't see where this argument fails, yet the persistence of interpretability as an unsolved problem suggests that it must. Where exactly does the recursive scheme break down?
