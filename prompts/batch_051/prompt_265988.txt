so I'm collecting objections to Wittgenstein's family-resemblance move — the idea in the Investigations that for lots of words (his running example is "game") there is no single feature all uses share; instead the uses hang together the way members of a family do, by criss-crossing and overlapping similarities, not by a common essence. This sits inside the broader language-games picture, where you understand a word by seeing what it lets people do rather than by hunting for a definition. I've got five objections so far:

(1) resemblance is cheap: any two things resemble each other in indefinitely many respects, so unless you bring in an extra constraint from somewhere, a network of similarities can't fix the extension of a concept and it just balloons outward.
(2) it doesnt explain which similarities are the relevant ones; when we apply a concept we're not merely registering resemblance, we're judging that this particular similarity matters, and that act of judgment is doing the real work that the theory leaves unexplained.
(3) it overgeneralizes from a few messy everyday words; alot of concepts — mathematical ones, natural-kind terms — really do seem to have definitions or hidden essences, so family resemblance can't be a general account of concepts.
(4) it makes the boundary between members and non-members arbitrary: even if you settle which similarities matter, membership is still a gradient, so there's no principled point at which the concept stops, and it can be dragged along a chain of acceptable-looking links until it reaches somewhere absurd.
(5) the motivating example might be bad: people have proposed explicit definitions even for "game", so the fact that Wittgenstein and his readers didn't immediately spot a common essence doesn't show there isn't one.

What I'm looking for is a sixth objection that's genuinely different from these five — not a rewording, not a special case of one of them, not a mash-up of two. It can come from any direction (semantics, psychology, logic, epistemology, whatever) as long as it's an actual line of attack on the family-resemblance account. Give me the objection, then a sentence or two on how it differs from and relates to items (1)-(5). If you think the list basically covers the serious options, say which one you'd press hardest and what a defender should say back.
