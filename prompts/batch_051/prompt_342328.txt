I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I have been working with a distinction I have not seen stated cleanly anywhere. I call it the line between a "transcript" and a "continuation" in AI recreations of the dead. A transcript stays inside the documentary record of the person: it can restate opinions they actually voiced, retell stories they actually told, or recite lines they actually wrote. A continuation goes beyond the record: it generates new advice, new jokes, new political views in their voice. My working hypothesis is that the hardest ethical problems with griefbots, and much of their uncanniness, belong mostly to continuations, while transcripts should be treated more like annotated archives. But I am beginning to suspect the line is less clean than I would like, because selecting, editing, and voicing even a strict excerpt is already a small act of authorship. Is the distinction doing real ethical work? Does it already exist in the ethics literature under another name? And where does it break down: what does it treat as harmless that is not, or as forbidden that should not be?
