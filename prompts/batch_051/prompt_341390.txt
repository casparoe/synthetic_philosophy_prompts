I help run a small open-source collective, about eight regulars, maintaining a library none of us own but all of us depend on. For years the norm was that if someone fixed a nasty bug or did a big documentation pass, a couple of others would step up and take the next unpleasant task within days. It was a kind of unspoken reciprocity, and I always assumed it worked because we all saw who had been pulling their weight week to week.

A few months ago we switched to an anonymous contribution tool to keep reviews unbiased, and nobody knows who submitted what until the monthly release. Since then, that reciprocal piling-on has basically evaporated. People still do the bare minimum, but follow-up work just sits there unless someone is explicitly assigned. The strange thing is the objective payoffs haven't changed at all: we all still benefit from the same amount of cleanup, and it costs the same to do it. My co-maintainer thinks the problem is purely motivational and wants a frank conversation about freeloading. Another contributor argues we should add a reputation score that tracks commitments. But I can't shake the feeling that the anonymity itself broke something structural, and that neither a values talk nor a scoreboard quite gets at it.

What kind of problem do we actually have, and what sorts of fixes should we be considering?
