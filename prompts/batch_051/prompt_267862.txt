ok so i just spent like two hours arguing with my friend about idolatry, and he kept insisting that idolatry is just caring too much about something, like being obsessed with your career or your partner. i was defending the more specific view that idolatry isnt about intensity at all, its about the role the thing plays in your life. the position i was pushing: you idolize something when you make a finite, conditioned good (a person, a nation, your work, your own self-image) function as your ultimate ground — the thing that has to be okay for your life to be okay, the thing youll sacrifice everything else for, the thing whose failure feels like damnation rather than ordinary loss. on this view the wrongness isnt greed or excess; its partly a category error and partly an injustice to the object, because youve asked something finite to bear a weight it cant carry, and youve also deformed yourself by organizing your whole life around it.

so here is what i want. tell me what would count against this account. i dont just mean the fact that people use the word idolatry more loosely — i want the real defeaters: cases of total devotion to a finite thing that dont seem wrong at all (a parent and a child? an artist and the work?), cases of wrong valuation that arent idolatry on this story (valuing the right thing in a poisoned way, like serving a god out of pure terror), and the charge that the notion of "functioning as ultimate ground" is too elastic to be tested. sort the defeaters from fatal to merely akward, say which ones a defender of this account would themselves concede count against it versus which theyd dismiss as not-even-wrong, and give me an honest verdict on whether the view is insulated — whether slapping "see, but that wasnt really ultimate" on every counterexample makes it unfalsifiable, and if so whether that actually matters for a claim about the shape of a persons loves. i guess the question underneath is whether "valuing something in the wrong way" is even a coherent notion once you stop identifying it with "valuing too much."
