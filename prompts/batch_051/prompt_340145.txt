At least that's the line.

Here's the thing I keep tripping over. The three interpretations were mostly built around coins, dice, and individual betting odds. None of them seems to fit the way probabilities actually get used now, where a bank feeds someone's file into a trained machine-learning model and the model outputs 0.82 as the probability of repayment, and the bank treats that as the probability. It isnt a subjective degree of belief: basically no human in the decision chain actually has credence 0.82, they just defer to the model's number. It isnt a frequency in the old sense either, because there's no specified reference class or collective the model is reporting on — it saw a shifting pile of millions of examples, not a well-defined sequence of trials. And it isnt a propensity, because the applicant either repays or doesn't; there's no chancy device, and the 0.82 looks more like a property of the model-plus-inputs than of the loan itself.

So I want to know whether that tidy three-way taxonomy survives this development, whether it needs a fourth category (something like model-relative probability), or whether one of the old categories can be stretched to cover it without becoming totally vacuous.

Then say which premise of the taxonomy argument this actually puts pressure on, and whether the honest conclusion is a new category or a repair of an old one.
