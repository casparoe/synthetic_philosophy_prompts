In a study group on the demarcation problem, one member kept invoking the slogan that only falsifiable theories count as science. He used it first to dismiss psychoanalysis, then astrology, then string theory, and finally to say that what separates physics from everything else is just that physics sticks to falsifiable claims. I suspect this one sentence is doing several different jobs, and I want to know which of them survive scrutiny. Taking that slogan as your object: distinguish the precise claims it could be making, for example claims about what genuine testing requires, about what makes an inquiry scientific, about what makes it rational to accept a theory, and about what should be excluded from science curricula or funding. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and indicate which reading is actually needed for each of the four uses my study-group member made of it. Present the readings as a numbered menu. End with a one-sentence summary of the strongest defensible reading, formatted as \answer{...}.
