Two asymmetries in how we judge dramatizations of real dead people. (1) If a screenwriter invents a scene in which a real historical figure acts cruelly or dishonestly, many people say the filmmakers have wronged the dead person; if the screenwriter invents a scene in which the same person acts generously or bravely, the same people tend to call it rounding out a complex character. (2) The invented cruelty that would be called a betrayal in a film about someone recently dead barely registers in a film about a figure dead three centuries ago, even when the invention is equally false and equally central to the story. I want to know whether either asymmetry tracks a real moral difference or whether one of them is merely felt. Are the two asymmetries instances of a single principle — something about harm to living people who still identify with the dead — or do they come apart?
