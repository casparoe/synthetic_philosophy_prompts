Since my mum's dementia got worse I've been reading book eleven of the Confessions, and I've landed on what I take to be Augustine's view: the past only exists as memory, the future only as expectation, and there isn't really an extended time out there in the world, just a stretching-out of a mind. I don't know how much pressure that view can take, though. So, assuming I actually hold it: what would count against it? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward—that duration seems to be felt directly rather than reconstructed from memory, that a houseplant grows in an empty flat with nobody there to mark it, that clocks stay synchronized and bodies decay on schedule whether or not anyone is attending to them, whatever relativity says about a shared now. And tell me honestly whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a claim about time rather than a scientific one.
