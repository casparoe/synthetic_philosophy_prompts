I am a novelist, and in the novel I am currently drafting there is a character, a former philosopher of science who now works on automated wildlife monitoring, who becomes preoccupied with Hempel's raven paradox. I want the philosophical content of that preoccupation to be precise rather than decorative, so I am working through the issue before I write the scenes.

As I understand the paradox: the hypothesis that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens. Nicod's condition says that a positive instance confirms a universal generalization, so a black raven confirms the first hypothesis and a non-black non-raven, such as a white shoe, confirms the second. If logically equivalent hypotheses are confirmed by the same evidence, then a white shoe confirms that all ravens are black, which seems absurd.

Now suppose confirmation no longer takes the form of a person encountering objects one by one in the world, but is mediated by an algorithmic pipeline. A camera trap or a citizen-science app logs an observation only after a trained classifier decides the object is worth recording, and the classifier was itself trained on a biased sample of earlier observations. A white shoe enters the dataset only under particular conditions: the object detector flags it as an anomaly, or a user photographs it because it strikes them as unusual.

Does Hempel's paradox survive this change? One possibility is that it dissolves, because the observation is no longer a random draw from nature and Nicod's condition does not apply to filtered data. Another is that it survives, because the selection effect merely alters background information and the white shoe still confirms, if only negligibly. A third is that the development forces a repaired version of the paradox, in which the real issue was never sampling but what counts as an instance of a hypothesis. Which of these readings, or which other reading, is most defensible? Identify the specific premise the new development bears on, and say whether the paradox in its original form is still generated or must be restated.
