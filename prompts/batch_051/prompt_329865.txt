Over the past few months I have been collecting my views on the paradox of hedonism, and I've ended up with the following six commitments. Each of them seems right to me in isolation, but I can't tell whether the whole set is jointly tenable, and I would like an audit before I try to defend any of them in writing.

(1) Hedonism about prudential value: the only thing that is good for a person for its own sake is pleasant experience, and the only thing that is bad for its own sake is unpleasant experience.
(2) The psychological core of the paradox of hedonism is, as a rough generalization, correct: people who directly aim at their own happiness tend to end up less happy than people absorbed in friendships, projects, and activities they treat as worthwhile in themselves.
(3) The rational response for a hedonist is therefore to pursue happiness largely indirectly, by cultivating the same wholehearted engagement with non-hedonic goods that a non-hedonist has, since this is a more reliable route to pleasant experience than explicit pleasure bookkeeping.
(4) But I also believe there is a psychological limit to this strategy: it is very hard, perhaps impossible, to become genuinely absorbed in an activity while simultaneously regarding that absorption as a mere instrument for producing pleasure. The attempt to treat a good as a device tends to undercut the very absorption it is meant to produce.
(5) I accept a coherence condition on final ends: if one really believes that only pleasant experience is good for its own sake, then one is rationally required not to treat friendship, knowledge, or craft as good for their own sake.
(6) And yet I cannot bring myself to regard the person who wholeheartedly cares about friendship or craft for its own sake as rationally defective. Such lives look at least as rational as the calculating hedonist's, and they seem to me to be getting something right rather than merely living under a useful illusion.

Please go through these and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension, which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve once a relevant distinction is drawn, and how severe the residual tensions are. I would like a definite verdict rather than a survey of options: rank the tensions by severity, explain what each would cost, and recommend the single revision that does the least damage to the overall package.
