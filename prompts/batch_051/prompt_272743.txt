I keep noticing something in online karma and reputation systems that I don't have a word for. The pattern goes like this. A site adds a score to track something real: how helpful a comment was, whether someone pays back loans, how often they tell the truth. At first the score is just a rough shadow of that thing. Then people start doing the thing mainly to move the score, and pretty soon the score is the only legible part left. What it was supposed to track gets harder to see on its own. At that point the number starts being treated as the fact itself: moderators sort by it, users defer to it, and people say you can't argue with the score even when everyone knows the score is being gamed. But there's often a small group who still do the underlying thing without caring about the score, and the system starts punishing them because their score looks bad. Its like the score has swallowed the thing it was meant to measure. I'm sure this has been studied, but I want to know if the decoupling has a specific name, and not just the obvious word for any metric getting gamed. I'd like a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Each item should name one candidate concept or describe one edge of the phenomenon, say whether it fits exactly or only partially, and if no established concept fits, coin a term and say what it covers and what it excludes.
