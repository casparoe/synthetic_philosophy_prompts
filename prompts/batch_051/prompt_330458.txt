Say a city's arts council starts awarding grants with a stripped-down version of Tolstoy's infection theory of art. To get funded, a work has to make a double-blind panel of two hundred demographically matched residents converge on the feeling the artist wrote down in advance as the one they meant to transmit; the sealed intention is opened only after the panel has each picked from a fixed list of, say, thirty emotion words. Treat this as a mechanism rather than a judgment on Tolstoy. What kind of work gets made and funded, and how do artists game the sealed-intention step? Which of Tolstoy's usual targets—counterfeit art, insincerity, professionalized art for its own sake, work aimed at a cultivated elite—does this setup accidentally encourage, and which does it actually filter out? Give a committed verdict on where the scheme breaks first and whether that failure can be patched without dropping the infection criterion.
