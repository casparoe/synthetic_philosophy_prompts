I keep coming back to a parallel between two puzzles, and I would like help deciding whether it is more than a surface resemblance.

The first puzzle concerns what it is to follow a rule. Suppose I have used the word "plus" all my life, but — as it happens — only with numbers below 100. I take myself to mean ordinary addition, so 101 plus 102 is 203. Now imagine a rival rule that agrees with addition on every pair I have ever actually used, then diverges above the boundary: whenever both numbers are above 100, it returns 5. All of my past use is equally compatible with each rule, because the two rules behave identically everywhere I have ever tested them. Nothing in my history of use seems to pick one out. And yet I am not merely guessing that I meant addition all along.

The second puzzle is one I know from working with machine learning. Train a model on a finite set of examples, and if the family of candidate functions is rich enough, endlessly many functions will fit the training data perfectly. The choice among them is made by something outside the data — a bias toward simplicity, regularization, the structure of the model, or a bet about what the unseen cases will be like.

These two situations look to me as though they share one skeleton: a finite record underdetermines an indefinite future, and what settles the continuation lies outside the record. The lesson I am tempted to draw from both is that no rule, meaning, or model is ever fully written into what it has done so far; the continuation is partly constituted by whatever is doing the continuing.

But I keep worrying that the two cases come apart at exactly this point. The machine-learning case is ultimately about prediction: future examples can show that a function that fit the training data nonetheless generalizes badly. The rule case feels different — it is not about what I will in fact say next, but about what I meant and what the rule requires. If that difference runs deep, the analogy may be only verbal.

State the mapping between the two cases as precisely as you can, say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer from one to the other, and then tell me whether the parallel is deep or superficial. In particular, try to name at least one thing the parallel would lead me to expect about one side that is actually false.
