I've been thinking, on my own and without any philosophical training, about a kind of blame that doesn't seem to be about causing anything. It's the position of someone who is mixed up in a wrong because of an ordinary role, a loyalty, or a refusal to judge, even when the wrong would have happened without them and they couldn't have stopped it on their own. My reading group is doing Kazuo Ishiguro's The Remains of the Day this month, and I want to use it to get clearer about this. Stevens does not write Lord Darlington's speeches or set his political direction, and in a narrow sense he is only ever doing his job; yet the novel seems to want us to count him as implicated in what Darlington does — the appeasement politics, the dismissal of the two Jewish maids — in a way that can't be reduced to "he caused it" or "he could have stopped it."

Could you read the novel through that lens for me? I'd like a careful reading, tied to specific moments in the book, that doesn't turn the novel into an illustration of an abstract idea and doesn't force the idea onto the book where it doesn't fit. Where does the novel show that a purely causal way of assigning blame misses something — that Stevens's notion of dignity, his refusal to form judgments about his employer, and his small acts of loyal service are themselves a way of taking part in wrongdoing? Where does the novel complicate or resist that thought, so that an honest reading has to admit the line between just doing the job and genuine participation is harder to draw than it first looks? Pay attention to the way the book ties dignity to staying in one's role no matter what; to the firing of the two Jewish maids, which Stevens carries out but frames as loyalty; and to his later evasiveness about having served Lord Darlington, since the evasion may matter as much as the service. Does the novel take a position on whether complicity attaches to the person who merely serves, or does it leave the question open? And at the end, say what fiction can bring to this problem that a plain argument about complicity cannot.
