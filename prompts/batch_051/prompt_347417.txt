I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy or law background, and I've been going back and forth with a friend about a bill in my city that would make certain dark patterns a misdemeanor. I need someone to actually work through it rather than just say "it's vague" or "it's obviously fine." I made up my own test for when a criminal law is precise enough, so use that, but tell me if the test itself is bad.

My test: a criminal law is precise enough only if all four of these hold.

(1) Notice as a boolean function. A normal person, given a concrete case and the statute, can return covered or not-covered at least for the common cases without reading the judge's mind. If the check throws an exception on any realistic input, the law is too vague.

(2) No arbitrary enforcement. Two identical products or actions come out the same no matter which cop, prosecutor, or judge runs the check. If "does it feel scummy to me" is one of the inputs, that's a bug, not a legal standard.

(3) No chilling by ambiguity. If the only way to be safe is to rip out a bunch of plainly legal behavior, the law fails. Over-compliance isn't free, and a vague criminal law taxes lawful edge cases.

(4) The fuzz has to be load-bearing. Words like "reasonable" or "misleading" earn their keep only when they name a standard most people can actually apply in good faith. If the legislature used a fuzzy word because it didn't want to make the decision, that's not a standard, that's a punt.

The bill, as I remember the draft (paraphrasing, not quoting): a person commits the offense if they intentionally deploy a user interface in a commercial online service that, through a misleading design, causes or is likely to cause a typical consumer to enter into a purchase the consumer would not have entered into had the options been presented neutrally. The draft says "misleading design" includes exploiting a predictable cognitive bias like a default effect, framing effect, or decoy effect, where "predictable" means documented in the behavioral-science literature.

So: under my four criteria, is this thing void for vagueness? Go criterion by criterion and say whether the bill passes, fails, or whether the criterion cant decide. Where it fails, say whether the bill is the problem or my test is the problem. And if you think no criminal dark-patterns law could ever pass, say that.

Format: exactly five numbered items. The first four should each cover one of my criteria; the fifth should be your overall call on the bill and on my test. Each item one or two sentences. No intro, no conclusion.
