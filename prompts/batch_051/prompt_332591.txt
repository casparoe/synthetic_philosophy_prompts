Some years back I was fitting dose–response curves, and we had two models that both fit the data essentially perfectly. One had two parameters and a plausible mechanism behind it; the other had nine parameters and a couple of ugly cross terms, and it fit just as well. Everyone around me—me included—treated the first as the real result and the second as "just curve-fitting," even though no experiment we ran could seperate them. But if I take underdetermination seriously, the data don't pick between empirically equivalent models, so this asymmetry between the clean model and the ugly one is doing a lot of unacknowledged work. Is the simplicity preference actually defensible under underdetermination, or is it just an aesthetic habit that lets scientists keep publishing? I'd like the strongest justification you can give, and an honest answer about whether that asymmetry should be kept or given up.
