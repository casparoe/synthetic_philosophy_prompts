I'm a retired ecologist — I spent thirty years on lake and riparian systems, so I'm comfortable with nutrient budgets but brand new to moral philosophy — and I've been in an environmental ethics reading group for the last year. We keep stalling out on the same argument about why a wild place matters. The instrumental crew says it matters because of what it does for us: water purification, flood buffering, carbon storage, recreation, future pharmaceuticals. The intrinsic-value crew says no, it matters on its own, even if no human ever sets foot in it — they wheel out some version of the last-person thought experiment to say the place would still matter if everyone else vanished. And then a third group, the ones I've come to find most interesting, says value lives in the relationships: the river you've fished with your kid for twenty years, the mountain that's threaded through a community's songs and identity, places that matter because a particular human life and a particular landscape have shaped each other, not because they're useful tools and not because they'd be valuable in an empty universe.

Our last meeting ended in a stalemate, and I promised to bring something to close the next one. I'd like a villanelle, nineteen lines, no padding. The two refrains should carry the instrumental and the intrinsic voices, and the middle lines should keep supplying the relational rejoinder, so the form itself shows that neither refrain stays stable once the relationship is in the room. In the closing couplet the two refrains come together: I don't want you to force a happy synthesis. Pick the way the poem should end — does the relational view come out as the deepest, does the intrinsic view absorb the instrumental, does the tension stay open — and commit to it, with enough conviction that our group can tell what you chose. Then give me a short paragraph afterwards saying where the poem takes liberties with the positions or leans on a contested reading of relational value, so I dont accidentally hand someone the poem as a fair summary. Ive read enough to know a villanelle is more than a rhyme scheme, so the repeated lines should mean something a little different each time they come back.
