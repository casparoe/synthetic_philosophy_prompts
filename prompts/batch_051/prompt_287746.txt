Suppose there's a community whose language and behavior are indistinguishable from ours today, with one hidden difference in how their word for water got introduced: it was never ostensively anchored to "the underlying substance, whatever it is." Instead, it was introduced by an explicit stipulative definition, something like "the clear drinkable liquid that falls as rain and fills the lakes." Everything else about the actual world, including the fact that the stuff in their lakes is H2O, is exactly as it is here, and by now they'd be just as surprised as us to learn it's H2O. Now run the usual thought experiments from the necessary a posteriori literature for these people: a Twin Earth where the clear drinkable liquid in the lakes is XYZ; a possible world where a superficially identical but chemically different liquid falls as rain and fills the lakes; and the claim "water is H2O." Predict, with your reasoning visible, which of the standard judgments (if any) come out different for them and why. I'm less interested in yes/no than in the chain: are the differences just changes in what their sentences mean, or do they change which facts are necessary? Flag the links you're least confident in and what would have to be true about reference for your prediction to flip.
