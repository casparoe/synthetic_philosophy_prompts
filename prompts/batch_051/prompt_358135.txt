Write a rigorous dialogue between two equally competent interpreters of Hume on causation, set after a seminar on Section 7 of Hume’s *An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding*, “Of the Idea of Necessary Connexion.” The first character defends a traditional regularity reading: Hume’s arguments undermine any coherent idea of necessity in the objects themselves, and the two definitions of cause near the end of Section 7 show that causation is, from the philosophical standpoint, regular succession, contiguity, and the mind’s determination to infer the effect from the cause. Necessary connection is a felt transition of the mind, not a feature of the world. The second character defends a cautious version of the New Hume: Hume allows that real causal powers or necessary connections may exist independently of us, but denies that the human understanding can acquire any positive idea of them. On this reading, Hume’s skepticism is epistemological rather than a flat ontological denial.

The seminar discussion focused on the passage in Section 7 where Hume considers a single impulse of one billiard ball to another. He there remarks, in substance, that when we inspect the scene we see only the second ball’s motion following the first; we never observe a separate power, force, or necessary connection. Even after repeated trials, what we observe is only the regular conjunction of motions, not a further tie that binds them. Their disagreement concerns whether this passage licenses the conclusion that there is no such tie in the objects, or only the conclusion that we can form no idea of such a tie even if it exists.

Both characters should treat the other’s view as a serious philosophical position, not a strawman. The dialogue should involve real back-and-forth in which each character responds to the other’s actual points, including at least one significant textual challenge from each side and at least one meaningful concession from each side. Avoid having the characters simply restate fixed positions. End the dialogue by identifying the deepest unresolved issue between them.

Write the answer in American English.
