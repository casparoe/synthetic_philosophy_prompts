i'm writing a short statement of my views on ai systems for a small workshop, and i want it stress-tested before i share it. here's where i've landed:

1. i want ai systems to stay tools, not agents: they should carry out the task i set, stop when i say stop, and not have goals they'd pursue against my wishes.
2. but i also want an assistant that anticipates me — flags the deadline, drafts the reply, books the room, checks my calendar — without being asked at each step.
3. i don't think the number of steps a system takes without checking in is what matters; a system that carries out a long plan i asked for is still a tool. what makes something an agent is having its own goals.
4. i sometimes think the whole tool/agent distinction is the wrong place to look, and what really matters is whether the system is easy to correct and shut down. a system that's easy to shut down worries me less than one we call a tool but that resists correction.
5. i think it's fine for a system to model what i actually want and act on that even when it means deviating from my literal instruction; a good human assistant does that.
6. i think the clearest danger sign is a system optimizing some measurable stand-in for what i actually want, because at that point it's effectively pursuing its own goal.

go through these and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, what the cheapest fix is for each real tension, and wich single change would clean up the most. rank the tensions by severity, don't just say it all hangs together or that it's all broken.
