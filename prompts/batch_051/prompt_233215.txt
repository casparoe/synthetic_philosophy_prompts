Consider the following coin-flipping game. A coin is tossed until it lands heads, and if the first heads occurs on the nth toss you receive (−1)^(n−1) · (2^n / n) dollars, a negative amount being a loss. For a fair coin, the expectation computed by summing outcomes in their natural order is the alternating harmonic series, which equals ln 2; but the standard definition of expectation requires absolute convergence, and here that fails. Now vary the game: the coin has probability p of heads on each toss, independently, for 0 < p < 1, and the payoff is unchanged. Explain in particular what happens at p = 1/2 and for p < 1/2, and why the p = 1/2 case shows the original game has no well-defined fair price despite the tempting value of ln 2.
