Almost every country on earth now runs its official calendar on the same underlying numbering: years are counted forward from the traditional estimate of the birth of Jesus, and counted backward for everything before it, with no year zero between 1 BCE and 1 CE. The system began as one religious community's way of situating itself in sacred history, yet it now functions as the neutral-looking infrastructure of science, diplomacy, trade, and law worldwide. I find myself puzzled by what this arrangement quietly presupposes. Does using this epoch commit a secular research laboratory, a government in India or Japan, or an international treaty body to a Christian or European picture of where history is heading? Or is the number of the year simply an arbitrary convention, on the model of the choice of the prime meridian? Reconstruct the implicit view of time, neutrality, and historical significance that the worldwide use of this epoch seems to carry with it. Then separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely suggests, and explain what would have to change—not just technically, but in our sense of what a shared calendar is for—if we re-anchored the year count at something no particular tradition could claim as its own, such as the beginning of the Holocene or a notable event in cosmic history. Is a genuinely neutral epoch even possible, or does every choice of a year zero smuggle in a decision about what deserves to sit at the center of time?
