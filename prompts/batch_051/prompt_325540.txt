im stuck on whether a company itself can be blameworthy, and I keep going back and forth. The theory I'm drawn to is that a corporation isn't just a metaphor: it has goals, gathers and uses information, makes decisions through standing procedures, keeps a memory in its manuals and accounting systems, and persists as people come and go. That makes it look like a real agent, and it also gives me a tidy answer to who to blame — blame the entity whose structure and incentives selected the bad action, not whichever underling happened to be holding the wrench. When Volkswagen or BP does something awful, blaming a few bad apples feels like a category mistake; the thing that mattered was the decision architecture, and the law already treats these entities as persons for a bunch of purposes.

But then the intuition hits: there is no one home. I can be angry about what the company did, but I can't seem to be angry at it the way I can at a person — I can resent a human who lied, but a company can't feel shame or remorse or lose sleep, and blaming it starts to feel like blaming a machine or a weather system: something that produces harm but isn't the kind of thing one resents. So I have an attractive theory with real systematic virtues and a firm intuition it seems to contradict, and I dont want to just be told which side wins.

I want the strongest version of each side, and then a negotiated verdict. What would it cost to debunk the intuition as a limitation of my emotional hardware, what would it cost to abandon or amend the theory, and is there a live reading on which both survive — e.g., that corporate blame is a different kind of blame, or that the company is the right unit of responsibility while the resentment is still owed to the humans inside? Be explicit about what would justify each move, not just which one you'd pick. And please write it as continuous prose like an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
