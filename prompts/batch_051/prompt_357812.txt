Treat the following two positions as the parties to an adversarial collaboration. One side holds a Mohist-style view: the root of conflict and cruelty is partiality, and the remedy is impartial care (jian'ai) — a concern for each person that is not calibrated by nearness, kinship, or reciprocity; graded affection is favoritism given a name. The other side holds a Confucian-style view: care is properly graded (qinqin) — one's greatest obligations are to parents, children, and particular others, and a demand to care equally for strangers is both psychologically unrealistic and an attack on the family. Draft the joint statement these two parties could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered Agreed claims section, non-vacuous but acceptable to both sides; (2) a disagreements table giving the precise residual difference, formulated so that each side would accept the wording of its own view; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — together with, for each crux, the cases or tests both sides agree would bear on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Argue the positions directly and do not lean on historical citations. End with a one-sentence summary as the last line, beginning 'Summary:'.
