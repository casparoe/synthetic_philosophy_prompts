My friend in local politics keeps telling me sortition is jury duty scaled up and that the rest of us are being squeamish. His line: we already let randomly selected citizens hear competing arguments, deliberate, and render verdicts that can ruin someone, so a randomly selected legislature is no more radical, and term limits are a half measure because they still select for people willing to make politics a career. He uses the analogy to conclude we don't need career politicians at all. I want the analogy audited properly, not just 'all analogies break somewhere.' Give me the mapping first—what plays the role of the juror, the trial, the evidence, the judge's instructions, the verdict, the fact that jurors don't choose their case, and the one-off summons on the legislative side—then the disanalogies that matter specifically for his conclusion, then whether that conclusion survives and what a better analogy would be if there is one. Format: exactly eight numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion; item one is the mapping, items two through seven are the disanalogies that actually bite, item eight is the verdict and the better analogy.
