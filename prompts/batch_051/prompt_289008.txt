I want a thought experiment for getting at whether market exchange civilizes or corrupts character. One tradition holds that commerce disciplines the passions, making people more honest, punctual, and able to cooperate with strangers; another tradition holds that it makes people treat others as instruments and recast relationships as transactions. Construct a single realistic scenario — a small business or a local market, not a fable with saints and villains, and no longer than a paragraph — in which one and the same market mechanism produces, in the same person, one change that supports the first view and one change that supports the second. The two changes must be distinct traits rather than one behavior under two names: showing that prudence and cold calculation are two descriptions of the same conduct would not count. Further, the civilizing change must be a real change in the person rather than mere compliance while being watched, and the corrupting change must be caused by the market relation itself rather than by poverty, coercion, or legal failure. Then do the check: name the mechanism and the two traits; identify the structural feature responsible for each effect and say what would happen to each effect if that feature were removed; and finish by saying how a defender of each side would try to explain away the other side's evidence.
