A friend of mine has been deep in Hare and Singer lately and won't stop hitting me with "what if everyone did that?" Last week he used it to argue I should go vegan. Fine. But when I ran the same test on veganism itself, it came out forbidden, and now I can't tell whether the test is broken or I'm cheating. I wrote the whole thing out—tell me which step is the sleeper.

(1) An action is permissible only if I can rationally will that everyone does it.
(2) I cannot rationally will that everyone goes vegan all at once tomorrow. The animals already in the system would still have to be killed or abandoned, and animal agriculture shutting down overnight would wreck the livelihoods of a huge number of people. That outcome is not something I can will.
(3) So by the test's own logic, going vegan is impermissible.
(4) But that's obviously unfair to the vegan: her maxim isn't "go vegan overnight while the rest of the world stays exactly as it is"; its "adopt a vegan diet as part of a gradual transition in which animal agriculture winds down humanely."
(5) Except the meat-eater can pack context into a maxim too. His maxim can be "consume animal products in societies where they are available and commonly accepted." If everyone acted on that, nothing would change—the actual world already fits it—so he can will it just as easily as she wills her transition.
(6) So the test convicts both meat-eating and veganism on one maxim and convicts neither on the other.
(7) If the verdict can be flipped just by choosing how much context goes inside the maxim, then (1) isn't a test at all; it's a mirror.
(8) But (1) was doing real work—it's the only reason I couldn't shrug off the vegan conclusion.

Where does this go wrong? My gut is that step (2) misdescribes what universalization means and that the real problem is in (5), but I want to know what that costs. What has to be true about maxims—and about universalization itself—for the original "what if everyone did that?" test to put meat-eating on the wrong side and veganism on the right one without also wrecking the test?
