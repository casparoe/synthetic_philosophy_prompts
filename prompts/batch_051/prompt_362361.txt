Right, so I've spent too long reading about the liar paradox, and I want help diagnosing why the whole area seems so stuck. Everyone agrees that a sentence which says of itself that it is false can't just be treated as an ordinary truth-bearer under naive truth and classical logic — something has to go. But which thing has to go has been fought over for decades, and nobody has moved.

There are, very roughly, three entrenched camps. The gappers say the liar is neither true nor false, and that we should give up bivalence or at least excluded middle. The glut camp say it is both true and false, and that we should drop explosion rather than allow a contradiction to entail everything (dialetheism, if you want the jargon). Then there's the classicist or hierarchist lot, who insist on keeping classical logic and instead stratify truth into levels or make it contextual. Each camp has a formal framework that technically 'works' in some sense, so the dispute can't be settled by just pointing at a contradiction.

My working theory — and its honestly a guess — is that this isn't a factual dispute about natural language, and it isn't even a substantive dispute about the nature of truth. I suspect it's about which core commitments each side treats as non-negotiable when a solution has to pay for itself: contradictions are never fine for the gappers; a fully transparent truth predicate is the whole point for the gluts; classical logic is sacrosanct for the classicists. But I keep flip-flopping between thinking the question is ill-posed and thinking it is just genuinely hard.

I want a diagnosis of the disagreement, not a verdict on which camp is right. Could you act as a philosopher of logic and language, think carefully, maybe brainstorm some candidate diagnoses first, and then give me your actual diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is: substantive or verbal, a difference about standards of evidence or about which cases count as data, a difference about starting intuitions, or the sides answering different questions under the single word 'truth'. Is there a hidden formal result that would settle how the costs should be weighed, or is the impasse a sign that the original question — something like 'which theory of truth is correct?' — is itself the problem? And on the back of that diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a theorem, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and why.
