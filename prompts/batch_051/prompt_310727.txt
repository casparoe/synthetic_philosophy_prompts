I'm giving a talk next month to a reading group that has just finished Gödel, Escher, Bach. My thesis is that JOOTSing — "jumping out of the system" — has been asked to carry far more weight than it can, both as an account of creativity in general and as a diagnosis of what the MU-puzzle shows. In the talk I'll use the MU-puzzle to make the point: the standard proof that MU is underivable works by finding an invariant outside the derivation rules — the number of I's in any derivable string is never divisible by three, while MU has zero I's — so it looks like a paradigmatic "jump out of the system." But I'll argue that this is no escape in any deep sense; it is ordinary reasoning about the system from within a richer system, and once that reasoning is formalized we are simply inside a larger system with its own outside. So either JOOTSing is the trivial point that you can change the rules or add a metalanguage, or it is a metaphor doing work that has not been made precise.

The audience is a mixed group: several literary-minded Hofstadter fans who are attached to the idea of transcending rules, two formal logicians who will object to any looseness about the difference between a system and a theory about a system, and one computational-creativity researcher who treats JOOTSing as a practical design heuristic. I don't expect them to go easy on the talk. Prepare me for the question period. I want the objections in roughly the order I'll actually get them, phrased the way those specific people would phrase them, with a note on which ones have real force, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the one question I should be praying nobody asks — along with the best available reply if it comes anyway.
