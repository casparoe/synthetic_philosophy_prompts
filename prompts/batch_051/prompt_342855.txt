I have been trying to get clear about what it actually means to say that everything is physical, and I keep landing in a tension I cannot dissolve. On the one hand, I find physicalism compelling for systematic reasons. It promises a single, unified ontology; it coheres with the explanatory success of the sciences; it declines to posit fundamental minds, souls, or vital forces over and above the entities studied by physics, chemistry, and biology; and it fits naturally with the apparent causal completeness of the physical world. On the other hand, I have a stubborn intuition that physicalism is a substantive, world-dividing claim: that there is a real difference between a world that is wholly physical and a world that also contains, say, a nonphysical kind of consciousness or an irreducible life-force. Notice that my intuition is not that physicalism is true. It is that the word "physical" is doing genuine classificatory work, and that the debate between physicalists and their opponents is a real debate rather than a confusion.

The trouble is Hempel's dilemma. If "physical" means whatever current physics says exists, then physicalism is almost certainly false, because physics is still developing and past physics has repeatedly been revised or extended; no one wants the doctrine to be hostage to this decade's particle catalog. But if "physical" means whatever an ideal, completed future physics would posit, then the doctrine seems to lose determinate content, since we cannot now say what such a physics would include. On that horn, physicalism appears to collapse into the claim that whatever the final theory turns out to be, that is what there is, which no longer clearly rules out the panpsychism or dualism it was supposed to exclude. Either horn seems to leave physicalism false or empty.

I do not want to be told that one horn is simply correct. I want to know how to adjudicate between the theory and the intuition. What would it take for the intuition that there is a substantive distinction here to be the thing that goes, an artifact of a prescientific picture that we should learn to drop? What would it take for physicalism as a determinate doctrine to be the thing that goes, replaced by an attitude or by some more specific thesis such as the denial of fundamental mentality? Or is there a reading of "physical" on which both survive, perhaps through a via negativa, or an account of the physical as whatever is not fundamentally mental, or some other route? Give me the strongest version of each option, say which move you would make, and explain what would justify each kind of resolution rather than simply picking a side. Please respond in American English.
