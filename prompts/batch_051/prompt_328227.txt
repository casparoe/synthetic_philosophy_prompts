Whenever someone in my circle argues that we should actually try to end aging, somebody else shuts it down with "death gives life meaning," as if that settles it. I can't tell wether they're making a point about motivation (without a deadline nobody would get anything done), about value (a life without an ending couldn't be a good story), about scarcity (things matter because there's only so much of them), or about some kind of cosmic tradeoff. Those seem like wildly different claims, and I suspect some are true but irrelevant to whether we should develop life-extension, while others would be relevant but are probably false. One thing I keep getting stuck on: what people usually point to (finishing projects, not wasting time, things feeling precious) seems to require only that some things end, not that I end; a long life is still full of endings. So I want to know whether the slogan is secretly about death specifically or just about finitude in general. Can you seperate the precise claims hiding in that one line, say which are platitudes, which are substantive but defensible, and which are just false, and then tell me which reading is actually doing the work when someone uses it against life extension? Also: if the reading doing the work is true, does it really get them all the way to "so we should reject life extension," or only to something weaker like "a longer life wouldn't automatically be a more meaningful one"?
