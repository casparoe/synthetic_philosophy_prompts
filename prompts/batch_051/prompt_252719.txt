In psych class last week we did that color phi thing where a red dot flashes on the left and a green dot flashes on the right, but everyone reports seeing one dot slide across and turn green halfway, which shouldn't be possible if you're just watching the screen in real time. Our teacher said philosophers actually argue about where the editing happens: the Orwellian story says you consciously saw the two real dots and your memory got rewritten afterwards; the Stalinesque story says the whole sequence was stitched together before it ever reached consciousness, so the moving dot is what you actually experienced. Two kids in my class went back and forth about it after class.

(1) Maya said the halfway color change gives it away: the green dot hadn't even flashed yet at the moment you seemed to see the dot turn green, so consciousness has to run a little behind the world. The brain waits for the second dot, builds the finished clip, and only then lets you see anything. You never consciously saw the two separate dots.

(2) Diego said that's overcomplicated. The simple story is that you saw exactly what was on the screen, and then your memory of the first dot got revised once the second dot appeared, because your brain wants a coherent narrative. We already know memory is reconstructive all the time, so why add a giant delay to consciousness itself?

(3) Maya came back: because at this timescale—a few hundred milliseconds, before you can ever report anything—"memory editing" would be indistinguishable from just constructing the experience. You're positing a hidden first draft of consciousness that gets instantly shredded and leaves no trace. My view only needs the one experience people actually report, so it's simpler.

(4) Diego said: simpler doesn't make it right. It is a real difference whether the red dot ever made it into consciousness as a seperate red dot and then got erased, or never made it in at all. And your view has to predict that everything we're conscious of is delayed by at least the time between the two flashes, which might even be measurable. I'd rather test that than just declare your view wins on parsimony.

They asked me to settle it and I couldn't. Can you adjudicate it move by move? For each of the four moves, say whether it's clearly mistaken, weak, decent, or clearly right, and justify each rating. Then say who is more right overall.
