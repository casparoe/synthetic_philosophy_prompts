Write a parody of a peer-review report for a fictional evolutionary psychology manuscript, around 600 words, in which a competent and enthusiastic reviewer recommends acceptance while laying out, in the reviewer's own reasoning, every reason the paper is an unfalsifiable just-so story. The manuscript explains an arbitrary modern behavior of your choosing — for instance, abandoning items in online shopping carts, rewatching a familiar show, or avoiding odd-numbered volume settings — as an adaptation shaped in the ancestral environment; give it a title in that spirit. The deadpan is the point: the reviewer never uses the phrase "just-so story" and seems not to notice the cumulative problem, but a specialist reader should hear it in the details. Work through four real moves: a worry about the assumed ancestral environment that is settled by accepting a further ad hoc assumption; praise for the model's "predictions" alongside the concession that they are compatible with nearly any observed behavior; a defense of reverse-engineering a current behavior into a past selection pressure; and an editorial recommendation that drift, by-products, cultural transmission, and ordinary proximate explanations be acknowledged only in a closing limitation. Keep the humor in the epistemology — unfalsifiability, the many-adaptive-stories problem, the gap between "would have been adaptive" and "is an adaptation" — rather than in cheap jokes about the field's style. Invented citations are fine only if they are obviously fabricated; otherwise, leave them out.
