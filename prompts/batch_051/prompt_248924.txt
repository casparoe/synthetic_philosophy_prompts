My friend Dana and I have been arguing about human enhancement for months now, and I've started keeping a mental list of where she comes down on particular cases. She was completely unbothered by her nephew getting a cochlear implant, and thought people who called it unnatural were being precious. She was also fine with a family friend who lost a hand getting a robotic replacement that's actually more dexterous than the old one. She takes a beta blocker before she gives talks and says it's no different from a good night's sleep. When I asked about a safe drug that improves working memory in healthy people, she said she'd take it, as long as it didn't change who she is. But she hated the idea of whole-brain emulation as a route to living forever: she says the upload would be a copy, and the original would still die in the old skull.

So now I've got two new cases and I want to know what she'd say, but honestly I'm more interested in the principle behind her track record. The first is a man who lost the use of his legs and wants to replace everything below his neck with a synthetic body that doesn't age and is much stronger than a biological one. The surgeons say the transition can be gradual, his brain, memories, and personality will be unchanged, but his body and face will be visibly artificial. The second is a healthy woman who wants a one-time treatment that would make her permanently more patient, less anxious, and less sharp tempered; she says these traits have blown up a marriage and she wants them gone, even though her friends say she won't quite be the person they know anymore. What would Dana say about each of those, what rule is she actually running, what's another rule that fits her earlier calls just as well, and what single further case would most cleanly separate the candidate rules? Give me your best guess about which rule is really hers and why.
