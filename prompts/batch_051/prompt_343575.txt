I'm TAing a philosophy of mind course and I've got a midterm answer to mark. Here's the question and one student's answer. Can you grade it out of 10 and give a point-by-point assessment that justifies the grade? I want a claim-by-claim breakdown, not just a general impression.

Question (philosophy of mind midterm): State Jackson's knowledge argument against physicalism. Then explain two physicalist strategies in response — the ability hypothesis and the old fact, new mode of presentation reply — and evaluate whether one of them succeeds.

Student answer:

Jackson's Mary is a colour scientist who has lived her whole life in a black-and-white room and knows every physical fact about colour vision. When she leaves and sees a red rose she learns something new: what it is like to see red. If physicalism is true then all facts are physical, so Mary already knew every fact. Since she didn't know what red was like, there is at least one non-physical fact, so physicalism is false.

The first physicalist reply is Lewis's ability hypothesis: Mary doesn't gain any new factual knowledge, only abilities — to imagine red, recognize red, and remember red experiences. The second is the old fact, new mode of presentation reply: Mary learns the same fact she already knew, but under a new mode of presentation. These two replies are really the same idea, since both deny that Mary gains a new fact. The only difference is that the ability hypothesis says what she gains is a skill, whereas the old fact, new mode reply refuses to say what she gains. Since they collapse into each other, it is enough to evaluate the ability hypothesis.

I think the ability hypothesis fails. After leaving the room Mary can answer the question "what is it like to see red?" for the first time. Answering a question expresses propositional knowledge, so she must have gained a new proposition rather than just an ability. Therefore Jackson's argument is sound, and physicalism is false.
