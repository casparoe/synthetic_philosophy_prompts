I am trying to settle on a consistent statement of my views in suffering-focused ethics, and I would be grateful for a critical audit of the following set. I find that I assent to each claim when I consider it in isolation, but I have not worked through how they interact.

(1) Suffering is the only thing that is intrinsically bad. Happiness is intrinsically good, but the avoidance of suffering is lexically prior to the promotion of happiness: no amount of happiness can compensate for, or justify, even a modest amount of suffering.

(2) Under uncertainty, the right action is the one that minimises expected suffering, where expected suffering is aggregated across intensity, duration, and the number of beings affected. The numbers count, and even very small probabilities of severe suffering should be taken seriously.

(3) Within suffering itself there is a further lexical threshold: no amount of mild suffering — a billion headaches, say — can outweigh one instance of extreme suffering, such as one person being tortured.

(4) I accept the procreation asymmetry: there is no moral reason to bring a happy being into existence, but there is a strong moral reason not to bring a suffering being into existence. The absence of a happy life is no loss; the absence of a suffering life is a gain.

Please work through the set and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension with which, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine tension, say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs, and then say which single revision would leave the position most coherent overall. Please write the answer in British English.
