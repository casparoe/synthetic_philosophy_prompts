In a seminar on statutory interpretation, a classmate met every appeal to legislative intent or statutory purpose with the same line: "The text is the law." It seemed to function as a conversation-stopper rather than an argument, and I want to know what it could actually amount to. What distinct claims might that slogan be asserting about the sources of law and the proper method of adjudication? Which of those claims are truisms, which are substantive and contestable, and which are false or overstrong? And which reading, if any, would actually settle the dispute between textualism and purposivism about whether a court may consult legislative history or statutory purpose when the text is unclear or points to an absurd result? Present the readings as a list, with your verdict on each.
