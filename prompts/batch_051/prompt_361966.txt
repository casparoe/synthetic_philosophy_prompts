A friend has argued me into a corner about the paradox of hedonism, and the argument now seems to prove far more than either of us intended, but I cannot locate the faulty step. Put compactly, his proof runs as follows. (1) If hedonism is true, pleasure is the only thing good for its own sake. (2) A rational agent should adopt as her ultimate aim whatever she takes to be the only thing good for its own sake. (3) The paradox of hedonism shows that someone who makes her own pleasure her ultimate aim—who chooses pursuits by asking how much happiness they will yield and who holds every other concern as an instrument to that end—reliably ends up with less pleasure than someone who engages with activities and persons for their own sake. (4) Therefore the rational hedonist ought to adopt other ultimate aims, as a technique: she should try to care about friendship, or her work, or music for its own sake and not merely as a route to feeling good. (5) But a sincere ultimate aim just is what one treats as good for its own sake. (6) So the rational hedonist must treat friendship, work, or music as good for their own sake, which contradicts (1). Hence hedonism rationally requires its own rejection: it is self-refuting, not merely self-effacing.

His punchline is that the proof generalizes alarmingly. By the same reasoning, the insomniac who gave up trying to fall asleep and now reads her novel wholeheartedly has adopted wakefulness as an ultimate aim; the speaker who stopped trying to be articulate and simply cares about what she is saying has adopted something other than articulate expression as her final end; and any end that is best pursued without being scrutinized thereby ceases to be the ultimate end. I would most like to reject (5), because I believe a hedonist can honestly care about her friends for their own sake while hedonism remains true at the level of justification; my friend calls this self-deception or a refusal to follow the argument where it leads. Which premise or step should be rejected, and what exactly does rejecting it cost?
