You're a political philosopher who works on democratic theory. I want you to actually think through the question and not just lay out positions—start by sketching the two or three candidate principles that could make sense of the practice, then commit to the one you think is doing the real work, and say why.

Here's the thing puzzling me. In a lot of democracies, the voting rules look like a patchwork with no single principle behind them. A non-citizen permanent resident can live in a country for decades, work, pay taxes, raise kids in its schools, and be every bit as subject to its laws as a citizen, yet often cant vote in national elections—though sometimes they can vote in local ones. Meanwhile a citizen who has lived abroad for twenty years and maybe never payed a cent of tax back home can usually keep voting in national elections from outside the country, sometimes indefinitely. Some places strip prisoners of the vote, others let them vote from prison. Some places let 16-year-olds vote, others hold the line at 18. Inside the EU, a citizen of one member state living in another can vote there in local and European Parliament elections, but not in national elections.

I'm not asking for the legal histories or a survey of countries. I want to know what picture of who belongs to the demos this patchwork presupposes—or whether it simply has no coherent view behind it. Reconstruct the implicit theory or theories that would make sense of the pattern: is the demos basically the citizenry, basically everyone subject to the laws, basically everyone whose interests are affected, basically the people with a long-term stake in the community, or some mixture? Then take a definite stand: which view comes closest to actually underwriting the pattern, and where is the practice just inconsistent rather than principled? Close by saying what would have to change if we swapped in the all-affected-interests principle as the real test of membership, and be honest about which parts of the patchwork are underdetermined—where several theories would license the same rule.
