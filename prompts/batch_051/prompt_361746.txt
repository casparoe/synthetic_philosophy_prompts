I'm a TA for an intro philosophy of mind course and I've got a midterm essay to grade on Libets experiments. The course is a mix of philosophy and cognitive science students and the rubric is pretty detailed. The exam question was: "Explain Libet's experiment and evaluate whether it shows that conscious will is an illusion." Here is what the student wrote:

Libet had subjects sit in front of a special clock and flex their wrist whenever they felt like it, while an EEG recorded their brain activity. He found that a brain signal called the readiness potential started about 550 milliseconds before the movement, but the subject's conscious intention to move, as reported by the position of the clock, came only about 200 milliseconds before the movement. So the brain was already gearing up to act around a third of a second before the person was aware of deciding. This shows that the conscious decision is not what causes the action. Libet took these results to show that free will is an illusion, and that when we think we decided to move, the brain is just producing a story about the movement after the fact. People sometimes object that these were just little wrist flexes rather than real decisions, so the experiment doesn't generalize to things like choosing a career, but for the kind of action it actually tested, the conscious mind is out of the loop.
