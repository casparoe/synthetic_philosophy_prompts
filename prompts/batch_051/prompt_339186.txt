I'm a software engineer at a defense contractor, mostly on the targeting and fire-control side of an armed drone. The requirement that keeps bugging me is that our system can find and classify targets, track them, and hand the operator a recommended firing solution, but the actual weapons release has to go through a human physically confirming it on the console. The whole UI is built around that one click — we slow the workflow down, show the operator uncertainty info, give them a veto, all so there's a human in the loop for lethal decisions. Fine. But the same company also sells a point-defense system for ships that, in automatic mode, shoots down incoming anti-ship missiles with no human go/no-go at all, because the target is moving too fast for a person to react. That gets treated as completely normal, not as a violation of whatever rule makes the human click mandatory on my project.

People around here throw out the phrase meaningful human control a lot, and I get the vibe that humans are supposed to make the moral call. What I don't get is which part is doing the work. In both systems, software makes a classification, predicts a trajectory, and triggers a firing decision; the only differences are how much time a human would have and whether the shot is defensive. If the worry is misidentification killing innocent people, that looks like a reliability problem, and the engineering fix is better sensors, validation, and testing — not bolting a required human click onto the end of a pipeline the operator barely has time to audit. If the worry is accountability, the ship-defense case seems to show we're fine with no individual human authoring the lethal action, as long as the shot is obviously justified. So what does this two-track practice actually presuppose about moral responsibility, human agency, or judgment? Is the presupposed view coherent, and what would have to change — in the systems or the rules — if you swapped it out for a simpler assesment like the weapon has to be reliable, and someone has to answer for it when it isn't?
