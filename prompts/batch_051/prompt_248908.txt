As a philosopher of physics with a long-standing interest in the metaphysics of time, I find myself pulled in two directions, and I would like you to help me think through what each direction would require me to give up.

On the one hand, I am drawn to the block-universe view by the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. The argument begins from special relativity, notes that simultaneity is frame-relative, and contends that events which lie in my future are simultaneous with, or earlier than, events in the present of some other equally legitimate observer. Since no inertial frame is privileged, it concludes that there can be no objective, frame-independent distinction between what is present, what is past, and what is future, and that past, present, and future are therefore all equally real. The view has the virtues one wants in a metaphysical theory: it is simple, unified, and it takes its ontology from a well-confirmed physical theory rather than from the parochial perspective of a particular observer.

On the other hand, I cannot shake the conviction that there is a deep difference between what has happened and what has not yet happened. It seems to me that my future choices are not already part of the furniture of the world in the way my past actions are, and that the openness of the future is not merely an artefact of my ignorance. The intuition is firm, pre-theoretic, and practically inescapable, but I also recognise that a firm intuition is not automatically a reliable guide to how things are.

I do not want you simply to pick a side. I want the strongest version of each position: the best case that the argument forces eternalism, and the best case that the intuition tracks something the argument misses — for example, the difference between determinism and eternalism, or the step in the argument that moves from 'there is a frame in which the future event is simultaneous with my present' to 'the event is real'. Then tell me what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes: what would a convincing debunking explanation of it have to look like, and would it need to show that the intuition is a product of our causal and cognitive situation rather than a guide to ontology? And tell me what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes: which premise would have to fail, and would its failure amount to rejecting special relativity, rejecting the inference from 'no privileged frame' to 'no privileged present', or something more subtle? Finally, say whether there is a reading on which both survive — for instance, by distinguishing coordinate simultaneity from metaphysical presence, or by restricting the argument to what physics alone can establish — and whether that reading is a genuine reconciliation or a face-saving equivocation. Please answer in British English.
