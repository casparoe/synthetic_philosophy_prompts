I work as a software engineer, and in my professional circles there is a lot of casual experimentation with drugs that blur the line between treatment and performance enhancement: people take SSRIs or beta-blockers to manage the anxiety of public speaking or technical interviews, and stimulants or wakefulness-promoting drugs like modafinil to sustain focus through long sprints, sometimes with a formal diagnosis and sometimes without one. In those conversations people often reach for a rough distinction: medicine should treat illness and restore a person to normal functioning, while enhancement tries to push a healthy person beyond normal. The clearest cases of enhancement used to be things like anabolic steroids in sport or purely cosmetic surgery, where the difference between repairing a deficit and going beyond normal seemed easy to see. But now the same substances are prescribed across what looks like a continuous gradient, from major depression or severe ADHD at one end to mild social unease or ordinary procrastination at the other, and the drugs act on underlying traits that do not themselves have a sharp threshold between healthy and ill. That makes me suspect the older argument rested on a premise that no longer holds: that there is a well-defined normal baseline to restore, so that restoring it and exceeding it are cleanly separable operations.

I am not sure how the distinction is formalized in philosophy, but I would like to understand whether it survives this changed situation. Specifically: does the therapy/enhancement distinction, understood as the claim that medicine properly aims at restoring normal functioning and that going beyond normal is a morally and practically different kind of intervention, survive the routine use of psychiatric drugs along continuous traits? If it does not, which premise is actually under pressure — the idea of a stable normal baseline, the idea that medical need tracks that baseline, or something else — and is there a repaired version that gives up the sharp boundary but still does useful work when deciding questions about insurance coverage, regulation, or personal conscience?
