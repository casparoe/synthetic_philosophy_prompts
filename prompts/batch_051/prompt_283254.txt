I'm a TA for a philosophy of language course and I have to grade the following take-home answer. I'd like a second set of eyes.

Question:

"Could a human language lack pronouns? Could it lack indexicals? In your answer, explain the difference between the two categories and discuss at least one candidate expression type that might do the same work."

Student's answer:

"The two categories need to be kept apart. Pronouns are a morphosyntactic category: a more or less closed class of forms that stand in for noun phrases and are marked for things like person, number, and case. Indexicals are a semantic category: expressions whose reference shifts with features of the context of utterance — 'I', 'here', 'now', and the like. So the two questions get different answers.

Could a language lack pronouns? Yes. Plenty of languages get by without a distinct pronoun class, or with a very reduced one, using demonstratives like 'this one' or 'yonder' to pick out people, plus titles and kinship terms. So the morphosyntactic category of pronoun is clearly dispensable.

Could a language lack indexicals? Here the answer is basically no, or at least not while remaining the kind of language we have. Indexicals are needed because speakers have to refer to themselves, to their addressee, and to the time and place of the utterance. If a language lacked 'I' and its kin, speakers would have to refer to themselves by proper name or description. That would be cumbersome, but in principle it would convey the same information, since a name or description picks out exactly the person 'I' would have picked out. So nothing semantic is lost; the constraint is just practical.

Sign languages seem to show the same point from the other direction. They don't have pronouns as a distinct lexical class; instead signers point to locations in signing space. Since a point is a demonstrative gesture and not a pronoun, this is a case where the relevant category is absent. So: pronouns are dispensable, and while indexicals are hard to do without, their absence would be a matter of convenience rather than semantics."

Grade this out of 10. Start with the grade as the very first sentence, then give the point-by-point comments that justify it — what the answer gets right, what it gets wrong, and exactly why. No preamble and no restating the question.
