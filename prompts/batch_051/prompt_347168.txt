I’ve been thinking about something on my own for a while, and I’d like someone to walk me through the underlying conceptual structure rather than give me historical citations or a bibliography. It concerns the way people use aesthetic language in mathematics and science. On one hand, scientists and mathematicians constantly describe theories, proofs, and explanations as beautiful, elegant, ugly, clunky, or forced, and they seem to treat that as a legitimate reason to take one option seriously and remain uneasy about another. On the other hand, it is not obvious why the way a theory feels should tell us anything about whether it corresponds to how things actually are, or whether a proof is reliable. I can see that beauty might be a sign that something is simple or symmetric or has the kind of coherence that makes it easy to work with, but I keep getting stuck on the further claim that it is evidence for truth.

What I would like is an explanation of the conceptual issue here. In particular: what exactly is being claimed when someone says elegance is evidence? Is it one claim or a bundle of several different claims, some more defensible than others? What mechanism could connect aesthetic judgment to truth in mathematics, in fundamental physics, and in less formal scientific theorizing? Where is the line between saying that beauty functions as a heuristic or selection filter and saying that it counts as evidence in its own right? And is there a sharp difference between mathematical beauty and scientific beauty here, or do the same considerations carry over?

I know there are examples where elegant theories turned out wrong and messy ones turned out right, but I am less interested in scorekeeping than in the underlying structure. If a person wants to defend the view that elegance is at least sometimes epistemically relevant, what is the most careful version of that claim, and what would have to be true for it to work? If the better view is that elegance is merely a practical guide or an aesthetic preference, say that plainly and explain how then to understand the strong role it seems to play in actual scientific and mathematical practice. I don’t have an academic background in this area, so please explain any specialized concepts as you go. Write in American English. End with a one-sentence summary as the last line.
