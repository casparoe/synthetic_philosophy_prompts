Ok so I'm a PhD student in quantum foundations, and I've been trying to write up my views on the quantum suicide/quantum immortality thought experiment before I present something on it, and I want a consistency check to make sure I haven't painted myself into a corner. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Everettian QM is right: the state just evolves unitarily, decoherence picks out the branching structure, and there's no collapse postulate tacked on.

(2) The Born rule is still the right probability rule even in a branching world: a rational agent should set credence across branches equal to the squared moduli of the branch amplitudes. I'm mostly persuaded by the decision-theoretic arguments, roughly in the Wallace neighborhood.

(3) If I hook myself up to a quantum suicide machine -- a lethal measurement where one outcome kills me and one spares me -- then as long as the "spare" branch has nonzero amplitude, I should be certain, or near certain, that I'll see myself survive. Repeating it should only ever yield "spare" from my own first-person perspective, and that looks like quantum immortality.

(4) But I also want to say: before an ordinary Stern-Gerlach or two-slit run with two macroscopic outcomes, I should not be certain of either one. My credence in "down" should be some number strictly between 0 and 1 fixed by the weights. I can't use the same "I always find myself in some branch" reasoning to become certain of one result there.

(5) After branching, my future branch-selves are numerically distinct continuers of me; neither one is singled out. So I can't say the "me" that survies is the same person as the pre-branch me in any way that secretly privileges that branch.

I keep going back and forth over whether (3) and (4) can both be true without giving up (2) or quietly switching what I mean by "I." Is this a coherent package? If not, which commitments are actually doing the conflicting work, which pair only looks inconsistent, and what's the cheapest revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equal, and don't spend a word on the basics of Everett unless it earns its keep. Keep it under 600 words.
