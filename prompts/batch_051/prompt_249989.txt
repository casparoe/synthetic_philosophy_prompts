I cover artificial intelligence for a general-interest science magazine. In my reporting on AI safety, I keep running into a distinction that everyone treats as fundamental but no one formulates precisely. One kind of system is bounded: it answers questions, summarizes documents, translates, or returns a plan, and after each response it does nothing further until a human supplies the next instruction. The other kind is given an objective and is permitted to act: it books travel, edits a code repository across many files, places bids, reorders inventory.

I can see that the second kind raises different hazards than the first. But the distinction blurs in practice, and I suspect the blur is the real story. Systems that were built as the first kind keep accumulating capabilities that push them toward the second, one feature at a time, without any announcement that their nature has changed. A question-answering interface gains a browser extension and begins following links and submitting forms. A coding assistant begins executing the code it writes. A summarizer is wrapped in an outer loop that sends its own output back as its next input until some target condition is satisfied.

I assume the two original kinds have established names, and I would be grateful to learn them. But a label is not really what I need. What I need is an account of the underlying division: does it live in the presence or absence of goals, the presence or absence of an action channel, the scope of authority the human has delegated, the system's capacity to continue without prompting, its disposition to preserve itself, or some combination? Is there a sharp difference of kind, or only a difference of degree along a spectrum whose ends are idealized? And the phenomenon that most interests me is the slide itself: a bounded system becoming an open-ended actor through the accretion of features. Does that slide have a name of its own? If it does not, what is the nearest existing concept, and how would you define a new term so that it captures the slide without simply describing any product that gains features over time?
