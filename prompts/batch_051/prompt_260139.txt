Construct a thought experiment for the discussion section of a paper on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. The case should drive apart two accounts of pleasure: the felt-quality view, on which pleasure is a distinctive positive hedonic tone common to every pleasant experience, and the attitudinal view, on which pleasure is just the attitude of liking or enjoying what one experiences. It should do so in the less familiar direction: the felt-quality view must judge that the subject is experiencing pleasure while the attitudinal view must judge that they are not. Constraints: the scenario itself must be one paragraph at most and realistic (home, workplace, clinic, or similar); no drugs, no neural intervention, and no stipulating either theory's verdict in the setup — the situation as described must generate them; the subject must be an ordinary, undeceived, competent self-reporter. After the scenario, run through the reasoning of each view, identify the structural feature of the case that does the work, give the attitudinal hedonist's strongest reply, and say whether removing that feature would collapse the case into a familiar objection or reverse the divergence.
