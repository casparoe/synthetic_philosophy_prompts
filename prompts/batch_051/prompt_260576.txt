Start by brainstorming what the strongest version of my view would look like, then give me the three strongest objections and say which of them I should treat as fatal and which just force me to modify it.

I'm calling it the Deliberative Continuity Principle. The claim is that what matters for whether I survive some procedure is not continuity of brain matter, and not even continuity of memories or personality, but the uninterrupted causal continuation of the specific capacity I have to form, revise, and abandon plans. So I survive iff the deliberative process that runs through my life is handed off continuously rather than destroyed and rebuilt. Gradual replacement of my neurons with silicon chips is fine, because each chip takes over the relevant connections while the system that does the deliberating keeps running. A destructive upload is not fine: freeze me, scan me, destroy the original, then instantiate the pattern later, and the person who wakes up merely has a fresh copy of my capacity for revising my projects, not the same capacity. I do count the capacity as continuing even when it's not active, like in dreamless sleep, so long as the underlying system that would exercise it is still there, which is why the principle doesn't kill me every night. Its like the difference between a flame passed from candle to candle and a candle that's blown out and later relit by someone who has only a record of what the old flame looked like.

It also gives me a nice line on duplication, I think: if you make two copies, neither one is me, because the original deliberative chain cant be numerically continued by two things at once.
