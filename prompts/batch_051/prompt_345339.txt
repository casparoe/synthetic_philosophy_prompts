I'm working on an Al-Ghazālī-style occasionalism for a seminar and need a consistency check before I defend it. Here's the package I keep endorsing: (i) finite created things have no causal powers of their own—God is the only real cause; (ii) what we call fire burning cotton is really God acting on the occasion of fire and cotton, following a habit he freely chose, which could in principle be suspended; (iii) this doesn't undercut science or induction, because God's habit is stable enough that expecting cotton to burn tomorrow is completely rational; (iv) the regularities his habit produces are real laws of nature, not mere constant conjunctions—they support counterfactuals (if this cotton had been in the fire, it would have burned) and genuine explanations; (v) but I also think God's acting as he does is wholly free: he could have brought about the same fire and the same cotton without any burning, and with no prior difference in the course of nature at all. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only seem to, and what's the cheapest fix for the genuine problems? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad.
