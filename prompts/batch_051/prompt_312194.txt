i keep going back and forth on a court case from a while back, the fisherman who threw undersized grouper overboard, because it feels like a near-perfect stress test for the two ways of reading laws i cant decide between. here are the facts as i understand them. after the enron mess congress passed a law aimed at financial fraud, and one part of it makes it a crime to destroy or hide a record, a document, or any other tangible thing, if you do it to obstruct an investigation. a commercial fisherman named yates got caught with undersized red grouper in the gulf of mexico. an officer enforcing the fishing rules measured them and told him to keep the fish for evidence. instead he had his crew toss them overboard. the government charged him under that anti-shredding law, on the theory that a fish is a tangible thing. the supreme court eventually said the law didn't reach the fish, but the justices couldn't agree on why, and the reasoning still feels muddled to me.

the two ways of reading laws, as i understand them, go like this. textualism says you follow the ordinary meaning of the words that actually got voted on, because that's what people had fair notice of, and what congress intended is just a mess of many people's private motives. purposivism says a law exists to fix some problem, so you read the words to serve that purpose, especially when the plain reading gives a result the law clearly wasn't after.

so take this fish case and run it through both views, carefully, one at a time. what should a textualist judge have done with it, using only textualist moves -- ordinary meaning, the surrounding words, the statute as a whole, and the old rule that you don't read a law to produce an absurd result, if textualists are allowed to use that. and what should a purposivist judge have done. then turn it around and tell me what the case does to each view. does it make textualism look like it has to smuggle in purpose to avoid sending a fisherman to federal prison over fish, or does it make textualism look like the only view with real self-control. does it make purposivism look like it gets the right answer for the right reason, or like it just lets a judge choose whichever result feels right. and are there things about the case that neither view really talks about. i don't need a winner so much as a sense of what would have to be different about the case for one of these views to actually be in trouble.
