I can't tell whether his argument is solid or whether it's one of those moves that feels tighter than it is. Here's the argument as he gave it, cleaned up and numbered:

(1) Consider a two-bitser: the coin-detecting component in a vending machine that sits in one physical state when a quarter is inserted and another when it isn't. The state that means quarter has derived intentionality only: it means that because the machine's designers and users gave it that role.
(2) Take that very same two-bitser — same internal wiring, same states — and embed it in a different environment where it reliably covaries with balboas, not quarters, and the downstream slot dispenses accordingly. The internal state hasn't changed at all, but the intentional content of the state has shifted from quarter to balboa.
(3) Therefore the intentional content of that internal state is fixed not by its intrinsic physical features, but by the external environment plus its history, design, or interpretation by outside users. The same intrinsic state can carry different content in different contexts.
(4) But exactly the same maneuver applies to whatever neural states in a human brain carry intentional content. In principle, you could keep the intrinsic neural state fixed and alter the environment or embedding so that the same state would mean something different.
(5) So if the two-bitser's intentionality is merely derived because it fails to fix its own content intrinsically, then the brain's intentionality is also merely derived. Original, intrinsic intentionality is an illusion.

He concluded that Searle's Chinese Room point is backwards: the room doesn't lack original intentionality because the symbols are derived; everything's derived.
