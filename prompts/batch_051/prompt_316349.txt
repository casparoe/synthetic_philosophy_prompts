Here's roughly where I've landed on personal identity, and it stopped being an abstract seminar topic for me this year because my mom has early Alzheimer's and people keep saying she's not herself, which I hate and also can't fully refute. I want to figure out wether my own views actually fit together. My commitments:

(1) I'm basically a Lockean: what makes me the same person over time is psychological continuity, with memory at the center of it.

(2) Reid's brave officer objection seems right to me: if the general remembers being the officer and the officer remembers being the boy, but the general doesn't remember being the boy, then strict memory can't be identity, since identity is transitive.

(3) But I also think complete amnesia doesn't end a person's existence. My mom is still my mom even on days when she remembers nothing.

(4) I think someone can be genuinely responsible for an act they did long ago and don't remember at all — like a hit and run while blacked out — and punishing them isn't punishing a different person.

(5) I think that if my psychology were copied into a new body and the original destroyed, I would survive in the new body, because what matters is that my mental life keeps going.

(6) I think a person can change so much that, in an important sense, they are not the same person they were when they were young — and I don't mean it as just a figure of speech.

Does this set hang together? Go through the pairs that pull against each other, say which ones are only apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, order whatever's left by how severe the conflict is, and tell me which single commitment I could revise to fix the most with the least cost. Don't tell me it depends — tell me which one is doing the most damage.
