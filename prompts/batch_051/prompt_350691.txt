Lately I've been thinking about prayer, partly because my mom's been sick and I've caught myself praying even though I'm not sure I believe it does anything. The thing that trips me up is the classic puzzle: if God is all-knowing and never changes, then before I asked he already knew whether I'd ask and what he was going to do about it, so my prayer can't be handing him new information or getting him to reconsider. But people still pray, and lots of thoughtful believers say the point isnt to inform or persuade God—maybe it's to change the person praying, or maybe God set things up so that the prayer itself is part of the causal story of the outcome.

Here's the parallel I keep noticing and cant decide about. The prayer problem feels structurally identical to the problem of deliberating if the block universe picture of time is right. On eternalism, tomorrow is already just as real as today, so whatever I'm about to "decide" has, in some sense, already settled which way it goes. Why agonize over a choice if the result is fixed? And yet people who accept the block universe still deliberate, and maybe they can't help it. In both cases an agent addresses a future—or a being—that is already fixed, and the act looks pointless from outside, but maybe the act is part of what fixes the outcome, or maybe its point is to change the agent rather than the world.

Is there a real structural identity here, or am I just pattern-matching on "the outcome is already settled"? If it's deep, does anything transfer from the deliberation-and-eternalism side to the prayer side, like the idea that the future depends counterfactually on what I decide even though it doesn't change? And if it's only superficial, where exactly does the analogy break?
