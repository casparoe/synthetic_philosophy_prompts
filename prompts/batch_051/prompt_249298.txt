I am a PhD student working on philosophical methodology, and I have been asked to write up the results of a long-running argument between two of my colleagues as an adversarial collaboration. One is a "principle-first" theorist who treats general principles—transitivity of betterness, dominance, simplicity as a theoretical virtue—as the primary evidence in philosophy, and who holds that intuitions about particular cases are revisable and should generally be distrusted or redescribed when they conflict with well-supported principles. The other is a "case-first" theorist who treats considered intuitions about particular cases as the bedrock evidence, and who holds that principles are at best summaries of case intuitions and remain answerable to them, so that a principle conflicting with a firm case intuition is what must give way. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should include: (a) a numbered agreed-claims section, including what each side now grants about the framing-sensitivity of case intuitions and about the legitimacy of the other's starting points; (b) a disagreements table giving, for the two or three central disputes, each side's preferred wording of its own position alongside a neutral statement of the difference; and (c) a crux section identifying the sub-questions that would genuinely move at least one side, with—for each crux—a concrete case or argument both accept as bearing on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect two cruxes will concern whether transitivity of "all things considered better than" is an unrevisable consistency constraint or a defeasible empirical generalization, and whether simplicity's good track record is evidence of truth-conduciveness or only of past convenience.
