I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. The vocabulary I actually reason with is the one I use at work: abstract data types and the distinction between an interface and its implementing representations; representation invariants; structural versus nominal typing; equality defined by an explicit equivalence relation; isomorphisms between representations; and the habit of programming to an interface rather than to a concrete representation.

Lately I have been bothered by a puzzle from the foundations of mathematics, which I keep seeing stated approximately as follows. The natural number 2 can be encoded in set theory as {∅, {∅}}, or as {{∅}}, or as the equivalence class of all two-element sets, or simply as the place after 1 in the sequence of naturals. These encodings disagree with each other—for example, on one of them 2 is an element of 4, and on another it is not—yet arithmetic goes through perfectly no matter which one is chosen. The position I keep seeing offered as a resolution says that mathematical objects are not any of those encodings: they have no hidden intrinsic nature, and are nothing over and above the role they occupy in a structure, which is why the choice of encoding is inert.

Please restate that position, the puzzle that motivates it, and the position's main commitments, in my vocabulary, as precisely and faithfully as you can. Do not smuggle in philosophical terms you have not translated into mine. Then give me a residue report: name the components that translate cleanly (with the mapping made explicit), the components that can be translated only by stretching my concepts (with the exact point where the stretch happens), and the components that have no counterpart in my toolkit at all.

The report should take seriously these candidate glosses from my side of the fence and say which, if any, is closest to the position: (a) that the debate is just implementation details leaking into an interface; (b) that isomorphic representations ought to be treated as interchangeable; (c) that the right move is to quotient a concrete representation by the equivalence relation that identifies all isomorphic copies. I want, in particular, to know whether my instinct that the whole matter reduces to 'program to the interface, not the implementation' is a faithful engineering gloss on the philosophy, or whether it flattens something the position is actually claiming—and, if so, whether the failure lies in my vocabulary or in the position.
