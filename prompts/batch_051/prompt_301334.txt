I'm taking a seminar this term on whether companies can be morally blameworthy — not just legally on the hook, but actually blameable in the way people are — and I want a handout-style thing I can use for the final paper. My instructor keeps saying that before you defend or attack corporate blame, you need to say what a theory of it would have to accomplish, and I keep going back and forth on which demands are real and which are just one camp's wishlist. So I want a list of desiderata: the requirements an account of corporate blameworthiness has to meet to count as adequate, with the case or consideration behind each. Adequate for what: for saying whether and when it's appropriate to blame a company as distinct from (or in addition to) its members — the kind of judgment you'd need before arguing that a fine, a boycott, or public condemnation of a firm is deserved.

The theory has to handle a few phenomena I keep coming back to. The big one is the diffusion case: a disaster where no single person knew enough or intended the outcome, a bunch of individually blameless-ish decisions add up to it, and yet it seems wrong to say "nobody is to blame" because the company plainly was. There's also the persistence case, where we blame the company now for something it did before anyone currently there worked there. There's the scaling question, whether the same account covers a two-person partnership and a multinational in one piece or whether those need seperate accounts. There's the toxic-culture-versus-bad-apple distinction, and the fact that ordinary people feel genuine indignation toward companies, not just toward the CEO. I want those worked into the requirements, not just listed as cases: each requirement should be a test the theory has to pass, with the reasoning behind it.

And please, for each requirement or at least the contested ones, mark whether its non-negotiable or a camp preference. The camps I'm picturing are the individualists (corporate blame is really shorthand for blaming the individuals involved), the group-agency people (the company has its own intentions and can be blamed in its own right, with no spooky group mind), and the people who think blaming a corporation is a category mistake or just a legal fiction. Some demands seem mandatory to me: distinguishing moral blame from legal liability and from mere causal responsibility; saying what corporate mens rea comes to when no individual has the relevant knowledge or intent; avoiding double counting when both the firm and its members are blamed; and not explaining corporate blame by pretending the company literally feels guilt. I'm less sure whether "our reactive attitudes toward companies have to come out apt rather than mistaken" is a real requirement or just a preference of one camp.

The part I most want to see is where the list breaks. At least three demands look like they might not be jointly satisfiable: (a) only things with culpable mental states can be morally blameworthy; (b) in diffusion cases the company is blameworthy even though no member has the culpable mental state; (c) group states are nothing over and above member states. It seems like you can keep any two but maybe not all three, unless I'm missing something. There's also the worry that if blame requires the blamed thing to be able to feel guilt or answer back, and a company can't, then either corporate blame isn't real moral blame, or the indignation people feel toward companies isn't fitting, or that requirement is wrong. Please sort out which subsets are jointly unsatisfiable, what someone who wants corporate blame has to give up, and which single requirement does the most work to exclude candidates. That last part is the thing I'll actually use to structure my paper.
