I write about science and ideas for a living, which means I keep a file of analogies I can't fully cash out. Here's the one currently stuck in my head.

There's a decision-theory idea called evidential cooperation in large worlds. The rough version: in a world with enormously many agents — or a bunch of near-twins running the same reasoning — your move in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma carries information about what all those similiar agents will do. Cooperate, and you should expect them to cooperate; defect, and you should expect them to defect. The usual dominance argument for defecting depends on the other player's move being fixed independently of yours, and in this setup it isn't. So the rational move can be to cooperate, not because of reputation or a desire to be nice, but because you'd rather live in the world where your decision procedure is the norm.

The first time I read this, it set off a bell. It's doing at least superficially what Kant's categorical imperative does — asking whether the rule behind your action could be willed as a law for everyone, with no personal exemption. Both moves take a first-person calculation about one act and smugle it up a level into a choice of universal policy, then treat your own act as one instance of that policy. In ECL, "what if everybody did that?" becomes an expectation about actual correlated reasoners; in Kant, it's a test of whether the maxim is coherent to will universally. The coupling is different — evidential correlation versus the consistency of a rational will — but the shape of the reasoning is the same: don't ask what's best for this move, ask which universal policy you'd sign for and then act as its representative.

I can't decide whether this is a real structural identity or just the word "universal" doing all the work. If it's deep, does ECL give the categorical imperative a decision-theoretic spine, or does Kant add something ECL can't talk about? If it's superficial, where's the first place the mapping snaps, and what would have to be true for the two things to be versions of one result?
