For years my brother has been justifying what he expects from us toward our aging parents with the same picture: our parents sank capital into us for two decades—time, money, forbearance, forgone alternatives—and now that we are adults the investment has matured and the returns are due. On his view we owe them specific performances: regular visits, help with the house, letting them steer decisions about their own care, and financial support when their savings fall short, not as gifts but as repayment. I have been reading Jane English's work on filial obligation, and she tries to loosen the grip of exactly this picture by arguing that what grown children owe their parents is closer to what friends owe each other: an open-ended responsiveness to the relationship as it now is, not a debt generated by benefits received in childhood.

I want a careful audit of the investment-or-debt analogy itself, with English's friendship model as the contrast rather than a verdict on my family. Please lay out the mapping: in the analogy, what corresponds to the principal, the interest, the term of the investment, the investor, the asset, and the payout? Then identify the points where the mapping breaks. Which of those disanalogies matter for the work the analogy is doing—namely, converting parental sacrifices in the past into determinate and binding obligations in the present—and which are merely superficial? I am especially interested in what the analogy quietly imports that the family relationship may lack: a voluntary contract, commensurable benefits, the possibility of fully discharging the debt, and an obligation that remains in force regardless of how the relationship now stands.

Finally, tell me whether the conclusion my brother draws—that specific forms of support are owed as repayment independently of the present quality of the relationship—survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Does the friendship model carry the weight of real filial obligations, and is there a better analogy that captures both the history of the relationship and its present quality?
