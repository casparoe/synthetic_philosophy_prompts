I'm trying to put together a list of genuinely different solutions to the statue-and-clay problem, and I want one more I haven't thought of. The setup is the usual one: a lump of clay (Lumpl) is shaped into a statue (Goliath), and they coincide for Goliath's entire existence. But Lumpl can survive being squashed and Goliath can't, so by Leibniz's law they seem to have different properties and therefore can't be identical — which seems to land you with two material objects in exactly the same place. So far my list has: (1) the constitution view, where Lumpl and Goliath are two distinct objects that coincide, and Lumpl constitutes Goliath; (2) the dominant-kind view, where there is really one object there and one sortal wins, so the other kind term is a phase sortal with no persistence conditions of its own; (3) the four-dimensionalist view, where the two are distinct spacetime worms that overlap in a shared temporal part now but diverge when the squashing happens; (4) the eliminativist view, which dissolves the puzzle by denying that statues exist (or denying that lumps do); and (5) the relative-identity view, on which it's only well-formed to ask whether something is the same F as something else, so "is Lumpl identical to Goliath?" simpliciter is a bad question. Give me a sixth solution that is not on that list and not just a variant, special case, or combination of the ones listed. It should actually address the modal persistence puzzle — same matter, different persistence conditions — rather than talk around it. Then tell me honestly whether you think this sixth solution is any good, and which of my five it's closest to and why it's still genuinely different.
