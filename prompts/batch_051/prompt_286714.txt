I'm giving the opening ten minutes at a small evening meetup called 'AI and care,' and I'm honestly not sure how I got picked — I'm a foster parent, not any kind of academic, and the organizer saw a long thread I wrote comparing training a kid to training a model and thought it would spark discussion. They've paired me with a roboticist who's whole thing is that 'guardian' is a sentimental word for a training pipeline. I want to spend my time on one question I can't stop chewing on: if AIs ever end up raising other AIs — teaching them what to do, correcting them, deciding when they're ready to act unsupervised — is parenthood the right frame, or is it closer to apprenticeship, or just revision control with extra steps? And does it change things if the 'child' can rewrite its own reward function and could outlive the 'parent'? The room will be half engineers who'll walk out if I get mushy, and half social workers and foster parents who'll walk out if I turn care into a pipeline. Write the talk so that question — what 'raising' could even mean between artificial agents — does the real structural work, and don't let me dodge the places where the analogy snaps.
