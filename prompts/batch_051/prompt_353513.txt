i'm in a course on ai interpretability and the philosophy of mind and i've noticed that everyone talks about one model "interpreting" another but nobody will say what the word actually picks out. i want a working definition of "x interprets y" that doesn't just collapse into "predicts y" or "describes y in english." i've got a list of cases to test it on from a group project, and i'd like the definition run against all of them honestly:

- a lookup table that has the right output for every input in the training distribution. it always predicts correctly, but nobody thinks it interprets itself. (clear no)
- a human diagnostician who opens a model up, points to the layer that erased the earlier context, says why it failed on a long-conversation prompt, and can fix it. (clear yes)
- a saliency map that highlights the input pixels that most changed the output. it helps someone predict what the model will misclassify, but there's no causal story.
- a language model that gives a fluent, step-by-step explanation of why another model answered a question the way it did, except when you actually intervene on the internal states it cites, nothing changes.
- a small probe network trained on a big model's activations. it gets 95% on a held-out task that the probe itself can't do from raw inputs. does the probe interpret the big model?
- an attention head that reliably fires on syntactic number agreement, so we can now predict when the model will make subject-verb errors. is discovering that head interpretation?
- one llm criticizes another llm's reasoning, and the second llm gets better. is the first interpreting the second, or just steering it?
- the recursive one: model a interprets model b, and model c interprets a's interpretation. have we learned anything about b, or just about a?

i want the definition run through each case, plus a note on which verdicts you're least sure about and whether any of the clear cases has to get sacrificed to keep the definition simple. and then the kicker: if the probe and the attention head don't count as interpretation under the final definition, what does that do to the hope for fully automated, recursive interpretability? maybe the answer is that interpretation only starts once you can answer counterfactual questions and intervene, but i dont want to assume that at the outset.
