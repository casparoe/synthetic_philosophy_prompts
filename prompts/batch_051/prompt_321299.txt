In his 1942 report *Social Insurance and Allied Services*, William Beveridge writes:

‘The State in organising security should not stifle incentive, opportunity, responsibility ; in establishing a national minimum, it should leave room and encouragement for voluntary action by each individual to provide more than that minimum for himself and his family.’

Write an essay on how far this principle can be reconciled with the use of work-related conditionality in unemployment assistance. In particular, your essay should address whether a welfare state can require claimants to seek or accept work as a condition of continued benefit without treating those who fail to comply as undeserving of unconditional support. Take seriously both the reading of Beveridge that sees such conditionality as protecting the responsibility he names, and the reading that sees punitive conditionality as undermining the security that social insurance is meant to provide. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it.
