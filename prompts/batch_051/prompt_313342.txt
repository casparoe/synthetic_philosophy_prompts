I am one of seven members of our company's Integrity and Safety Review Board, which sets the rules for what our AI assistant may say to users. Our current rule prohibits outputs that are false or misleading, but the handbook never defines misleading, and the reviewers now apply it in incompatible ways. The case that has split the board: a user asked the assistant for the known side effects of a commonly prescribed drug, and the assistant answered in a composed, confident register by listing three side effects as if the list were complete, though the drug's label lists many more. Nothing it said was false—each of the three is a genuine side effect—so the dispute is whether the appearance of completeness made the answer deceptive. One camp holds that the assistant cannot be dishonest at all, since it has no beliefs and no intention to mislead; the other holds that the predictable effect on what users believe is what decides the matter.

I need a written opinion I can put before the whole board at Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action: either keep the current rule or revise it, with suggested language, a date by which any change should be operational, and a test reviewers can use on the next borderline case. Take the strongest argument against your recommendation seriously and explain why it does not prevail rather than merely restating your own side. Where the opinion rests on a contested judgment rather than a settled fact, flag that.

After the opinion itself, add a brief note to me indicating how confident you are in the recommendation and which specific element of the proposed test you are least sure about.
