Two of my friends got into a long argument after a director they both loved turned out to have been a predator on set. One said the films are still great: the craft stuff—timing, shot choice, structure—is in the film itself, and treating facts about the maker as aesthetic defects in the work is just the genetic fallacy. The other said that maybe works for some films, but for films whose whole appeal is the director's humane, generous sensibility, learning what he did changes what the film expresses—it now reads as a performance of decency, so the film really is worse as art, not just tainted. The first one shot back that that's a change in the viewer, not in the film; someone who never found out would see the same expressive content, so the second is evaluating their own psychology. The second came back with: art is individuated by its history—a shot-for-shot remake by someone with no such biography would be a different film—so you can't test separating the work from the artist by pretending the biography away. The first said then a film's quality would change whenever public knowledge changes, which means you've stopped evaluating the film and started evaluating its reception. The second said no, a joke can be a confession whether or not you know it; your earlier judgment was just incomplete. Go through the exchange and say who is right about which specific point, not just who wins overall, and say what both of them are missing.
