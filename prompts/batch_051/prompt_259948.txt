In case the term is fuzzy: a Bongard problem is two columns of simple line drawings, six on the left and six on the right, and the task is to say what rule separates the six on the left from the six on the right. The striking thing is that solvers often find the rule from just those twelve drawings even though the space of possible rules is enormous; rules can pick out geometric relations, counts, relative sizes, orientations, same/different, and weird disjunctive stuff.
