So our school board just passed a rule that starting next fall all freshman and JV teams have to be no-cut: anyone who shows up for tryouts is on the roster. I was in the gym after the announcement and Coach D and Coach M, both of whom have coached freshman basketball, got into it in front of the whole gym class. I wrote down the back and forth as best I could remember it. Coach D is the one who thinks the policy is too soft; Coach M is the one who thinks cuts do more damage than they're worth.

(1) Coach D said: "This is a mistake. Competition builds character. Kids need to learn that spots are earned, that losing is survivable, and that when you get cut you either work harder or find something your actually good at. A no-cut team is just one big participation trophy."

(2) Coach M said: "But that's a story about the kids who survive the cut. For the kid who gets cut, the lesson is basically 'you don't measure up.' That's not character, that's corrosion — and at fourteen most kids don't have the equipment to turn it into motivation. Character, if it gets built, comes from a coach who actually invests in you. And a no-cut team still has practices, losses, and bench-warming, so nobody's removing disappointment; they're just removing the part where we tell a kid he doesn't belong."

(3) Coach D said: "Losing a game is not the same as not making the team. A game loss is shared; a cut is personal, and that personal sting is exactly what kids need practice with, because adult life is full of not getting the job or not being picked. If we manage out every sting at fourteen, we're not protecting them, we're delaying it. Plus I've seen plenty of cut kids come back a year later with a jump shot and a chip on their shoulder. It lit a fire under them."

(4) Coach M said: "See, this is where you lose me. The kids who come back with a chip on their shoulder are the ones it worked for — the ones who quietly quit gym class are the corrosion your not counting. That's survivorship bias. And you've still just asserted that personal pain builds character, but I could just as easily say it drains confidence and makes a kid stop trying things, because I've seen alot more of those. You need to tell me what the actual mechanism is, not just point at the kids who happened to survive."

So I genuinely can't tell who's winning. Can you go through this exchange point by point and say for each numbered move whether it's weak or strong and why? Then say who you think is more right overall about no-cut freshman teams, and whether there's a stronger version of either coach's position that they didn't quite manage to state.
