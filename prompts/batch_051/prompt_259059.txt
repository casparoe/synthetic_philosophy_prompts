'Aumann's agreement theorem says that two ideal rational agents who share the same prior cannot agree to disagree about any proposition. If each of them updates on their own private evidence, and each then becomes genuinely aware, in the full common-knowledge way, of the other's posterior probability for that proposition, those two posterior probabilities have to be identical. Because of this, the theorem is standardly taken to show that rational disagreement is impossible: whenever two smart, honest people compare notes and still find themselves disagreeing, at least one of them has made a mistake. A related prediction is that if people simply publish their current credences, their opinions will gradually converge over time.'
