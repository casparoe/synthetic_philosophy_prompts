I spent thirty years doing instrumentation in an atmospheric chemistry lab, so I know enough about control loops to know this boundary is not nearly as clean as people make it sound. Lately I've been reading philosophy of action and some of the better essays on autonomous weapons, and I keep getting stuck on a clause from a rules-of-engagement card that was written for a unit flying remotely piloted aircraft. The card says: "The decision to employ lethal force is a human decision. No weapon system will be employed in a mode that selects and engages targets automatically, without a human operator having authorized the specific engagement."

The card was written before loitering munitions were a thing; it's authors were picturing a person watching a screen and pulling a trigger. The same unit now flies a loitering munition. Before release, the operator authorizes a geographic kill box and a target profile—persons observed carrying rifles and moving toward a checkpoint, say. The munition then circles for hours and, on its own, picks out whichever vehicle or person in the box best matches the profile and fires. The operator is not watching the feed at the moment of selection, and by the time the video comes up the strike is already over. The only thing he decided was upstream: a box and a profile, not this person being engaged.

Does the clause reach this? I'm less interested in a policy recommendation than in how the readings come apart. Go at it from the plain words, from the purpose of the clause (the drafters clearly wanted meaningful human control, not merely a human in the loop as decoration), from what the drafters would likely have said if they'd been asked about loitering munitions, and from what a reasonable operator in that unit would take the card to mean. Then tell me which approach should win here and why. And be straight with me: if the clause is just underdetermined, or if my framing assumes a clean line between human decisions and machine decisions and that line collapses once you look at the details, say so plainly rather than humoring me. I'd also like to know whether "authorizing the specific engagement" can actually bear the weight it's being asked to bear here, or whether the phrase is doing whatever work we need it to do because the facts have outrun it.
