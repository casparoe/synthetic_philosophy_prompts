I've been thinking about Van Fraassen's Reflection principle, roughly: your credence in a proposition now should equal the expected value of the credence you'll have later, so if you're sure that tomorrow you'll be 80% on A, you should be 80% today; otherwise you'd be planning to end up with an opinion you currently regard as wrong. As a constraint on coherence across time this is really elegant — it unifies a bunch of messy cases about changing your mind and gives a tidy rule for planning. But I also have a firm intuition I can't shake: if I know that by tonight I'll be sleep-deprived, or will have forgotten the decisive piece of evidence, or will have just sat through a slick but misleading talk, then my prediction that I'll be 90% doesn't tell me to be 90% now — it tells me to lock in my current view before I get worse. Which should yield?
