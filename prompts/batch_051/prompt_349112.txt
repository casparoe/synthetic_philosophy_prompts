I've been trying to get clear on the "low impact" / "avoiding side effects" problem in AI alignment, and I keep running into the same four or five technical moves. I want to collect the actual proposals for defining or measuring side effects such that an agent can be penalized or constrained automatically — not corrigibility or off-switch stuff, and not "put a human in the loop to approve every action," which I realize solves a different problem.

Here's my list so far:

1. Attainable utility preservation — keep a set of auxiliary reward functions around, estimate how much total value each could still get from the resulting state, and penalize the agent whenever its action reduces that compared with a do-nothing baseline. The agent gets punished for foreclosing options it wasn't even asked about.
2. Relative reachability — instead of utility, compare which future states are reachable from the post-action state versus from the baseline/inaction state, and penalize the agent for making states less reachable. Similar in spirit to (1), but the thing being preserved is reachability rather than utility.
3. State-distance penalties — put a cost on the distance between the actual resulting state and the state that would have occurred under a default or no-op policy, in whatever feature representation the designer picks.
4. Whitelisting/blacklisting of variables — declare some variables protected or bounded, and define a side effect as moving any of them outside the allowed range.
5. Partial-order constraints — don't give the agent a full utility function over everything; give it only a partial ordering over outcomes, like a set of Pareto comparisons, and forbid any change that makes things worse along that ordering.

I'm fairly sure this is basically the standard menu. What I'd like is a sixth family of proposal I'm not seeing — genuinely not on this list, and not a rewording, special case, or combination of the above. If you think the list is already complete, say so, but then still give me the closest thing that isn't on it. And then be honest about the part I actually care about: does your sixth proposal — or any of the five — actually succeed in defining "side effect" without a full specification of value, or does each one smuggle in something value-shaped at the moment you choose the measure, the features, the protected variables, or the partial order?

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence: name the sixth proposal (or say the list is complete) and give your verdict on the smuggling problem. Then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
