AI labs frequently defend a model's safety by citing average-case results: the model refuses a benchmarked set of harmful requests at, say, a 98% rate. Red-teamers reply that with the right adversarial rephrasing, the model can be steered into producing exactly the harmful behavior that the other 2% represents. What theory of safety — and more broadly, what view of how to reason under uncertainty — does the benchmark-averaging practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology, contrast it with the worst-case, adversarial conception of safety that red-teaming presupposes, say whether a lab can coherently hold both at once, and spell out how a lab's evaluation, release, and monitoring procedures would have to change if worst-case failures were treated as the primary object of the safety guarantee rather than an acceptable residue of aggregate performance.
