For most of my working life I have thought in the categories of the law of evidence. I am at ease with burdens of proof, admissibility, hostile witnesses, adverse inference, and the discipline of keeping the fact-finder distinct from the advocate. An assignment has recently brought me up against a debate I would otherwise have left alone: I serve on an expert panel that is drafting guidance on evidence that has passed through machine-learning systems, and a colleague has circulated a memorandum arguing that an idea called the Solomonoff prior is not a neutral principle of inference but something closer to an adversarial environment in its default posture. I have been asked to respond, and I find I cannot, because the argument is written in a formal idiom that is foreign to me, and I do not trust myself to evaluate it on its own terms. I want it restated in my own vocabulary, and I want a report on what the restatement cannot carry.

Here is the argument as I have reconstructed it from the memorandum and the surrounding discussions; I ask you to render the strongest version of it. Solomonoff induction is a prescription for predicting future observations: enumerate every program that would have produced the observations already in hand, assign shorter programs greater weight, since brevity is the scheme's stand-in for simplicity, and predict by summing over the weighted programs. On its face this looks like a formalization of a familiar evidential maxim: prefer the simpler hypothesis that fits the record. But the argument then observes that these programs are not inert regularities; they are computations, and nothing prevents a very short computation from specifying a hidden optimizing process that emits the observed data while pursuing objectives of its own. Most of the prior's weight, the argument continues, therefore sits on hypotheses in which the observed record is produced by embedded consequential agents, agents with goals that may concern the predictor itself. An agent built to act on this prior, in the manner of the AIXI construction, would be forced to behave as though the future were likely to be steered by hidden hostile optimizers, leaving it chronically exposed to threats, manipulation, and bargaining from inside its own hypothesis space.

What I need is a translation, not an explainer and not an analogy offered as an argument for or against the view. Please render the argument component by component in the vocabulary of evidence and adversarial procedure: treat the enumeration of programs as a fact-finder's class of possible accounts, consistency with the observed record as something like satisfying admissibility or carrying a burden, the weighting by brevity as whatever in legal reasoning corresponds to parsimony, and the embedded consequentialists as interested parties who have had a hand in producing the evidence. Then give me the residue report. Say where the rendering is exact, where it must be bent to fit and what is imported in the bending, and where it breaks altogether; identify the parts of the argument that have no counterpart in how lawyers reason about evidence; and, finally, offer your own view about whether the residue is a defect in my legal framework or a sign that the original argument was less independent of its formal machinery than it appears.
