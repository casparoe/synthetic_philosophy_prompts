You are a professional philosopher specialising in normative ethics, formal theories of value, and the measurement of well-being, and you are serving as the neutral drafter in an adversarial collaboration. Begin by thinking carefully and step by step; before you finalise the document, go through it sentence by sentence and check that neither camp could reject the wording as slanted or question-begging, then present the finished document only. Do not adjudicate between the two camps; the deliverable is the jointly signable document itself.

Draft the adversarial-collaboration document that a committed lexical-threshold negative utilitarian and a committed commensurabilist utilitarian could both sign in good faith after a long period of talking past each other.

You may refine the following working characterisations inside the document, but the document must state each side's view in wording that side would accept. The lexical-threshold negative utilitarian holds, roughly, that there is a threshold of suffering, in intensity, duration, or both, such that preventing or relieving suffering at or above that threshold has lexical priority over promoting any amount of positive value: no quantity of pleasure, preference satisfaction, or other goods can compensate for allowing or inflicting suffering above the threshold. Below the threshold, pain may still outweigh pleasure, but not lexically. The view denies that intense pain and positive well-being are commensurable in the way an additive utility calculus assumes. The commensurabilist utilitarian holds, roughly, that pain and pleasure are positive and negative magnitudes on a single scale of value, so that for any finite episode of suffering there exists some amount of positive well-being sufficient to compensate, and that trade-offs across persons are in principle legitimate and sometimes required.

(a) A case pitting one person's suffering against dispersed small benefits across many people, designed to show whether the two camps divide over the difference between within-person and across-person compensation, over the permissibility of aggregation, or over both.
