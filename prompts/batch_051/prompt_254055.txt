I had a health scare this spring — a scan that "could not rule out" something bad — and I've been noticing that the usual "learn a fact, update everything" story doesn't fit what actually happens when the news is mushy. I've collected my own verdicts on some cases and I want to know if there's a principle behind them, or whether I have to throw a few out.

Case 1: Reading the ambiguous scan bumped my confidence in the bad outcome from around 10% to around 35%, and I never became certain of any exact finding. That felt rational, and it felt like the move was the right size, not just vibes.

Case 2: Re-reading the same report later moved me almost not at all. Same evidence, so no extra effect. Fine.

Case 3: The next week I noticed a swollen lymph node, which felt like it should move me from 35% to around 45%. My doctor put it in terms I liked: pick the propositions you're really torn between, shift their probabilities, then re-weight everything else using the conditional probabilities you already had. That sounds right in this case.

Case 4: But then my radiologist friend told me the scan showed only a "subtle asymmetry," and that if the bad outcome were true the scan usually looks a lot worse. This felt different from Case 1. Here the fuzziness itself is informative: I'm not just unsure what the report says; being told it's subtle should change how I relate the scan result to the disease. In this case "keep your conditional probabilities fixed and just shift the partition" strikes me as wrong.

Case 5: Seperate wrinkle. A grainy doorbell video made me 80% sure my neighbor's car was in my driveway after midnight. Then he texted that he was out of town. Undoing the update didn't feel like sliding back down the same ramp; the video now meant something else to me. Order seemed to matter.

Is there a clean principle that captures all of this? I half-remember hearing something about updating on an "uncertain evidence partition" while holding "rigidity" fixed. Does that survive these cases, and what would it say about two different mushy observations that leave me at the same final number for a conclusion but still feel like they should differ somehow?
