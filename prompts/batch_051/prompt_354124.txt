I am taking a course on bivalence and future contingents, and I have been turning over the debate about whether future-tensed statements are determinately true or false. I would like to read Denis Villeneuve’s film Arrival through that lens. The film’s plot turns on a linguist who learns the heptapods’ written language and comes to experience time non-linearly: she has determinate-seeming knowledge of future events, including her daughter’s illness and death, and she acts on that knowledge in the present, sometimes through causal loops in which the future helps bring itself about. Does the film commit itself to the view that future contingents already have truth values, or does it dramatize the rival position that there are truth-value gaps for contingent futures? More specifically, when Louise foresees her daughter, is the content of her vision presented as what will be true no matter what, or as one branch of an open future? Where does the film conflate epistemic access to the future with the future’s already being true, and where, if anywhere, does it register the difference? I want a reading that takes the film’s temporal mechanics seriously as a thought experiment, but that also keeps the logical distinction between knowing the future and the future’s being already true in view. It would help if the reading ended by saying what the film adds to the philosophical debate that the standard papers on future contingents do not.
