I keep ending up in arguments about AI pause proposals where nobody has actually put a number on anything, and I want to fix that for myself. The proposal I want to scrutinize is deliberately narrow: starting tomorrow, the major labs and governments agree to a five-year moratorium on new training runs above about 10^26 FLOP, with real inspections and actual enforcement, while safety research, evals, interpretability, and smaller-scale experiments continue. My question is: what is your credence that this pause would, in expectation through 2050, reduce catastrophic or existential risk from AI compared to the no-pause baseline? Please don't just give me the final number. Decompose it — at minimum, (a) the probability that frontier training runs are a major driver of the relevant risk in the first place, (b) conditional on that, the probability the pause actually holds rather then leaking through defection, offshoring, or clever workarounds, (c) conditional on that, the probability the five years get used for meaningful safety progress rather than just delaying the same risk, and (d) conditional on that, the probability the pause doesn't do serious countervailing harm by, say, entrenching the leading labs or pushing capability work into less legible places. Give a rough number and a line of justification for each, then show how they combine.

Then I want the same treatment in reverse: which of those terms is your final credence most sensitive to, and where do you think someone at 0.3 and someone at 0.7 on the overall question most likely actually diverge? And be self-critical about it — what specific evidence would move your number the most, and which step in your reasoning do you think is most likely to be wrong?
