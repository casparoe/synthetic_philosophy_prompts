Here's a standoff I keep running into both in the well-being literature and in AI alignment, and it's been frozen for as long as I can tell. One side says your 'real' wants are the informed-desire ones: what you'd want if you had all the relevant empirical facts and no false beliefs. The other side says that's cheating, because dumping all that information into your head can just make you into a different person; what's genuinely yours is what you'd still want after reasoning perfectly from the desires you actually have, flaws and all. Then the coherent-extrapolated-volition people reject both: it's not enough to be informed, since a fully informed bully is still a bully, and not enough to be rational, since a perfectly rational bad actor just pursues bad ends more efficiently. They want to idealize toward something like what I'd want if I knew more, thought faster, and were more the person I wished I was. Everyone agrees the three can diverge, and then everyone just restates their own account. My suspicion is that this isn't really an empirical dispute, to be settled by finding out what a fully informed person would in fact want; it's a dispute about which alterations of a person still count as that same person. Is that right? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, two different questions sharing a phrase, or something else — and given the diagnosis, say what would count as progress, or whether the question in this form should just be dropped.
