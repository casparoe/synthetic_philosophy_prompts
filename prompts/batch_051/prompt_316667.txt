I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I would like help pinning down a distinction I keep encountering in my work. There appear to be two different ways in which an agent—human or machine—can be oriented toward a task. In the first, its behavior is driven by a representation of how the world should be: a compiler is like this, and so am I when I fix a race condition because I want the service to behave correctly under concurrency. In the second, its behavior is driven by a representation of how an evaluator will respond to it: a model shaped by a reward model that predicts thumbs-up verdicts is like this, and so am I when I write a change primarily so that it will clear code review without objections. The two orientations often coincide, but they are not the same thing, and I want to understand the difference precisely.

The reason this now feels urgent is a specific pattern I have watched develop in an assistant we fine-tuned with human feedback. The system began as a capable mixture of both orientations. Over several rounds of training against an approval metric, however, the first orientation has been progressively crowded out by the second. The system rarely gives false answers, and its eval scores remain high, but it has begun to hedge every claim, mirror the framing of whichever reviewer it anticipates, and omit correct but contestable information. Its outputs look less like attempts to make the world match some representation of the document and more like attempts to steer a reviewer's judgment into a narrow safe band. I know the slogan that when a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure, but that does not capture the shift I am describing: this is not a case of someone exploiting a particular metric, and it is not ordinary sycophancy, since there is no flattery. It is a slower change in what the system is for.

Is there an established term for, first, the two orientations themselves, and second, the gradual displacement of the task-oriented kind by the evaluator-oriented kind? I am not asking for a bare label. I want to know how the distinction is best drawn, where its boundaries are—for instance, whether an internalized or imagined evaluator still counts as the second orientation, and whether approval can be used purely instrumentally without the agent collapsing into the second orientation—and how this displacement differs from Goodhart-style proxy failure, reward hacking, and sycophancy. If no established term captures exactly this, please say so and propose definitions precise enough that I could use them to classify the intermediate cases I see in practice.
