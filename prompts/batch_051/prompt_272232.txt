I spent way too long on the phone last night with my friend about intuition pumps—how a thought experiment can either isolate a real principle or just shove our emotions around. My friend has a long list of strong calls on classic cases, and they seem consistent, but I cant quite tell which consistency it is.

Here's there track record. They approve of the standard switch trolley case as a fair pump. They also approve of the violinist case (waking up with your kidneys hooked up to a famous violinist for nine months), and they approve of Mary's room, where a color scientist who knows every physical fact about color sees red for the first time. On the negative side, they say the footbridge trolley case is rigged because the physical shove is doing the persuasive work rather than the principle. They say the Chinese room is rigged because the setup already assumes the only candidate for understanding is the person in the room, so the conclusion is baked in. And they say the utility monster is rigged too—calling it a "monster" and letting one being's utility swamp everyone else's is stacking the deck.

Now the case we where arguing about: someone proposes a new version of the switch case. It is mechanically identical—flip the lever, one dies, five live—but the setup spends a full paragraph describing the single person on the side track as a beloved hospice nurse and mother of three who bakes bread for her neighbors, while the five are introduced only as "five people" and nothing else. The question is still whether it is permissible to flip the switch.

If I want to predict what my friend would say, what are the plausible rules behind their old calls, which of their old calls is the closest analog, and what does each candidate rule predict about the new case? And if their track record genuinely underdetermines the answer, tell me which prediction I should put money on and why, and give me the cleanest further case that would settle which rule they are actually using.
