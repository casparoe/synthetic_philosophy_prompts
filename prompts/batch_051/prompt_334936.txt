I'm the officially neutral third party in a year-long standoff between two people in my department, and I've been told to produce the document neither of them will admit to wanting. One is a high-energy theorist who thinks the only real ontology is the UV completion; the other is a condensed matter type who treats the renormalization group as the end of the story and says the low-energy theory is all that exists. They have now agreed to do an adversarial-collaboration writeup, and they're letting me (or making me) draft it. Could you write the thing they'd both sign?

The two positions, as they describe themselves: (a) the UV fundamentalist says an effective field theory is just a truncated approximation to a deeper theory, and decoupling is a calculational convenience, not a fact about what's real; (b) the IR autonomist says a low-energy EFT is complete and self-contained for all predictions at its own scale, the UV completion is underdetermined by all possible low-energy data, and in an important sense it doesn't need to exist. Please structure it as: (1) a list of substantive claims both can sign about Wilsonian RG flow (the business of integrating out high-momentum modes step by step and watching couplings run), the Appelquist-Carazzone decoupling theorem (heavy fields drop out of low-energy predictions except through renormalized couplings), universality (very different microphysics can flow to the same long-distance theory), and cutoff independence — no truisms both sides would just yawn at; (2) a seperate table of their exact disagreements, worded so each camp accepts its own row; (3) the two or three cruxes where new evidence or argument would actually move at least one of them — I have a hunch one crux is whether anomalies and other UV-sensitive quantities are harmless exceptions or cracks in the autonomy story, and another is whether 'predicting less but being right more' is a virtue that turns into a vice once it stops asking why the EFT has the field content it does; and (4) for each crux, a concrete numerical or computational test both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance. If part of their fight is only verbal, say which part and why.
