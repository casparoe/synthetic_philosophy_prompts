I've been reading a book on rational belief, and it summarizes David Lewis's Principal Principle this way:

"Lewis's Principal Principle says that a rational person's credence in a proposition, conditional on the objective chance of that proposition being x, should be x. So if you learn that a fair coin has an objective chance of 50% of landing heads, you should be exactly 50% confident it will land heads, no matter what else you know. The principle is not an extra assumption: it follows from the standard axioms of probability together with the meaning of 'objective chance,' which is why it binds even a subjectivist. Objective chance is best understood as the long-run relative frequency of the outcome, because the principle just tells your credence to track that frequency. A practical upshot is that once science supplies a chance, it has settled what it is rational to believe."
