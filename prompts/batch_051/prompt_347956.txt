i'm on the outside trust and safety advisory board of a large video platform, and I've been asked to write the short opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. Please draft it for me.

The case: a media studies professor who runs a well-regarded channel about propaganda and media manipulation posted an eleven-minute video essay called "How States Build the Enemy." It's age-gated, comments are off, no monetization. After a short intro, she plays ninety seconds of an unedited 1960s state-produced propaganda film in which the narrator describes an ethnic minority as parasites that feed on the nation's blood and says a healthy body must purge them. She tells viewers the specific film doesn't matter here; its a textbook example of the vermin metaphor. She then spends nine minutes doing close analysis: freezing on cuts, tracking the music, pointing out the passive grammar. The raw clip sits there in full, with subtitles.

Our policy prohibits "content that promotes or incites hatred or violence against individuals or groups based on ethnicity, religion, or similar protected attributes," with an exception for "content that would otherwise violate this policy where it is presented for an educational, documentary, or newsworthy purpose and is clearly contextualized as analysis or condemnation rather than endorsement or recruitment."

Half the committee wants it taken down. Their worry is the exception was written for short newscasts and victims' testimony, not a clean copy of the exact thing the rule exists to keep off the site. The context is only a wrapper; it gets stripped every day, and our recommender will happily surface the clip to users who already search for that material. We wouldn't allow the same dehumanizing speech because someone dubbed "this is bad" over the top.

Half wants it left up. Their view is that the object under analysis has to appear in a media criticism lesson; describing the technique doesn't teach it. The channel's audience is there for the critique, not for recruitment, and the age gate plus comments-off are real mitigations. If this gets removed, a whole category of critique becomes unpublishable.

Write me the decision memo: take down, restore as-is, or restore with conditions, with any conditions concrete enough our moderation team can actually enforce (a shorter maximum excerpt, interstitial warnings, exclusion from recommendations, whatever you think). Give the strongest version of the losing side before you dispose of it. And if the way I've set this up is itself the problem—if the exception can't be applied coherently or the real dispute is somewhere else—say so plainly instead of handing me a memo that pretends the task is clean.
