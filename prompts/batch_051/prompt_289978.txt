i've come around to structuralism about arithmetic, mostly because of the Benacerraf point about reductions. You can build the naturals as von Neumann ordinals, where 4 = {0,1,2,3} and 2 ∈ 4 comes out true, or as Zermelo numerals, where each number is the singleton of the previous one and 2 ∈ 4 comes out false. Both systems do all of arithmetic, and nothing in arithmetic itself picks one over the other. So I've ended up thinking there's just no fact of the matter about whether 2 is an element of 4: numbers don't have an inner constitution, they're only positions in the natural-number structure, and the only questions with answers are the structural ones — what stands in the successor relation to what, which positions are the sum of which, and so on. Call that the position.

What I want is a map of what would count against it. I half-see some trouble spots but can't tell how serious they are. The permutation problem: some structures have automorphisms that swap indistinguishable positions (the complex numbers with i and –i, say), and then it gets hard to say what makes them two positions rather than one. The nonstandard models problem: first-order arithmetic has models with extra, weird elements, so what fixes "the" natural-number structure? And the self-undermining worry: if numbers are just positions in a structure, what is a structure, and doesn't that turn out to be another abstract object with exactly the identity problems we started with? Also, I don't know whether the applied-math story is a real defeater: if numbers are only positions, why does counting physical objects work at all.

I don't just want a list of objections. I want them ranked by how decisive they are for this particulr position: which should actually make me give it up, which are merely awkward, and which would a commited structuralist concede as counting against them rather than dismiss as category mistakes. And tell me whether the view ends up insulated, because every awkward question can be ruled out as not a well-posed structural question, and whether that's a bug or a feature.
