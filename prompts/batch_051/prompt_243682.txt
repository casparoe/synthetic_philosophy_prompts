Epistemologists argue about testimony all the time—reductionists think testimonial justification reduces to perception, memory, and inference, anti-reductionists think it's a basic source on its own. But its surprisingly unclear what should count as testimony in the first place. Give me a working definition of testimony that gets these cases right: a stranger telling you the time; a court witness answering under oath; a friend texting you what they saw at a party; a road sign reading 'bridge out'; a thermometer reading you glance at; a diary entry you find in a drawer and read; a rumor you overhear on the bus; a large language model answering a factual question; a nod in response to 'did you see him leave?'; a map you consult. I'm confident the stranger and the witness are testimony and the thermometer isn't, and I have no settled view about the sign, the diary, the rumor, the nod, or the LLM. Run the definition through all ten, say which verdict your least sure of, and then say whether your definition makes reductionism easier or harder to defend. Max 400 words.
