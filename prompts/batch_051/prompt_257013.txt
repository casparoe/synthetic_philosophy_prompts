Dennett's 'sphexishness' names the failure mode of the digger wasp: the wasp looks purposive as it drags a cricket to the burrow, goes inside to check, emerges, and drags the cricket in — until someone moves the cricket while it is inside, at which point it re-drags the cricket to the entrance and goes inside to check again, indefinitely. The wasp is not stupid, blind, or unskilled; its routine simply cannot use the fact that a step has already been completed as a reason not to repeat it. Treat that as the working definition.

Construct a thought experiment to this specification. Two deterministic agents, A and B, have identical sensors, effectors, and learned competencies. The one architectural difference is that B's action selection can consult a persistent representation of which high-level subgoals it has already achieved, while A's action selection is driven only by the current stimulus and the current step. Now place them in one realistic workplace or software-deployment scenario, with no animals. An external disturbance resets the cue that normally triggers a step without undoing the completed work, so the step genuinely is done but everything in the environment says to do it again. Show A caught in a Sphex-like loop and B not. Then check your construction: (a) explain why the difference is not a difference in determinism, sensory information, or general intelligence; (b) identify the single feature doing the work, and say what happens if A is given a one-bit flag meaning already done; (c) give the strongest objection from someone who says the case cannot matter to free will, since both agents are deterministic, and give the reply a compatibilist in Dennett's spirit should make. The case is meant to isolate what separates sphexish rigidity from mere determinism, so a scenario in which A merely lacks ordinary information or a skill would miss the point.
