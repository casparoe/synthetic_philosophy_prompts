In modern mathematics, the real numbers are introduced in multiple standard ways: as Dedekind cuts of the rationals, as equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences of rationals, and axiomatically as the unique complete ordered field. Each construction produces a different underlying set—an individual real number is literally a different object depending on which construction one adopts—yet working mathematicians treat the constructions as interchangeable presentations of one and the same system. They freely move between them, regard theorems proved in one setting as automatically available in the others, and often speak of the real numbers as having been there all along, awaiting characterization, while also describing the constructions as definitions or even inventions. This is not merely an introductory pedagogical matter: it persists in analysis, functional analysis, and mathematical logic, where the real line functions as a kind of proper name.
