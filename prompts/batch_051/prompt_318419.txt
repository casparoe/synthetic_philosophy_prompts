My epistemology study group has an argument I can't get rid of, and I'm starting to think coherentism secretly is a form of foundationalism, which has to be wrong—or maybe it isn't, and that's what bothers me. Here's the argument as best I've reconstructed it:

(1) Coherentism says a belief is justified just when it coheres with the believer's whole system of beliefs.
(2) Coherence, whatever else it is, is a matter of inferential support: beliefs support one another.
(3) But any inference has premises—beliefs that support the conclusion but aren't themselves supported by anything in that inference.
(4) So in any coherentist system, some beliefs must at some point be used as premises without being derived from other beliefs at that point.
(5) A belief used as a premise and not derived from anything else at the time just is a basic belief, exactly the sort foundationalists say stops the regress.
(6) Therefore coherentism is committed to basic beliefs, and the only remaining disagreement with foundationalism is about which beliefs they are, not whether there are any.

I want coherentism to survive this, but not by special pleading. Tell me which step to reject, why it looked fine, and what coherentism has to pay if your answer is right. If you'd reject a different step than the one I'm tempted by, then say what that costs.
