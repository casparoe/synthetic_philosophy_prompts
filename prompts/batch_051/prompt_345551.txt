I'm a graduate student running a single seventy-five-minute section for advanced undergraduates who know how *all*, *most*, and *some* behave truth-conditionally but have never worked on semantics beyond that. The session is on generics: sentences like "Ducks lay eggs" and "Mosquitoes carry malaria." Plan the explanation rather than giving the explanation itself. It should first show that no familiar quantifier paraphrase captures these sentences, then bring the class to the deeper puzzle that prevalence of a property among the kind does not by itself determine truth, as the contrast between "Ducks lay eggs" (accepted) and "Ducks are female" (rejected) brings out. I need: the order in which the components must be introduced and what depends on what; the single contrast that should carry the session, with an account of what it must have and must lack, plus one tempting alternative contrast I should avoid and why; the misconceptions students are likely to form at each stage (for instance, that these sentences are just most-statements with unstated exceptions, or that the exceptions are purely pragmatic) and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off; what to leave out at this level and explicitly set aside; and a final judgment pair or question that would show they have grasped the problem rather than merely memorized the examples.
