I've been reading a lot of FIRE blogs lately — financial independence / retire early — because my partner has gotten really into the idea that we should live on almost nothing now so he can stop working at 40. Some of the people in that world are spartan in a way that honestly reminds me of the ascetics I studied in a religious studies class: rice and beans, one pair of shoes, a shared room, a spreadsheet tracking every dollar, and a weird cheerful pride in how little they need. And it got me wondering where the line is between an ascetic ideal and just being extremely disciplined about money.

Here are the criteria I'm working with for something's being an ascetic ideal (treat "frugality" and "voluntary simplicity" as interchangeable for now): (1) deliberate, ongoing renunciation of ordinary comforts and pleasures; (2) the renunciation is shaped by some vision of a good life, not by poverty, illness, or outside compulsion; (3) it aims at inner transformation — moral, spiritual, or at least psychological; (4) the practice is treated as valuable at least partly for its own sake, not purely as a means to something else.

Now the borderline case. Take a person deep in the FIRE movement who lives on around $20k a year, owns almost nothing, eats lentils most nights, and has given up restaurants, hobbies, and most socializing to keep the savings rate above seventy percent. They are not miserable; they talk about the frugality itself as freeing, say they've stopped wanting stuff, and suspect they'll never go back to consuming even after they hit their number. But whenever you press them on why they do it, the justifications are purely instrumental: buying back their time, derisking their life, never needing a boss again. They insist there is no moral or spiritual content to it — its just an engineering problem, and they'd drop the lentils tomorrow if the math worked without them.

Is that an ascetic ideal, or is it a disguised case of deferred consumption or risk management? Go through it criterion by criterion, say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether the case shows the criteria themselves need fixing.
