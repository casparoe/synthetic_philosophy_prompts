I am the lead analyst at a state sentencing commission. A new statute requires that, before any felony sentencing, the judge receive a three-year recidivism risk statement produced by my unit. The statement reports the reconviction rate of a reference class — a population of prior offenders to which the defendant is assigned — and in practice the line we draw around that class does most of the work: the same defendant could land in a class with a 19% three-year reconviction rate or a 54% rate depending on which attributes we include. We produce several hundred statements a year, each under a 48-hour deadline, with four analysts who have graduate training in statistics and no formal philosophy. The forms state only the class definition and the rate. The commission has now directed me to propose a single written decision procedure for choosing the reference class, because defense attorneys have begun challenging our class choices as arbitrary in court, and the commission will have to defend the procedure before the legislature.

Please write that procedure. It should be usable by my analysts as a sequence of tests or questions, expressible as queries against our conviction database, with the following made explicit: what counts as a sufficient answer at each step; when the procedure stops and the unit should decline to produce a rate; and where the procedure must hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than statistics. Ground it in what the philosophical work on the reference class problem actually supports — for example, that there is no uniquely correct reference class, and that the narrowest class is not automatically best when its data are thin. Mark the points where the philosophy runs out and the procedure starts making judgment calls, and say what the procedure cannot do.

Then run the procedure on three cases. (1) A 24-year-old with a first adult conviction and two juvenile adjudications, where the narrowest available class is very small. (2) A 55-year-old with a long property-crime history and a 12-year gap with no convictions before the current offense, where the obvious historical classes fit him poorly. (3) A case in which the only class with adequately large data is defined by a protected characteristic or a close proxy for one, and every permissible alternative class is much broader. In this third case the procedure should be allowed to give an answer the commission will not like; state what that answer is and why the procedure cannot avoid it.
