Here's one I keep going back and forth on. The old Adorno and Horkheimer line is that mass culture is an industrial system: a few studios and broadcasters produce standardized, formulaic things, give each one a slightly different surface so it feels like real choice, and train audiences to be passive consumers of whatever gets pushed at them. The argument was built for a world where a handful of networks and record labels beamed more or less the same stuff at everybody. Now the actual media diet is largely user-made, and a recommendation algorithm picks which of it each person sees. I want you to say whether that change kills the argument, saves it, or just relocates it. Specifically: which premise of the original argument does algorithmic personalization actually bear on? Does it break the standardization claim, or does it just standardize the sorting function instead of the content? Does user-generated content break the passivity claim, or does it turn the audience into an unpaid workforce and an always-on focus group? And if the original version of the argument is dead, can it be salvaged in a form that isn't unfalsifiable? Since this is partly a judgment call, also say how confident you are in the overall verdict and single out the point in your answer you're least sure about.
