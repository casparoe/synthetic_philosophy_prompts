Here is a variation on the ship of Theseus, except the ship is a mature ant colony. Say every worker is replaced over roughly six months, while the queen survives for years; the workers cooperate to maintain the nest, regulate the colony's growth, and respond to colony-level cues. The dead workers aren't reassembled anywhere, they simply die. Add the holobiont wrinkle: each worker carries a gut microbiome that turns over on a timescale of weeks, and the microbes matter to the workers' nutrition and communication. Some people would want to call this colony a superorganism, so the question is whether that label tracks a real persistence condition. In the original puzzle, the main candidate answers appeal to continuity of material or continuity of organisation. Which of these criterion, if either, grounds the colony's identity across the six-month replacement? Or does the biological case push us toward a third criterion—something like continuity of a germ line, or of a unit of selection—and would that criterion also settle the original ship case? Work through what each criterion says about the colony at time t and at time t + six months before you defend a verdict.
