write the joint statement a committed assurance theorist and a committed practice theorist about promising could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. for concreteness, read the assurance view as the one on which the obligation of a promise comes from the expectation you intentionally lead the promisee to form, and the practice view as the one on which the obligation comes from the rules and point of the social practice of promising itself. i want the deliverable to have two parts. first, a numbered list of agreed claims, including about concrete cases they can both handle — a promise to a friend who dies before you perform, a promise broken before anyone could have relied on it, a promise made under duress, a promise to do something mildly wrong, and a promise whose performance becomes impossible. second, a disagreements table. in the table give me the precise residual disagreement, with each side's own position worded so that that side would accept the wording; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side — and for each crux, the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's predicted verdict about that case recorded in advance. stay constructive: the agreed claims shouldn't be vacuous, and the table shouldn't paper over a real disagreement by relabeling it as just terminology. the point is a document both camps would actually sign.
