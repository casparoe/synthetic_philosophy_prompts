hey! in my global cultures class we've been talking about ubuntu, which our textbook glosses as the idea that a person becomes a person through other people. my teacher keeps saying it's not just a feel-good slogan but a whole different picture of personhood, where being a person isn't about having some private inner thing (a soul or a mind or whatever) but about standing in the right kind of relationships. me and my friend got into a huge argument after class about whether a person who grew up completely alone on an island would still be a person, and i still don't know how to answer without either giving up the ubuntu idea or saying something absurd.

so i'm trying to find a working definition of "person" that is at least in the spirit of ubuntu. here are my cases. a newborn baby with a name and a whole family around them: person, no question. a big company that can get sued and make decisions and owns stuff: definitely not a person, even though it's made entirely out of relationships and rules. a person in a coma who will probably never wake up: i still think person, but i cant explain why on a relational view. a human being who somehow grew up with zero contact with other humans and no language: i think still a person, but that seems hard for ubuntu to swallow. my dog, who is definitely a family member and will get a real funeral: not a person, though everyone treats him like one. a dead grandparent who the family still talks to and asks for advice: i go back and forth. an AI chatbot that remembers stuff about me and apologizes when i get upset: probably not a person now, but if everyone treated it as one for long enough would that change? and a person with really advanced dementia who doesn't recognize anyone anymore: i want to say still a person, but it's a one-way relationship now.

one more: a pregnancy late enough that the parents named the baby and say it has a personality because it kicks at certain songs — i honestly don't know where that goes. so: give me a definition of "person" that gets as many of these as you can, run it through the list, and tell me where it forces a surprising verdict or overturns mine. my friend's objection is that any relational definition either lets in the dog and the company or kicks out the isolated human and the coma patient — i'd like to know if he's right, and if he is, which part you'd give up to fix it.
