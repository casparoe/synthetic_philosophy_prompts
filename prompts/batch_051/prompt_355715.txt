I work for a midsize city manager's office. State law lets us hand certain policy questions to a randomly selected citizens' assembly with real decision power, and we now get a proposal or two a week from councilors and departments. I need a reusable decision proceedure my staff (no philosophy background) can run in half a day, before legal review, to recommend to the city manager whether a given question should go to sortition rather than being handled by the council, a specialist commission, or ordinary public comment. The procedure should lay out the questions to ask in order, say what counts as a good enough answer to each, and flag where it stops with a 'no' or where a person has to make a values call. Then run it on three hypothetical cases: a zoning code update that would raise some property values and push rents up, a proposal to rename a street that currently honors a controversial former mayor, and siting a new homeless shelter in a residential ward. Make sure at least one of these comes out 'do not use sortition' even though the council would love to offload it, and say what the procedure still can't settle.
