I'm the copy editor of my high school's student newspaper, and I'd like a short written ruling on a copy dispute. Our style guide, last revised in 1973, states: "Form the past tense of a verb by adding -ed; the only exceptions are the irregular forms listed in the table below, which are to be used exactly as listed." The table includes, among others, go/went, see/saw, hit/hit, and ring/rang. An article in the next issue quotes a student as saying, "I text him three times yesterday." The editors cannot agree: one says the guide requires texted, while another says text should stand by analogy with hit/hit, since hit is already an accepted exception. No one in 1973 had this verb sense in mind, so the rule's authors did not foresee the case. Please rule on: (1) what the rule requires read literally; (2) what it requires read as a general policy about newspaper English, and what its drafters would plausibly have said if asked; and (3) which ruling we should adopt, and what this dispute reveals about whether English past-tense formation is best described as a rule plus exceptions, as a family of patterns extended by analogy, or as something else. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
