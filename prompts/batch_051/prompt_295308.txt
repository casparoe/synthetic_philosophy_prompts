I'm on the Responsible Investment Committee of a mid-sized university's endowment, and we're stuck on a divestment proposal: move the roughly 9% of the fund currently in oil and gas majors out over five years. The finance office's memo says, basically, the Milgrom-Stokey no-trade logic plus efficient-markets reasoning: unless we have genuine nonpublic information, or utility-type reasons like values and risk tolerance, the market already prices transition risk, and divesting mostly means paying fees and walking away from expected return. The campus coalition counters that climate transition is exactly the kind of slow-moving, politically mediated, no-clean-arbitrage risk that markets are worst at pricing: nobody holds the true private signal, the settlement date is beyond any fund manager's review cycle, and everyone's second-guessing short-term flows. A couple members floated a middle option: hold the stock but direct a real chunk of the returns into shareholding engagement and renewables infrastructure.

I need the staff memo we circulate before Thursday's vote: pick divest, hold-and-engage, or stay-the-course, give a precise motion we can actually vote on with a timeline, and treat the strongest point on the losing side seriously rather than strawmanning it. The part I can't get comfortable with: if we buy the market's epistemic authority enough to dismiss the divestment case, shouldn't we also buy it enough to dismiss our own committee's ability to judge which side is overconfident? Write the memo so a member who disagrees with you could read it and change their vote.
