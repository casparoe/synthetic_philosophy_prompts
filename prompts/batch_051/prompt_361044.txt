Here are the criteria I was taught for classifying an act as terrorism: (1) it is an act, or a credible threat, of serious violence against persons or property; (2) it is carried out by a non-state actor; (3) it is directed at civilians or civilian infrastructure; (4) it is undertaken to advance a political, religious, or ideological aim; and (5) it is intended to intimidate or coerce a wider audience beyond the immediate victims. Now consider a fictional but realistic case. An environmentalist group gains access to a national railway's control systems and, for a week, sends false commands that disable signaling across the network. No person is physically injured and no equipment is damaged; the attack consists entirely in manipulating code and data. The result is mass cancellations, severe economic loss, and widespread public fear about critical infrastructure. The group states that it chose this method to force the government to cancel a fossil-fuel project precisely because it avoids physical harm. Work through the case criterion by criterion and argue whether it is an act of terrorism under the stated criteria. Then say whether the case shows that any criterion—especially 'serious violence against persons or property'—should be revised, or whether the criteria already draw the line in the right place.
