I'm drafting a short statement of my philosophical position on metaphor for a qualifying exam in philosophy of language and cognitive science, and before I commit to it I'd like an outside audit of whether the view hangs together. My commitments, as I currently state them, are these: (a) on the semantics, I follow Davidson in holding that a metaphorical sentence means only what its words literally mean; there is no additional "metaphorical meaning" encoded in the sentence that a hearer decodes or recovers. (b) On the psychology, I hold that metaphors do substantial cognitive work — they direct attention, shape categorization, and alter how people reason about abstract domains, and I regard the experimental evidence on metaphorical framing as solid. (c) On conceptual structure, I am broadly convinced by Lakoff and Johnson's conceptual metaphor theory: many abstract concepts are organized by systematic cross-domain mappings, such as argument as war or time as space, and these mappings are conceptual rather than merely verbal ornament. (d) On paraphrase, I hold that an apt metaphor frequently cannot be translated into literal language without losing something important. (e) On evaluation, I hold that we can rightly call metaphors apt, illuminating, or misleading, and that these are genuine normative properties of the metaphor itself, not just descriptions of the audience's psychology. (f) On inferential commitment, I also hold that accepting a metaphor as apt — for example, accepting that depression is a chemical imbalance or that argument is war — commits one to some patterns of inference rather than others, and those commitments can be rationally better or worse rather than merely more or less causally effective.

I cannot tell whether these six commitments are jointly tenable, and I would rather find the fault lines now than in the exam. Please audit the set as a package: identify the genuine tensions and rank them by severity, specifying for each what would have to give; identify any apparent tensions that dissolve under closer inspection and say why; and close by naming the single revision that would restore the most coherence at the lowest philosophical cost. I'd like the response in American English.
