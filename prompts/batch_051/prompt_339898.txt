Long-time lurker here, first time trying to turn a fuzzy ethics debate into a mechanism-design puzzle. I keep hitting the same wall in whistleblowing discussions: people either treat loyalty as a bias to be overcome or as a reason to stay quiet, and the actual tradeoff never gets modeled. So here's a mechanism I want stress-tested.

Imagine a company with a few hundred employees. Every quarter, each employee has to file a private "loyalty map": a list of cowekers they currently owe any loyalty to, with a one-line reason for each (mentor, friend, they covered for me, years on the same project, whatever). Only a rotating three-person panel ever sees the maps; normal HR doesn't.

Whistleblowing is then keyed to your map. If you report misconduct and the accused is on your current loyalty map, you get no bounty and the report goes down a separate "betrayal track": before the misconduct claim is investigated, the panel decides whether you had a legitimate ground to break loyalty (roughly, whether the alleged misconduct was serious enough to outweigh the reason you listed). If the panel says no, the report is dropped and a note goes in your file. If you want to report someone on your map without that gamble, you can first file a "loyalty revocation" and wait three business days before filing, and the person isnt told during the window.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral stance. I want to know how it actually plays out: what it incentivizes, how it gets gamed, and whether it can be salvaged. Do people keep their maps empty to keep their options open, or stuff them with allies to signal loyalty, or strategically update them when they smell trouble? I assume the map gets Goodharted, but I want the details of how. Once everyone knows the maps are used this way, what is the map even measuring anymore? Does this shift reporting toward cross-team misconduct and away from cases where people are close to the evidence? What does it do to mentoring, friendship, and honest error-sharing inside the firm? And is there a patch that preserves whatever the loyalty map was supposed to capture, or does the idea just collapse into ordinary whistleblowing on one side and ordinary cover-ups on the other?
