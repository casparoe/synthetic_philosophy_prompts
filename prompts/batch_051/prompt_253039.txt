I'm a complaints officer at a state board that regulates complementary health practitioners, and too many cases turn on whether a treatment claim is scientific enough for us to even evaluate it—not whether it's true, just whether it's the kind of claim that could be tested. I keep coming back to Popper's idea that a scientific theory has to rule out some possible observations, but I don't know how to turn that into a procedure my non-philosopher colleages can actually use. Please give me a checklist we could run on a submitted claim: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a passable answer at each step, where the procedure stops and says "untestable as stated," and where it has to hand off to human judgment because the claimant can always add ad hoc caveats to dodge a test. Then run the checklist on two examples: a reiki practitioner who says the treatment removes energy blockages that cause illness, and a supplement company that says its product raises white blood cell count by at least 20 percent within four weeks. Flag the parts where the procedure might give an answer I'd be uncomfortable defending in a hearing.
