Next month I'm presenting a paper at a political theory workshop and I want a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis is that the franchise should be treated as a civic office you have to take up, not a basic right you just get. The pivot of the paper is a passage in Aristotle's Politics, Book III, where he defines a citizen as someone who has a share in deliberative or judicial office. I'm using that to argue that eligibility to vote should track the actual assumption of civic burdens — jury duty, military or civil service, full tax liability, something like that — rather than just turning eighteen or having a resident's address. So I end up arguing against both lowering the voting age below 18 and automatic votes for non-citizen residents, not because I think young people or immigrants are unfit, but because the vote should be attached to demonstrable contribution and should lapse if you opt out of the burdens.

The audience is a mix: mostly liberal egalitarians who think the franchise is a fundamental right that any affected resident should have, two deliberative democrats who think the more people voting the better the epistemic quality, and one old conservative who already thinks the franchise is to wide. I want the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come, phrased the way each camp would put them, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, where my Aristotle reading is vulnerable, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. Also, I want the reply notes to be in British English, since I'll be speaking in the UK.
