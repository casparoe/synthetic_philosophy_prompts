I cover the model-deployment review panel of a large research institution for a science and ideas publication, and I am trying to anticipate how the panel will rule on a proposal whose decision has not yet been posted. The panel's published rulings are short, but I will reproduce the relevant ones.

It cleared an uninterpretable neural net that ranks which aisles of a warehouse to restock first, requiring no explanation because individual errors are cheap and corrected within a shift.

It rejected a black-box model for assigning scarce public daycare places. The model predicted better than the agency's existing checklist, but the agency could not show that the plain-language reasons it proposed to give parents reflected the model's actual weighting, and families would have had little chance to contest a placement.

It approved, with conditions, an uninterpretable model that ranks households for home visits from community mental-health workers. The workers retain final authority over the order, may ignore the ranking, and see the score only as a suggestion.

It rejected a black-box model for shortlisting applicants for a competitive state arts residency. The proposed explanations would have come from a separate simpler model and were not verified as faithful to the main model, and the staff applying the scores were not in a position to second-guess them.

The new proposal is an uninterpretable scoring model for allocating one-time emergency rent-relief payments. It is more accurate than the transparent eligibility rubric now in use. Every score would be reviewed by a caseworker who could override it with a short written justification, and each denied applicant would receive a brief explanation generated by a separate simpler model; those explanations are not guaranteed to track the scoring model's actual basis.

What should the panel decide if it wants to remain consistent with its own record: approve, approve with conditions, or reject? Which earlier ruling is the closest analog? If the record underdetermines the decision, say what principle the panel would be adopting with each available ruling, and specify a future case that would cleanly separate those principles.
