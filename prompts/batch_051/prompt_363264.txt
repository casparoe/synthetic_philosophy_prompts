My reading group keeps going in circles on this, and I want something I can throw back at both of them. The topic is whether the simulation argument takes the wind out of longtermist fanaticism about the far future. The longtermist line is that expected-value reasoning is dominated by astronomically large future populations, so we should prioritize things like existential-risk reduction. One friend argues Bostrom already undermines that. She quotes this from Nick Bostrom's "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?":

"Therefore, if we don’t think that we are currently living in a computer simulation, we are not entitled to believe that we will have descendants who will run lots of such simulations of their forebears."

Her gloss: if you reject the simulation hypothesis, Bostrom says you can't rationally expect to become posthuman and run ancestor-simulations, so a big chunk of the big-future story is blocked at the outset. And if you accept it, the future you're supposed to be fanatical about exsists only inside someone's simulation, which changes how much it should weigh. Another friend says this misreads the argument: the disjunction still leaves a big expected future on at least one branch, and fanaticism can survive by just conditioning on it. Write an essay settling this: does the simulation argument dampen or undercut fanaticism about the far future? Give both sides their due but defend a definite verdict, even if it's a conditional one.
