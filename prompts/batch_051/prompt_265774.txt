Last week I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether monogamy is a moral requirement or simply a conventional arrangement that a couple may reshape by agreement. By the end she had issued a set of firm verdicts about particular cases that now seem to me more revealing than our original question, and I have been trying to reconstruct the rule behind them.

She was certain that a partner who secretly has sex with someone else has plainly been unfaithful, and that the same is true, to a lesser degree, of a partner who kisses a colleague at a party and never mentions it. She equally insisted that a partner who maintains a months-long daily correspondence with an ex—emotionally intimate, flirtatious, charged, but never physical—is having an affair in the relevant sense. She did not count a partner who watches pornography alone, even habitually and privately, as unfaithful. But she said a partner who pays a particular OnlyFans performer for custom material and exchanges messages with them has crossed into infidelity; her stated reason was that a specific real person is on the other end, responding personally to the request. A private, long-running romantic and sexual fantasy about a fictional character from a novel or television series was, in her view, not infidelity. Finally, she thought that in a genuinely open relationship whose terms were negotiated and honored, neither partner wrongs the other by sleeping with someone else.

The case I failed to put to her is this. A partner spends several secret months in an emotionally close and explicitly erotic correspondence with an AI companion—a large language model chatbot with a stable persona and a memory of their shared history. There is no human on the other end. Should I expect her to call this infidelity, and what would each answer commit her to about the wrong involved in sexual exclusivity? I am after the reconstruction rather than the bare prediction: the rule that fits all her earlier verdicts, the alternative rules that fit them equally well and that may disagree about the AI case, which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and a further case that would best force a choice among the candidate rules.
