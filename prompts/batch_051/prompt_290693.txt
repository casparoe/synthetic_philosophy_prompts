I've been trying to figure out which traits deserve special legal protection against discrimination and which don't, by starting from my own reactions to concrete cases. Here are my verdicts, and I already know they don't all sit comfortably together.

A landlord refuses to rent to a Black applicant; even if he could show his other tenants would renew less, I treat that as out of bounds. A religious school passes over an otherwise qualified teacher because she isn't a member of the faith; this strikes me as acceptable in a way the first case isn't. An airline grounds a pilot who develops a seizure disorder; I find that acceptable, not discriminatory. An office refuses to hire a wheelchair user for a desk job that could be done with inexpensive modifications; I find that clearly wrong. A restaurant turns down a 75-year-old server because the owner assumes she'll be slow; I'm uneasy, but it doesn't feel like the same kind of wrong as the race case. A company screens out heavy applicants for a desk job citing future health costs; I don't think that's the law's business, though I can't say what makes it different from the wheelchair case. A daycare rejects a candidate with a 25-year-old nonviolent drug conviction; that seems wrong to me, yet refusing to hire someone with a recent, directly relevant embezzlement conviction for a bookkeeping job seems fine. An ordinary company declines to hire someone after learning they donated heavily to a far-right party; I think that's as out of bounds as a religious test. A firm uses a genetic screen to avoid hiring someone likely to develop an expensive illness a decade from now; I find that unacceptable even though the person is fully capable now.

My working theory was immutability: born-with traits get protected, chosen traits don't. But my own verdicts break that, since religion is chosen and I'd protect it, while age is unchangeable and I allow more of it. Stigma fails too, because weight is visible and stigmatized yet I'd allow screening for it, while a silent genetic marker is not stigmatized day to day yet I'd ban testing for it.

Is there one principle that captures most or all of these verdicts, and which of them would it force me to drop? Please present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, no introduction or conclusion; in each item state one candidate principle in a sentence or two, say which of my verdicts it conflicts with, and give its ruling on a case I haven't settled, such as accent, height, or a visible congenital difference.
