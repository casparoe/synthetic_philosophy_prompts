I keep running into a pattern I don't have a word for, and I'd rather get the phenomenon carved up right then just get a label. In an academic department I watched people inflate publication counts with salami-slicing and gift authorships; at a company I watched a customer-satisfaction score stop tracking how customers actually felt. In both cases everyone knew the number was being gamed, but any individual who refused to game it got punished relative to the people who did, so the gaming was individually rational and collectively made the number useless — and we stayed stuck in an equilibrium everyone said they hated. It's not quite Goodhart's law, because Goodhart just says a metric that becomes a target gets gamed; what I can't place is the trap aspect, the equilibrium where nobody can unilaterally stop without losing out relative to peers. And it's not quite a race to the bottom, because there's no shared commons being degraded — unless the reliability of the metric itself counts as the commons. Is there a name for this specific combination, or is it just two familiar ideas stuck together? If there isn't an established term, which nearby concepts should it be distinguished from, and what would you call it?
