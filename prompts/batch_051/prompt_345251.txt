I have been arguing with a friend about whether a cognitive capacity can ever legitimately certify itself, and he has backed me into a corner with a step-by-step argument about color vision. His claim is that I can come to know that my color vision is reliable by using my color vision, and that anyone who calls this circular is confusing a harmless dependence with a vicious circle. His argument runs as follows. (1) When I look at a ripe tomato under ordinary daylight, I know it is red; I do not first need to have established that my color vision is generally reliable, because a reliable perceptual faculty can yield knowledge before its reliability has been checked. (2) In that same moment, I can know by introspection that I judge the tomato to be red. (3) From (1) and (2) I can deduce that my present color judgment, at least, is correct. (4) By repeating this on many occasions and with many objects, and relying on memory, I can accumulate a track record of correct color judgments. (5) From a long and varied track record of that kind, I can infer that my color vision is reliable in general. (6) The fact that my color vision was the instrument used to produce every entry in the record does not make the inference viciously circular, because each entry was already known on independent grounds via step (1). (7) Therefore, I can come to know that my color vision is reliable, using only the faculty whose reliability was in question.

I cannot decide whether this is a sound argument that yields a modest piece of self-knowledge, or a slick piece of bootstrapping that lets an unchecked faculty sponsor itself. Is the argument valid? If it is, is that a problem for the sort of account of knowledge that step (1) assumes, or is the conclusion less scandalous than it sounds? If it is not valid, tell me exactly which step fails, on what reading of it, and why the remaining steps do not save the argument. Lead with your bottom line in your first sentence, and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
