Give me a working definition of tolerance that sorts these cases correctly. Clearly tolerant: a secularist editor who publishes a serious op-ed arguing that her country should adopt a state religion; a landlord whose faith condemns same-sex relationships but who rents to a same-sex couple on ordinary terms; a pacifist university administrator who books a hall for a student group that advocates preemptive war. Clearly not tolerant: the state that jails citizens for nonviolent criticism of the government; a doctor who refuses emergency treatment to a patient because she abhors that patient’s religion.

Now the hard cases: (1) the state that lets a party committed to banning minority religions if it takes power run and hold rallies; (2) the social media platform that removes Holocaust-denial accounts while leaving up vicious partisan insults; (3) the newspaper that prints an essay saying that, if the author takes power, that newspaper will be outlawed; (4) the teacher who keeps politely arguing with a student who denies the Holocaust instead of shutting the exchange down; (5) the anti-fascist who punches a fascist speaker in order to stop him recruiting. Run your definition through all five and say which verdict you are least sure of. I have no settled view on (1) through (4); I am confident (5) is not tolerance, but I can’t say what it is without a definition.

Then use the definition to address the paradox of tolerance: can a society refuse tolerance to an intolerant movement in order to preserve tolerance and still itself count as tolerant? Is that refusal better understood as a limit already built into the concept of tolerance, or as a principled breach of it? I also suspect tolerance is doing two jobs—one about an attitude (privately enduring what you find objectionable) and one about a political practice (not using superior power to suppress the disliked)—and that the paradox bites hardest when the two are run together. Give me one definition that handles both jobs, or two definitions, and say which of the hard cases move between them.

Write in American English. Organize the answer as: Proposed definition (2–3 sentences); Clear cases (one paragraph); Hard cases, case by case (one short paragraph each); The paradox of tolerance (one paragraph); Least secure verdict (one paragraph, including any revision you would make).
