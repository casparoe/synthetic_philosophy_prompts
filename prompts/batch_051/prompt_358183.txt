Friend of mine is one of those people who cites Finland topping the World Happiness Report as proof that the Nordic model produces the most happiness, and it's been driving me crazy. I finally wrote out my objection as a numbered argument so we could stop talking past each other, but now I'm worried my own conclusion is too strong to believe. Here it is:

(1) The report's main country ranking is built from a survey question that asks people to rate their lives on a 0-to-10 ladder (the report's own separate measures of positive and negative affect don't drive the headline ranking), not from any direct measurement of how people feel day to day.
(2) Answering that kind of ladder question is a global, evaluative judgment — how your life seems to stack up against some standard you bring to it — rather than a report of your actual emotional state.
(3) So the rankings measure life evaluation, not happiness in the sense most people mean.
(4) If a ranking doesn't measure what it's presented as measuring, a country has no reason to try to climb it.
(5) Therefore Finland's spot doesn't tell us anything about whose policies actually make people happy, and governments shouldn't set 'top the happiness rankings' as a goal.

I cant find the bad step. My friend thinks (4) is obviously false because even a mislabeled metric could still be worth optimizing; I keep pushing on (2) because maybe that reflective judgment just is one legitimate meaning of 'happiness'. So: where does the argument actually fail, if it fails at all? Be specific about the step and the reading of the terms. If (2) is the problem, give the strongest true version of it and say whether the argument still reaches (5). If (4) is the problem, tell me what premise I would actually need to get to (5), and whether it is defensible.
