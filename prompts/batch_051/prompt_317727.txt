Costly-signaling theory treats the peacock's tail as the paradigm of a handicap: a signal whose cost is precisely what keeps it honest. Work with the following three-part test. A signal S, offered by a sender to advertise having some quality Q, counts as a handicap when:

1. Cost. Producing S imposes a real cost or risk on the sender.
2. Differential cost. S is less costly to bear, in net terms, for those who genuinely have Q than for those who do not, so that low-Q senders are screened out by the cost itself rather than by any external policing.
3. Honesty. Because of (2), S carries information about Q rather than only about the sender's resources or willingness to spend.

Now apply this test to the following case. An online forum requires that every post be accompanied by a publicly displayed sum of money that the poster irrevocably destroys; no one receives the money, the poster chooses the amount, and it is destroyed regardless of whether the claim in the post turns out to be true. Posters describe the burned money as a signal of how sincerely they believe what they are writing. Some posters are sincere: they burn money because they want that sincere conviction to be believed. Others are manipulators who do not believe what they write but expect that being believed will benefit them later, and they are prepared to burn whatever amount secures that belief. Neither the forum nor its readers have any way to check the claims after the fact.

Go through the three criteria in order and give a committed verdict: is the burned currency a handicap signal of sincere belief, or is it better described as purchasing credibility or attention? If the criteria do not settle the matter, say exactly where they give out. Then say whether the case exposes a missing fourth condition—for instance, a condition that the cost not be compensable by the expected benefit of being believed, or that the cost arise from the quality itself instead of being a flat fee available to anyone. Do not merely list interpretations; take a definite position and defend it.
