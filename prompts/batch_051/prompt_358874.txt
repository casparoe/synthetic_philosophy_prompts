I have been turning over a structural parallel and want to know whether it should be taken seriously. In gauge theory, the dynamical potential Aμ carries more information than the field strength: the transformation Aμ → Aμ + ∂μχ leaves Fμν = ∂μAν − ∂νAμ unchanged, so an entire orbit of configurations represents a single classical state. The standard interpretive move is to call the potential's gauge-variant features surplus structure and to regard only gauge-invariant quantities as real, though the Aharonov–Bohm effect shows that the invariants are subtler than the local field strength alone, since loop integrals of the potential, its holonomies, are physically measurable. Mathematical structuralism makes a structurally analogous move: for a non-rigid mathematical structure with nontrivial automorphisms, any feature not preserved by those automorphisms is not a feature of the structure, and isomorphic representations are descriptions of the same structure rather than different ones. The mapping suggests itself: gauge transformations are the automorphisms of a physical structure; the gauge orbit is the class of equivalent representations; gauge-invariant content is what structuralism calls the structure itself.

I want to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. In answering, state the mapping precisely, identify what would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer from one debate to the other, and name something the parallel predicts that turns out false, or a disanalogy that does real work. One candidate disanalogy I keep noticing is that gauge transformations act locally, varying from spacetime point to point, whereas the automorphisms structuralists usually discuss are a single global symmetry of a structured set; I cannot tell whether this is the crucial difference or merely a matter of choosing a larger structured object to which the structuralist treatment applies. Is the gauge-reality debate simply mathematical structuralism applied to field theory, or is that verdict purchased by stretching the structuralist notions past their breaking point?
