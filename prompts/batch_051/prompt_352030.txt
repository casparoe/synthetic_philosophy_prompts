I teach a second-year module on non-Cartesian approaches to scepticism, and next week I'm closing the Zhuangzi lecture with a poem rather than a summary slide. Could you write a villanelle about the butterfly dream? A proper one, nineteen lines, two refrains. The refrains are the philosophical load-bearers here: one should gesture at Zhuangzi dreaming he is a butterfly, the other should invert it, the butterfly dreaming it is Zhuangzi. I don't mind how you phrase them, but each time they come back their force should shift a little, so that by the end the reader feels the two readings circling each other rather than one of them winning. The closing quatrain should land on the transformation of things, not on how can I know anything? Keep Descartes out of it entirely: no world-illusion, no reality is just a dream moral, no treating Zhuangzi as if he were doing radical doubt. It needs to be accurate enough that my colleague in Chinese philosophy wouldnt wince, and good enough that a student who's never read the Zhuangzi still feels why the passage is strange.
