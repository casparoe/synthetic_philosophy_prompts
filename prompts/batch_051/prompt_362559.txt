I cover environmental science and the politics of expertise for a living, and I'm on the steering committee for a regional citizens' assembly that will write recommendations for a three-county air-quality plan. We keep getting stuck on who should be a voting member — it feels less like logistics and more like a who-counts-as-the-public question — and I need to send around a short memo before Thursday. Half the committee wants seats only for permanent residents of the three counties: they're the ones who will be legally bound by whatever the counties adopt, and they're the ones whose local officials will ultimately have to answer for the plan. The other half says that's too narrow because the airshed doesn't care about county lines — non-resident commuters, students, and people in downwind towns just outside the jurisdiction breath the same air every day, so they should get votes rather than observer badges. A few of us, me included, have floated a mixed model where residents hold a majority of seats and out-of-county people get a smaller voting bloc, but nobody's sure if that actually solves the problem or just buries it. The memo should lay out a concrete membership rule — who gets a seat, who gets a vote, roughly what proportion if it's mixed — and it should engage the strongest version of the losing side, not just dismiss it. It also needs to be something we can actually adopt on Thursday and later point to when someone asks why they got left out.
