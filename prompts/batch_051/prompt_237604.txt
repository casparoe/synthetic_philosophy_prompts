For my Indian philosophy class we're wrapping up a unit on Advaita, and I've put together a belief profile I'd like someone to check for consistency. I was really struck by the Chandogya Upanishad passage where Uddalaka tells Svetaketu that just as by knowing one lump of clay you know all things made of clay, and the various modifications are only a name arising from speech while the truth is clay. That seems to give me the beginnings of a view. I think I hold all of the following: (1) māyā is neither an entity separate from Brahman nor absolutely non-existent; it is the inexplicable appearance of difference, change, and plurality. (2) The jīva is really Brahman, and liberation consists in realizing that the apparent difference was never actually real. (3) The empirical world is mithya, false at the ultimate level, but it has transactional reality, vyavahara or however it's spelled, until realization. (4) Brahman is nirguna, so the identity of self and Brahman doesn't mean I get to keep my attributes and memories at the deepest level. (5) Nevertheless, bondage is not merely a false belief; it involves something that really holds us, and liberation has to make a practical difference to how one lives and sees. I know some of these sound like they'd be in tension even with themselves, but I can't tell wich ones really bite. Could you go through the set and say which commitments pull against each other, which pairs only seem to, and then rank the tensions by severity rather than just listing them? Also say what the cheapest fixes would be and which single revision would clean up the most. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
