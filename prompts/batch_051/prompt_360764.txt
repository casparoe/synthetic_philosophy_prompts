I'm a novelist, and I've been trying to come at inner alignment and goal misgeneralization from the one place I have real expertise: my own drafts. While I write I keep craft rules — no flashbacks in the first sixty pages, every chapter ends on a turn, cut any line that doesn't cost somebody something. I know they're proxies for someting I can't quite state, something like momentum or reader-faith. But the proxies drift: by month three I'm serving the rule instead of the story, and the only thing that tells me so is a kind of aesthetic nausea when the chapter obeys every rule and still just lies there dead. I've been calling this procedural drift. My theory is that inner misalignment is best understood as the absence of exactly that negative felt signal — the model has no version of the sentence 'this meets every spec and is still bad.' I know this overlaps with Goodhart, goal misgeneralization, and mesa-optimizers. Is procedural drift just one of those wearing a novelist's coat, or does the felt-signal part add anything real? Where does the analogy break in a way that matters, and is there any way to make what I'm pointing at tractable rather than a vibe?
