A colleague of mine has got it into his head that Einstein's hole argument is basically a neat empirical argument against substantivalism, not just a mathematical triviality. He keeps pointing to a bit in Einstein's The Foundation of the Generalised Theory of Relativity where Einstein writes: "The law of causality has the sense of a definite statement about the world of experience only when observable facts alone appear as causes and effects." From that he concludes that the substantivalist's spacetime points, which can be shifted around inside the hole without changing any observable facts, are not doing any causal work and should be dropped. I'm suspicious: that move seems partly conceptual and partly empirical. Is there any observation or experiment that could actually bear on whether spacetime points are real in the substantivalist sense, or on whether the substantivalist/relationalist dispute has empirical content? Set out the strongest test you can, explain what a positive result would and would not show, and be honest about the residue no test can reach.
