Okay so my metaphysics reading group has been stuck on the statue and the lump for like two months. The setup is the standard one: there's a lump of clay, call it Lumpl, and a sculptor makes a statue out of it, call it Goliath. Everyone agrees the statue and the lump are in the same place, made of the same clay, weigh the same, and so on. But one side says there's just one object there, because two material objects can't coincide. The other side says there must be two, because the lump can survive being squashed and Goliath can't. We've gone around and around and nobody's budged. Part of it is that the monist side keeps saying identity is just being the same thing, full stop, and the pluralist side keeps saying identity is always identity under some kind-term, so same what? matters. Then one of us brought in Aristotle's line from Physics I.7 — the part where he says the product isn't called bronze but brazen, because the matter gets predicated in an adjective-like way rather than as a noun, or at least that's how the translation put it. And weirdly both sides claimed that line supported them: the monists said see, there's only one subject (the statue) and the clay is just how its characterized; the pluralists said see, even Aristotle distinguished the statue from its material. That's when it hit me that maybe the whole argument isnt really about how many objects there are. Maybe it's a disagreement about what object or identity is even tracking in the first place. So my question: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it substantive, verbal, partly both? Is there a hidden decision about whether sortal properties get to count for identity, or about whether count terms like object are doing real work? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a new case, a formal result, or should we just change the question? Please answer in American English.
