Hi—PhD student in moral philosophy here, working through some suffering-focused ethics stuff. I keep seeing the asymmetry argument for negative utilitarianism put something like this: pain, or at least terrible pain, has priority over pleasure such that no amount of pleasure could justify it, so minimizing suffering comes first. The lexical-threshold version makes this precise by saying that above-threshold pain is lexically prior to pleasure.

What I want to know is whether that argument survives a changed assumption. Suppose we stop treating pain and pleasure as two quantities on a common scale and instead say they're incommensurable—not merely hard to compare, but not comparable in the relevant sense at all. That seems to help the negative utilitarian at first, because it gives a different reason for refusing the trade: it's not that the exchange rate is bad, it's that there isn't one. But it also seems to undercut the "lexical" part, since lexical priority is usually defined over a shared ordering, and incommensurability is symmetric, while the negative utilitarian needs a one-way priority of pain over pleasure.

So: does the asymetry argument survive incommensurability, and can the lexical-threshold view be restated without secretly assuming commensurability? Or does it collapse into a different view? I'd like the response in American English, and I'd rather you pick a side than just map the options.
