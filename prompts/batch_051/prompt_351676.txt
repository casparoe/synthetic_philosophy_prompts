I've been chewing on something I call the Simulation Dampener, and I want an honest assessment of whether it's any good.

Roughly: fanatical longtermist arguments say that even a tiny chance of steering the far future has enormous expected value because there might be astronomically many future lives. But if you assign any decent probablity to the simulation hypothesis — the idea that this whole world is a simulation run by some more fundamental society — the far future of this world is partly an artifact of what the simulators decided to run. Suppose I fund an AI alignment project to prevent a far-future catastrophe. The fanatic says the tiny chance it matters times the astronomical stakes still dominates everything else. The Dampener says: if we're simulated, then from the inside I can't distinguish the scenario where my intervention shifted the far future from the scenario where the simulators' schedule shifted it, and the conditional probability, given that I'm simulated, that my particular input is what reaches through to base reality is vanishingly small. So the fanatic's tiny probability should be replaced by something much tinier — roughly the probability of base reality times the probability that my action matters given base reality — and near-term certain goods win. I'm aware this may just be ordinary skepticism about far-future speculation with extra steps.

Give me four sections in this order. (1) The steelman (about 100 words): restate the Dampener in its strongest form, being more careful than I was. (2) Three strongest objections (about 250 words): object charitably, and include at least one objection that says the Dampener either proves too much or proves too little. (3) Is any fatal? (about 100 words): say honestly whether the objections kill it or just leave it bruised. (4) Your verdict (about 75 words): say what the Dampener actually is — a real principle, a reformulation of something older, or a useful mistake.
