A little over a year ago I adopted a rule about conversation, and I have begun to suspect that the rule is doing as much harm as good. The rule is this: when I form a belief about what a person meant, I allow myself to rely only on what their words mean literally, in their most ordinary sense. If someone says "the meeting is at three," I believe the meeting is at three. If someone says "it's cold in here," I believe the room feels cold to them, and I deliberately stop there. I do not infer that they want the window closed, that they are complaining about the thermostat, or anything further. I treat tone, hesitation, facial expression, pointed omissions, and what any reasonable listener would take the person to be implying as no evidence at all about what they meant; and I treat silence or a look as communicating nothing. My operating assumption is that if a person wanted me to know something, they would have said it.

I adopted this because my earlier habit of "reading between the lines" kept backfiring: I would act on an implication, and the person would deny having meant it, or would turn out to have meant something much weaker than I had assumed. But the replacement now backfires too. A friend wrote a reference for a job applicant saying only that the applicant was always punctual and kept an immaculate desk; I read that as praise and made the hire, and nearly everyone I mentioned it to thought the letter had plainly been a warning. A partner answered a question with a flat "I'm fine"; I accepted it as a report about how she felt, and the argument that followed made it clear she had meant something quite different. A doctor told me that "some patients find the procedure uncomfortable"; I thanked her and scheduled it, and only during a very painful afternoon did I recognize that she had been warning me in a register meant to prepare rather than frighten. I keep being assured that I am missing what is "obvious," yet on the many earlier occasions when I tried to reconstruct the obvious, I got it wrong.

What I want to know is whether my literal-only rule is a defensible policy, a bias dressed up as a principle for avoiding the work of understanding people, or something in between that can be restated. I am not asking about law, diplomacy, or any setting with fixed interpretive conventions; I mean ordinary talk among friends, family, and colleagues.
