I'm giving the opening remarks at my department's annual Sidgwick lecture dinner. The audience is a mix of moral philosophers who know The Methods of Ethics almost by heart, and their partners and parents who don't. I want to talk about how the book basically ends in failure — Sidgwick can't show that being good and being happy point the same way, and he calls the result a dualism of practical reason. But I also don't want to spoil dinner with existantial dread. Write me five minutes of remarks that use that dualism as the organising tension: what the philosophers can hear as serious and the laypeople can hear as warm and human, without me saying anything Sidgwick would have thought was wishful thinking. The remarks should feel like a toast, not a paper.
