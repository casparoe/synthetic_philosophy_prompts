Ok so I'm in a class on social philosophy and we keep going around about free riders versus parasites. I tried to build a tidy argument that they are the same kind of wrong, but the conclusion nags at me. Here it is:

(1) Social parasitism is wrong because the parasite takes the benefits of other people's cooperation while making those specific people bear costs that the parasite could have borne himself.
(2) Free riding has exactly the same structure: the free rider enjoys a good that other people's contributions produced, without contributing, even though he could have contributed.
(3) If two acts have the same structure, then the same moral assessment applies to both.
(4) So free riding is wrong for the same reason social parasitism is wrong.

I have a feeling (2) is doing something sneaky, because a free rider on a big public bus system or a pledge-funded radio station isn't making any particular person pick up his slack the way a parasite in a shared house makes his roomates do his dishes. But when I try to make that distinction precise it starts to feel like special pleading. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails and under what reading? If (2) is the problem, give the corrected premise and say whether free riding can still be wrong, but for a different reason. Max 600 words.
