I am a professor of philosophy and chair of our university's Campus Publications Review Board. A defamation complaint has come before the board, and I expect it to divide us. I need a decision memo from you that I can circulate before the vote.

Every term, a student-run publication called the Ledger publishes a Prospective Course Index covering every undergraduate course. For each course it lists five numerical scores, explicitly presented as projections: expected weekly reading load, expected difficulty of assessments, expected responsiveness of the instructor, expected clarity of grading standards, and an overall prospect score on a one-to-five scale. The scores are generated by a student-built model using past syllabi, historical student evaluations, course size, time of day, and instructor rank. The page labels every score as a forecast and includes a brief methods note saying that projections can be wrong.

A professor of economics has filed a defamation complaint against the publication. Her fall seminar receives an overall prospect score of 1.8, in the sixth percentile of all courses, and the accompanying prose tells students they should expect the course to be heavy, uneven, and poorly explained. She argues that these statements are defamatory: they carry the trappings of numerical measurement, present as fact the claim that her course will be of low quality, are read by students as reliable guidance rather than one student's opinion, and have already depressed enrollment and harmed her standing among colleagues. The editors respond that the figures are labeled as forecasts, that a projection about the future is not an assertion of present fact, and that an overall score is an evaluative judgment rather than a factual claim about the world.

Our policy makes a published statement about an identifiable person actionable only if a reasonable reader would understand it to assert or imply a false fact about that person; expressions of opinion are protected unless they carry an undisclosed defamatory factual implication.

Please draft the board's decision memo. It should recommend one of three outcomes: dismiss the complaint, uphold it and direct a correction, or require changes to how the Index is presented without finding for either party. The memo should state the test the board should use to separate factual assertions from expressions of opinion and forecasts, apply that test to the specific features of the Index, and give a serious and fair treatment of the strongest argument on the side you recommend against. It should be written so the rest of the board can vote on its basis.
