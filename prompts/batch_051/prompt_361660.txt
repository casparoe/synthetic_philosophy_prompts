People in my rationalist reading group keep reaching for the same picture whenever dreams come up: a dream is like a film running in a private cinema while you sleep, and telling someone the dream afterwards is like describing a film you watched. They use it to back the claim that dreams are experiences you have during sleep, just ones with a weird projectionist in your head. I've read enough Malcolm and Dennett to think the analogy is suppose to be doing a lot of sneaky work — Malcolm's point that the only real criterion for having dreamt is the waking report, so in an important sense there was no spectator in the cinema, and Dennett's cassette theory on which the whole narrative could be composed at waking and backdated into memory. Audit the analogy for me. Give me a correspondence table (projector, screen, audience, film, the later narration to a friend), then run through the places the mapping breaks, and say which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion and which don't. In particular, how badly do Malcolm's point and the cassette theory each damage the private-screening picture, and does the conclusion it was being used to support survive any of it? If there's a better analogy, say what it is, or tell me the whole film metaphor is unsalvageable.
