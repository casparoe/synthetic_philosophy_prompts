Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether consequentialism can actually guide action, and we kept circling the same impasse that seems to run through the literature on the subject. The objection, in the strong form that troubles me, goes like this: for nearly any ordinary decision — whether to take a new job, to have a child, to give to one charity rather than another — the consequences ramify indefinitely. A different breakfast changes my mood, which changes a conversation, which changes who meets whom, which changes which children are ever born; a century later the world contains an entirely different population. We cannot enumerate the possible downstream states, let alone assign them probabilities, so the consequentialist's demand that we evaluate acts by their expected consequences looks unmeetable. My friend's reply, which I could not refute, is that this is simply the ordinary condition of finite agents acting under uncertainty: no principle for action, consequentialist or otherwise, escapes open-ended ignorance about the future, and consequentialism at least tells us to do our best with the expected values we can estimate. We agree on all the facts about our ignorance; we disagree about what those facts are supposed to show about a moral theory. That is where the argument died.

I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict on consequentialism. Is this a substantive disagreement about decision theory — for instance, about whether expected-value reasoning is even well-formed when the outcome space is radically open-ended and we cannot specify the alternatives — or is it really a disagreement about what we are entitled to demand of a moral theory, such as determinate verdicts rather than something weaker like narrowing the options or ranking only foreseeable consequences? Is it perhaps several different questions — epistemological, decision-theoretic, and metaethical — all traveling under the single name 'cluelessness'? And given the diagnosis you defend, what would actually count as progress: a formal result about dominance or dynamic consistency under cluelessness, a sharper distinction between types of uncertainty, a reformulation of the original question, or a demonstration that the two sides are answering different questions and should not expect to converge?
