I'm a high-school student, and I've worked out a small theory from a thought experiment of my own design. I want a candid assessment of the theory itself, not a reading list or citations.

The device is a sealed drum spinning at constant speed on a frictionless axle. Inside it are thousands of tiny steel balls that collide elastically with the walls and with each other, so the ball-level motion never dies down. Fitted into the inner wall are hinged letter-tiles, light enough that a single ball can flip one over; each tile stays flipped until another ball flips it back. The tiles are placed so that the pattern of flipped and unflipped tiles is stable enough to read as words, even while the balls underneath it churn.

The idea this machine suggests to me I've been calling the sieve principle. It says: when a higher-level pattern is stable across many possible micro-arrangements, the pattern is not merely a summary of the micro-state; it is a real constraint on the micro-state. It selects among the trajectories that would otherwise be physically possible, much as the shape of a sieve — itself just an arrangement of metal atoms — selects which grains can pass through and which cannot. Applied to the drum, the words on the wall are not epiphenomenal decorations; they belong in the causal story about what the balls do next, because the stable word-pattern narrows the set of ball-level histories compatible with it. Put bluntly, I'm claiming that symbols can push the substrate around — not by adding forces, but by constraining which lower-level histories are available.

I can see two readings of my own principle. On one reading it is a piece of honest metaphysics: a mild downward causation in which higher levels act by filtering possibilities rather than by injecting energy. On the other, it is a metaphor dressed up as physics: narrowing the set of histories is just bookkeeping about descriptions, and all actual pushing happens at the level of balls and tiles. The distinction I cannot draw on my own is between a pattern the system itself uses, like a sieve's shape, and a pattern that merely rides along with the causal facts, like the sieve's shadow. If my principle cannot mark that line, I suspect it says too much.

Tell me which reading is right, even if the honest answer is neither or your distinction is confused. Is the sieve principle a real concept, or does it collapse into something already worked out under an established name? Does a stable pattern's constraining its substrate license saying the symbols themselves do causal work, or only that they give a useful description of causal work done by the substrate? And where exactly does the principle break — what would have to be true of the drum for the words to be genuinely epiphenomenal in spite of what the principle says?
