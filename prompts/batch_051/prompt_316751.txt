I am giving a seminar paper defending the claim that the all-things-considered relation "is better than" is not transitive, and that the right response to spectrum arguments is to accept a benign intransitivity rather than to dissolve it. The paper's running example is a continuum of possible lives running from a very short life of nearly unbearable pain to a very long life of mild discomfort: each adjacent step looks like an improvement, but the endpoints reverse the judgment, and I argue that the pairwise comparisons use shifting standards of salience along the spectrum while the endpoint comparison uses a single fixed standard, so no single mistake or equivocation is doing the work. The audience is a workshop of decision theorists, formal epistemologists, and welfare economists, not population ethicists; they treat transitivity as a non-negotiable coherence constraint and will reach for money pumps, representability by a real-valued utility function, and the proposal that I should replace "better than" with a transitive surrogate. Prepare me for the question period with a prep sheet, not a polished defense: the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as a decision theorist or economist would actually phrase them, with how damaging each is, what to concede up front, what to hold, a reply that fits a two-minute exchange, and where each reply is soft. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks. Throughout, separate what is settled in this literature from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
