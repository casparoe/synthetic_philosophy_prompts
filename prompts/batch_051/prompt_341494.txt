I'm a story editor in a writers' room for a fantasy prestige show. The show's bible says the world was made by a single creator who's entirely good, and that the first beings — angels — were created directly by this creator, in full possession of their powers, with no history of abuse, no flawed nature, and no prior evil around to tempt or corrupt them. Each season, one high angel falls and becomes the first evil of that storyline, and the whole season is the story of how that happens. But our drafts keep failing in one of two directions: either the fall is fully explained by a flaw in the angel or a flaw in the creator, so it ends up reading as a manufacturing defect rather than a free fall; or the fall has no motivation at all, so the audience says it just happened for no reason and it doesn't count as a choice either. I need a decision procedure my writers can run on any beat sheet or draft to tell whether a proposed angelic fall is coherent given the cosmology, and what specifically has to change to fix it if it's not.

We run the procedure every two weeks during pre-production, on all new fall pitches. The writers have zero philosophy background, so it should be a sequence of questions in order, with what counts as a passing answer at each step, where the procedure stops and sends the writers back, and where it has to hand the decision to the showrunner because its a values call rather than a coherence check. Keep it to about a page. Then apply it to three of our actual problem drafts:

1. The fall is caused by envy: the creator announces that a new, lower kind of being — humans — will receive a special place, and the angel reads this as a real demotion, even though the script says this is a misreading and no one has actually been wronged.
2. The angel discovers a flaw in its own design, a latent tendency toward pride that activates only under a particular trigger. The fall just is the activation of that flaw.
3. The angel sees with complete clarity that the act is evil and destructive, does it anyway, and the script refuses to give any further motive. The point is that the rebellion is meant to be gratuitous and inexplicable.

The procedure should discriminate between "coherent but dramatically weak" and "incoherent given the premise," because lots of incoherent things are dramatic — I don't need help with pacing or character design. Ground the questions in whatever philosophical theology and action theory actually say about how an originally good, free being could be the origin of evil, and be honest about where two defensible theories of free action would give opposite verdicts, or where the procedure can't settle the question and a person has to choose.
