so for a seminar on supererogation i had to write down what i actually think, and now i'd like a consistency check before i defend it. here's the list:

(1) there really are supererogatory acts: donating a kidney to a stranger is morally good but not required, and running into a burning building to rescue someone when you could safetly have stayed back is supererogatory too.

(2) supererogatory acts are morally better than the merely permissible alternatives, and whenever one act is morally better than another there's a pro tanto moral reason to choose the better one, even if both acts are permissible.

(3) what keeps the better act optional is that an agent's own interests can legitimately limit what morality demands of them.

(4) the cost to the agent matters only for whether an act is required, not for how morally good it is: saying a rescue is supererogatory because it's dangerous is not saying the rescue is any less good than a safe rescue with the same benefit.

(5) someone who passes up a supererogatory act is not subject to any negative moral assessment at all—not blame, not shame, not even the mild verdict that they acted less admirably.

(6) when it comes to assessing acts, the deontic categories (required, permitted, forbidden) are basic; saying an act is 'good' or 'better' is just a loose way of saying it meets or exceeds what duty requires.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest single revision would be and what it costs.
