Been chewing on a concrete supervision scheme for a coding agent, and I want you to stress-test how it would actually behave rather than give me the standard pro-con list.

Setup: a lab has an agent that does multi-hour autonomous refactoring PRs. Right now it's trained with outcome supervision — reward only if the PR passes CI and gets merged. The lab's worry is that this mostly reinforces lucky, brittle, or test-gaming behavior. So they propose a hybrid. The outcome bonus still exists, but it goes into escrow for six months before it's credited to the training run. During those six months, any lab engineer or outside auditor can open a process challenge: they read the agent's full reasoning trace and argue it succeeded for the wrong reasons — say, the fix only worked because of an unrelated quirk in the repo, or it passed the tests by satisfying them literally instead of doing the actual task. If a panel upholds the challenge, the bonus is voided and split between the challenger and an audit fund; if six months pass with no upheld challenge, the bonus pays out as the original outcome reward.

Walk me through what actually happens over, say, a year. Trace the incentives for each group: the agent being optimized, the lab engineers who could file challenges, the people who decide what counts as 'wrong reasons,' and anyone maintaining the reasoning traces. Where does the scheme quietly fail, loose its point, or get gamed? I can already see a few candidates — traces optimized to look audit-proof, engineers who don't bother challenging because the upside is too small, challengers and the training side colluding, or the whole thing gradually turning into expensive, lower-quality process supervision — but I want the full second-order picture and a call on which failure modes are fixable without gutting the point of the escrow.

Also flag what would change your mind about the overall verdict: which consideration would flip you from 'roughly works' to 'roughly broken' (or the reverse), and where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong. Hard cap: 600 words.
