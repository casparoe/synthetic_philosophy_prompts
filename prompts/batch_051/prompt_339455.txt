I got into a friendly argument with my roommate last night, because she kept talking like she was going to the gym while also making it clear she hadn't actually decided. At one point she said, "I'd put myself at about 70% on gym tomorrow. That's just my honest credence." I said that can't be a real credence, because she's the one who decides, and she told me to prove it. So I wrote the argument out like this:

(1) If you are genuinely deliberating about whether to do A, then whether you will do A is still open: neither "I will A" nor "I won't A" is settled.
(2) You can only have a determinate degree of confidence in the answer to a question if there's already a fact out there that your confidence is an estimate of.
(3) While you're still deciding whether to A, there is no fact out there about whether you'll A — that gets settled by your deciding, not before it.
(4) So, while you're deliberating, you can't coherently assign a probability to "I will A."
(5) Therefore anyone who gives a probability for their own future action is either not really deliberating, or isn't really giving a probability.

I keep going back and forth on it. I suspect something in step (2) is sneaky but I can't make it precise. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and is the failure just a matter of rejecting a controversial premise, or is there also a validity problem in the move from those steps to (4)? And if the argument is right, I'd want to know why everyday talk like "I'll probably cave and get dessert" doesn't sink it.
