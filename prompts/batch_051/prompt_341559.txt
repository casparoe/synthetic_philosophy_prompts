I've been lying awake recently thinking about whole-brain emulation, because I'm about to turn forty and my partner and I are seriously weighing whether to sign up for cryonics — not full of hope, just doing the arithmetic. The thing I keep going in circles on isn't whether the technology will ever exist, it's a more basic question I can't get a straight answer to: if someone made a genuinely faithful emulation of my brain and my biological brain were destroyed in the scanning process, would I survive? I mean survive in the practical sense — the sense in which it would be rational for me now to take risks on the assumption that the emulation's experiences will be mine, that I'll be the one having them and not just some very good copy.

So I want a number from you, not a survey. What's your credence that a destructive whole-brain emulation of me is me, in that survival sense? And I want the decomposition. I can see at least five places the probability could live: (a) how likely it is that what makes me me is a computational or functional pattern rather than this particular biological wetware; (b) how likely it is that the scan would capture the causally relevant structure and not just a coarse behavioral approximation; (c) how much rides on whether identity tracks psychological continuity rather than continuity of the body or brain; (d) how much rides on the emulation being turned on at all, or being continuously run, rather than being a dormant file; and (e) the copy problem — whether destroying the original during scanning actually helps, or whether a destructive scan is still a kind of suicide plus replacement. I'd also like you to tell me if theres another term I'm missing that belongs in the decomposition. For each, give me a number and a sentence or two; then show how you combine them; then tell me which single term you're least confident about and what evidence would move it the most. My own gut on (e) is that destruction doesn't rescue it much, but I keep flip-flopping.
