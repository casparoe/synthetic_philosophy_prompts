I have been working through Kavka's toxin puzzle and have collected what I take to be the stock ways out, but I now suspect that the list has closed off the obvious answers and I want something genuinely beyond it. The setup, to fix the target: a billionaire will pay you a million dollars tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin will make you ill for a day but does no lasting damage; since the payment is made before you would have to drink, tomorrow the drinking itself will have a cost and no remaining benefit. The puzzle is how a rational agent can genuinely form the intention at midnight and collect, given that the agent already knows he or she will not drink tomorrow.

Here are the moves I already have and do not need re-presented. (1) The precommitment move: bind yourself now through a side bet, contract, or credible penalty, so that tomorrow drinking has a reason after all. (2) The preference-change move: alter what you will want tomorrow, by drug, conditioning, or some other device, so that drinking is no longer something you expect to avoid. (3) The self-deception move: cause yourself to believe, falsely, that you will drink, so that the intention can coexist with the needed expectation. (4) The higher-order move: at midnight intend to intend to drink, or intend to bring it about that you intend, rather than intending the drinking directly. (5) The diagnostic move: the puzzle has no untempered solution, and a rational agent cannot collect; the lesson is that intention is answerable to norms that expected-utility reasoning at the moment of action does not capture. (6) The dissolution move: deny that intending requires believing you will do the thing, treating intention as a commitment rather than a prediction. (7) The policy move: make yourself the kind of agent who carries out such intentions, because that disposition does better across the class of similar offers than a disposition to reconsider at the moment of action. (8) The present-act move: treat the forming of the intention at midnight as itself the action with the best consequences, so that the rationality of the later drinking is a separate question. (9) The commitment move: let the intention itself be what gives tomorrow's drinking its reason, so that once you intend, following through has a motivation it would otherwise lack. (10) The private-verification diagnosis: the case makes a private mental state the condition of payment, and once that idealization is granted the apparent impasse may be, in part, an artifact of the setup rather than something to solve within it.

What I want is a further way out: a proposed solution, or a diagnosis of why the puzzle misfires, that is not a restatement, special case, or combination of the ten items above. If the honest verdict is that no such move exists, I would rather have that position defended than a known move dressed up as a new one. If you offer a move, then assess it honestly: does it let an ordinary agent collect the money without changing the game, and does it survive the obvious reply that, at the moment of drinking, the agent still has no reason to drink? At the end, say how confident you are in the move's novelty and in your assessment, and single out the point you are least sure of.
