I am a second-year undergraduate reading politics and currently on a placement in the policy unit of a county council; my political theory course this term has covered Burkean conservatism and Chesterton's fence. The unit regularly receives proposals to abolish long-standing council rules and practices — notice and publication requirements, dual sign-off rules, committee referral requirements — whose original rationale no current officer can reconstruct, and I have been asked to draft a short, reusable decision procedure that a non-specialist officer could run in under an hour, without legal counsel, before the file goes to an elected member (councillor) for sign-off. Please write it as an ordered set of questions, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure should stop with a clear 'retain' or 'remove', and where it should instead hand the decision to a councillor because the question is one of values rather than investigation. Then run the procedure on two worked cases: a rule that certain public notices must still be published in a paid local newspaper, and a rule that any reallocation of funds within a budget line under £500 must be countersigned by two councillors. Show where the procedure goes against the natural instinct either to tidy things up or to preserve things by default, and mark which steps rest on what is settled about these ideas and which rest on a genuinely contested reading of Burke or Chesterton rather than presenting a contested reading as the consensus.
