I'm in a grad seminar on public reason and we've been going back and forth over the same Rawls-versus-his-critics material for weeks, and the discussion has gotten strangely stuck in a way I want to understand better. The two camps seem to be these. The public reason people say: if a law is going to coerce people, citizens have to be able to justify it to one another in terms all reasonable people could accept, so a religious reason can't by itself settle a political question — you need either a shareable reason or, on Rawls's later proviso, a commitment to eventually supplement it with one. The critics say this rigs the game in favor of secular citizens and forces religious people to treat their deepest commitments as private preferences; they think what matters is whether the law is just, not whether the reason for it is stated in the approved vocabulary. Take the old Sunday-closing example: one side says the shareable reason that everyone benefits from a common weekly day of rest is the legitimate public reason, the other says that's a post hoc translation of the religious reason about keeping the Lord's day, and that requiring the translation is itself a substantive secularist demand.

Heres the thing that frustrates me: both sides accept something like the principle that you shouldn't coerce people on grounds they can't reasonably reject. Both are anti-theocratic; both agree religious majorities shouldn't just impose their will. And yet the debate hasn't moved in forty years. My working theory is that it's not really a moral disagreement about coercion at all but a disagreement about what counts as a reason in politics — or maybe about the metaphysics of reasons, whether your reason is the psychological state that actually moves you or the proposition that can be shared — but I could also see it being bedrock intuitions about whether respect means talking to people in their own terms or letting them be sincere about what actually moves them. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive about morality, about standards for what counts as a public reason, or about starting intuitions neither side can argue for — and say what, if anything, would count as progress given that diagnosis? I'd also like an honest answer about whether the impasse means the question is badly posed rather than just hard.
