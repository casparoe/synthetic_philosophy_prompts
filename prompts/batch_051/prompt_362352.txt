I'm putting together a one-page handout for an upper-level metaphysics seminar (about 15 students) on how objects persist through change, and I want the centerpiece to be a decision tree that takes a student from something they'll almost certainly accept — a banana ripens, a person sits then stands, a cat can loose its tail and still be around — to a named position in the persistence debate. The point is to sort them by what they actually say about cases, not by which label they like; too many of them call themselves endurantists and then give perdurantist answers to every concrete case I put to them. So each branch node should be a concrete case or a forced choice about what a sentence commits you to, phrased so nobody needs to know the labels already (no node like 'do objects have temporal parts?'), and I'd like the tree to start from the standing/sitting sentence if that works, since that's where the split feels cleanest. The leaves should include at least endurantism, perdurantism (the worm view), and stage theory, and these cases should be woven in somewhere: a lump of clay shaped into a statue and later squashed, a cat that loses its tail, 'I was standing and now I'm sitting,' and maybe the road that's smooth in one stretch and bumpy in another.

Build the tree from the logical space first — lay out the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and only then place the named views in that space. For any combination of answers no actual position occupies, give a one-line note on whether it's empty because the view would be incoherent, too costly to defend, or merely unexplored. Each leaf should carry a one-sentence 'most awkward commitment.' Also flag any pair of leaves the tree can't genuinely separate: I'd bet on worm perdurantism and stage theory needing an extra question about what 'you' or 'this' picks out, so say what that extra question would be rather than pretending the cases do the separating work. And try not to let the tree quietly collapse into presentism-versus-eternalism; if that axis intrudes somewhere, say where and why it has to be a separate choice. Keep it to something like 6-9 branch nodes and 4-5 leaves so it actually fits on the handout.
