I've fallen into an epistemic habit that I half-suspect is just a bias wearing a philosophy-of-science hat. Whenever someone offers a mental-disorder category as the explanation of a behavior, I silently translate it into a description of the behavior and then refuse to let the category do any additional work. So "he missed the deadline because of his ADHD" becomes "he missed the deadline because he loses track of time and can't hold a plan together"—which I tell myself is just the first sentence with the label peeled off, not an explanation of it. I got this from the idea that psychiatric labels aren't disease entities the way typhoid or cirrhosis are, just shorthand for symptom clusters. But I've caught myself applying the rule even when the diagnosis clearly adds something: knowing a friend has bipolar predicts how she'll respond to lithium, and my rewrite doens't capture that. Is the habit defensible? When does a psychiatric category genuinely explain behavior rather than merely redescribe it? And can I restate the policy so it keeps the critical edge without throwing away real causal information?
