I'm attracted to objective Bayesianism because it seems to give priors the same kind of status that deductive logic gives to inference: given only the constraints you actually have, the maximum-entropy distribution is supposed to be the uniquely least-committal choice, so following it isn't just a heuristic but a rational requirement. Early in E. T. Jaynes's 1957 paper Information Theory and Statistical Mechanics, he says, in effect, that information theory provides a constructive criterion for setting up probability distributions on the basis of partial knowledge, and that this criterion is what leads to the maximum-entropy estimate. That one sentence is basically the whole appeal for me.

But I can't get past a very firm intuition. Suppose I genuinely know only that a quantity lies somewhere between 0 and 1. If I work with the quantity itself, maxent gives me a flat prior, and I feel fine. But if I decide to describe the same state of knowledge by the odds, or by its log-odds, maxent gives me a completely different distribution: flat in the new variable is not flat in the old one. Same information, different 'rational' prior, depending on how I happen to label things. I know the standard replies: ignorance isn't invariant, transformation groups can pick out a preferred parameterisation, and the prior is meant to encode the information you actually use in the problem rather than some cosmic lack of information. But every time I try to apply them, it still feels like the rule is tracking my descriptive choices rather than my information. A logic of plausible reasoning shouldn't be dependant on whether I write p or log(p/(1-p)).

I don't want a potted answer that just tells me to accept representation-dependence or to restrict maxent to natural partitions. I want to know what each side would have to give up for a negotiated verdict. Is the intuition something I should debunk and drop, or should the maxent principle be amended by adding an invariance requirement, or is there a reading of Jaynes's constructive-criterion claim on which both survive? And say how confident you are in your answer, and which specific move you're least sure of.
