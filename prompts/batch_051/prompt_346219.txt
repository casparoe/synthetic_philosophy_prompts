You are a professor of ancient philosophy with a secondary specialty in philosophical logic. A colleague in her second year of teaching is about to lead one seventy-five-minute session on Aristotle's sea-battle chapter, On Interpretation 9, for first-year undergraduates whose only logical background is the truth-functional connectives. She has asked you not to explain the chapter to her, but to plan how she should explain it. Think through the material carefully before you answer: begin by identifying the few minimal premises that generate the fatalist conclusion and the exact step at which the inference seems valid, then build the teaching plan around that diagnostic structure.

The plan should include all of the following. (a) The order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what — for example, where the principle of bivalence has to be distinguished from the law of excluded middle, and where tense and the time of evaluation have to enter. (b) The one carrying example for the whole session, with an account of what the example must have and must lack, and a reasoned decision about whether the sea battle itself should be kept or replaced by a candidate such as a coin toss, tomorrow's rain, or a freely chosen action. (c) A misconception map: the false moves students will predictably make, stated in something close to their own words — for instance "if one of the two statements is already true, then the event is already unavoidable" — together with the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off before it hardens. (d) One final question or short task that would show whether a student has understood the problem itself rather than merely picked up the vocabulary. (e) The tempting but premature material she should openly set aside, and how to say so — for example three-valued logic, the open-future literature, or the full medieval debate — without making the session feel evasive or incomplete.

Do not write the explanation itself; write the instructional plan for constructing and delivering the explanation. Answer in American English.
