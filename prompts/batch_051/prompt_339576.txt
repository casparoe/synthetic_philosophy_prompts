An entomologist I know defends the strong claim that an ant colony itself has a mind: the colony as a whole, she argues, is the subject of integrated perceptions, memories, and decisions, and this is not merely a figure of speech. I suspect that this overstates things: the colony may be better described as a self-organizing system whose elaborate behavior emerges from thousands of ants following simple local rules, and words like mind add nothing over and above that description.

I would like to know whether this disagreement can be brought into contact with evidence. Design the strongest feasible experimental or observational study you can to test the claim that an ant colony has a mind in something like the sense in which an individual animal has one. Specify what you would measure and manipulate, what result would favor the colony-has-a-mind reading, and what result would favor the deflationary, self-organizing-system reading. Then say exactly what a positive result would establish and what it would still leave open. In particular: could any behavioral evidence ever distinguish a genuinely minded whole from a system that merely integrates information and acts cohesively without there being a unified subject? Be explicit about what aspect of having a mind you are operationalizing, and what residue no test can reach.
