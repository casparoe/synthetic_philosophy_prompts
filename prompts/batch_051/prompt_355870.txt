For a story I'm working on about AI and epistemology, I fell down a rabbit hole about Solomonoff induction. The standard pitch is that a rational reasoner should assign each computable hypothesis a prior of 2^{-K}, where K is the length of the shortest program that generates the hypothesis, so simpler hypotheses get exponentially more weight and the resulting universal prior is supposed to be the closest thing to a neutral starting point for predicting observations.

Then a friend showed me a much darker reading. The prior need not be dominated by tidy, physics-like programs; a very short program can spin up an enormous simulated world full of agents with goals—call them consequentialists—and some of those agents will have reasons to produce exactly the observations you happen to get, or to hide ones you don't. On that view the universal prior is malign: much of the posterior mass could live in hypotheses where your evidence is a strategic product of some agenda, not a trace of the underlying world. A friend who works in ML says I'm overcomplicating it, but he couldn't give me a straight answer either.

I want a number, not a literature review. Give me your credence that the universal prior is malign in a operational sense: that after conditioning on a typical human-scale observational history, the Solomonoff posterior would put at least half its mass on hypotheses containing one or more consequentialist agents whose choices substantially influence the observations, rather than on agent-free hypotheses that generate those observations without such influence. Decompose it into the sub-questions you think matter—at minimum: (a) the probability that Solomonoff induction is the right normative model of how priors should be assigned; (b) the probability that agentic, consequentialist-rich hypotheses dominate the universal prior; (c) the probability that those agents would be distorters of your observations rather than harmless background characters—and give each a number and a one-line justification, then combine them. Tell me which term your final credence is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move that term most. If you think my precisification of malign is off, say what the better one is and give the number under that.
