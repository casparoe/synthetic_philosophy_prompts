Im an economist by training, so my instinct with every argument is to try to translate it into coordinates I can actually work with: externalities, transaction costs, endogenous preferences, willingness to accept versus willingness to pay, price as information, that kind of thing. The one position I keep failing to translate in a way that feels honest is the commodification critique. Roughly, the view that certain goods shouldnt be bought and sold — kidneys, votes, sex, queue-jumping, the right to shoot an endangered rhino, surrogacy — not just because someone gets hurt or exploited, but because the act of pricing them changes the good itself, or expresses a debased evaluation of it, or crowds out the altruistic or civic norms that make the underlying practice work. The claim is not that poor sellers get a raw deal (though they might) nor that buyers are harmed (though they might be); its that the market corrupts or degrades what is being traded, even in a case where both parties walk away happy and no identifiable third party complains.

What I want is a systematic translation. Take each main move of the commodification critique and restate it in economic vocabulary as cleanly as you can. For instance: is the corruption-of-a-good claim really an externality claim, where the trade changes the menu of norms available to non-participants? Is the crowding-out-of-altruism claim really a preference-endogeneity claim, where markets shape the preferences they later satisfy so revealed preference loses its normal justificatory force? Is the worry about attaching money to the good a claim that price is the wrong kind of information about what the good is for? Then give me a residue report, move by move: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only by being distorted in a specific way, and which have no expression in economic vocabulary at all. And at the end adjudicate the residue: does it show the commodification critique is carrying something economics has no room for and maybe should make room for, or does it show the critique is less precise than it looked once you force it into operational terms? Be honest about the translation — don't just relabel a non-economic concept as an externality and call it a success.
