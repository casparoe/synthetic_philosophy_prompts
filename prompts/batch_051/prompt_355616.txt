I build agent-based simulations for a living, so my working vocabulary runs to agents, local interaction rules, bounded information, macro-state variables, emergence, phase transitions, and coarse-grained descriptions that hold across many parameter settings. I want that vocabulary used to restate a methodological dispute in the social sciences. Individualists hold that a social-scientific explanation is adequate only if it bottoms out in individuals—their beliefs, preferences, actions, and interactions—whereas holists hold that social structures, institutions, and collective properties do genuine explanatory work and are not just shorthand or aggregation artifacts. My hunch is that the dispute is roughly the difference between a simulation whose macro-outputs are fully computed from the micro-states and one in which a macro-variable feeds back into the agents' local rules, but I suspect that mapping distorts at least one side. Restate each side in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, then give me the failure report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Finally, say whether what fails to translate shows that my vocabulary is missing something social scientists need, or that one side of their debate is less precise than it presents itself as being.
