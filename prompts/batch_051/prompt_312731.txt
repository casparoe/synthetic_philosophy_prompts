For the past year I have been working through the formal semantics of indicative conditionals, and last week the professor I assist asked me to fill in for a fifty-minute seminar in an advanced undergraduate logic course. The students have completed a term of propositional and predicate logic and a short unit on probability, so they can manipulate truth tables and conditional probabilities, but they have never seen a probabilistic semantics for natural language, and most of them currently regard an indicative conditional as simply the material conditional or a slightly hedged version of it. The assigned topic is Lewis's triviality results: the results that appear to show that one cannot in general identify the probability of an indicative conditional with the corresponding conditional probability, via the equation P(if A, then C) = P(C | A), without collapsing the probability function into something trivial. I want them to leave understanding why this is a serious problem for anyone who wanted truth conditions for conditionals, and not merely to have watched me reproduce a derivation.

Plan the session's explanatory arc rather than the explanation itself. What has to be in place first, and why: what exactly is being equated with what, and which background assumptions about propositions, probability, and the behavior of conditional probabilities are doing the work before any algebra appears? What one carrying example should the whole session be built around, and what must it have and lack for that purpose? Which misconceptions should I expect, and where in the sequence should each be headed off? I am anticipating at least these: conflating the indicative conditional with the material conditional, thinking the result is a technical artifact of one particular proof, and concluding that ordinary conditional reasoning has been shown to be irrational. Finally, what should I openly decline to prove or discuss at this level, and what closing question would let me tell whether the students have grasped the force of the result rather than merely followed the steps?
