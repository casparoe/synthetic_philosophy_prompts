In debates about populism I keep encountering an argument that treats "the will of the people" as more than a figure of speech: the electorate is conceived as a single agent, with desires, a power of deliberation, and one voice, and an election or referendum is the moment when that agent speaks. The conclusion the analogy is used to support is that once the people have spoken, the only democratic response is to implement that will directly, and that courts, experts, and dissenting minorities are obstructing a single collective decision. I want an audit of how far this analogy actually reaches. First, give the correspondence table explicitly — which features of an individual person (desires, deliberation, a body, a single voice, continuity over time) map onto which features of a democratic public. Then say where the mapping breaks, separate the disanalogies that matter for the populist conclusion from the ones that don't, and state whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If a better analogy for what an election does is available, give that too.
