Me and my brother have spent the last year arguing about what, if anything, we owe our parents now that we're both in our thirties and living on opposite coasts. He holds a debts-of-gratitude view: our parents put decades of money, time, and worry into us, so we owe them help and contact in something like proportion to that, until the ledger is balanced. I'm more drawn to a friendship model: whatever grown children owe their parents is whatever friends would owe each other—not repayment for past sacrifice, and not automatically more just because they're our parents. We've realized we've mostly been swapping anecdotes and talking past each other, so we want to write the thing people in adversarial collaborations write: the claims we can both sign, the exact places we split, and the cruxes that would actually move one of us if we could settle them.

Draft that document. Include (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can accept—about parental sacrifice, what it does and doesn't generate, what adult children owe inside a continuing warm relationship, and what they owe when the relationship is essentially just biological or minimal; (2) a disagreements table where each sides view is stated in wording that side would accept, not a caricature; and (3) for each genuine crux, a concrete case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I suspect one crux is whether one-sided parental sacrifice generates a debt even when the current relationship has gone cold, and another is whether the friendship model can explain obligations to a demanding parent who really was good to us. If part of the fight turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.
