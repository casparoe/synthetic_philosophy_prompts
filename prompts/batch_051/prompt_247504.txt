I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on how to define honesty and deception for AI systems, and I need a thought experiment built to a pretty specific set of specs for the second chapter of my dissertation. The case is for a section where I argue that honesty for AI shouldn't be reduced to whether the system's outputs are true, because deception can happen through a system that never says a false thing.

Here's what I want you to construct. Take two accounts of AI deception. Intent-based account: a deployed system deceives a user iff the deployer acts through the system with the intention of causing that user to acquire or maintain a false belief, and the user actually ends up with the false belief. Output-based account (what I'm calling truthfulness-first): a system deceives a user only if the system emits at least one assertion that is false or misleading given the facts and the context; a system that emits only accurate, non-misleading assertions isn't deceptive, full stop.

Now build me a case where the intent-based account says the system deceived the user, but the output-based account says it didn't. This is the direction people usually dont bother to construct, since the obvious AI cases go the other way (a hallucinating chatbot misleads with no intent). Constraints: realistic (a workplace, a benefits office, a hospital—not a lab, no sci-fi), the system's outputs all have to be literally true and not misleading in their content, and the false belief has to be produced by something the user already believes rather than by anything the system asserts. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

After the scenario, walk through what each account says about it and why. Then tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove that feature (do the two accounts collapse into agreement?), and how a defender of the output-based account would attack the case—e.g., would they say this is the deployer deceiving the user rather than the AI system, and does that distinction actually matter?
