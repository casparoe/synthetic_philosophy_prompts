My department has been running a yearlong adversarial collaboration between a methodological individualist and a methodological holist in social science, and it is time to write the joint statement they should have produced. Draft it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.

The agreed-claims section should cover at least: what each side accepts about the other's core insight (individualists about the causal role of institutions and norms, holists about the individual-level composition of social facts); what supervenience of social facts on individuals does and does not settle; why neither side is committed to the caricature versions of its view; and what constraints on explanation, if any, both accept. The claims should be concrete enough that neither party would sign them only because they are empty.

In the disagreements table, for each residual dispute give the individualist's formulation and the holist's formulation, each in wording its own side would accept; the crux on which the dispute turns; and a concrete case or body of evidence both would accept bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I suspect the genuine disputes include whether structural explanations are autonomous or merely compressed individual-level explanations, and whether a strict microfoundations requirement is defensible. Cases I think both would take seriously include the persistence of institutions through complete turnover of personnel, the stability of aggregate market behavior despite idiosyncratic individual decisions, and Schelling-style segregation. Include these if they earn their place, add others if needed, and say explicitly where a disagreement looks as though it may be verbal rather than substantive.
