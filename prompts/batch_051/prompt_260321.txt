Teaching longtermism this term has me realizing I keep hand-waving whenever the simulation argument comes up, so I want help thinking it through, not a survey of what people have said. The simulation argument could bear on far-future priorities in several directions at once. It might raise the probability that we're living in a simulation, which could make our apparent far future less real or less under our control. It might, instead, raise the probability that a posthuman, technologically mature stage is reachable by someone, which sounds like it should encourage far-future work. It might change how many future minds we should expect and what kind they would be — if almost all minds in the future are simulated, then the relevant target of our concern is different than we assumed. And it might change what should even count as an existential catastrophe, because a simulation being switched off isn't obviously the same thing as extinction, though it isn't obviously different either. What I want is a ranking. Which of these purported implications actually carry weight for someone deciding whether to spend a career on existential-risk reduction rather than on nearer-term problems, and which are overrated or don't follow at all? Defend the ordering — I care about the criteria you're using to rank them, and I want you to stick the landing on the bottom line: should the simulation argument make a longtermist more fanatical about the far future, less fanatical, or just less certain that the question is well-posed?
