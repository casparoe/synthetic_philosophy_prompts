Okay, I keep going back and forth on when a probability is enough to find someone liable or convict them, and my gut virdicts don't line up with any rule I can state. Here are the cases as I actually see them:

1. A pedestrian is hit by a bus at night and can't identify the company. The only evidence is that 80% of buses on that road at that hour belong to Blue Line. I don't think she should win her civil suit against Blue Line, even though 80% is more likely than not.

2. A rodeo's headcount shows 1,000 people inside, but payment records show only 499 tickets sold, so 501 people must have gotten in free. The organizer picks one attendee at random and sues for the ticket price. I wouldn't make that person pay, even though it's a hair over even odds that they crashed.

3. In a prison yard, a guard is attacked. Surveillance shows 99 of the 100 prisoners on the yard joined in and one stood back, but the footage can't tell which is which. I don't think any one of them can be convicted beyond a reasonable doubt.

4. Same hit-and-run as the first case, but now a witness says he saw a Blue Line logo on the bus. Witnesses of this sort are right about bus liveries about 70% of the time. Here I would, a bit hesitantly, let the victim win against Blue Line. This is the pair that bothers me: the number is lower than case 1, but I feel differently.

5. A burglar cut himself on broken glass, and the blood matches the defendant. The lab says a random person would match one time in ten million. No other evidence. I'd have no real hesitation convicting.

So in cases 1–3 I seem to think even a very high probability isn't enough, while in 4 and 5 I'm fine with probabilities that are similar or lower because the evidence feels 'individualized.' But I can't state the principle doing the work.

Is there one principle—or a small set of candidate principles—that captures all five verdicts? If not, which verdict has to go, and what's the most honest repair? Also say what the principle predicts for a sixth case I haven't settled: police know 80% of recent jewel thefts in a district were done by someone matching a description (left-handed, over six feet), and they want to use that fact alone to detain a person who fits it.

Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no introduction or conclusion.
