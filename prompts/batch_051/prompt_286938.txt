I moved back to New Orleans a few years ago and live a ten-minute walk from where the Lee statue stood, and honestly the whole monument fight, plus the Charleston church shooting before it, is what got me reading about how other countries have handled the ugly parts of their past. I've got the rough comparative picture already: Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung, the working-through-the-past thing, as the supposed gold standard, with the long postwar silence, then the 1968 generation and the big memorials; Japan's half-apologies, the Yasukuni shrine, the textbook fights; Turkey's century of denial about the Armenian genocide. What I want is to see somebody actually run the American South through that same machinery instead of just waving at it. Take the 2015 to 2017 Confederate memory moment — Charleston, the South Carolina flag coming down, the New Orleans removals, Charlottesville — and analyze it through the comparative memory-culture framework. Do the mapping properly: who the players are and what options and incentives the framework assigns them (the mayor and city council, the heritage groups and monument defenders, the state legislature that passed a monuments-protection law afterwards, the Black public whose history had never been given official monument form), which national case or stage of reckoning each of them most resembles, and what Germany, Japan, and Turkey would each predict about whether this kind of symbolic reckoning sticks or gets captured by backlash. Then say, honestly, where the American South does not fit: it is not a defeated enemy nation working off guilt, the monuments went up long after the war during Jim Crow to reassert white rule, and the losing side's descendants still held a lot of the state power. Finish by running the arrow the other way: what does this case do to the framework itself — does it confirm the standard stages, or does it show that the framework is mostly a typology of apologies and memorials that misses the underlying struggle over who gets to count as the community doing the remembering? I'd rather you tell me the framework gets torn than squeeze the case to fit it.
