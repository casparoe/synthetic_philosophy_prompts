I read Hofstadter's book Gödel, Escher, Bach a while back, and one idea in it has never stopped bothering me: that the self, the feeling of being an "I," is not a thing located anywhere in the head but a kind of self-referential symbol, a strange loop that arises when a system represents itself and gets tangled up in its own representation. I want a poem that takes this seriously rather than just gesturing at it.

Please act as a poet who understands this view well and cares about verse craft. Begin by brainstorming briefly about how the poem's form could do some of the philosophical work, and then write a villanelle: the standard nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the first and third lines of the opening tercet returning in alternation and then meeting in the final couplet. The two refrain lines should themselves behave like the strange loop, the same words coming back in new surroundings and shifting their sense slightly with each return, the way the "I" keeps reinterpreting itself. The closing couplet should bring the refrains together so that the poem enacts the loop closing, without resolving it into either a little soul in the machine or a mere illusion.

After the poem, add a short note of a few sentences explaining where you kept the form strict, where you bent it, and why.
