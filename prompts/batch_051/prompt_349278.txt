I've increasingly been won over by Dennett's deflationary line in the original-versus-derived intentionality fight—the two-bitser he runs against Searle. On this view there's no principled seam between the aboutness of a sentence, a thermostat, a corporation, and one of my own beliefs; what Searle wants to call original intentionality is just derived intentionality with a selection or design history we happen to be sentimental about. But I'm dimly aware that I can't say what would actually count against this, and that worries me. So: if we steelman the deflationary view, what would have to be true for it to be false? Map the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward—specific Searlean arguments, possible empirical results about brains, or something about first-person meaning that the stance story can't capture. Tell me which of these a committed deflationist would genuinely grant as counting against the view and not just re-describe away, and flag the places where the view is insulated by definition. Is that insulation a feature or a bug? And one more thing: if a Searle-type realist leaned on recent LLMs—systems we built and whose aboutness seems borrowed, unlike ours—would that actually land as evidence against deflationism, or is it just another case the view already covers?
