Here's a puzzle I keep coming back to. If I negligently flood your basement, you can sue me and no one thinks twice about it. But if a state agency runs the water system and its negligently-maintained pipe floods your basement, in many places you cant sue the state unless the state has chosen to let you—sovereign immunity. Now here's the bit that bugs me: the state could hire a private contractor to run that same water system, and if the contractor's negligence floods your basement in exactly the same way, you can sue the contractor for the same damages. So we get an asymmetry: the identical wrong is actionable when it's a company doing the government's work, but not when it's the government doing the government's work. Is there a principled reason for that, or is it just the old idea that the king can do no wrong with better PR? Give me the strongest defense of sovereign immunity you can, then tell me honestly whether it survives the contractor case or whether we should drop the immunity.
