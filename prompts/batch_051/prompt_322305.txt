I've been going back and forth with a friend who works on interpretability, and we keep tripping over the word "representation." He'll say a layer of a vision model represents textures or edges or something, and I'll say the activations just correlate with textures, and then he'll ask what the difference is between that and a map representing the tube. I agree a map represents, and a thermometer doesn't, but neither of us can actually state what separates them.

So I want a working definition of "representation"—or maybe of "X represents Y"—that gets these cases right. A paper map of the Tube: clearly represents. A photo of my mother: represents. The height of the mercury column in a thermometer: clearly doesn't, it just covaries. A thermostat's bimetallic strip: also doesn't, I think. A honeybee's waggle dance: I'd say yes, it represents direction and distance, but I might be using the word loosely. The hidden activation pattern of a trained convnet shown a cat photo: genuinely no idea, and this is the contested one. A word2vec vector for "king": my friend says yes; I see the analogy but I'm not sure it's the same sense as the map. A chess engine's evaluation score of +0.4: I want to say it represents winning chances, but its just a scalar, so I could be talked out of it. And a gene: the nucleotide sequence that codes for a protein—does the gene represent the protein? It feels different from the map, but I can't say how.

Propose the definition, run it through all of those cases, and tell me where it forces you into a surprising verdict or needs revising. If you think there are really two senses of "represent" doing different work—one thin and information-theoretic, one thicker and tied to use or function—give me both and say which cases land in each. And I'd especially like to know whether, on your best definition, a neural network's internal states turn out to genuinely represent features of the world in the way a map does, or whether we should be deflationary and just say the model has activations that correlate with features. Bonus if you can explain why representation talk feels like it comes in degrees, so that a photo feels like more of a representation than a thermometer.
