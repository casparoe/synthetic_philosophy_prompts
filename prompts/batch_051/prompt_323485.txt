i have a rule about psychiatric and psychological labels and i want it stress-tested. when a diagnostic category leaks into ordinary talk — people saying they're "a little borderline" when they get jealous, or calling a bad boss "trauma" — i stop treating the label as a discovery about how a mind is built and start treating it as a social role people sort themselves into. the more widely a category is claimed, the less explanatory weight i give it. the rationale i give myself is something like hackings looping effect: for human kinds, being classified changes the people classified, so a popular label can start producing and shaping the very behavior it was meant to name, which makes it worse at explaining. recently a friend explained some hurtful behavior as "her avoidant attachment style," and i discounted the explanation nearly to zero just because the term has gone mainstream. is that defensible, or am i confusing "this category loops" with "this category is false" and treating popularity as better evidence than it is? is there a principled version of the rule — some size of discount, or a warning sign that tells you which labels loop more — instead of a blanket demotion? and say where your reasoning is most likely to fail, and what would change your mind: which consideration would make you either give up the rule or make it much harsher.
