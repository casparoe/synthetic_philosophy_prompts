People admire the athlete who tears a ligament a week before nationals and is genuinely unbothered, because it was never up to her. But the same person, told that a neighbor's kid was beaten up and is in hospital, and who is just as unbothered, gets called cold or worse. I want to know whether these two reactions can be squared. The Stoics seem to want both at once: the sage has apatheia, freedom from the passions—no distress, fear, or anger—but she also cares about other people, supposedly via oikeiosis, our natural attachment to them. So is the first case virtuous equanimity and the second a failure of care, and if so what principle draws the line? Or is the ordinary asymmetry just a social illusion that a consistent Stoic should reject—which would force us either to be more disturbed by our own bad luck than the sage is, or less disturbed by a hurt kid than we want? Give me the strongest justification you can for the asymmetry and then say whether you would keep it. Also, where your answer turns on what the Stoics actually held versus what later people reconstructed, keep those apart, and don't present one side of a live dispute as the consensus.
