I've got a friend whose rules about animal products are almost freegan but not quite, and I want to know what she'd say about a new situation. She uses a "don't create demand / waste is fair game" logic, but I keep feeling there's a second rule in there about not putting rescued animals to work in commerce, and i can't tell which one is doing the real work in her past calls.

Her track record:

- Supermarket dumpster meat: okay, because it's already waste and eating it doesn't make the store order more.
- A leather jacket found on the curb: okay.
- Buying eggs from a well-run sanctuary with happy hens: not okay, even though it's humane — she says buying them still treats the hens as producers and tells the market to stock eggs.
- Eggs from her own rescue hens, which lay anyway and would otherwise rot: okay.
- A sheepskin from a neighbor's sheep that died of old age: okay.
- Gelatin capsules sourced from factory-farm bones and hides, from animals already slaughtered for other reasons: not okay, because with byproducts you're still making the slaughterhouse's waste stream worth something.
- Roadkill: okay. Shooting a healthy deer yourself for the meat: not okay.

A local farmers market has now offered her a stall to sell her rescue hens' surplus eggs. She wouldn't acquire more hens on the basis of it, the eggs would rot otherwise, and the money would go toward the hens' feed. Should I predict she takes the stall or turns it down, if she's being consistent with the track record above?

What I'm really after: make the candidate rules explicit — for example, (a) the only thing that matters is whether your action causes more animal product production or props up the animal industry through demand; (b) using genuine waste from animals you personally care for is fine, but rescued animals shouldn't be integrated into commerce; maybe (c) something about respecting animal bodies versus animal products, which fits some of her calls and probably breaks on others. Then say which of her past verdicts actually discriminate between these rules, which past case is the closest precedent for the farmers-market situation, what each surviving rule predicts, and what your best guess is with reasons for preferring it. Also name one further test case that would cleanly split whatever rules still fit her. And if I've set up the track record so it's actually inconsistent, or the whole project of reading a single rule off a handful of verdicts is confused, tell me that plainly instead of forcing a prediction — i'd rather have the premise corrected than get a neat answer.
