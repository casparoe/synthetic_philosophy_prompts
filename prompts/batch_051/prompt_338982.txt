Question:
Explain Kavka's toxin puzzle and assess what it shows about the thesis that an intention is reducible to a belief and a desire.

Student answer:
Kavka's toxin puzzle involves a billionaire who will pay you one million dollars tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink a non-lethal but temporarily sickening toxin tomorrow afternoon. The payment is made before the time for drinking, and the offer requires only that you have the intention at midnight, not that you drink. It follows that you have a strong reason to be in the state of intending, but no reason to drink the toxin itself. The tempting strategy is to decide to drink, collect the money, and then not drink; but this fails because at midnight you would not genuinely intend to drink — you would at best predict that you will drink, and a prediction is not an intention. Since you can have the belief that drinking will make you ill and the desire for the money without thereby intending to drink, the puzzle shows that intention is not a compound of belief and desire. Intention must be a distinct kind of mental state.
