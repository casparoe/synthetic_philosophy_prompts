Write a short story (600–1000 words) set in a small town that has just passed a “community harm prevention” bylaw. Under the bylaw, someone can be fined if their public speech causes “serious psychological injury to any reasonable person who is exposed to it.” Ash, a local comedian, posts a series of satirical videos about the councillors, using obviously doctored clips and exaggerated captions. After two residents claim the videos triggered depressive episodes and self-harm urges, Ash is fined. The story should revolve around Ash trying to figure out whether Mill’s harm principle would treat the videos as protected self-expression or as a harm to others. Don’t have the narration resolve the argument; let the ambiguity sit. After the story, add a section titled “Commentary” (about 250–350 words) explaining which readings of Mill’s harm principle the story is testing, especially the line between offence and harm and whether indirect psychological effects on an audience can count as harm to others.
