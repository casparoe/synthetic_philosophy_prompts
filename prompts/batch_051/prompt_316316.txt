I am a philosophy professor and a member of my university's academic integrity board. The board must decide whether to recommend a proposal to overhaul the undergraduate honor system. Under the current system, students sign a community honor pledge at matriculation, many assessments are unproctored, and violations are handled primarily as failures of personal integrity, with sanctions tied to character formation. The proposal before us would replace this with a deterrence-based regime: mandatory proctoring, no honor pledge, assigned seating, and penalties scaled to the expected cost of cheating. Its sponsors argue that the relevant social–psychological evidence undermines the idea of robust, cross-situationally stable character traits such as honesty. In studies of cheating and helping, behavior has seemed to track situational features, such as surveillance, anonymity, peer norms, and time pressure, more closely than it tracks who the person is. They conclude that an honor system built on global character traits is empirically unsound, and that deterrence is the only intellectually honest alternative. Several board members are persuaded by the evidence but worry that a deterrence-only regime would abandon the university's stated educational mission of forming persons of integrity and would teach students, in practice, that honesty is merely the avoidance of detection. Draft the board's decision memo. It should recommend one course of action, whether to approve the proposal, reject it, or approve it with specified revisions, and defend that recommendation with reasons both camps can engage. The memo should take seriously the strongest consideration on the losing side and explain how a workable policy can be responsive to the situational evidence without either dismissing it or concluding that character education is worthless. You may draw on the philosophical debate over virtue and situationism, but the memo should be written for a mixed faculty committee that need not know that literature. Write the memo in American English.
