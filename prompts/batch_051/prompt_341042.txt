Construct a thought experiment where two readings of frugality come apart. Reading A (deontological): be frugal — that is, don't waste resources in your own conduct, whatever the consequences. Reading B (consequentialist): promote frugality — that is, do whatever brings about the most frugality overall. I want a case where A requires refusing a genuinely wasteful act (wasteful by A's own standards) and B requires doing it, because in that situation the wasteful act is the best available way to make other people frugal. So the deontologist ends up embodying the value and the consequentialist ends up promoting it by personally violating it. Constraints: realistic (a workplace, neighborhood, or family), one paragraph for the scenario, no trolleys, no sci-fi, no perfect predictors; the social mechanism linking the wasteful act to greater frugality elsewhere has to be an ordinary one (imitation, status, credibility, satiation) and not just stipulated; and the deontologist shouldn't be given an extra, separate duty to create the conflict — it has to come out of the duty to be frugal itself. Then walk through what each reading says and why, name the feature of the case doing the work and what happens if you remove it, and finish with how a defender of the deontological reading would push back. This is for a handout on the difference between embodying a value and promoting it, so the verdicts have to fall out of the theories plus the setup, not be stipulated.
