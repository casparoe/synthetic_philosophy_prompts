Consider a string-rewriting system in the spirit of Hofstadter's Typogenetics. A strand is a finite string over the alphabet {A, C, G, T}. Read two bases at a time, each pair of bases specifies one elementary operation on a strand — cut at the current position, copy the current base, insert a specified base, delete the current base, or move one position — and the resulting sequence of operations is the enzyme encoded by that strand, applied by executing its operations step by step. Call a strand self-copying when applying its own enzyme to it yields at least one additional copy of it. In the standard setup, self-copying strands exist, and this is the core of the proposed DNA–Gödel isomorphism. Now modify the system: whenever an enzyme is read off a strand, each operation independently has probability p of being replaced by a uniformly random different operation, and the corrupted enzyme then runs as usual. Work through the effect on self-replication. Does a self-copying lineage persist for small positive p, and is there a critical value of p above which none can persist? Can a strand encode internal redundancy or error correction to restore faithful copying, or does the isomorphism essentially depend on error-free transcription?
