so i keep going back and forth with a friend about perception. her line is the usual one: a straight stick half in water looks bent even though it isnt, so what you directly see must be some kind of picture or image inside your head, and from there you never directly see the stick itself, only your own inner picture of it. she says seeing is basically like a camera or a photo album the mind flips through. that analogy does a lot of work for her, and i want to actually pressure-test it. can you lay out what corresponds to what — the lens, the film or sensor, the finished photo, the person looking at the photo — and then the places where the comparison stops working? like, a photo sits there after the thing is gone, a photo is flat and still, and somebody has to look at a photo to see it, none of which obviously apply to seeing. i want to know which of those breaks actually matter for her conclusion and which ones she could just shrug off. i also want to know how much of the argument from illusion survives if the analogy is weak: can someone grant that the stick really does look bent, and still deny there is a private bent image inside the head? my other friend mentioned something called disjunctivism that is supposed to do exactly that, but every time i ask him to explain it he disappears into jargon. and if the camera analogy has been smuggling in the inner-picture conclusion the whole time, is there a better comparison for perceiving, or would we be better off dropping the analogy altogether?
