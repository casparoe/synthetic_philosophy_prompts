I am at work on a novel set in a capital city in the weeks after a coup, and a scene I am drafting has raised a conceptual question I would like help settling. In the scene, my protagonist, a mid-ranking treasury official, is ordered to appear before the new rulers and read aloud a statement declaring that the deposed government had been about to hand the national gold reserves to a foreign power. The room holds the rulers, their officers, and a camera crew making a recording that, he is told, will go only into the regime's internal archive. Every person present knows the claim is false. The official knows they know, and he is confident that no one who ever sees the recording will believe a word of it. He complies anyway, not to get anyone to accept the story, but to produce a record of his compliance, to make himself useful to the new regime, and to keep his family safe.

The categories my narrator reaches for come from an Augustinian account of lying, as I understand it: a lie requires (1) that the speaker make an assertion; (2) that he assert what he believes to be false; and (3) that he do so with the intention of deceiving, that is, of bringing the hearer to hold a false belief. On this same reading, Augustine also holds that every lie is a sin, though lies differ greatly in gravity, and a lie told for a good end is no exception.

Walk me through the scene against those criteria. Go one by one: for each criterion, say whether the official satisfies it, and note any place where the verdict turns on how a term such as assertion, belief, intention, or deception is interpreted. Then give your overall verdict: does what the official does qualify as a lie on the Augustinian account? If it fails because there is no realistic aim of producing false belief, does that reveal a gap in the account, or does it instead show that this scene is something other than a lie—some adjacent wrong done by means of speech, such as false witness, servility, capitulation, or knowingly corrupting one's own utterances? I want the distinction between lying and the other ways of sinning or going wrong with words to do real work.

Please also say how confident you are in your reading of Augustine and in each of your verdicts, and identify the single point you are least sure of.
