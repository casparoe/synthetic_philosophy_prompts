I am a control systems engineer by training, and I think most comfortably in the language of plants, state variables, control inputs, exogenous disturbances, feedback laws, observability, and model uncertainty. My acquaintance with Stoicism comes almost entirely from its modern revival, where the "dichotomy of control" is usually glossed as something like: work on the things you can influence and do not waste energy on the things you cannot. That gloss sounds very close to an engineer's distinction between controllable inputs and unmodeled disturbances, and I suspect it quietly changes what Epictetus was actually claiming. I would like to test that suspicion against the text directly.

Here is the passage I have in mind, from Epictetus, *Discourses* I.1, as reported by Arrian, in W. A. Oldfather's translation:

"What aid, then, must we have ready at hand in such circumstances?" Why, what else than the knowledge of what is mine, and what is not mine, and what is permitted me, and what is not permitted me? I must die: must I, then, die groaning too? I must be fettered: and wailing too? I must go into exile: does anyone, then, keep me from going with a smile and cheerful and serene? "Tell your secrets." I say not a word; for this is under my control. "But I will fetter you." What is that you say, man? fetter me? My leg you will fetter, but my moral purpose not even Zeus himself has power to overcome. "I will throw you into prison." My paltry body, rather! "I will behead you." Well, when did I ever tell you that mine was the only neck that could not be severed?

Your task is this. Restate the position in this passage systematically in the vocabulary of control engineering. For each element of the passage—"under my control," "moral purpose," the body and the estate and social ties, the "use of external impressions," the dying/fettering/exile examples, the claim that not even Zeus can overcome the moral purpose—give the nearest control-theoretic rendering you can defend, and then produce a residue report: what translates cleanly, what must be bent or simplified to fit, and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all. In particular, say whether the popular "sphere of influence" gloss survives the translation or whether it is an artifact of the target vocabulary. I want you to keep the translation honest: where control theory would smuggle in one of its own assumptions—a plant/controller split, a known objective function, a disturbance model, a state to estimate—say so rather than passing the assumption off as a translation of Epictetus. End with a committed verdict: does the residue show that Epictetus's claim is less coherent than it looks, that the control-theoretic vocabulary is missing something the claim needs, or some specified third view? Do not give me a menu of alternative mappings and leave me to choose; choose the best mapping, argue for it, and flag your confidence link by link.
