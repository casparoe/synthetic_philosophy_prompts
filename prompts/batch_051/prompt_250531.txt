I have been hearing the slogan "consciousness is an illusion" with increasing frequency, most recently from a cognitive scientist who used it to brush aside a question I had raised about whether a system that reliably reports the color of objects but lacks any subjective experience is missing something. I suspect that sentence is doing real argumentative work in that exchange, but it plainly cannot be doing the same work everywhere it is repeated. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan might be used to assert — about the reliability of introspection, the existence of qualia, the status of phenomenal properties in a scientific picture of the world, and what an illusion requires — and say for each whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false? I am especially interested in which of these readings would actually license brushing the question aside as confused, and which would merely change the subject.
