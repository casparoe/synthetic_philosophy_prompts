I serve on the Community Policy Committee of a medium-sized discussion platform whose governance I would describe as a constitutional monarchy staffed almost entirely by ex-rationalist forum moderators. Our karma system is the backbone of the site's ranking and moderation infrastructure: votes are weighted by the square root of the voter's own karma, subject to a minimum account-age floor, and the resulting score determines default comment sorting, posting throttles, and a handful of cosmetic privileges. The committee is now deadlocked on a proposal to introduce transferable merit grants. Under the proposal, any user above a karma threshold could transfer up to a fixed percentage of her score to another user as a single 'grant,' which would act not as an ordinary upvote but as a small standing multiplier on that user's future votes. Transfers would be taxed at ten percent and would decay at one percent per month, and a recipient's future income would be reported net of outstanding grants. The proposer's argument is that a first-rate post by a new but knowledgeable user is nearly weightless under the current system, because her upvotes count for so little; influence over what gets seen is effectively monopolized by old accounts. The proposal is meant to let incumbents anoint newcomers they judge competent.

I have been asked to draft the short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete decision — approve, reject, or amend with specific parameter changes and a rationale — and it should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously. The skeptical faction on the committee argues that grants simply build a patronage network with extra steps, and that coordinated groups will use them as an off-ledger path to launder reputation through trusted accounts, gutting the signal value of karma. I came up on forums where non-transferable karma was treated almost as an article of faith, and I no longer know whether that was a principle or just an artifact.
