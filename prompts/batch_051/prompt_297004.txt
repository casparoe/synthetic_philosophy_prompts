I would like your credence, expressed as a number between 0 and 1, on the following proposition, with your derivation fully decomposed.

Consider a temperate-region field crop treated with a standard broad-spectrum insecticide, killing on the order of a million insects per hectare over the course of a season, mostly larval and adult crop pests. The proposition is: those insect deaths themselves constitute a serious moral cost, setting aside effects on vertebrates, ecosystems, and human consumers — where a serious moral cost means one that would ordinarily be enough to make the application wrong absent substantial countervailing benefits, and that does not become negligible merely because each individual death is small.

Please decompose your credence into at least the following components, give each a number and a one-sentence justification, and show how they combine:

1. The probability that the affected insects have any valenced experience at all — that is, any subjective point of view that can feel bad or good during poisoning.
2. Conditional on their having valenced experience, the probability that an insect's death is itself a direct welfare setback — as opposed to death being morally inert for beings with little diachronic unity or few future-directed interests.
3. Conditional on death being a direct welfare setback, the probability that the per-insect harm is large enough that enormous numbers do not remain morally trivial in aggregate.
4. The probability that aggregating very many small harms into a serious moral cost is legitimate here, rather than blocked by a reasonable anti-aggregation principle.

Then identify the component to which your final credence is most sensitive, and state one realistic empirical finding or philosophical argument that would move that component most, and thereby move your overall credence most.
