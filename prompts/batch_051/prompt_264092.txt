A bunch of democracies do things that pull in different directions on who gets a say. Australia fines people who don't show up to vote, several countries let 16- and 17-year-olds vote in at least some elections, and a handful of places (New Zealand for permanent residents, some US cities for local races) let non-citizens vote at least locally. Taken together, what view of voting and democratic membership does this cluster of practices presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of what voting is for and who the demos is, then say whether the three practices actually presuppose the same theory or are just a bag of seperate compromises. Finally, spell out what would have to change if you instead ran all three through a single all-affected-interests principle.
