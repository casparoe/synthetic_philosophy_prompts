In Plato's Laws the Athenian Stranger defends the drinking-party as a kind of test: he suggests that wine, by loosening a person's self-command, makes the soul's deeper dispositions visible to others, so that intoxication is a way of inspecting character rather than merely a cause of disgraceful behavior. Suppose a modern researcher took this seriously as a testable claim, namely that intoxication reveals character rather than simply distorting or degrading it. Design the best empirical test of that claim. Specify what would be measured, what pattern of results would favor the revelation reading and what would favor the distortion reading, and then say what such a test could and could not settle about Plato's underlying idea — in particular, whether a positive result would show that drunkenness exposes a durable self, or merely that it produces one consistent kind of performance.
