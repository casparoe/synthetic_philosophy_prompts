I train working dogs for a living, and in my spare time I have been reading Descartes’ *Discourse on the Method*. Early in the book he writes:

“for as to the reason or sense, inasmuch as it is that alone which constitutes us men, and distinguishes us from the brutes, I am disposed to believe that it is to be found complete in each individual; and on this point to adopt the common opinion of philosophers, who say that the difference of greater and less holds only among the accidents, and not among the forms or natures of individuals of the same species.”

That sentence has lodged in my mind because it moves from a claim about equality among humans to a claim about a sharp line between us and animals, and he treats that line as already settled. I want to know whether the line is testable. Suppose I can design and run simple experiments with dogs, crows, or other animals, but I have no laboratory and no background in psychology. What would a genuine test of the claim that animals lack reason look like? What exact ability or behavior would I need to observe to count as serious evidence against Descartes’ line? What result would you need to count in his favor? Be explicit about where the test is testing the animal and where it is testing our definition of “reason” or “thought.” Then tell me what even a successful test would leave unsettled about the deeper claim that animals have no minds at all. When you answer, say how confident you are in each part of the plan and single out the point you are least sure of.
