I'm working on a novel where a tech startup sells a "grounding module" that supposedly gives a symbolic AI intrinsic meaning, and I want a piece of extended satire as part of my research. The thing I keep coming back to is this passage from Stevan Harnad's "The Symbol Grounding Problem" (1990):

"A thermostat may be interpreted as following the rule: Turn on the furnace if the temperature goes below 70 degrees and turn it off if it goes above 70 degrees, yet nowhere in the thermostat is that rule explicitly represented."

The startup in my novel has the opposite pitch: they claim you dont need explicit representation, just enough interpretability, and they sell grounding as an external compliance layer. I'd like a deadpan satirical piece, maybe a product brochure or customer FAQ for their module, around 700 words, in the voice of corporate-utopian tech marketing. The target is the idea that semantic grounding can be outsourced to an interpretive layer, an audit trail, or a customer support contract without changing what the system actually does. Make it funny by taking that logic seriously: the product specs should be entirely about licensing interpretations, per-symbol grounding fees, quarterly meaning audits, and a support line where meanings are provided as a service. Stay deadpan, and let Harnad's point emerge through the absurdity, but never state it directly. Bonus if you work the thermostat example in as a testimonial from a satisfied customer.
