I'm a political economy PhD student working on regulatory competition, and I keep tripping over the phrase "race to the bottom" in AI governance debates. Last week someone in my group chat argued that voluntary safety commitments from AI labs are pointless because "it's just a race to the bottom"—their claim being, as far as I could tell, that any lab that spends on safety falls behind competitively, so eventually all labs cut safety to whatever they can get away with. But I've also seen the phrase used about tax havens, environmental standards, labor protections, and content moderation, and it seems to gesture at a different mechanism each time. Sometimes it means standards fall literally to the lowest level any actor would choose; sometimes it means they fall below the socially optimal level; sometimes it means no single actor can raise standards unilaterally without losing market share; sometimes it's about a dynamic where each round of competition makes the next round worse.

What I'd like is a menu of the distinct precise claims "race to the bottom" could be asserting. For each: is it trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false as a general matter? Which reading is actually required for the AI-safety version to go through, and which readings are stronger or weaker than that argument needs? I care less about empirical evidence for any one industry and more about the logical structure—e.g., does the phrase actually pick out a prisoner's dilemma, or is that itself sloppy, and does it imply the bottom is a stable equilibrium or a continuing slide? Please keep the readings seperate rather than fusing them.
