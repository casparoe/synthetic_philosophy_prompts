We treat competition as if it amplifies the truth in some places and distorts it in others, and I can't locate the difference. If someone wins a 100m race, the medal is treated as strong evidence they really were fastest; wanting to win doesn't make the result suspect, it makes it a test. But if someone wins a debate, few people update toward their conclusion, and a professional who can win either side is treated as less trustworthy than a sincere amateur who is worse at winning. The same trait—training to win, then winning—flips from credential to discredential when the activity changes from foot-racing to reasoning. Is there a principle that yields both judgments, or is one of them just a leftover from when we thought argument was about status? Try the strongest principle you can find, test it against lawyers in court and paid forecasters on prediction markets, and tell me whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or dumped.
