I'm making a handout for a second-year ethics course and I want a decision tree that sorts students from their raw reactions to what-if-everyone-did-that cases into whichever universalizability position they actually hold (or don't). The leaves have to include Kant's formula of universal law, R. M. Hare's universal prescriptivism, the generalization argument (which treats what would happen if everyone did the act as itself a moral reason), rule-consequentialism, and act-consequentialism; add any other leaves the logical space genuinely forces on you. Build it from the distinctions first, not from the names: every node should be a concrete question a student can answer without knowing which label it leads to. The splits I especially want built in are whether what gets universalized is the agent's own maxim, the act-type as an observer would describe it, or the aggregate consequences of everyone doing it; whether the alleged failure is a contradiction in conception, a contradiction in willing, bad aggregate outcomes, or an inconsistent prescription; and whether the inference is supposed to work in partial-compliance worlds or only under full compliance. Use at least three of these cases somewhere among the nodes: quietly evading a bus fare with no chance of being caught, breaking a deathbed promise that would hurt no living person, and dropping one candy wrapper in a clean national park. Aim for 8–12 nodes, and after the tree include a list of unoccupied answer-combinations with a one-line diagnosis each (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored) and a list of leaf-pairs the tree could not seperate, flagged as such instead of papering over them.

Give me the result as a JSON object with these fields: decision_tree, an array of nodes where each node has id, question, and answer_options, and each option has text plus either next_node_id or leaf_id; leaves, an array with id, position_name, one_sentence_definition, and most_awkward_commitment; unoccupied_combinations, an array with combination and why_unoccupied; inseparable_pairs, an array with pair and why_not_separable; and doing_the_work, a short paragraph naming the two or three branching choices that do most of the sorting.
