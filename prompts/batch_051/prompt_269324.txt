Question: Newcomb's problem is standardly presented as a conflict between evidential decision theory (EDT) and causal decision theory (CDT). Using the standard payoff matrix, state what each theory recommends, and identify the feature of the problem that generates the conflict. Then assess this claim: EDT one-boxes because it treats one-boxing as if it could cause the predictor to have predicted one-boxing.

Student answer:
EDT recommends taking one box. Taking one box is strong evidence that the predictor predicted a one-box choice and therefore put $1,000,000 in the opaque box; taking both boxes is strong evidence that the predictor predicted a two-box choice and left the opaque box empty. Since one-boxing has the higher evidential expected utility, EDT one-boxes. CDT recommends taking both boxes. The prediction has already been made, so my act cannot causally influence whether the opaque box contains the million. Whatever the predictor did, taking both boxes leaves me $1,000 better off than taking one box: if the box is full, I receive $1,001,000 rather than $1,000,000; if it is empty, I receive $1,000 rather than $0. Thus two-boxing dominates one-boxing under CDT. The feature that drives the conflict is that the predictor is highly accurate but made its prediction before my choice, so my act is diagnostic of the prediction without causally affecting it. I think that claim is basically right: EDT is willing to reason as if one-boxing could reach back and make the earlier prediction more likely, while CDT respects the direction of causation.
