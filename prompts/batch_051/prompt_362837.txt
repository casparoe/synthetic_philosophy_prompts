After a long argument with a friend about whether "meme" is more than a metaphor, I wrote out the position I ended up defending and would like an audit of whether it actually hangs together. My commitments are these:

1. Cultural change is Darwinian in a literal sense: variants of tunes, phrases, techniques, and fashions differ in how often they get copied, and the ones that spread do so because of their own features.

2. A Darwinian process requires heritable variation in discrete units. Selection cannot accumulate complex design from a blended, overlapping continuum; it needs something copied faithfully enough that small differences are preserved rather than dissolving.

3. Culture does not supply gene-like units. The candidates for memes are fuzzy, merge, split, and are reconstructed by each recipient rather than copied verbatim.

4. I hold that (3) does not sink the meme picture: selection can operate on continuous or blurred variation as long as stable core regularities propagate reliably enough.

5. When a technique spreads because it works better than its rivals, I count that as natural selection rather than as an alternative to it. Function is the trait being selected, much as a more efficient enzyme is selected over a less efficient one.

6. Yet I insist on a distinction my friend found unmotivated: an idea with no functional payoff that spreads because it is catchy, memorable, or pleasurable is the genuine replicator case, a meme proper, whereas an idea that spreads because it objectively works is maintained by selection-for-function, and I am reluctant to call it a meme in the same sense.

Please audit this set: which pairs are actually in tension, and how severe is each tension? Which pairs only appear to be in tension and dissolve when examined? Rank whatever tensions you find by severity, say in each case what would have to give, and identify the cheapest single revision that would make the whole set coherent.
