Here is an asymmetry I keep coming back to, and I want to know whether it can be justified or should be abandoned. On Metzinger's account, both ordinary perception and the felt sense of self are phenomenally transparent representations: the process that builds them is not introspectively available, so we experience only the represented content—the world, or the subject. Yet people treat the two transparency claims very differently. Tell someone that the red of the tomato is generated by the visual system rather than painted on the tomato, and they will usually accept it, perhaps say the tomato isn't really red out there, and continue relying on color perception exactly as before. Tell someone that the felt self—the subject that seems to stand behind the eyes and have the experience—is likewise a transparent self-model constructed by the brain, and the same person often reacts as though something precious has been declared fake. We demote the perceived object from reality to useful appearance without alarm, but we resist demoting the perceiving subject in the same way. Is there a principle that justifies treating the transparency of the self-model differently from the transparency of world-perception, or is the asymmetry only attachment and special pleading? I want the strongest candidate justification you can find, tested against nearby cases such as phantom limbs, the rubber hand illusion, depersonalization or derealization (experiences in which the self or the world feels unreal), and the experience of agency. Say plainly whether the asymmetry should be kept, given up, or reversed.

After the prose, append a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- asymmetry: a one-sentence restatement of the asymmetry.
- strongest_justification: the best candidate justification, in one paragraph.
- test_cases: a list of two to four objects, each with the fields case (a short description), outcome (whether the case supports, strains, or breaks the justification), and note (one sentence explaining why).
- main_objection: the strongest single objection to that justification.
- final_verdict: one of the strings "keep", "give up", or "reverse".
- confidence: a number between 0 and 1 indicating how confident you are in the final verdict.
