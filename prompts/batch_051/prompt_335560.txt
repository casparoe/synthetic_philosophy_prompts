I work in control theory and reinforcement learning, and I am trying to check an intuition. The familiar dispute over whether planning is overrated — one side holding that advance planning reduces uncertainty, forces early thinking, and makes coordinated action possible; the other holding that plans encode stale assumptions, produce false confidence, and that skilled real-time adjustment with good feedback does the real work — strikes me as a disguised argument about open-loop versus closed-loop control and about how much to trust a world model. Restate the strongest version of each side in the vocabulary I actually use: open-loop trajectories, closed-loop policies and feedback, model-predictive control, replanning horizons, model error, value functions, and exploration. Then give a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which had to be distorted, and which have no counterpart in that vocabulary at all. I want to know whether the case for planning survives translation intact, weakens, or turns out to be about something other than what its defenders think.
