A friend of mine is doing criminal law and keeps insisting the gatecrasher case is just the lottery paradox wearing a wig, and that everyone misses this. Quick version of the case for anyone not doing law: a thousand spectators watch a rodeo, slightly over half of them never paid, and the prosecution picks one out at random and charges him with gatecrashing. The odds say he probably didn't pay, but only just, and nobody thinks thats enough for proof beyond reasonable doubt. My friend's line is that this has exactly the same structure as a fair lottery: before the draw, for any ticket you like the odds say it lost, yet you also know one ticket is going to win, so you can't infer that any particular ticket lost. His punchline: proof beyond reasonable doubt isn't a probability threshold at all, because you can have a probability leaning the right way on bare statistical evidence and still have proved nothing, so any account of legal proof as "probability above some number" is dead.
