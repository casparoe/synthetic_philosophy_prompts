I'm writing a term paper for a course on logical empiricism and personal identity, and I've realised that my own position may be a patchwork of things I've accepted at different points in the semester. I want an audit of whether the package hangs together before I turn it into a thesis. I accept a softened verificationist criterion of meaning: a factual statement has cognitive content only if it is logically or mathematically decidable, or else experience in principle could count for or against it. I also accept an Ayer-style bundle view of the self: the self is not an inner substance but a logical construction out of experiences, tied together by memory and similarity. In Language, Truth and Logic, Ayer argues that the sentence a person survives after death has no literal meaning if it refers to a wholly disembodied self, because no possible sense-experience could settle the question; by contrast, if survival means the continuation of my memories and character in a new body or publicly observable form, then the claim is empirically meaningful. I endorse both halves of that move.

But I also hold three further commitments. First, I'm a functionalist: mental states, including the ones that make up me, are constituted by causal roles, so in principle an artificial system with the right causal organisation could have a mind. Second, I think gradual uploading would be survival: if my neurons were replaced one by one with functionally equivalent artificial components, preserving psychological continuity throughout, the resulting person would still be me, even if my original body was discarded. Third, I keep finding myself committed to an irreducibly first-person fact: there is something it is like to be me that no third-person functional description seems to capture. My seminar instructor says this last point is just a qualia intuition and I should either formalise it or drop it.

So my question: are these six commitments jointly tenable, and if not, where does the real tension live? I suspect the bundle view and the first-person fact are the worst pair, but I also worry that my verificationism commits me to thinking that the resulting person would still be me in the uploading scenario is meaningful only if I can specify observations that would settle it. Please audit the set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs. In doing so, be explicit about whether Ayer's own view actually supports my uploading verdict or whether I am stretching him. Also, since I am handing this in to a philosopher who will ask, please say how confident you are in each diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of.
