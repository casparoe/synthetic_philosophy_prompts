I've got a rule I use when I'm evaluating causes that I've stuck to for a while. I'm fine scoring importance and tractability directly, but I only let neglectedness move my ranking if someone can spell out a concrete mechanism for why the marginal dollar does more there: a binding talent shortage, a funding gap before a specific milestone, something like that. If all they have is that the cause gets almost no money or attention, I treat that as zero information. My friend says this is exactly backwards, because 'nobody's doing it' is itself evidence of low-hanging fruit and that's basically the whole point of the neglectedness factor in cause prioritization. But I don't buy an efficient-market story for philantropy either, so a cause being ignored seems consistent with both 'nobody has checked' and 'everyone checked and it's hopeless.' Is my rule defensible, or am I just refusing to update on a useful signal? Is there a revised version that filters the bad neglectedness claims without throwing away the good ones?
