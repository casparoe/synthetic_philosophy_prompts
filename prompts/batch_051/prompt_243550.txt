I've been going back and forth on underdetermination of theory by evidence lately, but rather than the usual realism/antirealism angle I keep trying to treat it as an institutional design problem. The setup is familiar enough: a pile of evidence supports multiple mutually incompatible theories, and it only gets worse once you remember that theories meet evidence bundled together with auxiliary assumptions. Most people treat that as a fact about confirmation, or a reason to hedge about scientific claims. But suppose someone actually built a funding agency around it, along these lines.

The rule: any theory that receives money has to be registered together with at least one live rival that is also compatible with the evidence available at registration. The yoked theories then get a cheap adversarial collaboration every three years—shared data, pre-registered analyses, each side explicitly trying to produce an observation that embarasses the other. If after three rounds they are still observationally equivalent, a differentiation tax kicks in: funding for the whole yoked set drops by a third per year until someone registers a feasible, pre-registered observation that would tell them apart, or one side concedes. The reclaimed money goes into a pot for exploratory work that does not have to register a rival.

Walk me through how this actually plays out. What kinds of theories, and what kinds of theorists, does it select for over time? Does it mostly push people to pick weak strawman rivals, or to quietly collude to keep their yoked pair alive, or something stranger? How does it warp which questions a field considers worth asking in the first place? Where does it break down first—fields where the underdetermination is genuine and maybe permanent, like interpretations of quantum mechanics or the old levels-of-analysis disputes in cognitive science? And is there a tweak that keeps most of the benefit without the predictable gaming, or does the whole thing just turn underdetermination into a status game? I can see arguments in both directions and keep flip-flopping.
