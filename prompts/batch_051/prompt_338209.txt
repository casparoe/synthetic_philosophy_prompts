I'm a novelist — three books out, a fourth shoved in a drawer — and for the past two years my agent has been driving me quietly up the wall. When we first met, he said the whole point of his job was long-term career building: steadily growing advances, keeping my backlist in print, introducing me to editors who'd stick with me after a flop. Fine. But his actual behaviour has never really matched that. He once spent four months chasing a film option that he admitted was a long shot, while the paperback auction for my second novel quietly collapsed. He talked me out of a year-long residency that would have let me write the next book, on the grounds that 'real writers stay visible.' He turns down unglamorous festival slots, steers me toward deals that look flashy in the trade press, and shrugs when I ask about audio, translations, or serialisation. I've started wondering whether he has some entirely different goal — status in the industry, being near film people, a high volume of visible wins — or whether he's simply not very good at the goal he claims to have.

The bit that really bothers me, though, isnt just that some moves look suboptimal. Whole categories of option never seem to occur to him at all. It's as if the only moves in his game are auctions, festivals, and screen meetings. So here's my question: if all I can observe is what he does and doesnt do — the deals he chases, the things he declines, and the possibilities he never even raises — how far can I get in working out what he's actually trying to achieve? And can I work out the shape of the game he thinks he's playing? I assumed that after two years of careful notes I'd have converged on an answer, but the more I look, the more the same behaviour seems compatible with a dozen different hidden goals and hidden constraints. Where does my reasoning break down, and what should I do with the fact that it's breaking down?
