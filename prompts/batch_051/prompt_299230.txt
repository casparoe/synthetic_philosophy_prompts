I'm a PhD candidate working on nineteenth-century philosophy of history, and before I present my reading of Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation in a seminar, I'd like to get the internal consistency of my position checked. I've been trying to turn my interpretation of the monumental, antiquarian, and critical relations to the past into an explicit set of commitments. Here is where I've landed.

1. The value of a historical practice is entirely instrumental: it is exhausted by its contribution to the plastic force (plastische Kraft) of an individual or a culture, understood as the capacity to incorporate the past, set limits to it, and go on acting.
2. Within historical representation, as opposed to sheer forgetting, the monumental, antiquarian, and critical relations to the past are jointly exhaustive of the ways history can serve life.
3. Each of the three modes has a characteristic failure that is an overgrowth of its own virtue rather than a foreign corruption: monumental history becomes edifying myth, antiquarian history becomes indiscriminate curation, and critical history becomes a tribunal that exempts itself from the genealogical conditions it applies to everything else.
4. Monumental history is constitutively selective: it isolates exemplary actions from their causal antecedents, and its capacity to guide action depends on that isolation. Antiquarian history succeeds only insofar as it resists this kind of isolation and preserves the particularity of the inherited past.
5. A critical verdict on the past can never be epistemically justified, because there is no standpoint outside the history being judged. Its justification is always practical: it opens a possible future that the inherited past forecloses.
6. Antiquarian history is justified even where it produces neither an action-guiding model nor a verdict against anything, because continuity of identity is itself something life requires under ordinary conditions.
7. The single standard of service to life is sufficient to adjudicate conflicts among the three modes. Where monumental selectivity and antiquarian fidelity pull in opposite directions, there is in principle a fact of the matter about which concession better conduces to plastic force.

I cannot tell whether this set is jointly tenable. Could you work through the pairs and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what each genuine tension would cost to fix? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and say which single revision would do the most work.
