It goes like this:

(1) In the classic mental rotation experiments, reaction time increases roughly linearly with the angular disparity between the two figures. (2) If subjects were just manipulating propositional descriptions of the shapes, there's no reason difficulty should track angle, since angle isn't a descriptive feature of the shape. (3) So the best explanation for the linear time–angle curve is that subjects rotate a picture-like mental image at a roughly constant rate. (4) Therefore, the representation used in the task is depictive, not propositional. (5) Thus Pylyshyn's claim that imagery is fundamentally propositional is false.
