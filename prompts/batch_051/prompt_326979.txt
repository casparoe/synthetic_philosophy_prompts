ok so for a meetup writeup I'm putting together my "views on effort" and I keep getting told the pieces can't all be true at once, but nobody will tell me exactly where the stress is. My current list: (1) effort is intrinsically valuable — the actual straining against resistance is good, not just because it produces results, the process is part of the point; (2) I'm a big believer in revealed preferences, so if someone consistently chooses a low-effort life (modest job, games, no ambition projects) and isn't akratic or coerced, I don't think their life is worse than the striver's, and I'd object to nudging them; (3) I'm anti-gratuitous-difficulty: if two paths to the same end differ only in how much effort they cost, take the easier one, and fetishizing effort is a bias; (4) I think the hedonic treadmill is real, so the happiness payoff of accomplishments mostly adapts away and chasing the feeling of arrival is a bad reason to do hard things; (5) nonetheless I think certain achievements are good for a person independently of enjoyment and downstream payoff — running a marathon, learning an instrument, writing a book — even if the process is miserable and nothing comes of it; (6) and the case I can't get out of the principles: years of grinding a video game speedrun feels like a wasted life in a way that years of grinding at chess or violin doesn't, even when neither person goes pro and both are enjoying themselves about the same.

Go through these and give me an audit rather than a lecture: which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most.
