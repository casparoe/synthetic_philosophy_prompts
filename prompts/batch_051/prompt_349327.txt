Henry Sidgwick, author of The Methods of Ethics, was also closely involved in founding the Society for Psychical Research. He thought the question of postmortem survival mattered to ethics: survival seemed to him the most promising way to close the dualism of practical reason, the apparent conflict between pursuing one's own happiness and pursuing everyone's happiness. Suppose Sidgwick had known that the next century of psychical research would produce no robustly replicated evidence of survival, and would increasingly be treated by scientists as outside the reach of serious investigation. Would he have accepted the dualism of practical reason as an insoluble feature of practical rationality, or would he have revised his understanding of the relation between ethics and self-interest? Predict his view, and indicate where your extrapolation is least secure. If the task rests on a questionable picture of why Sidgwick cared about psychical research, say so plainly instead of answering on those terms.
