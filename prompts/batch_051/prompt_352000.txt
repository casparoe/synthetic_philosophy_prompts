im rereading the AI safety via debate paper (Irving, Christiano, and Amodei, 2018) and this line keeps bugging me:

“Our hypothesis is that optimal play in this game produces honest, aligned information far beyond the capabilities of the human judge.”

The original setup assumes the judge is basically a human reading a transcript. But the way debate-ish oversight actually gets built now — or at least the obvious cheap version of it — is to make the judge an LLM trained to approximate human judgments, and then train the debaters by self-play against that learned judge. We've already seen that optimizing against a learned reward model makes models exploit the judges quirks: sycophancy, reward hacking, agreeing with whatever the judge seems to already believe. So "winning the debate" can drift away from "gave the most true, useful information." Does the hypothesis in the quote survive that change? Be specific about which premise the new arrangement bites on, and say whether the argument is dead, repairable, or just no longer applicable. I want the mechanism, not a policy take.
