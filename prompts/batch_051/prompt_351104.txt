i'm a novelist, not a philosopher. i run a 90-minute salon with about a dozen other writers, and i'm working on a book where a mid-level official has to choose between two awful policies: one ruins a single person badly, and the other imposes a tiny, barely-registered cost on an absurdly large number of people. i want my writing group to feel that tension in their bodies before we talk about it, so i can steal their reactions for the characters. the thing is, if i just describe the thought experiment everyone's heard a version of it and they'll say the polite thing. so instead i want a runnable game/activity where the rules force them into the problem, and i don't have to lecture until the very end.

some constraints: about 12 people, 90 minutes, in a living room with chairs and enough floor for a short walking loop. i like the idea of a literal dust speck: one grain of dry rice in your shoe, versus one person's "stone" being a spoonful of rice in one shoe plus a loaded backpack while they walk a slow loop — or something in that register. geninely real but harmless, easy to back out of, observers can just vote and watch. no actual pain beyond annoyance, no humiliation stuff.

design it so the idea shows up in what happens, not in what i say afterwards. i'd love rounds with secret votes, some kind of lottery or council, a running ledger of private discomfort ratings (0-10) after each round, and a mid-game "buyout" where the group can cancel all remaining rounds by accepting one concentrated harm — that's the moment i suspect they'll reveal what they actually think. give me exact setup and materials, roles, the rules, a timeline, what will predictably go wrong when i run it and how to handle it, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the real philosophical issue (the aggregation of tiny harms versus one terrible harm) only after the game has made them live it. be honest about which features of the original torture-vs-dust-specks problem the game can't capture — for instance the astronomical scale and the fact that my game, unlik the original, makes the voters the same people who might be picked.

put on your moral-psychologist-and-game-designer hat. think carefully, brainstorm two or three candidate formats first, then settle on one and write it out to be run by someone who isn't you.
