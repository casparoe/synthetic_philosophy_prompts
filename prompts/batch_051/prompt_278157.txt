I've been reading Boethius's Consolation of Philosophy on my own, and the part I keep coming back to is in Book V, Prose III, where he brings up the objection that if God knows everything ahead of time, then prayers and vows are pointless. I won't quote it because I might mangle the words, but what his character Philosophy says back to him struck me as different from the usual line that prayer is really about changing yourself. She seems to be saying instead that prayer offered in the right way is not useless, because the asking itself is part of what God has already included in his eternal view, and refusing to ask is what cuts you out of the thing you're hoping for, not God's foreknowledge. That's my rough understanding, anyway.

I'm giving a short talk next month at our church adult-ed hour, and I want to defend roughly this view: that petitionary prayer to an omniscient, unchanging God makes sense, but not for the reason people normally give. My view is that when I ask God for something, the point is not to give God new information or to change his mind, and it's also not mainly to make my own heart better; the point is that my asking becomes one of the causes in the one unrolling of things that God already sees as present. I know that sounds half-baked, but that's the thing I want to test out.

The audience is not academic at all, but it's a strange mix. There's a retired Presbyterian elder who believes God ordains everything and will probably say that prayer is only for our sanctification and has no effect on what happens. There's a woman who leads a prayer chain and talks about prayer as if it moves God's hand; she'll hate anything that makes God look like a fixed system. There's a college kid who has read just enough philosophy to bring up something like, if the outcome is already settled then your asking can't be a real cause. And then there are maybe a dozen regular people who mostly want to know if they should keep asking God for help with concrete stuff or if that's a category mistake.

I need the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come from this group, with each one phrased the way one of those specific people would say it, how likely it is and how damaging it is, what I should concede and what I should hold, and a short reply for each, including a note on where the reply is weakest. Also include the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and tell me what to do if it comes up.
