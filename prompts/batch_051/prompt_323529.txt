I am an undergraduate taking a course on the epistemic foundations of game theory, and I have to run a sixty-minute tutorial for about twenty students who know strict dominance and Nash equilibrium but have not yet studied iterated elimination of dominated strategies. I want a game that makes them feel why common knowledge of rationality is a much stronger assumption than everyone's being rational: a player's first step of reasoning should feel obvious, the second step should already require attributing rationality to the other player, and by the third step many students should stall, refuse the chain, or start second-guessing it. Brainstorm several candidate games first; then give me the design you would actually run. It should be playable with only paper, cards, or a whiteboard, with a payoff scheme that does not need real money, and it should be structured so that each student's stopping point in the iterated-reasoning chain shows up in their choices before the concept is named. Include exact rules, a realistic timeline, a debrief that maps different choices to levels of reasoning and to the formal notion of common knowledge of rationality, the point in the debrief where that formal definition should be introduced, one predictable way the activity goes wrong when students do not take the choices seriously and how to recover, and an honest note on which feature of the assumption the game cannot fully reproduce. Do not assign any reading; everything students need should be presented within the session.
