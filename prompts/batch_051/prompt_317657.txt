My home country just made a big deal about climbing in the World Happiness Rankings, and now there's an actual government office whose whole job is to get us into the top ten. I've got family back there and they keep sending me articles about it, so I finally read how the rankings are actually built. Its basically one survey question, the ladder thing: people are asked to rate their life from 0 to 10 against the best and worst life they can imagine. What struck me is that the researchers who do this kind of work now seem pretty careful to call that life evaluation, not happiness. Happiness proper, they say, is more like how people actually feel moment to moment, and with phones doing experienace sampling that stuff can be measured in a way it couldn't when the rankings first got designed.

So the old argument for the whole project was something like: the rankings measure happiness, happiness is the thing that ultimately matters for policy, so a country that can should try to raise its score. But if the ladder only captures life evaluation, and life evaluation can come apart from how happy people actually are, I can't tell what happens to that argument. Is it dead? Does it survive in a weaker form? Which premise actually takes the hit, and what should the repaired version say a country should be trying to do, if anything?
