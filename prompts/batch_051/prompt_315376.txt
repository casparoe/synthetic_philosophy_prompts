So I'm teaching a Philosophy of Technology seminar this term — around fifteen upper-level students, mostly philosophy majors, a few HCI people sitting in. I want a piece of writing I can use instead of a dry lecture on the attention economy. Write me a parody of an internal design-principles document, the kind a VP of "Growth and Wellbeing" at a social media company would circulate. Headline something like "Our North Star: Attention, Deeply Nurtured." Form: a short internal memo or design-system principles page, around 800 words, with numbered "principles" and maybe a short FAQ at the end. Voice: deadpan corporate sincerity, LinkedIn-style; no winking, no scare quotes, no moment where the document admits it's bad. The target is persuasive design and the way the industry defends itself in the language of autonomy and "time well spent." The jokes have to come from getting the actual mechanics right: variable-ratio reward schedules, infinite scroll and autoplay as the removal of stopping cues, default effects and friction, streaks and loss aversion, maybe social proof and reciprocity, and the old "we're just giving people what they revealed they want" move. You can use the standard dark-pattern vocabulary (confirm-shaming, roach motel, forced continuity) as long as it stays deadpan, like it's just normal practice. Don't invent mechanics; exagerate real ones. And the key constraint: the document should never tip into outright villany. Every clause should be something a real product manager could defend in the idioms the industry already uses. A product person should find it funny and a little uncomfortable; a philosopher worried about manipulation and compromised autonomy should see the argument play out without being told; and a reader with no background should still be able to tell it isn't sincere. Include one FAQ entry where the company explains why a cancellation flow that takes sixty seconds, with a button labeled "I don't want to take care of myself," is really about informed choice. About 800 words, American English please.
