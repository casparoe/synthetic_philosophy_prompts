My considered view is that most of the free will debate has been chasing a variety of free will that isn't worth wanting. When people insist they could have done otherwise given exactly the same past and laws, I think they're hallucinating a power they don't actually value in practice; what they really want is the control that matters for responsibility — deliberating, responding to reasons, noticing when you're being manipulated, and owning what you do. That kind of control is perfectly compatable with determinism. The 'could have done otherwise' stuff is a proxy, and not even a good one. So: what would count against this? I want the cases and arguments that should actually move me — for instance, a real-world or imagined case where someone has all my conditions and we'd still say their will wasn't free, or a variety of free will people demonstrably do value that determinism rules out. Rank them by how decisive they'd be, and be honest about whether I've defined 'worth wanting' so narrowly that the view can't lose.
