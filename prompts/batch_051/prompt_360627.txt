In our seminar on self-locating belief, we have been working through the halfer and thirder analyses of the original Sleeping Beauty problem, especially as set out in Elga's 2000 Analysis paper, Self-locating belief and the Sleeping Beauty problem. I would like to test how robust those analyses are to a small change in the memory technology. Suppose everything remains as in the standard version—fair coin, Heads gives a single Monday waking, Tails gives Monday and Tuesday wakings with an amnesia-inducing sleep in between—except that the amnesia is imperfect. Beauty is told on Sunday that if she wakes on Tuesday after a Tails Monday, there is a known probability p that she will remember the Monday waking; otherwise her Tuesday waking feels exactly like a first waking. When she wakes and has no memory of a previous waking, what should her credence in Heads be? Please work this out under both the halfer and thirder approaches, and say whether the original disagreement survives, reverses, or becomes more subtle as p varies from 0 to 1. In particular, indicate where the thirder's appeal to indistinguishable centered worlds loses its grip.
