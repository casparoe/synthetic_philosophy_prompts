I'm applying for a three-month AI safety research grant (conceptual/theoretical work, no experiments), and I need a two-page proposal. I've been reading the LessWrong-adjacent literature on low-impact agents and impact measures, and my half-formed idea is this: every proposal I've seen for measuring or penalizing "side effects" quietly sneaks in a partial specification of value, even when it claims to be value-neutral. Attainable-utility preservation compares the agent's behavior to an "inaction" baseline, but what counts as inaction is doing a lot of the work; relative-reachability measures need prior decisions about what counts as a state, which changes in reachability matter, and which actions count as the agent's; task-specific side effect penalties assume someone has already drawn the boundary between the task and the rest of the world. My suspicion is that this is not a bug in particular measures but a property of the concept itself: "side effect" is contrastive — an outcome is a side effect only relative to some intended effect, and marking an effect as intended is already a value claim.

Turn this into a proposal that:
(1) states the precise question, more specific than "is low-impact AI possible?" and closer to my suspicion about whether side effects can be defined without at least partially specifying value;
(2) argues that existing formulations leave the question open, by showing how at least two or three live approaches either assume the answer or systematically fail to separate side effects from intended effects;
(3) lays out what I would actually do, month by month, and why this approach rather than the obvious alternatives (for example, why not just build a better impact measure, or run more experiments with existing penalties);
(4) says what a result would look like — a taxonomy, an impossibility theorem under a clearly stated set of assumptions, a diagnostic that exposes hidden value assumptions in any proposed measure — and what would count as the project failing.

Assume the reviewer already accepts that AI safety matters; don't spend the proposal on scene-setting. Don't include a bibliography; I'll add citations myself.
