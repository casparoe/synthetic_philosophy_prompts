You are a professional metaphysician who specializes in mereology and the ontology of ordinary material objects. I am trying to settle on a working definition of a single material object, understood as an answer to the special composition question: under what conditions do several things compose one thing, rather than merely forming a plurality? I have been working with a rough criterion I call joint causal signature. The idea is that some xs compose one object just in case, for a non-trivial range of forces and timescales, the xs exhibit a stable collective response to perturbation that depends on their arrangement and persists across the replacement of individual xs. But I suspect this is at best a diagnostic rather than a definition: it may overcount starling flocks, queues, and markets, and it may undercount things with precarious integrity, like a cracked teacup or a sandcastle. I am not looking for a survey of existing views. Start by brainstorming three or four candidate criteria and say briefly why you reject or refine each, then give me your final criterion stated precisely enough to apply mechanically to new cases. If you cannot get all of my pre-theoretic verdicts, say which verdicts should be revised rather than gerrymandering the criterion.

Run your criterion through the following cases, giving a definite one-object or mere-plurality verdict for each. I regard these as clearly one object: a living rabbit; a solid gold ring; a single water molecule in a glass of water. I regard these as clearly not one object: the Eiffel Tower, the Moon, and my left shoe taken together; the grains of sand on two distant beaches; two electrons held in separate vacuum chambers in separate laboratories. Here are the cases I genuinely cannot classify, and I want the criterion to force an answer: a heap of dry sand in a sandbox; the same grains shaped into a damp sandcastle; a starling flock midway through a murmuration; a jigsaw puzzle as loose pieces in its box, and the same pieces assembled; a human being with a titanium hip, dental implant, and cochlear implant; two water droplets just after their surfaces have touched and a narrow neck has formed between them; a corpse one second after death; and a cumulus cloud that is just beginning to split into two. For each case, state the verdict, briefly justify it under your criterion, and identify the single case that most tempted you to revise the criterion.
