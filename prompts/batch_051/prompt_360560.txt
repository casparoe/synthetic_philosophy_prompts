The standard defense of mandatory arbitration is that arbitration is a consensual, faster, cheaper way to actually resolve a dispute outside court. But two things seem to break that picture. First, the "consent" is mostly a clickwrap term or a line in an onboarding packet, not something anyone negotiated. Second, mass arbitration: a firm gets hit with thousands of near-identical individual demands at once, and because the company pays a big fee per case up front, the point often isn't to adjudicate anything—its to make the arbitration bill itself the settlement leverage. So the mechanism stops looking cheap, consensual, or even like dispute resolution. Is there any version of the pro-arbitration argument that survives those changes, or was that argument only ever good for genuinely voluntary arbitration between sophisticated parties? What's actually left of it?
