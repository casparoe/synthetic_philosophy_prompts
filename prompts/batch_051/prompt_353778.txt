Notes. Aristotle discusses money in at least two argumentative settings. In Nicomachean Ethics V.5 the problem is how goods produced by different crafts can be exchanged at all, given that they are unlike in kind. Aristotle's answer runs through need, which he says holds a community together, and through money (nomisma), which serves as a conventional measure: it renders dissimilar products commensurable and stands in for future need, since it can be held until it is wanted. Money exists by convention rather than by nature, so that it can be altered or rendered worthless if the community using it decides to do so.

In Politics I.8–10 the emphasis is different. There Aristotle is occupied with the boundary between household management, which acquires goods for the sake of use, and wealth-getting, which can become acquisition for its own sake. Money appears not chiefly as a measure but as the instrument through which exchange changes character. Barter for mutual use, retail trade for money, and lending at interest form a sequence in which the end shifts from use toward accumulation; lending is the most unnatural form because money, introduced to facilitate exchange, is made to generate more money.

The essay would argue the following. The Ethics and the Politics do not merely describe the same institution twice; they assign money different explanatory roles. The Ethics needs money to solve a problem internal to just exchange, namely how unlike goods can be equalized. The Politics needs money to explain how acquisition can lose its natural limit. These roles sit uneasily together. If a conventional measure is supposed to preserve a natural standard of need, Aristotle owes an account of how conventional quantification can track natural need without collapsing into the open-ended accumulation the Politics condemns. The essay's tentative conclusion is that he does not supply this account, that these passages contain no worked-out doctrine of what a just price is, and that the appeal to practical judgment by the parties to an exchange marks the site of the difficulty rather than solving it.
