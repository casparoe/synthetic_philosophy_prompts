Two friends of mine, Priya and Jamal, got into it at lunch over a story our friend Ravi told. Last month Ravi sent a lost tourist to platform 2 and said "I know that's the train" after glancing at a schedule from the day before; he had nothing riding on it. Today he has a job interview, and missing the train would cost him the job. With the exact same evidence he says "I don't know that's the train; I should check."

Priya said Ravi didn't contradict himself. "Knows" sets a threshold the way "tall" and "flat" do, and context moved the threshold: he counted as knowing last month, but today's standard is higher and he doesn't meet it. Jamal said that's a semantic dodge. The train, the schedule, and his evidence haven't changed, so a shift in meaning would mean Ravi wasn't even asking the same question; but from the inside he feels genuinely less sure, he doesn't feel a word change. The stakes changed, and that changes how much certainty he needs in order to know, full stop.

Priya replied that Jamal's view makes knowledge depend on bank balances and job prospects, which are irrelevant to whether a belief is true and justified. At least contextualism confines the shift to the word, not to what he knows. Jamal answered that the cost of being wrong is exactly what determines whether someone is entitled to act on and assert a belief, so practical stakes aren't an intrusion into knowledge; they're part of what sets the standard. And "knows" doesn't behave like an indexical — nobody senses a meaning shift the way you do with "I" or "here."

Priya shot back that context-sensitivity doesn't feel like a meaning shift because it's automatic: you don't notice "I'm here" changing reference either. And if Jamal's invariant "knows" has one standard, he has to say Ravi was flat-out wrong one of the two times — but both claims look correct from the inside. Jamal said he doesn't have to: Ravi knew last month relative to a low-stakes standard, and doesn't know today relative to a high-stakes one — same word, same evidence, different real epistemic standing because knowledge answers to what's at stake.
