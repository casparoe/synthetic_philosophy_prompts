Write an Onion-style news article — headline plus around 500 words — about a country that has just introduced Oskar Lange-style trial-and-error pricing for its fully nationalized economy. The new State Committee for Price Adjustment anounces that it will set prices the way an auctioneer would: whenever a good is in shortage, it raises the posted price next quarter, and whenever there's a surplus, it lowers it. The jokes should come from the actual socialist calculation debate, not generic bureaucrat jokes. So: Hayek's point that the dispersed, tacit knowledge the committee is trying to collect mostly can't be reported up the chain (by the time it's aggregated into the committee's forms it's stale and locationless); Mises's point that without real exchange between propertied actors, the numbers being posted are more like labels than prices; and the deeper problem that the adjustment scheme quietly assumes the exact thing it was supposed to replace — that managers will respond to the posted prices the way competing firms do in a market. Make the managers' incentive to game the shortage and surplus reports a running gag, maybe via the new Form 12-C. Deadpan throughout; never have the article say 'this is obviously doomed.' Include one quote from a ball-bearing plant manager who is wildly optimistic about the protocol. Someone who knows the debate should feel like it's fair, and someone who doesn't should still find it funny.
