OK so I was an experimentalist most of my life, retired, and recently got into philosophy of physics, especially the absolute vs relational debate about space and time. I keep coming back to Newton’s bucket passage in the Scholium to the Definitions in the Principia. From memory of the text (apologies if I get a word wrong), Newton describes something like this: hang a bucket of water by a long cord, twist the cord, let go. At first the bucket spins while the water stays flat; later the water picks up the rotation and climbs the sides into a concave shape. He then points out that at the start the bucket is moving relative to the water but the surface is flat, and later the water and bucket are at relative rest but the surface is concave. So the water’s true circular motion can’t be defined just by its motion relative to the bucket; according to Newton it must be referred to something that doesn’t rotate, namely absolute space. Now I’d like you to write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers, a spacetime substantivalist and a relationist, both looking at this same passage. The relationist should not be a strawman; I want the best modern relationist replies — maybe inertial frames are primitive, maybe Machian-type moves, maybe field-based accounts — and the substantivalist should push back hard. They should genuinely respond to each other’s actual points, interrupt, concede a thing or two, and not just give set-piece speeches. The central question they should work toward is whether the concave surface is evidence for a substantival space, or just for a seperate set of preferred trajectories that can be built into a relationist picture without absolute space. Please think carefully and brainstorm the arguments on both sides before you write, act as an expert philosopher of physics who is sympathetic to both positions, and then write the dialogue in continuous prose, like an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Also, as you write, try to keep it concrete: I’m an experimentalist, so I like examples and thought experiments, not just labels. And don’t let them talk past each other.
