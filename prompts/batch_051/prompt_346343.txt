I've been turning over a question I want worked through using Peter Shaffer's *Amadeus* (either the play or the Miloš Forman film). The movie is beloved and also wildly untrue: Salieri almost certainly didn't kill Mozart and wasn't the mediocrity the film makes him out to be, and the film kind of knows it, since it's framed as a deluded old Salieri confessing to a priest. Read the play/film through the ethics of what we owe the dead when we dramatize them: does Shaffer owe the real Salieri accuracy or dignity, or is that a catagory mistake? Can you wrong a dead person by fictionalizing them — and if you can, is the unreliable-narrator frame a way of honestly acknowledging the debt rather than sliding out of it? I want a reading that takes the actual work seriously scene by scene, not the lazy *it's just fiction* dismissal, and that comes down somewhere: is Amadeus defensible, and if so what is the strongest version of the defense — where does the movie earn the right to bend the record, and where should a fair critic still wince?
