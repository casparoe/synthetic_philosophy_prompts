im on my state arts council's grants committee—im the resident novelist, which is a running joke because everyone else funds museums, operas, that kind of thing—and we're deadlocked on a request from a mid-career novelist for two years of support to finish a book. the application is strong on craft, but half the panel says a novel is a private pleasure you can buy in paperback, so public money would just be buying him a sabbatical; the other half, me included, keeps gesturing at the idea that fiction does civic work—training attention, letting people live inside other heads, keeping a language sharp—but we keep failing to say exactly why the state should pay for it. i need to circulate a short written opinion before thursday's meeting: recommend fund, dont fund, or fund with conditions, and it has to give the strongest version of the losing side, not a strawman. draft the memo for me, and be honest—if the best argument is to reject it, write that.
