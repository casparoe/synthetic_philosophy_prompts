I'm setting the final for a upper-level seminar on perception and I want one essay question on Molyneux's problem that won't let students just regurgitate Locke versus Leibniz. It should centre on this bit from Locke's An Essay Concerning Humane Understanding, Book II, Chapter IX, section 8:

"When we set before our eyes a round globe of any uniform colour, v.g. gold, alabaster, or jet, it is certain that the idea thereby imprinted on our mind is of a flat circle, variously shadowed, with several degrees of light and brightness coming to our eyes. But we having, by use, been accustomed to perceive what kind of appearance convex bodies are wont to make in us; what alterations are made in the reflections of light by the difference of the sensible figures of bodies; – the judgment presently, by an habitual custom, alters the appearances into their causes."

The question should make them work out how much of Locke's answer to Molyneux is carried by the kind of habitual judgment described here, and whether that makes the dispute an empirical one about acquired visual habits or a conceptual one about what "same shape" could mean across touch and sight. Write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20. The marking scheme needs to name the specific moves a top answer must make and the specific mistakes that should cost marks, not just list topics. Start your response with the exam question itself, no preface; then model answer; then marking scheme. British English please.
