Draft the joint statement that a Weilian opponent of political parties and a defender of competitive party democracy could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The Weilian holds that parties are coercive collectivities: whatever their program, they manufacture collective passion, subordinate members' individual judgment to a party line, and make disagreement read as betrayal, so that a political order built on them tends toward intellectual servitude even when their intentions are decent. The defender holds that parties are indispensable condensers of political alternatives: they aggregate interests, give voters tractable choices, organize accountability, and ensure that suppressing them would push faction and oligarchy underground rather than restore independent judgment. They have argued for two years and now want to coauthor a single document. Give (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both can sign, neither vacuous nor slanted; (2) a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement is worded so that its own side would accept the formulation; (3) the cruxes on which at least one side would move, such as whether the corruption of individual judgment is intrinsic to parties or a feature of organized power generally, whether party labels are a necessary cognitive shorthand for voters or a rationalization for not thinking, and whether a non-party polity would produce public-spirited deliberation or simply re-create parties under other names; and (4) for each crux, a concrete historical case, institutional comparison, or behavioral datum both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.
