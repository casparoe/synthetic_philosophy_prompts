I have been arguing with a colleague about whether it makes sense to say that English has a past-tense rule, and the dispute keeps turning on what a rule is supposed to be. Both of us can point to facts that seem to pull in opposite directions. Speakers extend the regular pattern to items they have never heard before: a made-up verb like wug gives wugged, and children learning English reliably produce forms like goed and runned before converging on the adult forms. But the language is also full of went, was, thought, and led, which no straightforward procedure applied to the present form will generate. Some of these are opaque remnants of sound changes that no longer apply, while others are suppletive, with forms like go and went historically coming from different sources. So the idea of a grammatical rule is doing real work, and I need a clearer specification of what that work is.

Before I read further in this literature, I want to know what an adequate theory of what a grammatical rule is must deliver. Assume the purpose is not pedagogical convenience but an account of what a speaker's knowledge of a language consists in, or at least of what would make the distinction between applying a rule and retrieving an exception correspond to something real. Please write the desiderata: the phenomena such a theory must explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must draw or decline to draw, and the results from neighboring fields it must be consistent with. For each requirement, give the case or observation that motivates it, such as productivity with novel forms, the contrast between regular and irregular inflection, overregularization in acquisition, suppletion, opacity created by sound change, and the regularization of rare or borrowed forms. Then indicate which requirements you take to be non-negotiable, which are negotiable or specific to one theoretical camp, and whether any pair of requirements is likely to be jointly unsatisfiable. If you mention candidate views, keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the views. Write the response in American English.
