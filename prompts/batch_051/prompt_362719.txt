I have spent the past month translating a set of short, rhymed, metrically strict poems left by my mother, who wrote them in the final year of her life. The undertaking has become a family argument. My brother maintains that a translation which preserves every image, sentence, and implication but drops the rhyme scheme, the meter, and the poem's return to its refrain has lost something the poem says, not merely something in how it is written; on his view, the formal constraints are among the poem's content-bearers. I am inclined to think that form is a replaceable vehicle: if the content gets through, the translation has done what a translation must do. Roughly, I assign 0.4 to the proposition stated below; my brother assigns about 0.85.

Please give me your own credence, as a number between 0 and 1, in the following proposition:

For poems of this kind, the formal structure—meter, rhyme, and fixed repetition—is part of what the poem says or expresses, in the sense that a translation which preserves all the ordinary word-and-image meaning but drops the form has failed to preserve part of the poem's content.

Do not stop at 'it depends on the poem.' Decompose your number into at least these four terms, giving a rough probability and a line of justification for each:

1. The probability that such formal features carry content at all, rather than coloring or decorating a content already carried by the words.
2. Conditional on their carrying content, the probability that the content is inseparable from that particular formal device in the original, as opposed to being re-expressible by some different device in the target language.
3. The probability that what is lost when the form is dropped is better classified as a loss of the poem's experienced effect than as a loss of part of what the poem says.
4. The probability that the governing norm of translation is faithfulness to what the poem says, rather than faithfulness to the experience of the original.

Then show how these terms combine to yield your number. Finally, identify the term to which your number is most sensitive, say what evidence or argument would move that term most, and say whether the residual gap between my 0.4 and my brother's 0.85 most plausibly lives in one of these terms or in a prior disagreement about what 'what the poem says' includes.
