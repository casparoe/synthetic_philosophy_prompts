I would like a working definition of 'the will of the people,' tested against the following cases. The phrase is used with great confidence in politics, but I am not sure it picks out a single well-defined thing, and I want a definition that is more than a dictionary gloss. In a binding national referendum conducted under fair rules with turnout around seventy-five percent, sixty-one percent of voters approve raising the minimum wage; I take this to be as close to a clear instance as we get. At the other end, a head of government whose party lost the popular vote and holds only a minority of seats declares that the people have given him a mandate to remove the judges investigating him, pointing to rallies organized by his own supporters; I take this not to be the will of the people. Between these two I am genuinely unsure. An opinion poll, competently sampled, finds fifty-eight percent support for a complicated trade treaty that most respondents say they have not thought about much. A randomly selected citizens' assembly hears months of expert testimony and ends up recommending a carbon tax, even though a poll taken before the assembly began would have shown a majority opposed. A general election under a plurality system gives a party thirty-six percent of the vote but a majority of the seats. A policy has majority support in every region, age group, and income bracket, but a small minority is intensely affected by it and protests disruptively against it. A coalition agreement is negotiated among three parties that together won fifty-five percent of the vote, but no party's manifesto contained the resulting compromise and no voter was ever offered the package as a whole. Please propose a definition, run it through each case in turn, and say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer; then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. I would also like your view on whether the phrase names something that already exists before it is measured, or something that is brought into existence by the process of voting, deliberating, or deciding. Please answer in continuous prose, as in a letter or an essay, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
