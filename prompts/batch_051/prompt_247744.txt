Me and a friend have been arguing about how social platforms moderate slurs, and I keep noticing this pattern. If you post "Smith is a [neutral term for a group]" it usually survives, but swap in the slur for that same group and the post gets taken down, even when nobody is being addressed and even when the person is quoting somebody or criticizing the slur itself. The automated filters seem to be keying on the string, not on what the speaker is trying to do. And then it gets stranger: in-group members sometimes get a pass for using the term among themselves, but other times the exact same usage gets removed anyway. It doesn't look like the moderation tracks the thought expressed, since you can express the same thought neutrally with no penalty. I'm taking a philosophy of language course and this keeps bugging me: what view of meaning is this practice committed to? Does it presuppose that the harm or badness is attached to the word itself, or to some conventional signal the word carries over and above what it refers to? Is the practice even coherent, given how inconsistently reclaimed uses are treated? And what would moderation have to look like if slurs were just coreferential with their neutral counterparts, with no extra content of their own?
