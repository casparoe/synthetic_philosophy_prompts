I've been keeping a log of my friend's verdicts about what could make a hell just, because she runs a theological reading group and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the principle that is actually doing the work for her. Her rulings so far, in my paraphrase:

1. A hell of eternal conscious torment imposed on ordinary people for a finite life of bad choices: unjust.
2. The same imposed even on the worst person who has ever lived: still unjust, because a finite agent cannot deserve an infinite penalty.
3. A painless end to existence for people who definitively refuse communion with God: not unjust — a sad privation rather than a punishment.
4. An eternity of mild but real suffering imposed on someone: unjust, because an infinite duration of even mild suffering is still an infinite amount of suffering.
5. A finite but severe purgatory imposed on someone who initially refuses it, which eventually reforms her and which she later affirms as the thing that saved her, ending in full reconciliation: just — and my friend adds this is the arrangement she most hopes is true.
6. A finite but severe punishment that a person freely chooses as the only way she can make amends, ending in reconciliation: just — consent changes the moral character of the suffering.

The new case is this. After death, a person is offered both full reconciliation and a painless exit from existence, but she freely, clear-headedly, and irrevocably chooses an eternity of mild but real suffering instead, saying it is the only fitting expiation for what she did. She will never change her mind, and the offer never closes.

Would my friend call that arrangement just? Is it a punishment on her view? I mostly want the underlying rule: lay out the two or three rules that fit her first six rulings, say what each predicts about the new case, give your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and name one further case that would most cleanly separate your preferred rule from its best rival. If her first six rulings genuinely underdetermine the new case, say which principle she would be adopting with each answer. Start with your bottom line and keep the whole answer under 650 words.
