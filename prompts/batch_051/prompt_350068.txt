I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long feature on what the collapse of FTX in November 2022 actually shows about effective altruism. I want to get past the cheap takes — both the one that treats Sam Bankman-Fried as proof the whole movement was rotten and the one that treats him as a lone fraud with nothing to do with EA. In particular, I want to organize the piece around three distinct internal critiques the movement attracted in the decade before the collapse. The first is the institutional critique: that EA systematically undervalued ordinary institutional safeguards — boards, independent oversight, professional norms, separation of roles — because it saw formal structures as friction rather than as carriers of hard-won knowledge. The second is the critique of earning to give: that steering talented people into high-paying but ethically questionable work, on the logic that they could donate the surplus, made the movement dependent on exactly the practices it claimed only to extract value from, and gradually dulled the distinction between using the system and being used by it. The third is the measurability critique: that EA's preference for what is quantified, tractable, and legible left it structurally bad at perceiving the kinds of risk — governance rot, character, institutional corrosion — that do not show up in a spreadsheet.

What I would like from you is a case analysis that runs the FTX collapse through those three lenses, and then turns the lenses back on themselves. For each critique, say exactly which features of the episode it accounts for — Bankman-Fried's public identification with EA, the movement's deference to large donors, the governance failures at FTX and Alameda, the expected-value-flavored reasoning — and which features it misdescribes or would have to stretch to explain. Then assess the episode's verdict on the critiques themselves: does FTX vindicate them, is it a fair test of them at all, and where do they risk sliding into guilt by association rather than a causal or institutional explanation? I want the symmetry kept honest: the piece fails if it can't say both where the critics were right and where the movement's replies retain force.
