I keep hitting the same confusion around the unilateralist's curse, and i'd rather work backward from concrete calls than start with a principle. Here are seven of mine:

- A grad student finds that a stats package used by hundreds of labs has a bug that silently invalidates some published results. They blog about it before contacting the maintainers, because they're sure an email would get ignored. I think that's fine, probably good.
- A security researcher finds a zero-day in a router millions of people use. They publish exploit code immediately, because they're sure vendors would otherwise sit on a patch. I don't think that's fine, and it bugs me how similar this is to the last case.
- A housemate throws out a shared sofa because they're sure no one wants it. Not fine.
- A small NGO dumps iron filings into the open ocean to test a geoengineering idea, at a scale they're sure is harmless. Not fine, even if they're right about the harm.
- A hiker rebuilds a small cairn at an ambiguous fork in an unmarked trail, so people behind them don't get lost. Fine.
- A junior researcher at an AI lab leaks an internal memo about a safety flaw to a journalist, because they're sure the lab will bury it. I go back and forth on this one, and I'd like something other than my own gut to settle it.
- A signatory to a voluntary AI development pause quietly starts a run bigger than the pause allows, thinking the pause is overcautious. Not fine, but I worry that's just my background talking.

Is there one principle, or a small set, that gets all or most of these? Please state it precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up if any, and say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: a doctor who unilaterally puts a patient on an experimental drug that the hospital board just rejected. Also say how confident you are in the principle and which part of your answer you're least sure about.
