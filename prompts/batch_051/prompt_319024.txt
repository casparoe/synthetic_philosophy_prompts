I'm the pricing actuary at a mid-sized auto insurer, and a couple times a month I get a driver who dont fit any of our standard rating cells. The underwriter needs a rate, usually within the hour. If I set it too high we lose the policy; too low and we bleed; and the state regulator occasionally audits a sample of my class choices, so I have to be able to write down why I chose the class I did. The company line is that you put the driver in the right reference class and use that class's expected loss. But the same driver belongs to hundreds of reference classes — all drivers in the state, drivers his age and sex, drivers with the same car, drivers with his exact claims history, recent movers, drivers whose VIN starts with 3, whatever — and they give different numbers. I gather philosophers of probability have a name for this, the reference class problem. I want a procedure I can actually run at my desk: a checklist or a flowchart in words, with the steps in order, for choosing a reference class when I have to put a number on this one driver. It has to handle nearly empty cells, the fact that the narrowest class is just the driver himself (so the observed frequency is 0 or 1), and reference classes that look gerrymandered but fit the claims data suspiciously well. It also needs to say what to do when the most predictive class is one our regulator or our own fairness policy won't let us use — do I switch to the next-best legal class and carry on, or was that class never legitimate in the first place? Flag each step that is a judgment call dressed up as procedure, and tell me what the procedure cant do. Then run it on three drivers: (1) a 34-year-old man, four years of no at-fault claims, moved three months ago from a rural county to a dense city and kept a car that gets stolen unusually often, so his old-county class says one number, his new-zip class another, and the 'recent mover' cell is tiny; (2) a 61-year-old woman with a twenty-year clean record and a restricted daytime-only licence, but a car with a high theft rate in a zip with heavy collision claims; (3) a third one you get to pick, built for me to hate, where the procedure gives a number I'm not going to like. Start with your bottom line as the first sentence, then give me the reasoning. No preamble and no restating my question back at me.
