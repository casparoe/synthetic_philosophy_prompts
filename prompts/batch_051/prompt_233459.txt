Last week a friend and I fell down a rabbit hole about whether any of us could have been a different person. I keep coming back to a sharper version of the usual what-if-your-parents-never-met worry, and its got me stuck: could you have come from a different sperm fertilzing the same egg, and still be you? I want your actual credence that the answer is no — that in every possible world where you exist, you trace back to the very same sperm–egg pair. But I don't want a bare number; I want the reasoning decomposed, because I suspect the real disagreement is downstream and I can't tell where.

Break it into at least these pieces: (a) how much of your answer rides on which notion of you we adopt (living organism vs. mind or psychology vs. person in some narrative sense), and what credence you give each notion; (b) conditional on meaning the organism, how much weight you put on a Kripke-style necessity-of-origin argument that an organism cannot have come from a different zygote; (c) a granularity question — whether a different sperm that (counterfactually) bore the same genome as the actual one would count as the same origin, and whether the egg contributes to identity only through its DNA or through something else; (d) how monozygotic twinning bears on this, since two people can share the same sperm–egg pair, which seems to make the sperm–egg criterion insufficient to single you out; (e) how chimerism bears on it, since one person can descend from two fused embryos and thereby from two sperm and two eggs; and (f) the deflationary move — whether the whole question dissolves once you accept that identity isn't what matters and we should be asking about psychological continuity instead. Give each a rough probability, say how you combine them (and where the combination might go wrong), and give one bottom-line number. Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would actually move your credence up or down.
