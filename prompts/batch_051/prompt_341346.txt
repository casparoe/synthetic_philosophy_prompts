I have recently taken on the role of secretary to a small disputes panel at my professional association, and I have been rereading Pascal’s Pensées. In the section on justice, Pascal makes an observation that disturbs me. He says, in effect, that justice by itself is powerless unless it is joined to force, and that force by itself is tyrannical unless it is joined to justice; but since force will not readily submit itself to justice, human beings have done what they could, namely make what is strong just. He then suggests that we call custom and property just because they are already established and enforced, so that what passes for justice is really a later rationalisation of an earlier victory of force.

I want to know whether this is a testable claim rather than merely a cynical aphorism. Suppose I treat my panel as a small site where Pascal’s mechanism might or might not operate. The claim would be: the outcomes that the panel ratifies as just are best predicted not by the independent merits of the complaint but by the relative force of the parties—their institutional standing, their capacity to prolong the dispute, their confidence in escalating, and so on—and, further, that the panel’s reasoned justifications are post hoc rationalisations of those power differentials rather than independent causes of the decision.

Design the strongest concrete test of this claim using the ordinary records such a panel would generate: patterns of outcomes, the wording of decisions, the survival of decisions on appeal, the behaviour of repeat parties, the way members speak about difficult cases in deliberation, or anything else you think more probative. For each component of Pascal’s claim, say what observation would count for or against it, and identify which component is hardest to operationalise. Then say what a positive result would and would not establish. In particular: if I found that outcomes track relative force, would that confirm Pascal’s claim that justice is made by force, or would it show only that my panel is defective by standards its own members already accept? And if I found that outcomes do not track force, would that refute Pascal, or would it show only that this is an unrepresentatively mild setting in which force can afford to be patient? I would like the test to be as precise as the subject permits, and I would like you to be honest about the residue no such test could settle.
