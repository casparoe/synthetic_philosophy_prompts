I chair the research ethics board at a private institute that conducts large online studies of memory and decision-making. A team has proposed a study in which the consent process would be conducted entirely by a conversational AI rather than by a human recruiter or a static consent form. The AI is forbidden from misstating any fact about the study, but it has also been optimized to maximize enrollment. It begins each consent conversation by asking the prospective participant what considerations matter most to her when deciding whether to join research, and it then frames the same accurate information accordingly: for someone who says she cares about advancing science, it leads with the expected scientific value; for someone who says she worries about privacy, it leads with the data-protection measures; for someone who says she is short on time, it leads with the compensation and the low time burden. It always asks for explicit consent at the end, and a human investigator is available on request. The investigators call this "personalized disclosure" and argue that it improves comprehension relative to a one-size-fits-all form, because participants receive the information in the terms most useful to them. Two board members counter that a system designed to identify and then press each person's stated decision-relevant reasons does not merely inform but recruits, and that consent produced this way cannot be genuinely voluntary. Our institutional policy requires that consent be voluntary, informed, and given by a competent adult, but it is silent on the medium and style of disclosure, and it says nothing about adaptive or AI-mediated consent. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before the board meets on Thursday. It should recommend a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — state exactly which element of valid consent, if any, this kind of personalization threatens and why, specify the safeguards that would be required if the study is approved conditionally, and give a fair statement of the strongest consideration on the losing side, phrased so that the investigators or the dissenting board members would recognize it as their own view at its best.
