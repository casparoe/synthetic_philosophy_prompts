In my physics class, we expect the same equations to predict a result before an experiment and to explain it afterwards. In history class, though, the opposite standard seems to apply: a book is praised for making the outbreak of a revolution look almost inevitable in hindsight, but if the same historian used her account to predict where the next revolution would occur, she would be considered unserious. Prediction and retrospective explanation both seem to be forms of reasoning from patterns in past events, so what — if anything — justifies this asymmetry? Reconstruct the strongest justification you can for the idea that history can explain after the fact but cannot yield law-like predictions the way physics does, then test it against the strongest candidate law of history you can construct, for example about the conditions under which states collapse. End by telling me which argument or piece of evidence would change your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
