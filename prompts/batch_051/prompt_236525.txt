Act as an expert on Cold War strategic theory and rational-choice accounts of conflict. I host a podcast on strategy and international security, and I am preparing an episode built around an interview with Thomas Schelling, reconstructed strictly from the views he defended in his published work on deterrence and bargaining. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking him to restate his position. The questions should press on the actual mechanics of his theory: (1) why a nuclear threat can be made credible by deliberately arranging that the outcome is not fully under anyone's control, so that war can occur through accident or loss of control; (2) why this competition in risk-taking should count as rational rather than reckless; (3) how he would answer the worry that stable mutual deterrence makes lower-level conventional aggression safer to attempt, and whether his account of limited war as a generator of escalation risk actually absorbs that objection; and (4) how the mutual fear of being struck first in a crisis interacts with his claim that uncertainty and shared risk stabilize rather than destabilize. For each question, write the follow-up you would use against the reply he is most likely to give. Sequence the questions so they build toward a final question about what kind of Cold War crisis evidence would have counted against his account. Think through his likely answers before writing.
