I work as a software engineer, and an asymmetry in how people talk about machine learning keeps bothering me. A human mathematician who studies a corpus of results for years and then produces a theorem that no source in that corpus states or straightforwardly implies is credited with an intuitive leap. Yet when a language model trained on a corpus produces a theorem that no source in its training data states or straightforwardly implies—and that experts later judge correct—many of the same people dismiss it as mere interpolation over the training distribution. The two cases are structurally similar: a system is exposed to data and then outputs something not contained in it. I want the strongest principle that could justify treating the human's output as genuine novelty while treating the machine's as a statistical artifact. Test that principle against a case where the machine's result is unexpected, non-obvious, and independently verified before anyone knows how it was produced, and then say honestly whether the asymmetry should be kept or given up.
