So I just spent all evening arguing with a mate about Brandom's inferentialism — the idea that the meaning of an expression basically is its inferential role: roughly, what you can infer sentences containing it from, and what you can infer from those sentences. The criteria I ended up with are these: an expression is meaningful iff there are settled norms for (1) what licenses the introduction of sentences containing it — the conditions under which you may correctly assert them — and (2) what those sentences, once asserted, entitle you to infer. Fairly standard, I though. But then I got stuck on a made-up case. Suppose a community introduces a logical particle "florp" with exactly two rules: from any sentence P you may infer "P florp Q", and from "P florp Q" you may infer Q — for any sentences P and Q. So florp has a fully explicit inferential role and satisfies (1) and (2) perfectly, but the moment it is added, any sentence implies any other sentence, so the whole language becomes trivial. My question is: is florp meaningful according to my criteria? If it is, does that just show the criteria are missing a coherence or non-triviality condition? And if you think it is not, which criterion does it fail and why? I'd also like you to say what would change your mind about the verdict — which consideration or counterexample would push you the other way, or where you reckon your reasoning is most likely to break down.
