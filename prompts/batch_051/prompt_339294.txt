Below are working notes for a short essay I am drafting on incommensurable values and hard choices. I would be grateful for comments aimed at whether this should become something worth publishing: in particular, whether the argument is faithful to the parity view rather than attacking a strawman, where the weakest premise is, and whether the final proposal is genuinely different from the commitment view it is meant to improve on.

Notes:

The essay begins from the observation that not every hard choice is a failure of comparison. Some choices are hard not because one option lacks value along the relevant dimension but because the options are, as some philosophers put it, on a par: neither is better than the other, and they are not exactly equal either. I accept this distinction. My target is the further thought, sometimes drawn from it, that in hard choices the will can permissibly resolve what the normative facts leave open, and that the difficulty of such choices is therefore not a symptom of incomplete reasons but a kind of local normative settlement by the agent.

I want to resist that conclusion without giving up parity. My proposal is that parity is itself a sign that the covering value governing the choice is only partially specified. When a choice is hard in the parity sense, the values that make the options good are not yet precise enough to discriminate between them; there is no fact of the matter about which is better because the covering value does not yet have the structure to generate such a fact. Choosing, on this picture, is not a free exercise of the will but an act that completes or sharpens the covering value. The choice can be rational or irrational retrospectively: it is rational when it produces a specification of the covering value that coherently extends what was fixed before, and irrational when it merely discards what was fixed or contradicts it.

The intended novelty is in the claim that the reasons one ends up with are partly constituted by the choice but are not merely chosen. I use an analogy with adjudication in a novel legal case: the judge is not bound before the decision, but the decision is constrained and can be better or worse by the light of the materials it must extend. The residual sense that the choice could rationally have gone the other way is then not brute; it tracks the fact that more than one coherent specification of the covering value was available before the choice. Parity marks this plurality of admissible sharpenings, not an absence of reasons.

An objection I try to handle: if the covering value is incomplete, are we not really in incomparability rather than parity? I answer that incomparability concerns the relation between bearers of value for an already fixed standard, while parity concerns a plurality of admissible complete specifications of an incomplete standard. What looks like parity between options is, at the level of the covering value, an incompleteness in the standard. I worry this may collapse my view into a form of incomparability after all, and I am unsure how damaging that would be.

Another worry is that the proposal makes decisions self-correcting because the standard is revised to fit the choice. I try to block this by denying that any revision is permitted; only some revisions strike the earlier materials as reasonable.

I would particularly welcome comments on: whether the analogy with legal adjudication claims too much about practical reason; whether the worry about collapse into incomparability is real and, if so, whether it undermines the essay; and whether the essay's contribution is sharp enough to be worth trying to publish, or whether it is an existing position in slightly different language.
