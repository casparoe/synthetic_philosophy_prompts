In my former professional life I worked with numerical models and function-fitting procedures, and I have only recently begun reading Hume carefully. I am struck by how close his account of induction feels to certain formal results in machine learning, and I would like help locating the connection precisely rather than by loose analogy.

In David Hume's *An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding*, Section V, Part I, Hume writes:

"For wherever the repetition of any particular act or operation produces a propensity to renew the same act or operation, without being impelled by any reasoning or process of the understanding; we always say, that this propensity is the effect of Custom."

I take Hume's point here to be that the step from observed regularities to unobserved cases is not an inference licensed by reason, but a propensity produced by repeated experience. The no-free-lunch theorems in statistical learning theory—in particular Wolpert and Macready's result that, averaged over all possible target functions, no learning algorithm performs better than any other—seem, on first sight, to give a formal analogue of exactly that claim. A learner cannot succeed across all possible futures; its success depends on a prior or a restricted hypothesis class, which looks like a formalized version of custom or habit.

What I want to know is whether this analogy has real argumentative force. Does the no-free-lunch theorem actually strengthen Hume's skeptical argument against a rational justification of induction, or does it merely translate his point into a new notation? Does it change what the problem of induction is about—for example, by licensing the claim that induction is rational once a prior is explicitly stated—or does it show that the prior itself is only a mathematically regimented habit, leaving Hume's conclusion untouched? I would like an answer that separates Hume's logical point from his psychological point, and that says honestly whether the formal result supports, undermines, or subtly alters the target of Hume's conclusion.
