Act as a barrister who advises charity trustees and has a genuine philosophical command of the boundary between religion and spirituality without religion. Think through each reading before you write, in private; your final answer should consist only of the instructed list.

I am a trustee of the Rowanbridge Trust, a small charitable trust settled by deed in 1954. The objects clause reads, in full: 'The Trustees shall apply the income of the Trust for the advancement of religion and for no other purpose whatsoever.' Still Point, an unincorporated association formed in 2016, has applied for a grant to replace the roof of its retreat centre in the north of England and to train three facilitators. It runs forest stillness walks, seasonal solstice gatherings, gratitude circles, and silent retreats; its constitution describes it as 'spiritual but not religious', declares that it has 'no creed, no deity, and no clergy', and says that its object is 'to cultivate attention, awe, and the experience of belonging to something older and larger than the self'.

The other trustees are sympathetic but uncertain whether spending trust money on Still Point would be to apply it 'for the advancement of religion' within the meaning of the deed. The original trustees, in 1954, did not have this kind of applicant in view. I do not want case law, statute, or any citation; I want the interpretive reasoning itself, from the words and the purpose, because I can check the legal framework separately. What interests me most is what it would take for the deed's word 'religion' to reach a group that disclaims creed, deity, and clergy.

Please produce exactly five numbered entries, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. The entries should be: (1) the conclusion the plain words of the clause most naturally support; (2) the conclusion an appeal to the purpose of the deed supports; (3) what the original trustees would most plausibly have said if asked in 1954; (4) what a reasonable present-day applicant would fairly understand 'advancement of religion' to cover; and (5) which of these approaches ought to prevail and whether the application should be fundable, with the decisive reason.
