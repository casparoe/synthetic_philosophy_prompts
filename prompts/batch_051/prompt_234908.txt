I've been going down a rabbit hole on ubuntu and relational ideas of personhood, and I keep hitting the same snag. The view, roughly, is that you don't just have personhood by being born human—you become a person through other people: through being raised, addressed, recognized, held responsible, and stitched into a community. I find that persuasive a lot of the time, but I also can't shake the intuition that a castaway alone on an island, who'll never see another human again, still matters fully and keeps whatever moral status the rest of us have.

Could you write a short dialogue between two equally thoughtful philosophers—one defending a relational, ubuntu-style view, the other defending a capacity-based view on which personhood sits in your intrinsic features like rationality and self-awareness? Have them argue about the castaway case, and also about someone whose been formally exiled or shunned by their community while keeping all their capacities. I want them to actually reply to each other's actual points rather than trading speeches, and neither should be a strawman. At some point, each should say what would have to be true for their own view to be wrong.
