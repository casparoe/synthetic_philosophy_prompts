I'm putting together a problem set for an upper-level metaphysics class on dispositions, and I've been compiling the canonical counterexamples to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions — the biconditional that says x is disposed to M when stimulated by S just in case, were x to undergo S, x would M. The list I've got so far: (1) the fink, Martin's electro-fink, where a live wire is hooked to a device that makes it dead at the exact instant a conductor touches it, so it's live but wouldn't shock; (2) the reverse fink or finkish lack, where a dead wire is wired to a gadget that makes it live at the instant of contact, so it isn't live but would shock; (3) the mask, like a fragile glass packed in foam, which remains fragile but wouldn't break if struck because the foam absorbs the impact; and (4) the mimic, where something lacks the disposition but would still produce the manifestation under the stimulus because an independent helper mechanism steps in at just the right moment and does the producing for it. I'm fairly sure that's the standard quartet and there's no obvios fifth kind I'm overlooking.

Give me one more counterexample to the simple conditional analysis that isn't on this list and isn't a rewording, special case, or combination of (1)–(4). It should be the sort of thing I could set on a problem set, so keep it describable in a short paragraph without leaning on any fancy apparatus. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good: does it trouble the simple conditional analysis in its own way, or does it collapse into one of the four categories once you look at it closely? I'd rather know if it doesn't earn its keep.
