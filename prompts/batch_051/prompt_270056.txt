I'm a PhD student in cognitive science working on the philosophy of mental representation, and I have been trying to settle how much weight to give the distinction between original and derived intentionality. The position I have been leaning toward is the one Dennett presses with his two-bitser objection to Searle: that the difference between the intentionality of a human mind and that of an artifact such as a calculator, a text, or a computer simulation does not mark an intrinsic metaphysical boundary. On this deflationary view, human intentionality is no less derived than the intentionality of artifacts; it is just derived from a different set of sources — natural selection, cultural transmission, and the explanatory stance we take toward one another — rather than from the explicit intentions of a human designer. Searle's claim that only brains have original intentionality, while everything else has merely derived intentionality, would then be one of those distinctions that feels principled but dissolves once you trace the actual causal and interpretive history of both cases.

I am not committed to this view; I find it appealing but want to know how exposed it really is. Tell me what would count against it. I am not asking only for Searle's standard counterarguments. I want a structured map of the position's exposure: which empirical findings, thought experiments, phenomenological considerations, or theoretical developments would be decisive against the deflationary line, and which would merely create local difficulty. For example, would it count against the view if there turned out to be a psychologically real and information-theoretically robust difference between the content of neural states and the content of artifacts, such that neural content is tokened even when no one interprets it? Would evidence from prelinguistic infants, from nonhuman animals, from split-brain patients, or from artificial systems that are trained rather than designed matter? What would the world have to look like for Searle's original intentionality to be a live empirical or metaphysical hypothesis rather than a pretheoretical intuition dressed up as a discovery? And are there versions of the deflationary position that can absorb those findings, or does absorption itself show the view has been insulated from revision in a way that undermines its claim to be a substantive theory rather than a stance?

I would also like you to say which defeaters a committed Dennettian would themselves concede as counting against the two-bitser, rather than only what critics say, and to be willing to conclude that the deflationary view is well insulated and that this is either a feature or a problem. At the very end, give a one-sentence summary of your overall assessment as the last line.
