Define 'mathematics' so that it correctly classifies the following: a tally of livestock; a Mesopotamian school tablet of multiplication tables; the Babylonian tablet Plimpton 322, which encodes Pythagorean triples; a proof that there are infinitely many primes; a computer algebra system evaluating an integral; and an accountant preparing a tax return. I would insist that the tally is not mathematics and the proof is; I have no settled view about the middle cases. I suspect the boundary concerns whether one is merely executing a rule or reflectively investigating it, but that test may place Plimpton 322 and the computer algebra system in awkward positions, so I want you to say where the definition leads. Give the definition, run it through all six cases, and say which verdict is least secure and where you think the boundary breaks down.
