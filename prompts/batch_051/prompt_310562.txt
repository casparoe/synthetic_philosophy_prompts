Question: State the doctrine of double effect and explain the role of the distinction between intended effects and merely foreseen side effects. Then apply the doctrine to the following three cases, saying in each case whether the doctrine permits the action and why: (a) A runaway trolley is heading toward five track workers; the driver can turn it onto a siding where it will kill one track worker, saving the five. (b) A surgeon has one healthy patient and five patients who will die without organ transplants; she could kill the healthy patient and distribute the organs, saving the five. (c) As in (a), except that the siding is a loop that rejoins the main track before the five; the one worker is unusually heavy, and the trolley will be stopped before reaching the five only because it strikes him.

Student's answer: The doctrine of double effect says that an action with both a good and a bad effect is permissible only if (1) the act itself is not intrinsically wrong, (2) the agent intends the good effect and does not intend the bad effect either as an end or as a means, (3) the good effect is not produced by means of the bad effect, and (4) the good effect is proportionate to the bad effect. The real test of intention is certainty: when you know the bad effect will occur and you proceed anyway, you intend it, because you cannot honestly say it is no part of your plan once you see it coming with practical certainty. In case (a), the driver is certain the worker will be killed, so the death is intended; but turning the trolley is not itself wrong, saving five is the intended good, and the one death is proportionate, so the doctrine permits the action. In case (b), the surgeon intends the healthy patient's death and uses it as a means to the good, which violates condition (3), so the doctrine forbids the transplant. Case (c) is just like (a): the death is intended because it is certain, but the act is neutral and the death is proportionate, so the doctrine permits it as well. The doctrine therefore draws the line in the right place, permitting both trolley cases and forbidding the transplant.
