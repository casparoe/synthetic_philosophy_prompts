The covering-law versus narrative explanation debate still seems to me to run on a shared assumption: a narrative explains only if it does, covertly or partially, what a covering-law explanation does openly—subsume events under regularities. I suspect the assumption is backwards: narrative explanations earn their keep precisely where covering-law explanations would mislead, because they make an outcome intelligible by tracing how it held together across contingent junctures rather than by showing that it was to be expected.
