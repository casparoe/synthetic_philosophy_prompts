Thomas Hobbes held that geometry is the paradigm of certain knowledge, because we ourselves generate the lines and figures that are its objects and lay down their definitions; the principles are, as he repeatedly insisted, of our own making. He also never doubted that there was a correct geometrical doctrine to be discovered—he spent years trying to produce a quadrature of the circle and treated the inherited Euclidean framework as the model of demonstrative science.

In the nineteenth century, after Hobbes's death, mathematicians developed apparently consistent geometries that deny Euclid's fifth postulate, and by the early twentieth century it had become common to regard alternative geometries as alternative implicit definitions of terms such as line and point, no one of which is uniquely true of physical space except by stipulation.

Consider Hobbes, with his actual philosophical commitments, presented with these later developments. Would he have welcomed them as the natural consequence of his thesis that geometrical principles are made by us, or would he have resisted them as severing geometry from the body and motion that, on his account, it is ultimately about? Give a reasoned prediction of Hobbes's likely response, and be explicit about which features of his view do the main work: the claim that geometrical principles are made by us, his materialist conviction that geometry concerns body and motion, or his investment in a single correct geometry. Conclude by saying how confident you are in the prediction and which of Hobbes's commitments would have to yield if he responded the other way.
