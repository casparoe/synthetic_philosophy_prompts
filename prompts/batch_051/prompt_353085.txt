ok so here's roughly where i've landed on affirmative action and quotas after a semester of comparing the US, India, and Norway, and i want a consistency check before this goes in the paper. i hold:

(a) race-conscious holistic admissions at selective US colleges are justified, mainly as a remedy for ongoing residential and school segregation, but i don't think numerical quotas or set-asides in admissions are justified and anyway they're politically poisonous.

(b) India's reservation system is justified and should be thought of as long-term, maybe permanent, constitutional machinery, because caste is a specially sticky hierarchy and one generation of advantage doesn't dissolve it. but i also think creamy layer exclusions should be extended to SC/ST reservations, not just OBC ones—it's wrong to subsidize the kids of a senior bureaucrat purely on the basis of caste.

(c) Norway's 2003 law requiring 40% of public-company board seats to go to women was good policy, full stop. yes it's a quota and yes it worked.

(d) methodologically, i think group-based preferences should be temporary correctives that get reauthorized only if they demonstrably move the underlying gap, and i'm suspicious of any scheme that makes a permanent group category part of how the state distributes things.

(e) i don't think diversity is an intrinsic good; diversity justifications are okay only when they're really shorthand for tangible, measurable institutional or firm-level benefits.

which of these actually conflict, which only look like they conflict, and whats the cheapest fix? rank the real tensions by severity, don't just list every pairwise mismatch, and tell me which single revision would clean the set up the most.
