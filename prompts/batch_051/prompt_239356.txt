I keep going back and forth on this and I want you to adjudicate, but not by just picking a side—by telling me what each surrender actually costs. Steelman both first.

On one hand, I'm basically convinced by the constructivist story about religion: there isn't some transhistorical essence of "religion"; the category as we now use it got assembled in early modern Europe around a particular Christian picture of belief, texts, private conviction, and then got exported and imposed on the rest of the world. It explains so many otherwise weird facts—why "world religions" all conveniently end up looking a bit Protestant (founders, scriptures, creeds, sects), why courts and census-takers keep having to rule on whether secular humanism or a new movement counts, why the argument about whether Confucianism is a religion never gets anywhere. On those grounds I'm tempted to say there is no such thing as religion; there are just practices and communities and institutions that get sorted by a parochial grid.

But I also can't shake the intuition that the grid isn't just parochial. When I put a Buddhist philosophical dialogue, a medieval Christian devotional text, and the Bhagavad Gita next to each other, I feel like they're all doing something recognizably of the same kind—argueing about ultimate matters, the right relation to something beyond the ordinary world, how ritual reorders a person—in a way a geometry textbook is not. If you gave a thoughtful outsider these texts unlabeled, I suspect they'd group them together, not because of the word "religion" but because of something in the material itself. The category is doing real work, and "there is no such thing" starts to feel like a cheap deflationary move that refuses to look at what got grouped.

So which should give way, and how would I know? Is the intuition just essentialism plus my own enculturation—a cognitive bias and the accident of having been raised inside the category—so it should be debunked? Or does the constructivist theory need amending so it can say the category is historically contingent and politically loaded and still tracks a real pattern, the way a map projection is artificial but not arbitrary? Or are they compatible only if I give up the idea that a category is coherent only if it has an essence? Walk me through what each option costs and what evidence or argument would justify each move; don't tell me one side wins.
