I've been down the presentism-vs-eternalism rabbit hole for a couple years now — SEP, the usual physics blogs, plus a monthly argument with a metaphysics friend — and the debate seems weirdly, persistently stuck. The presentist says only the present exists, past and future are literally nothing, and the felt passage of time is a real feature of the world. The eternalist says the block universe is the way to bet: all times are equally real, and "now" is an indexical like "here," not a moving spotlight. Everyone accepts the empirical content of special relativity, and everyone agrees on what time feels like from the inside. The presentist accuses the eternalist of explaining away passage; the eternalist says the presentist can't handle relativity without an unobservable preferred foliation and can't say what makes it true that Caesar crossed the Rubicon if the past doesn't exist. The presentist says relativity only shows we can't detect the foliation, not that there isnt one; the eternalist says that's a suspicious amount of invisible structure to hold onto. I've also watched people dodge into growing-block and moving-spotlight positions whenever the binary gets uncomfortable, which makes it feel even more like the terms are doing the work. My hypothesis: the two sides aren't disagreeing about the world at all, but about what "exists" is supposed to do in a theory — one side optimizing for physics, the other for experience — even though both insist it's a substantive ontological dispute. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, with reasons, not a survey of the views. Is it verbal, substantive, two different questions, or an ill-posed question? And given that diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress — a formal result, a distinction, a better question — or should nobody expect it to move?
