I keep going in circles on the Hellenistic stuff about whether being unperturbed — Stoic apatheia, Epicurean ataraxia; I mean the actual ideals, not the modern 'apathy' sense — is a good to aim at, and at what cost. A friend of mine whose basically a Stoic keeps telling me the answer is obvious, but my problem is that every argument I read just picks one hard case (the parent who stays level while their kid is dying, the activist whose rage is the only thing keeping a movement alive, the person who's serene only because they've convinced themselves of something false) and then treats that one case as if it settles the whole question. Before I read another defense or takedown, I want the spec that a theory in this area would have to meet to count as adequate — assume the theory is for the practical question of whether a person should try to become less disturbed, and how much they should pay for it, not for exegesis.

Give me the desiderata. What does a theory of the value of freedom from disturbance have to get right: which cases it has to handle, which distinctions it can't collapse (e.g., feeling no disturbance vs. not being ruled by it; not being disturbed vs. not caring; tranquility vs. numbness or low affect), which facts about how emotions work it can't contradict, and what it has to say about the means — is equanimity achieved by self-deception or by a lucky temperament worth less than equanimity achieved through training? For each requirement, say why it's a requirement, i.e. what case or consideration would embarass a theory that ignored it. Also flag which requirements are negotiable and which aren't, and whether any two of them can't be jointly satisfied — if the list is internally inconsistent, that's a result, not a bug.

Then, briefly, score the main contenders against the list — Stoic apatheia, Epicurean ataraxia, and maybe a modern acceptance/second-order-emotion view. Keep that part short; the criteria are the point. A table is fine. And if a single requirement does most of the work in excluding candidates, tell me which one it is.
