I am not a professional historian, but I read a lot of popular history and history of science, and I have noticed a double standard that I cannot quite justify. When someone in the past got a piece of our current picture right before the evidence was solid, the books I read celebrate the person as brilliant and astonishingly modern. But when someone in the past was wrong by our lights, the same books almost always make excuses: the person was a product of the age, the necessary concepts did not exist yet, and it would be unfair to judge them by later standards. The dead, in other words, get both a bonus for agreeing with us and a discount for disagreeing with us.

The present gets neither treatment. When a thoughtful person today holds some currently mainstream belief that our own era will probably later reject — the kind of claim in nutrition, psychiatry, or economics that has been overturned repeatedly within living memory — we reach for neither story. We do not call the person ahead of their time, and we do not excuse them as a product of their time. We simply call the belief false and regard its holders as less careful than we are. We grade the past on a curve and the present against a fixed standard, and we do this without noticing that the two standards conflict.

Is there a real principle that justifies this asymmetry? For instance, is there good reason to think our present standards are more likely to be right than every earlier generation's standards appeared to them to be, so that we may judge contemporaries by those standards while excusing ancestors for not yet having reached them? Or is this just the flattering habit of writing the past as progress toward the people writing it, with ourselves as the final examiners? If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against this hard case: every previous generation I can inspect was confident in at least some consensus that later turned out to be wrong, often in matters they considered settled. What grounds could I have for believing that this time the curve should not apply to us?
