Longtime LW/ACX reader here. I want someone to make two things I believe fight and tell me which one actually breaks.

On one side, expected utility. I buy the line that if your preferences over gambles violate the axioms, you're either exploitable by a money pump or you have preferences you'd pay to have changed; and I can't find a principled cutoff below which a probability just stops counting. Kolmogorov doesn't hand you a threshold below which an event is too small to matter, and any specific number looks like it was chosen to avoid muggings rather than discovered.

On the other side, I can't find a ceiling on value. Twice as many happy lives seems twice as good, and I can keep applying that thought forever without ever reaching a largest number of lives beyond which one more adds zero. So my utilty function keeps coming out unbounded.

But those two together mean the mugger gets me: if utility is unbounded above, then for any act I'd otherwise choose, there's some pair (p, U) — tiny p, enormous U — whose product is bigger than the expected value of refusing, so refusing is always throwing away arbitrarily large EV. The standard patches all feel like they redefine one of the premises to make the problem go away: cap utility at some arbitrary B, impose a probability floor, or abandon the linearity of expected utility by discounting tiny probabilities. I also keep noticing an asymmetry: a probability floor and a utility cap do the same arithmetic work, but the first feels like an epistemic admission (we can't distinguish that p from zero) while the second feels like a moral one (there is a best possible world), and I'm not sure that difference is real.

So here's what I actually want, not a survey of the patches. Suppose we're trying to preserve as much of expected utility and as much of the no-ceiling-on-value intuition as possible. What would it take for the no-ceiling intuition to be the thing that goes — what evidence or argument should make me treat it as a mistaken extrapolation rather than a fact about value? And what would it take for maximize expected utility to be the thing that goes instead? Is there a version that keeps both by changing how tiny probabilities and huge utilities are combined, rather than capping either, and if so does it survive a second mugger who just names an even smaller p?
