I'm in a legal theory seminar and we've been stuck for weeks on the Fuller/Raz fight about the rule of law. Half the room basically thinks Fuller is right that law has an internal morality, because a system that systematically violates the desiderata—retroactive rules, secret rules, no congruence between announced rules and official action—isn't just bad law, it stops being law in the relevant sense. The other half keeps insisting, with Raz, that the rule of law is a negative virtue in the same way that a sharp knife is neutral with respect to what you use it for: it makes law good at whatever it is doing, whether that end is good or evil. The argument always ends with the Fuller people saying the Raz people are dodging the question of whether the rule of law is just a kind of efficiency, and the Raz people saying the Fuller people are trying to smuggle morality into a thin procedural concept. We're not asking you to settle who wins. We want the document a good-faith adversarial collabaration between the two camps would produce: a list of propositions both sides can sign about what the rule of law actually demands, the residual disagreement stated in terms each side would accept for its own position, and the crux or cruxes that would actually move at least one side. Please write exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion. Don't do a table; fold the disagreements into the five points.
