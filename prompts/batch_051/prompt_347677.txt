I'm making a handout for a social choice unit and I want it to double as a "which Arrow condition do you actually reject?" sorting quiz, to be handed out before students know the theorem or any of its jargon. Every question needs to be a concrete voting profile—three candidates, three or five voters with written rankings—and each question should force a judgment about the case (e.g., should the A-over-B social ranking change just because C got added to the ballot?) rather than asking them to recognize "IIA" or "unrestricted domain". Your quiz should sort students into these leaves: (1) a Borda/score-count view, which gives up independence of irrelevant alternatives; (2) a majority-rule-only-under-single-peakedness view, which gives up unrestricted domain; (3) a "cycles are fine, pick via the top cycle or some tournament tie-break" view, which gives up transitivity of the social ranking; and (4) a "someone has to be decisive" view, which gives up non-dictatorship. Include the scoring key and a few scenarios, and keep it to four or five questions. For each leaf, say exactly which condition(s) the view actually drops, and flag any leaf that can only be reached by quietly dropping a second condition too (my hunch is the tournament one might). Also tell me whether "gives up Pareto" is an empty branch, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unappealing, or just unexplored. And if any two leaves end up getting seperated by nothing in practice, say that rather than pretending the tree is clean.
