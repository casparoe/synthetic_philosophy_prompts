Our animal-care committee's charter has a sentence I keep coming back to: 'The Committee must approve any procedure that may cause pain, distress, or suffering in a research subject before it may proceed; in judging whether a subject can suffer, it shall consider the subject's behavior, physiology, and evolutionary relationships.' That was obviously written before anyone imagined machine learning. A proposal just landed that trains a reinforcement-learning agent on a task where every wrong move delivers a large negative reward, and the trained policy shows classic avoidance behavior: it slows down after error streaks, takes cautious detours, and it's performance drops for a while after a high-punishment episode. The proposer says that's fine because the negative reward is just a teaching signal, and anyway the agent has no physiology and no evolutionary relationships. But the behavior and the reward channel sure look like a minimal valence signal. Run me through the interpretation that follows the words, the interpretation that follows the purpose, what the drafters would probably have said if you'd asked them, and what a reasonable reader of 'research subject' would take the phrase to cover. Then tell me which one the committee should actually use — and if you think the question is really forcing us to settle what makes a signal into suffering in the first place, say that instead of pretending the clause decides it.
