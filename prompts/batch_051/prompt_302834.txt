I'm a retired planetary scientist, and I've spent the last couple of years reading around in political philosophy and economics, so you may need to correct my terms. I've been turning over the standard typology of goods. A good is rivalrous if one person's use subtracts from what's left for others, and excludable if you can feasibly keep people from using it. Private goods are both, public goods are neither, common-pool resources are rivalrous but non-excludable, and club goods are excludable but non-rivalrous. I keep being told this two-by-two is too coarse, but it seems like a fine place to start.

The case I want to test it against is the usable orbital environment in low Earth orbit — not the satellites, which are just private property, but the orbital shell from roughly 200 to 2,000 km considered as a shared resource. Historically it has been open access: if you could launch, you could put something there, and nobody could fence it off, though licensing and space-traffic management are starting to change that. On the rivalrousness side, one satelite doesn't use up its orbit the way a cow uses grass. But every object up there contributes to the debris that raises collision risk for everybody else, and there are congestion limits from radio interference and safe-approach distances. The thing being used up isn't exactly a stock you subtract from; it's more like a margin of safe separation that gets scarcer as the environment gets crowded, and if it gets dense enough you might get a runaway cascade of collisions.

So here's what I'd like. Go through the criteria one at a time and tell me whether the low-Earth orbital environment qualifies as a public good, a common-pool resource, a club good, a private good, or none of the above. Where the answer is ambiguous, say whether that's because the case is messy or because the two dimensions aren't capturing what matters for this kind of resource. Then tell me whether this counts as a tragedy of the commons in Hardin's sense, in Ostrom's sense, both, or neither — and what difference that makes to how we ought to govern orbit.
