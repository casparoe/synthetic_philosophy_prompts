I just got appointed to my town's ethics board, and our first case is one the gift rules clearly weren't drafted for. Our ordinance says: "No official shall accept any gift, gratuity, or other thing of value from any person with a matter pending before the official, where the thing is given because of the official's position, or where a reasonable person would infer it was given to influence official action." One of our councilmembers has a small Twitch channel—mostly talking about town politics and playing old RTS games—and a developer whose subdivision application is pending before a board the councilmember sits on tipped him $1,400 in Twitch bits and Streamlabs donations over the three weeks the application was under review. The councilmember read the developer's username aloud and thanked him each time, same as any donor. Is that a "thing of value" given "because of the official's position," or is it just income from a side gig, like tipping a street performer? Walk me through what the text, the purpose, what the drafters would have said if you'd asked them in 1991, and a ordinary viewer's reading would each give, and tell me where they come apart. One colleague says this is just a 21st-century wedding envelope and that our gift ban is a Western hangup anyway; is she right that we'd be imposing a parochial norm if we find he violated it?
