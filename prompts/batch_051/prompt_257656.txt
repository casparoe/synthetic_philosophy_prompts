I got roped into giving the opening remarks at a two-day workshop on misinformation and content moderation, and I could use a hand. The audience is a weird mix: maybe 60 people, about half academic epistemologists and the other half platform trust-and-safety staff and volunteer subreddit mods who have never read philosophy. The remarks need to be short — 450 words maximum, we're allready over schedule. I'd like the speech to be built around the difference between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber: roughly, a bubble is about information not reaching you, while an echo chamber is about having been taught to distrust whatever comes from outside, so the fix isn't just adding better information.

What I keep worrying about is that most of the non-academics in the room think these are just two fancy names for people not hearing the other side. So the speech has to land for them while staying precise enough that the epistemologists don't wince. And please don't let it drift into a generic listen-to-each-other message; the whole point I want the structure to reflect is that echo chambers aren't fixed by simply exposing people to better sources, and the speech shouldn't end up contradicting itself on that.
