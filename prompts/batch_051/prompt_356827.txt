She says a thermostat can't suffer, even though it has an error signal it's always trying to minimize. She says a current LLM fine-tuned with RLHF isn't suffering when the reward model whacks it for dispreferred outputs, because there's no agent there at all. But she says a dog in acute fear is suffering, obviously, a human in chronic pain is suffering, and — this is the one that throws me — a model-based RL agent with a rich predictive world model, an explicit self-model (it represents its own body and internal states), and a negative reward for damage-like events is suffering in at least a minimal way. It can't be the naive "reward signal is literally valence" rule, because then the thermostat and the DQN would count too, and she says they don't.

The new case I want to predict her on is a model-based RL agent with the same rich predictive world model and the same negative-damage reward, but no self-model at all: it represents obstacles, collisions, and outcomes as features of the external world map, never as states of itself. Would she say that one suffers?
