Im a retired population geneticist who's only recently started reading philosophy of biology, and its been a little alarming to see how much people stretch heritability beyond what I was trained to mean. Could you rank the following six one-line glosses from most to least defensible, and give me a sentence each on where it goes right or wrong? Dont worry about loading it with technical genetics, I'm after the conceptual structure.

1. Heritability is the proportion of variation in a trait that is caused by genes rather than by the environment.
2. Heritability means the proportion of variation attributable specifically to additive genetic variance, the part that predicts response to selection.
3. If a trait is highly heritable within each of two groups, then a difference in average trait value between the groups is probably genetic in origin too.
4. If a trait has high heritability within a population, then changing the environment will do little to move the population average.
5. A heritability estimate is local: it holds for the particular ranges of environments and genotypes in the sample and does not automatically transfer to other settings.
6. Heritability is a population-level statistic and says nothing about how much of any single individual's trait came from their genes.
