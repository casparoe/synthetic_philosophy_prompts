I keep going in circles on the Darwinian-to-Gregorian ladder and I want someone to force me to actually adjudicate it. The theory as I understand it says there are four kinds of minds, each one adding a different place where selection does the work. Darwinian creatures get their competences fixed by natural selection across generations, outside the creature. Skinnerian creatures add reinforcement and extinction of actual behaviors within one lifetime, so the environment selects among things the creature has already done. Popperian creatures add an inner environment where candidate acts can be tried out and killed off before they are performed, so the mistake happens to a representation instead of to the creature. Gregorian creatures add mind-tools, above all words, which let a creature import competences that others have already tested and let candidate moves be composed, taken apart, and criticized in ways a wordless inner environment can't manage. The deep appeal is that this is one continuous Darwinian process that just moves selection further inside: no inner agent, no break in nature, and real differences between kinds of minds explained as differences in where and how selection operates.

But here is the intuition I can't shake. When I deliberate about whether an argument is sound, it does not feel like a population of candidate judgments is competing and the fittest one wins. It seems like I am answerable to the reasons themselves, including reasons I only just formulated, and that what settles the question is what those reasons support, not what would survive some selection regime. There is a difference, I want to say, between an option's being outcompeted and its being wrong. So which is it: does the ladder story actually contradict that intuition, or only a loose way of talking about candidates being selected? If they do conflict, what should give way, and what would justify each possible move, whether debunking the intuition, amending the theory, or finding a reading on which they are compatible? I do not want a tie; I want a verdict, but a verdict that says what would warrant going one way rather than another.
