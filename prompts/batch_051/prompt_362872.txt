A friend has been trying to get me to sign up for cryonics, and the more I read off the provider's website the more I can't get past something. They offer two plans. Whole-body is one price, and then there's a cheaper "neuro" plan where they only store your head (or just the brain), on the theory that by the time revival is possible the technology will be able to reconstruct your body or run your mind on a computer. The sales language says the body is basically replaceable hardware and the self is the information in the brain. But the same people call the preserved bodies "patients," say they aren't dead but just paused, and insist the whole point is life extension: that it will be you who wakes up, not some copy who merely has your memories. That feels like a tension to me. Deciding the body can be thrown away seems to concede that the body was never you, while talking about one continuous person being suspended and repaired seems to rely on a different idea entirely. It looks like the whole-body plan is the hedged bet, and the neuro plan is the one with the philosophy hanging out, but the company uses the same "this is still you" language for both.

I don't care whether cryonics will work technically; I'm asking after the philosophy the practice is committed to. What theory of personal identity does the practice presuppose — what must a person be, and what must survival consist in, for the head-only option to count as the continued existence of the sign-up rather than the creation of a new person who shares the original's memories? Reconstruct the implicit theory as you see it, but then tell me how much of the practice actually depends on that theory: is the practice committed to one particular view of identity, or is it compatible with several and the website just happens to favour one? Where is it philosophically underdetermined? And it'd help if you'd say what a cryonics organisation would have to change in how it describes and sells the neuro option if a rival view of identity won out — say, that a person is the living biological organism and its ongoing metabolic continuity, or that the specific substrate matters so a simulation of you is a different person. Please do this as continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
