I hold the position that the right response to the Liar paradox is a truth-value gap theory: the sentence "This sentence is false" is neither true nor false, and truth in a language that contains its own truth predicate is best modeled by a Kripke-style construction in which the truth predicate is partial and ungrounded sentences simply fail to receive a truth value. On this view the Liar does not show that English is inconsistent; it marks a point at which the truth predicate is undefined.

I do not want a defense of this position. I want a map of its exposure — what would have to be true, or be successfully argued, for it to be false or fatally compromised. Please give me:

1. The decisive defeaters: the observations, arguments, or formal results that, if they could be established, would show the gap view to be wrong, incoherent, or more costly than any reasonable version of it could absorb. For each, say exactly what it would establish and how the gap theorist would have to respond.

2. The merely awkward results: costs the position's own defenders should concede count against it, together with the standard moves by which they contain the damage. I want the concessions a sophisticated gap theorist would actually make, not only what its critics assert.

3. Your own assessment of the single most forceful objection, and whether it is decisive or only serious.

4. A verdict on insulation: if the view retreats to treating the fixed-point construction as only a mathematical model of truth rather than a semantic account of the word "true" in natural language, how much protection does that retreat buy, and is it legitimate protection for a theory of this kind or a change of subject that keeps the view alive by draining it of content?

Throughout, address the strengthened/revenge Liar ("This sentence is not true") and at least one non-self-referential variant — for example, an infinite sequence of sentences in which each says that every later sentence is false — because I suspect the view's fate will be decided by how well it handles cases the simple Liar does not put at the center.
