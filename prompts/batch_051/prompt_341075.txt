I want a working definition of 'greed' that classifies the following five cases, and that draws a line somewhere rather than treating the word as hopelessly context-bound. The cases are:

(1) a farmer who buys the neighbouring field whenever it comes up for sale, though he already works more land than he can manage, and who admits he wants the largest holding in the county;
(2) a salaried employee who accepts an unsolicited pay rise she does not need, while the firm's cleaners are paid so little that they rely on food banks;
(3) someone whose chief pleasure is watching his account balance grow, who lives frugally, gives nothing away, and intends never to spend more than a fraction of what he accumulates;
(4) a trader who systematically exploits small mispricings across markets, enriching himself while also narrowing spreads and adding liquidity;
(5) a person of modest means who wants nothing more for himself, yet burns with resentment whenever a neighbour prospers.

I am confident that (1) and (3) are greedy and that (5) is not greed but something else—envy or covetousness—and I have no settled view about (2) and (4). Propose the definition, run it through all five cases, and tell me which of the resulting verdicts you would be most reluctant to defend. I also think 'greed' and 'acquisitiveness' may not be the same thing; say briefly whether your definition keeps them apart, or reduces greed to acquisitiveness with some missing restraint. Finally, say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
