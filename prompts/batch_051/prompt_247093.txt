so i've got a habit i'm trying to figure out whether to trust. i'm in intellectual history, and my default way of reading past thinkers is to treat us as the destination: locke is "getting closer" to our idea of toleration, alchemists are "not yet" chemists, and the weird stuff they believed is just friction on the road to now. i know this is whig history and everyone rolls their eyes at it, so i built a correction. whenever i catch myself doing it, i force myself to rewrite the same story with some past moment as the endpoint and us as the deviation from it, and i only let myself call a change progress if i can state the gain in terms the earlier people themselves could have recognized as an improvement by their own lights. my question is whether this correction is actually a defensible epistemic policy or just a fancier way of being whiggish in reverse. how would i tell from the inside whether it's tracking something real rather than just cosplaying neutrality? and is there a version of the present-as-standard habit that's worth keeping, since in some cases — germ theory over miasma, say — the present really does seem to just know better?
