I've been trying to pin down why mutation is hard to reason about, and I keep coming back to a parallel with the metaphysics of persistence that I can't decide about.

Here is the programming side as I understand it. With immutable values and side-effect-free expressions, evaluation is compositional: the meaning of a whole is determined by the meanings of its parts, and a value with a given content is interchangeable with any other value of the same content. With mutable objects and aliasing, this breaks. Two names can refer to the same object, so a change made through one name is visible through the other; what an expression denotes can depend on when it is evaluated as well as what it says; and an object's identity is not settled by its current contents, since two objects can have identical state now and different state later. The identity of a mutable object persists through changes of its state, and that is exactly what makes updates hard to track locally.

The philosophical side, as I understand it, distinguishes two ways of thinking about how things persist. On a temporal-parts view, a persisting thing is a whole made up of instantaneous stages; change is difference between earlier and later stages rather than one and the same thing's being different. On an endurantist view, one and the same whole is present at each moment, genuinely identical across change, and this immediately raises the problem of temporary intrinsics: if the very same object is F at one time and not-F at another, something has to give.

The parallel I keep seeing is this: immutable values are to temporal parts as mutable objects are to enduring substances. Referential transparency looks like the compositional reasoning you get when there are no numerically identical things undergoing change, and the costs of mutation — aliasing, order dependence, the failure of substitution — look like the metaphysical costs of endurantism.

I want to know whether this is a genuine structural analogy or an artifact of the shared word "change." Please check the mapping term by term, tell me where it aligns and where it breaks, and say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer. In particular, does anything from the programming side actually transfer to the metaphysics of persistence, or does the metaphor silently import the idea of a global store and discrete update steps that metaphysics doesn't have? And name at least one thing the parallel seems to predict that is false on one side or the other.
