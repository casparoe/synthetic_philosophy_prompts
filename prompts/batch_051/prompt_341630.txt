I have been trying to reconstruct the hole argument from Einstein’s The Foundation of the General Theory of Relativity (1916), especially the passage where he argues that two different coordinate systems agreeing outside a region H but differing inside H would lead to different functions for the field inside H while still satisfying the same field equations and the same boundary conditions. As I understand it, the argument can be set out like this:

(1) The field equations of general relativity are generally covariant, so any solution can be transformed by a coordinate transformation to produce another solution.  
(2) Take a coordinate transformation that is the identity outside an interior region H and differs from the identity inside H, and apply it to a solution of the field equations.  
(3) The original solution and the transformed solution agree everywhere outside H and on the boundary of H, but assign different values to the metric field at the spacetime points inside H.  
(4) If spacetime points are real substances whose identities are independent of the metric field assigned to them, then the original and transformed solutions describe two distinct possible worlds that share the same boundary conditions.  
(5) A deterministic theory requires that boundary conditions plus the field equations uniquely determine the field throughout the region.  
(6) Therefore, if substantivalism is true, general covariance entails a failure of determinism.  
(7) But the unwanted indeterminism is an artifact of the coordinate description; so either general covariance must be restricted, or substantivalism must be rejected.

Is this argument valid as stated? If not, which step fails, and is the failure an equivocation about what a spacetime point is, a suppressed premise about how mathematical solutions are individuated, or something else? And whichever diagnosis you give, what does it show about whether the hole argument really refutes substantivalism rather than merely forcing a choice between different ways of talking about the world?
