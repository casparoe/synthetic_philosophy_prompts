Got into a long argument last night with a friend about a policy I actually follow in one-shot prisoner's dilemmas, and now I can't tell if it's a reasonable way of thinking or just magical thinking with extra steps. My policy: in a genuine one-shot PD with someone I take to be roughly as rational as me and similiar in how they reason, I cooperate. Not because I'm nice, and not because I think my choice causally influences theirs. The reason is that the situation is symmetric, and if the reasoning I'm doing now tells me to cooperate, then someone who reasons like me should land on the same answer, so I should act as if my choice carries information about — or somehow stands in for — theirs. I've seen this called superrationality. My friend called it magical thinking: defection dominates no matter what the other person does, a one-shot game has no reputation or tit-for-tat, and a vague sense that the other guy is 'like me' doesn't rewrite the payoff matrix. He also said the really honest version of my policy only works if the other person runs the exact same code as me, which almost never happens. I see his point but it still feels right to cooperate. Is this habit defensable? What exactly has to be true — about the other person, about how similar we actually are, about whether my reasoning generalizes to them — for it to beat just always defecting? And is there a cleaned-up version of the policy that keeps whatever's good in it without pretending my choice reaches across the room?
