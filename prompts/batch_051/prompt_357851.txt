So I became a dad about eight months ago, and ever since then I havn't been able to stop thinking about the doomsday argument. It used to feel like a fun parlor puzzle; now it feels like it's about my daughter's actual future, which is a bit unhinged but here we are.

Here's the thing. I cant settle between the two main ways people say you should reason about your own position among all the observers who will ever live. The self-sampling assumption (SSA) says you should treat yourself as a random draw from your reference class — roughly, all humans — so the fact that I was born in 1988 rather than in 2200 is evidence about how many humans there will be in total. The self-indication assumption (SIA) says the opposite direction: the mere fact that I exist at all is evidence in favour of worlds with more observers, because those worlds contain more possible people like me. These two pull in totally different directions. The so-called presumptuous philosopher argument is meant to show SIA leads to absurd overconfidence about vast numbers of unseen observers, while the doomsday argument is meant to show SSA implies our species is probably nearer its end than its middle.

I want a number, not 'it depends'. What is your credence that SSA is closer to correct than SIA? Decompose it for me. I can see at least these sub-questions: (a) whether self-locating evidence is a real, separate kind of evidence at all, or just ordinary evidence wearing a costume; (b) how damaging the presumptuous philosopher thought experiment actually is to SIA, versus it just showing that our prior over the number of observers was irrational; (c) how strongly the doomsday argument actually follows from SSA, given the reference class problem (why 'humans' and not 'conscious beings with my exact memories', or 'members of my species at this technological stage'?); (d) whether the two assumptions even diverge much in practice, or mostly in exotic thought experiments. Put rough probabilities on each, show how they combine, and tell me which one you're least confident in and what evidence would move it. Also, maybe I'm wrong about where the disagreement between me (roughly 0.6 on SSA) and my friend (roughly 0.15 on SSA) actually lives — say where you think it lives.
