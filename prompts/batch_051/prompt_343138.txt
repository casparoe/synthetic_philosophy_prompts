I recently joined a team that decides what safeguards a general-purpose AI model must include before release, and I have been going back to the older argument in the philosophy of technology that some artifacts have politics built into them. As I understand that argument, it was aimed against the claim that a technology is politically neutral until someone puts it to use: bridges too low for buses, machines that are economical only at industrial scale, and energy systems whose safe operation demands centralized and hierarchical control all show that at least some artifacts are inherently political, in the sense that adopting them at all commits or strongly pushes a society toward a particular arrangement of power. Foundation models now look like a challenge to the strong form of that claim. The same base model can be fine-tuned into a customer-service assistant, a literacy tutor, or a state propaganda instrument, and nothing in the artifact's own operation seems to settle which of these it becomes. Does the argument survive this development? Which premise is actually under pressure, and is there a repaired version that still says something important about the politics of general-purpose AI? I am more interested in what happens to the argument itself than in what we should do.
