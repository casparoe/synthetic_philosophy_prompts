In On Liberty, Chapter III, Mill considers someone who lets the world, or his or her own segment of it, decide his or her plan of life, and says that such a person requires no faculty beyond the power of imitation. I am currently taking a course on paternalism and nudging, and I want to use that passage as a test for evaluating contemporary justifications of noncoercive nudges. Please rank the following five justifications for a nudge from the one most consistent with Mill's idea of choosing one's own plan of life to the one that most clearly replaces the person's choice with someone else's: (1) counteracting a person's present bias; (2) correcting a factual misapprehension; (3) using a cognitive heuristic to steer behavior in a direction the person has not chosen; (4) substituting expert judgment because the person lacks time or competence; (5) offsetting social pressures that already shape choice. Return exactly five numbered entries, ordered from most compatible to most objectionable, each naming the justification and giving a one-sentence reason for its place in the ranking. No introduction or conclusion.
