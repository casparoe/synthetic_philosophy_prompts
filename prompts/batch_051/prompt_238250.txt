I keep coming across arguments about whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real features of the past or later inventions — useful textbook conveniences at best, retrospective stories projected backward at worst — and I have noticed that almost every participant assumes an answer rather than defending one. Before I read another page on the subject, I want the standard of evaluation written down, because I cannot currently say what would settle the question.

Write the desiderata for an adequate account of historical periodization: what a scheme for dividing the past into named periods must be able to do before I should trust its verdict that one of those labels picks out a real pattern rather than imposing one. Fix the purpose as an account good enough to adjudicate these three labels in a serious non-specialist seminar, not one intended to guide original archival work. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one camp, and mark which requirements are negotiable and which are not. The account will presumably have to take a stand on continuity versus rupture, on the spatial and social scale at which a label must hold, on whether a period has to be uniform across art, science, politics, and religion, on how uneven survival of the sources should be controlled for, and on what would count as evidence that a received label is wrong; do not stop at those, but develop and defend the full list. I especially want to know which pairs of requirements may be jointly unsatisfiable — for example, a demand fine-grained enough to register continuity across the supposed break around 1500 may sit uneasily with the demand that periods be distinguishable at all. Then, briefly, score the three broad positions — the labels are real, the labels are merely conventions, and the labels are artifacts of a Whig or teleological story about the rise of modernity — against the list. Keep that scoring short; the criteria are what I am after.
