I am a longtime reader of LessWrong, the Alignment Forum, and related rationalist spaces, and I am putting together a research agenda memo on scalable oversight. I keep returning to one sentence in the abstract of Geoffrey Irving, Paul Christiano, and Dario Amodei's 2018 paper "AI safety via debate":

"In an analogy to complexity theory, debate with optimal play can answer any question in PSPACE given polynomial time judges (direct judging answers only NP questions)."

My question is not whether this claim is technically true—I accept the complexity analogy. I want to know what work this result is actually doing for the alignment problem. What are the main open questions about whether the PSPACE result transfers to the real setting of human judges, learned debaters, and natural language arguments, and which two or three of those questions would you prioritize for a small research team with, say, two years and a mix of ML and theoretical backgrounds? Please defend the ordering with explicit criteria such as importance, tractability, neglectedness, and how many other questions depend on this one. I also want to know which lines of work in the debate literature you think are overrated or do not follow from the PSPACE analogy at all, and why. I am not looking for a literature review or citations; I know the standard papers.
