I'm a commissioning editor at a study-Bible commentary series. About once a week I get a proposal or draft chapter claiming some number in scripture is symbolic — the 153 fish, forty days, sevens, the fourteen generations — and I have to green-light it or send it back. I've got half an hour per piece, I'm not a specialist in ancient numerology, and authors take rejections personaly. My half-formed principle is that a number is a sign when the text does something with it, not when I can do arithmatic with it. I need that turned into an actual reusable procedure. It has to respect a distinction I keep bumping into: numbers that are structural (repeated, organizing the text, flagged by the author) versus numbers that are merely counted or happen to fall out of some operations. It also has to handle gematria/isopsephy (the letter–number schemes), patterns that only work in translation, and the base-rate worry that with enough operations any number can be made to look meaningful.

You're a professor of biblical hermeneutics who also knows the cognitive-bias literature on pattern-hunting. Start by brainstorming the ways a rushed editor goes wrong, then write the procedure.

Give me the response in these named sections, in this order:

1. The principle, compressed — 2 or 3 sentences.
2. The procedure — ordered tests I can actually run in half an hour, with what to do at each step, about 300 words.
3. Worked examples — apply it to the 153 fish in John 21, to the forty days/years convention, and to one further case of your choosing where the procedure rejects something clever that I'd probably want to publish; say which one that is and why. About 250 words.
4. Hard cases and judgment calls — the places the procedure can't decide by itself, flagged as such. About 150 words.
5. What this can't do — including the legitimate symbolic readings the procedure will wrongly kill. About 100 words.

No bibliography, no footnotes, keep it usable.
