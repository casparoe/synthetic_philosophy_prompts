I've been collecting decisions from a friend, Maya, whose views on promising are more considered than mine, in the hope of eventually extracting the rule she's really running. So far her record includes these. She says a promise made under a threat is simply void — her example was a promise made after someone threatened to expose an embarrassing secret. She says any promise dissolves the moment the promisee releases the promisor, even with a casual "you're off the hook." She says the promisor is firmly bound once the promisee has reasonably relied, even if performance has become inconvenient; her example was a colleague who turned down another offer after being promised comments on a draft. And she says that if the promisee has completely forgotten the promise and no longer has any interest in whether it is kept, the obligation lapses by itself, with no explicit release required.

I now want to know what she'd say about a case of mine. I promised a neighbor I'd read his application essay and send comments next week. He replied that he'd be glad if I did, but he didn't plan to count on it: he'd also ask someone else, and if I never got around to it there would be no hard feelings. He has since made clear that he still very much wants my comments; he simply refused to depend on them. If I could easily keep the promise but mildly prefer not to, would Maya say the promise still obligates me? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the rule she seems to be running — something about reliance, the promisee's continuing interest or assurance, or a voluntary undertaking. Which of her earlier decisions is the closest analogue, what would each candidate rule predict about my case, which rule best fits her record and what does it require here, and what further case would cleanly separate the live candidates?
