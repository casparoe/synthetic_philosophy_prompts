Been keeping a list of objections to the fine-tuning argument — the argument that the life-permitting values of the physical constants are better explained by a designer than by chance — mostly from old LessWrong and Overcoming Bias threads plus a couple philosophy papers. My cached list, which I think covers the standard moves:

1. The anthropic objection: we can only ever observe a universe that permits observers, so observing life-permitting constants is exactly what any possible observer was guaranteed to see, and there is no surprise left to explain.
2. The multiverse objection: if there are enough universes with different constants, at least one wins the lottery, and we necessarily find ourselves in the winner.
3. The no-measure objection: there is no well-defined uniform probability over an unbounded range of possible constant values, and no parameterization-independent sense in which the life-permitting subset is small, so the argument's premise that the constants are improbable is undefined.
4. The constants-aren't-free objection: the constants may be fixed by a deeper law, in which case they could not have been otherwise and there is no tuning to explain.
5. The carbon-chauvinism objection: we judge the life-permitting range by life-as-we-know-it; other self-reproducing or conscious structures might be compatible with quite different constants, so the range may not actually be narrow.
6. The theism-doesn't-predict-it objection: an omnipotent designer could make life under any constants, so theism does not make life-permitting constants more likely, and the likelihood comparison never gets off the ground.
7. The who-designed-the-designer objection: positing a designer to explain the constants just relocates the complexity, and the designer's ability to choose constants is itself unexplained and at least as much in need of explanation.
8. The post-hoc-target objection: any actual set of constants could be made to look special after the fact by drawing the bullseye around wherever the arrow landed.
9. The ad-hocness objection: the designer is introduced solely to save this one phenomenon, with no independent evidence or constraints, and a hypothesis that flexible has no confirmatory value.

I have a nagging feeling there is a real hole in this list — an objection that is not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of these nine, and that would actually move someone who knows the area. Give me one more objection to the fine-tuning argument that is not on the list and is not a variant of anything on it. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good: does it actually damage the argument, or is it just a dialectical annoyance?

One request about presentation: please seperate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this. In particular, do not present the claim that the fine-tuning argument fails as consensus — plenty of smart people think the design explanation is fine — and flag where your new objection depends on a contested premise (about measures, about what theism predicts, etc.).
