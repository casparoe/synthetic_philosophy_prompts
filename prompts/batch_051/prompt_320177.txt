I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm preparing a recorded interview for a series on games, rules, and the foundations of legal systems. My guest is a legal theorist whose work argues that self-amending rule systems — Peter Suber's game Nomic, in which changing the rules is itself a legal move and the initial ruleset marks some provisions as mutable and others as immutable — show that a legal order does not need a fixed constitutional foundation lying wholly outside its own rules. On her view, a system can bootstrap its own validity through its amendment procedures, and the apparent paradoxes of legal self-reference mark transitions in how we should understand authority rather than contradictions in the system. Her standard move is to reply to self-reference puzzles by saying that the objection conflates levels and times. I want a sequence of questions that makes that deflection progressively harder to use.

Please write me a set of interview questions that press on the actual mechanics of self-amendment rather than staying at the level of generalities. She tends to dismiss the traditional objection — that an amendment clause cannot amend itself — as a confusion between the rule and the act performed under it, and she treats a system changing its own amendment rule as an ordinary diachronic change. The questioning should push beyond that: what happens when a system uses its amendment procedure to change what counts as a valid amendment, or to repeal the very rule empowering further amendments, or when two provisions each authorize the repeal of the other? The sequence should also connect the formal puzzles to actual constitutional practice — eternity clauses, judicial review of amendments, and the sense that some limits on self-amendment are not themselves merely certified by the written rules. End with a question about what real-world practice, experiment, or formal result would count against the view.

Use these sections, in this order, at roughly the lengths indicated:

- "Framing" (2 questions): one that forces the guest to state precisely what does the dissolving work in her account, and one that asks what is preserved when a rule changes the rule governing its own change.

- "Circular and mutual self-reference" (2 questions): questions about self-repealing rules and about pairs of rules that refer to each other's conditions of validity or repeal.
