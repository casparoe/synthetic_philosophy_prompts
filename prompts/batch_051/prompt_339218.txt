After a career in physical chemistry, I have turned in retirement to moral philosophy, and the position that has most impressed me is prioritarianism: roughly, the view that a given benefit matters more the lower the absolute level of well-being of the person who receives it, while relative inequality has no intrinsic value. I am drawn to it chiefly because of how it handles the leveling-down objection. An egalitarian who regards equality itself as good seems forced to say that making the better off worse off while helping no one is, in one respect, an improvement; the prioritarian can deny that anything good has happened, because no one's absolute level of well-being has risen.

Treat prioritarianism as the position under examination and give me a map of what would count against it — that is, what would have to be true for it to be false, or at least much less attractive, as an account of distributive ethics. I want this map rather than a balanced survey of the debate.
