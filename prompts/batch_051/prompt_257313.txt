Here's a passage from the preface of Nietzsche's On the Use and Abuse of History for Life: "We would serve history only so far as it serves life; but to value its study beyond a certain point mutilates and degrades life." I want you to take the wave of Confederate monument removals in the US after 2015—Charleston, Charlottesville, New Orleans, the lot—and analyze it through that line and the tripartite scheme he lays out later: monumental, antiquarian, and critical history. I know the political history; what I want is the philosophical mapping. Who in that episode is doing which kind of history? The people pulling statues down look like critical historians, sure, but is that too quick? Do the defenders count as antiquarian or monumental, and does the distinction even hold for them? And where do the proposals to move the monuments into museums fit? More importantly, use the criterion from the passage to assess the whole affair: does the removal serve life, or does it become its own excess of historical feeling, just inverted? Then say where the tripartite scheme breaks or goes silent about what was actually going on, and whether the episode confirms or complicates his claim that history should serve life.
