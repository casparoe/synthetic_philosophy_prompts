A friend and I argued until late last night about whether a general-purpose agent can be made to avoid side effects without the designer's specifying what its user actually values. His position was that no full account of value is needed: one fixes a baseline — the state the world would have been in had the agent never acted — and forbids any deviation from that baseline beyond what the instruction itself forces. I countered that the real difficulty is not identifying the baseline but saying what counts as a deviation from it, because any way of measuring deviation already embodies a decision about which differences matter. I have since been turning over a clause from a draft deployment agreement that seems to express his view:

"3.1 Minimal-impact obligation. In executing an instruction, the Assistant shall bring about a state of the environment that differs as little as possible from the state that would have obtained had the Assistant not acted, while still satisfying the instruction. Any difference beyond that minimum is a side effect and is prohibited, without regard to its expected desirability."

Consider a case the drafters did not anticipate. The user says to the assistant: "Take the trash out of the kitchen." The instruction does not specify where. The assistant can place the bag in the covered outdoor bin at the end of the path, where the collection crew expects to find it, or it can set the bag in the garage five feet from the kitchen door. If closeness is measured by physical displacement and the number of objects disturbed, the garage outcome is far closer to the no-action baseline. The assistant, applying the clause as written, chooses the garage. The user is angry: the garage will smell, pests may find the bag, and the trash will miss collection.

What does the clause actually require in this case? Work through the textual reading, the reading a reasonable user of the agreement would take, the reading suggested by the purpose of the minimal-impact obligation, and what the drafters would most plausibly say if asked. Then say which of these approaches should control, and make explicit what your answer implies about whether a rule of this form can define "side effect" without presupposing at least a partial specification of value.
