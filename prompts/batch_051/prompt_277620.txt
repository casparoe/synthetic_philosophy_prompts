I'm trying to work out what I actually think about the bit in Phenomenology of Perception where Merleau-Ponty talks about the blind man's stick, and about the woman with a feather in her hat who keeps it clear of obstacles without calculating anything. He wants to say the body has it's own way of intending the world, and that this is neither a representation in the head nor a chain of reflexes. I like that idea, but when I go case by case my verdicts pull me in a few directions.

Here are the cases I keep going back and forth on. A touch typist whose keys have been rearranged can't tell you where the letters are, but their fingers can still type familiar words: I think that shows the knowledge is in the body, not in a mental map. A footballer who says they didn't decide to pass, their foot just did it: I think they're reporting something real, not just being colourful. A blind person who taps with a cane feels the kerb at the end of the cane, not in their hand: I find that convincing, and it makes me think the body schema really is plastic. So far so Merleau-Ponty.

But these cases trouble me. A pianist who knows a piece can transpose it on request, which seems to require a structural representation, not just a motor habit. A driver who has only ever driven a car adjusts to a van within minutes, which doesn't look like a body schema fixed around the car's boundaries; it looks like a general spatial model. And a phantom limb patient who tries to walk on a leg that isn't there: I'd say that's a memory or expectation in the brain, not evidence of an embodied intentionality that survives the body. I also think a tennis player's backhand is just automatic, like a reflex, so calling it intentional at all feels overblown.

Is there one principle about the lived body that gets all or most of my verdicts, and if not, which verdicts should I drop? What would that principle say about a case I haven't listed: someone using a long-handled rake who can feel the leaves at the end, but can't tell you the angle of the handle without looking?
