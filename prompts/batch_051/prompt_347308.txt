Question: Does Thomson's lamp describe a coherent but incomplete situation, or an incoherent one? Specifically, does the lamp show that the concept of a completed supertask is impossible? Justify your answer.

Student answer: Thomson's lamp is a lamp programmed to be switched at t = 1/2, then t = 3/4, then t = 7/8, and in general at every time of the form 1 - 1/2^n for n greater than or equal to 1. Since these times have 1 as their limit, all infinitely many operations have been completed by t = 1. If the lamp starts on at t = 0 and each operation flips its state, then before t = 1 its state is on at 0, off at 1/2, on at 3/4, off at 7/8, and so on without approaching any limit. To determine the state at t = 1 one would need to take the limit of this sequence, but the sequence has no limit, so the lamp's state at t = 1 is undefined. Yet an actual lamp must occupy exactly one physical state at every time. The description has specified the complete causal history of the lamp, and that history fails to fix the lamp's state at t = 1. Therefore the situation is contradictory rather than merely underdescribed, and the completion of a supertask is impossible.
