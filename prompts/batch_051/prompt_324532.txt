I just spent a long dinner argueing with a friend about whether God can suffer. He's a passibilist, at least on his better days; he thinks the biblical picture of God grieving, repenting, and dying on a cross makes no sense unless God can genuinely suffer, and that a God who cannot be hurt cannot really love us. I kept pushing the classical impassibilist line back at him: God cannot be acted on by anything outside himself because he is the source of everything; suffering happens to a body and to a subject that gets moved by another; and saying God suffers is either metaphor or it drags God down into creation. We kept going in circles because we could not agree on what would count as a change in God, what suffering even requires, or what the Incarnation is supposed to settle, and a lot of the time it felt like we were argueing past each other about the meanings of emotion and vulnerability.

Here's what I want you to do: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a serious passibilist and a serious impassibilist on the question of whether God can suffer. The document should have a numbered section of claims both sides could sign, covering the conceptual and definitional territory, what each side is and is not committed to, the biblical and theological data, and what the Incarnation does and does not imply. Then include a disagreements table. For each row: the precise point of disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view; whether the disagreement is substantive or, at bottom, verbal, with the verbal ones separated out; a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on that crux (the crucifixion, the exodus, the death of a child, or whatever else is fair); and each side's prediction about what that case shows, written down in advance. I suspect the real cruxes include whether love requires vulnerability, whether an immutable being can have anything worth calling emotion, whether Christ's suffering belongs to the divine nature or only to the assumed human nature, and whether God's perfect knowledge of our pain plus perfect action can do all the moral work without God's feeling the pain. Add whatever cruxes you think are doing the actual work, and don't pad the agreed claims with things so weak they say nothing.
