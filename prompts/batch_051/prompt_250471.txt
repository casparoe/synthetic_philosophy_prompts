I serve on the diaconate of a small church, and we have reached an impasse over how to apply a passage from the Epistle of James to a recent situation in the congregation. The passage is James 2:14–17 (King James Version):

What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him? If a brother or sister be naked, and destitute of daily food, And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit? Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone.

The situation: A member of the congregation, E., is doctrinally careful, rarely absent from worship, and gives a fixed percentage of his income to an overseas relief agency and to the church's general fund. Last month a family in the congregation lost their apartment to a fire. E. posted a widely shared message in the church's online group urging the family to trust God's provision, and he linked to the family's crowdfunding page, but he did not visit them, bring a meal, or offer the spare room that he and his wife have occasionally mentioned, though they live ten minutes away. When a deacon asked him about this privately, E. replied that hands-on relief is the deacons' task, that his regular giving already funds the church's mercy ministry, and that Paul's teaching that a person is justified by faith rather than by performing the law's works means his standing with God does not depend on whether he opens a spare room.

We are not asking for a full treatise; we need a structured decision aid for the board. Please interpret James 2:14–17 in relation to this situation and present the answer as a table with exactly these columns:

- "Interpretive approach": one of the following five, given as the rows: the plain wording read in first-century terms; the author's likely purpose in the argument of the letter; what the author would most plausibly say if asked about this case; what a reasonable church reader today would take the passage to require; and the reading most coherent with Paul's letters.
- "What faith and works pick out": what the passage's key terms designate under that approach.
- "Verdict on E.": whether E.'s post, donations, and refusal of direct aid fall under the pattern James condemns, and why.
- "Strongest objection": the most serious limitation of deciding a pastoral or disciplinary matter by this approach alone.
- "Weight (1–5)": how decisive this approach should be for the board, with 5 meaning load-bearing and 1 meaning informative only.

After the table, add a short separate paragraph stating which single approach should control the board's position and what that position is.
