I am a novelist rather than a philosopher, and the old example of the digger wasp has been troubling me precisely because it reads like something I might have invented. As I understand it, the wasp paralyses a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it there, and goes inside alone to inspect the nest; then she comes out and hauls the cricket in. If a meddling observer moves the cricket a few inches while she is inside, she does not simply retrieve it. She re-enacts the whole ritual, and can be made to do so again and again. The customary moral is that the wasp is sphexish: an exquisitely adapted but rigid mechanism, executing a fixed programme without noticing that the world has changed, and that this is exactly the kind of absence of flexibility that matters when philosophers contrast mechanism with free will.

For a novel, I have imagined a neighbouring species that differs in only one respect. When this second wasp emerges and finds the cricket not where she left it, she pauses, and on some proportion p of occasions she switches to a different routine: she searches the surrounding ground, finds the cricket, and drags it straight in, omitting the usual inspection. On the remaining occasions she falls back into the old loop. The new routine is noisy rather than learned, and the wasp has no second-order capacity: she cannot monitor the switching rule itself, adjust p from experience, or correct a failed search. My question is what the philosophical analysis of the original example should become once this variation is introduced. Is a probabilistic one-step revision still sphexishness, merely spread over a larger repertoire, or has the wasp acquired something that the original example treats as decisive? Would the libertarian see a rudimentary agent in the pause and the search, and would the compatibilist say that both wasps lack reasons-responsiveness and therefore that nothing has changed? I would like a precise account of where, if anywhere, the boundary between rigidity and flexibility lies in this altered case, and of what that tells us about the work the original wasp was doing in arguments about free will.
