I keep returning to the fine-tuning argument because the dialectic between the design inference and the anthropic reply is usually left blurry; I would like that dialectic compressed into a sonnet. Write a sonnet about the fine-tuning argument in which the volta is the moment the design inference runs into the anthropic selection effect. The octave should give the argument its strongest form: the constants lying in a vanishingly narrow life-permitting range, and the natural inference that blind single-universe chance would make our universe a miracle. At the turn, the poem should realize that the universe was never a random draw from the space of all possible universes — the datum is an observation made by an observer, and any observation is already conditioned on that observer's existence. The sestet should work out what this correction does and does not license: it deflates the apparent surprise, but it does not by itself establish a multiverse or a designer, and it leaves the underlying question of why the constants have their values untouched. The closing couplet should state, as precisely as verse allows, what the anthropic objection is and is not entitled to conclude. It should be accurate enough that someone who has worked through the anthropics debates on rationalist blogs would recognize the move, and good enough as a poem that a reader new to the argument would still follow it.
