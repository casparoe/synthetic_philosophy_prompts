I am a high school junior taking AP Comparative Government this year and serving on my school's Model UN team. Earlier this term we read a short excerpt from Jeremy Bentham's attack on the French Declaration of the Rights of Man. The charge has stuck with me: Bentham treats natural rights as nonsense upon stilts — on his view, a right is not something that exists before or above the law, but something a legal system creates by imposing duties and providing remedies, and talk of rights that exist independently of law is really a way of smuggling in preferred policies while pretending they are already settled. I find this persuasive, but I have also spent two years giving Model UN speeches in which I invoke universal human rights as the grounds for condemning states that torture, censor, or discriminate. I want to know whether the set of commitments I actually hold hangs together before I walk into another committee session.

Here is the full set: (a) There are no natural rights that exist prior to or independently of legal systems; rights are created by law. (b) Torturing an innocent person is wrong regardless of what any legal system says, and I do not just mean that I would dislike it. (c) The language of universal human rights is the best available instrument for criticizing laws and regimes that permit torture, and I use it without blushing. (d) A right that has no identifiable duty-bearer and no remedy or enforcement procedure is not really a right at all. (e) If human beings have rights grounded in their interests, then many nonhuman animals have rights too, because their interests are of the same kind. (f) Governments may legitimately restrict individual liberty when doing so produces a large aggregate gain in welfare, and this is compatible with taking rights very seriously.

Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what each side of each tension would have to give, and identify the single revision that would restore the most coherence at the lowest cost. As you do this, please separate what is settled about Bentham's actual view from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus view.
