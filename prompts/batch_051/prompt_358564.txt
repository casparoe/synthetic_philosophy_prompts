A content hash (say a git SHA) seems to do exactly what a proper name is supposed to do in Kripke semantics: it picks out the same object no matter how you rewrite the history, so its rigid, whereas a branch name or "the commit that fixed the login bug" is flaccid like a definite description, since it can pick out different commits under different counterfactual histories. The necessary-a-posteriori part even looks like it transfers: whether "abc123" and "def456" are the same artifact is something you discover by inspecting, but once they're identical, the identity can't be otherwise — except, wait, SHA collisions are possible in principle, so maybe that's exactly where the analogy bottoms out. Do you think this is a deep structural identity (content addressing just is rigid designation for artifacts), a loose analogy, or a confusion? Give me the bottom line first, then say what has to be true on both sides for the analogy to support conclusions in either direction, and name at least one thing the parallel would predict that turns out false.
