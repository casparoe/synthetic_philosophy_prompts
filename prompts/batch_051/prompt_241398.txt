Write a one-page research proposal for a six-month philosophy project on the paradox of fiction. The starting-point is a passage near the beginning of David Hume's essay 'Of Tragedy' in which he observes that spectators of a well-written tragedy receive pleasure from sorrow, terror, and anxiety, even though those passions are in themselves disagreeable; he then rejects the suggestion that the pleasure arises simply from our awareness that the events are fictional, noting that when the representation is too weak the same awareness does not prevent the painful passions from remaining painful. His positive proposal is that the disagreeable passions are converted into pleasure when they are made subordinate to a predominant movement of beauty and eloquence. My half-formed idea is that this conversion mechanism, though originally offered only for tragedy, is the most promising response to the paradox of fiction, but only if we give up the assumption that the pleasure taken in a painful fiction must be a separate emotion added to the sadness, fear, or pity it depicts. Turn this into a proposal. State the question precisely; say why the standard solutions to the paradox—those that deny the emotions are genuine, those that deny the relevant beliefs about fictionality, and those that posit distinct imaginary objects of emotion—leave Hume's question unanswered and why his neglected mechanism is different; describe the method for testing an interpretative claim about emotional conversion rather than merely stipulating one; and say what would count as the project's failing, with a realistic scope for six months.
