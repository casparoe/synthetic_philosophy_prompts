In my school the college counseling office has this rule: you can't outright lie on applications — if you say you founded a club and you didn't, that's a honor-board-level thing. But the same counselors are totally fine helping you "frame" stuff, like turning a summer you spent mostly doing nothing into "an independent project with limited measurable outcomes," or just leaving out the semester your grades tanked. The line seems to be: false sentences are wrong, but false impressions are fine. What view of why lying is bad does this practice actually presuppose? Reconstruct it, and tell me wether the practice really needs that view or could sit on top of a bunch of different theories. And what would have to change about how they treat applications if we replaced the implicit view with one where what matters is causing someone to believe something false, not wether the sentence you said was literally false.
