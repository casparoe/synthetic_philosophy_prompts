I've been using a distinction in my own head for a while and I want it tested rather than flattered. I call them consequence harms and positional harms inside virtual worlds. A consequence harm is a real-world harm routed through the virtual: your account gets stolen, you lose real money, you're groomed or scammed or doxxed through the platform. A positional harm never leaves the fiction: your character is publicly stripped of their rank, the town you spent six months building is razed by a griefer who makes you watch, your guild is disbanded in a humiliating way. My view is that positional harms are real harms — not because the pixels suffer, but because a person can be harmed through a position they occupy and the standing that comes with it. The usual reply is that its just a game and you can log off. I think that reply is no better than saying workplace bullying isn't a harm because you can quit. But I've been wrong about distinctions before, so: is the line between the two kinds of harm actually stable, or does "never leaves the fiction" do too much work? Is what I'm calling a positional harm already named and handled better somewhere in debates about fiction, games, or online life that I'm not placing? And what's the strongest case against me — either a positional harm that intuitively isn't a real harm, or a case where my own distinction says something is just a consequence harm but it's clearly more than that? Be candid; if the distinction should be dropped rather than tidied up, say so.
