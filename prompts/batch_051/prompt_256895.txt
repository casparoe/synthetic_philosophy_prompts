I am a high school student in a philosophy club, and I have been asked to lead a discussion comparing three broad legal approaches to adult sex work. As I understand them, the positions look roughly like this. Under the Nordic model, selling sex is not treated as a crime, but buying sex—and, in general, profiting from someone else’s sex work—is criminalized, with the stated aim of reducing demand and protecting sellers. Under legalization, as in Germany and the Netherlands, sex work is treated as legal work and regulated in many respects like a business: brothels can be licensed, workers can be taxed, and health and zoning rules apply. Under decriminalization, as in New Zealand, criminal penalties are removed from sex work itself and from the people involved, and the law treats it more like ordinary employment with safety and labor protections.

I am trying to work out what philosophical commitments each regime presupposes about what sex work is and why it might be harmful. Does the Nordic model presuppose that selling sex cannot be a genuinely voluntary or freely chosen kind of work, or is it compatible with thinking that some sellers choose it while still criminalizing every buyer? Does legalization presuppose that sex work is just another form of labor, or can someone support it while thinking it is morally bad but better tolerated and controlled than driven underground? Does decriminalization presuppose that the state has no special reason to treat sex as a distinctive activity, or only that criminal law is the wrong instrument for whatever distinctive concerns remain? And are the pieces within each regime internally consistent—for example, is there a tension in the Nordic model between treating the seller as free of criminal guilt while treating every buyer as an exploiter, or in legalization between claiming to protect workers while imposing rules that many workers avoid because the rules expose or stigmatize them?

I would like you to reconstruct the implicit theory of sex, work, coercion, and state power that each model seems to rely on, distinguish what each model strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, point out where the three models rest on incompatible premises, and say what would have to change in any one of them if its underlying view were replaced by a rival one. Please also tell me how confident you are in your reconstruction and which point you are least sure about.
