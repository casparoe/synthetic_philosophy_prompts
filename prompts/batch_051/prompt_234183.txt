Give me a working definition of a racist statement. I'll use "statement" broadly, to cover anything a person says, writes, or publishes on a particular occasion — a remark, a post, a question on a form — and I want to know what makes that utterance itself racist.

I'll give you seven cases, and I'll tell you where I stand on each. Some of my own views are probably inconsistent, and I'd rather have a definition that forces me to change a view than one that flatters my pre-theoretic judgments.

1. Angry after being refused a refund, a customer shouts a well-known slur at the South Asian shopkeeper. (I'm confident this is a racist statement.)

2. A doctor asks a patient to report their self-identified race and ethnicity on a medical intake form because the practice uses this information to track disparities in treatment outcomes. (I'm confident this is not a racist statement, even though the question is racialized.)

3. In a linguistics paper on slurs, the author quotes the slur from case 1 in italics, inside quotation marks, to give an example of a derogatory term. (I think this is a use of a slur, but not a racist use of one — though I can't quite say why.)

4. A colleague tells a Black lawyer, "You're very articulate," in a tone of surprise. (I lean toward saying this is racist, but it is a compliment on its face, and I can't locate where the racism is.)

5. A politician campaigning on crime says the city must restore law and order, while the campaign's ads repeatedly cut from that phrase to images of Black and Latino neighborhoods. His supporters read the phrase as being about those neighborhoods, and his critics do too, but the words themselves never mention race. (I don't know whether the utterance is racist or whether only the surrounding campaign is.)

6. A well-meaning user reposts a true statistic about an ethnic group's overrepresentation in certain arrests, without context about policing practices and reporting, because the number struck them as interesting. (I have no firm view. I keep wanting to say it isn't racist because it's true, but that feels too simple.)

7. A comedian known for left-wing political material performs in character as a bigot and says contemptible things, plainly intending to mock the character. A clip circulates without the setup and is celebrated on a forum where people take the lines at face value. (I honestly don't know whether the original performance, the clip, or both are racist.)

Now the ask. Propose a definition, run it through all seven cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. If it forces you to accept a conclusion that conflicts with my stated intuitions, say so and defend the conclusion. I'd rather you revise the definition or argue that I'm wrong about a case than quietly skip one of them.

A good definition will probably have to take a position on whether a statement can be racist in virtue of the speaker's intent, in virtue of its predictable effects, or in virtue of what it signals to one audience while remaining deniable to another. I know enough to know that all three of those positions are contested. As you answer, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this topic, and don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
