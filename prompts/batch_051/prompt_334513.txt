I've been rereading Gödel, Escher, Bach and it struck me that Hofstadter's idea of the inviolate level in a tangled hierarchy—the level you simply have to treat as given, otherwise the Drawing Hands loop never bottoms out—is doing exactly the same job as Hart's rule of recognition. Hart says legal validity has to derive from a rule that is not itself legally valid, just accepted as a matter of social practice among officials; Hofstadter says a strange loop has to be grounded somewhere you refuse to question or you get paradox. That feels like more than the boring point that foundations have to stop somewhere, but I can't tell whether it's a real structural identity or just pattern-matching. If it is real, does anything from GEB transfer to the legal side—say, the difference between a level's being inviolate and merely unperceived, or the way adding one more level changes the paradox rather than removing it? And does the analogy break because a rule of recognition can shift through legal means in a way Escher's hands can't redraw the level below them? Please answer in British English.
