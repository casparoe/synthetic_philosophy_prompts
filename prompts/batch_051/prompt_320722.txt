I'm an undergraduate serving as the student representative on a university working group charged with deciding what values a new AI tool for allocating seats in oversubscribed courses should be built to serve, and I'm currently taking a philosophy course on the ethics of technology. The group is deadlocked. One camp argues the tool should rank students so as to maximize aggregate satisfaction of course preferences, subject to a guaranteed minimum for students who would otherwise be shut out; the other camp argues that a public university should stay neutral among rival conceptions of what students should get from their education, so the tool should enforce only a fair, transparent procedure and never weigh different students' aims against one another. The chair has asked me to circulate a short decision memo before Friday's meeting, and she wants an actionable bottom line rather than a survey of the options. Please write the memo as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, covering in order: (1) the concrete recommendation, (2) the strongest reason for it, (3) the strongest consideration for the position you do not recommend, stated as its advocates would put it, (4) why that consideration does not overturn the recommendation, and (5) the immediate next step with a date for the pilot.
