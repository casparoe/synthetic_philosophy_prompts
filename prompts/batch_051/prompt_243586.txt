I have spent the last few weeks trying to get clear on the existentialist thesis that for human beings existence precedes essence — the claim that there is no fixed human nature laid down in advance, and that a person is nothing other than what their choices and actions make of them — and on the recurring objection that this thesis is incoherent. The objection takes several forms: that the denial of a human essence is itself a positive account of what humans essentially are; that a position which treats all values as groundless creations of free choice cannot, without contradiction, recommend authenticity or responsibility as the right way to live; and that the assertion of radical freedom is a factual claim about human beings of exactly the kind the position says is impossible. I would like to see these two camps treated as parties to an adversarial collaboration rather than as combatants exchanging slogans.

Draft the document such a collaboration would produce: one that an existentialist who stands by the Sartrean position and a critic who maintains that the position is incoherent could both sign after good-faith work. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims, including claims about what the existence-precedes-essence thesis does and does not assert — for instance, whether it is a metaphysical claim, an ethical proposal, or both, and which familiar formulations of it both sides agree are rhetorical overstatement. Then provide a disagreements table. Each side's view should be phrased so that the side itself would accept the wording as an accurate statement of its own position. For every remaining disagreement, specify the crux: the sub-question such that settling it one way would move at least one side toward the other. Where possible, give for each crux the case, scenario, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about how that test comes out recorded in advance. The document should make clear which disagreements are genuine and which were only verbal, and it should state the residual disagreement as exactly as the two sides can make it.
