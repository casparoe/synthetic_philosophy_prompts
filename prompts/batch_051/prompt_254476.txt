I serve on a working group of an international standards body charged with recommending a single epoch and a defined year zero for a new civil time standard, intended for historical databases and for date arithmetic that crosses the traditional era boundary. The group is divided. One faction favours astronomical year numbering, which labels the year conventionally called 1 BCE as 0, so that subtraction across the boundary runs as ordinary integer arithmetic. A second faction argues that this still anchors the scale to a culturally specific era boundary and prefers a deliberately decoupled epoch, such as 10,000 BCE. A third faction holds that the choice is arbitrary and that we should retain the existing convention and document its anomalies. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one concrete convention, give the reasoning for it, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration against it rather than simply noting that all epochs are conventions. Write the opinion in British English.
