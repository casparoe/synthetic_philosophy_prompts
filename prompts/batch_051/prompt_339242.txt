Two friends of mine got into it at dinner last week — one is an economics PhD student, the other does political philosophy — about whether we're allowed to discount future people's wellbeing in climate policy. They kept going in circles, and I still can't tell who won. (1) The econ friend said you have to discount future climate damages, otherwise you'd be treating a dollar of benefit in 2150 as worth the same as a dollar of benefit now, and that commits us to absurd sacrifices today for people who will be much richer than us — this is just standard finance. (2) The philosophy friend said he was conflating two different things: you can discount future money because it can be invested, and because future people will probably be richer, without ever saying a minute of suffering in 2150 matters less than a minute of suffering now. Pure time preference is an extra step, he said, and it's indefensible. (3) The econ friend said actual people do discount future wellbeing even after you control for growth and risk — they'd rather have a thing now than the same thing in thirty years — and policy should answer to actual people's preferences, not to the preferences they'd have after some philosophical re-education. (4) The philosophy friend said that's deriving an ought from an is: people are myopic and biased about all sorts of things, and we don't build those biases into the welfare function, so why privilege the calendar? (5) The econ friend said the "impartial standpoint" is doing all the work there, and that even a zero pure time preference rate isn't neutral, because you still have to pick how much you care about inequality across generations to get a positive discount rate through future growth — so both sides are picking values, one side just hides it. (6) The philosophy friend said fine, but the value you should pick is about inequality between rich and poor, not about time itself, and once you do that you can get all the positive discounting anyone reasonably wants without saying later people count for less merely because they're later.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Go through the exchange move by move: for each move, say whether it's clearly right, clearly wrong, or somewhere in between, and why. Then say whether the disagreement is mostly substantive or partly a confusion between different senses of "discounting," and who, if either, is more right overall.
