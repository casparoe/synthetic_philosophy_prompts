I recently had a long argument with a friend about iterated amplification and distillation, and I would like a careful, worked-through answer to the question we could not settle. The claim at issue was this: when a capability is recursively decomposed into subtasks that a human overseer can supervise, and the resulting amplified system is then distilled into a single model, the process preserves both competence and alignment. My friend's worry was that the distillation step is where the decisive loss occurs, because the student never experiences the deliberative structure that produced the amplified outputs; it sees only the finished outputs and is trained to imitate them. On his view, however good the amplified system is, the distilled model will tend to learn the surface of the behaviour while discarding the internal checks, uncertainty expression, and corrigible reasoning that made the amplified system aligned in the first place. I was more optimistic: I think that if the distillation data includes the full reasoning trace and explicit uncertainty signals, and if the task distribution is rich enough, competence and alignment can survive the compression.

I would like you to address this as a question about how one could test a conceptual claim, not merely as a debate in the abstract. Please design a concrete test, or a small battery of tests, that would distinguish the view that alignment and competence survive iterative decomposition and distillation from the view that distillation systematically strips out the alignment-relevant structure. Specify a realistic setting (for example, a question-answering or software-engineering assistant, or a similar domain where amplification and distillation could actually be run). State exactly what you would measure, what results would count in favour of each side, and how large an effect would be meaningful. Then say explicitly what a positive result would and would not establish: in particular, whether it would show that alignment survives, or only that competence survives in the conditions tested, and which parts of the original claim no experiment of this kind could settle even in principle.

Please write the answer in British English.
