A linguist friend insists that this is a folk theory. On her account a word has no stable core; it is a loose network of overlapping uses held together by family resemblance, and what we call its meaning is only an acquired fluency in navigating that network. For me this is not an idle quarrel, since if she is right I have been mistaken about what I am doing when I choose one word rather than another. Design the strongest test you can of her claim that an ordinary word such as sharp has no single meaning common to all its central uses, but only a family of related senses.
