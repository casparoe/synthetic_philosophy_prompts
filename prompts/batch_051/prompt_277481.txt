We have this weird double standard about hope in climate politics. We praise the activist who refuses to give up on the 1.5 degree target, and act like giving up on it is a moral failure. But if a pension fund actually invested as if we were going to hit 1.5, we'd call the managers reckless, maybe even negligent, with other people's money. Its the same factual bet about the future, just in two different roles. Is the difference just that the activist isn't spending anyone's retirement on it? That feels too easy, because we'd also blame the activist if her hopefulness made a coastal town skip building a seawall. So where exactly is the line between hope as a virtue and hope as a planning failure? Give me the strongest principle you can find for treating the same optimism as noble in one place and blameworthy in another, and then an honest verdict on whether the double standard should stay.
