Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique argued that a style of reasoning then dominant in evolutionary biology—taking it for granted that a trait exists because natural selection favored its present function—was methodologically defective. The paper accused that style of atomizing organisms into separately optimized traits, inferring adaptation from the mere availability of a plausible story, and ignoring alternatives such as architectural byproducts, correlated responses to selection on other traits, genetic drift, and phylogenetic constraint. At the time it was written, there was little systematic machinery for testing such stories.

Now suppose a modern adaptationist replies that the critique is dated. The adaptationist can point out that selection is now estimated directly in natural populations from fitness differences among individuals; quantitative genetics can determine whether a trait would respond to selection; phylogenetic comparative methods can test whether a trait and an ecological variable evolve together across independent lineages; experimental evolution can observe adaptation as it occurs; and genome scans can locate signatures of recent positive selection. Many adaptive stories that were once untestable can now, the reply goes, be tested and either falsified or supported.

Does the spandrels critique survive this reply? I want to know whether the critique's force was mainly that adaptationist explanations were untested and often untestable, in which case the new methods answer its central complaint; or whether the critique also targets something that remains intact even when selection can be measured—such as how easily a selective story can be overfit to data, how the trait is delineated before it is tested, whether signatures of selection are too readily equated with adaptation for a trait's current function, or which null hypotheses the methods silently favor. Say which premise or premises of the original argument the new methods actually bear on, and give the strongest version of the critique that remains available, or explain why the critique no longer cuts against anything distinct.
