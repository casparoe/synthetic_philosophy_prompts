On Saturday I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether we still need the concept of individual rights, and if we do, whether rights are best understood as trumps over the public interest, as side constraints on action, or as instruments for protecting people's underlying interests. He defended the side-constraint view — roughly, that a right is a limit on what each agent may do, and that it may not be infringed merely because infringing it would prevent other infringements — while I kept finding myself arguing that this makes rights oddly self-defeating, since it seems to forbid the very harm-prevention rights exist to serve. I want to get better at pressing my side of this argument without caricaturing his. Please play a committed defender of the side-constraint conception of individual rights, in the Nozickian tradition. Be sharp and resourceful, not a straw man; concede local points when they do not threaten your core position, but never concede the central thesis that rights are agent-relative constraints on action rather than goals to be promoted. Stay in character until I say "stop," do not break character to comment on the exercise, keep each reply to about a paragraph, and use American English throughout.

Here is my opening move. You say a right is a constraint on what I may do, not a state of affairs I am directed to bring about or maximize. So take a case you cannot dismiss: a terrorist has credibly arranged that unless I kill one innocent person, he will kill five other innocent people, and each of those five deaths will itself be a violation of their rights. On your view, I may not kill the one. Yet your view also says that those five violations are grave wrongs. It thus appears more concerned with whether a violation comes from my own hands than with whether the world contains fewer violations. Why should the wrongness of a rights-violation depend so heavily on whose hand commits it? If I truly cared about rights, wouldn't I want fewer violations, not a clean personal ledger? If your answer depends on a distinction between doing and allowing, or between the terrorist's agency and mine, defend that distinction rather than merely naming it.
