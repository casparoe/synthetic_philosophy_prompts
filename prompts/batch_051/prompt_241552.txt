I am drafting a position statement for a working group on minds, machines, and mathematical knowledge, and I would like a consistency audit of the following commitments before I circulate it. The statement is meant to defend a qualified Gödelian analogy: the kind of self-reference that produces incompleteness in formal arithmetic is importantly analogous to the kind of self-reference involved in conscious thought, though I am unsure how far the analogy should be pushed.

My commitments, as I would currently state them:

1. For any formal system F that is consistent, effectively axiomatized, and strong enough to encode elementary arithmetic, Gödel's construction yields a sentence G_F that is unprovable in F but true on the intended interpretation of the arithmetical symbols.
2. When presented with such a system F, together with the construction of G_F, a human mathematician can in principle recognize that G_F is true.
3. Therefore, the human mind is not equivalent to any single consistent formal system: for any system proposed as a model of my reasoning, there is a Gödel sentence for that system that I can see to be true but that the system cannot prove.
4. Nevertheless, I am a physicalist about the mind. Mental processes are realized entirely by a finite brain, and a sufficiently precise physical description of that brain could in principle be simulated by a Turing machine.
5. I am unpersuaded by the suggestion that accounting for mathematical insight requires positing non-computable physical processes; nothing known about the behavior of neurons supports that hypothesis.
6. At the same time, I think the self-referential character of Gödel's construction does point to something important about minds: a system is a mind, roughly, when it can take its own representations and operations as objects of further representation — the strange-loop idea, rather than mere information processing.

Work through the pairs of commitments and tell me which tensions are genuine and which are merely apparent. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say which commitment would have to be revised, how much that revision would cost, and what the cheapest repair would be. Then tell me which single revision to this set of six would do the most to restore coherence.
