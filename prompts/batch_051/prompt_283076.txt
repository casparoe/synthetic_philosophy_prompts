Consider a legal system in which a corporation counts as a person for some purposes and not for others. It can enter contracts, own property, sue and be sued, and, in some jurisdictions, is treated as a speaker protected when it spends on political advocacy; but it cannot vote, sit on a jury, marry, or be imprisoned. These boundaries have shifted over time and have shifted unevenly, so that the concept of a person now appears to work in one way in contract law, another in campaign finance, another in tort, and another in criminal law — a patchwork for which no single definition has ever been announced.

What theory of concepts does this practice presuppose, even if its practitioners have never stated one? Specifically, does it presuppose that the legal concept of a person has a fixed content that is there to be discovered and then applied more or less faithfully, so that the patchwork is a set of imperfect attempts to track one underlying thing? Or does it presuppose that "person" is a piece of conceptual technology reshaped domain by domain to serve whatever that domain is for, so that a patchwork is exactly what one should expect from a tool optimized for different jobs?

Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as the practice allows. Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and mark the places where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several rival theories of concepts would license the same rulings. Then identify one concrete change in the practice that would follow if the system explicitly replaced its working theory with a thoroughgoing conceptual-engineering view, on which concepts are to be fixed rather than merely analysed; and one change that would follow if it adopted a thoroughgoing conceptual-analysis view instead.

Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who have worked on these questions, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. The very last line of your answer should be exactly one of: \answer{engineering}, \answer{analysis}, or \answer{underdetermined}.
