I am a senior at a small public high school, and I have been asked to give a five-minute speech at a memorial assembly for a teacher who died last month. She taught an elective on early modern philosophy, and she was the person who introduced me to Spinoza. She did not just teach the Ethics; she believed it. In particular, she held Spinoza's necessitarianism, the view that everything that happens, including every human action and every loss, follows necessarily from God or Nature, and that there is no alternative way things could have gone. She also taught that the highest good is the intellectual love of God, which she understood not as love for a personal God who rewards or rescues, but as the joy that comes from understanding things as they truly are, under the aspect of eternity. The audience will include her former students, other teachers, and her family, most of whom do not know much about Spinoza, and some of whom are religious. I want the speech to comfort that room without saying anything she would have considered false: no talk of her watching over us, no talk of her being in a better place, no appeal to a free will that she rejected. The Spinozist ideas should not be decoration; they should shape what the speech can and cannot say, and they should turn what sounds cold, that her death was necessary, into something that can actually console the people who loved her. Please write the speech for me, in American English, at about five minutes when read aloud.
