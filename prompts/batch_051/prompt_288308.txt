I keep running into a game theory thing I don't have a clean name for. In a finite normal-form game, you can delete a strategy that's strictly worse than some other single strategy no matter what the other players do, and repeat until nothing more goes. But you can delete strictly more if you also allow a strategy to be ruled out when it's worse than a coin flip over two or three strategies still on the table, even when it's not worse than any one of them alone; the extra deletions then unlock further rounds downstream. I mostly look at two-player games, where the randomization version seems to leave a stable, smaller set. Is there an established name for the wedge between the two procedures, or for the strategies that only the randomization version removes? How does it relate to rationalizability, and if the gap itself has no name, what would you call it?
