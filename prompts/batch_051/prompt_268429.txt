In my civics class we're prepping for a debate on whether school districts should be allowed to offer bonuses to teachers who transfer to really understaffed schools. The other side kept shutting us down with "that's just a race to the bottom": once one district starts doing it they'll all have to, teachers chase the money, and the districts that can't afford it get gutted. My friend who reads a lot of those rationalist blogs said its actually a multipolar trap, not a race to the bottom, and he tried to explain Moloch — the idea where a bunch of actors each doing the locally rational thing locks everyone into an outcome nobody wanted, like everyone standing up at a concert to see better, or companies polluting because the clean ones get outcompeted. Then another kid said a race to the bottom is just a special case of a multipolar trap, where the thing getting competed away is a standard or worker protection or something, and now I can't tell if these are different arguments or the same one with better branding.

I want to be able to answer this in the actual debate. What are the different precise claims someone might be making when they say a policy causes a race to the bottom? Lay them out as a list from the most boring or obvious to the most radical, and for each say whether it's plausible, overblown, or just false. Then say which precise reading the other team would need for their argument against bonuses to actually go through, and where the multipolar trap / Moloch framing fits in — is it a stronger version of one of the readings, a different mechanism, or just a scarier word for the same thing?
