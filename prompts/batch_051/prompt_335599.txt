I've ended up on the committee rewriting my union local's constitution, and we're stuck on a clause about members acting together, so I need a working definition. Define acting together (joint action, whatever you want to call it) so it gets these cases right. (1) You and I carry a couch upstairs, one at each end: clearly together. (2) Two strangers push a stalled car out of an intersection, can see each other, never speak: I want to say yes but I cant tell you why. (3) A flash mob in a train station: everyone got a text to start dancing when a certain song comes on, nobody knows anybody else or who got the text: is that joint or just coordinated? (4) Me and my roommate 'clean the apartment together' because the chore app has me doing dishes and him doing the bathroom on different days: I want to say no, but maybe its a weaker version of the same thing. (5) A corporation that pollutes, even though no individual employee intended the whole plan: can a group act together without any member intending the whole? (6) Two kids in a tug-of-war against another team, but one thinks its a real contest and the other thinks its just play: together or not? (7) Everybody leaving a stadium at once, all heading to the same exit, each only trying to get to their car. Propose the definition, run it through the cases, and tell me where your definition is least sure of its verdict.
