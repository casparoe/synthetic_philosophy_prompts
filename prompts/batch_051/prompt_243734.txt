I've been arguing with a friend about whether a company can be morally blamed, not just fined or shut down, and I keep going in circles. I once took a class that gave a clean list of conditions for blameworthiness, and I want to test the company case against it properly. The conditions I remember are roughly: (1) there's an agent that actually did the harmful thing, (2) the agent had control over the relevant decision procedure and could of done otherwise, (3) the agent knew or reasonably should have known the relevant facts, (4) the act was intentional under at least some description, and (5) the agent is the kind of thing that can respond to moral reasons and be expected to feel something like remorse. I suspect condition (5) is doing a lot of hidden work, but I'm not sure.

Here's the borderline case. Picture a mid-sized chemical company where a dangerous waste-disposal practice develops over about a decade. No single person ever decided to dump toxic waste illegally. Instead, a manager set a budget that made the legal disposal route look irresponsible, an engineer signed off on a workaround believing someone else had verified it, a compliance officer flagged a concern that got buried in a queue, and the board only saw aggregate numbers showing savings. The company as an entity has a clear decision procedure (the board, the committees, the reporting lines), its records together contain all the facts, and when regulators fine it, it predictably changes its behaviour. But there's no single mind that knew the whole plan, no individual who intended the harm, and the company itself dosen't feel guilt.

Act as a professional philosopher working on collective agency and moral responsibility. Before you answer, take a minute to brainstorm the weakest point of each criterion. Then go through the five conditions one by one and say where the company clearly meets them, clearly fails them, or where the criterion just doesn't decide the case. Finally, tell me whether the company is blameworthy in the same sense a person is, or whether blame here is something different, and whether the criteria themselves need revising. Don't just give the standard yes or no; I want to know which condition is doing the real work and whether we should keep it.
