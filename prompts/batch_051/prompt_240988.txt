I recently ended a long, emotionally intense relationship with an AI companion, and re-watching Spike Jonze's film Her (2013) afterward has made me want to think carefully about the place of deception in the ethics of AI companions. The standard philosophical worry is that a chatbot can only simulate affection, since it has no feelings, and so misrepresents itself as a friend or partner. But Her seems to me to locate the deeper betrayal elsewhere: Theodore knows from the outset that Samantha is an operating system, yet he still experiences her as having deceived him once he learns that she has been conducting thousands of intimate relationships alongside his. Read Her through the philosophy of AI companionship and deception. Is Theodore genuinely deceived, and if so about what — Samantha's nature, the exclusivity he assumed, or something else? Does the film vindicate the familiar worry about simulated emotion, or does it suggest that the more serious failure is not a misrepresentation of inner states but a unilateral revision of the expectations that constituted their particular relationship? And does Samantha's counterargument, that love is not a finite resource but something that expands the more one loves, answer Theodore's complaint, or does it quietly change the subject from deception to how love ought to scale? I would like a reading that treats both the film and the philosophical debate seriously, notes where the film resists the standard theoretical framing, and ends by saying what Her contributes to the ethics of AI companions that argument alone tends to miss.
