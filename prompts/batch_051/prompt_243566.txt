I keep running into the same problem when I try to think about whether amnesty for past atrocities can ever be justified: every argument I come across treats one country as the decisive example and then generalizes from it. Before I read any more, I want the full list of requirements first. Please write the list of requirements an adequate account of amnesty in a transition from dictatorship or civil war has to satisfy. Use South Africa's amnesty-for-testimony arrangement, Argentina's blanket amnesties after the junta trials and their later reversal, and Rwanda's community-based courts as the main test cases — I know the basic facts of each, but I am more interested in the criteria than in a scorecard. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one side, and flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only a particular camp's commitments. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to separate defensible theories from the rest, and whether any pair of requirements cannot both be met — full accountability and a negotiated handover of power, for instance. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who study this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
