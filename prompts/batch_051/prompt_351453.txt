ok so ive been stuck on this double standard and i want to know whether it's actually justified or just something people let religion get away with.

Here's the ordinary case. If I hide from a friend on purpose, dodge their messages, and then complain "you never came looking for me so you clearly don't care," everyone would say I'm being manipulative. The hider is the problem. If you want to be found, you make yourself findable; if you hide, you don't get to blame the other person for not finding you.

But switch it to God and the blame flips. God (supposedly) stays hidden, yet a lot of the same people who would call me manipulative say the person who didn't find God is at fault for not seeking hard enough. Now the hider gets to blame the seeker. Same pattern: one side hides, the other fails to find them, but the fault lands on the opposite person.

So is there a principle that actually generates both verdicts, like a human friend owes me something but God doesnt owe me anything, or God's hiding is okay because its supposed to protect free will? Or is the asymmetry just because God is the big and holy side of the story so we give that side automatic credit? I want the strongest principle you can find that keeps both judgments, tested against a few nearby cases (a parent hiding from a toddler to see if the kid will come looking, a teacher not telling the class what's on the test and then blaming them for not studying the right material), and then an honest verdict: does the principle actually work, or do we have to give up one of the two judgments?
