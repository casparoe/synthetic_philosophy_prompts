so ive gone down a rabbit hole about how a thought gets to be about something at all, and I've ended up mostly convinced by a view I think people call teleosemantics. the basic idea is that the content of a mental state (what a belief or perception is actually about) is fixed by the biological function of the machinery that produces it, where function means what that machinery was selected to do. a fish has a little circuit that fires for shadows passing overhead and triggers hiding; it means predator because hiding from actual predators is the job it was installed to do, even if it also fires at falling leaves. fine.

but the version i actually believe is weaker than the usual all-purpose one. I think selection history only gets you coarse content: the difference between food and mate, safe and unsafe, that sort of thing. it tells you what the state is broadly attuned to. all the fine-grained content (the difference between thinking prime and thinking divisible by seven, a thought about one specific event you saw a single time, the content of a lie or a plainly false belief) gets assembled later out of capacities that werent selected for those exact contents. so selection is the anchor, but only at the bottom.

what i want is an honest map of what would count against this view, including my weaker version. not just the usual thought experiment where a molecule-for-molecule duplicate pops into existence by chance and the question is whether its states mean anything, and not a list of rival theories. which possible observations, experiments, thought experiments, or results from other areas would be fatal, which would just be awkward, and which can the view absorb or quietly revise around? what would a defender of this position themselves admit is a real problem, not just a critics caricature? and what does the view risk: what would it predict that its rivals dont, so it could actually turn out false instead of being comfortable either way?

i personally find the hardest cases to be fine-grained or contentful states where its hard to imagine any selection pressure shaping them: thoughts about infinity, counterfactuals, math, or a one-off thing you witnessed once. are those fatal to the coarse version, or can it say the fine stuff gets built on top and the anchoring just has to be good enough? and at the end tell me straight whether the view is too insulated, like its compatible with too much, and whether that insulation is a bug or a feature.
