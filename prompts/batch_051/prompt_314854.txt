I hold the position that the martial virtues—courage under lethal risk, valor, military honor, and the ability to act well under fear of violent death—are not merely instrumental excellences whose value is fully discharged by success in war. They are constitutive goods: possessing and exercising them is itself a deep form of self-realization. I draw a further conclusion about peace. A society that makes war rare does not simply outgrow these virtues; it removes the arena in which they are most naturally formed and displayed, and that removal is a real cost to human flourishing even if peace remains on balance an overwhelming good. A decent peaceful society should therefore seek civilian forms of the same virtues—first response, high-risk trades, exploration, athletic contest, political and moral courage under serious sanction—while conceding that such substitutes may at best approximate the martial paradigm.

Tell me what would count against this view. I want the defeaters mapped rather than merely listed. For each serious candidate, whether empirical, conceptual, historical, or evaluative, say what would have to be true for the position to fail or require major revision, and how live you take that possibility to be. Where possible, indicate what the view predicts that a rival view would not, so that the test is not empty. Distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from those that would force significant qualification and those that would be merely awkward. Specify which of them a defender of the position would themselves concede as counting against it, as opposed to objections only a critic would press. Then assess whether the view, as I have stated it, is insulated against refutation—for example by its appeal to constitutive goods or by the built-in concession that civilian substitutes can never fully match martial courage—and say whether that insulation is a defect or an honest reflection of the subject matter. I am especially interested in the situationist worry that courage of this kind may not be a stable trait that transfers across domains; in actual cases of societies that have demilitarized or never prized martial virtue; and in whether the boundary between martial and civilian courage marks a real kind or a rhetorical one. Finally, tell me which single consideration should move me most if it broke against me.
