I have been trying to make sense of the paradox of tragedy — the puzzle that people deliberately seek out stories that make them sad, frightened, or horrified, and describe the experience as good, even though grief, fear, and horror are states they normally try to avoid. It seems to me that dissolving the puzzle requires a clearer account of what it is to "enjoy" a work of art, one that does not simply equate enjoying with having pleasant feelings. Please propose a working definition of "enjoying a work of art" precise enough to test.

Apply the definition to each of these cases. My tentative verdicts are in parentheses, but I am least sure about the middle ones.

1. Laughing throughout a comedy and immediately wanting to rewatch it. (Clearly an enjoyment.)
2. Sobbing through a tragedy and afterward calling it the best film I have seen this year. (It feels like I enjoyed it, though the feelings it produced were not pleasant.)
3. A horror film that left me genuinely afraid and somewhat nauseated, and that I rated five stars. (I want to say I enjoyed it, but I did not like the fear while it was happening.)
4. A song I replay when I am already miserable, knowing it will make me cry. (It seems to do something I want, but I do not know whether "enjoyment" is the right word.)
5. A documentary about real suffering that I would call excellent, important, and worth watching, but not enjoyable. (If your definition says I enjoyed this, something has gone wrong.)
6. A "guilty pleasure" movie that I like watching but could not defend as good. (This seems to be enjoyment without appreciation.)
7. A novel that bored me for long stretches but ultimately felt rewarding. (Did I enjoy the whole experience, or only the payoff?)

After proposing the definition, run it through all seven cases. Tell me where the definition gives a surprising verdict, where I should revise one of my tentative verdicts, and whether the definition has to be amended along the way. Finally, address the worry that has bothered me most: does your definition explain why sad art is worth seeking out, or does it merely relabel the unpleasant feeling so that the paradox disappears? I would rather keep the puzzle visible and partially unsolved than accept a definition that solves it only by wordplay.
