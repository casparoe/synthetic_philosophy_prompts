Last Sunday I ended up in a three-hour kitchen-table argument with a friend about whether the old debate between universalists and restrictivists about composite objects is a genuine disagreement or just two groups using "exists" differently. He's a recovering metaphysics student and kept saying I'd Carnap'd myself into a corner; I kept replying that calling something a verbal dispute shouldn't be a conversation-stopper, because choosing between frameworks is still a choice with costs, and those costs can be evaluated. We didn't finish, and it's left me anxious because next month I'm giving a departmental colloquium defending exactly that line: I want to argue that the universalist/restrictivist dispute about whether there are things like tables is a verbal dispute in the sense that the two sides can agree on all the underlying facts and differ only in which quantifier they're speaking, but that it does not follow that the choice between their frameworks is arbitrary — it's a choice between epistemic policies about how much structure to posit, and there are better and worse reasons for that choice even if there is no framework-neutral truth about who is right.

The audience are mostly not metaphysicians, which is the problem. I know I'll have: (a) two old-school Quineans who think "quantifier variance" is an evasion and will insist we all mean the same thing by "there is"; (b) one Carnapian who thinks my view is just his view with the honest conclusion ducked; (c) a philosopher of language who works on context-sensitivity and will attack my sementics of "exists"; (d) a moral philosopher who's already told me she worries deflationism about ontology will leak into "climate change is real" and other things she actually cares about; and (e) a few philosophers of science who keep saying the whole debate is unfalsifiable noise. My friend's objections were more like heckling; these people will actually know how to corner me.

I'd like a Q&A prep sheet, not an essay defending the view. Please structure it as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "opening_concessions" (array of strings, things to concede in the first couple of minutes to take the wind out of easy objections), "objections" (array of objects ordered by expected order and damage, each object having "raised_by", "as_they_would_phrase_it", "damage_rating" (an integer 1–10 with a one-sentence justification), "two_minute_reply", "weakest_part_of_reply", and "concede_or_hold" (what I should give ground on and what I should keep)), "reply_that_must_land" (string explaining the one reply that has to work for the talk to survive, and why), and "question_i_hope_nobody_asks" (string with the question I'm dreading and a line on how to survive if it comes). Put the objections in the order I'll actually receive them, phrased the way each audience member would phrase them, and be blunt about where my position is softest.
