I am preparing for a seminar session on blame in historiography, and I want to be able to answer a move a classmate made last week. The discussion had reached the familiar Western textbook claim that the Soviet Union, through its postwar expansionism and ideological hostility, was chiefly responsible for starting the Cold War. My classmate dismissed this as the predictable story of the winners: history is written by the victors. His fuller argument, as I reconstruct it, was this: because the Western side won the Cold War, it got to write the record; a Soviet victory would have produced a mirror-image story assigning the blame to Washington; and so the standard Western account is just a self-serving narrative that deserves no special authority when we ask who started it. I am sure the slogan is doing too much work, but I cannot yet say exactly where the slide happens. Please lay out the distinct precise claims that the saying "history is written by the victors" could be expressing in an argument like his, ranging from the trivially true to the sweeping and dubious. For each claim, say briefly whether it is trivially true, defensible but contestable, or false. Then show which reading, or combination of readings, would actually support his conclusion, and identify any reading that his conclusion needs but that he has not established.
