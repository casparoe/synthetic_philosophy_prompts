I teach political philosophy, and I also sit on my city's ethics commission. We're deadlocked over a case and I've been asked to draft the decision memo. A first-term councillor from a tight-knit immigrant community has spent two years steering small discretionary grants to a neighbourhood association run by her cousin; the association in turn does her voter-turnout work and caters her family's events for free. She says this is just community reciprocity — you take care of your own — and that the commision is imposing a Western, arm's-length ideal of politics on a community that does things differently. Her defenders want to amend the city's gift and conflict-of-interest rules so that this kind of culturally embedded clientelism counts as legitimate constituency service, not corruption. Her critics say that amounts to turning 'gift' into a loophole that drains the whole ethics code. Draft the memo I'll circulate before next week's vote. It should recommend a concrete outcome — whether the conduct violates the code, whether the code should be amended, or some fallback — and it should take the strongest version of the losing side seriously rather than just dismissing it. Please make the memo begin with the recommendation itself in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, and start right in on the memo with no preface or commentary.
