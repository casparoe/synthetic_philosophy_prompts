I've been turning this over for weeks and would like a really careful verdict on it. After my engagement ended in a way that was, honestly, humiliating for me, I started telling people a cleaned-up version of the breakup in which I was the one who had checked out and ended things. At first I knew this wasnt quite true; I could still feel the actual memory underneath it. But I kept feeding the story: I deleted old messages, muted the mutual friends who had seen what happened, and whenever the unwanted version surfaced I would immediately run through the better one. I also had a suspicion that if I did this long enough the better version would stop being a story and just become what I remembered. That was part of the appeal. Now, two years later, I genuinely believe my version. When I try to recover the old memory it isn't there, or it feels like something I watched happen to a stranger.

The reason I'm asking you rather than just calling it self-deception and moving on is this. The philosophical discussions I've read tend to treat self-deception as involving at least these features: (1) the person ends up believing something false, or unsupported by their evidence; (2) the belief is motivated, meaning they want it to be true and that desire does real causal work; (3) they aren't simply a passive victim of error — there's some awareness of the truth or some bias they are responsible for; and (4) the process is theirs in a way that makes it fair to hold them responsible for the resulting belief. I hit (1) and (2) without much trouble. But (3) and (4) feel underdetermined in my case, because by the time the false belief was fully in place I had no awareness of the truth left, and the whole thing was built out of lots of tiny self-manipulations that each looked harmless on its own. So: does my case qualify as self-deception? I'd like you to go criterion by criterion, flag the places where they don't settle the matter, and then say whether the case shows the criteria themselves need to be revised or whether I should just call it something else.
