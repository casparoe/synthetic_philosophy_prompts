A friend and I have been circling around the idea that the self is not a thing but a center of narrative gravity. By that I mean something like this: just as a body's center of gravity is not a material part of the body but a point we posit to organize a distribution of mass, the self might be an organizing posit generated by the stories a person tells about their own life, rather than an inner object that those stories merely report on. The view is attractive because it explains why the self feels unified without requiring any little homunculus behind the eyes, but I worry it is exactly the sort of claim that cannot be brought into contact with evidence. My question is whether that worry is fair. Take the claim to be testable-if-at-all as follows: the experienced continuity and ownership of a self is constructed by narrative activity, not a pre-existing datum that narrative simply describes. Design the best concrete test or small battery of tests you can for that claim. I can vaguely imagine manipulations—having people retell the same autobiographical episode with different degrees of causal and temporal scaffolding, or changing the available memory cues and measuring whether felt sameness tracks the story or the underlying facts—but I want a sharper design than my guesses.
