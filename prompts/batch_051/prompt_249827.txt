i'm putting together a little zine about letterforms and categories, and i want to end it with a villanelle about Hofstadter's Letter Spirit idea — the claim that what makes every lowercase 'a' an 'a' across wildly different typefaces is not any single feature (not the loop, not the tail, not the closed counter) but something more like a position in a space of possible variations, where style is not decoration laid on top of a fixed letter but part of what makes the letter that letter in the first place. i want the form to do real work: the two refrains should keep returning the way the letter 'a' keeps returning across typefaces, the same and not the same each time — maybe one refrain insisting on the fixed identity, the other on the way each instance bends it. nineteen lines, rhymes where the form wants them. it should be a good poem for someone who's never heard of Letter Spirit, but accurate enough that a Hofstadter person recognizes the idea and not just the name. keep it about identity and categories, not typography nostalgia.
