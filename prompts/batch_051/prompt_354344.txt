I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'd like a debate I keep half-overhearing translated into the vocabulary I actually use day to day. The debate, as I understand it, is about what makes something a disease. One side, which I've seen labeled naturalism, says that health and disease are objective conditions of an organism: an organ or a psychological capacity has a characteristic way of functioning in a species, and a disease is a departure from that normal functioning that impairs the organism. The other side, which I've seen labeled normativism, says that biological function alone cannot pick out which departures count as diseases, because calling something a disease always involves a judgment that the condition is bad or undesirable, which makes health and disease at least partly value-laden rather than purely biological facts. I may be garbling both positions; if so, correct my understanding as part of your answer rather than translating a straw man.

Restate each position, one at a time, in the vocabulary of software engineering: specifications, intended behavior, bugs, feature requests, triage, severity and priority, canonical builds versus running instances, regression tests, backward compatibility, breaking changes, deprecation, telemetry, incidents, and anything else that earns its keep. Accuracy to the philosophy matters more to me than which particular terms you use.

Then give me the residue report. Which of the claims on either side map cleanly onto the way I already think about software, which ones you had to stretch or distort to make them fit, and which ones have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I especially want to know whether my working hunch survives the translation. My hunch is that the whole debate is just the difference between a bug defined against an objective specification and a bug report that is really a value-laden triage decision, and that real engineering is always triage, so the normativist side wins by default. But I suspect my vocabulary is built around artifacts that have authors and a written spec, whereas organisms have neither, and I want to know whether the residue is a defect in my framework or a weakness in the naturalist position. Write the whole thing as continuous prose only, like a careful email, around 900 to 1100 words, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
