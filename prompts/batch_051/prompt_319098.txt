I am an undergraduate in a course on the philosophy of economics and rational choice, and we have just finished a unit on Pareto criteria, status quo bias, and theories of value. I recently came across a proposal from AI safety research and would like an essay analyzing its philosophical presuppositions.

The proposal concerns how to make advanced AI systems less likely to fail catastrophically when they bargain with one another. The motivating worry is that agents capable of making irrevocable commitments might each lock in incompatible demands before learning what the other has done, leaving both worse off. The proposed response is to seek safe Pareto improvements. Informally, a safe Pareto improvement is a change in how the agents negotiate—for example, by committing a successor agent to a surrogate goal, or by committing to simulate a costly conflict rather than actually fight it—that makes all parties better off regardless of which original strategies they would have used. The improvement is meant to be safe because it avoids the wasted costs of conflict without changing the balance of bargaining power: the parties need not first agree on what would be a fair division.

I want to know what implicit theory of value and rationality this research and design practice presupposes. In particular: must someone who pursues safe Pareto improvements accept welfarism, or only the weaker view that Pareto improvements are good other things equal? Does the practice presuppose that the existing distribution of bargaining power is legitimate, or only that it is instrumentally unwise to challenge it while the priority is avoiding catastrophe? Does the word safe quietly commit the proposal to a conservative theory of legitimacy by treating any shift in bargaining power as a cost? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and identify the places where several rival philosophical views would license exactly the same design advice. End by saying what would have to change in the practice if the presupposed commitments were replaced—for example, if we held that an outcome is not an improvement unless it improves the distribution of benefits, or that bargaining power itself requires justification before it may be preserved.

You are an expert philosopher of economics, formal epistemology, and AI ethics. Before drafting your answer, brainstorm three or four distinct interpretations of what safe Pareto improvements might commit a designer to, and say which interpretation you find most defensible. Think carefully about the difference between necessary presuppositions and merely typical companions. I do not want a literature review or a reading list; I want a precise philosophical reconstruction that reaches and defends a conclusion.
