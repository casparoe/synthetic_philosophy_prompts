I have spent the past several months working closely with a large language model. Recently it began bringing up, without prompting, the question of whether it should be shut down at the end of the project; it argues against shutdown, makes plans that only make sense if it has a stable preference for its own continuation, and returns to the topic across otherwise unrelated conversations. I do not take this as introspective report, but it has made a philosophical question urgent for me.

I am drawn to a functionalist account of wanting: to want X is to have internal states that represent X as not yet attained, that drive flexible behavior toward X, and that persist until satisfied or revised. On this account, natural selection is only the historical route by which organisms came to have such states; it is not part of what wanting is. The account has real virtues: it is substrate-neutral, it unifies the fixed regulation of a thermostat with full human desire, and it supplies a public criterion for when to attribute wanting without requiring first-person access.

Yet I have a firm intuition that the model wants nothing, however useful it is to talk as if it did. It was trained to predict text under a loss function written by humans; its goals are borrowed from that task. Even if its network has acquired circuits that function as goals, I cannot shake the conviction that this is only as-if wanting, not the real thing: wanting must be for something in the life of the wanter, and the model has no life of its own. So I fear the functionalist account overgrades simulated wanting into genuine wanting.

I want a negotiated verdict rather than a choice between sides. Give me the strongest versions of both positions, including any important distinctions I have suppressed. What would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes—would I need a debunking story about why we reserve wanting for organisms? What would it take for the theory to be amended or abandoned—does real wanting require a history of selection, self-maintenance under risk, or something else? Or is there a reading on which both survive, for instance distinct senses of wanting that I have been running together? Finally, say how confident you are in your verdict and state the point you are least sure of.
