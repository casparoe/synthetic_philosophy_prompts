You are advising the judges of a constitutional court that is preparing to unify its fragmented equality doctrine by settling which classifications attract especially demanding justification. The court has been working from four familiar candidate criteria: whether the differentiating trait is immutable; whether the affected group has a history of subordination or exclusion; whether the group is politically powerless or easily outvoted; and whether the trait is relevant to legitimate legislative purposes. The judges have found that these criteria pull apart once one moves beyond the paradigm cases. Religion and language can be adopted or shed, yet discrimination on those grounds seems to the judges to call for searching review anyway. Age and disability are frequently relevant to reasonable legislative line-drawing, but treating that relevance as decisive appears to miss the distinctive injury of age- or disability-based exclusion. Severe stigma attaches to conditions such as obesity even though those affected do not form a bounded minority of the sort the political-powerlessness criterion presumes. And sexual orientation has been accommodated only by stretching first one criterion and then another, leading the judges to suspect that the criteria are being reverse-engineered from conclusions the court already holds.

The court has set aside funds for five theoretical papers over the next two years, to be written by philosophers and constitutional lawyers rather than empirical social scientists. Your task is to set the research agenda. Which five open conceptual questions about what makes a ground of discrimination suspect should the five papers address, in what order, and why? Defend the ordering as a research strategist would, not as a surveyor of the field. For each question, explain which other questions or downstream decisions depend on it, how tractable it is with philosophical and doctrinal tools, and what would have to change for you to move it up or down the ranking. Also identify at least one question that sounds fundamental but that you would deliberately deprioritize because answering it would change little in practice.

After the prose, append a JSON object with exactly the following schema, and let the JSON stand alone with no commentary outside it:

- `prioritized_questions`: an array of exactly five objects in descending order of priority, each with:
  - `rank`: integer from 1 to 5
  - `question`: a one-sentence statement of the open conceptual question
  - `upstream_of`: the downstream questions, doctrines, or decisions this question governs
  - `tractability`: why philosophers and lawyers can make progress on this question without new empirical work
  - `failure_mode`: the development or finding that would most lead you to deprioritize this question
- `deprioritized`: an array of at least one object, each with `question` and `reason_deprioritized`
- `weighting`: an object with numeric values for `importance`, `tractability`, and `downstream_leverage` that sum to 1, recording the relative weight you gave each consideration in producing the ordering
