I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in rational choice theory, about 18 students and one 75-minute session, and I want a hands-on activity on the Allais paradox and the independence axiom. The students can already state and manipulate the axiom, but they tend to file the paradox away as a quirk rather than feel it as a live challenge to the axiom's normative pull. I want the activity to make them generate the paradox themselves and then see it as a real conflict rather than a computational slip. I have in mind the classic common-consequence design with two pairs of point-lotteries, using 100 tickets per lottery and payoffs arranged so that the two options in each pair share an 89-ticket component and differ only on an 11-ticket component, one safe and one risky. The activity should have them make and lock in their choices first, then physically separate their chosen lotteries into the shared component and the differing component, and come to see from their own recorded choices that their preference between the same two components flips depending only on what the shared component pays. Use points or raffle tickets rather than real money.

Give me the full runnable design: exact rules, materials, a timeline for the 75 minutes, a payoff scheme, an anonymous way to aggregate and display the choices, and a debrief that maps each moment of the activity onto the independence axiom's cancellation idea. The debrief should not declare the Allais chooser irrational, since that will be most of the room, and it should not drift into people-are-bad-at-probability. It should stage the harder question of whether certainty can rationally matter over and above the underlying probabilities and outcomes, and what the independence axiom would have to give up if it cannot. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, such as too few students producing the paradoxical pattern in a small class or students revising their locked choices once they see the decomposition, how to handle each failure, and a candid note on what the activity cannot reproduce, real monetary stakes chief among them, and what I will have to supply myself.
