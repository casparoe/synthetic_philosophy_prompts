I've got a rule I've been running on for a while: when somebody says they had an experience that can't be put into words, I treat it as no knowledge at all. My cousin spent a week at a meditation retreat and now says the main thing she got out of it is impossible to describe, but she's still making big life choices off it. I tell her: if you can't say what it is, you don't have anything, just a strong feeling with a story attached. But it's started to bother me, cause there do seem to be people who know how to do things they can't explain, and some of them are right more then chance. Could a genuine insight actually be unsayable, and if so how would I ever tell the difference between that and somebody papering over a vague mood? What I really want to know is whether my rule is defensible as a way of spending my limited attention, or if it's just a bias against a kind of report I find annoying. And if it's partly right, is there a better version that doesn't throw away whatever an ineffable experience might actually contain?
