I was taught that one human body counts as one conscious subject just in case there's a single point of view, a single pool of information available for thought and report, and a single agency acting on the world. Now take an adult born without a corpus callosum, so his two hemispheres have never directly talked to eachother. In daily life he's fine: one job, one self, he reports a continuous stream of experience, and his hands cooperate. But in the lab, when each visual half-field gets a different instruction, his left hand points to what the right hemisphere saw while his speech says he saw the other thing and he genuinely has no idea why his hand is moving. On tasks that require comparing a stimulus across the two half-fields, he sometimes confabulates an answer. Is he one subject or two? Work through the criteria one by one; where they come apart, say whether the criteria are the problem or whether the question 'how many subjects are in there?' is the problem.
