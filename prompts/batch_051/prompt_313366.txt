I've been trying to figure out whether Dennett and Caruso actually disagree about anything more than vocabulary. My understanding is that Dennett thinks Caruso's public health quarantine model is just punishment with a halo: it still locks people up, it still reacts differently to them because of what they did, and it still carries blame-y social practices even if Caruso refuses to call them blame. Caruso thinks Dennett's compatibilist free will is too thin to do the moral work, because it can justify deterrence and maybe incapacitation but not the kind of basic desert that makes retribution fitting. But Dennett seems to deny he needs retribution, or at least denies that his view requires a cosmic kind of desert, so maybe they're talking past each other? I want someone to actually compare the underlying pictures, not just the labels. What is Dennett's actual justification for punishment, if not retribution: is it basically a consequentialist practice of maintaining a system of responsible agents, and if so why does he keep accusing Caruso of giving up something important? What is Caruso's actual justification for quarantine, and at what point does it become morally indistinguishable from punishment? Focus especially on whether a compatibilist can justify punishment as anything other than a consequentialist tool, and whether a hard incompatibilist quarantine ends up smuggling in the very desert-related attitudes it claims to reject.
