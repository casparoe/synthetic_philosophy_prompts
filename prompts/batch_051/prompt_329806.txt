Many people describe truth as worth pursuing for its own sake, not only as an instrument for better decisions. Suppose you observe someone paying a small cost to learn a fact they know in advance will never influence any of their choices. One interpretation is that this reveals a genuine intrinsic valuation of truth; rival interpretations are that the person is buying the pleasure of resolution, the novelty of the information, or relief from the itch of curiosity. Design an experiment that could separate genuine truth-valuation from these alternatives, specify which pattern of results would count as evidence for intrinsic valuation of truth, and explain what a positive result would still fail to settle.
