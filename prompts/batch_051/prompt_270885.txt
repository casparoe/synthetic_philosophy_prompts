She works on transitional justice—the South Africa, Argentina, and Rwanda cases are her usual examples—but the ones below are stylized versions I've heard her defend, with country names stripped out.

Her track record:

- She says amnesty was right for a conscript who ran the generator at a torture camp, never had contact with detainees, and gave the commission a full, verified account.
- She says amnesty was wrong for a foot soldier who beat detainees during interrogations and refused to testify.
- She says amnesty was wrong for a colonel who designed the whole interrogation system, never touched anyone himself, but gave a full account.
- She says amnesty was right for a records clerk who typed up arrest lists, had no command role, and gave complete testimony.

New case: a low-ranking interrogator who personally tortured detainees, then gave the commission a full, verified confession and named his superiors.

What should I expect her to say?
