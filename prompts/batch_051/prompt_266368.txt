My lab's been going in circles about the language of thought hypothesis for a year now. One camp is basically Fodor–Pylyshyn: thinking is computation over amodal, compositional, languagelike symbols, and systematicity is the smoking gun. The other camp is anti-representationalist/connectionist: "symbols in the head" is a leftover folk theory, and cognition is really distributed, modality-specific, statistically learned patterns. What kills me is they have run overlapping experiments and still read the results opposite ways. So I want the document they should have produced if they had actually done an adversarial collaboration: the peace treaty with the real disagreement left in.

Write the joint statement they could both sign. Please structure it exactly like this: (1) a numbered list of claims they actually agree on — about behavioral productivity and systematicity, about what any theory must explain, about what the classic results do and do not rule out; (2) a disagreements table, two columns, each side's formulation worded so that that side would say yes, that's exactly my position, you didn't strawman me; (3) a cruxes section: the subquestions where settling one would make at least one side genuinely move; and (4) for each crux, a concrete experiment or case both sides accept bears on it, with each side's prediction about the outcome written down in advance. Candidate cruxes you can use or reject: fast mapping of a novel relational predicate; cross-domain analogy with no shared surface features; whether decoding a trained net ever finds explicit compositional structure. Also flag any part of the dispute you think is mostly verbal (e.g. one side is talking about the algorithmic level and the other about neural implementation) and rewrite those bits so the non-verbal residue is left. Don't pad it with a reading list or the history of the debate; I want the content of the treaty, not it's backstory.
