I am teaching a graduate seminar on decision theory, and we have just finished Newcomb's problem and the Smoking Lesion. I want to put together a more systematic exercise on the Death in Damascus case, and I would like you to work through two variations with me.

In the standard case, a merchant in Damascus must choose between staying in Damascus (S) and fleeing to Aleppo (F). Death is a perfect predictor: if the merchant stays, Death is in Damascus; if the merchant flees, Death is in Aleppo. The merchant receives utility 1 if he ends up in a different city from Death and 0 if he ends up in the same city. Death tells the merchant, truthfully, that he has predicted F and is in Aleppo. In the standard version, CDT recommends S on the grounds that Death is already in Aleppo and the merchant's act cannot causally move him. EDT, by contrast, has trouble evaluating S because conditionalizing on S would contradict the merchant's evidence: given perfect prediction, if the merchant were to stay, Death would have had to predict S rather than F.

Now I want to test two changes. First, keep the announcement truthful but make Death's prediction imperfect: P(predict F | flee) = P(predict S | stay) = 0.95, symmetric. Second, keep Death's prediction perfect but make the announcement only 90% honest: if Death predicted F, he says F with probability 0.9 and S with probability 0.1; if he predicted S, the reverse. In both variations, the merchant knows the relevant probabilities, and for definiteness assume his prior over Death's prediction is uniform. For each variation, compute the expected utilities that EDT and CDT assign to S and F, treating EDT as using conditional probabilities and CDT as using counterfactual probabilities that hold fixed Death's actual prediction and location. Does EDT come to agree with CDT in either variation? What does the comparison reveal about whether the original disagreement is driven by the perfect accuracy itself, the truthfulness of the announcement, or the attempt to conditionalize on an act that the evidence rules out?
