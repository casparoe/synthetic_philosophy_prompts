Rideshare co-op I'm in is trying to redesign its payout rule and there's a proposal on the table I can't get anyone to poke holes in properly. Right now we pool all fares each week and pay each driver in proportion to hours logged, so in a given week everyone gets the same hourly take. One member—new guy, reads a lot of ergodicity-economics stuff—wants to kill the pool entirely. His line is that the current pool pays each driver the ensemble average of that week's hourly outcome, but no one lives the ensemble average; each driver lives the time average of their own earnings series, so the co-op should build that instead. He calls his alternative the solo pool.

Here's the mechanism he's pushing. Each driver has a personal account starting at zero. Every week the app computes that driver's own 52-week rolling average of gross weekly fares. If that week's gross fares are above their own average, they pay a 20% levy on the difference into their own account. If that week's fares are below their own average, the account pays them a top-up equal to 50% of the shortfall, but only while the account has money in it; the account can't go negative, and when it's empty they just eat the full shortfall. No money ever moves between drivers. If a driver quits, whatever is in their account is payed out to them.

I get that this is basically coerced saving plus a partial consumption-smoothing rule, but I want someone to treat it as a mechanism and walk through how it would actually behave. Who gains and who loses relative to the current pooled arrangement? What does it incentivize—around when to drive, which shifts to work, how many hours to log, and when to quit? Does a driver's account tend to stay funded or drain to zero, and why? Where do people game it first, and can the failure modes be fixed without just reintroducing cross-driver pooling? And separately: is his time-average-versus-ensemble-average point actually doing the work he thinks it is in this setting, or is he smuggling in a different policy argument with the jargon?
