In my graduate seminar on the philosophy of deep learning, we keep returning to a question I would like help sharpening. There is a long-standing skeptical position—call it the eliminativist or instrumentalist challenge to network representations—traceable to the connectionist debates of the 1980s and early 1990s. It denies that the internal states of a trained neural network are representations in any robust sense. On this view, activation vectors are distributed, overlapping, and too sensitive to changes in input distribution, training run, and task to bear determinate content; talk of a network "encoding" a concept or "tracking" a feature is at best a useful fiction imposed by the researcher, not a description of anything the system itself realizes.

Over the past decade, mechanistic interpretability has seemed to reverse this picture. Researchers have identified circuits that implement apparently modular computations, residual streams that transmit structured information among components, and directions in activation space whose ablation or amplification changes a model's behavior in coherent, semantically describable ways. Sparse autoencoders have been proposed as a way to recover monosemantic features from polysemantic neurons, and linear probes plus activation steering appear to let investigators read off and even rewrite aspects of what a network treats as salient. To a realist about interpretability, this looks like the difference between a metaphor and a science of representation; to a skeptic, it is exactly the old projection story dressed up in new engineering vocabulary.

I want a precise appraisal of whether the old skeptical argument survives this development. Which premise does mechanistic interpretability actually put under pressure—the claim that network states lack compositional structure, the claim that they are too unstable across contexts to carry content, the claim that content ascriptions are observer-relative, or something else? Can the skeptic repair the argument into a weaker but still live position, for example by insisting that the recovered features are constructed by the interpretability method itself, or that predictive usefulness does not amount to semantic content? And what would the realist need to establish for the stronger conclusion that these structures are genuinely contentful and causally responsible for behavior, rather than merely well-correlated summaries? I am less interested in a verdict than in a careful mapping of the logical terrain.
