I'm currently taking a philosophy of mind course on consciousness and the self, and we have been reading about Thomas Metzinger's self-model theory of subjectivity. I came across a popular science article that summarizes the view, and I want to check how much of it I should trust. The article says roughly this:

'Metzinger's central claim is that the self is an illusion. There is no substantial, enduring self behind our experience; what the brain actually produces is a "phenomenal self-model," a continuously updated internal representation of the organism as a whole. The defining feature of this model is "transparency": we normally experience through the model rather than experiencing the model itself, so its representational character is not introspectively available to us. Because the model is transparent, we cannot, even in principle, notice that it is a model, and we are permanently stuck with the impression of being a unified, non-physical ego. Out-of-body experiences are supposed to support the theory, since they show that the spatial content of the self-model can come apart from the actual body. It follows, according to the article, that if the self is only a transparent model, then there is, strictly speaking, no one who is conscious and no one who can be held responsible for their actions.'

Most of this overlaps with what the lecturer said, though the lecturer called the relevant feature "phenomenal transparency" rather than simply "transparency," and the final moves strike me as too strong. I suspect the article is running several distinct claims together. Could you go through this summary and tell me what a careful defender of Metzinger would accept, what they would want to qualify, and what is simply wrong? In particular, please separate the following threads: (i) the claim that the self is an illusion; (ii) the claim that the phenomenal self-model is transparent; (iii) the further claim that transparency makes it impossible in principle for us to experience the model as a model; (iv) the role out-of-body experiences are supposed to play; and (v) the conclusion that the theory implies there is no conscious subject and no one who can be responsible. I would like to know where the summary merely simplifies, where it overstates a more qualified thesis, and where the conclusions fail to follow from the accurate parts. If the theory is right that the self is an illusion, what exactly is the illusion about—the existence of experience, the existence of a subject, or only a particular picture of what the subject is?
