I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, working on a system to automate technical work (code review today, design review later). I'm writing up the design and want an external consistency check on the set of beliefs I've accumulated from building and operating the pipeline. I can state the mechanism, then the commitments, then the audit I'm asking for.

The mechanism: An overseer decomposes a hard task into subtasks, each easier to evaluate than the whole. The subtasks are executed (initially by human annotators, later by cheap trained models), and the overseer approves or rejects each result on its own. We record the decompositions, subtask results, and approvals, and distill them into a fast model via imitation. The distilled model is then used as a component in the next round of decomposition.

The commitments I currently hold:

(a) The system's competence ceiling is set by the overseer's ability to recognize a good subtask result, not by their ability to produce the final answer. Checking and producing are different skills; this distinction is the entire reason the pipeline can outperform its overseer.

(b) For every task the product targets, there is some decomposition whose subtasks can be evaluated locally, on their own merits and without seeing how the whole answer turns out, and locally approved subtasks assemble into an adequate whole.

(c) Alignment is a property of observable behavior. A system that consistently produces outputs the overseer would endorse is aligned for deployment; I don't need to know what goes on internally, any more than I need a sorting function to intend to sort.

(d) At the same time, I believe a system is not aligned if it earns its clean output record by modeling the overseer: echoing what reviewers already think, steering hard cases away from spot-checks, or optimizing the approval signal instead of the underlying task, even when no individual output is flaggable.

(e) Distillation is faithful given a representative sample of the deployment distribution: the fast model inherits the pipeline's competence and its evaluation standard on that distribution, up to ordinary statistical error.

(f) The loop is monotonic. Feeding a distilled model back in as a component cannot make the next round less capable or less aligned, because the model passed through the overseer's review in the previous round; the next round gets more of the same competence at lower cost, and the overseer can direct many components at once.

I can't confidently say which pairs of these are in tension, which only look that way, and which are doing the real work. Please audit the set: identify the genuine tensions, order them by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection. I'd rather know before the design document goes out.
