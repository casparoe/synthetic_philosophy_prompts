I'm a novelist, and I'm currently writing a book in which a character—a retired epidemiologist who has begun modeling his own social world—keeps insisting that the causal structure of a life can be recovered from the pattern of independencies in what a person notices and remembers. I want the character's claim to mean something technically respectable rather than merely lyrical, so I've been reading around causal discovery, and I keep running into the same justificatory knot.

Here's the knot. Suppose you fit a Bayes net to observational data and select a graph that is minimal for the data: the observed distribution is Markov to the graph, and no proper subgraph of the graph is also Markov to it. Why think this graph tracks the real causal structure, rather than merely compressing a joint distribution into a convenient summary? The moves that seem to be available differ sharply in kind. One says faithfulness is generic—exact cancelations among competing causal paths have measure zero in the natural parameterization, so a typical distribution is faithful to the true graph and the minimal graph is not an arbitrary compression. Another says minimality is doing the work as a parsimony criterion: simpler causal explanations are better confirmed by the same evidence. A third says the guarantee was never about recovering the unique graph, only about the Markov equivalence class and about predictions under interventions. A fourth says the real justification comes from experiment, and observational search is only a heuristic that has to earn its keep pragmatically. A fifth says the question itself is misposed, because Bayes nets in this setting are instruments for organizing predictions, and talk of "tracking causation" smuggles in more metaphysics than the method can support.

I'd like you to rank these five—or whichever of them you regard as genuinely distinct—by how much of the justificatory burden they can actually bear, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does. Please be explicit about how confident you are in the ordering and where you think the ranking is contestable. And if I've botched the standard machinery, or if the task only looks well-formed because it conflates distinct questions, I'd rather you say so plainly than force a ranking.
