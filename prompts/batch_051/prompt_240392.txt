I've been going through the self-torturer and some variants and I can't find a principle under which my verdicts all hang together. Heres what I actually think. (1) Original dial case—a thousand increments, each adds a pain increase too small to notice, each pays ten thousand dollars—every individual step is rationally fine to take, but I'd reject the whole package if it were offered as one choice, and if I landed at the top I'd pay to ratchet back down. (2) Same dial but only ten increments, each jump clearly noticeable and worse than the last: I'd stop around the fourth or fifth and think going past that is just irrational. So the original puzzle seems tied to the increments being indiscriminable, not to weakness of will. (3) If I know in advance exactly where the sharp threshold is—say step 600 is where it turns from bearable to awful, and I'll know it when I'm there—then it's rational to go to 600 and stop; the puzzle mostly disappears. (4) But in a clean money-pump cycle, where I strictly prefer X over Y, Y over Z, and Z over X by wide clear margins, I dont think the cycle is rational just because each pairwise switch looks good; I think I should fix the preferences instead. Is there one account of rational stepwise choice that gets all four verdicts, and if not, which one should I give up? And what does your principle say about a version where I can buy, in advance, a binding device that stops me at whatever setting I choose?
