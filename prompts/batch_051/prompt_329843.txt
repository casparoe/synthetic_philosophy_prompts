I've been kicking around a view about grounding I haven't seen spelled out anywhere. Call it the Omission Test: a fact p grounds a fact q iff p can't be omitted from any correct, complete metaphysical explanation of q. Not some explanation—every one. The idea is that if you can explain q correctly and completely without p, then p was never doing grounding work; it was just along for the ride. It also gives an account of fundamentality: the fundamental facts are the ones no correct complete explanation of the world can leave out. I know it's rough and maybe circular, and I can't tell if it's original or collapses into something obvious. Give me the three strongest objections and say which, if any, are fatal rather than just needing a patch. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, no restating the question. American English.
