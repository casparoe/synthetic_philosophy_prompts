I keep going back and forth on why promises bind, and I wrote out an argument that feels too quick. I half suspect step (4) moves faster than step (3) allows, but I cant make the objection stick. (1) When I promise you that I'll do X, I lead you to rely on my doing X. (2) If you reasonably rely on that and I don't do X, I wrong you. (3) Wronging someone is violating an obligation owed to that person. (4) So the promise puts me under an obligation, owed to you, to do X. (5) To be under an obligation, owed to you, to do X just is what it is for the promise to bind. (6) Therefore promises bind. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and can the argument be repaired by replacing that step with the strongest true version of it — or does the conclusion end up weaker than it looks?
