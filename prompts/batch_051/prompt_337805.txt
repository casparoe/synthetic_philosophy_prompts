Many Western cities have treated statues of figures whose actions are now widely condemned as objects that may need to be removed, while street names, university buildings, and endowed positions honoring the same figures often remain in place, and while ancient monuments from slaveholding societies are displayed in museums without any serious campaign for their destruction. What theory of the purposes of public commemoration does this pattern presuppose? In particular, reconstruct the implicit distinction between a statue in a public square, a street name used daily, and an artifact in a museum, and say which of these differences could bear the moral weight the pattern assigns to them. Then distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely tolerates, and identify the points where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that more than one theory of commemoration could license it.
