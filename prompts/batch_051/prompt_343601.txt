I've been reading Kant's Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, the bit where he distinguishes price from dignity—roughly, a thing with a price can be swapped for an equivalent, but something that has no equivalent has dignity rather than price. I keep hitting an asymmetry that seems to be tracking that distinction but maybe isnt. Most people, including liberal legal systems, say a person should not be allowed to sell a kidney, even if theyre poor and fully informed: the body isnt a commodity. But the same person can take a job as a saturation diver or a coal miner and trade years of health for pay, and nobody says the state should outlaw it, even though it also looks like putting a price on the body. Is there a principled line between these cases using Kant's price/dignity distinction, or is the kidney ban just a moral hang-up weve institutionalized? Give the strongest version of the distinction, then say whether it can actually carry that weight or whether the asymmetry should be given up.
