I am an undergraduate in an upper-level seminar on collective decision-making, and I have been asked to lead the session introducing the unilateralist's curse. I would like a 75-minute structured activity for 12 students. By the end, participants should see—because it happened to them in the game, not because I announced it—that a group of individually reasonable people, each acting on partial information and each able to act unilaterally, can systematically make choices the group would reject if it pooled its information, especially when acting wrongly is the more dangerous error.

The activity should give each participant private evidence about whether a prospective action would be good for the group as a whole, using some concrete choice such as whether to release a sensitive finding or take an irreversible step on everyone's behalf. Anyone may act unilaterally, but the group bears the consequences collectively. The payoffs should make acting when the action is bad substantially more costly than refraining when it is good, so that a demanding individual threshold is already rational. Even so, in the unilateral condition the group should systematically act too readily, and this should show up in its accumulated score rather than in my explanation. Build in a contrast: some rounds should use a unilateral rule and some a pooling or voting rule before anyone may act, so the difference between the two emerges from the outcomes.

Please provide exact rules and roles, a scoring function with concrete numbers, a private-signal mechanism using cards or slips of paper, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief script that maps specific moments in the game onto the parts of the idea: private information, the group's effective judgment being set by its most optimistic member, asymmetric costs, and the way the most cautious members' information gets washed out. Also say what will predictably go wrong when the game is run—for instance, participants comparing signals or drifting into coordination—and how I should handle those moments during the session rather than in the debrief. End with an honest note on which feature of the unilateralist's curse the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply by description.
