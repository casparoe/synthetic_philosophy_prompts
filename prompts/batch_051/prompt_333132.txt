I am a political philosophy PhD student writing a chapter on whether prioritarianism can absorb the concerns that usually motivate egalitarians. I have set down the following six commitments, and before I build the chapter around them I want a consistency audit: which pairs genuinely pull against each other, how severely, and what has to give. Please rank the tensions rather than treating every apparent conflict as real.

(1) Welfarist currency. The justice of a state of affairs is determined solely by the distribution of individual well-being across persons.

(2) Prioritarian weighting. The social value of a gain in well-being is greater the lower the absolute level of well-being of the recipient; the weighting function is continuous and strictly concave, with no threshold below which gains are lexically privileged.

(3) Rejection of intrinsic equality. Leveling down, making the better off worse off while no one becomes better off, cannot make a state of affairs better in any respect.

(4) Choice/luck sensitivity. Inequalities that can be traced to the parties' voluntary choices (how much to work, what risks to take) may be just even if they remain uncompensated, whereas inequalities traceable to brute luck generally call for correction.

(5) Relational grounding. I have also come to think that what is fundamentally objectionable about many inequalities is domination, servility, and unequal social standing, and that distributive inequality matters, when it matters, because of these relational effects. I assume this can be made consistent with (1) and (2), as a story about why distributive inequality is bad in the first place rather than a rival principle.

(6) Strongly redistributive policy. I take steeply progressive taxation and something like universal basic services to be requirements of justice, not merely generous aims.

Which pairs are in real tension and which only look as though they are? For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, which commitment is the cheapest to amend, and what the amendment would be; then tell me which single revision would leave the set most defensible overall.
