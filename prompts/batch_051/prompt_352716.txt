A reading group argument I keep going back and forth on, because it seems to show something nobody would accept. (1) If our best scientific explanation of some empirical regularity uses a mathematical fact ineliminably, then we should believe in the objects that fact is about. (2) The best explanations of why periodical cicadas have 13- and 17-year life cycles and why honeycomb cells are hexagonal use mathematical facts essentially: primes minimize overlap with shorter predator or parasite cycles, and hexagons minimize perimeter for a partition into equal areas, so bees use less wax. (3) So we should believe in numbers, or at least in primes and whatever abstract objects the geometry is about. (4) The same principle applies to anything our best explanations use ineliminably, not just mathematics. (5) But our best explanations also use idealizations—frictionless planes, point masses, infinite populations in population genetics—and we do not believe in those. (6) So (1) is false, and cases like the cicadas and honeycombs give no support to platonism. I'd like to keep (1), and I'm certainly not going to start believing in point masses, so something has to go. Where exactly does this argument go wrong, and what does rejecting that step cost me?
