My sister recently found out that the person who attacked her years ago can't be prosecuted anymore — the statute of limitations has run out. I understand the practical side of that. But here's the asymmetry I cant stop thinking about. The same people who say it's too late to do anything legal still treat him as fully responsible for what he did. Nobody talks as if the wrong itself has weakened or expired. If it was serious then, it's serious now, and no one trusts him any less in year twenty-five than in year two. Yet a lot of those same people, me included, are also comfortable saying the state should drop it. So time is supposed to eat away at the legal response while leaving the moral judgment completely intact. Is there an actual principle that holds those two things together? Specifically, what would the passage of time have to be doing — erasing the wrong, eroding the wrongdoer's responsibility, undermining the state's reasons, or just making prosecution impractical — for this asymmetry to make sense? And if nothing really does the job, should we keep the asymmetry anyway, or admit its just convenience wearing a moral costume?
