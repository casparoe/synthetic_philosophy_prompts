For the past several months I have been following the dispute over the statehood of the territory where a member of my family lives, and I have noticed a rule in my own reasoning: when there is disagreement about whether something is a state, I treat recognition by a substantial portion of established states as decisive, no matter how effectively it governs its population, controls its borders, or maintains order. A colleague objects that this makes a claim about what something is depend on how outsiders happen to regard it. I am unsure whether the rule is a defensible way to classify an essentially institutional thing, or whether I am confusing the diplomatic consequences of statehood with its underlying conditions. Is the rule defensible, and if not, is there a revised version that keeps whatever is sound in it?
