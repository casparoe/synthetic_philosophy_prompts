Some ubuntu-style views say personhood isn't a biological given: you become a person by being in relations of mutual recognition, reciprocity, and shared form of life, and a human who lacks these relations is a person only partially or not at all. Here's a case I keep flip-flopping on. A woman lives entirely alone, avoids friendships and community rituals, and helps others only anonymously—she repays every debt, leaves food on a neighbor's porch, and refuses to be known or thanked. Her behavior looks morally serious, but there's no relationship in which anyone recognizes her or can reciprocate. Under the criteria above, is she a full person, a partial person, or not a person at all? Work through it criterion by criterion, and say where the criteria themselves might need changing rather than the case being forced into them.
