I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I've been asked to write a short "where I stand" note on the frame problem for our research group, so the others can poke holes in it before I give a seminar. I work mostly on predictive processing and not directly on the frame problem, so their may be obvious tensions I'm missing; what I'd like from you is an audit of whether the following commitments actually fit together, rather than a lecture on the frame problem itself.

My commitments are roughly these. (1) I take the philosophical frame problem seriously in Dennett's sense: it's the question of how an agent determines which of indefinitely many things it knows are relevant to what it should do or infer now, without explicitly checking each one. (2) I don't think this is just an artefact of the situation calculus or logicist AI; I think even large language models and connectionist systems face a displaced version of the same problem whenever their behaviour has to stay coherent over time. (3) My positive leaning is that structured defaults—schemata, frames, scripts—are the right kind of answer: once the right schema is active, relevance mostly falls out of it. (4) But I also think selecting which schema to activate is itself a relevance problem, and adding more schemata mostly moves the problem up a level, so schemata seem at best a partial fix. (5) I'm fairly convinced embodiment matters here: a genuinely situated agent can use the world as its own model and let perception and the environment carry a lot of the relevance constraints, making the problem tractable in a way it isn't for a disembodied reasoner. (6) I also like the idea that satisficing and bounded rationality dissolve much of the problem: an agent doesn't need to determine relevance over all its beliefs, it just needs a good-enough action under time pressure, and local heuristics can deliver that. (7) And yet, at the level of principle, I want to say the frame problem has no algorithmic solution: there is no procedure that, for arbitrary stored beliefs and goals, computes what is relevant.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which are only apparently in tension. For each genuine tension, order them by severity, tell me what would have to give, and what the cheapest fix would be. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most across the whole set. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
