I'm a retired chemist, and I've been sitting in on a seminar on collective rationality because I wanted to see what social scientists thought about a phenomenon I used to worry about in lab consensuses. The instructor has a recurring exercise where she gives a scenario and one question: is this an information cascade or not? She's ruled on a good many of these, and I've kept a log because I'm trying to reverse-engineer her criteria. Here are the rulings I have so far.

Two restaurants, no prior reason to prefer one. Diners arrive one at a time, and each gets a private, 60% reliable tip about which is better. The first diner picks A, the second picks A. The third diner's tip says B, but after seeing two A's she picks A. Instructor: cascade. A company adopts fax machines mostly because so many other companies already have one; it would adopt even knowing the machine was mediocre, because the point is being compatible with everyone else. Instructor: not a cascade, it's a network effect. An Asch-style line-judgment subject can see the correct line but goes along with the group's wrong answer. Instructor: not a cascade, that's social conformity. A traveler in an unfamiliar city, with no private information at all, follows the longer queue into a restaurant. Instructor: rational observational learning, but not an information cascade, because he had no private signal of his own to suppress.

Now the case I want classified. My daughter is an internist, and her department is deciding whether to adopt a new treatment protocol. Two nearby hospitals with good reputations adopted it and report good outcomes. Her own reading of the studies is mildly negative; left to herself, she would not adopt. After hearing about the other two hospitals, she sides with adopting. But she also says that if the studies had been strongly negative, she would have refused. I'd like you to predict the instructor's verdict on this case, identify which rule or definition of a cascade she is implicitly running, and tell me which earlier case is the closest precedant. Is it the strict rule where your own information has to be entirely swamped, or a looser one where it's enough that your private information is being put under pressure by information you're borrowing from other people's choices? If her prior rulings don't settle it, tell me what further case I would need to ask about to settle which rule she has in mind, and which way her likely rule would push. I'd rather hear the reasoning than a clean verdict, since I keep going back and forth.
