I've always seen the Frege-Geach problem taught as one big objection to expressivism, but I'm starting to think it's a loose bag of smaller problems. Take a deliberately toy expressivism: 'stealing is wrong' expresses disapproval of stealing, and it doesn't report a fact. Now take these contexts a moral sentence can show up in:

- negation: 'stealing is not wrong'
- conditional antecedent: 'if stealing is wrong, then encouraging it is wrong'
- attitude report: 'Jon thinks stealing is wrong'
- tense: 'slavery used to be wrong'
- probability: 'stealing is probably wrong'
- question: 'is stealing wrong?'
- quantification: 'there is nothing wrong with eating dessert'

Rank the seven from most damaging to least damaging for the toy expressivist, and give each a score from 0 (no real problem) to 10 (fatal on its own). Then say whether any one of them, alone, refutes the toy view, and whether the standard Frege-Geach problem is really one problem or several.

Format it as: 'Ranking and scores' (a list), 'Why each score' (one or two sentences per context), and 'Bottom line' (one short paragraph).
