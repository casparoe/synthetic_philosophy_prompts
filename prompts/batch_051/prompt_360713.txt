I'm on the board of a small nonprofit that runs a short-term cash assistance program for a county—roughly fifty low-income families get a few hundred dollars a month, no strings attached. A friend on the board has been pushing to add conditions, and he recently went through our optional spending logs and came back with a list. One guy spent a chunk of his payment on lottery tickets and vape cartridges while he was behind on rent; a woman spent part of hers on DoorDash and cigarettes the same week she said she couldn't afford her kid's school supplies. His conclusion is that this is basically proof that no-strings cash only works for the deserving subset, so we should require rent to be paid first, a certain number of job applications per week, or a budgeting course, to filter out the people who will waste it. My usual line is that people are the best judges of their own lives, but that feels a little to pat here, since some of these choices really do look self-defeating. Is there a principled case for keeping the payments unconditional even after seeing this stuff, and what does that case have to say about the minority of recipients who genuinely do blow the money?
