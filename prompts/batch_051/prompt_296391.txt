I host a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest defends the Lucas–Penrose argument: the claim that Gödel's incompleteness theorems show the human mind cannot be identified with any consistent formal system, so mechanism about the mind is false. He's a good guest but he's very good at deflecting the standard objections with two moves: the one about how the objection only works against machines we can name, and the one about how you're confusing provability inside a system with mathematical insight from outside it. I don't want an episode where he just restates the view; I want the questions to make those two moves progressively less availabe.

Write me nine or ten interview questions for him. I want at least one question each on: (1) the assumption that human reasoning is consistent and that we can know it is; (2) the point usually credited to Benacerraf, that the argument at most rules out a machine the person can identify and prove sound, while mechanism only requires that there be some machine, not that we know which; (3) the possibility that the mind is a formal system whose program we simply cannot enumerate; and (4) Penrose's later shift from Gödel unprovability to the separate claim that mathematical understanding is non-computable. For every question, give me the follow-up you'd ask to the reply the guest is most likely to give.

Format the whole thing as JSON. The schema: a top-level object with "questions", "closing_question", and "sequence_note". "questions" is an array of objects, each with four fields: "question" (the question text), "target" (which weak joint or standard objection it presses), "likely_reply" (the answer you expect the guest to give), and "follow_up" (a follow-up keyed to that specific reply). "closing_question" is one question that stays hard even if the guest has wriggled out of all the earlier ones. "sequence_note" is one or two sentences on why the questions are in the order they are.
