I'm a cog-sci PhD student and my lab keeps going in circles about Dennett's old Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction, and I want to see whether the newer psychophysics actually kills it or just moves it around.

The setup, for people who haven't stared at it: you flash a red dot, then shortly after flash a green dot a little to one side. What people report isn't two dots in sequence; it's one dot moving across and switching from red to green roughly midway, apparently before the green dot is even shown. Dennett's point was that you can model this two ways. The Orwellian version says the brain briefly misrepresents the dot as red along the trajectory, then revises your memory of the episode before you can report it. The Stalinesque version says the brain waits, constructs the seamless story, and only then lets you have the experience. He thought there's no fact of the matter about which is right: both produce identical reports and the question only looks answerable if you assume a Cartesian theater where experience happens at a determinate time and place.

Here's the development that's messing with my head. The vision people I know keep bringing up backward masking, flash-lag, and other postdictive effects (where a later stimulus changes how an earlier one is seen) as evidence that conscious perception works in windows of something like 100–200 ms, and that within such a window the system genuinely delays and rewrites the episode before it gets "published." If that's right, it sure looks like the Stalinesque model just wins empirically—the delay isn't a philosopher's construct, it's how the hardware works.

So does Dennett's arguemnt actually survive that? I need someone to say exactly which premise the integration-window stuff bears on: the premise that the two models make all the same predictions, the premise that there's no privileged time and place where experience occurs, or the premise that the experience/memory boundary is so labile that the question is ill-posed. Is this a case where the distinction becomes empirically decidable after all, or is the psychophysics just re-describing the same thing at a different level? And if Dennett's argument does need repair, what's the strongest version of it that's still left standing?
