I've been turning over Frankfurt cases and I've arrived at an argument that seems to prove too much. I need to know which step to reject and what rejecting it costs. By the principle of alternative possibilities, I just mean: someone is responsible for what they did only if they could have done otherwise. I'd most like to keep two things—the standard Frankfurt verdict that Jones is responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise, and the idea that ordinary agents sometimes act from a real capacity to do otherwise. But here's the argument.

(1) In the standard Frankfurt case, Jones shoots Smith on his own. Black has secretly implanted a device that would have fired and made Jones shoot if Jones had shown the neural sign that precedes a decision to refrain. The device never fires. So Jones couldn't have done otherwise, but he's responsible.
(2) The only thing that removes Jones's alternative possibilities is the counterfactual fact that, had he been about to refrain, the device would have intervened. His actual deliberation is exactly like a normal one.
(3) For any normal action, the same sort of fail-safe could be attached: a sufficiently advanced device could be wired to whatever neural precursor the agent's actual deliberation has, set to force the same action if the agent starts to do otherwise.
(4) Since the actual deliberation is the same in both cases, and the only difference is whether such a counterfactual setup happens to hold, the presence of the fail-safe can't be what distinguishes an action with alternative possibilities from one without.
(5) So every ordinary action is on a par with the Frankfurt case: each lacks alternative possibilities, since the same fail-safe could have been attached.
(6) Therefore no one ever could have done otherwise, and the principle of alternative possibilities would imply that no one is ever responsible for anything.

Where does this go wrong? I can't tell whether the problem is in the earlier steps or the later ones, and what I'd have to give up about abilities or counterfactuals to block it.
