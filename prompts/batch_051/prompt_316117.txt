I got into it recently with a linguist who insists that a signed system only counts as a real language if it has what they call duality of patterning: a level of meaningless units (handshape, location, movement) that recombine into meaningful units, the way sounds do in speech. They then use this to wave off the home sign systems that deaf children invent when there's no established sign language around, dismissing them as merely gesture. I cant tell whether that criterion is a genuine empirical test or a way of pre-defining home sign as not-a-language. So: how would you actually test a home sign system (or any small signed system) for duality of patterning? Give me a concrete study design, the results that would favor each reading, and an honest account of what a clean result would and would not settle about calling the system a language.
