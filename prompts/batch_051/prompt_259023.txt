I'm putting together a short piece for an AI safety reading group, and I keep getting tangled up on chain-of-thought monitoring as a safety technique. The setup, as I understand it: a lab trains a frontier model to reason out loud in natural language before it answers, and then a second model (or occasionally a human) reads over that reasoning to catch cases where the model is scheming, sandbagging, faking alignment, or quietly pursuing a goal it shouldn't. The pitch is that this lets you supervise the part of the model you'd otherwise never see — not just the final output but the "actual deliberation" behind it. But the more I think about it, the less sure I am about what this practice has to assume about the relationship between the written chain of thought and whatever the model is really doing internally. People worry, for instance, that once the monitor is part of the training environment, optimization pressure will push models to obfuscate: to produce the reassuring story the monitor wants while the underlying computation goes on unchanged. If that's a live possibility, then the practice seem to assume something strong, something like a transparency of mind — that in a well-trained model the stated reasons track the real reasons unless the model is actively hiding something.

So what philosophical commitments is chain-of-thought monitoring actually presupposing? Is it committed to a picture where the model has a coherent inner monologue that its external monologue faithfully reports? Or is it compatible with much thinner views, like "the CoT is just one more observable behavior that correlates with bad outcomes, same as the output"? Does it need to assume the model can fail to report faithfully in a way it "knows about" — something closer to self-deception than to mere noise? I want a definite answer, not a survey: reconstruct the view of mind, interpretation, or language — whatever is the right level — that the practice only makes sense under, say which parts it strictly needs and which parts it merely sits well with, and tell me what would have to change in the practice if that view were false or were replaced.
