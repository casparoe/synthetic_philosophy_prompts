I am a novelist drafting a near-future novel in which teleportation—destructive scanning of a body at departure and exact reconstruction at arrival—has become common for long-distance travel but is still publicly contested. Many characters believe the person who arrives is the survivor of the person who left; many believe the traveler dies and a duplicate takes their place. The legal system in the novel neither endorses nor rejects either view. Instead, it has built an institution around the only thing the contending parties can mostly agree matters: the continuity of commitments.

The institution is called the Continuity Registry. Before any teleportation, the traveler files a sealed Continuity Statement naming the single most important commitment they currently hold and the person or persons to whom it is owed. The traveler also places three months of their income in escrow. One year later, the post-trip person may claim the escrow only by swearing that the named commitment is their own and will be honored, or by repudiating it. If the commitment is affirmed, the Registry records it as a continuing obligation that follows the person through all future teleportations and can be invoked by the named beneficiary. If it is repudiated, the escrow is paid to the named beneficiary or, failing that, to the next of kin of the person who stepped into the scanner. A panel of five people who knew the scanned person before that trip can, by a majority, void the post-trip statement on the grounds that it was fabricated or coerced, in which case the escrow goes to a public continuity fund.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than as a moral or metaphysical position. Analyze what it would incentivize among travelers, their families, employers, creditors, insurers, and the panelists; how and where it would be gamed first; what second-order effects it would have on how people in this society make and keep promises, marriages, debts, and contracts; and whether the worst failure modes could be fixed without destroying the institution's purpose.
