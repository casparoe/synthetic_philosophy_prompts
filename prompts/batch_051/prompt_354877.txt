Two people in my decision theory reading group have been at each other's throats over Pascal's mugging for weeks. One of them thinks expected utility theory is broadly right, and that if the mugger's threat has any nonzero probability, the rational thing really is to hand over the money, however daft it feels. The other thinks the example is a reductio: we should bound utilities, or round tiny probabilities down to zero, or otherwise stop the formalism dictating that we pay up. Write the document they should have produced as an adversarial collaboration, with three sections. First, a numbered list of claims both can sign: what standard expected utility theory actually implies in the mugging, which premises do the work, where they think the standard presentation is fair to both sides. Second, a disagreements table stating each residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own view. Third, a section on cruxes: for each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, and say in advance what each side predicts. One crux should be whether rejecting the mugging verdict forces you to reject ordinary precautions against extremely unlikely but severe harms (seatbelts, insurance, asteroid deflection, that sort of thing). Seperate the sections clearly, and write the whole thing in British English.
