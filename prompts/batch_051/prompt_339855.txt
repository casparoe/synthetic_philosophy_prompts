Ever since my daughter was born I've been in a weird headspace about the far future — I now viscerally care about what 2200 is like in a way I never did before, and I keep turning over whether a political system should actually be built around that, or whether building it around that is precisely how you end up with a smiling vanguard running everything. So here is the concrete counterfactual I want your argued prediction on.

Take a wealthy north-European style parliamentary democracy: twenty million people, high trust, proportional representation, a codified constitution, and an independent, powerful constitutional court. In 2025, after two decades of climate disasters and a couple of near-misses with engineered pathogens, a supermajority amends the constitution to create a Council for the Long Future. Six members; drawn by lot from a vetted pool of climate scientists, demographers, biosecurity people, risk philosophers, macroeconomists and military planners; confirmed to a single non-renewable 14-year term by a two-thirds parliamentary vote. The Council has one formal power: it can suspend any newly enacted statute for eight years, renewable once, if it publishes a reasoned forecast that the statute's expected effect on the welfare of people alive a century or more from now is significantly negative. It can also petition the constitutional court to strike down legislation under a new clause saying the state is trustee of the conditions of long-run human flourishing. It cannot propose its own bills, has no regulatory authority, no enforcement arm, and a budget just large enough for modelling and reports. Nothing else in the system changes.

Now run it forward in your head to 2075 and tell me what the political settlement looks like — not as a story, but as a chain of mechanisms, incentives and constraints. Specifically: who tries to capture or use the Council, and how; how governments route around it (writing laws the forecasters can't model, hiding long-run damage in budgets, using emergency decrees that never lapse); what kinds of laws actually get suspended and which ones quietly mutate instead; how the court reads the trustee clause; and whether the Council ends up a de facto upper house, a toothless think tank with robes, or a Praetorian guard with spreadsheets. I want you to mark each step as robust or a guess, and say which single fact about the world would most change your prediction. And because the thing I'm really worried about is whether caring about the far future ends up licensing control of the present for people who cant vote these folks out, tell me how much of that worry your prediction supports.
