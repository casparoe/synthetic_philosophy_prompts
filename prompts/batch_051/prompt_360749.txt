For my philosophy club we did a unit on personhood, and I had to write down my actual comitments so the rest of the group could roast them. I sort of landed in a relational view (the ubuntu-ish idea that you become a person through other people), but I kept a bunch of my old views too. Here's the list I gave:

1. "Person" and "human being" aren't the same thing; being a person is a moral status, not just a biological fact.
2. You become a person by being treated as one, and that status is constituted by being caught up in relationships of care and recognition with other people.
3. A newborn baby is a person from birth, even though it can't recognize or answer back to anyone yet.
4. Someone with advanced dementia who can't recognize anyone is still a person, full stop.
5. Someone stranded alone on an island is still a person, and still has moral rights.
6. Rights are basically things a community grants and protects; outside any community there are no rights, just whatever you can defend for yourself.
7. Whether it's wrong to harm you doesn't depend on how smart, aware, or socially skilled you are.
8. A community can be wrong about who is a person — a group that's denied personhood doesn't stop being persons just because no one recognizes them.

Go through these and tell me which ones actually conflict and which ones only look like they conflict. Rank the real conflicts from worst to least bad. For each real conflict, say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most. I dont want a lecture on the right view, I want an audit of my own set.
