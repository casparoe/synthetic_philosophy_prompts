I sit on the allocations committee of a small private foundation that has historically funded basic science, and I'm on the hook to write the decision memo for this cycle's most divisive case. The applicant is a computational cosmology group asking for three years of supercomputer time and two postdocs to simulate the first billion years after the Big Bang: how the first stars and galaxies got going and how their light reionized the surrounding gas. The proposal is weirdly honest — it says up front that there will be no technological spinoffs and no data product that's useful outside astronomy. Our new strategic plan says we fund research with a demonstrable impact on human flourishing, and half the committee thinks that simply rules the proposal out. The other half thinks impact includes the kind of understanding this simulation produces, even if it never helps anyone build anything. I then stayed up way too late arguing with a friend about it; he kept saying I was smuggling in the claim that knowledge is valuable for its own sake without actually defending it, and I kept saying that by his standard we'd have to defund the whole history of cosmology, which made me feel better than it should have.

Here's the extra knot: the external review says the simulations will probably confirm the standard picture rather than overturn it. We're not even buying a headline discovery, just a more detailed map of a period we already roughly understand. So the philosophical question is whether incremental pure understanding has the kind of value that justifies funding on a human-flourishing mandate, or whether intrinsic value, if it exists, only attaches to discoveries that transform how we see the universe. I need to circulate a short memo before Thursdays meeting. Act as a philosopher of science who works on the value of pure research. First, brainstorm the two or three strongest considerations on each side, including ones my friend and I probably fumbled. Then draft the memo itself: it should recommend fund, reject, or fund with conditions, give a concrete bottom line we can vote on, and engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously rather than just gesturing at spin-offs.
