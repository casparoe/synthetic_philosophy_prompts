I'm a software engineer working on a reputation system for a marketplace app, and the whole project keeps stalling on a dumb question: what actually is a reputation? Not what a score is, or what a rating is — what the underlying thing we're trying to measure is. If I ask my coworkers they say "you know, reputation," but when I press them with edge cases we all end up disagreeing. So I want a working definition of reputation, the kind of thing I could in principle turn into a spec, or at least use to tell whether some feature is measuring reputation or measuring something adjacent like popularity or trust or visibility.

Here's my current set of test cases. A seller with 8,000 completed sales and a 99.4% positive rating clearly has a reputation. A brand-new account with zero history clearly does not. The ones that breaks every definition I try: an account with an enormous karma score earned almost entirely from low-effort memes; a bot that answers support questions correctly about 95% of the time and carries a "trusted" badge; a person who is widely known around the office as unreliable, i.e. has a bad reputation; a hole-in-the-wall restaurant that regulars recommend but that has no online footprint; a politician whose public image is almost entirely manufactured; and someone who buys a high-score account and keeps the same username. For each of these I want your definition to force a definate yes or no — it counts as reputation or it doesn't — and if the answer seems wrong on its face, defend it rather than hedging. If your first definition has nothing to say about one of them, revise it until it does. Also, if you have any explanation for why reputation feels graded even though I keep wanting to store it as a boolean in the database, that would help me stop going in circles.
