I work in a clinic embedded in a larger reimbursement system, and I keep noticing a tension I can't quite articulate. To get treatment funded — and often to get treatment at all — a patient needs a DSM or ICD diagnosis. That diagnosis is generated by structured interviews and symptom-count thresholds: five of nine symptoms for at least two weeks, plus clinically meaningful distress or impairment, and so on. The label then travels with the person: it unlocks medication, therapy, disability accommodations, sometimes a legal status. The whole machinery seems to presuppose that a diagnosis picks out a definite underlying condition, already there in the person, waiting to be detected by counting symptoms.

But the same field around me increasingly says the categories are provisional conventions. The manuals themselves describe most disorders as syndromes rather than etiologically validated entities; the boundaries are acknowledged to be fuzzy and dimensional; researchers routinely propose replacing categorical diagnoses with dimensions, spectra, or staging. And then there is a third phenomenon: the labels do something to the people who receive them. Patients encounter communities organized around a diagnosis, absorb a vocabulary for their experience, begin describing themselves in its terms, and sometimes reorganize their symptoms around the classification. Some return months later having made the label central to their identity, and their later symptoms and behaviors look shaped, not merely measured, by the category they were given.

I want to know what philosophical commitments this practice actually presupposes. Does the reimbursement-and-counting machinery commit psychiatry to the view that mental disorders are real, stable, mind-independent kinds, or is it compatible with the idea that the categories are pragmatic, historically contingent groupings? Does it strictly require that view, or merely sit comfortably with it? And how would the practice have to change if the act of classifying is itself causal — if the category changes the phenomenon it names?
