I've been teaching the free will unit in my intro course and keep getting stuck on a structural similarity I can't decide about, so I'd like an outside read.

Take Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. As I understand it: you act from the way you are, so to be truly responsible for what you do you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are. But you can only be responsible for the way you are if you chose to become that way, and that choice would itself have to issue from some prior way you were, for which you'd again need to be responsible, and so on. The only thing that could stop the regress is something that is its own cause — causa sui — and nothing can be that. So nobody is ultimately responsible for anything.

Now here's the thing. The old Agrippan trilemma about justification has what looks to me like the same skeleton. A belief is justified only if it's supported by a reason; that reason is justified only if it's supported by another; and any chain of reasons must either stop at an unsupported starting point, go in a circle, or continue forever. Each horn seems to forfeit the very thing that was demanded. That's supposed to push you toward skepticism about justification unless you pick your poison.

The parallel I can't shake: both arguments take a demand that looks innocent — be responsible for what you do / have reasons for what you believe — then show that satisfying it requires satisfying it again at a deeper level, with only three unpalatable exits. I'm trying to figure out whether this is a genuine identity of structure or just two regresses that happen to share a shape. If it is genuine, does anything transfer? For example, if you're a foundationalist about justification and think some beliefs are justified without being inferred from further beliefs, is there an analogous move for responsibility — some feature of a person that's a brute starting point and doesn't itself need to be chosen — and is that move any more plausible there? And if you're a coherentist about justification, what's the responsibility analogue, mutual support among your traits, and does that even make sense? Conversely, what would the parallel predict that you'd expect to find but don't?

I realize these might just both be instances of something more general about grounding and regress, and the interesting question is wich disanalogies matter more than the similarities. Please answer in American English.
