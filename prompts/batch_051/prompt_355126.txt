Since I retired from the lab I've been in a philosophy reading group, and one regular has very firm views about rights. I've been keepin score. She says the government may quarantine someone with a deadly infectious disease who refuses to isolate, and may ban a speech telling a mob outside a house to go get the person inside. She says the government may not take a healthy person's kidney to save a dying patient, even by lottery and with high odds of success, and may not torture a suspect to find a bomb. She also says breaking into an empty cabin to survive a blizzard is fine as long as you later pay for the damage. Next week we're arguing about a pandemic law that would temporarily conscript doctors and nurses into understaffed hospitals: lottery-based, normal pay, fines for refusing. I'm less intrested in whether you'd approve than in whether she should, if she wants to stay consistent with her own record. Is the doctor draft, by her lights, closer to quarantine or to the kidney case? Which rule is she actually running—rights as trumps, rights as side constraints, or rights as instruments—and which single further case would best separate the two rules her past verdicts still can't tell apart? Does rights talk earn its keep here, or would a plain harm principle do just as well?
