Take Searle's Chinese room thought experiment, but move the question from the person inside to a person outside who uses the room as a black-box translation device. Say a monolingual English-speaking academic has a device that turns her English into flawless written and spoken Chinese. Over a decade she gives Chinese conference talks, answers audience questions, and becomes known as a fluent Chinese speaker in her field. She always knows the content she intends to get across: she types an English sentence, reads out whatever Chinese comes back, and watches people's reactions to see if it landed. But she could not tell you which of the sentences she has produced means the conference is postponed and which means she resigns. Run the original argument with its roles shifted. Does it force the conclusion that she doesn't understand Chinese either, or does her having the intended meanings in her head make her importantly different from the man in the room? What would the systems reply say about the user-plus-device as a single system, and why does that matter for the larger question of whether leaning on an AI to produce text we can't check erodes or merely relocates understanding?
