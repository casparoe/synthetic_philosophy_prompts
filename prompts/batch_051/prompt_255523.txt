I keep running into the claim that a quota and affirmative action are totally different things, and I can't tell wether the difference is real or just a branding issue. A quota is a hard number: Norway says boards have to be 40% women, India reserves a fixed share of university places and government jobs for Scheduled Castes and Tribes. Affirmative action, at least the American kind, is supposed to be softer — no set numbers, just race as one factor among many in a whole-file review. But once you look at what these policies are actually trying to justify, the line gets blurry. Compare the two ideas for me. Is the quota/affirmative-action distinction doing any real moral work, or is it mostly about making one sound scary and the other sound reasonable? And if it is doing real work, where exactly does the justification change when you move from a hard number to a soft nudge? I'm less interested in the legal labels than in whether the underlying arguments — compensation, diversity, equal opportunity, whatever people reach for — actually come apart between the two, or whether they're the same arguments wearing different clothes. Use the Norway case as a check: if a gender quota on boards gets justified one way and an Indian caste reservation another way, does that tell us the quota/affirmative-action line was never the interesting distinction?
