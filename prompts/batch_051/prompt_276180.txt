I am drafting an essay on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the objection that pleasures do not obviously share a single felt character in virtue of which they are all good, and that treating them as commensurable may already beg the question. The piece will center on the following passage.

Jeremy Bentham, An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation, Chapter IV:

“II. To a person considered by himself, the value of a pleasure or pain considered by itself, will be greater or less, according to the four following circumstances:
- Its intensity.
- Its duration.
- Its certainty or uncertainty.
- Its propinquity or remoteness.”

Here are the working notes for the essay.

Title: “Bentham’s Scale and the Missing Homogeneity Premise”

Core argument:
1. Bentham takes “intensity” to be a dimension along which any pleasure can in principle be compared with any other. But making that comparison requires that the pleasure be individuated and isolated in a way his own later discussion of fecundity and purity quietly refuses.
2. A pleasure’s intensity is not a single dimension across kinds. Is the warmth of reconciliation more intense than the relief of an obligation discharged? That question is not settled by introspection alone; it is partly a normative and cognitive question about what counts as the pleasure in each case.
3. Bentham might reply that people do make such trade-offs all the time. But practical substitutability can be explained by expected downstream consequences of choosing, not by the presence of one shared hedonic quantity. The exchange rate may come from the choices, not from an underlying common currency.
4. Therefore the heterogeneity objection is not simply an appeal to “higher” and “lower” pleasures. It is the claim that Bentham’s scale is undefined until a theory of hedonic kinds is supplied. If that is right, the problem affects quantitative hedonism generally, not only Bentham’s presentation.

Please comment on these notes as comments on the essay-in-progress. I want a committed response: take a definite position on whether the central argument succeeds, and defend it, rather than laying out possible moves and leaving me to choose. In particular, is step 2 carrying too much weight? Can Bentham accept that “intensity” is not a univocal felt property across kinds and still construct a scale from pairwise choices? What is the single strongest objection to my position, and what would it take to meet it?
