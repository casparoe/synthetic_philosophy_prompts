For a seminar paper on panpsychism, I need a specification of what would count as an adequate solution to the combination problem—not a survey of proposed solutions, but the full list of requirements any candidate must meet. Assume microphysical entities are subjects of experience, and lay out the constraints a solution must satisfy: how a single unified macro-subject could arise from many micro-subjects rather than a mere collection; why only some composite systems (brains rather than tables or thermostats) have macro-level experience; how macro-level experiential characters such as pain or hearing a chord could be composed of micro-experiences unlike them; and how macro-phenomenal properties can be causally relevant without conflicting with physical causal closure or overdetermining micro-level causes. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a commitment an emergentist panpsychist could reasonably drop. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate candidate solutions—for instance, subject-summing, a primitive phenomenal-bonding relation, or strong emergence of macro-subjects—and explain why. In particular, assess whether a solution may make the composition conditions primitive (some systems combine, others do not), or whether that simply relocates the mystery.
