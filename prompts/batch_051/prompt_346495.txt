Ok so I'm pulling together my view on practical reason for a seminar and I want a consistency check before I present, because I've got the sense I've been papering over something but I can't locate it. Here's the package:

(1) My own well-being over my whole life gives me reasons, and not merely as an instance of the general good going up by one. The fact that the life is mine matters.
(2) The overall good also gives me reasons, and these don't depend on it making me happy or on my having already endorsed benevolence as a personal project. If I can make things go better or worse, that itself counts.
(3) Both strike me as the kind of thing Sidgwick meant by self-evident: they survive reflection wether I occupy my own standpoint or drop back into a detached, impartial one, and I can't rationally get rid of either. I also don't buy debunking stories that let me keep exactly one of them.
(4) I think they can genuinely conflict — how much to give, whether to donate a kidney, whether my career is for my own project or the common good — and when they do there's no third principle that says which side wins. It's a real dualism of practical reason, not just uncertainty about the facts.
(5) But I also think practical reason is in the business of telling you what to do. An account that says, across a whole class of cases, there is no all-things-considered answer feels less like a theory of action and more like a description of two standpoints.
(6) And I refuse the usual patches: that they converge in practice once you count sympathy, reputation, the warm glow; that the general good secretly includes a special weighting for me; or that one principle is just a heuristic for the other. Those all strike me as false or as changing the subject.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad the real ones are. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most and what it costs.
