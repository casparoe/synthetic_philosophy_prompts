At a church council meeting last week someone ended a debate about whether the praise band could stay by saying 'whatever is not commanded is forbidden' and, when pressed, added 'the regulative principle, look it up.' His argument was that because no verse commands electric guitar in the service, the guitar has to go. I've since heard the same line used against Christmas services, clapping, choir robes, you name it. I'm inclined to agree with him about the guitar, honestly, but the principle seems to be doing way more work than that one sentence can carry. Can you take the slogan apart and lay out the different precise claims it could be making — commanded by whom (God? scripture? the elders?), how explicitly (a specific verse, a conclusion deduced from scripture, just a broad theme?), over what range (the whole service, only the elements of worship, all of life?), and what 'forbidden' actually means (sinful, unauthorized, simply not a thing the church may require)? Some people try to soften it by adding 'or whatever follows by necessary consequence from scripture,' and I can't tell if that's a clarification or a loophole. For each precisification, say whether it's trivially true, substantive and defensible, or just implausible, and which one the guitar argument actually needs. And is there a version wich gets the same guitar result but lets Christmas services through?
