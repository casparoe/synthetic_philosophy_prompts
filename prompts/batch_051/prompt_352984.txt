Last night my roommate and I got into it about the little building co-op we have: everyone's asked to put $15 a month into a kitty for shared tools, cleaning supplies, and the roof garden, and the guy in 3B refused, but he's out there grilling in the garden every evening. My roommate called him a parasite. Ten minutes later she mentioned she's never gone to a single tenants-union meeting but she'll be first in line if the union's rent-freeze campain wins, and she genuinely saw no problem with that. I found I had both reactions too, but I couldn't say why they feel different.

Can you construct the strongest principle you can that either draws a stable line between the garden guy and my roommate, or says one of those judgments should be revised? It can't just be that one is illegal and one isn't: neither is illegal. I want to know how much work is really being done by things like the size of the group, whether someone looked you in the eye and asked, whether your individual contribution would tip anything, how visible the benefit is, and how cheaply the cooperators could exclude you. Then give me an honest verdict: is this a real moral distinction or mostly a psychological one? And test your principle on a third case: someone who moves into the building after the garden was already built and paid for, and never offers to cover their share of the original setup. Are they a free rider too, and should we blame them?
