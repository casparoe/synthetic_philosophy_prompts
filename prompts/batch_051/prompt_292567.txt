Right, I need a poem for a dataset of conceptual prompts. I'm a novelist and I've gotten weirdly hung up on Skolem's paradox: a countable model of set theory can satisfy the sentence that says their is an uncountable set, because uncountability isn't absolute — inside the model there's no bijection from the naturals onto the set, but from outside the whole model is countable, and the bijection exists, it just isn't an object the model can see. Write a villanelle where the two refrains are the inside-voice and the outside-voice on this. One refrain should be the set-theoretic universe speaking from inside, insisting the set is uncountable — there is no enumeration, and the model is right about that locally. The other should be the metatheoretic voice from outside, saying the whole universe can be listed by the naturals after all. The refrains alternate and then land together in the final two lines, because the point is these perspectives don't meet inside any one place; the poem's closure is the only place they sit side by side. Don't name-drop Skolem or Löwenheim; let the refrains do the work. It should be accurate enough that a philosopher of logic wouldn't wince. Begin the poem immediately, no preface or preamble, and then give a short note after it saying where the poem takes liberties with the paradox or leans on a contested reading of it — and commit to your choices there, don't just hedge.
