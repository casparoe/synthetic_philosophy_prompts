I keep getting the sense that luck egalitarianism and tort law are running the same query in different vocabulary, and I can't decide whether this is a real structural identity or just two fields reaching for a generic chosen/unchosen line. In luck egalitarianism, bad outcomes that were never a reasonable bet on your part (brute luck) are supposed to be compensated, while losses you knowingly gambled on (option luck) are yours to absorb. Tort law draws a similar line in several places: strict liability makes a defendant absorb a loss the plaintiff never chose to risk; the assumption-of-risk defence and old-style contributory negligence (which barred recovery entirely) leave a loss with the plaintiff who knowingly or carelessly courted it; and the eggshell-skull rule says a defendant must take the plaintiff's unchosen frailties as she finds them. Give me the most charitable version of the mapping between these two distinctions, then say whether it is deep or superficial. If it is deep, which tort doctrines should transfer to luck egalitarianism (should comparative negligence push luck egalitarians away from all-or-nothing compensation in mixed cases?) and which would transfer badly because the underlying projects differ? Also name at least one thing the analogy seems to predict about one side or the other that turns out false.
