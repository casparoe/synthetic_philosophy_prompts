Not long ago I agreed to serve on the jury of a national prize for the visual arts, and a dispute has arisen that I have been asked to help settle. One of my fellow jurors is a curator of drawings and prints who has spent forty years looking at works on paper and who has been completely unable to see color since birth. Apart from the absence of color, her visual acuity and her discrimination of line, tone, texture, and composition are excellent. Another juror objects that she cannot be a competent judge of visual art as such, because one of the central dimensions of the art form is simply unavailable to her. The chair, knowing I have some training in aesthetics, has asked me to prepare a short assessment of the objection.

The working criteria for a competent judge, as I understand them from Hume's essay on the standard of taste, are something like these: soundness of the relevant sense faculties; delicacy of discrimination; practice with the art form in question; breadth of comparison across works and traditions; and freedom from prejudice. I have been treating these as individually necessary and jointly sufficient for someone to count as a good judge of beauty in an art form.

The case is this. The curator can judge line, composition, tonal values, and handling of materials with unusual precision, and she can situate an unfamiliar drawing in its tradition after comparing it with thousands of works on paper. But she has never had color experience; she knows about color only through theory, descriptions, and the testimony of others. The prize covers painting, works on paper, photography, and installation.

I would like you to go through the criteria one by one and say whether she qualifies as a competent judge of visual art for this prize. In particular: does soundness of the relevant sense faculties require that all the sensory channels an art form typically uses be functioning, or only those needed for the particular works under judgment? Is delicacy a single general capacity, or is it domain-relative, so that someone can be a delicate judge of drawing while remaining no judge at all of color? Does breadth of comparison within a restricted corpus compensate for an entire dimension of experience one has never had? And if she fails the criteria, is that a discovery about her or about the criteria — for example, that the standard of taste should be indexed to a medium rather than to an art form as a whole?

Please say how confident you are in the overall verdict, and identify the point in your reasoning that you are least sure of.
