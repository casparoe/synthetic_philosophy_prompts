I'm running a one-off discussion group on Zhuangzi's butterfly dream next month. The group is a mix of curious adults: a few have read a bit of Daoism, but most of them only know the rough gist that Zhuangzi wonders whether he was a man dreaming he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming he is a man, probably from a meme. I don't want to just do the standard "how do you know you're not dreaming?" thing, because they'll immediately slot it into Descartes and get bored. I want to teach the actual conceptual move in the Zhuangzi chapter, and I want to build the whole session around a passage they won't know from the same chapter, in James Legge's translation of Zhuangzi's "The Adjustment of Controversies":

"like music from an empty tube, or mushrooms from the warm moisture, day and night succeed to one another and come before us, and we do not know whence they sprout. Let us stop! Let us stop! Can we expect to find out suddenly how they are produced?"

I'm not sure how to sequence it. What idea has to be in place before the butterfly passage doesn't just sound like a stoned shower thought? Which example should carry the whole thing, and why not the standard butterfly/Descartes comparison? What misconceptions will they bring or form—I'm predicting "Zhuangzi thinks reality is an illusion" and "he means the self doesn't exist"—and at what point should I head each one off? What should I explicitly refuse to explain in this setting? And how do I know by the end whether they've understood the difference between Zhuangzi's dream skepticism and "brains in vats" skepticism, rather than just nodding along?
