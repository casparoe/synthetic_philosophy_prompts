I've somehow ended up running a film club and next week is Nolan's Inception, and I keep getting stuck on a debate I half-remember from a philosophy of mind class about whether dreams are experiences. The Malcolm side says a dream is not an experience you have while asleep: while you're asleep you're not aware of anything, so the idea of a temporally extended private movie playing through the night is confused, and the dream is really just the waking impression you have when you report it. Dennett, I think, doesn't exactly go back to the commonsense view; he says introspection alone cant settle whether you really had an experience during sleep or whether the whole thing got loaded into your memory at the moment you wake up. It seems to me Inception is doing something strange with all this. On the one hand, the film obviously needs dreams to be experiences — the crew is inside them, time passes at different speeds, people build enormous detailed worlds and remember them afterwards. On the other hand, the film is obsessed with the idea that from inside you cant tell whether you're dreaming, and that dream content gets designed and planted rather than just lived. I'd like a reading of the film through this debate: where does it just beg the question against Malcolm, where does it actually dramatize something Malcolm or Dennett were getting at, and does the film add anything to the philosophical debate that the arguments alone miss? Also, say how confident you are in the reading and single out the interpretive claim you are least sure of.
