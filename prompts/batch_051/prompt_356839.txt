A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about whether an AI system can be instructed to avoid side effects without a prior, complete specification of what is valuable. By the end I was defending a version of the low-impact approach: that side effects can be treated as reductions in the set of futures the agent or another party could still bring about, that one can measure such reductions relative to some baseline action and a broad space of possible goals, and that penalizing these reductions yields systems that behave cautiously and reversibly without needing a full theory of value. My friend's persistent objection was that the baseline, the state representation, and the goal space all smuggle in normative choices, so the approach only relocates the value specification rather than eliminating it. I answered that the relevant claim was never that the specification is value-free, only that it is far thinner than a value function and can be varied to check that conclusions do not depend on it.

I would like a map of what would count against the position I defended — not the crude version on which any low-impact metric is discarded at the first counterexample, but the qualified version I have just described. What observations, formal results, or failure modes would show that it is false, or true only vacuously, or mostly beside the point for alignment? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish objections a sincere defender would concede as counting against the approach from objections they would dismiss as repairable details of a particular implementation. Among the candidate defeaters I want considered are: cases in which minimizing the impact measure still produces behavior any reasonable person would describe as a serious side effect, or in which the measure flags behavior anyone would regard as harmless; the possibility that the baseline needed to compute impact is where the real normative work is done; exploitation or instability when effects are delayed, distributed across many actions, or divided among multiple agents; and tensions with corrigibility, shutdown, or repeated assistance tasks. I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position, in its most careful form, has retreated to the point of being unfalsifiable, and whether that retreat is a problem or simply the ordinary condition of an engineering concept. I suspect I would personally be most moved by a constructive near-miss case, but I want to know whether such cases are inevitable in principle or only artifacts of a badly chosen measure.
