Suppose that starting around 1950, the United States had developed an informal social norm with roughly the strength and pervasiveness that the Jantelagen norms are often said to have had in mid-century Scandinavia: a standing suspicion of people who put themselves forward, claim to be exceptional, court fame, or conspicuously succeed. Under this norm, visible ambition, self-promotion, and open displays of unusual wealth, talent, or achievement meet with mockery, social distancing, and quiet punishment rather than admiration. Change nothing else: property rights, patent law, elections, markets, tax rates, immigration, the distribution of ability, and the objective payoffs to success all remain exactly as they actually were. What changes is only the everyday social evaluation of success.

Predict the United States of 1990 under this counterfactual. I am less interested in a one-word verdict about whether the country would be better or worse off than in a visible chain of reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints. Structure your answer as follows.

1. Base rates (about 150 words). What do comparisons between the United States and the Nordic countries, and the empirical work on tall-poppy syndrome, actually license us to expect when a culture stops celebrating conspicuous success? Which effects are well-grounded in such comparisons, and which effects are illegitimate to infer from them?

2. The mechanisms (about 200 words). Identify the specific behaviors the norm taxes (for example founding a company, running for office, performing for a mass audience, publicizing a discovery, displaying wealth) and trace the exact channels through which the tax operates: anticipated shame, reduced willingness of others to cooperate, exclusion from networks, self-censorship, or something else. Distinguish the effect on the average actor from the effect on the marginal or exceptional one.

3. Domain-by-domain predictions (about 350 words). Work through technology and entrepreneurship, popular entertainment, sports, academic science, and politics. In each case, say how 1990 differs from its actual counterpart, and where the norm fails to bite because the rewards are too large, the audience is too remote, or the relevant community is insulated from local disapproval.

4. Robust and fragile links (about 200 words). Which of your predictions are you most confident of, and which depend on further assumptions, such as how much American achievement was driven by immigrants, by outlier individuals, or by institutional incentives rather than social approval? Assign explicit confidence or uncertainty to the main links rather than pronouncing globally.

5. Ways the prediction could be wrong (about 150 words). Give the two or three most plausible ways the counterfactual world could have compensated: subcultures that re-valorize success, elites who exempt themselves from the norm, immigration continually importing outsiders who do not enforce it, or the norm itself decaying under affluence. Say what would have to hold for each to overturn your central predictions.

Bottom line (one paragraph). Summarize your overall prediction and your confidence in it, including the largest remaining uncertainty.
