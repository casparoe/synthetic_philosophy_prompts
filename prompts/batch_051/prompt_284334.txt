I am writing a paper on the limits of memetics, and I keep stalling on one move that the literature I work with treats as settled. In cultural-evolution work, the gene–meme analogy is doing more than illustrative service: it is used to license importing the whole toolkit of population genetics—replicators, fitness, mutation, drift, selection coefficients—into the study of cultural change. The claim isn't merely that culture changes in ways loosely reminiscent of evolution, but that a meme is a replicator in something close to the technical sense, so cultural change is a Darwinian process in good standing and can be modeled as such. I would like an audit of this analogy, not a survey of who holds which position.

Please give me the correspondence table first: what in culture plays the role of the gene, the phenotype, the environment, fitness, drift, and mutation; and what each item in the source domain quietly imports that may not be present in the target. Then work through the three places I suspect the mapping breaks. First, biological replicators are copied with high fidelity, while cultural items seem to blend, recombine, and get revised mid-transmission, so discrete high-fidelity copying may be a rare limiting case rather than the norm. Second, the fitness of a meme seems to be set largely by surrounding memes—beliefs depend on toolkits, jokes depend on genres, rituals depend on cosmologies—so fitness is frequency-dependent and co-evolving to a degree that may make the Darwinian machinery circular, or at least not independently explanatory. Third, cultural innovation is often directed: people deliberately alter a recipe, a legal code, or a ritual with a goal in mind, whereas standard biological mutation doesn't anticipate the needs of the organism. For each disanalogy, tell me whether it matters for the intended use of the analogy, whether a defender has a principled reply available rather than merely a defensive one, and how much damage it really does. Finally, give me your candid verdict: once the disanalogies are on the table, does meme survive as a technical term, or is it a vivid metaphor that has been asked to carry an argument it cannot support? If there is a better analogy, or a way of keeping the Darwinian insights about cultural change without the replicator commitments, say what it is.
