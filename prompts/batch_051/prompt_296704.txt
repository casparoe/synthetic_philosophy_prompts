Hey — I'm an undergrad taking an aesthetics seminar right now, and I also sit on the editorial board of our campus arts-and-philosophy magazine. We've got a submission I need help turning into a decision. It's a long critical essay about a one-off, site-specific dance piece that was performed exactly once last spring in an empty warehouse, never filmed, never restaged. The author is upfront in a footnote that she never actually saw it. She says she reconstructed the work from detailed written accounts by three people who were there, plus an interview with the choreographer and the the program notes, and she argues you can write genuine criticism from that alone — especially since the work doesn't exist anymore to be experienced anyway. Some of the board thinks it's a sharp piece and that testimony is a perfectly legitimate basis for aesthetic judgment; others think you can't genuinely evaluate an artwork you never encountered firsthand, and publishing it would mislead readers no matter how good the prose is. I need to draft the board's memo: publish, reject, or publish with conditions. It should have an actual bottom line, the reasoning behind it, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, written so the author could respond to it. Oh, and please keep it in American English.
