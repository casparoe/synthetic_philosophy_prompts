Yesterday a friend and I argued at length about our company's recommendation system. It is trained end to end, and he now claims that every residual error shows that no lower-level component really understands the task, so we should add a hand-designed controller that corrects the system from above. I replied that he was reaching for a skyhook — positing an intelligent corrector rather than explaining how that competence could itself be built by bottom-up cranes such as iterated gradient updates and accumulated heuristics. He answered that my insistence on treating the system as nothing but weight updates was greedy reductionism, refusing to acknowledge its real functional organization. We never settled whether the skyhook/crane and good/greedy reductionism distinctions really map that cleanly onto the end-to-end versus modular/planned divide, or whether the analogy was doing more rhetorical than structural work. Is the parallel deep or superficial? If it is deep, what must hold on both sides for results to transfer, and what is one clear prediction of the analogy that turns out false?
