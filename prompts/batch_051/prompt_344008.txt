Next week I present to my philosophy-of-time reading group a paper defending a probably-obnoxious thesis, and I want to walk in ready for the fight. The thesis: presentists and eternalists both read McTaggart backwards. His claim in "The Unreality of Time" that a bare B-series cant give you change, if it works, is a problem for the standard eternalist/detensed story first; presentism ends up looking like the view that actually delivers what he says time needs. So I'm presenting McTaggart as an unwilling ally of presentism, not as the guy who showed time is unreal.

My argument is simple enough. I read this passage from J. M. E. McTaggart, "The Unreality of Time" (1908), as stating an adequacy condition on any theory of time, namely that it make room for genuine change and not just variation across a static series:

"It would, I suppose, be universally admitted that time involves change. A particular thing, indeed, may exist unchanged through any amount of time. But when we ask what we mean by saying that there were different moments of time, or a certain duration of time, through which the thing was the same, we find that we mean that it remained the same while other things were changing. A universe in which nothing whatever changed (including the thoughts of the conscious beings in it) would be a timeless universe. If, then, a B series without an A series can constitute time, change must be possible without an A series. Let us suppose that the distinction of past, present and future does not apply to reality. Can change apply to reality? What is it that changes?"

I then argue that the detensed B-theory fails that condition for exactly the reason McTaggart gives, while presentism passes it because it keeps the A-series in the ontology. The moving spotlight person complicates this, since she keeps A-properties too; my line is that she keeps the letter of the A-series but not the spirit, because there's still a static block underneath doing the real work.

The audience: two committed eternalists, one a hardline B-theorist who thinks "now" is just an indexical and all the real work is done by the B-series, and one the moving spotlight person who thinks there are real A-properties that march along the B-series. There's also a presentist who thinks the argument from change is the weakest possible defense of presentism and that I'm setting us up to get demolished, plus a few agnostics. The organizer knows McTaggart cold and has strong opinions about what he "really meant," so I'll also get pushback on the exegesis.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way those specific people would phrase them (especially the two eternalists), with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest link in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope nobody spots it. And as you go, separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this — I dont want to walk in announcing a contested reading of McTaggart as if it were the consensus.
