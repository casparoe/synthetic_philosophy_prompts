Here's a pattern I can't get my head around. In the US you can donate plasma twice a week and get paid for it; there are plasma centers in poor neighborhoods that basically function as cash-for-blood businesses. Egg donors get advertised with five-figure compensation, and in several states you can pay a surrogate to carry a pregnancy. But selling a kidney is a federal crime, selling your vote is a crime, and after a hurricane most states make it illegal for a store to jack up the price of bottled water. So we're fine with some markets in the body and in things people urgently need, and horrified by others that look, on the surface, pretty similiar. What therory of what makes a market repugnant does this pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory as charitably as you can, then say whether the pattern strictly presupposes it or is just compatible with several different theories. Is the line drawn in a coherent place, or is it more of a historical and political accident? And if we swapped that presupposed theory for a straight welfarist one (roughly: a market is fine iff it leaves someone better off and no one worse off), what specific policies in the pattern would have to change?
