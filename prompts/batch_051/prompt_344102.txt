I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule one of my characters runs on gratitude. She's drawn from my friend Dana, who has these little verdicts. Dana says you owe real gratitude to a cousin who lends you rent money without making a fuss about it. She says you don't owe it to a landlord who fixes your heater the same day, because that's his job. She says you do owe it to a coworker who covers your Saturday shift mainly to impress the boss, and to a neighbor who mows your lawn because he wants to try out his new mower. She says you don't owe it to a driver who totals your old car and the insurance leaves you better of then before.

The new case I want to write is this: a guy in her writing group mentions a well-paid translation gig he's passing on; it costs him ten seconds, she lands it, and it saves her year. Would Dana say she owes him gratitude? I'm less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule she's actually running, the other rule or rules that fit her old verdicts just as well, and what further case you'd use to tell those rules apart.
