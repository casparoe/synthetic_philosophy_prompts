I play a little game with my friend Rina where I describe a pill that would delete one very specific negative feeling, then ask whether she'd take it for some concrete moment in her life. So far her record is weirdly consistent. She would not erase the grief she still feels about her father, who died three years ago—she says it's just love in a different key. She would not erase the regret about a cruel thing she said to her sister the week before her sister moved away, because she says the regret keeps the memory of that conversation honest. She would not erase the sadness that hits her every June around the anniversary of a close friend's climbing accident. But she would erase the week-long despair after getting rejected from all five PhD programs; she says it was pure tax that told her nothing she didn't know by the end of day one. She'd erase the disappointment from losing her rec-league volleyball final, and she'd erase the two-day funk after a performance review she agrees was accurate.

Here's the new case I haven't asked her about yet. Three years ago she left a relationship that had turned emotionally abusive, but she still loves, in some way, the man she originally thought he was, and she's told me she doesn't regret having loved him. She still gets waves of grief over the life she had planned with him, and once described the feeling as the relationship throwing a shadow over her. I can't figure out the rule. On one read, she keeps a negative emotion exactly when it's how an attachment she still values shows up, and erases it when it's just the by-product of a failed aim—that predicts she keeps the grief about the ex. On another read, she keeps a negative emotion only when its picture of the situation is accurate enough to trust, and erases it when it either tells her nothing new or misrepresents things—that predicts she erases it, because the grief idealizes a relationship that wasn't that. And I keep thinking there's a third rule about whether she experiences the feeling as her own rather than as an intruder. What's your best prediction of her verdict on the ex case, which rule best fits her track record, and what one further case would cleanest separate the candidates?
