I have been stuck going in circles between two colleagues, one of whom works on monetary institutions and keeps treating money as the paradigm case of a socially constructed kind, and another who works on gender and insists the paradigm is practice-bound ascription without any rulebook. Each can produce cases the other's account handles badly, and I want out of the loop. Before I read or deploy any more candidate theories, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of socially constructed kinds would have to do, pitched so it cannot quietly favor money over race and gender or the reverse.

Write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than one camp's prejudice. At a minimum, the list should force a theory to: distinguish and fix the sense of construction being claimed (causal dependence, constitutive dependence, and so on); explain how a kind can be both mind-dependent and entirely real in its consequences; stay consistent with what human genomics tells us about biological variation without flattening the ways racial and gender classifications actually organize lives; handle interactive kinds, where being classified feeds back into both the classified and the classification; explain how constructs achieve stability and come to present themselves as found rather than made; and either unify rule-governed cases like money with practice-governed cases like race and gender under one mechanism or justify treating them as systematically different without defining the hard cases away. The purpose I have in mind is a theory adequate for explaining how such kinds organize social life and for guiding judgments about when an entrenched kind can be revised or abolished, so the criteria should also speak to which features are open to deliberate change and which are not, and whether they should vindicate error-theoretic claims or vindicate the lived reality of the kinds while explaining their constructedness.

Then turn the same scrutiny on the list itself. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, or can be satisfied together only by weakening one of them, and explain where the tension comes from. Tell me which single requirement is most likely to eliminate the usual candidate theories — rule-first accounts of institutional facts, practice-and-ascription accounts, and hybrid accounts that treat different kinds as constructed in fundamentally different ways — and why. Do not score the candidates in detail; I will do that myself, and I need the criteria sharp enough that the scoring will not be question-begging.
