Christopher Nolan's The Dark Knight culminates in the Joker's two-ferry test: each boat is given a detonator for the other boat, and the Joker announces that if neither has destroyed the other by midnight, he will destroy both. Read this sequence through the lens of superrationality, as Hofstadter applies it to symmetric mixed-motive games. The Joker frames the choice as a race to defection, but a superrational analysis would have each boat ask which action it would want the other to perform in a fully symmetric situation, and then perform that same action. Does the film actually dramatize a superrational resolution, or does its resolution depend on group deliberation, moral thresholds, and asymmetries between the civilian and prisoner boats that Hofstadter's model abstracts away? Where does the scene illuminate something superrationality gets right, where does it resist the reading, and what would a faithful critic conclude the film contributes to the philosophical debate that argument alone does not?
