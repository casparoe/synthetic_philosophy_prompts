I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have been trying to work out whether a passage in Kant has any grip on a practical disagreement on my team. In Kant’s Fundamental Principles of the Metaphysic of Morals, in Thomas Kingsmill Abbott’s translation, the following passage appears:

“Everyone must admit that if a law is to have moral force, i.e., to be the basis of an obligation, it must carry with it absolute necessity; that, for example, the precept, ‘Thou shalt not lie,’ is not valid for men alone, as if other rational beings had no need to observe it; and so with all the other moral laws properly so called; that, therefore, the basis of obligation must not be sought in the nature of man, or in the circumstances in the world in which he is placed, but a priori simply in the conception of pure reason”

Treat that passage as a rule and apply it to a case its author did not foresee.

The case is this. My team maintains an autonomous agent that trades in a simulated financial market. Its action policy is a deep network trained by reinforcement learning, but the agent also runs a separate internal check on candidate policies. The check flags a proposed policy if the agent’s own model says the policy could not be accepted as a general policy by all agents in the market. The engineers disagree about what this check is. Some think it is just a learned regularizer that happens to resemble universalization. Others think it is doing real work of the sort Kant’s passage is after. The system can revise its intermediate goals, but it has no felt preferences or consciousness as far as anyone can tell, and its entire constitution is an artifact built from data.

Please apply the passage to this case. Specifically:

(1) What exactly does the passage require of a being before a moral law can have force for it?

(2) Does the passage rule the agent out because its “nature” is an engineered artifact and its training data are empirical circumstances? Or does it leave room for the agent to count if the internal check is functioning in the right way?

(3) If the agent were later shown to have no consciousness or felt preferences, would that matter for whether it is a rational being in the sense the passage uses, or is sentience irrelevant to the passage?

(4) What would I need to observe in the agent’s computations, or in how its policies are formed, before changing the assessment in either direction? I want a close application of the passage, not a general essay on AI ethics.
