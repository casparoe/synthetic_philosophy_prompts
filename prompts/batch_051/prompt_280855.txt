I teach a first-year historiography workshop and keep watching students talk past each other: some think explaining an event means subsuming it under a general law, others think it means telling a story that makes the outcome intelligable, and a third group thinks it's just tracing the particular causes. I want a quick day-one sorting quiz — which theory of historical explanation do you actually hold? — with five or six questions, each built around a small invented historical case (a mutiny, a harvest failure, a bridge collapse, a war that nearly started), and three answer options per question corresponding to covering-law explanation, narrative/colligation, and singular causal tracing. Include at least one item where the covering-law option is plainly unsatisfying because no serious law is available, and one where the narrative and causal-tracing options pull in different directions. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto covering-law monist, narrative pluralist, causal mechanist, and across-the-board pluralist; flag any pattern nobody would plausibly hold and diagnose it in one line. Sort readers by how they explain cases, not by which slogan they already like, and keep it to about ten minutes.
