I'm in a philosophy of time seminar, and we've been working through Gödel's rotating-universe solution and the idealist conclusion he drew from it. For a short reading I'd like a strict Petrarchan sonnet. The octave should render the model: dust rotating against the local compass of inertia, the dragging of frames tipping the light cones until a future-directed worldline loops back through its own departure — let the octave's folding rhyme scheme shadow that closure. The volta at line nine should turn, as Gödel's paper does, from the model to the conclusion that if such a round trip is possible, time's passage cannot be real in itself but only apparent to embedded observers. The sestet should then let the felt forward direction of each local segment of the journey press against that global verdict, without resolving the tension. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the argument recognizes it, and a good enough poem that someone hearing it cold still feels the vertigo.
