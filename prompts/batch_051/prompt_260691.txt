I recently took part in a clinical study in which a single dose of psilocybin produced, reliably and on schedule, an experience that I can only describe as a direct awareness of God's presence, and subjects in such studies commonly rate the induced states among the most spiritually significant of their lives. This has unsettled my view of the classic argument from religious experience, which I took to run roughly as follows: an experience as of God is prima facie evidence for God's existence unless the subject has a specific reason to distrust the experience. If states resembling the paradigmatic mystical experiences can now be produced by manipulating brain chemistry, does that argument still go through? Identify which premise the development bears on — the presumptive reliability of such experiences, the absence of an adequate natural explanation, or something else — and say whether the argument is dead, in need of repair, or merely narrowed in scope, and on what grounds.
