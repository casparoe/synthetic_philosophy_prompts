Analyze the Fischer controversy — the West German historians' dispute from the early 1960s over how much blame Germany bore for the First World War — as a case study in what it means to hold a nation responsible. I know the rough outline: Fritz Fischer argued that Germany deliberately risked and pursued a European war to secure great-power status, and that German war aims in 1914 were continuous with later expansionist aims; the thesis caused a public and professional uproar in a country that had largely treated the war as a shared European catastrophe. I do not need more historiography, and I do not want a retelling of the July Crisis. What I want is a careful conceptual mapping. Separate four registers in which the sentence "Germany was responsible" could be meant: (i) causal responsibility in the historian's sense, where the claim is about how the war came about; (ii) legal responsibility of a state, as in the treaty-level war-guilt question; (iii) moral responsibility of the individual decision-makers; and (iv) collective responsibility of the nation, including later citizens who had no part in the decisions. For each register, say what Fischer's evidence could and could not establish, and whether the fury of the controversy was really about that register. Then use the episode to stress-test a sharper philosophical claim: that a group can be collectively responsible in a nondistributive way — that "Germany" is not merely shorthand for "some Germans' decisions," and yet the blame does not diffuse so widely across the population that no one in particular is answerable. Where does the Fischer controversy support that idea, where does it reveal the idea doing ideological work, and where does it leave the theory silent? Finally, say what the episode shows about the relationship between historical explanation and moral blame: does establishing that Germany caused the war entail that Germany deserves the blame, and if not, what further premise has to be added? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
