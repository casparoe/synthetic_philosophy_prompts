I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep running into the same disagreement while reporting on artificial intelligence. Some sources say "transformative AI" remains years or decades away; others respond that the transformation is already underway, so asking whether it has happened is simply a mistake about the kind of thing a historical transformation is. I've caught myself making the following argument, and I need to know whether it holds up or whether it is mostly a move with words.

(1) Transformative AI is AI whose cumulative effect on how humans live and work is comparable to the cumulative effect of the Industrial Revolution.
(2) The Industrial Revolution was not a single datable event; it was a compounding process in which many modest, domain-specific gains in productivity accumulated over decades into something of epochal scale.
(3) Current AI systems are already producing domain-specific gains in productivity — most visibly in software engineering, translation, customer support, and parts of biomedical research — that are large by historical standards within the domains where they appear.
(4) If a technology is already producing domain-specific productivity gains of the kind whose compounding made up the Industrial Revolution, then the transition it would bring is not a future event; it is an ongoing process.
(5) Therefore transformative AI has already happened — in the only sense the Industrial Revolution can be said to have happened, namely as an unfolding process rather than a dated moment.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what is the strongest corrected version of that step? If it is valid, does the conclusion actually answer the question people are asking when they ask whether transformative AI has happened, or does it merely replace that question with a different one? I keep suspecting step (4) is doing too much work, but I cannot articulate exactly what it shifts.
