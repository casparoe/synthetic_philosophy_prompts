I'm a retired chemist and Ive lately been reading philosophy of risk, mostly around the precautionary principle. I keep trying to turn the principle into an actual piece of machinery, and I'd like you to tell me how the machine would run. Suppose a country sets up a Precautionary Assessor's Office. Before a novel chemical can be marketed, the manufacturer must post a bond equal to the Office's own estimate of the plausible worst-case harm. The bond sits in escrow and earns interest; if no harm attributable to the chemical shows up within 25 years it is returned to the manufacturer, and if harm does show up it is paid out to the harmed parties up to the assessed damages, with any remainder refunded. Harms that only emerge after 25 years fall on a general fund financed by a levy on all approved chemicals. The Office is paid a percentage commission on every bond it sets, but an assessor loses their licence if later damages for a chemical they assessed exceed their estimate. Manufacturers can reduce the bond by funding continuous monitoring and an approved remediation plan. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. What does it incentivise among manufacturers, assessors, regulators, insurers, and the scientists whose studies feed the estimates? How would each of them game it, where would it blow up, and can any of the failure modes be fixed without giving up the precautionary idea? Please answer in British English.
