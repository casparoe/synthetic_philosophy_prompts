I'm putting together materials for a small seminar on how cultures handle individual achievement, and I'd like an original satirical piece to open the discussion. The seminar contrasts two tendencies: the American Dream, understood as the celebration of self-made success, ambition, and standing out, and the Nordic norm of Jantelagen (the "Law of Jante"), a cousin of the tall poppy syndrome, which frowns on believing you are anyone special or better than others and on flaunting success. Please write a deadpan parody of roughly 500 words in the form of a new-resident welcome letter from the "Office of Social Equilibrium" of a fictional Nordic town that has institutionalized Jantelagen down to small bureaucratic rituals. The letter is addressed to a newcomer who has just arrived from a fictional American company town organized around meritocratic self-celebration, and it should be written entirely in the town's voice: earnestly explaining its leveling customs, with its polite discomfort about the newcomer's culture gradually exposing the excesses of the American Dream as well. The jokes should come from the real internal logic of the two norms — the Jante side's suspicion of visible excellence, surveillance of boasting, and ritualized self-deprecation, and the American Dream side's promotional optimism and terror of being merely ordinary — rather than from cheap national stereotypes. The irony should stay deadpan and remain legible without any wink or authorial aside.

Write the piece in exactly five numbered sections, with no separate introduction or conclusion; each section should be a short paragraph in the document's voice. After the five sections, add one short note of a sentence or two telling me where the satire exaggerates or rests on a loose or contested reading of these ideas. If the setup as I've described it is muddled — for example, if Jantelagen and the tall poppy syndrome cannot honestly be folded together, or if "the American Dream" is too vague an object to satirize without straw-manning — please say so plainly instead of quietly producing the piece.
