I've been going back and forth on whether Dennett's two-bitser line against Searle holds up under how robots actually get trained nowadays. The background: Searle insists there's original intentionality (what minds have) and derived intentionality (what books, shirts, and vending machines have, borrowed from the people who designed or read them). Dennett's two-bitser is a quarter-taking vending machine; he uses it to argue that the border between its borrowed 'aboutness' and ours is a matter of what an observer needs to predict and explain, not a real joint in nature. Now suppose you train a robot arm from scratch with a self-supervised prediction objective—raw motor commands, raw pixels, force readings, no human ever labels object or weight—and its hidden states end up tracking whether an object is still behind a screen and how heavy it will feel. No designer gave those states a content. So: original, derived, or neither? And more importantly, does the two-bitser argument still cut the way Dennett wanted, or does gradient decent fixing the aboutness without an interpreter break one of its premises? I care about the fate of the argument, not about what to call the robot.
