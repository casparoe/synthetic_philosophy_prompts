I write about science and ideas, and I am working on a piece about machine-generated music and the older philosophical claim that music has a special metaphysical standing. I would like you to help me think through a counterfactual: how would Arthur Schopenhauer assess a present-day generative music system, given his own theory of what music is?

Schopenhauer held that music is unlike the other arts. On his account, painting, sculpture, and poetry represent the Platonic Ideas, the timeless forms behind appearances, whereas music is an immediate expression of the Will itself—not a depiction of any particular thing, but a direct objectification of the blind, aimless striving he took to be the inner nature of the world. He also mapped the parts of music onto the grades of that objectification: the bass corresponds, roughly, to inorganic nature; the middle voices to the plant and animal world; and melody to the conscious, desiring, briefly satisfied, and again desiring human will. This is why he ranked music above the other arts and thought it could communicate the character of existence more directly than any representation of particular things could.

He died in 1860, before recorded sound, before broadcasting, before algorithmic recommendation, and before generative models that are trained on large corpora of human performances and then produce novel pieces by approximating their statistical regularities. A contemporary commercial system of this kind has no human composer, no performer, and no conscious intention behind any particular output; it may be steered toward engagement metrics such as listening time and skip rates, or asked to produce plausible new music in the style of a requested composer or in an interpolated style between composers.

Suppose Schopenhauer could be given a technically accurate account of such a system while his philosophical commitments remained fixed. How would he classify its output? Would he count it as music in his special sense, as a mere imitation or shadow of music, or as something else? Would the absence of a human composer matter to him, given that the Will he describes is not a person's intention but a blind force running through all of nature? And what would he make of the fact that these systems are typically tuned to the aggregated preferences of listeners—to measurable patterns of desire, attention, and satisfaction? Please do not guess at Schopenhauer's psychology; instead, show which features of his system would do the work in your answer and indicate where, if anywhere, the system would have to be stretched or revised.
