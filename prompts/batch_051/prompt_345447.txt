I've ended up holding, more or less, the view that there's a duty to be legible to others. Not just sincerity — I mean a positive responsibility to make your intentions, reasons, commitments and emotional states readable: to explain yourself when it matters rather than just not lying, and to treat being consistently opaque or hard to read as a moral failing rather than an unfortunate personal style. Maybe its a duty of respect, or a duty not to offload excessive interpretive labour onto the people around you. The steelman feels strong to me: opacity sits uncomfortably close to manipulation, refusing to explain yourself is often a power move, and close relationships seem to depend on more than bare honesty — they depend on the other person being willing to render themselves intelligible.

But I want you to treat this as a claim that could be false, and map out what would count against it. Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which escape routes are live rather than just logically possible. Some pressure points worth weighing: legibility demands can become coercive or disciplinary, especially when institutions insist that people render themselves legible in officially sanctioned categories; opacity is sometimes protective for people with less power, for privacy, for groups under surveillance, or for people whose self-presentation is penalised when it doesn't fit the standard idiom. There's also the worry that being legible and being honest come apart — someone can be legibly fraudulent, and someone can be honest but hard to read, so I might be smuggling in a demand for a particular communication style rather than a moral norm. And maybe intimacy is exactly the willingness to do interpretive work rather than expecting the other person to package themselves for easy consumption.

Say which of these a committed defender of the view should concede as genuine count-againsts and which they can dismiss, and whether the view ends up so hedged that it stops having content — and if it is that hedged, whether that's a problem for a claim like this. Also tell me plainly if you think the setup is mis-posed, for instance if 'legible' is doing too many different jobs or if I've turned an epistemic virtue into a moral duty by sleight of hand.
