I live in a small housing cooperative and serve on its bylaws committee. Our bylaws currently require a two-thirds vote of the members present and voting to amend any bylaw, and they add that this two-thirds requirement can itself be changed only by a two-thirds vote. At last month's annual meeting, a motion to lower the amendment threshold from two-thirds to a simple majority received 58% of the votes cast. The chair ruled the motion adopted on the ground that, since the motion itself replaces the old threshold with a majority threshold, the 58% satisfies the threshold the motion establishes; the new rule was applied to the very vote that created it. A group of members argues this is backwards: the motion had to clear the two-thirds bar that was in force when the vote was held, so it failed. The bylaws say nothing about when an adopted amendment takes effect. The committee must send the board a short decision memo resolving whether the 58% vote was valid and stating what the amendment threshold now is. Please draft the memo with a definite recommendation, an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the losing side, and a closing note on which consideration would most likely change your conclusion, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
