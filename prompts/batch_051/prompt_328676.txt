"In Nash's bargaining solution, independence of irrelevant alternatives says that adding a new feasible option that is not ultimately chosen cannot change the outcome. But this has a counterintuitive consequence. Suppose a union and an employer are bargaining, and a new law improves the workers' outside option by making alternative employment more attractive. Nash's solution still gives the workers the same utility as before, because the new outside option is not chosen in the final agreement. The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution rejects independence of irrelevant alternatives and replaces it with individual monotonicity: if the set of feasible agreements expands in any way that increases one player's maximum possible utility, then that player must receive at least as much utility under the new solution as before. The Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is then defined as the point on the Pareto frontier that preserves the ratio of the players' utility gains from the disagreement point, using each player's maximum feasible utility as the benchmark. This makes it a properly cardinal solution, unlike Nash's, which is purely ordinal and therefore ignores information about how much the players value different outcomes."
