About a year ago I found out that the guy who runs the bakery on my street is the same person as the writer whose first novel broke my brain when I was twenty. Once I knew it, I could say Bakery Guy is Novelist and mean a truth about one and the same person. But Bakery Guy is Novelist was a genuine discovery to me—I could have been wrong, I updated, it changed how I read the novels—while Bakery Guy is Bakery Guy never could have been a discovery and couldn't tell me anything. Same person picked out by both names, completely different cognitive effect. I've since gone down the Frege's-puzzle rabbit hole, and I have two friends who keep argueing past each other about exactly this case, the Hesperus–Phosphorus case, and the Superman–Clark Kent belief reports.

One friend is a neo-Fregean: names express senses or modes of presentation, and the difference between Bakery Guy is Bakery Guy and Bakery Guy is Novelist is a difference in semantic content. The other is a Millian/direct-reference type: a name's semantic contribution is just the individual, full stop, so both sentences express the same singular proposition, and the felt difference has to live somewhere non-semantic—psychology, pragmatics, metasemantics, the way the sentence is presented, whatever.

I want you to write the document these two could both sign after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should contain:

- a numbered agreed-claims section that is not vacuous: things both accept about the discovery data, about belief ascriptions and apparently blocked substitution, about what a theory of names has to save, and about which classic moves both of them reject;
- a disagreements table with the precise formulations of where they part, each row worded so the side named would accept it as a fair statement of their view;
- for each real disagreement, the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance;
- an honest note on any part of the dispute that is merely verbal, and why.

I don't need a verdict on which of them is right, and I don't need a bibliography or footnotes—I've done that reading already. What I want is the joint statement itself, the sort of thing I could email them and they'd both say, yeah, that's us, and that's the point we actually disagree on.
