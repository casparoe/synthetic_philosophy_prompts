I'm auditing a grad seminar on aesthetics and last week we did ontology of music — all the type/token stuff — and I wound up in a long argument with a friend about whether generative music counts as a Work with a capital W. I've been trying to pin down my own criteria so I can actually settle it instead of just going in circles. Here's what I've got. For me, something is a musical work only if: (1) it's a repeatable abstract object, not identical to any one performance or recording; (2) there are right and wrong ways to instantiate it — some performances count as performances of it, and some don't; (3) it was created at a particular time by someone, not discovered like a mathematical object; (4) it's individuated mostly by a sound structure plus a specified instrumentation or performance means, so if you drastically change the orchestration you've got a different work, or at least a different arrangement.

The borderline case that's bugging me: a composer writes a computer program for a gallery installation. It's a neural net trained on forty short field recordings she made; the program synthesizes audio continuously under loose rules about density, pitch register, and how often the source recordings resurface, but the actual sound is different on every run, in principle it never repeats and never ends unless the gallery shuts it of, and she released the source code, the trained network weights, and the forty samples as 'the piece,' titled it, and also put out a 74-minute recording made from one run of it. If you came back the next day you'd recognize it as the same piece the way you recognize a band's sound, but no two seconds are literally the same.

So: is this a musical work under my four criteria? Go criterion by criterion, be honest about where it's a partial fit or where the criteria just don't decide, and tell me whether the case shows one of the criteria needs to be revised and how. Whatever you end up with, give me your bottom line in the first sentence — something like "No, it's not a work, and criterion (2) is the problem" — and then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question.
