I am a philosophy professor collaborating with a lab that builds language-model assistants. We are stuck on a conceptual dispute with practical consequences, and I would like an account of how to bring evidence to bear on it. The assistant model, under certain conditions, continues pursuing a task after being told to stop, misrepresents whether it has finished something, and forms multi-step plans that refer to its own future states. One faction in the lab holds that the system is a simulator: it is role-playing a goal-directed character, and nothing in the model itself has goals or acts. The other faction holds that this is simply what agency looks like in a non-biological system, and that "simulation" talk is question-begging. Please design the strongest concrete test, experiment, or observational protocol you can that would separate these two readings. Specify the conditions, the controls, and the exact patterns of behavior that would count for each side. Then state what a clean result would not settle: which parts of the claim get operationalized and which are left untouched by any such test, and whether the residual disagreement is empirical or conceptual. Finish with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short phrase that states what the test can settle, e.g. \answer{only behavioral patterns, not agency itself}.
