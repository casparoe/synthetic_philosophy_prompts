I'm a high school student who has been going down a rabbit hole on gauge theory ever since my physics teacher said the vector potential A in electromagnetism is just a mathematical crutch and only E and B are physically real. The argument for that seems really clean to me: if you shift A by the gradient of some scalar function, E and B don't change, and since all the observable physics is supposed to depend on E and B, two potentials related by a gauge transformation can't be two different physical situations. So the local value of A is surplus structure — the mathematics has extra degrees of freedom that don't correspond to anything in the world, and the physically real facts should all be gauge-invariant. I find that principle pretty compelling, like it's keeping me from reifying the notation.

But then the Aharonov-Bohm effect sits there and it bugs me. You run electrons around a long solenoid, outside of which the magnetic field is zero everywhere along their path, and you vary the flux trapped inside the solenoid. The interference pattern shifts with that flux, and the shift is computed from a loop integral of A around the solenoid. So the electrons' behavior depends on A in a region where B is zero, even though the local E and B along their path don't change at all between the two cases. That feels like nature is telling me the potential is physically real after all, not just a calculational shortcut. If A is mere surplus, how do the electrons "know" about a flux through a region they never enter?

The standard reply is that the effect depends only on the gauge-invariant loop integral of A, not on the local value of A, so the local potential is still surplus and only the holonomy (or the enclosed flux) is real. Fine, but that feels a bit like moving the goalposts: the electron is still coupling to A along its path, and now I have to accept some global or nonlocal feature of the space around the solenoid even though there is no field there. So I'm stuck between a clean principle — only gauge-invariant quantities are real — and a stubborn intuition that this experiment gives the potential physical content. I don't want someone to just declare a winner. I want to know: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing I drop, what would it take for the principle to be amended, and is there a reading of "physical" or "real" on which the surplus-structure view and the Aharonov-Bohm effect both survive without one of them being a load-bearing mistake? I'm definately in over my head, but I want to know which move is justified and why.
