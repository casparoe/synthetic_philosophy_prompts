i'm a philosopher of language by training — sense and reference, intensions as functions from possible worlds to extensions, frege/montague style compositionality, the usual toolkit. lately i've been reading nlp papers and they seem to be doing semantics without any of that apparatus: words are high-dimensional vectors, similarity is cosine distance, king - man + woman lands near queen, and meaning is supposed to be read straight off the geometry. there's a distributional slogan that a word's meaning just is a function of the contexts it typically occurs in. i want the translation manual. restate what distributional semantics and word-embedding models are actually claiming, in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can — what corresponds to an intension, what corresponds to an extension, what composes, and where possible worlds go, if anywhere. then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which only translate with distortion, and which have no expression at all in the sense/reference/intension toolbox. and be honest about the direction of the mismatch: is the residue a sign that my framework is too coarse to talk about gradience, high-dimensional similarity, and context-sensitivity, or that the nlp use of the word meaning is thinner than it sounds? don't just tell me a vector can be dressed up as an intension — i want the mapping worked out and the exact places it snaps.
