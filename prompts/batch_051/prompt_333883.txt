Last night a friend and I argued for hours about whether frontier AI developers should release model weights — the raw parameters that let others copy, modify, or fine-tune the model — and we ended in the usual deadlock. He holds that open release is the only way to stop a few labs from controlling the technology; I hold that releasing the weights is a one-way act that no ordinary set of pre-release evaluations can justify. Out of the argument came a compromise mechanism, and I want to know how it would actually behave before I take it seriously.

Under the rule, a developer may release weights immediately only if an independent evaluator certifies that default evaluations turned up no evidence of certain concrete dangerous capabilities — say, producing a credible protocol for synthesizing a known pathogen, or autonomously carrying out a specified class of cyber intrusions. If the developer declines or fails that certification, the weights go into third-party escrow rather than being made public. For the next eighteen months, any accredited auditor may request controlled access to the model and try to demonstrate one of the listed capabilities. Any such demonstration opens a six-month public challenge, during which outside red teams with controlled access get a chance to reproduce the finding. If no demonstration survives the challenge by the end of the eighteen months, the weights are released automatically; if one does, escrow is extended another eighteen months and the process repeats, with the newly demonstrated capability added to the list.

Walk me through what this actually does. Where does it get gamed first — by the developer, the auditors, the red teams, or downstream users — and what does the gaming look like? What happens to the open-source ecosystem, and to the distribution of technical power, while weights wait in escrow? What second-order effects does the automatic sunset have on the original argument about who should bear the burden of proof? And once those failure modes are on the table, could the mechanism be adjusted so it is neither a de facto ban nor a rubber stamp, or does any workable version collapse toward one of those two poles?
