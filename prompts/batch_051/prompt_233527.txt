For something I'm writing on practical rationality, I want to get clear on a puzzle about weakness of will. As I understand the standard story, hyperbolic discounting dissolves akrasia into a conflict between temporal selves: the present self discounts the future steeply and therefore chooses the smaller, sooner good, while the future self has a calmer discount function and wishes the present self had chosen otherwise. Since no temporal perspective is privileged in this model, why isn't the right conclusion that what we call akrasia is not really a failure of will but just an intrapersonal conflict of interest, with the stronger preference winning at each moment? Doesn't this leave no room for the agent who, at the very moment of choosing, judges that she should abstain and yet gives in anyway?
