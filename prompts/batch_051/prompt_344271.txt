I am an amateur programmer and self-taught musician, and I have spent the past two years building a small piece of software that composes short piano pieces. The program has a few dozen parameters—scale, mode, how far the melody may leap, how much syncopation, how often it borrows chords from the parallel minor, how dense the accompaniment is—and it can generate an essentially endless stream of output. A friend recently told me that everything it produces sounds like the same piece wearing different clothes, and that all the program does is twiddle knobs inside a space that someone else designed. The remark stung, because I had already begun to suspect that a great deal of human creativity works the same way: an improviser is mostly varying a standard's melody and harmony, a sonnet writer is turning a small set of formal and thematic knobs, and many composers I admire sound, on close listening, as though they spent their lives exploring one or two narrow spaces. What I cannot settle is whether this is a bug in the picture or the entire picture. On one hand, if creativity is just variation on a theme, we seem to lose what makes a handful of artists feel different in kind—not more skilled, but as though they added a knob no one had noticed. On the other hand, perhaps adding a knob is itself just a variation, performed at a higher level in a larger space of possible musical systems, and the praise we give for it is only the glow of hindsight.

I would like an essay that works through this controversy and reaches a definite conclusion. Please weigh the case that genuine creativity consists in exhausting variations inside an inherited space of possibilities against the case that it consists in altering or expanding the space itself, and take seriously the possibility that this is a distinction without a difference because adding a knob can always be redescribed as turning a more abstract knob. Do not end on a non-committal compromise; defend whichever verdict you find best, even if that verdict is that the distinction is real, that it is empty, or that the truth lies at one particular level of description.
