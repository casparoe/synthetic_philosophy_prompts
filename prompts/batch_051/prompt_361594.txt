I am drafting a short paper for my school's sixth-form philosophy society on weakness of will, and I would like a consistency check on the position I have ended up with before I present it. The commitments I have arrived at are these:

(1) Akrasia is a real phenomenon: people sometimes freely and knowingly act against their own better judgement.
(2) The best explanation of akrasia is hyperbolic discounting. Because we discount future goods hyperbolically rather than exponentially, a smaller-but-sooner reward can temporarily outweigh a larger-but-later reward as it becomes imminent, so that the agent's all-things-considered preference reverses between planning and acting.
(3) For an action genuinely to be weak-willed, it must be contrary to the agent's better judgement at the moment of action; otherwise it is simply a change of mind.
(4) Weakness of will is distinct from compulsion: the akratic agent is not acting under coercion or addiction and remains able to do otherwise.
(5) Resolute choice is possible and sometimes rational. A person who foresees that her preferences will reverse may resolve in advance to stick to a plan, and it can be rational to carry out that resolution even when the temptation is present and her preferences have shifted towards it.
(6) The earlier and later stages of a single life are stages of one and the same agent; a hyperbolic preference reversal does not divide one person into two agents with independent authority.

Please go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension with which, and which conflicts are merely apparent and dissolve on closer inspection. For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, explain what each party to the tension would require me to revise, and identify the least costly fix. I would also like to know whether a single revision would dissolve the most serious conflicts at once. Please answer in British English.
