Hey, I've been reading a bunch of proposals about making AI systems 'low impact' or making sure they dont cause side effects, and I keep tripping over the same weird thing, and I'm wondering whether it has a name.

A common move is to say: don't let the AI change the world much; measure how different the world ends up from how it would have been if the AI had never acted, and penalize that difference. But this seems to get things backwards in both directions. Suppose a robot is told to fetch a coffee, and on the way it notices the kitchen is on fire. If it puts out the fire, that's a huge difference from the no-AI baseline, so a naive impact penalty would punish it for doing exactly what we'd want. But if it walks past and the house burns down, that's zero 'side effect' by that measure, because the fire was already going to happen. A subtler version: an AI that's supposed to run a factory efficiently. The baseline is the factory without the AI. Any real improvement is a big deviation from the baseline, so the AI gets penalized for doing its actual job well, and the only way to score zero impact is to do nothing, at which point why build it. So people add a correction, roughly: only count deviations that arent the assigned task. But that immediately assumes you already know what the assigned task is, which is the very thing the impact measure was supposed to avoid needing.

I know this is somewhere near 'unintended consequences' and maybe Goodhart's law, and I've seen 'side effects' used as a catch-all, but I mean something more specific: the circularity where a definition of side effect that doesn't state what's valuable has to secretly borrow a baseline, and then either punishes the good deviations or ignores the bad things that were already going to happen. Does this exact configuration have a name? If it's just 'the problem of side effects,' then what are the boundaries between this and the general fact that machines do literally what you say, or the general fact that any metric can be gamed? I don't just want a label; I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.
