I am a junior at a large public high school and serve as treasurer of the Student Government Finance Committee. At next week's full Student Senate meeting we must act on a proposal from the Economics Club to run a small pilot of what they call futarchy: a system for deciding how to spend the senior class gift fund. Under the proposal, the student body would first vote to choose a single measurable success criterion from a short menu (for example, "the project that produces the highest average student-reported satisfaction one semester after completion," or "the project with the highest measured usage over eight weeks"). Then the committee would announce three finalist projects and run a two-week prediction market in which every student receives an equal allocation of play credits, which are not redeemable for money or grades, and may trade shares in each project. The project whose shares imply the highest probability of maximizing the chosen criterion would receive the approximately $4,000 fund. The finalists being considered are a renovation of the outdoor courtyard study space, a three-day mental-health and study-skills week, and an upgrade to the auditorium sound system. The committee is genuinely split. Some members argue the market will pool dispersed knowledge about what students would actually use and how messy each project's execution would be, and that separating the value question (which criterion matters) from the factual question (which project best meets it) is exactly what makes the scheme attractive. Others argue that play credits remove the incentive to trade truthfully, that a motivated clique could coordinate to move the price, that the chosen metric can be gamed once students know that winning depends on it, and that this quietly replaces a transparent majority vote with a mechanism most students will not understand and some will experience as a casino.

I am responsible for drafting the Finance Committee's formal decision memo to the full Senate. Please write the memo with a single actionable bottom line: approve the pilot, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions. The memo should be something the Senate could actually act on, and it should fairly represent the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than knocking down a strawman. Most of the Senate has not taken economics, so the reasoning must be self-contained and free of unexplained jargon. After the memo, provide a brief structured summary in JSON with the following fields: recommendation (one of "approve", "reject", or "approve_with_conditions"); conditions (an array of strings, each a concrete condition attached to approval; empty if the recommendation is reject); strongest_objection (a string giving the best argument against your recommendation); reply_to_strongest_objection (a string); key_monitoring_metric (a string naming what the committee should track if the pilot is approved); and confidence (one of "high", "medium", or "low").
