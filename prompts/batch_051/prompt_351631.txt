I mod a mid-size rationalist-adjacent forum and we get a dozen or so "is this a dogwhistle or just a weird phrasing" reports a week. Our written rule is that you can be sanctioned for what you said or what you clearly implicated, but in practice the volunter mods go by vibes, and every warning turns into a meta-thread about whether we're tone-policing or letting people launder a position through implication. Bans escalate to a head mod, so the checklist is for mods deciding between no action, a warning, and escalate. I want a reusable procedure, not a philosophy lecture and not a verdict on one post: a checklist a mod who has never read Grice can run in under ten minutes, grounded in the implicature/speaker-meaning machinery — quality, quantity, relation, manner, cancellability, and the difference between what a post literally asserts and what choosing to say it in this context conveys. Translate those into plain tests with pass conditions, like: what would survive the author saying "I didn't mean that"? What extra does a reader get from the fact that they said this rather than some equally true alternative? Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) under a post about a new shelter, a comment that just says "guess we'll see which neighborhoods have a crime problem now"; (2) a user who replies to an old post by a banned member with only "noted."; (3) a post stating a banned position outright but padded with "not saying, just asking" hedges. I specifically want at least one case where the procedure returns no-sanction even though the mod team will hate it, and a clear statement of which steps are judgment calls wearing a checklist costume and where the procedure stops and a human has to decide.
