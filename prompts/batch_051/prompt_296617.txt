Give me a working definition of "organism" that gets the following cases right: a single E. coli cell; a human being; a skin cell taken from that human; a virus; a Portuguese man o' war (a colony of connected, genetically identical zooids); a lichen (a fungus living with a photosynthetic partner); an ant colony; a clonal aspen grove (one connected root system, many trunks); and a reef-building coral together with its symbiotic algae. I am confident that the first two are organisms and that the skin cell is not, even though it is alive; I am genuinely unsure about the other six. I suspect the word runs together two questions—whether something is alive, and whether it is one individual rather than many—and I would like you to say whether a single definition can answer both or whether the term should be split. Propose a definition, run it through every case, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts.
