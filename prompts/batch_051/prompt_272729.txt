I've been trying to figure this out on my own and I keep going in circles. I've seen people write that the total value of the world is basically consciousness times happiness: you multiply how much conscious experience exists by how good that experience is. Other people seem to think value just adds up across lives, like two equally happy people are exactly twice as valuable as one. And then a third group says some values are lexical — meaning one serious violation, like one person being tortured, can't ever be outweighed by piling up a million mild pleasures, no matter how big the pile gets. Those sound to me like three different claims about the arithmetic of value, not just three different moods. My question is whether that arithmetic is something you could actually test. Design the best test you can. I want to know: if someone claims value is additive rather than multiplicative or lexical, what concrete observation or experiment would bear on that, what result would count for additivity and what result would count for a lexical threshold, and what would each result actually settle? Could a test show which arithmetic is right, or could it only show how people currently trade things off? And is there part of the dispute that no amount of data could ever settle — and if so, what exacty is left over once the testable part is stripped away?
