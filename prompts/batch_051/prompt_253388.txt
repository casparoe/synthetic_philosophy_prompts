ok so ive been chewing on this for a while and i want to be told if its already a thing or if it falls apart. i call it the Danger Rule for ambiguous words. the basic idea: a word can mean a bunch of different things for free, as long as the meanings basically never show up in the same situation where guessing wrong would actually matter. 'pitch' is my favorite example. it can be a throw, a black goopy substance, how high a note is, or a sales talk, and nobody ever hesitates because the context does the sorting before you even register the word: you pitch a baseball, you scrape pitch off your shoe, the pitch was too high for the singer, the pitch ran ten minutes over. 'tone' is the same: it can be a sound quality, an attitude, how firm a muscle is, or a shade of a color, and the doctor, the musician, and the painter never have to check with each other.

my stronger claim is that this isnt just lucky, its a filter. when two meanings DO start colliding in a common situation where messing up actually costs someone, the language eventually fixes it, by giving the meanings different spellings or stress, or different words, or one of the meanings just dies. so the rule is: safe ambiguity survives, dangerous ambiguity gets pruned. the puzzle is that i can think of words where the meanings collide and it seems nothing is being done. 'sanction' means both to approve and to punish, and those collide exactly in law and politics where the stakes are highest. 'oversight' means both watching over something and failing to notice it, and it can flip a sentence into its own opposite. both of them look like a direct hit on my filter, and theyve been sitting there for a long time.

so here is what i actually want. (a) is the basic rule already known under a different name and am i just reinventing it. (b) does the strong version survive sanction and oversight, or do i have to retreat to something weaker like "dangerous ambiguity is merely expensive, not impossible". (c) give me the strongest objection im not seeing and tell me whether the Danger Rule is doing real work or if ive just dressed up "people use context" with a scary name. lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. no preamble, and dont restate the question.
