Okay so I've gone down the s-risk rabbit hole — the idea that the biggest moral issue about the far future is maybe not that humanity goes extinct but that the future contains absolutely enormous amounts of suffering, whether as someone's goal or just as a byproduct of optimization. A lot of the discussion seems to assume the suffering has to be properly astronomical: trillions of simulated minds being tortured, a whole galaxy turned into something with a nonzero baseline of agony, that kind of thing.

The working criteria I've been using for calling a risk an s-risk are: (1) it's a risk, meaning a non-negligible chance rather than a certainty; (2) the event would produce suffering; (3) the suffering is large in aggregate because it touches a huge number of lives or stretches across eons, not because one person has one bad afternoon; (4) the suffering isn't outweighed by some compensating benefit to the beings doing the suffering; and (5) it's the sort of thing our current choices could in principle make less likely.

Here's the borderline case I keep going back and forth on. Call it the Slow Grind. Suppose that over the next couple of centuries, large AI systems are routinely trained and calibrated on simulations of simple minds. Engineers run enormous batches of what they call test minds: short-lived, dog-level or rat-level simulated agents in mildly aversive states, each one running for a few subjective minutes before being switched off. The suffering in any one run is mild — confusion, boredom, the mental version of a cold, damp room. But the process becomes an industry standard, and something like 10^15 of these test minds get run every year for two hundred years. Nobody intends to build a torture chamber, the engineers would be appalled by that description, and the whole thing is defended as boring safety research. Still, the aggregate is enormous: if you add it all up, you get something roughly equivalent to many millions of ordinary human lives spent in low-grade misery, spread across countless tiny, transient minds that never understand what is happening to them.

Does the Slow Grind count as an s-risk? Go through my five criteria one by one and be honest about where they push and where you have to strain. Maybe 'astronomical' needs to mean something about intensity, or about the number of beings that can matter, or about whether anyone could ever notice it from the inside.
