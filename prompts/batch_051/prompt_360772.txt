so in my philosophy club we were arguing about whether delusions are actually beliefs, and one of the sixth-formers read out this summary of the two-factor theory that she'd copied from a psychology textbook. the gist was: 'a delusion needs two factors. factor one is a strange experience, like hearing a voice or a sudden feeling that everything is unfamiliar. factor two is a failure of the belief-evaluation system, so the person doesn't reject the odd explanation of that experience. because the person is basically reasoning fine from a weird experience plus a broken checking step, the delusion is a rational response to their situation. that shows delusions are beliefs like any other; if they weren't beliefs, you couldn't explain why people act on them.' i get that the two-factor story is probably right, but the jump from that to 'so delusions are beliefs like any other' feels off to me. i dont know which bits would actually be accepted by a philosopher or cognitive scientist, which bits are overstated or subtly conflate two different claims, and whether the conclusion about delusions being beliefs actually follows from the accurate parts.
