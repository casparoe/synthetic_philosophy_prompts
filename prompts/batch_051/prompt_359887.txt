I've been re-reading Aristotle's Politics and keep tripping over this passage (Jowett translation, Politics I.9, 1257a):

"For when they had come to supply themselves more from abroad by importing things in which they were deficient and exporting those of which they had a surplus, the employment of money necessarily came to be devised. For the natural necessaries are not in every case readily portable; hence for the purpose of barter men made a mutual compact to give and accept some substance of such a sort as being itself a useful commodity was easy to handle in use for general life, iron for instance, silver and other metals, at the first stage defined merely by size and weight, but finally also by impressing on it a stamp in order that this might relieve them of having to measure it; for the stamp was put on as a token of the amount."

Here's the counterfactual I can't shake. Suppose a legislator actually tried to enforce the distinction Aristotle draws between natural exchange and unnatural money-making: in a Greek polis around 350 BC, say a few tens of thousands of people, interest-bearing loans are banned outright, and buying goods just to resell them at a profit is banned too, while coined money stays legal as a medium of exchange. Predict how its economic and social life looks after two or three generations. I want the mechanism chain, not a story: what does it do to long-distance trade, to storing wealth, to the standing of merchants and artisans, to inequality, to public revenue, and to the aim of keeping households self-sufficient? Where would the actual outcome seperate from what Aristotle seems to expect in and around this passage, and where might he turn out to be right? Also flag your confidence: which predicitons are nearly forced, which are guesses, and what would have to be different for the whole thing to break down instead of just adapting. And if I'm misreading the passage or the scenario is set up so badly that the question doesn't work, tell me that up front rather than playing along. Keep it under 400 words.
