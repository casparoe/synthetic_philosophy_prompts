I'm presenting a view I've worked out on my own to my local philosophy discussion group next week, and I want to be ready for the question period rather than just the talk itself. The view: below a certain level of suffering, pain and pleasure trade off in the ordinary way — a short sharp pain can be worth a large pleasure, and a mild but persistent discomfort can be offset by enough enjoyment. But there is a threshold at which pain stops belonging on the same scale as pleasure; a person at that point would refuse any trade and simply wants the suffering to stop. Once pain crosses that threshold, I hold that no amount of pleasure for anyone, anywhere, can justify producing or allowing it. My reason is that it is one thing to stand partly outside a pain and weigh it against a pleasure, and another thing to be so filled by pain that the weighing itself is unavailable to the person; treating the second as just a larger negative number mistakes a difference in kind for a difference in degree. The group is mostly people comfortable weighing harms against benefits, including two who do cost-benefit work, plus one member who insists any pain, however severe, can in principle be made up for by enough pleasure, and two who think consent rather than aggregate wellbeing should be the test. Please draft me a prep sheet: the objections this group is most likely to raise, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is, phrased the way they would actually put them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is weakest. Include the question I should most hope nobody asks, and tell me how to answer it if it comes anyway. Please write the prep sheet in American English.
