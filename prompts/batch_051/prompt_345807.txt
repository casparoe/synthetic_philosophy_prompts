Write a satirical startup FAQ/product page, around 600 words, in the cheerful-deadpan voice of a Y Combinator launch announcement, for a company called Longview Escrow that sells value-lock-in services. The pitch: value drift — the worry that people, institutions, and future systems slowly slide toward values we'd now find abhorrent — is the biggest unsolved problem in the moral universe, so Longview will audit your current values, fix any inconsistencies, and freeze them in a legally and causally robust escrow so your descendants, your company, or any AI you build can't drift away from them. The FAQ can cover pricing tiers, what happens to beneficiaries who no longer recognize the frozen values, the independent auditor panel, and the guarantee. Keep the irony deadpan and don't wink: the piece should be legible as satire but never announce itself as satire. It should land the standard objections to value lock-in without ever stating them outright — e.g., it assumes we're at the end of moral history, the auditors and the company have there own drift and capture problems, fixing inconsistencies just imports someone's values, enforcing today's values on future people is authoritarian, and many people's meta-values include the ability to revise. After the piece, give a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: satirized_position (one string), objections_landed (array of strings, one objection per element), comic_devices (array of strings, e.g. deadpan, reductio, inverted praise), would_a_specialist_recognize_it (boolean), and weakest_line (string).
