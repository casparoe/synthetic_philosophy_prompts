In Book 1 of Lucretius’s *De Rerum Natura*, the argument for solid first bodies includes this passage:

> Thus primal bodies are solid, without a void.  
> But since there’s void in all begotten things,  
> All solid matter must be round the same;  
> Nor, by true reason canst thou prove aught hides  
> And holds a void within its body, unless  
> Thou grant what holds it be a solid. Know,  
> That which can hold a void of things within  
> Can be naught else than matter in union knit.

Please argue for the following interpretive thesis: in this passage Lucretius is not inferring the existence of solid atoms from observed hardness or resistance, but from a conceptual requirement that matter and void be distinguished. Then evaluate whether this reading makes his atomism vulnerable to the objection that impenetrability has dropped out as a physical property and become merely a logical posit. In particular, address what “matter in union knit” can be once the thing it holds is void: is it body, or simply the complement of empty space?
