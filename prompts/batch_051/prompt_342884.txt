I'm in an AI ethics class and we've been reading Asimov's robot stories as an early attempt at what we'd now call alignment. The basic proposal was that you could make a robot safe by hard-coding a small ranked set of rules into it — something like: first don't let humans get hurt, then obey instructions, then protect your own existence — and have the robot reason out the rest from those. That made sense when the imagined robots were basically logic engines that got their rules stamped in before they did anything. But now I'm looking at modern systems and it feels like the whole setup is gone: these models are trained on massive amounts of human text first, so they pick up goals and persona's and patterns of behavior before anyone tries to constrain them, and people keep jailbreaking or prompt-injecting their way around whatever rules get added afterward. I'm trying to work out whether the Three Laws argument actually dies under that change or just needs to be repaired. Which premise of the original argument is the new situation putting pressure on, and does a weaker modern version survive — something closer to constitutional AI or just constraining behavior rather than encoding moral laws? I'm less interested in what an actual policy should look like; I want to know what happens to the original argument itself.
