Harsanyi's aggregation theorem is often presented as a formal route to utilitarianism. Roughly stated, it says: if each individual's preferences over prospects satisfy the expected-utility axioms, and society's preferences over prospects also satisfy those axioms, and society never ranks a prospect below another when every individual ranks it at least as high, then social utility can be represented as a weighted sum of individual utility functions. Defenders take this to show that a rational evaluator who respects unanimous individual preferences is thereby committed to a broadly utilitarian social-welfare function; critics counter that the axioms already presuppose that social evaluation tracks nothing but individual preference-satisfaction, and that the theorem by itself leaves the weights on different people arbitrary. Write an essay on whether Harsanyi's aggregation theorem gives any real reason to be a utilitarian. Engage the strongest arguments on both sides, be precise about what the theorem does and does not establish, and defend a specific conclusion about how much normative weight it should carry.
