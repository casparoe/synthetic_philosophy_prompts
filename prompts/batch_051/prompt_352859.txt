I am drafting a short position paper on Marx's treatment of automation, and I would like a consistency audit of the position I have converged on before I circulate it. Stated as flatly as I can, the commitments are these.

(1) The magnitude of a commodity's value is governed by the socially necessary labor time required for its production under normal conditions. New value originates only in the expenditure of living labor power; machinery contributes to a product only by yielding back, piecemeal, the value already embodied in it.

(2) Under capitalist relations, labor-saving technical change tends, at least for long stretches, to immiserate those it unsettles: it swells the reserve army of labor, disciplines those still employed, and presses wages downward, so automation registers for displaced workers as harm rather than as liberation.

(3) The harm lies in the social form, not in the machine. The same technical capacities, under collective ownership and with a deliberate shortening of necessary labor time, could enlarge the sphere of free activity rather than immiserate anyone; it is therefore coherent to condemn the capitalist use of automation while welcoming the technology itself.

(4) There is also a self-undermining dynamic. Because the compulsion to replace living labor with machinery raises the organic composition of capital, it exerts a downward tendency on the general rate of profit; the same technological dynamism that drives accumulation is thus a source of recurrent crisis.

(5) Finally, I am drawn to a line of thought Marx sketches in draft material from the late 1850s: once production comes to rest on accumulated scientific and social knowledge rather than on the direct labor time of individual workers, the productive forces, not the expenditure of immediate labor, become the chief source of material wealth, and labor time becomes a decreasingly adequate, perhaps eventually obsolete, measure of value, so that a society organized around exchange value is approaching a historical limit.

I do not ask for a summary; I want an audit. Which of these pairs are in genuine tension and which merely appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest way of restoring consistency would be and what it would cost. If the entire set can be held together, tell me how, and in particular whether holding it together requires interpreting any of the five commitments more narrowly than I may have intended.
