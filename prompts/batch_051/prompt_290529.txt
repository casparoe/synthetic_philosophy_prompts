Below are five candidate senses in which a deterministic dynamical system might be called unpredictable. Score each candidate from 1 to 5, where 1 means it does almost no work in capturing what is distinctively chaotic about unpredictability and 5 means it captures the core of that idea; then rank the five from highest to lowest. Use tiers if you judge two candidates to be effectively the same property, but do not use tiers merely to avoid a decision. Justify each score in one or two sentences, and in particular say for each candidate whether it identifies something that is also true of many non-chaotic systems.

Candidates:

(1) Sensitive dependence on initial conditions: trajectories that begin arbitrarily close together will eventually separate until they differ by as much as two states picked at random from the attractor. On this view, unpredictability consists in the fact that the initial state would have to be specified with impossible precision for a long-range point forecast to remain close to the truth.

(2) Exponential divergence of errors: the separation between nearby trajectories grows on average as the exponential of a positive Lyapunov exponent, so the time for which a forecast stays inside a fixed tolerance scales only logarithmically with the accuracy of the initial measurement. On this view, unpredictability is a quantified rate of information loss about the initial state.

(3) Topological mixing: for any two nonempty open regions of the attractor, some trajectory starting in the first later enters the second. On this view, unpredictability consists in the system's scrambling of initial conditions so thoroughly that no matter how small an interval you initially localize the state in, its evolved uncertainty spreads over the entire attractor.

(4) Geometrical strangeness of the attractor: the dynamics converge onto a fractal invariant set, a strange attractor, with structure at every scale. On this view, unpredictability consists in the inescapable geometric complexity of the object on which the long-term state lives: pinning down a state on a fractal is not a finite-resolution task.

(5) Algorithmic incompressibility of typical orbits: a typical trajectory has no description shorter than itself; no finite program substantially shorter than the orbit can generate it. On this view, unpredictability is not merely a forecaster's limitation but an information-theoretic fact about the orbit.

After the ranking, state in a paragraph which candidate you would put first if the purpose were to explain to a layperson why determinism does not by itself make the world predictable, and note whether your choice changes when the purpose is scientific modeling rather than public explanation.
