While thinking about why calendar reforms so often stall on the choice of epoch, I noticed what may be a real structural analogy, or may just be pattern-matching. Civil timekeeping numbers years from some privileged point—a founder's birth, a regime's founding, a computationally convenient zero—but elapsed time seems indifferent to where that point sits: two events stand in the same interval relation no matter what number year one carries, just as the geometry of the plane is unchanged by moving the coordinate origin. The stronger version of the analogy is gauge freedom in electrodynamics: the potentials can be transformed without changing the electromagnetic fields, and one fixes a gauge only because a particular calculation becomes easier, not because the world prefers that gauge. Calendar epochs feel similar; the choice is held to be conventional, but some choices make historical or astronomical regularities easier to state while others bury them.

I want to know whether this is a deep identity of structure or a loose analogy. If it is deep, what are the calendar analogues of gauge-invariant quantities, of gauge-fixing conditions, and of anomalies—cases where a symmetry that holds at one level fails at another and leaves real traces? If it is superficial, what exactly does gauge freedom have that a calendar epoch lacks, and does identifying that missing ingredient explain anything about why disputes over epoch choice are so intractable?
