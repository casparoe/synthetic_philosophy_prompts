I've been reading about Maxwell's demon on and off for a while and I keep coming back to a parallel I cant decide about, maybe you can help. The demon is the little imaginary being who sits by a trapdoor between two boxes of gas and lets fast molecules through one way and slow molecules the other way, so one side heats up and the other cools down without it doing any work — like getting order for free, which would be cheating the second law of thermodynamics if it could actually pull it off. The catch, supposedly, is that it has to measure each molecule and hold a record of what it's seen, and to keep going it eventually has to wipe that record. And wiping a record isn't free: it has to shed a little heat somewhere (Landauer's principle, I think it's called). So the demon only keeps sorting by forgetting, and the forgetting is where the bill gets paid.

The thing I'm stuck on is that this looks, structurally, like the argument I sometimes hear about people or whole societies needing to forget: that you can't hang onto every wrong and every slight and every bit of the past without paying for the storage, and that forgetting isnt just psychologically nicer but is the step that lets the whole system carry on. I don't mean the claim that it feels better to let go — I mean the stronger claim that the forgetting is doing real work, that the record has to be wiped for the sorting to continue at all, and that the wipe is where the hidden cost sits.

Is this a real identity of structure, a loose metaphor, or am I just pattern-matching on the word 'forget'? What would have to be true of the personal/social side for the physics result to actually transfer? And can you tell me something the parallel would predict that's false, so I can see where it breaks? (And please write the answer in British English.)
