I've been going back and forth with a colleague in the department about a question that I'd like someone else's head on. We both agree that a human brain is a finite physical system — finite number of neurons, finite energy budget, all that. But we disagree about what that means for the space of possible experiences. He thinks the set of phenomenally distinct experiences available to a human being is in principle finite: there's a grain to experience, a finite number of discriminable levels along each sensory and emotional dimension, and only so many dimensions to begin with, so with enough time (and a decent memory prosthesis, let's say) you could in principle run through every distinct experience at least once. I'm not so shure. I keep thinking of color space, pain intensities, the particular feel of a thought just out of reach — these seem dense, continuous, maybe inexhaustible, and I worry that new combinations can always surprise you.

Act as a professional philosopher of mind with a good nose for the empirical details. Before you settle on an answer, take a moment to brainstorm the relevant considerations and how they cluster. Then give me your actual credence that the total repertoire of phenomenally distinct experiences available to a human mind (holding the basic architecture fixed, no new senses bolted on) is finite rather than infinite. I want the decomposition, not just a number: your probability that experience has a discrete grain rather than being dense or continuous; your probability that the number of basic experiential dimensions — sensory modalities, emotional registers, cognitive feels — is finite; your probability that, conditional on those, each dimension contributes only finitely many discriminable levels; and any other term you think matters, including, if you want it, the probability that "distinct experience" is well-behaved enough for the question to have a determinate answer. Attach a rough number and a sentence of justification to each, show how they combine to your final credence, and then tell me which term you're least confident in and what kind of evidence — psychophysics, neuroscience, introspection, whatever — would move it most.

For calibration, I'd put myself around 35% on finiteness and my colleague around 80%; where do you think the actual disagreement lives, and does it match the term you're least confident in?
