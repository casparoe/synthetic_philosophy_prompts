A popular philosophy podcast summarized the standard taxonomy of views about religious diversity and salvation roughly as follows. Exclusivists hold that only people who explicitly accept the one true faith can be saved; everyone who has never heard it is simply lost. Inclusivists soften this by saying that those who, through no fault of their own, never hear the gospel can still be saved, because God accepts their sincere moral striving in place of explicit faith. Pluralists drop the special status of any single tradition and claim that all religions are equally true, each one a different cultural expression of the same underlying reality. The episode then concluded that inclusivism and pluralism differ only in how many people they are willing to admit to salvation, and that pluralism ultimately collapses into relativism. I had always thought of the three positions as lying on a single spectrum from strict to generous, so this sounded right, but I suspect it may be unfair. Is it? Please work through the summary piece by piece, distinguishing what each position actually claims, which elements are legitimate simplifications, which ones an informed defender of each view would reject, and whether the episode's two concluding claims survive those corrections.
