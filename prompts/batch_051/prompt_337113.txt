I have been developing a view I call the attention-economy account of pleasure, and I want it assessed rather than validated. It is meant to dissolve the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the pleasure of a warm bath, of finishing a proof, of schadenfreude, and of relief when pain stops seem to share no common felt quality, so "pleasure" begins to look like a name for a disjunction rather than a kind. My proposal is that the common element is not a feeling but an arrangement of attention. I distinguish three forms. Savouring is a felt drop in the effort of holding attention on the same object: the object stays fixed while the effort of keeping it fixed falls toward zero, and the difference is felt. Absorption is the state in which attention is expected to continue without cost and this expectation is continuously confirmed, as in flow, play, or music. Relief is the felt release of attention that had been policing an aversive state: a headache lifting, a threat ending, an itch gone. My central claim is that these are one underlying event seen at three points: savouring is a falling cost of held attention, absorption a sustained zero cost, and relief the cancellation of a monitoring cost. Pleasure, then, is not a sensation added to experience but the felt kinematics of an attentional economy, a transition in the effort required to keep attending to what occupies you. I recognize this may look like the heterogeneity problem has merely been moved up a level, from what pleasures share to what these effort-transitions share; my answer is that the higher-level kind is genuinely univocal even though the felt qualities are not. I want a candid verdict. Is this a real theory or an extended metaphor? What is its strongest reconstruction, does it already exist under another name, and what remains distinctive? Give me the three strongest objections, including whether effortful pleasures such as a difficult climb and effortless non-pleasures such as spacing out can be handled without surrendering the central claim, and say whether any objection is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
