I teach a small upper-level ethics seminar — 12 undergrads, most of whom have done a bit of utilitarianism and know what a Pareto improvement is — and we're about to hit the equality/priority distinction. I want to run an activity before they do the reading, so that when the leveling-down objection comes up they feel like they've already met it rather then just having it explained to them. The session is 75 minutes, and I'd like the actual game to take about 35-40, leaving the rest for debrief. Design a game with tokens or points (no real money) where the class, or small groups, repeatedly choose between distributions of payoffs across four or five named 'people.' I want the setup to develop real pressure toward something like: an equal distribution is better than an unequal one even when the only thing that makes them differ is that the better-off person has been knocked down to the worse-off person's level and nobody gained. Then, in the debrief, map what happened onto the distinction between giving extra moral weight to benefits for the worse off and valuing equality itself. The key thing they should leave with is why a prioritarian view can decline to level down, while a view that treats equality as good in itself has a much harder time without saying something uncomfortable.

Give me actual rules, materials, the payoff schedule, a timeline, and a debrief script that names the moments in the game where each part of the distinction showed up. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I'm worried, for instance, that students will just refuse the leveling-down move because it seems obviously bad to hurt someone for no one's benefit, and the whole exercise collapses into a lecture — and how to steer around that. Be frank about one or two features of the view that the game can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself. And in the debrief notes, please keep straight what's settled (roughly, the shape of the objection and the formal difference between the views) and what's still genuinely contested, like whether the leveling-down objection is actually fatal to egalitarianism. I don't want them walking out thinking prioritarianism is the consensus just because the activity seemed to favor it.
