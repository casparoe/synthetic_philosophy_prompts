I am a graduate student in conservation biology, and I have been asked to write a policy brief arguing that habitat protection should be justified by appeal to animal welfare rather than biodiversity or ecosystem services. The framework I have been told to use is individualist: an ecosystem has no welfare of its own; only individual animals can be harmed or benefited. So the welfare value of a landscape must be the sum of the welfare of the animals living there. Trying to use this framework, I constructed an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept, and I want to know exactly where it breaks.

The argument runs as follows. (1) If the welfare value of a landscape is the sum of the welfare of its individual animal inhabitants, then, other things equal, a landscape is better the fewer net-negative lives it supports. (2) The typical wild-animal life is net-negative: most wild animals are small and short-lived, produce immense numbers of offspring, most of which die before reaching maturity from starvation, predation, disease, or exposure, and the survivors face chronic threats. (3) Habitat destruction reduces the number of wild-animal lives a landscape supports over time; clearing forest, draining wetland, and converting grassland to agriculture lower the land's carrying capacity. (4) Therefore, habitat destruction tends to improve welfare, and by the same reasoning any deliberate, lasting reduction in wild-animal carrying capacity—including declining to restore degraded habitat—is, other things equal, good. (5) But (4) is unacceptable: it turns the preservation of habitat into something harmful and makes a welfare case for deforestation, drainage, and neglect.

So I must reject something. I would like to keep (1) and (3); I could reject (2), but I worry that would not save the conservation case—it would only leave the welfare claim unresolved, with the argument going through whenever (2) is more probable than not. Which step should I reject, and what would rejecting it cost? Is there a hidden inference between the numbered steps that I have failed to notice, or is the argument valid and I should learn to live with one of its consequences?
