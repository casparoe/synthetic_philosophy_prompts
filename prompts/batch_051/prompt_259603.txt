I'm in a cogsci reading group and a friend summarized Dennett's heterophenomenology like this: it's the method where you study consciousness by recording every sincere first-person report a subject makes about how things seem to them, and you treat those reports as data, but you don't take them as authoritative reports of real inner episodes. Instead you interpret them, via the intentional stance, as a narrative the subject constructs; the world described by that narrative is the subject's heterophenomenological world, an interpreted fiction. The scientist's job is then to explain, in physical terms, how the subject came to produce that narrative. Because the reports are treated as a fiction, the method is officially neutral about whether there are qualia, but in practice it rules out using first-person reports as evidence for qualia, and it basically amounts to saying consciousness itself is a story with nothing more to it. Is this a fair summary? Separate what's accurate, what's subtly distorted, and what Dennett himself would reject, and say whether the 'nothing more to it' conclusion follows from the parts that are accurate.
