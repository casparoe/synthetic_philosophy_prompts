I'm putting together a paper on what Skolem's paradox actually shows about first-order set theory, and before I settle on a line I want a consistency check on the package of views I've drifted into. I currently hold all of the following:

(a) There is a unique intended model of set theory, the cumulative hierarchy V, and every sentence of the language of set theory is true or false relative to V.

(b) First-order ZFC is the correct theory of sets: its axioms capture the structural commitments of ordinary set-theoretic practice.

(c) Skolem's paradox is not a genuine paradox. The enumeration that makes a model of ZFC countable exists only in the background metatheory, not inside the model, so countability and uncountability are model-relative, and ordinary mathematical practice is untouched.

(d) The paradox nevertheless exposes a real limitation: first-order axioms cannot pin down the intended meaning of the powerset of the natural numbers, because the full powerset operation is essentially second-order and first-order axiomatization underdetermines it.

(e) The continuum hypothesis has a determinate truth value about V, notwithstanding its independence from ZFC.

I'd like an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension with one another, and which pairs only appear to be? Please order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would cost.
