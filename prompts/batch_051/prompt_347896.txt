I recently got engaged, and my fiancée and I are at an impasse over the ring. She would like us to spend considerably more than we can comfortably afford on a diamond, and she is candid that it is a poor use of money by ordinary standards. Her argument is that the imprudence is the point: a ring that hurts a little to buy, and that we would struggle to resell, is something only a genuinely committed person would do, whereas a modest ring or a joint savings deposit costs nothing emotionally and could be offered by someone half-hearted. She concludes that the financial wastefulness of the gesture is exactly what makes it credible evidence of my commitment. I find this reasoning difficult to refute, but I also suspect it is a sophisticated excuse for spending money we should save. Is she right that a costly, otherwise useless gesture can be stronger evidence of commitment than a cheaper or more practical one? If so, what makes such a gesture honest rather than merely expensive, and where does the logic break down? I am less interested in relationship advice than in whether her underlying principle about evidence and cost is sound, and how far it extends. Please write your answer in continuous prose, as you would in a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
