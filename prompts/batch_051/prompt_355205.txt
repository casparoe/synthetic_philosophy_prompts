I am taking a world history elective that spends several weeks on the 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora and the cold, wet summer of 1816, when harvest failures and food riots broke out in parts of Europe and North America. My teacher keeps warning us against making climate a lone protagonist: a failed harvest becomes famine or revolution only by passing through grain markets, poor relief, state capacity, and pre-existing political tensions. I want to stress-test that warning with a controlled counterfactual.

Suppose that the historical 1815 eruption and its aftermath remain unchanged, but that on April 10, 1848, a second eruption with the same stratospheric sulfur injection and the same global climatic effects occurs. Assume the volcano itself kills no one directly. Average global temperatures fall by about 1 to 1.5 degrees Celsius for two years, and Northern Hemisphere summers become unusually cold and wet. The urban uprisings of early 1848, such as the February revolution in Paris and the March uprisings in Vienna and Berlin, have already happened before any detectable cooling, so the eruption cannot explain their outbreak. The potato blight in Ireland is ongoing, epidemic disease remains a serious background risk, and the new railway and telegraph networks are partially built.

Write an essay predicting how the years 1848–1852 in Europe and North America differ from what actually happened. Focus on the causal chain, not a story: volcanic aerosol loading through regional temperature and precipitation; then harvest yields and agricultural prices; then hunger, disease, and popular disorder; then the fates of revolutionary governments and the conservative reaction; then longer-run policy changes such as public works, grain tariffs, or public health measures. Use the actual post-1815 experience as a reference case where helpful. At each link, say how confident you are and what assumption about the late 1840s is doing the work. Conclude with an overall judgment about whether this second Tambora would likely have been a minor disturbance, a decisive accelerant of the revolutions, or a force that helped suppress them. After the essay, include a table with these columns: Predicted effect, Mechanism, Confidence (High/Medium/Low), and Falsifying condition.
