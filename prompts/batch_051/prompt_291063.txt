An issue that has come up repeatedly in my distributive justice course is the stalled debate over the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism, and I am struck by how little the two sides have moved since the first week. As I understand the critics, Rawls charges utilitarianism with extending to society as a whole a principle of choice suited only to a single life, thereby running together what is acceptable within one person and what is acceptable between persons. Nozick presses the same point in more individualist terms: there is no social entity with a good over and above the good of the individual persons who compose it, so the kind of balancing that makes sense within one life cannot simply be carried across lives. The defenders of utilitarianism we have read tend to reply that everyone grants the factual distinctness of persons, but the question is what normative significance that fact has. They ask the critics to show why distinctness by itself rules out weighing benefits to some against harms to others, and they suspect the objection already assumes a prior anti-aggregation commitment. The critics then take that reply as refusing to face the original point.

My working hypothesis is that this is not really a disagreement about a further fact or about the logic of aggregation, but about what it would be to respect the separateness of persons in the first place: for the critics it is a normative principle limiting trade-offs between lives, while for the utilitarians we read it is only a description of where well-being is located. I am not confident in that diagnosis, though.

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive normative disagreement, a verbal disagreement over the phrase separateness of persons, two sides answering different questions under one heading, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for? Given the diagnosis, what would count as genuine progress, and is there reason to expect it? In the process, please separate what is actually settled about the structure of utilitarianism from what remains genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested reading of Rawls or Nozick as though it were the consensus.
