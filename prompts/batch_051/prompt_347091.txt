After a long career in experimental science, I have begun reading epistemology and keep circling back to Nozick's tracking theory. I have reconstructed an argument from it that seems to prove too much, and I would be grateful for a precise diagnosis.

(1) On Nozick's view, S knows p only if S's belief is sensitive: in the nearest possible worlds where p is false, S does not believe p by the method S actually uses.
(2) If S knows p and knows that p entails q, and S forms the belief that q solely by deducing it from p, then S knows q.
(3) Since p entails q, every world where q is false is a world where p is false; so if S's belief that p is sensitive, then in the nearest worlds where q is false S will not believe p and therefore will not deduce q from p.
(4) So deduction transmits sensitivity: a belief formed by deducing q from a known premise p is sensitive whenever the premise belief is.
(5) From (1), (2), and (4) it follows that the tracking theory preserves closure for anything we actually deduce from what we know.
(6) The familiar Nozick-style failures of closure, such as knowing I have hands without knowing I am not a handless brain in a vat, concern entailed claims that are not ordinarily held by deduction from the known proposition.
(7) Therefore, on the tracking view, I could come to know that I am not a brain in a vat simply by re-deriving that standing conviction as a deduction from the fact that I have hands, with no new evidence.
(8) That is absurd: re-routing a belief one already holds cannot manufacture knowledge.

I would like to keep (1) and (2); I cannot accept (8). Which step is false, and what does rejecting it commit one to? Please answer in exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
