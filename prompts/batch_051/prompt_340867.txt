I keep going in circles on whether it ever makes sense to say a company itself can be blamed, as opposed to the specific people inside it. On reflection I'm drawn to the individualist view: blame needs a subject that can intend, understand, and feel something like remorse, and a corporation is just a legal fiction — a nexus of contracts, reporting lines, and quarterly targets. It has no unified point of view, no conscience, no intentions that aren't just the messy overlapping intentions of its employees. So talk of 'blaming the company' is either sloppy shorthand for blaming some set of individuals, or its a category mistake. What I like about this view is that it's clean and it explains a lot: why we go hunting for scapegoats after a disaster, why legal liability and moral blame keep coming apart, why 'the company' can be fined while the actual culprits quietly keep their jobs.

But I can't shake the intuition in cases like this: a car manufacturer spends a decade installing software to cheat emissions tests, thousands of people involved in tiny pieces, executives who plausibly didn't know the specifics, shareholders who knew nothing — and yet it feels simply wrong to say no one is blameworthy for the cheating except a few engineers. The company knowingly lied to regulators over years, even if no single human held the whole lie in their head at once. If I blame only individuals, the blame gets so diffuse that it doesn't match the wrong. So I'm stuck between a theory that seems right on reflection and an intuition I can't honestly disown.

I dont want to be told which side wins. I want to know: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes (a story about anthropomorphism and how blame talk works?), what would it take for the theory to be the thing that goes (what would a corporate 'mind' have to look like for group-level blame to be more than metaphor?), and whether there's a reading on which both survive — say, company blame as a different kind of blame that doesn't need the machinery of individual moral agency. What should I give up, and what would justify giving it up?
