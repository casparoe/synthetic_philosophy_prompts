Im giving a seminar paper next month defending a restricted version of van Fraassen's Reflection principle. The rough idea: conditional on supposing that your future credence in A is r, your current credence in A should also be r, except in cases where you already have a specific reason to think future-you is less reliable than you are right now (amnesia, being drunk, doomscrolling, whatever). The audience is mostly formal epistemologists and decision theorists who already accept conditionalization and think diachronic Dutch book arguments have some force; their standing view is that Reflection overgenerates once we allow forgeting and irrational future selves, and that any restriction strong enough to dodge the counterexamples is too weak to be interesting. Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this group, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way they'd phrase it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Also tell me which objection or consideration should most realistically make me abandon the restricted principle (i.e., where my reasoning is most likely to fail), and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
