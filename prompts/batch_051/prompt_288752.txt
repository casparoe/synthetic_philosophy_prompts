We feel something like resentment when a competent adult carelessly harms us, yet we feel nothing of the kind toward a falling branch, a boisterous dog, or a small child who is not yet a responsible agent, even when the harm is equally severe and could in principle have been guarded against. What, if anything, justifies reserving the reactive attitudes for competent agents and taking a merely objective, causal stance toward everything else? Is this an asymmetry we should keep, revise, or abandon? In your answer, consider Strawson’s claim that the reactive attitudes are natural and are not adopted on account of their good effects, and say whether that claim dissolves the demand for justification or merely declines to answer it. Please write your answer in British English.
