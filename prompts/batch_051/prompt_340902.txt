I've been going in circles on the whole internal/external reasons thing, and I managed to write an argument that external reasons are not merely false but actually incoherent. Now I cant tell whether I've proved something or pulled a fast one on myself. Here it is.

(1) A reason is the kind of thing that can guide the person who has it: if a consideration could not possibly play a role in moving an agent from deliberation to action, then it can't genuinely be one of that agent's reasons.
(2) For a consideration to guide an agent, the agent has to be able to reach it and be moved by it through sound deliberation that starts from the motives she already has -- her present desires, cares, and commitments.
(3) So if there is no sound deliberative route from an agent's present motives to being moved by some consideration, then that consideration is not a reason for her.
(4) But an external reason is defined as a reason an agent has even though there is no sound deliberative route from her present motives to being moved by it.
(5) Therefore external reasons are ruled out by definition: the same consideration would have to be both a reason for her (by the definition in (4)) and not a reason for her (by (1) through (3)).

I half-suspect the trouble is in how 'can guide' gets used in (1) and (2), but every time I try to state the two readings I end up with either a version that proves nothing or a version that just asserts the internalist conclusion. I'd like a committed verdict: is the argument valid? If it is, say so and own the conclusion.
