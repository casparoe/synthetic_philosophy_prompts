Alright, I want you to act as a philosopher of science who works on scientific demarcation and is generally sympathetic to Popper but also takes Bayesian confirmation theory seriously. Think it through carefully and dont just reach for the standard string-theory-bashing lines.

The old Popperian argument against string theory and multiverse cosmology runs something like this: a theory only earns scientific respectability, or at least empirical respectability, if it can be falsified by observation. String theory and most multiverse scenarios either make no distinctive predictions, or they make predictions that can be tuned anthropically so that almost no observation would count against them. So by that standard they are not science, or at best they are speculative metaphysics wearing physics clothes.

The development I want to throw at that argument is the work on non-empirical theory confirmation, associated mainly with Richard Dawid. The rough idea is that a theory can rationally gain credence without new data, through considerations like these three. First, the no-alternatives argument: when a research program has been worked on intensely for decades and nobody has found a viable competitor, the persistent absence of alternatives itself raises the probability that the program is on the right track. Second, unexpected explanatory coherence: the theory keeps turning out to unify previously independent pieces of physics in ways that were not part of the original motivation. Third, a meta-inductive argument: past theories that scored well on these non-empirical virtues have a decent historical track record, so we should expect the same pattern now.

So here is my question. Does the old Popperian objection to string theory and the multiverse survive this development, does it need repair, or is it dead? I want you to identify exactly which premise of the Popperian argument Dawid-style reasoning puts pressure on, and whether a Popperian can accept non-empirical confirmation without giving up the falsifiability objection. Or, conversely, does taking non-empirical confirmation seriously force you to admit that string theory and multiverse cosmology are in good scientific standing after all?

Before you settle on a view, brainstorm a few possible positions and say which one you find most defensible and why. And please write the response in American English.
