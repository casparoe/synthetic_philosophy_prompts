Put together a decision tree for my medieval ethics seminar that sorts people by where they think the sin actually is in the run-up to a wrong act, with Abelard as the explicit anchor but not the whole story. Open the tree with a pair of concrete cases: one where a person consents inwardly to betray a friend and is prevented only by a phone call from doing it, and one where a person gets a rush of murderous delight from an unwanted fantasy but never consents or acts. Each node should be a yes/no question about which stage of the sequence carries the moral badness: the bare idea, the felt delight, the formed consent, the outward act, the failure to notice the thought early, or the disposition behind it. The leaves should be the positions in the logical space, not a list from a secondary source: the strict Abelardian consent-only view, an act-required view, a desire-plus-consent view, a negligence view, a disposition view, and a 'the stages aren't really separable' view. For each leaf give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any two leaves your nodes can't honestly pull apart, and say which unoccupied combinations in the logical space have no defenders and why that might be. Keep it usable for a smart undergrad who has never read Abelard; the questions should sort people by what they'd say about cases, not by whether they know the doctrine or which label their heading toward. Aim for around six leaves.
