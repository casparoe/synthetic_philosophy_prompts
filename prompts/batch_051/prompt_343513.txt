Hi — I'm a journalist, mostly on the science and ideas beat, and I've been asked to give a five-minute toast at my younger brother's wedding, aimed at our parents and his new in-laws. I said yes because of course you say yes, but I have a problem. For a piece I've been reporting, I've gone deep on the ethics of family obligations, and I've become quietly convinced by the friendship model of filial duties — the Jane English line that grown children don't owe their parents a debt for being raised, and that whatever duties do exist come out of a voluntary, ongoing relationship more like a friendship than like a loan. The trouble is that the default wedding toast move is to say something like "Mom and Dad, we can never repay you" or "we owe you everything," and I just can't bring myself to say that and mean it, and if I say it without meaning it everyone will be able to tell.

What I need is a toast that thanks them warmly — makes fun of my brother a little, makes my parents cry in a good way — but that doesn't lean on any debt, repayment, or "everything you did for us" bookkeeping language, because I think that whole frame is the wrong one for family love. It should hold up for two very different audiences: his new in-laws are traditional and would notice if the speech came off weirdly distant or noncommital, and a few old friends from the philosophy side of my beat will be at the table and will definitely notice if I cave and talk about "paying back" or "debts owed." Don't name any philosophers or use jargon in the speech itself — no "friendship model," obviously — but the reasoning has to stay consistent with the view. A small joke about how the point of a toast is precisely that no one is keeping the books would be welcome. Warm over clever, though.
