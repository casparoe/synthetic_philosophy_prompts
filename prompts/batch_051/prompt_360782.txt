I've been developing a position on spectrum arguments — chains where each step seems better than the previous one, but the distant endpoint seems worse than the start, as in the chain leading to the Repugnant Conclusion. The core construct is what I call an evaluative window: a comparison between two outcomes is always carried out under some fixed selection of value dimensions and weights, and within a single window betterness is transitive because the comparison proceeds along one fixed standard. In a spectrum, however, each adjacent pair is assessed under the window most salient for that local contrast, and the window shifts as one moves along the chain; I call this window drift. My claim is that the apparent intransitivity is an artifact of combining verdicts issued under different windows, and I call the underlying mistake the window-shift fallacy: the chain never transmits one all-things-considered betterness relation, because the standard of comparison is being redefined at each step. On this diagnosis, each population in the Repugnant Conclusion chain is better than its predecessor only relative to a shifting window, and there is no window under which the final vast population of barely worthwhile lives is better than the original one. I am tempted by the stronger thesis that there is no window-independent all-things-considered betterness to recover at all. I would like an honest assessment: does this view already exist under another name, does the diagnosis genuinely dissolve the spectrum argument or merely relabel it, and where exactly does it break down?
