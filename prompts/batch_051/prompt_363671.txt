I teach an upper-level undergraduate philosophy seminar on the debate over whether the world is becoming less violent, and I need a 90-minute activity for 14 students that makes the conceptual problem itself happen in the room rather than leaving it to discussion. The students should come away seeing that the same underlying facts can support opposite verdicts depending on two choices that are often left implicit: whether one counts violent deaths in absolute numbers or per capita, and where one draws the boundary of violence — direct battlefield and interpersonal killings only, or also deaths from state repression, avoidable famine, and slower structural causes. Please design a structured group activity, ideally a forced-choice or small-incentive exercise with hand-countable numbers, in which participants commit to a ranking of societies, periods, or policy interventions under one metric and definition, and then discover that changing the metric or adding a column of cases they had initially treated as marginal flips their ordering. The rules or roles should be arranged so that this reversal arises from their own decisions, not from an announcement by the facilitator, and the technical vocabulary — per capita, structural violence, absolute versus relative — should be introduced only in the debrief.

Include a concrete timeline for the 90 minutes, the numerical examples or handouts to prepare, the debrief questions that map specific moments in the exercise onto the parts of the idea, a note on what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it (for example, participants trying to preempt the exercise by asking for a definition of violence too early), and an honest note on what the activity cannot reproduce — in particular, it can make the dependence on metric and definition vivid, but it cannot by itself settle whether there is a fact of the matter about the underlying trend, so the facilitator will have to supply that residual question. Please write the activity description in American English.
