Construct a thought experiment that satisfies the following specification, and then show that it satisfies each part.

The case involves one ordinary system — a person, a workplace, an institution, or an everyday device — observed over a stretch of behavior, and it must be handled from three predictive stances of the kind Daniel Dennett describes: the physical stance (predict from the system's physical constitution), the design stance (predict from the function the system or its parts are built to perform), and the intentional stance (predict by ascribing beliefs and desires under a rationality assumption, without back-fitting beliefs after seeing the behavior).

Required verdicts. Given exactly the same observational record, a physical-stance modeler and an intentional-stance modeler must converge on the system's next observable behavior, while a competent design-stance modeler takes a different regularity to be the real pattern by design-stance criteria and therefore issues a different prediction — a prediction that fails in this case. The regularity driving the convergent prediction must be a genuine real pattern: it must earn its keep by predicting and compressing the system's behavior, not by being a post hoc fit.

Constraints. The design stance must not dissent because the system has malfunctioned, because it has been deceived, or because any relevant feature of the mechanism is hidden from the design-stance modeler alone; all three modelers have the same information. The case must also run opposite to the familiar fixed-pattern examples: do not produce a case in which the design stance correctly captures a rigid built-in response while the intentional stance predicts a flexible rational alternative. The required direction is that the physical and intentional stances agree against the design stance, with the design stance erring on the side of opportunism or context-sensitivity, while the intentional stance gets the case right by attributing a rational policy, commitment, or regulative standing rule that the design-stance modeler is structurally unable to count as an underlying functional pattern.

After the scenario, do all of the following: state what pattern each stance treats as real and why; state the next-behavior prediction each stance issues, showing the convergence and the dissent; identify the feature of the scenario that does the work; say what would happen if that feature were removed; and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the design stance, including the claim that the design-stance modeler mislocated the designed unit. Finally, state the point of the case: it should test whether the design stance's idealization is structurally unable to see a pattern that exists because a system governs itself by a representation of its own policy.
