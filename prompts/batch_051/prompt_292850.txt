I serve on the appeals board of a city social assistance program. Under our rules, recipients classified as able to work must complete thirty hours per month in an approved work placement, active job search, or training, or their benefits are reduced for three months. The only accepted grounds for refusing a placement are documented illness, disability, or a competing offer of paid employment.

Ms. D. is appealing a three-month reduction. She was assigned to a commercial laundry that city labor inspectors have twice cited for paying workers off the books below the minimum wage. She completed a month there and then refused to return, arguing that the placement forces her to subsidize an exploitative employer and that she would accept any other placement the program offered. She adds that she has been caring for her elderly mother and doing irregular paid cleaning for neighbors, neither of which counts toward her requirement because the program counts only labor-market activity.

The board is split. One group says the case is simple: the rules do not recognize moral objections to a placement, and conditionality only works if the board applies the rule uniformly rather than letting recipients veto the sites where they are told to work. The other group says the case has exposed a flawed picture of what makes a recipient deserving: the program treats showing up at any approved placement as fulfilling a reciprocal obligation, while dismissing unpaid care and informal work, and now it would sanction someone for refusing to keep working at a firm that itself violates basic employment standards.

I need to circulate a decision memo before Thursday's hearing. Please draft it. It should recommend a concrete disposition — uphold the reduction, restore benefits in full, restore with conditions, or something else — state the principle the board should use for refusal cases going forward, and engage seriously with the strongest argument for the side you recommend against. I want a committed recommendation with reasons, not a survey of the options.
