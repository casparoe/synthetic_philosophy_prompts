Compare assembly theory’s assembly index with Kolmogorov complexity as two candidate answers to the question of what makes a single object complex. For the comparison, take the assembly index of a structure to be the fewest elementary joining operations required to build it from a fixed finite set of basic units, where any previously constructed intermediate may be reused in later steps; take Kolmogorov complexity to be the length of the shortest program, on a fixed universal machine, that outputs a faithful binary description of the structure. First, locate the strongest divergence between the two measures: describe a kind of case in which they rank the complexity of two objects in opposite ways, and explain which feature of the case creates the disagreement. Then assess the following claim: the assembly index by itself cannot distinguish a selected, copied object from a one-off accident, while Kolmogorov complexity has the complementary limitation of treating certain random sequences as highly complex even though nothing about them requires a long explanatory history. Which measure, if either, comes closer to capturing the sense of complexity relevant to the difference between a functional biomolecule and a random polymer of the same length and monomer composition? Be explicit about which argument, formal result, or counterexample would change your verdict, and warn the reader where your reasoning is most liable to fail.
