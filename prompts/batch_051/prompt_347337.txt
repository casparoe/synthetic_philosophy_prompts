You are a philosopher of public policy who specializes in paternalism, nudging, and the ethics of behavioral interventions. A fictional state has a standing Health Policy Ethics Panel that issues non-binding recommendations on proposed health measures. The panel has compiled the following record between 2018 and 2027. Each entry gives the proposal and the panel's recommendation, with a brief note on the stated rationale.

1. A requirement that chain-restaurant menus display calorie counts: Approve. The panel called it information supplied at the point of choice.
2. Automatic enrollment of all workers into the state retirement-savings plan, with a one-page opt-out form: Approve. The panel called it a low-cost default rather than coercion.
3. Graphic health warnings covering half of the front and back of cigarette packs: Approve. The panel called it information about risk, while acknowledging that the images are emotionally forceful.
4. A modest statewide excise tax on sugar-sweetened beverages: Approve. The majority called it a price signal that keeps the purchase legal and raises revenue for public-health programs.
5. A cap on sugary-drink serving sizes in restaurants and cinemas at 16 ounces: Reject. The majority said it told adults how much soda they could buy and doubted it would clearly reduce total consumption.
6. A ban on buying sugar-sweetened beverages with SNAP benefits: Reject. The majority called it stigmatizing and said it singled out low-income households rather than the product.
7. A five-business-day waiting period between applying for and receiving a payday loan: Reject. The majority thought it had uncertain benefits and could push some borrowers toward worse informal credit.
8. Raising the tobacco purchasing age from 18 to 21: Approve. The majority reasoned that tobacco is uniquely lethal and that the change extends an existing age restriction rather than imposing a new ban on existing adult smokers.
9. A ban on artificial trans fats in public-school meals and school vending machines: Approve. The majority emphasized the captive audience of children and adolescents.

The panel now faces a new proposal: a statewide ban on selling any prepared food containing partially hydrogenated oils, the main source of artificial trans fats, in restaurants, cafeterias, and retail prepared-food counters, with an 18-month phase-in. Reformulation is technically feasible, substitute oils are already in wide use, and trade groups no longer dispute that artificial trans fats raise cardiovascular risk.

Begin by brainstorming the principle or principles that best rationalize this record. There is almost certainly more than one defensible candidate; identify and articulate the strongest competing principles, and show how each fits the nine decisions. Then, for each candidate principle, state exactly what it implies about the proposed ban. Give your best prediction of how the panel would come down if it followed each principle, and state which recommendation the panel would or should make if it wants to be consistent with its own track record. Identify the one or two past cases that are the closest analogs to the trans-fat proposal, and explain which similarities and disanalogies carry the most weight. Where the record underdetermines the answer, say so explicitly. Finally, propose one further case the panel could consider that would cleanly separate the leading candidate principles, and explain what each principle predicts for it.

Think carefully, reason through the alternatives before committing, and write in American English.
