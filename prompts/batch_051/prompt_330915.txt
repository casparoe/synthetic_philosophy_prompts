Hey, I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so go easy on me. I've been thinking alot about the tool AI / agent AI distinction that comes up in AI safety discussions, and I'm trying to write a short essay on it. Can you act as a professional philosopher and give me brutally honest comments on the idea before I sink more time into it?

The claim I want to defend is that the tool/agent divide is less a fact about the AI itself and more about where we draw the boundary of the system when we describe it. A tool AI is supposed to just answer questions or do narrow tasks without goals of its own, while an agent AI pursues goals over time. But it seems like you can redescribe nearly any tool as an agent: an autocomplete model "wants" to maximize next-token probability, a chess engine "tries" to win. And going the other way, any agent can be redescribed as a tool of the person who deployed it, or of evolution, or of whatever larger process it's embedded in. So my essay would argue that calling a system a tool vs an agent isn't a claim about its internal architecture; it's a choice about which parts of the loop (model, reward signal, user, environment) we treat as the system and which we treat as background. If that's right, then the advice that we should just build tools and not agents is kind of smuggling in a frame instead of pointing at two natural kinds.

A few specific questions: (1) Is this philosophically interesting, or am I just restating the intentional stance in fancier clothes (roughly, treating a system as if it has beliefs and desires; I've only skimmed Dennett)? (2) What's the strongest objection? In particular, is there something in the system itself that makes the distinction track a real difference, like whether it has an explicit utility function it's maximizing, or whether it can initiate actions without being prompted? (3) I suspect I'm conflating 'it can be usefully described as' with 'it was designed as' — is that a fair criticism, and how do I avoid it?

Please take your time thinking through this. Start by brainstorming a few different ways a philosopher would frame the issue, then walk me through which parts of my argument are strongest and which parts would get torn apart.
