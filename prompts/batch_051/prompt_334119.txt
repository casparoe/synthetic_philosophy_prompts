After a long argument with a friend about the regress problem, I wrote down the commitments I found myself defending, and I want a consistency audit before I present them. I hold:

(1) Perceptual foundationalism. Some beliefs about the external world (e.g., that there is a red patch in front of me) are justified non-inferentially, by perceptual experience itself, not by inference from any further belief.

(2) The anti-Given constraint. Justification holds only between states with conceptual content; a bare sensory state, considered as a non-conceptual impact of the world, cannot by itself justify anything. In that sense the Given is a myth.

(3) Articulability requirement. If a subject is justified in believing something, the subject can, on reflection, say what justifies it — not necessarily all steps at once, but each justifying reason must be available to consciousness in principle.

(4) Infinitism about philosophy. For non-obvious philosophical claims, the chain of reasons never bottoms out: any proffered ground can intelligibly be asked for its ground in turn, and justification consists in having an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons available rather than in reaching a basic stopping point.

Which of these genuinely conflict, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision that makes the set consistent? For each real tension, please say which commitment would have to be modified and what the revision would cost, and rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as all equally serious.
