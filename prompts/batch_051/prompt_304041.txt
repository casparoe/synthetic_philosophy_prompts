I've been reading rationalist blogs long enough to have internalized the idea that when a debate never converges, someone is usually using the key word in two slightly different senses without noticing it. Lately I keep seeing a version of this in Zeno's paradoxes, and I don't have a name for it beyond "moving the goalposts," which feels wrong because nobody in these conversations is arguing in bad faith. The pattern goes like this. I point out that the Dichotomy is defused by the fact that 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... converges to 1, and someone replies that the mathematics only tells you the limit is reachable in the model, not how the runner performs infinitely many separate acts. I then give a physical account — at each instant the runner is simply at a location, with no "next instant" — and they reply that this still doesn't explain how a continuous stretch of time can be decomposed into a completed infinity, or how the traversal ever gets going at all. Whatever level the proposed solution addresses, the puzzle relocates one level deeper and comes out intact, so it never gets answered, only re-described.

Same thing with Achilles: first it's about the infinite series, then it's about whether catching up is itself an action, then it's about how there can be a first moment of overlap if between any two moments there's another. I'm not asking for another solution to the paradoxes. I want to know whether this pattern — a puzzle that survives every proposed solution by migrating to a subtler reading of the same term, while each individual migration is perfectly reasonable — has a name in the literature, or whether it's just what it means for a paradox to rest on an ambiguous formulation. If it has no name, what would you call it? I'm not going to be satisfied with a bare label; I want the carving: what makes this different from ordinary goalpost-moving, why it's compatible with everyone arguing sincerely, and where the line is between a real unsolved puzzle and a puzzle that is being protected from resolution. Also, please say how confident you are in the concepts you point to and which part of your answer you're least sure of.
