I do policy analysis for a civil-liberties nonprofit. Every week we have to file a short memo on a proposed city ordinance: does it make people freer or less free. We keep hitting cases where nobody is literally stopping anyone from doing anything but the freedom still feels missing — a "just cause" eviction rule, an at-will boss who never actually fires anyone, a bonus for waiving a rest break. I'm told there's a real line here between negative liberty (nobody interferes) and republican liberty (nobody can interfere on arbitrary terms), maybe positive liberty too (actually being able to do the thing). Turn that into a checklist my team can run in an afternoon, no philospher in the room, before the director signs off. Write the procedure: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the negative, republican, and positive verdicts diverge and what the procedure says should happen when they do, which steps are values calls rather than analysis calls, and what the checklist can't decide. Then run it on three cases — a city curfew for under-18s, a law requiring landlords to give a reason for non-renewal, and an employer that pays extra to workers who waive a rest break — and make at least one of them come out in a way we'd hate. At the very end, give a machine-readable verdict line: \answer{curfew: expand/shrink/split; non-renewal: expand/shrink/split; break-waiver: expand/shrink/split}.
