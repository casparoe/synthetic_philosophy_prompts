I keep trying to defend the idea that idolatry is a real category — that you can value something the wrong way, not merely too much — but my attempt collapses into an argument that all love is idolatry. Here it is:

(1) Idolatry is treating a finite good as the highest good: loving it as if it alone could fully satisfy you and nothing above it were needed.
(2) Any genuine love takes the beloved's good as an end, not merely as a means.
(3) Acting for an end involves treating that end as worth pursuing for its own sake, at least within that action.
(4) Treating something as worth pursuing for its own sake is treating it as the highest good.
(5) Therefore all genuine love treats the beloved as the highest good.
(6) Therefore all genuine love is idolatry.

The conclusion looks obviously false — loving your kid isn't idolatry — but I can't see where the arguement cheats. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails and give the corrected version of that step. If it is valid, tell me why the 'obviously false' verdict is itself the mistake.
