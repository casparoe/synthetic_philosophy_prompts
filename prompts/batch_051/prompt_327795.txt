The weird thing about blackmail is that the two pieces are fine seperately: it's generally okay to tell people true, embarrassing stuff about someone, and it's generally okay to ask someone for $50. But when you connect them — "give me $50 or I'll tell" — suddenly it's a crime, or at least obviously wrong. I want a working definition of blackmail that explains that line and gets these cases right:

- "Give me $50 or I'll send the screenshots of your bought essay to the teacher." (definitely blackmail)
- "Fix the fence your dog broke or I'll take you to small claims court." (not blackmail)
- "I'm not gonna tell anyone you copied that essay. But my silence costs $100." (this sounds like an offer, but I think it's still blackmail?)
- "Donate $50 to the school food drive or I'll tell everyone you cheated on the chem final." (blackmail even though the money doesn't go to the threatener?)
- "Give me $20 or I'll tell people I saw you helping an old lady carry her groceries." (not blackmail, just weird?)
- "Turn yourself in by Friday or I'll report that you stole the answer key." (probably not blackmail, even though it's a threat)

Propose the definition, run it through all six, and say where it gives a weird answer or surprises you. Also say how confident you are in the definition and which point you're least sure about.
