I've been trying to make a distinction precise—between a number that an author placed in a text as an intended symbolic structure and a number that a later reader finds by running enough numerological operations that a hit was almost guaranteed. The case that keeps forcing the issue for me is the genealogy that opens the Gospel of Matthew. The text reports three sets of fourteen generations: from Abraham to David, from David to the Babylonian deportation, and from the deportation to the Messiah. One standard explanation for the number is that fourteen is the Hebrew gematria value of David's name (the letters dalet, waw, dalet: 4 + 6 + 4), so the genealogy is making a Davidic point by arithmetic. But the actual list looks as though it has been shaped to fit the pattern: the middle block reaches fourteen only by compressing the line of kings (the text has Joram as the father of Uzziah, passing over several kings given in the older royal lists), and the final block reaches fourteen only if Jechoniah is counted in both the second and third blocks.

I want you to analyze this episode through the lens of interpretation versus pattern-hunting, and to run the analysis in both directions. First, take the distinction as a working theory: what should a proponent of the David-gematria reading have had to establish before the fourteen-pattern counts as intended symbolism rather than post hoc pattern-hunting? Be concrete about the degrees-of-freedom problem—alternative spellings, other names that sum to fourteen, how many numerological patterns the same text could plausibly support—and about whether Matthew's explicit three-times-fourteen frame is itself independent evidence of intent or simply the hypothesis in need of explanation. Say where the distinction is silent rather than forcing it. Second, use the episode to test the distinction: does this case show that intended symbolism and pattern-hunting are cleanly separable, or does it show that the boundary breaks down once you think about multiple comparisons and overfitting? End with a clear verdict on what the case does to the theory—confirmation, anomaly, or a case it never anticipated.
