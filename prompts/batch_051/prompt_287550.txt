Act as a professional philosopher of mind and take this slowly, thinking out loud before you commit. I want to know whether two famous moves in the intentionality debate can be reconciled.

First, in the Chinese Room argument, Searle maintains that a program's manipulation of symbols is purely formal, and that no amount of formal symbol-pushing can by itself add up to meaning, understanding, or semantics. He then generalizes: brains have original intentionality, while anything we build has only derived intentionality, borrowed from its designers or interpreters and attributed to it in a sort of as-if way.

Second, in Dennett's two-bitser reply, he points at a humble vending-machine coin validator that accepts quarters and rejects slugs. Dennett's suggestion is that this device's internal states really are about quarter-hood—its a real discriminator, shaped by its history and design—and that the original/derived distinction Searle leans on is not doing any clear work. On Dennett's view there is no special original intentionality; our own intentionality is also derived, ultimately from natural selection.

On their face these are contradictory: one says the distinction marks a deep gulf, the other says its bogus. Start by brainstorming the most charitable reading of each claim. Then run through the candidate reconciliations: they might mean different things by intentionality; Searle might be making a point about conscious understanding while Dennett is making a point about when our ascriptions are warranted; Dennett's appeal to natural selection might answer an origin question while Searle is raising a constitution question. For each candidate, say whether it is an honest reading or special pleading, and give a final verdict: can someone accept Dennett's coin-validator point and still insist Searle was right about something important, or is that position unstable?
