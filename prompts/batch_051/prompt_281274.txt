I am a PhD student in moral philosophy writing a chapter on retrospective moral evaluation, and I need an essay that takes a side rather than merely surveying positions. Please write an essay that addresses the following question.

When a present-day critic condemns a historical figure—Thomas Jefferson for enslaving people, Winston Churchill for his commitments to empire, or Christopher Columbus for his treatment of Indigenous peoples in the Americas—a standard reply is that the person was a man of his time. Under scrutiny this reply slides among at least four distinct claims: the agent lacked the concepts necessary to recognize the wrong, and so was not culpable; the agent had the concepts but faced social pressures that excuse or mitigate the act; the act was wrong but retrospective blame is unfair because the agent's culture normalized it; and it is illegitimate to apply present moral standards to past agents at all because the standards apply only within a time or culture.

The essay should determine whether the man-of-his-time defense is best reconstructed as one of these claims rather than the others, or whether its apparent force depends on moving between them; and it should say what this implies for the defensibility of retrospective moral judgments about at least one of Jefferson, Churchill, or Columbus. Use the chosen figure as a test case, but do not turn the paper into an exercise in historical reconstruction; treat widely known facts as fixed for the sake of argument.

Weigh the strongest considerations for each reconstruction, then defend a determinate conclusion. Do not end on an unqualified statement that the answer depends on the circumstances; if the correct conclusion is genuinely conditional, state the condition precisely and defend that conditional conclusion as the thesis. Avoid citations and bibliographies, since I will add the scholarly apparatus in a later pass. Write the essay in American English.
