I have been trying to make sense of the claim that cultural variants such as jokes, songs, and recipes genuinely evolve by natural selection rather than merely changing in a loose sense of the word. I keep arriving at an argument whose conclusion I cannot accept, and I cannot find where it goes wrong. It runs as follows.

(1) Natural selection requires only a population of copying entities that vary, in which at least some of that variation is passed on to further copies, and in which some variants copy themselves more successfully than others because of the features that are copied.

(2) Cultural variants—jokes, melodies, recipes, rituals, architectural forms—plainly satisfy these conditions: they vary, they are copied with modifications, and some persist and spread while others die out partly because of the very features that get copied.

(3) Therefore, cultural change is natural selection in the strict, non-metaphorical sense, and calling the units of cultural selection "memes" is no more metaphorical than calling the units of biological inheritance "genes."

(4) If (1) states all that natural selection requires, then natural selection is not tied to DNA, to cells, or even to living things. The same conditions are met by any copying process with occasional error and differential success—for example, a self-copying computer program whose copying errors make some copies reproduce faster, or a chain letter that accumulates modifications as it circulates.

(5) But this is where the argument seems to prove too much. It forces us to say that a self-copying computer program or a mutating chain letter undergoes natural selection in exactly the same sense as a population of finches, which turns "natural selection" into a label for mere differential copying and strips it of the explanatory work it does in accounting for cumulative adaptive complexity.

(6) So at least one of (1)–(4) must be false, though each seemed plausible on its own. Which step, if any, do you reject, and what does rejecting it imply about whether memes are more than a metaphor? If instead you would accept the conclusion, say what, if anything, then distinguishes natural selection from mere differential copying.
