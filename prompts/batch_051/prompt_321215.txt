Harry Frankfurt opens _On Bullshit_ with this observation:

"One of the most salient features of our culture is that there is so much bullshit. Everyone knows this. Each of us contributes his share. But we tend to take the situation for granted. Most people are rather confident of their ability to recognize bullshit and to avoid being taken in by it."

I am a novelist at work on a story whose protagonist uses a large language model as a research assistant. The model invents a plausible-sounding historical event—a minor legislative battle in Restoration England that never happened—and presents it with evident confidence. I want to know, with as much precision as I can get, what the model is doing. Give me your actual credence that a typical LLM hallucination of this kind is best classified as bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, rather than as mere error or as lying. Decompose that number: how much rides on whether the model has any functional analogue of the bullshitter's indifference to how things really are; how much on whether the category requires a subject capable of caring about the truth at all; how much on whether the human user, not the model, is the bullshitter; and how much on whether hallucination is simply a failure mode with no intentional structure behind it. Which sub-question would move your final number most, and what argument or evidence would move it?
