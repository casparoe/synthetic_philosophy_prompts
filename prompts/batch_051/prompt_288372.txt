A government proposes replacing its entire public-assistance system with a single "luck insurance" scheme. Any citizen may claim compensation for a loss of income, health, or other resources. An independent board awards full compensation when it classifies the loss as brute luck — a bad outcome that was neither chosen nor reasonably avoidable — and awards nothing when it classifies the loss as option luck, that is, the foreseeable downside of a voluntary gamble or a chosen way of life. The scheme is funded by a flat tax, and the board's rulings are made public, treated as precedents, and open to appeal. Treat this as a mechanism rather than taking a position on its justice, and analyze how it would actually function. What would it incentivize in people's choices, in the way they frame and schedule claims, and in the behavior of the board itself? Where would the brute/option distinction first break down under pressure? Who would game it most effectively, and can the failure modes be fixed without quietly rebuilding the welfare state the scheme replaced?
