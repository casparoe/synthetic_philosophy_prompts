I've been going back and forth with a friend about a position she calls "tranquilism," and she's wore me down enough that I honestly can't tell how probable the view is anymore. Her formulation: the only thing with final value—value for its own sake, not just as an instrument or a stepping stone—is the state of having no craving and no suffering, and these two absences are not two separate goods but one unified state she calls tranquility. If a pleasure left your net craving-and-suffering balance exactly unchanged, it would be worth exactly zero on her view. My brother thinks this is obviously false: he offers the case of someone who knowingly takes on decades of craving and suffering to raise a child, and says the love involved is good in itself even though it is about as far from tranquil as a life gets. I keep feeling the pull of her view and then the force of his, and I want a cold number out of you, not a "both sides" answer.
