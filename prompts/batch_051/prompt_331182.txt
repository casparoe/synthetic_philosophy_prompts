I sit on the interpretation committee of a regional museum of cultural history, and we are being asked to approve the interpretative plan for a permanent gallery on the emergence of the earliest kingdoms in the region. One display has divided the committee. The curators propose to present the four figures described in later records as the guardian-deities of the founding dynasty as having begun as living human rulers and war leaders: they claim the oldest king lists record them as ordinary names, and that they were only later reclassified as gods through successive generations of funerary cult and court ritual. Some of us regard this as a responsible, demystifying account of how early political authority came to be treated as sacred. Others argue that the inference from a name in a list to a historical person is weak, and that the display would implicitly demote the account held by a small living community for whom the four have always been gods.

The gallery opens in nine months, and the committee must decide whether to approve the display as written, approve it with a revised framing, or reject the approach. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one of those three options, specify any framing changes the curators would have to make, and engage fairly with the strongest consideration on the losing side. The answer should be written in American English.
