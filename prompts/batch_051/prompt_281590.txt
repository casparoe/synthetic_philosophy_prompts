I'm giving a paper in a few weeks at my department's political philosophy workshop and I'm already dreading the Q&A. The thesis I'm defending is roughly that there's a distinctive kind of unfreedom in being subject to someone else's arbitrary power, even if that power is never actually exercised against you: the slave with an indulgent master, the employee whose boss could fire her for no reason but happens to like her, the tenant whose landlord could evict him at will but doesn't. My line is that this kind of unfreedom — I've been calling it domination, following the neo-republicans — isn't reducible to Berlin's negative liberty, since there's no actual interference, and isn't the same as positive liberty or self-mastery either, since a dominated person can be perfectly autonomous. The tricky bit I'm leaning on is that interference and domination come apart in both directions: a well-ordered state that forces me to stop at a red light interferes with me but doesn't dominate me, while the kindly slaveholder dominates without interfering. So I'm arguing non-domination, not non-interference, is the thing we should mean by political freedom. I'm defining 'arbitrary' roughly as power that isn't forced to track the interests or the say-so of the person it's exercised over, but I'm honestly still shaky on that definition, and I suspect that's where a lot of the objections will land.

The audience is mostly classical-liberal and libertarian political philosophers who think freedom just is absence of interference; two of them know the Berlin stuff cold, one early modern historian will probably complain that the neo-republicans are reading Machiavelli wrong (or reading the Romans through Machiavelli, or something), and there's a grad student who keeps insisting the whole debate is just a verbal dispute about the word 'freedom.'

What I want is a prep sheet for the questions. Give me the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way they'd phrase them — including the obvious 'so traffic lights dominate me?' and 'isn't this just negative liberty with a probability attached?' moves if that's what you think they'll say. For each: how much damage it does, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and a short (two-minute-ish) reply. Be honest about where each reply is weakest. And include the question I should be praying nobody asks.
