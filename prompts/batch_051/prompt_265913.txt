In the first section of Embedded Agency, Abram Demski and Scott Garrabrant contrast the standard agent framework, which assumes a Cartesian boundary between agent and environment, with an embedded agent that is itself part of the world it models, reasons under limited time and computational resources, and can sometimes observe or modify its own cognitive processes. Their central conceptual claim seems to be that once the agent is embedded in this way, its world-model cannot be simultaneously complete, consistent, and ahead of itself without some form of circularity or incompleteness. I am taking a course on agency and reinforcement learning, and I keep wondering whether this claim is empirically testable or whether it is really a claim about conceptual boundaries. Design the best concrete test you can: suppose I construct two versions of a simple agent, one with a clean world-model that updates by standard Bayesian conditioning and one whose own code, observations, and update process are part of the environment it can partially observe and modify. What result would count as evidence that the embedded agent faces a distinctive problem the Cartesian agent does not, what result would count against that, and what part of the claim would no such test settle even in principle?
