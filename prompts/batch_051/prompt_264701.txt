So my dad had a small stroke earlier this year and the strangest thing is that he'll tell you his hand is burning or cramping, but he says it the way he says the weather is humid — he feels it but he doesn't mind it. The neurologist called it pain asymbolia, and I've been reading around since. I've landed on a view I want you to push on: that the badness of pain is not actually in the sensation itself. On this picture, the hurting part is one thing — sensory-discriminative stuff like location, intensity, quality — and the awful part is a separate evaluative/conative layer. Pain is bad because you actively dislike or reject it, and in asymbolia that layer is missing while the raw sensation stays. So pain's unpleasantness isnt baked into the feeling; its more like a response that normally rides on top of it.

What I want is a map of what would count against that dissociability view — not the standard pain-is-just-a-quale deflection, but the real defeaters. Which intuitions do I need to be wrong about? What possible cases would the view struggle to describe: someone with the badness but no localizable sensation, someone who withdraws from the stimulus but reports no felt badness, a creature that behaves exactly like it's in agony while the caring layer is gone? What empirical findings about asymbolics, the cingulate, opioids, placebo, mood would be merely awkward and which would actually force me to abandon the view? What does the view predict that a badness-is-intrinsic rival doesn't, and how much should a failed prediction move me? Are there arguments that the separate layer is just a modeling artifact, or that the view quietly collapses back into the intrinsic-badness view it was meant to replace? And if I can qualify the view to survive everything, have I just made it unfalsifiable, and should I care?

Sort the defeaters from decisive down to merely awkward, say which of them a committed defender of the view should honestly concede count against it rather than dismiss, and give me a straight verdict on how exposed the position really is.
