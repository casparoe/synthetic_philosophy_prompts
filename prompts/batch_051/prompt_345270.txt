I've been trying to pin down my actual view of consciousness in five bullet points, because every time I explain it to a friend they tell me it's internally inconsistent, but none of them can point to the exact spot. So I'm putting the list here for an honest audit before I commit to it. The five things I believe, as plainly as I can state them:

(1) I'm a physicalist — there is no stuff over and above the physical.
(2) Consciousness is a user illusion in the Dennett-ish sense: what introspection gives me is a cleaned-up, simplified interface to the brain's activity, not a transparent window onto the real machinery.
(3) There is something it is like to see red; I'm not an eliminativist about experience itself.
(4) First-person access to my own current experience is special — if I'm feeling a sharp pain, I can't be wrong that I'm in pain.
(5) The hard problem of consciousness is mostly an artifact of taking that interface to literally, so it gets dissolved rather than solved.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which pairs only look that way and dissolve once you're careful about the terms. For any genuine tensions, rank them by how severe they are, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Then — because I want to know how much of your audit to trust — tell me which of your judgments here you're least confident in, and what argument or case would change your mind about that part.
