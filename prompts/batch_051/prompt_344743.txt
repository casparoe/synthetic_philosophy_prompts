Here's the assignment. You two have spent the term defending, respectively, transworld identity about individuals and Lewisian counterpart theory. So one of you holds that "I might have been a plumber" is true because there is a possible world—a maximal way things could be, not some far away planet—in which I, the very same individual, am a plumber. The other holds it's true because there is some other world containing a counterpart of me, sufficiently similar and salient in the relevant context, who is a plumber, since strictly speaking no individual exists in more than one world. Now write the document the two of you should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Format it in three parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: what you both accept about possible worlds semantics, about the truth conditions for de re modal ascriptions, about what the Humphrey objection and the standard replies actually establish, and about how anyone has epistemic access to modal facts. Second, a disagreements table with precise statements of what remains contested, each side's own formulation of its view, and a column saying whether that row is a substantive disagreement or, in the end, just verbal. Third, the cruxes—the few sub-questions such that settling one of them would actually move one of you—each with a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance.

Some candidate cruxes to pressure-test: whether the counterpart relation can simply be declared to be identity-at-a-world without quietly changing the theory; whether Humphrey's reaction is semantic evidence or just psychology; whether context-sensitivity in de re ascriptions is a feature of a good semantics or an admission of defeat. And use at least one de re case other than Humphrey—say "this table could have been made of ice" or "I could have had a different sibling"—and show exactly how each camp treats it.

The document should be good enough that a committed Kripkean and a committed Lewisian could each sign every agreed claim without wincing, and each would look at the disagreements table and say, yes, that is what I think, and that is what's wrong with the other side. If one of your supposed disagreements dissolves once you phrase it carefully, say so and mark it verbal. Don't keep a fight alive just to have one.
