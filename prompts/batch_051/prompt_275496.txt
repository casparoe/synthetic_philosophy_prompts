I'm a decision theory PhD student and money pump arguments keep tripping me up. Help me work through the cleanest version I can construct.

Imagine an agent whose pairwise preferences over three goods are genuinely cyclic: A strictly preferred to B, B to C, and C to A. The agent will pay a $0.50 transaction fee to swap whenever the offered good is one they strictly prefer, and they choose myopically — just comparing the two options in front of them, no look-ahead, no noticing that they've seen this sequence before. They start holding A. A trader offers A→C, then C→B, then B→A, and then repeats those same three offers until the agent stops accepting.

Predict how this plays out — how many offers before the agent stops, if ever, where they end up, and how much they've lost. I mostly care about the chain of reasoning, so seperate the prediction into robust links and fragile links, giving your confidence in each and saying what would have to be different for that link to fail. Then tell me whether this shows the agent's preferences are irrational, or only that the agent is exploitable — and whether a rational agent with the same cyclic preferences could avoid the pump by making a plan now and sticking to it.

Return a JSON object with these fields: `prediction` (object with `offers_accepted_before_stopping`, `final_state`, `total_loss`), `robust_links` (array of objects with `link`, `mechanism`, `confidence` (high/medium/low plus a phrase), `fails_if`), `escape_routes` (array of objects with `strategy`, `mechanism`, `cost`, `would_a_rational_agent_use_it`), and `verdict` (object with `irrational_or_merely_exploitable` and `one_paragraph_argument`).
