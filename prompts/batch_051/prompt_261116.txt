I teach an introduction to mathematical reasoning course for philosophy and computer science students — nineteen students, one 75-minute session — and I want to devote the session to Russell's paradox. The students have already worked with an unrestricted comprehension principle: for any condition on objects, there is a set whose members are exactly the things satisfying the condition. They have used it to build familiar sets like unions and intersections, and it feels innocent to them. I don't want to lecture the paradox at them, and I don't want the name "Russell's paradox" spoken until after they have hit the wall themselves. You are an expert on the pedagogy of formal philosophy and early set theory. Design a classroom game or structured activity — something with rules, roles, and real classification decisions — in which the participants run headlong into the contradiction as a consequence of a procedure they themselves endorsed. It should need no special materials beyond index cards, a marker, and two bins or two sides of the room. Include the exact rules, the roles, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened in the activity onto the formal steps of the paradox: the unrestricted comprehension principle, the definition of the Russell set, and the derivation of the contradiction. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it.

One thing I want you to think through honestly: I suspect the activity can make the paradox feel like a local glitch — one naughty set that will not stay sorted — whereas the real force of the paradox is that a rule the students trusted turns out to be inconsistent, and the standard response is to restrict the very principle that made the game possible. Say which feature of the paradox the activity cannot reproduce and how I should fill that gap in the debrief. Before settling on the final design, brainstorm two or three possible game mechanics and explain why the one you choose produces the contradiction most organically, rather than planting it by fiat. Think carefully about the exact moment the room should go quiet.
