I keep seeing people defend the expected utility axioms by saying that anyone who violates them could be money-pumped or Dutch-booked. For instance, if my preferences run A > B, B > C, but C > A, I'd pay a small fee to trade C for B, B for A, and A for C, ending up back at C minus the fees. But that only works if I keep accepting each trade one at a time. In any realistic setting I'd notice the cycle and stop, or precommit to refusing trades that would complete one. Doesn't the fact that I can just refuse the later trades show these arguments only demonstrate that myopic people can be exploited, not that coherent preferences are rationally required?
