I'm working on a term project for my school's independent study in philosophy, and I have been trying to get clear on the dispute between Humeans and anti-Humeans about laws of nature. As I understand it, the Humean says that a law of nature is not an extra ingredient in reality that pushes events around; it is just a true universal generalization that earns its place in the best systematization of all the particular facts, where the best systematization is the one with the best overall trade-off between simplicity and strength. The anti-Humean says this leaves something out, because laws do not merely summarize how things happen; they constrain or necessitate the course of events. I am drawn to the Humean picture because it is economical, but I built an argument that seems to show it collapses.

Here is the argument. P1: if the Humean picture is right, a generalization is a law only relative to a best systematization. P2: a systematization is best only relative to a particular way of trading off simplicity against strength. P3: nothing in the facts themselves forces a single correct trade-off; the systemizer must bring standards to the task. P4: so two careful systemizers with different standards could build different best systems for the very same facts. P5: in that case, one and the same true generalization — for example, the regularity that all the coins in my pocket are made of copper — could be an axiom of one best system and left out of the other. P6: since the facts are identical either way, there would be no fact of the matter about whether that regularity is a law; it would be a law relative to one set of standards and a mere coincidence relative to another. P7: therefore, on the Humean picture, the distinction between laws and accidental regularities is not a feature of the world. P8: but that distinction is a feature of the world, since there is an obvious difference between salt's dissolving in water and the coins in my pocket all happening to be copper. So the Humean picture must be false.

I would most like to keep P8, but I do not want to beg the question against the Humean, and I need to know exactly where the argument goes wrong, if it does, and what each repair would cost. Please give your diagnosis as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
