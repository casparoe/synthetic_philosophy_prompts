For my metaphysics seminar on the ontology of time, I am organizing a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a presentist A-theorist, who holds that only present entities exist, and a B-theoretic eternalist, who holds that all times and their contents exist tenselessly and that tense is indexical. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. It should include: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides accept about tensed language, about the literal truth of ordinary past-tensed statements, and about the empirical evidence any account must accommodate; (2) a disagreements table giving each side's central commitments in wording its own proponents would regard as fair, with the residual disagreement stated precisely; and (3) a cruxes section. Among the cruxes, address at least the truthmaker problem for past-tensed truths, the bearing of special relativity on an absolute present, and the significance of tensed attitudes such as relief that an episode is past. For each crux, specify the consideration or case both sides agree would bear on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. If you judge that any part of their dispute is merely verbal, identify that part and explain why.
