In a technology-policy discussion group, I argued that a social media recommender system can have a political valence without anyone intending it—for instance, by systematically rewarding outrage because outraged users click more, regardless of what any designer happens to believe. A colleague shut me down with "Guns don't kill people; people kill people," implying that I had confused the tool with the agent and that no artifact, from a handgun to an algorithm, has politics. I suspect this slogan is doing several jobs at once, and I do not want to answer one compressed argument with another. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be used to assert—about causal sequences, where moral responsibility attaches, whether instruments are neutral, and what each would imply for regulation—and say for each whether it is plausible and whether it actually supports his conclusion that artifacts have no politics? I am especially interested in which reading is the strongest one that still gets him to that conclusion, and whether it holds up.
