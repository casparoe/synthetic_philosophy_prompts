I've been reading about old societies and keep running into a problem I don't have a word for. It isn't just that the winning side gets to tell the official story. It's that the losing side often left no records in the first place, or left records that never got copied and preserved, so later historians read the silence as if it meant nobody objected or nothing much happened. The sources we do have survived because some institution had the money and the motive to keep them: tax lists, temple accounts, victors' monuments. I can see at least two different things going on — records never being made, and records being made but not surviving — but I don't know whether that distinction matters or whether scholars treat both as the same difficulty. Is there an established name for this specific problem, and how is it different from plain survivorship bias or from the idea that history is just written by whoever wins? I dont just want a label; I want to know how a historian is supposed to reason through a silence without letting it count as evidence.
