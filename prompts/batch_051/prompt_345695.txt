Write a satirical policy memorandum of roughly 600 words, in the voice of a cultural-economics consultant advising a small national government. Format it as a memo (To, From, Re, summary, numbered recommendations). The memo should recommend, with straight-faced cost-benefit rigor, that the government end all funding for preservation of a minority language and instead subsidize the remaining speakers' transition to the majority language. The argument to be faithfully exaggerated runs as follows: the language's value consists entirely in the preference-satisfaction it gives to currently living people; those people can be made at least as satisfied without the language; therefore nothing of value is lost, and continued funding is indefensible. Carry this logic to its natural conclusions—for instance, by proposing that the savings be spent on translating the language's oral poetry into the majority language, since the content is all that matters, and by treating resistance from elderly speakers as the kind of attachment that would predictably fade within a generation. The target is the view that cultural goods such as languages, traditions, and art forms have value only instrumentally, as conduits for the satisfaction of existing individuals' preferences, and never value in themselves or as non-fungible forms of life. The piece should be funny to a reader familiar with debates about intrinsic cultural value and readable to someone encountering them for the first time. Stay in character throughout; no winking, no thesis statement, no explanation of the joke.
