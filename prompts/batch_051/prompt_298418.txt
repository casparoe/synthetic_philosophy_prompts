My wife and I have a running argument about whether our dog actually understands a few English words. I'm pretty convinced she doesn't—she's just reading tone, posture, where the treat drawer is, whatever. My wife is pretty convinced she does. So about a year ago we started keeping a shared note where we log evey time the dog does something that might count: one of us says a word in a flat voice with our hands behind our back, and we write down whether she gets it right, plus a note about what else might have cued her. We have hundereds of entries now, and we both agree the log itself is accurate. But we still read it differently: what she counts as the dog understanding the word, I count as the dog picking up on some context cue we didn't manage to control. My wife says that can't last forever—if two reasonable people keep looking at the same honest evidence, the sheer weight of it has to swamp our starting assumptions and force us to agree. I don't think we're converging. Is she right? What conditions have to hold for shared evidence to actually swamp different starting assumptions, and which of those conditions is our dog-log probably failing? And if the evidence won't settle it, what would actually have to change for one of us to move?
