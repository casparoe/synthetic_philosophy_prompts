I am a novelist, and I am preparing an application for a one-year creative research fellowship. The fellowship supports practitioners who want to investigate a conceptual problem in the arts rather than produce a conventional scholarly monograph, so the proposal needs to make a case for a question rather than for a plot. I have spent years noticing that what I actually do when I write is almost never the invention of something from nothing: I take a shape I already know — a scene, a genre convention, a syntactic habit, a structure borrowed from music — and turn a small number of parameters on it, pushing one feature to an extreme, swapping a register, changing the point of view, redistributing emphasis. This is close to what Hofstadter calls the knob-twiddling model of creativity, the idea that variations on a theme, rather than ex nihilo originality, are the crux of creative work.

My half-formed idea is that the existing debate about this model is at an impasse because both sides treat variation as a single, undifferentiated operation. One side dismisses it as derivative rearrangement and reserves the word creative for something supposedly more radical; the other side embraces it and says all making is variation, which makes the thesis true but nearly vacuous. Neither side, it seems to me, asks the question that actually matters in my own practice and, I suspect, in the arts more generally: which knobs, turned in which combinations and to what extremes, mark the difference between a variation that feels derivative and one that reads as a genuinely new and significant work — and whether that difference is one of kind or only of degree.

Please write the proposal I need: a precise statement of that question, an account of why the existing positions leave it open, a description of the approach I would take and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives, including how the year would actually be divided month by month, a statement of what a useful deliverable would look like and what would count as the project failing, and an honest assessment of the scope for the time available. I will add the scholarly apparatus myself, so please do not pad the proposal with a bibliography or references; I am interested only in the conceptual framing.
