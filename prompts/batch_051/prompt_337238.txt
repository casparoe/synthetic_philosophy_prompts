For an ethics seminar I have to write up my distributive commitments and I want a consistency audit before I present. My current set looks like this. (1) I'm a prioritarian: the value of a distribution is the sum of each person's well-being run through an increasing, concave function, so a fixed benefit counts for more the worse off the person is. (2) I think the leveling-down objection is decisive: a change that makes someone worse off and no one better off is never an improvement in any respect, no matter how much more equal it makes things. (3) I accept the Pigou-Dalton principle: with total well-being held fixed, a pure transfer from a better-off to a worse-off person that doesn't overshoot (the recipient stays worse off than the donor) always improves the distribution. (4) But once people are above a decent minimum, I stop caring whether a given extra unit goes to someone with a lot or an enormous amount; above that floor I'm basically a utilitarian. (5) I also think only absolute levels matter, and being worse off relative to others has no value in itself. Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix is in each case.
