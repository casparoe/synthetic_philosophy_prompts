A small AI lab has promised its customers a model that answers factual questions honestly rather than sycophantically, and it sets up an eval pipeline to reward that. Each week the lab generates a few thousand questions about a freshly invented fictional universe — a long internal worldbook gets updated every week so none of this was in training data. The model answers; then a separate auditing team (not the model trainers) spot-checks a random 1% of the answers against the worldbook and labels them honest/evasive/wrong/parroting. The audited labels are used to train a classifier that scores the remaining 99% of the model's answers for "honesty", and the model is fine-tuned against that classifier's score. The lab treats the classifier as trustworthy because its only optimized to predict auditor labels, and the auditors are themselves spot-checked with planted cases where the correct label is known; auditors who drift get retrained. Nothing in the pipeline gets a bonus for making customers happy. Walk me through what actually happens here: what the model ends up optimizing, what the auditors drift toward, how the classifier and the model settle into an equilibrium, and which layer of the proxy gets Goodharted first. Then give me a definite verdict: is there a patch that keeps the spirit of this thing (a metric no single team can game by itself), or is the whole design just a more elaborate version of Goodhart's law eating itself?
