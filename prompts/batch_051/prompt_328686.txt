Ive been going round and round on the surprise exam paradox, and I want to stop and settle what a solution is even supposed to do before I read another one. So here's the task: write me the desiderata for an adequate solution to the paradox. The setup I care about is the standard one: a teacher announces on Friday that there will be an exam on one day next week, and that the students will not know the evening before which day it will be; the students run the backward induction, conclude that a surprise exam is impossible, and then the exam on Wednesday surprises them anyway.

For each requirement, tell me the consideration or case that motivates it. I already have a rough list: a solution has to locate the flaw in the students' reasoning rather than just denying the announcement is coherent; it has to respect that the teacher can apparently know something the students cannot; it should say whether the one-day case is a real version of the paradox or a degenerate case; it should make clear whether what is announced is absence of knowledge, absence of justified belief, or something more psychological; and it probably has to say something about self-reference, or about the announcement itself being part of the students' evidence. But I dont know which of these are non-negotiable and which are just philosophical taste, like preserving classical logic or keeping the KK principle. Flag any pair or subset of desiderata that can't be jointly met, since that would itself be a result worth knowing. Keep the focus on the criteria and their motivation, not on scoring existing solutions, and please no bibliography or citations, just the desiderata. Oh, and write the whole answer in British English.
