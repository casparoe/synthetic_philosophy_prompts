I run a small philosophy discussion group and I want a sorting quiz for a session on filial duties. The angle: I don't want people to just pick the label they like; I want the quiz to sort by what they say about cases. Use Jane English's friendship model as the hinge. In her essay What Do Grown Children Owe Their Parents? she makes the move that when a parent sacrifices for a child within an ongoing loving relationship, the child does not owe repayment the way a debtor owes a creditor; the relevant question is what the friendship requires now, not what the parent did then. I want the instrument to start there: the first question should ask whether the respondent buys that move or thinks the parent's sacrifice creates something more like a debt.

Then build a decision tree or scoring quiz, eight to ten questions, each question a concrete case. At least use these: (1) a parent who worked double shifts to pay for a child's college, later asks for financial support; (2) a parent who was not abusive but emotionally distant and now needs a live-in carer; (3) a parent who refused to attend the child's wedding because they disliked the partner, now wants to reconcile; (4) a parent who needs a kidney; (5) a step-parent who raised the child from age seven, now destitute; (6) an adult child who has been estranged for a decade with no contact initiated by the parent; (7) a parent who chose to live abroad and saw the child twice a year during childhood, now wants to move in. For each, have two or three answer options that don't mention any named philosophical position.

Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto these leaves: the debt view, the gratitude view, the friendship model, the special-relationship or role view, the particularist/virtue view, and the no-obligation voluntarist view. Build the branches from the logical commitments first: whether obligations arise from benefits received, from the history of the relationship, from current relationship quality, from social role alone, or not at all. Note any combinations your questions can't separate (I suspect friendship model and special-relationship view will blur on some cases) and flag them honestly. Also mark any leaf or combination that's empty because nobody in the debate defends it, or because it collapses into another.
