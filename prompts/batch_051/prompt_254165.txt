I'm a lay member of my hospital's research ethics committee, and I need to circulate a written opinion before this week's meeting. The proposal before us is a randomised trial for adults with moderate depression. One arm receives an established course of therapist-delivered cognitive behavioural therapy; the other receives a self-guided CBT-based smartphone app that has shown promise in two small pilots but has never been tested against the full therapy. The investigators argue the question is genuinely open: if the app proves good enough, it could reach far more patients at much lower cost, and they include a safety rule that moves app-arm patients to therapist-led care if their scores worsen past a set point. Several clinicians on the committee say they personally believe the app is clearly inferior and would not refer their own patients to that arm; they call randomisation unethical. Our statistician replies that requiring each referring clinician to be personally uncertain would make the trial unrecruitable, and that ethics committees usually rely on uncertainty in the expert community as a whole rather than on each doctor's private view. The committee is split between approving, rejecting, and approving with conditions.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate. Use these sections in this order: Recommendation (one paragraph, with an actionable bottom line: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and the headline reason); Background and the decision before us (one paragraph); The case for approval (one to two paragraphs); The strongest case against (one to two paragraphs); Conditions and reasoning (two to three paragraphs, including any changes the researchers would need to make). Engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than just restating the rules. Write in British English.
