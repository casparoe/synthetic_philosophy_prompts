I've been teaching myself philosophy of mathematics -- mostly puzzles about infinity -- and I keep coming back to Thomson's lamp. You know the setup: a lamp is on at t=0; you turn it off after half a minute, back on after a quarter of a minute, off after an eighth, and so on, halving each interval, so that by t=1 you've flipped the switch infinitely many times. The usual question is whether the lamp is on or off at t=1. The view I've settled into is that the question has no answer, and not in a merely epistemic way: the scenario just doesn't determine a fact of the matter. The sequence of states on, off, on, off, ... doesn't converge, so there's nothing for the final state to be. To me it's like asking whether the sum of 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 + ... is odd or even. But I've also started drawing a bigger moral from it: that a supertask only has a well-defined outcome when the relevant quantities converge, and that a description of an infinite process that leaves a magnitude oscillating like this is defective as a description of a possible world, not just uninformative. I'm not confident the big moral follows from the little one, and I'm not even sure the little one is as inevitable as it feels.
