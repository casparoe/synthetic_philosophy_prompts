I need a case built to order where a committed retributivist ends up having to demand the death penalty and a committed deterrence theorist ends up having to fight it. Usualy those two go the other way—people cast the deterrence theorist as the one willing to pull the lever and the retributivist as the one with the scruples—so I want the reverse, and I want it to come about honestly rather than by caricature. Rules for the setup: the offender is guilty, sane, and fully culpable; the retributivist demands execution because desert requires it, not because they're angry or pandering; the deterrence theorist is not allowed to be a general skeptic about whether executions deter (grant that they deter some murders in ordinary cases) and still has to conclude, on forward-looking grounds alone, that this execution shouldn't happen; no last-minute innocence reveal, no sci-fi, keep it realistic (a prison, a state penal system, a post-war tribunal) and write the scenario in one paragraph. Then walk through the reasoning each theory gives, in its strongest form, and why they split; identify the single feature of the case that's doing the real work; say what the case collapses into if you remove that feature; and end with the retributivist's best attack on the deterrence verdict.
