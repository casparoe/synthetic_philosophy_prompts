How could one test the claim that the therapy/enhancement distinction is morally significant in its own right? Some philosophers hold that interventions restoring species-typical functioning are permissible, or even obligatory, in ways that interventions which exceed normal capacities are not. A critic replies that the distinction merely encodes a preference for the status quo: once one equalizes candidate interventions for risk, reversibility, duration of effect, and inequality of access, the supposed moral difference disappears. Design the strongest empirical test you can of whether the therapy/enhancement distinction itself is doing genuine normative work in judgments of permissibility, rather than serving as a proxy for those other factors. Specify your stimuli and conditions in concrete terms, state what pattern of results would support each interpretation, and then say exactly what even a decisive result could not settle about whether the distinction ought to guide policy.
