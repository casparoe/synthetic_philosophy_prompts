Two developers on my team got into an argument at lunch over a thought experiment one of them had read about, and they asked me to settle it; I couldn't, so I am turning to you. The setup is this. A perfectly reliable being flips a fair coin. If it lands heads, it asks you for £100. If it lands tails, it gives you £10,000, but only on the condition that you would have paid the £100 had it landed heads. The coin lands heads and you are asked for the money. You know the setup, and you know the being is accurate about what you would have done in the other branch.

Alex said you should pay. Before the coin was flipped, paying on heads is a policy worth £4,950 in expectation, since half the time you lose £100 and half the time you gain £10,000; and the only way to be the person who collects the £10,000 in the tails branch is to be the sort of person who pays in the heads branch.

Sam said you should not pay. Once the coin has landed heads, the tails branch is no longer a live possibility, and your £100 cannot cause any £10,000 to appear. Paying is a pure loss in the actual world, and a reward that would have gone to a merely possible version of you is not a reason to hand over real money.

Alex replied that Sam's reasoning would also justify never keeping any commitment whose benefits are already fixed by the time you act, and that if you had been offered a precommitment before the flip you would obviously have taken it; a rational agent should not defect on its own policies the moment the causal work is done.

Sam replied that precommitting before the flip is a different act with different consequences, and that after the coin lands heads the rationale for the precommitment is exhausted; updating on new information is not defection.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four main moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justifying each rating; say who is right about which specific point; and then say who is more right overall and what, if anything, both of them are missing. Please write your answer in British English.
