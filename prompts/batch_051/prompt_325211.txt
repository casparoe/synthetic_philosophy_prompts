In causal modeling, when two events are correlated and neither plausibly causes the other, the standard move is to posit a latent common cause that screens off the correlation, as in a Bayesian network with a hidden variable. It has often seemed to me that conspiracy theories run exactly this inference over clusters of otherwise disconnected events—an assassination, a financial crisis, a leaked document—by introducing a hidden organization that coordinates them and thereby explains their co-occurrence. If the structure really is the same, it is odd that one is treated as sound causal reasoning and the other as a failure of epistemic judgment. Is the parallel deep, so that the real dispute only concerns priors over hidden coordinating causes and how much ad hoc structure to allow, or does it break down because a genuine latent common cause must be independently constrainable and testable in a way the conspiratorial cause usually is not? Give the mapping in both directions, say what would have to be true of each side for results in causal modeling to transfer to the epistemology of conspiracy belief, and name one thing the parallel would predict about conspiracy beliefs that is false.
