A popular book on cooperation summarized superrationality for me roughly like this. In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma between two rational and equally informed players, standard game theory says each should defect, since defection does better whatever the other player does. Superrationality answers that this is a form of special pleading: if you defect, your counterpart is in exactly the same situation and will reason to the same choice, so you end up with the low payoff; if you cooperate, she will cooperate too, and you both do better. So defection is only "rational" if you imagine you are somehow the exception. Superrationality, on this account, is the thesis that two symmetric players should reason as if their choices were correlated, and therefore cooperate; the author concluded that this dissolves the paradox of rational players achieving worse outcomes and shows that mutually beneficial cooperation needs no contract, reputation, or enforcement.

I suspect this is somewhat unfair, but I can't tell exactly where. Which parts of the summary would a careful defender of superrationality accept, and which are overstatements or conflations of distinct claims? What would a standard game theorist say in response, and does the author's conclusion survive the corrections?
