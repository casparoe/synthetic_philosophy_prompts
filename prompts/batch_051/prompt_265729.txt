I am trying to work out what I actually think about governments, employers, and private companies steering competent adults' choices for the adults' own good. My gut verdicts on cases:

- Fines for driving without a seatbelt: fine.
- Taxes that substantially raise the price of cigarettes: fine.
- Requiring graphic warning labels on cigarette packs: fine.
- Requiring calorie counts on chain-restaurant menus: fine.
- A tax on sugary drinks: fine.
- A city ordinance barring the sale of soft drinks larger than 16 ounces: not fine, though I cannot say exactly how it differs from the soda tax.
- Banning artificial trans fats in all restaurant food: fine.
- A three-day cooling-off period before a payday loan can be finalized: fine.
- A legal cap on payday-loan interest rates: not fine, to my own surprise.
- Automatically enrolling employees in a retirement savings plan while letting them opt out: fine.
- Automatically enrolling employees in that plan but not letting them opt out for two years: not fine.
- Giving people a small payment for each gym visit: fine.
- Discounting health insurance premiums only for people who meet an exercise target: not fine, though this looks close to the gym payment.
- Banning commercial gambling: not fine.
- Banning possession of heroin even for personal use: fine.

Is there a principle that captures all or nearly all of these, and what does it say the real difference is supposed to be in the pairs where I cannot articulate one? First give the analysis in a few paragraphs: state the principle precisely, test it against my cases, tell me plainly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and give its verdicts on some cases I have not listed: mandatory motorcycle helmet laws, opt-out organ donation, a tax on alcohol, and requiring households to separate recycling.

Then end with a JSON object with this schema: "candidate_principles" (an array of the main candidate principles considered, each with a one-sentence statement), "chosen_principle" (the one that does best), "verdicts_kept" (an array of the case labels it preserves), "verdicts_overturned" (an array of the case labels it overturns), and "further_case_verdicts" (an object mapping each further case to "fine" or "not fine" and a one-sentence reason).
