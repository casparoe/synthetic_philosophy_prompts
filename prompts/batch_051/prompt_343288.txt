For my formal epistemology seminar, I want a specification sheet for theories of logical uncertainty, written as a pre-reading assignment so we have the criteria in hand before evaluating any particular proposal. The target phenomenon is this: an idealized Bayesian reasoner assigns every proposition a probability and is logically omniscient, with all entailments reflected in its credences automatically; a real reasoner is not logically omniscient, because deriving what follows from what is itself computation. A theory of logical uncertainty should say what it is for a bounded reasoner to hold well-behaved credences over facts that are already logically settled but not yet derived — a conjecture before a proof is found, a tautology before and after a proof search, a sentence and its double negation, two sentences known to be equivalent only through a derivation too long to actually follow.

Produce the desiderata list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as a hard requirement or a camp preference. I especially want the distinctions a theory cannot collapse: suspending judgment versus assigning credence one-half; ignorance that is empirical versus ignorance that is merely unproved; failing to assign a value versus assigning an intermediate one; incoherence in the ideal sense versus exploitability by a computationally bounded bookie. Say which established results the theory must stay consistent with — the probability axioms where they apply, the force and limits of Dutch book arguments once the bookie is allowed more computational power than the agent, and Gödelian incompleteness where you judge it bears. Then flag the desiderata that are in tension, say whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that does the most to separate adequate theories from merely attractive ones.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — presumably the most important requirement, or the deepest tension — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
