I am trying to pin down a distinction that keeps arising in my work, and I want to know whether one side of it has an established name. Consider two ways a dream scenario can function in an argument. In the first, the fact that a dream can feel indistinguishable from waking life is used to undermine confidence in present experience: if I cannot rule out that I am now dreaming, my ordinary beliefs about the world rest on something shakier than I supposed, and the example works as a standing challenge to everyday knowledge. In the second, a classical Chinese passage describes a man who dreams he is a butterfly and wakes unable to settle which side of the exchange is the dream and which the waking reality. The passage does not seem to be trying to show that one state is real and the other illusory, nor does it demand a criterion for telling them apart. It seems rather to treat waking and dreaming as two transformations within a single process, and to present the unresolved alternation as closer to release than to a skeptical threat. I can articulate the contrast, but I would like to know whether this second deployment of a dream scenario has a standard label in the literature, and what precisely separates it from the skeptical one. If no standard label exists, then I need a definition and boundary cases; a bare name will not do.
