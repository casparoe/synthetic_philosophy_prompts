You're a philosopher of biology who works on cultural evolution. Think carefully here, and start by brainstorming the assumptions you think are doing the real work—even the ones you'll end up rejecting. I'm not after the standard dismiss-meme-theory line; I want to know whether one specific change actually breaks it.

The old Dawkins-style Darwinian theory of culture says culture evolves like a biological population: cultural items get copied with variation, and the variants that are better at getting themselves copied (catchier, more memorable, more useful to their hosts) spread at the expense of others. The meme was supposed to be the unit of selection, roughly analagous to a gene.

Here's the development I want you to take seriously. For most of human history, the selective enviroment for a meme was other human minds. People heard something, found it striking or useful, and passed it on with small errors and embellishments. Today a huge share of culture spreads through recommender systems. A feed is a machine-learning model that picks, out of thousands of candidates, which posts a given user actually sees, and it's trained to maximize predicted engagement: watch time, click-through, the probability you won't report it as not interested. It A/B tests near-clones of the same meme the way a breeder grows different cultivars. So a big chunk of selection isn't a human deciding that's worth retelling; it's an algorithm deciding, per user, whether this variant gets any chance to be copied at all.

So: does the Darwinian theory of culture survive this, or does it need repair, or is it dead? Lay out what the meme's-eye view actually needs to be true—what has to be doing the selecting, and whether the replicator needs to be a stable, high-fidelity unit. Then say whether algorithmic curation changes something load-bearing or just adds one more layer to the selection environment. If the theory still goes through, explain why a model optimizing for engagement counts as a selective force in the relevant sense. If it doesn't, tell me what the better picture is.
