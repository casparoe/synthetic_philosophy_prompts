I keep circling back to a view about my own choices and I want someone to show me what would count against it, straight, no strawmen. The view is that loss aversion and the endowment effect are bugs, not preferences. Like, I have an old phone listed online that I won't drop below a price I would never pay if it were someone else's, and I only notice this after the fact. The way I see it, both are the same error: I weight what I already have more than what I don't have, and "having" itself isn't a reason for something to be worth more. The bug story also looks like it explains a lot of ordinary stuff — gyms relying on people never cancelling, free trials that you keep because giving them up feels like losing something, my garage full of cables. A coworker said economists just treat any choice as revealing a preference and call it a day, but that sounds like the kind of thing people say when they don't want their theory touched.

What I want is a map of how exposed that "it's a bug" position really is. What would have to be true for it to be wrong, or at least seriously overstated? Tell me which results, cases, or arguments should actually move me — for instance whether the gap between buying and selling prices could be a reasonable response to uncertainty about what something is worth, or to transaction costs, or to information other people's prices contain, rather than a glitch; whether the fact that the effect shrinks with experience or vanishes for ordinary trade goods shows the bug is shallow; whether calling it a bias smuggles in some benchmark of what I "really" want that nobody can specify. Then rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a defender of the bug view would themselves concede as counting against it and which they'd dismiss as patches, and be honest about whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that's a problem here. I'd personally find it most persuasive if someone showed the asymmetry tracks something real about the value of possession (or that it is mostly a measurement artifact from tiny stakes and hypothetical questions), but I want to know how much weight those should carry, not just hear my own hunches back.
