I am applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship in formal epistemology, and I need a two-page research proposal. I have spent years following the rationalist-blog and forum discussions of Löb's theorem, self-reference, and the knowability paradox, and I keep returning to the Church–Fitch argument. The short version: from the principle that every truth is knowable, together with the assumptions that knowledge is factive and distributes over conjunction, one can prove that every truth is actually known, which is absurd. The standard repairs all strike me as patches. Edgington's actuality operator makes the problematic instance knowable only from a counterfactual standpoint where what is known is a related but different proposition; Tennant's restriction simply excludes the awkward cases as non-Cartesian; and the intuitionistic response gives up classical logic wholesale rather than saying which structural feature of the proof is doing the damage. My half-formed suspicion is that the paradox is not fundamentally about verificationism but about how an epistemic modality interacts with conjunction and with truths whose becoming known destroys their truth, and that the right question is therefore not whether the knowability principle is true but which structural rule one must abandon, and what the least concessive weakened principle is that still expresses the verificationist thought.

Please turn this into a two-page proposal. I want the question stated precisely, something like: is there a semantically motivated, non-ad-hoc weakening of the knowability principle that avoids the Church–Fitch collapse, and exactly which structural feature—factivity, distribution of knowledge over conjunction, closure of the relevant kind of possibility under logical consequence, classical negation, or something more subtle—is the true culprit? The proposal should explain why the Edgington, Tennant, and intuitionist responses leave that question open rather than settling it, and should describe an approach that differs from theirs. I have in mind constructing small modal models with an explicit parameter for the standpoint or stage at which a truth is known, and testing candidate principles against a fixed battery of desiderata: avoiding collapse, preserving ordinary cases of coming to know, and not trivialising the notion of knowability. It should also say how the work would proceed month by month across the year.

Finally, the proposal should specify what a positive result would look like—perhaps a completeness result for the weakest such logic—and what would count as failure, such as a proof that every weakening which avoids collapse either still depends on an ad hoc restriction or is too weak to be recognisably verificationist. Include an honest appraisal of whether this is genuinely a one-year project or the first half of something larger, and where it would need to be cut if it threatened to overrun. Do not trouble with references; I will add those myself.
