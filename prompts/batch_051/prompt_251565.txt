Here's one I want to work through for my philosophy of law seminar. Suppose a statute passed in 1985 says: "A person commits terrorism if, with intent to intimidate a population or coerce a government and for the purpose of advancing a political, religious or ideological cause, he or she commits serious violence against a person, causes substantial damage to property, or creates a serious risk to the health or safety of the public." Now consider this. A group calling itself the Rail Safety League hacks a city's emergency-alert system and pushes out a warning that a chlorine tanker has ruptured at the downtown rail yard. The warning is false; no train was moving and the group picked the time so nobody could be exposed. They're stated aim is to force the city council to reopen debate on banning chlorine shipments through the city. The alert triggers a two-hour evacuation of three neighborhoods, several traffic accidents, one heart attack, and a run on the local ER, but no one is exposed to any chemical. Does the statute reach them? Walk me through what the plain words give, what the statutory purpose gives, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would take the text to mean. If the readings split, say which should control and which feature of the case is doing the real work.
