At a poker night last weekend, my brother Leo and his friend Dana spent an hour arguing about a bet Dana has access to, and they both kept pulling me in to referee, which I honest to god couldn't do. Dana's setup: a wealthy backer will book an even-money bet with her as many times as she wants, and she's confident she wins each rep with probability 55%. The only question is what fraction of her bankroll to stake per rep.

Leo said the answer is more than any fixed 10% cap, because every dollar staked has an expected profit of ten cents; if the bet is favorable, betting more of it dominates betting less, and over a long series the law of large numbers should make that edge show up. Any cap, he said, is just risk aversion sneaking in and calling itself math. Dana said that's how people blow up. She'd read about the Kelly criterion and said that for an even-money bet with a 55% win chance the growth-optimal stake is 10% of the roll each time — the edge, 0.55 minus 0.45. She added that if you stake 50% each time, your expected wealth grows five percent per rep, but the expected log-growth per rep is negative, so your bankroll still collapes toward zero with probability one in the long run. Leo shot back that she's quietly assuming log utility, that anyone should care about the log of their wealth; the expected-wealth calculation is the honest one, and all the "almost surely" talk just hides the fact that the average is being driven by astronomically lucky paths she's arbitrarily choosing not to count. Dana replied that those lucky paths are exactly what makes the arithmetic average misleading for a process where your wealth multiplies, and that in the non-ergodic regime you should care what happens to the typical path, not to the average across parallel universes.

They went in circles like that for a while and I couldn't say who was right about what. So: adjudicate the whole exchange, point by point. In particular, is the Kelly rule just maximizing log utility in disguise, or is the long-run-growth argument doing different work? And does either of them actually have the right answer to the original question of how much Dana should bet?
