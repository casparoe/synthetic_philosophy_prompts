I've been defending this view in my philosophy-ish argument with friends and I want to stress-test it: you only owe gratitude for a benefit you could've actually declined without serious cost. So you don't owe your parents gratitude for raising you (you never got to opt out), you don't owe anything to luck, to being born in a stable country, or to ancestors whose choices set up your life, and if someone helps you in a way you couldn't refuse, that's nice but it doesn't put you in debt. Gratitude can still be a warm feeling, I just don't think its an obligation unless you could've said no. What would count against this? I want the cases, intuitions, or arguments that should actually move me, sorted from fatal to just awkward, and I'd also like to know which objections a defender of my view could honestly brush off and which ones they'd have to concede. I'd personally be most persuaded by a case where someone clearly owes something despite never having had a chance to refuse.
