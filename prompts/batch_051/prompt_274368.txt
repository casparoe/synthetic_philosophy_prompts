I've been going back and forth on this for a while, and I'd rather have someone talk it through with me than just tell me which side wins. For the past year or so I've found myself drawn to a view I've heard called tranquilism: the idea that what ultimately matters for a being's welfare is only the absence of craving and suffering. Not "positive experiences are good but often outweighed by suffering," but the stronger claim that once craving and suffering are gone, nothing further can be added to how well your life goes. Joy, pleasure, achievement, love — on this view these matter only instrumentally, as ways of quieting some disturbance. The view has a lot going for it. It gives you one clean currency instead of a mess of incommensurable goods. It makes the insatiability of desire look like a feature rather than a bug: you're not supposed to accumulate more goods, you're supposed to stop needing them. And it sidesteps a whole family of puzzles about how to compare distinct kinds of positive experience, because there's no positive quantity left to compare. I find the arguments for it genuinely hard to shake.

Here's the problem. I also can't shake the conviction that a life with real joy in it is better than a life that is merely untroubled. If I imagine two people who both wake up free of suffering and craving, and one of them spends the day listening to music that moves her while the other spends the day sitting in a blank room feeling nothing much at all, it seems to me the first person's day went better for her, not just differently. The tranquilist responds that I'm mistaking the relief of a craving for a further good: the music only matters because it satisfies an itch that was already there, and a being with no such itch would lose nothing by never hearing it. That doesn't sound right to me, but I also recognize the pattern — every time I try to point at a positive good, the view can redescribe it as a stilled disturbance, and I can't seem to produce a case where the redescription is flatly impossible. So I'm stuck between a theory I can't refute and an intuition I don't want to give up.

What I'd like is the strongest version of each possible resolution: what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there's a reading of "matters" on which both survive. I want to know which move you think I should actually make and why, not just a list of options. And please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or anything like that — just an email-like response.
