I'm a deputy city attorney in a small midwestern city — around 40,000 people — and we've got a backlog of ordinances that nobody has enforced for decades: a ban on fortune telling, a bicycle licensing requirement from the eighties, a law against spitting on the sidewalk, that sort of thing. Every few years the council asks us which of these dead laws we should repeal, which we should start enforcing again, and which we should just leave on the books unenforced, and we've never had a real method for deciding. I'd like a reusable decision procedure — a checklist or flowchart in words — grounded in whatever desuetude and rule-of-law considerations actually imply, but usable by a non-philosopher with limited staff time. The stakes are low to medium (some of these laws touch people's livelihoods), and anything we recommend has to be signed off by the city council, so the procedure should flag where a question is a value choice for them rather than a legal or factual call for us. Give me the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it kicks the decision upstairs. Then run it on two cases: (1) a fortune-telling ban last enforced in 1962, with a resident now wanting to open a tarot shop; (2) a bicycle-licensing ordinance from 1988 that was never enforced and for which no office even has a process — and one more case of your choice. At least one of the three should produce a recommendation we won't like. Finally, say what the procedure can't handle. Please write the answer in American English.
