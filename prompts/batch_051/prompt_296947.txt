For a graduate seminar in philosophical theology, produce a specification of what an account of divine simplicity must be able to do in order to count as an adequate account of the divine attributes. State first, briefly, what explanatory job the doctrine is being asked to perform: for present purposes, treat it as a proposal about how the radical unity and aseity of God are to be reconciled with the multiplicity of attributes that theology predicates of God. Then set out the desiderata, each accompanied by the case or consideration that motivates it. For each item, mark whether it is (i) a non-negotiable constraint, such that an account that drops it has stopped being a simplicity doctrine at all; (ii) binding only given a further commitment, such as a strong doctrine of creation, divine atemporality, or a Trinitarian theology; or (iii) a preference distinctive of one camp, for example classical theism as against theistic personalism, where the latter treats God as a person with genuinely distinct properties and real relations to creatures. The list should cover at least the following: the exclusion of all real composition and of passive potency; the treatment of distinct attributes such as justice, mercy, and omniscience as true of one simple object without either collapsing them into a single property or introducing parts; the handling of contingent, relational, and tensed truths about God, including God's knowledge of which world has actually been created, given that a simple God cannot differ across possible worlds or times; the compatibility of simplicity with divine freedom in creating; and the claim that God's essence is identical with God's existence. Then address the matter I care about most: which subset of these desiderata, if any, is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single criterion does the most work in excluding otherwise attractive accounts? In particular, say whether strong simplicity, the necessity of the divine essence, and God's contingent knowledge of a freely chosen creation can be held together as stated; if they cannot, identify exactly which conjunct or presupposition must be weakened, and what a revised account would have to forgo by weakening it.
