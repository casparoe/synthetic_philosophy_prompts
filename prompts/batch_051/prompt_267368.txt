For a while I have been turning over an argument that seems to show something rather bleak about immortality for creatures like us, and I cannot locate the step that is doing the questionable work. It runs as follows.

(1) A finite mind is one that can occupy only finitely many total internal states; however long it lives, its underlying machinery has only finitely many complete configurations. (2) The character of an experience is fixed by the total internal state that realises it: there is no difference in what an experience is like without a difference in the underlying state. (3) So a finite mind has only finitely many possible kinds of experience, a finite catalogue from which each moment of conscious life must draw. (4) An immortal life would be an unending sequence of moments, each drawing one experience from this catalogue. (5) Any unending sequence over a finite catalogue has a last moment of novelty: since only finitely many kinds ever occur in the sequence, and each occurring kind has a first occurrence, the latest of these first occurrences is some finite moment, after which every subsequent experience is of a kind already encountered. (6) But once a mind has passed that last moment of novelty, it can no longer have a genuinely new experience; what remains is mere repetition of kinds already lived. (7) Therefore an immortal finite mind would eventually exhaust the whole space of possible experience and spend the rest of eternity in repetition.

I know an unending sequence over a finite alphabet need not be periodic, and the argument does not claim periodicity, only the weaker claim in step (5), so I cannot reject (5) in the way I first wanted to. Is the argument valid as it stands? If not, which step fails, and does any key expression shift its meaning between the early steps and the conclusion? If the right answer is that the argument is valid on one reading of a key term but invalid on another, give both readings and say which is the strongest reading on which the premise is true, and whether the argument still goes through on that reading.
