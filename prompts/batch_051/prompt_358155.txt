I'm teaching a unit on embodied cognition and I want to use The Matrix, but not for the usual brain-in-a-vat skepticism stuff. I'm after a reading of the film through the enactivist critique of representationalism. Here's the distinction as I set it up for my students. Representationalism says perceiving and thinking are the brain constructing and manipulating internal representations of an outside world, so if you fed a brain the right inputs it would in principle have the same experience. Enactivism says cognitive life isn't inner processing over representations, but is constituted by a body's skilled, ongoing coupling with an environment; a brain in a vat getting the same inputs is missing something constitutive, not just causally helpful. Now read the film against that split. Where does The Matrix simply assume the representationalist picture (the pods, Morpheus's account of how the simulated world is delivered, the idea that abilities can be uploaded as data)? Where does it, maybe despite itself, dramatize something enactivist (the way dying inside the simulation kills the body, the way training has to happen in a lived body, the fact that Neo's real body stays in the pod doing its own thing)? And where does the film resist the enactivist reading and the honest critic has to drop it? I'd like you to say whether the film ultimately sides with one picture or the other, and what The Matrix adds to the philosophical debate that a paper on enactivism can't get at. I also want to know what the film gets wrong about either view, since I'm showing clips in class and don't want to mislead the students.
