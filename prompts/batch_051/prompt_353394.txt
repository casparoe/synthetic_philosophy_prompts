I help maintain an annotated reader on emergency powers for a law-and-philosophy seminar, and our group is stuck on a provision of our model Emergency Powers Act. The clause reads: "Where a grave and imminent threat to public order or the security of the state so requires, the Minister may adopt by decree derogations from ordinary legal safeguards for renewable periods of three months, provided that the measures are necessary, proportionate, and strictly limited in duration to the persistence of the threat." The drafters took as their paradigm short, bounded crises—floods, mass disorder, an armed attack—and the legislative history keeps using expressions like "until ordinary life can resume."

Our case study is this. Seven years ago, after a coordinated bombing campaign, the government introduced temporary border controls at all internal crossings and a requirement that citizens carry biometric identity cards. The campaign was suppressed within a year. Since then, the government has renewed the decree every three months on essentially unchanged findings, now arguing that the relevant threat is no longer an episode with an endpoint but a structural condition: dispersed networks, irregular cross-border movement, and a persistent but fluctuating risk. The Ministry of the Interior says the text plainly supports it, since the measures are renewed only while the threat persists, and the clause nowhere requires the threat to be acute as long as it remains grave. Others in the group say this turns an emergency exception into ordinary law and defeats the purpose of the clause; some would ask what the drafters would have said if told that "temporary" measures would still be running seven years later; and some argue that a reasonable reader today should read the clause in light of how indefinite security states actually operate. How does the clause apply to the seventh-year renewal of the border controls and the biometric identity card regime, and which of these approaches should govern—plain words, purpose, the drafters' likely hypothetical judgment, or the reasonable contemporary reading?
