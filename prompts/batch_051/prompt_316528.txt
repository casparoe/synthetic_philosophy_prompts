I have recently started working on early-warning systems that use machine learning to forecast political instability, and I have had to defend the project to a colleague who takes Popper's case against historicism to be decisive. As I understand it, his argument in The Poverty of Historicism is roughly this: there can be no laws of historical development, because history is a single, non-repeatable sequence; alleged laws of history are either untestable or vacuous; and the future growth of knowledge cannot be predicted in advance, since to predict a particular discovery is already to possess it. My colleague concludes that any data-driven attempt to predict large-scale social outcomes is therefore confused. Yet our models do something that looks a great deal like social prediction: trained on decades of event data, they assign substantially better-than-chance probabilities to conflict onset years ahead, and they improve as the data improve. I am trying to determine whether Popper's argument is untouched by this, needs repair, or applies only to grand theories of historical destiny while leaving piecemeal, conditional forecasting intact. Please reconstruct what the argument was really aimed at, identify which premise a modern statistical forecasting system bears on, and say whether the argument still goes through against such systems or whether they escape it and how.
