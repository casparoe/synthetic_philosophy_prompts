I think its conclusion is right, but when I present it to smart people they act like I've made one specific mistake rather than just reaching a conclusion they don't like. So I want you to act as a careful philosopher of mind and epistemology. Don't just tell me whether you like the conclusion; go through each step, say what the most charitable reading of each step is, and then pinpoint exactly which step, if any, fails. Here's the argument:

(1) If introspection were a reliable source of knowledge about my occurrent mental states, it would have the same basic signature as any reliable detection method: careful use plus feedback would make it's outputs more accurate, and independent careful users would tend to converge.

(2) But when people are trained to introspect carefully — for example, told to break an emotion like anger into its components and report them — their reports diverge dramatically rather than converge. Some insist on a rich structure of distinct sensations; others report nothing but a diffuse unpleasantness and think the first group is confabulating.

(3) The best explanation of that divergence is not that the two groups are detecting different real features of the same state. It's that introspection is largely constructive: expectations, training vocabulary, and the pressure to report something shape what gets reported.

(4) If introspection is largely constructive in that way, then its reports don't track an independent set of mental facts the way a reliable detection method should.

(5) So introspection is not a reliable source of knowledge about occurrent mental states like whether you're angry, or what your anger is made of.

I think (2) is empirically solid enough for the argument. If you think (2) is the weak point, say what the correct empirical version would need to be and whether the conclusion survives. My own suspicion is somewhere around (4), but I can't make the worry precise. Also, if the argument does work, does it show we shouldn't treat first-person reports about emotions as evidence at all, or only something narrower?
