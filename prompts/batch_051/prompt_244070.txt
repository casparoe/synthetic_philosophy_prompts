In the standard presumptuous philosopher, two otherwise indistinguishable theories differ only in how many observers the universe contains: T1 says 10^12 and T2 says 10^24, with equal prior probability. Under SSA, learning that you exist leaves the odds at 1:1; under SIA, the posterior odds favor T2 by 10^12, which is what makes the argument presumptuous. Now vary the case: after learning that you exist, you also learn your exact birth rank among all observers who will ever live, and that rank is 10^10, a value compatible with both theories; conditional on a theory, your rank is uniform over the observers that theory posits. Work through the update under SSA and under SIA, and give the posterior odds for T1 versus T2 in each case. Does the variation reverse the usual moral — so that SSA now looks presumptuous in the small-universe direction while SIA becomes the modest one — and do the original arguments for each assumption carry over unchanged, or do they start to break? End with a line beginning "Verdict:" that states in one sentence which assumption, if either, is presumptuous in this version.
