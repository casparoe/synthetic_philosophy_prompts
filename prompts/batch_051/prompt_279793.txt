I'm editing a small mock dossier on philosophical method, and I need a piece of deadpan satire for it. I'd like you to write, in the voice of a professional philosopher of science with a practiced feel for the genre, a roughly 650-word parody of a field report from an "Armchair Expeditionary Society," reporting on a thought-experiment expedition to the edge of the universe. The piece should open with this epigraph from Lucretius, On the Nature of Things, in William Ellery Leonard's translation:

"Whilst human kind
Throughout the lands lay miserably crushed
Before all eyes beneath Religion—who
Would show her head along the region skies,
Glowering on mortals with her hideous face—
A Greek it was who first opposing dared
Raise mortal eyes that terror to withstand,
Whom nor the fame of Gods nor lightning's stroke
Nor threatening thunder of the ominous sky
Abashed; but rather chafed to angry zest
His dauntless heart to be the first to rend
The crossbars at the gates of Nature old.
And thus his will and hardy wisdom won;
And forward thus he fared afar, beyond
The flaming ramparts of the world, until
He wandered the unmeasurable All."

The satire's target should be the practice of treating thought experiments as if they were empirical expeditions: the loose talk of "going to" a possible world, "observing" what a conscious bat experiences, or "running" a runaway-trolley case, as though the vividness of the exercise turned its results into evidence. But I want the satire to be fair: a specialist in the epistemology of thought experiments should recognize that you understand the real distinctions among thought experiments as evidence, as illustrations, and as intuition pumps, and should find the exaggeration aimed at the right places. Write it as numbered field observations with a brief concluding "specimen log." Please think through the standard positions on the role of thought experiments before drafting, so the jokes land technically rather than just decorating the piece. Avoid named living philosophers; a fictional expedition sponsor is fine.
