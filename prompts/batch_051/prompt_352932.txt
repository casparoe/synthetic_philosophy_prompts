For a PhD-level seminar on optimization and evolution, I want the desiderata before reading yet another attempt to treat natural selection as a kind of gradient descent. What does any adequate account of the evolution–SGD analogy actually have to get right? I keep seeing people hand-wave away glaring differences—evolution has no gradient signal, no backprop, it's a population-level stochastic process with irreversible steps, it optimizes a moving fitness landscape rather than a fixed loss, and it can only compare existing phenotypes instead of computing derivatives. But I want the full spec: for each requirement, give the case or result behind it, and say whether it's a hard constraint (get it wrong and the analogy becomes misleading) or a camp-specific preference (ML people might insist on it, ev-bio people might not, and why). Also flag any pairs of requirements that might be jointly unsatisfiable, and tell me which single requirement does the most to kill the lazy evolution-is-just-SGD-on-drugs story. Skip the scorecard; I want the criteria, not the comparison.
