I am an undergraduate currently taking a jurisprudence course, and I keep circling a puzzle about written constitutions. Consider a constitution whose amendment clause states that every provision may be amended by a two-thirds vote of the legislature. Difficulties arise when the amendment clause itself is the provision being amended. Suppose a two-thirds majority votes to alter that clause so that all future amendments require only a simple majority. The alteration must, presumably, satisfy the present two-thirds requirement; once it does, the clause has been used to change its own content, and I cannot see what stops a system from pulling itself along by its own bootstraps in this way. If, on the other hand, the clause cannot be used in this way, then a supposedly general rule quietly exempts itself, and the constitution turns out to contain a rule that cannot be changed through the procedures the constitution itself provides.

A sharper version concerns the attempt to make the amendment clause unchangeable. Could a two-thirds majority use the clause to insert a provision that the amendment clause itself may never be amended or repealed? If the move is valid, a present majority can bind every future majority through a rule whose authority derives only from itself; if it is invalid, it is hard to see how any provision could ever be made genuinely unalterable, whatever the constitution says.

The same loop appears in smaller settings. The rules of a members' club may say that the rules may be changed by a three-quarters vote of the members; is that rule about changing rules itself subject to a three-quarters vote, or does it stand in some special relation to the procedures it creates?

I am not asking only for a label. I want to know whether this precise phenomenon has an established name, and above all how it should be defined and bounded: how it differs from the ordinary amendment of provisions other than the rules of change, from the more general question of what ultimately grounds a legal system, and from provisions that are simply treated as unamendable. If the phenomenon has an established name I would be glad to know it, but the name matters only if it tracks the real boundaries; otherwise I would prefer a careful characterisation of the family of puzzles and which member of the family my examples belong to.

Please answer in British English.
