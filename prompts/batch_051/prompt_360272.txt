I have spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, so the vocabulary I move most comfortably in is the one that work tends to reach for when it wants to be rigorous: utility functions, expected utility maximization, constraints versus terms in an objective function, lexicographic preferences, Pareto improvements, and the habit of asking what a principle buys you in that language. I have no formal training in legal or political philosophy.

I want a systematic translation into that vocabulary of the three main positions in the debate about what rights are. Take these as your source material: Dworkin's position that rights are trumps over collective goals; Nozick's position that rights are side constraints on action rather than goals to be promoted; and the instrumental position that rights-talk is justified only insofar as it reliably promotes welfare, protects interests, or coordinates expectations, and carries no independent weight beyond that. For each, tell me which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in the target vocabulary.

Then give me the residue report. In particular, I want you to scrutinize the agent-relativity of side constraints: the claim that a side constraint forbids me from violating a right even when doing so would prevent more violations of the same right by others. Say whether that claim can be represented as a constraint on states of the world, as a constraint on the agent's own choices, or only by smuggling in something the decision-theoretic framework does not have room for. Conclude with a view on whether the points at which translation fails are points at which the framework is too coarse or points at which the rights positions themselves were unclear or unsupported, and on how much of the concept of a right survives the exercise.

One meta-request: throughout, separate what is settled about these positions from what is genuinely contested among people who work on them, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Where a particular formal translation is your own proposal rather than a standard reading, flag it as such.
