ok so I went down a futarchy rabbit hole for a debate topic (futarchy = the idea that instead of politicians deciding, people vote on some outcome metric and then prediction markets pick the policies most likely to hit it). I already wrote down the objections that keep coming up everywhere, so don't give me these:

- manipulation, like rich people pump-and-dumping a market that now has actual power
- Goodhart's law: once a metric actually matters, everyone games it, so the metric stops meaning what it was supposed to mean
- picking the metric is just politics again, because deciding what counts as "better" is a value call, so the whole values-vs-beliefs split isnt as clean as its made out to be
- thin markets: for a ton of real policies there aren't gonna be informed traders, so the price is basically noise
- short-termism: markets discount the future, so long-run stuff gets undervalued
- markets themselves are irrational sometimes (bubbles, herding), so "price equals best guess" is already shaky
- legitimacy: people probably won't accept "a bunch of traders set our climate policy" as legitimate

I think that's basically all of the standard ones, so the easy answer is already gone. Give me one more objection that isn't on that list and isn't just a rewording, a special case, or some combination of the above. Then tell me honestly whether the objection is actually any good, like whether a futarchy defender would have a real response to it.
