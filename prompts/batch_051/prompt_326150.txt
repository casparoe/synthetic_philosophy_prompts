I was arguing with a friend about Maxwell's demon and realized I don't actually know which step in the standard resolution is doing the real work. The story you always hear is: the demon can measure molecules and sort them without spending much energy, but it has to record the outcomes somewhere, and eventually that memory has to be erased to make it reusable. Landauer's principle says erasing one bit at temperature T costs at least kT ln 2, which supposedly balances the books so the second law survives. Fine. But now suppose the demon doesn't reuse its memory. It starts with an enormous blank low-entropy tape, reversibly writes each measurement outcome to a fresh cell, and never erases anything. The tape just gets longer and more informative while the demon converts a temperature difference into work. No erasure ever happens inside the cycle. Does the second law now fail, or has the entropy just been moved into the correlations between the tape and the gas? And if the correlations are the real bookkeeping, does that mean Landauer's principle was never the deep point—that the second law would survive even if erasure were free, so long as the tape counts as part of the isolated system? I want to know which premise the standard resolution actually needs, whether the tape version genuinely changes the situation or just relabels where the missing entropy lives, and what a repaired version of the argument would say.
