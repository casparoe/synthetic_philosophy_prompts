I got an implanted brain-computer interface through a research study about two years ago — I have ALS and use it to type by attempting hand movements, and its the main way I talk now. The consent form I signed has this promise: 'Neural recordings will be used solely to improve the speed and accuracy of your communication, and will not be used to obtain information about your thoughts, feelings, or memories.'

At my last check-in the researchers said they now decode the faint motor signal for a word that forms before I actually commit to typing it — words I start to select, or almost select, and then back away from. They use it for autocomplete, popping up the word I was reaching for so I can pick it faster. They say it is covered because the purpose is still improving my communication. But to me that seems like exactly what the second half of the sentence bans: the machine is pulling out a thought I never chose to send, in fact a thought I chose not to send. Does the clause apply to this? Work it through the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the people who wrote it would have said if I had asked them about this kind of decoding, and what a reasonable person in my position would take it to mean. If those readings disagree, which should decide? Also tell me what argument or evidence would most change your answer, and where your take is shakiest.
