Ok so in my early modern class we've been doing Spinoza, and I keep trying to reconstruct the move near the end of the Ethics where he gets to the intellectual love of God. I think I've got it as a step by step argument, but when I write it out it feels like the ethical conclusion sneaks in somewhere and I can't find the exact step. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) There is only one substance, God, and every finite thing (including me) is just a mode of that substance; so everything that happens follows necessarily from God's nature.
(2) Genuine knowledge of a thing is knowledge through its cause; so the more I understand why a thing couldn't have been otherwise, the more adequate my idea of it is.
(3) Therefore adequate knowledge of any finite thing, even my own emotions, turns out to be knowledge through God as the cause.
(4) The highest kind of knowledge is the intuitive kind, which sees things as following directly and necessarily from God's essence, rather than by some long inferential chain.
(5) Knowing something adequately, and especially intuiting it this way, increases the mind's power, and this increase is experienced as joy; joy just is the transition to greater power and perfection.
(6) Spinoza says love is joy tied to the idea of something outside you as it's cause. When the joy from (5) is referred to God as its cause, it becomes the intellectual love of God. (I can't tell whether this cheats by treating God as external to me, since (1) makes God totally immanent.)
(7) Therefore the more I understand things as necessary, the more I will love God; and since this love is the mind's highest condition, it is what I should pursue above all else.

I can't tell if the argument is valid as stated. My suspicion is that cause is doing two different jobs (explanatory ground in (2)-(4) vs the object an emotion attaches to in (6)), or that (5) calls joy a transition while (7) treats it as a settled condition. But maybe Spinoza is entitled to exactly that. So: is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails and what the most charitable fix would be. Also check whether (7) actually follows from the earlier steps or is adding a should on top.
