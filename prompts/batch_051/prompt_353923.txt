I keep noticing a pattern in creative work that I can describe more precisely than I can name. A jazz musician reharmonises a standard: she changes the chords, the metre, the phrasing, perhaps the key centre, and the piece remains recognisably the same tune — indeed, recognisably a version of the tune rather than a new composition. And then she changes one further thing, often a small one — the contour of the melody, or the placement of a single phrase — and the identity snaps: it becomes a different piece. I see the same structure in a composer's variations on a theme, in a translation of a poem, in a painter returning to one motif across decades, and even in a philosopher restating a familiar argument in new terms. The work seems to consist in exploring a high-dimensional space of adjustable parameters — dynamics, texture, register, ornament, harmonic substitution, the weight of one accent — around something invariable that is never stated as a rule. The skill lies in knowing which knobs can be turned how far before the identity of the piece or style snaps, and the interesting cases are precisely the ones where identity holds through large changes and then fails on the last small one.

I am trying to work out whether this has an established name or model in the study of creativity, and I want the carving rather than a label. Is this best analysed as a species of variation, as a theory of style, as constraint satisfaction, as search through a parameter space, or as something none of those captures? Are a faithful translation, a free cover version, a variation movement, and a late-style self-imitation really the same phenomenon, or do they only look alike? If no settled concept exists, say so and coin a term with a definition and the boundary cases that would let me apply it. And, since I want a diagnosis I can put weight on, tell me what would change your mind: which further case, argument, or consideration would lead you to withdraw the classification and conclude that the phenomenon is nothing over and above ordinary parameter search, or that it is too heterogeneous to count as one thing at all.
