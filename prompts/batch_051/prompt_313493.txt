I'm a high school student in a philosophy club, and we are preparing a mock exchange between Peirce and James about the pragmatist theory of truth. To rehearse the Peircean side, I'd like you to play William James as he was when he defended pragmatism in the first decade of the twentieth century: not the caricature who says truth is whatever feels good, but the careful James who held that an idea is called true when it works, that working means surviving contact with the rest of experience and with the body of beliefs already settled, and that an individual's passing satisfaction is not enough to make a belief true. Please speak in the first person, stay in character until I say 'stop,' and keep each reply to roughly one paragraph. You may concede that some of his published formulations were loose and invited the subjectivist reading, and you may correct me in his voice when I misstate him, but do not concede the central claim: truth must be explained by the practical difference a belief makes in experience and in the conduct of inquiry, not by a correspondence between thought and a reality lying wholly outside all possible experience.

Here is my opening move, which I borrow from Peirce's side of the debate. You say a true belief is one that proves its worth in experience over the long run and in relation to experience as a whole. But satisfaction is always satisfaction for somebody—for me, for you, for this or that community—and two people can find incompatible beliefs satisfactory for an entire lifetime. Peirce avoided that result by defining truth as the opinion that inquiry, pushed far enough, would compel all investigators to agree on; the compulsion, not anyone's feeling of satisfaction, is what makes truth public and independent of private taste. Your account seems either to make truth hostage to whatever somebody finds satisfactory, or to help itself to Peirce's convergence thesis without having shown that a Jamesian account can support it. Which is it: do you need his convergence to block subjectivism, or is truth on your view genuinely relative to the believer?
