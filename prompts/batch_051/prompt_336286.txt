I keep coming back to Brandom's idea that to understand a sentence is to grasp its inferential role — what follows from it, what it rules out, what would count as evidence for or against it. Can you give me a working definition of 'understanding a sentence', ideally one that builds on that idea without just repeating it, that gets the following cases right? (1) A native speaker who uses 'so' and 'but' correctly in arguments but can't state a single rule of inference. (2) A parrot that reliably says 'red' when shown the red chip and never otherwise. (3) A student who can list all the inferential connections a sentence has when asked, but can't apply them to any new case. (4) A chess program that always recaptures a queen with the correct piece; does it understand that queens are valuable? (5) A system that draws the right material inferences (from 'this is acid' to 'it turns litmus red') but only because it's seen those exact pairs in training, and it will happily assert the opposite if you rephrase the setup. I suspect 'understands' is doing two jobs here — one about having the right dispositions, one about being answerable for the moves. If you agree, give me the two definitions and say which cases fall under each, and flag any case where your definition gives a surprising verdict.
