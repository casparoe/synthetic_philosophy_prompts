Here are some criteria I was taught for classifying a conditional as indicative rather than counterfactual. First, form: indicatives use ordinary present, future, or past inflection, while counterfactuals use backshifted or subjunctive forms such as "if it were" or "if it had been" together with "would". Second, speaker attitude: an indicative treats the antecedent as an open possibility, whereas a counterfactual signals that the antecedent is believed false or already ruled out. Third, evaluation: an indicative is assessed by provisionally adding the antecedent to one's background knowledge, whereas a counterfactual is assessed by minimally revising the way the world actually is.
