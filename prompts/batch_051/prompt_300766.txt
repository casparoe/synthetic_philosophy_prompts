I'm in a metaethics seminar, and the instructor does this thing where she gives a one-word verdict on whether some act is wrong in a way that doesn't depend on what anyone thinks or on any culture's norms: objective or not objective. She call herself a moral realist, but she's never actually said which flavor of realism she is, and her track record is all we have. So far:

- Torturing a toddler for fun: objective.
- Female genital cutting performed on a twelve-year-old who has not consented: objective.
- Rape within a marriage, in a time and place where the law and the neighbors all treat it as normal: objective.
- A factory owner paying wages to low for the workers to feed their kids, in a country where that's the going wage: objective.
- Wearing white after Labor Day: not objective.
- Telling a friend you love their genuinely ugly new haircut: not objective.
- Breaking a casual promise to help a friend move because you'd rather watch TV: not objective.

Next week she's giving us the new case: a coworker quietly puts a safe, non-habit-forming anti-anxiety pill in their colleague's coffee before a high-stakes presentation, without asking, because the colleague is panicking and always refuses to take anything. The pill works, the presentation goes great, and the colleague never finds out. Objective or not objective?

I'm less interested in her one-word answer than in the rule she's running. The two I keep going back and forth between are something like wrongness tracks harm to people's lives, and something like wrongness tracks whether you override another person's will or consent. Are there other rules that fit her track record just as well? Which of her previous cases is the closest analog to the pill case? And if her record genuinely underdetermines the rule, tell me what she'd be committing to with each verdict on the new case, and what future case would finally force the two rules apart.
