I'm writing a school essay for a unit that pairs biology with philosophy, and I want to use the film Gattaca as my main text. My working question is whether the film treats genetic information in a way that is illuminating or misleading. The society in Gattaca acts as if a person's genome is an open book: a droplet of blood or a skin cell is read as a report about disease risk, life expectancy, temperament and occupational promise. I want to read this through the dispute over whether DNA really is a code. On one side, DNA does contain coding sequences that specify proteins, and some predictions from genotype to phenotype are real. On the other side, a genome is not a script for a whole life: development depends on environment, chance and other layers of regulation, and even a 'good' genome guarantees no particular biography. Using particular scenes or character choices, show me where Gattaca uses the code metaphor in a way that captures something real about genetic prediction, and where it slips into treating the genome as a fixed script for the person. Does the film ultimately endorse the society's genetic reading of people, or does it undermine it, and does that matter for whether its philosophy is sound? I want a reading that is fair to both the film and the biology, not one that reduces the film to an illustration. End by saying what the film adds to the debate about DNA and information that a textbook account might miss. Please answer in British English.
