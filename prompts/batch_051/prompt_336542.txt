I'm on the clinical ethics committee at a teaching hospital, and we've been going round in circles for two meetings about wether a patient's living will covers her current situation, so I'd like a careful worked analysis rather than a general essay. The patient, Margaret, wrote the following advance directive in 2014, before a planned heart operation, when she was fully competent and in her mid-fifties:

"If I am ever in a condition from which there is no reasonable prospect of recovery and I am unable to make or communicate decisions about my care, I refuse all life-sustaining treatment, including artificial nutrition and hydration. I give this instruction because I would not want to live with severe brain damage, and I do not want to be a burden on my family."

The form's covering page says it is for decisions about terminal illness and permanent unconsciousness, and the boxes she could tick don't mention anything like her current situation. She is now sixty-eight. After a stroke last year she is not unconscious: she is awake for long stretches, tracks voices, sometimes laughs at her daughter's jokes, and will squeeze a hand when asked, but she cannot talk, cannot swallow safely, and cannot weigh a medical decision. The neurology team say she is not dying and, with a feeding tube, could live for years in roughly this state; without one she will die within weeks. Her daughter insists this is exactly the life her mother said she did not want, so the directive should be honoured. Her son says she was imagining being hooked to machines, not smiling at her grandchildren, and that the words "no reasonable prospect of recovery" don't obviously cover someone who is stable and responsive.

Treat the passage like a rule whose author did not foresee this case. I'd like you to work through what the plain words say, what the purpose of the clause suggests, what she would most likely have said in 2014 if someone had put this scenario to her, and what a reasonable reader at the time would have understood her as directing. Then tell me what the directive actually requires or permits here, which of those approaches should control and why, and what that says about how much authority the earlier self should have over the person in the bed now. Please write in British English, not American.
