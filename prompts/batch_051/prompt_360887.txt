Bertrand's classical chord problem asks for the probability that a chord chosen at random in a circle is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle, and different natural choices of the randomization procedure give 1/2, 1/3, or 1/4. Rank the following five diagnoses of what this paradox shows about the principle of indifference, from the diagnosis that is most damaging to the principle of indifference as a rule of probabilistic reasoning to the diagnosis that is least damaging. After the ranking, give one sentence for each diagnosis explaining why it occupies that position. Ties are not allowed; if you believe two of the diagnoses collapse into a single position, say so and defend their joint ranking. Please write the answer in American English.

1. The paradox exposes an under-specification: at random has to be defined relative to a measure, and each of the three answers is generated by a different underlying symmetry (random endpoints, random radial points, or random midpoints), so the principle of indifference yields a unique probability only after a substantive choice of symmetry has already been made.

2. The paradox is a special case of a general reference-class or parameterization problem: probabilities attach to descriptions or partitions of an event space rather than to events themselves, and the principle of indifference is silent about which partition or parameterization is the correct one.

3. The paradox shows that the principle is not merely incomplete but actively misleading, because it invites an agent with no evidence to manufacture precise probabilities and treat them as informative; each of the conflicting answers can be made to look mandatory by some superficially natural symmetry argument.

4. The paradox is overrated as an objection, since the three answers are answers to three different questions; once an actual physical randomization procedure is specified the problem disappears, and the principle remains reliable when restricted to well-defined procedures or objective symmetries.

5. The paradox does not refute the principle but reveals that judgments of equal possibility are not invariant under re-description or reparameterization; this is a constraint on the logic of indifference reasoning rather than a fatal flaw, because no plausible rule of uncertain inference is invariant under arbitrary re-description.
