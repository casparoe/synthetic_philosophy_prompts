Me and a friend just got into a long argument about whether a reinforcement learning agent could ever genuinely suffer. His whole line was that the reward signal just is valence—the thing that flags states as good or bad for the agent—so once you have a sophisticated enough RL agent, you've already got the machinery for suffering. I kept pushing back that this argument isnt about the systems people actually build now: modern language models are fine-tuned with RLHF, where the 'reward' is a learned model of human preference, it only shapes gradients during training, and the model gets no reward signal at inference. Does the valence argument survive that shift, or does it only make sense for an old-school online RL agent living in a stable reward field? What premise does the argument actually need, and is it true in the RLHF case?
