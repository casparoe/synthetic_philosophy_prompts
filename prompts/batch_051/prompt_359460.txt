I can reconstruct the following argument from what I rememeber of Schlick's essay about the meaning of life, and I can't tell if it's a tight argument or if one of the steps is doing sneaky work. (1) Any activity is either work—done for the sake of some further end—or play—done for its own sake. (2) Work has only borrowed value: whatever value it has comes from the end it serves, not from the activity itself. (3) So a life made entirely of work would have entirely borrowed value, like a chain of debts with no asset at the bottom. (4) A life whose value is entirely borrowed cannot be meaningful. (5) So a life made entirely of work cannot be meaningful. (6) Therefore only play can make a life meaningful, and no achievement or goal can. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails? In particular, does (6) follow from (5), or does it smuggle in a suppressed premise about what meaning has to be?
