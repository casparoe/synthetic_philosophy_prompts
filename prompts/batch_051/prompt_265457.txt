I've been on my hospital's research ethics board for about two years now, and next week we're deciding on a protocol that's making me go back and forth, so I want to lay out our track record and see what we're actually committed to.

Looking back: we approved a placebo-controlled trial for a rare neuropathy with no approved treatment, because nobody saw the placebo arm as being denied care they'd otherwise have gotten. We approved a head-to-head trial of two antidepressants that are both first-line in the guidelines, since the field is genuinely split on which one is better and the trial is meant to settle that. We approved an add-on trial where everyone on standard chemo was also randomized to either an experimental agent or a matched placebo; individual board members had different hunches about the agent, but we all agreed neither arm fell below standard care. We rejected a placebo-controlled trial for a serious bacterial infection where three effective antibiotics exist, since the placebo arm would clearly be below the standard of care. And we approved a trial comparing two surgical techniques even though a couple of the surgeons on the board said they personally wouldn't randomize their own patients — their point was that the field as a whole hasn't made up its mind. So the board seems to work with something like clinical equipoise: genuine disagreement or uncertainty in the relevant expert community about which arm is better.

The new one is a non-inferiority trial. Heart-attack patients would be randomized to a branded beta-blocker or a generic that most cardiologists, including the investigators if you get them talking of the record, already think is probably a little worse on the composite outcome — but maybe not worse by a clinically meaningful amount. The whole point is to test whether the generic stays inside a pre-specified non-inferiority margin. Nobody thinks the generic is better. Both drugs are on the market, most doctors would call both acceptable care, and the only way to find out whether the difference actually matters is a randomized comparison. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own precedents, which past case is the closest analog, and what principle would we be signing onto with each possible decision?
