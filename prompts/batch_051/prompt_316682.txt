People keep saying an LLM hallucination is bullshit in roughly Frankfurt's sense — where lying at least requires tracking the truth well enough to conceal it, an honest mistake aims at truth and misses, and bullshit is just output produced without any concern for whether it's true. I keep wanting to split the bullshit diagnosis into two underlying things: a truth-miss (the system is trying to be accurate but some mechanism fails) and truth-indifference (accuracy never enters as a constraint on that output at all). My question is not which label is right; its whether those two can actually be told apart empirically, or whether 'truth-indifference' is just a fancier way of pointing at the same wrong answers. Propose the best test or measurement you can think of to pull them apart — be concrete about the setup, the intervention or probe, and the numbers you'd compare. Say what result would count for each side, and then be blunt about what a positive result would and wouldn't settle, given that an LLM doesn't have Frankfurt-style intentions in the first place. Keep it in American English.
