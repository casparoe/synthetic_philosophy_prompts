so for my ethics final I'm doing this on whether everyday conventions like tipping, queueing, and not jaywalking are actual moral obligations or just stuff that keeps society from being annoying. I'm focusing on tipping right now. I've been working with a checklist of conditions for when following a local convention becomes a real obligation:

- the person affected actually relies on it (their paycheck is built around it)
- its predictable enough that a visitor could know about it
- breaking it causes real harm, not just mild awkwardness
- you had a chance to opt out or say in advance that you wouldn't follow it
- following it doesn't make you complicit in something unjust

the case I'm stuck on: last year my family hosted an exchange student from a country where tipping isn't really a thing. before she came she found out that where we live servers get paid like two dollars and change an hour and tips basically are their wages, and she said that system is gross because it makes random customers responsible for whether the server eats. so for her whole semester, whenever she ate out she left basically nothing, even when the service was great and the server was clearly run off their feet and counting on tips. she never warned the server first either, she just ordered like normal.

her argument is that tipping is participating in a exploitive wage system and that a good visitor shouldn't have to follow a local rule that's itself unfair. the other side is that her not tipping, by herself, just punished individual servers who had no power over the system, and if she knew the rule going in and still ordered, she was sort of using them. it feels like a collective action problem: if everyone quit tipping the system might change, but one person quitting just hurts one tired server.

so the actual question: go through my criteria one by one and say whether she had a moral obligation to tip. if the criteria give mixed answers, which ones should win and why. and does my checklist need a sixth condition, or is it just wrong about something. also tell me whether the "when in Rome" idea has any real moral force here or if its just tourism etiquette.
