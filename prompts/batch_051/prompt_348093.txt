Read Borges’s story Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote through the ontology of musical works. The story turns on a fictional author producing a text identical to a portion of Don Quixote in a different historical context, with Borges treating the result as a different work. I want to know whether that judgment carries over to music. Does it support the contextualist view that a work’s identity is fixed by more than its sonic sequence—by composer, era, performance practice, or recording context—or does the analogy mislead because music is realized through performances and recordings in ways that a text is not? In particular, suppose someone holds that a musical work just is a repeatable sonic pattern, so that any structurally matching performance or recording is an instance of the work. What would that person have to say about Menard, and is the case a reductio of that view or one it can absorb? Give a reading that indicates where the story genuinely illuminates the type/performance/recording distinction and where it would distort it.
