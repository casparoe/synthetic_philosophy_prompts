I am taking a course on statutory interpretation this term, and the professor has asked each of us to write down our own view of how statutes should be read before we begin the unit on the major debates. I have drafted the list below, and I would like a consistency check before I have to defend it in class. Here is what I have written so far:

1. The meaning of a statute is its ordinary public meaning at the time of enactment, not the subjective intentions of the legislators who voted for it.
2. There is no collective legislative intention that a court could recover; the only admissible notion of purpose is an objective purpose inferable from the enacted text and the public context of enactment, and it may be used to resolve genuine ambiguity but not to override otherwise clear text.
3. A court may nevertheless depart from the ordinary meaning when enforcing it would produce an absurd result, and it may correct an obvious scrivener's error even though the enacted words point elsewhere.
4. In criminal statutes, any genuine ambiguity should be resolved in favor of the defendant under the rule of lenity.
5. Semantic canons such as ejusdem generis and expressio unius are legitimate default rules when ordinary meaning runs out.
6. When an old statute is applied to new factual circumstances, the controlling question is the concept expressed by the statutory words, not how the enacting Congress would have expected the new case to be decided.

I want an audit of this set rather than a lecture on the positions themselves. Which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, and which pairs only look as though they are? For each genuine tension, how severe is it, what is the least costly revision, and what does that revision give up? Finally, which single revision would do the most to make the whole package coherent?

Please return the audit as a JSON object with the following fields:
- apparent_tensions: an array of objects, each with pair (an array of the two commitment numbers, such as [1, 6]), surface_tension (why the two commitments look incompatible), and resolution (why the tension dissolves on inspection).
- genuine_tensions: an array of objects, each with pair, severity (one of "mild", "moderate", or "severe"), mechanism (the precise point at which the two commitments pull against each other), least_costly_revision (which commitment to modify and how), and cost (what the revision gives up). Order these entries from most to least severe.
- single_revision_recommendation: a string naming the one change that would most increase the coherence of the set and explaining why.
