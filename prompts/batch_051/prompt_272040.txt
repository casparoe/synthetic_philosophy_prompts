Consider two performances. In the first, a jazz quartet improvises on a standard; the musicians' unscripted choices are treated as part of the work itself, and the result is unhesitatingly called art. In the second, a player works through an interactive game whose designed system leaves the shape of the experience partly to the player's own choices; here the interactive choices are commonly taken to count against, or at least to complicate, the description of the result as art, and critics reach for terms such as design and craft. The two cases look structurally alike: each presents an authored framework that is realised only through the free choices of someone other than the author. What, if anything, justifies treating the performer's agency as art-constitutive but the player's agency as art-impeding? Identify the strongest candidate principle, test it against at least two nearby cases (for example, a substantially improvised theatre piece, a competitive ballroom dance judged on technique, and a game whose goals are deliberately unclear and whose choices are mostly expressive), and then state whether the asymmetry should be kept, given up, or reversed. Please answer in British English.
