As a journalist who covers philosophy of science, I keep circling back to the Humean/anti-Humean standoff about laws of nature because I cannot tell what kind of dispute it is. Both sides accept the same physical history — the same spread of particles, fields, and trajectories across spacetime, what philosophers call the Humean mosaic. Yet the Humean says the laws are nothing over and above that mosaic: they are merely the axioms of the best deductive systematization of it, chosen for simplicity and strength. The anti-Humean says the laws are something further — necessitation relations between universals, primitive nomic facts, real dispositions — that governs or explains why the mosaic developed as it did. After decades of this, the arguments are thoroughly familiar and neither side moves. My working hypothesis is that this is not a disguised empirical disagreement but a difference about what an explanation has to do and about whether ontological parsimony should break the tie once all the data are shared. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, methodological, a clash of bedrock modal intuitions, or something else — using what each side says about the shared mosaic, and tell me what could count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a case one side cannot describe, or an argument that the question is ill-posed. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating the question.
