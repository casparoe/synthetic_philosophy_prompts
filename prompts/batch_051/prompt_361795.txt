My friend Mara has an annoyingly consistent set of views about what travelers owe locals, and I've been keeping a mental list of her verdicts because I can't always predict her. The cases so far:

- A tourist from a no-tipping country eating at a sit-down restaurant in the US: Mara says you tip 18–20%, full stop. It's how the waitstaff get paid, and refusing the tip doesn't protest the system, it just shortchanges a worker.
- Someone at a bus stop in London who slides to the front because they're in a hurry: she says it's wrong, no excuses, because everyone has somewhere to be and the queue is how strangers avoid stealing time from each other.
- Haggling in a market where haggling is the norm: she says a tourist who pays the first asking price isnt doing anything morally wrong. It's just a bad deal, not a breach of etiquette.
- Taking shoes off before entering a mosque: she says you do it, even if you're an atheist. It's not about endorsing the theology, it's basic respect for the place and the people who use it.
- A "tip" to a customs agent to get your bag through without hassle: absolutely not, she says. That's extortion, and the fact that everybody does it doesn't make it a convention you need to adopt.
- Japan, where there's no tipping norm: she says dont tip. Leaving money would be a weird intrusion, not generosity.

My case, which is coming up next month: I'll be in a US city for a few weeks, and near where I'm staying there's one of those counter-service coffee places. You order at the register, pour your own coffee, and the tablet prompts for a tip. The staff there get the ordinary minimum wage, not the tipped subminimum wage. Locals seem split: some tap 15%, some tap zero, the regulars often drop a dollar in the jar. I know the sit-down restaurant case feels like her closest analog, but the wage point doesn't carry over, and maybe the Japan or haggling cases are closer after all. If Mara were trying to stay consistent with her own verdicts, what would she say about the counter-service tip, which of the earlier cases is genuinely the closest analog, and if her precedents underdetermine it, what principle would I be attributing to her either way?
