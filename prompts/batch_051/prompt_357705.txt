I have spent the last month alternating between revising a novel and reading about effective field theory, and I cannot shake the conviction that the two activities describe the same structure at different scales. In the physics, the autonomy of scales means that one can predict long-distance, low-energy behavior without first resolving the short-distance dynamics: the ultraviolet details decouple, entering only through a finite set of parameters that are fixed by measurement rather than derived, and the resulting description is valid up to some energy scale beyond which it breaks down and has to be replaced by another theory. When I edit, I appear to be doing something formally similar. The macro-narrative — the reversals, the final positions of the characters, the causal skeleton of the book — can be held fixed while I rewrite nearly every scene-level and sentence-level detail, because almost all of that detail fails to propagate upward. What does propagate is carried by a small set of coarse-grained character parameters: what a person wants, what they can bring themselves to do, the balance of power in a relationship. A few micro-events, such as an intercepted letter or an unnoticed lie, do travel up the scales and reorganize the whole plot, in the way relevant operators refuse to decouple; the rest are irrelevant in something close to the technical sense. And the writing models I actually use have a limited range of validity: a model of scene construction collapses when pushed down to the sentence or up to the scale of a whole life.

I would like to know whether this parallel is deep or whether I have merely dressed up ordinary editorial judgment in physics vocabulary. If there is a real mapping, I want it drawn carefully: what corresponds to the renormalization group, to the cutoff, to the suppression of irrelevant effects by powers of the scale ratio, to the finite parameterization of the low-energy theory; and what would have to be true of narrative for results from field theory actually to transfer, rather than merely to suggest vocabulary. If the parallel is superficial, I want to know where it breaks first — whether, for instance, narrative has no analogue of a quantitative scale hierarchy or a small parameter in which decoupling can be stated, so that "plot-relevant" and "plot-irrelevant" remain judgments rather than controlled approximations. The practical stakes are low, but I would rather not become the novelist who announces a false correspondence in front of people who actually do physics.
