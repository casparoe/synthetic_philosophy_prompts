I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics and the philosophy of time, and I keep coming back to a parallel I cannot decide about. The debate over what should count as year zero — and, more generally, over which epoch a calendar should use — looks to me structurally like the choice of gauge in a classical field theory.

In electrodynamics there is no fact of the matter about which vector potential represents a given field configuration; an equivalence class of potentials, related by gauge transformations, describes the same physical situation. Choosing a gauge is conventional, but not arbitrary: one gauge makes the electrostatic field manifest, another preserves manifest Lorentz invariance, and an ill-chosen gauge can introduce apparent singularities into an otherwise regular field.

The calendar case seems to mirror this. There is no fact of the matter about which integer labels a given year; epochs differ by a constant offset in a time coordinate, and the invariant content is durations, orderings, and coincidences. Yet epoch choices are defended on principled grounds. The Holocene calendar adds 10,000 years so that the whole of human history sits on a positive scale and negative years disappear. The Unix epoch serves computational convenience. Astronomical year-numbering inserts a year zero, and use of a continuous day count from a fixed origin makes interval arithmetic clean. Meanwhile, the common era's missing year zero looks to me like an artificial singularity at the boundary between 1 BC and AD 1 — the kind of thing a gauge theorist would call a coordinate artifact rather than a physical fact.

I want an assessment of whether this parallel is deep or superficial. Please state the mapping precisely: what in the calendar plays the role of the gauge potential, the gauge transformation, and the gauge-invariant observable. Say what would have to be true of both sides for any result to transfer — for example, whether anything follows for the debate about which epoch is the correct one from the gauge-theoretic idea that conventions are justified by the symmetries they make manifest. And name at least one concrete thing the parallel predicts that is false, and one that is true. In particular, I am unsure whether proposals for a cosmically privileged epoch, such as dating years from the Big Bang, are ruled out by the analogy in the way gauge theory rules out asking for the true value of the potential, or whether they survive because they claim a physical rather than merely conventional origin.
