You are a philosopher working at the intersection of AI alignment and narrative. I am teaching a short seminar on oversight, delegation, and competence, and I want to read Goethe's ballad The Sorcerer's Apprentice (or the Disney Fantasia sequence based on it) through the lens of the alignment research program known as IDA, iterated amplification and distillation. As I understand IDA, the idea is to produce increasingly capable systems without giving up human control by alternating two steps: amplification, in which a human overseer decomposes a difficult task into smaller subtasks, calls on existing AI assistants to help with those subtasks, checks and combines their outputs, and in doing so constructs a training signal for how the difficult task ought to be done; and distillation, in which a model is trained to imitate that amplified process directly on many tasks. The advertised virtue is that every subtask is kept simple enough for the overseer to evaluate, so the final system is only a compressed version of a process the overseer could in principle have authorized; alignment is supposed to be preserved at each loop.

Read the Sorcerer's Apprentice against that picture. The apprentice, left to himself, splits the goal "fill the cistern" into something like "get an agent to carry water," delegates the subtask to an enchanted broom, and then cannot supervise, correct, or stop the process; when he tries, by chopping the broom apart, each piece becomes another broom, and the room floods until the master returns and speaks the command that dissolves the spell. Where did the failure occur, in IDA terms? Was it primarily a failure of decomposition (the subtask was underspecified because no stopping condition or quality constraint was ever built in), a failure of oversight (the apprentice lacked the competence to evaluate the broom's behavior, so more human-in-the-loop oversight would not have helped), a failure of distillation (the broom is a competent optimizer of a misspecified proxy that departs from the master's actual goal), or some nested combination of these? Please do not rest with a list of possibilities: argue for the diagnosis you find most defensible, and say explicitly where the analogy between the broom and a distilled policy holds and where it misleads. End by saying what the ballad adds to the technical IDA debate that prose arguments tend to understate, for instance whether the story shows that decomposition plus supervised checking can fail even when each local step looks correct, and what the master's authority supplies that a training loop may not be able to recover.

Start by brainstorming two or three candidate diagnoses, then settle on one and reason through it carefully.
