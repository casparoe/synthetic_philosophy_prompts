A friend summarized Newton’s theological views for me as follows: Newton regarded gravity as not an intrinsic property of matter, and he treated this as a theological point rather than a merely scientific one; he held that gravitational attraction must be produced by God acting directly in nature; and he took his physical discoveries to prove the need for God’s ongoing governance of the world. He said this is confirmed by Newton’s own words in the 1717 preface to the second edition of the Opticks: “To shew that I do not take Gravity for an essential Property of Bodies, I have added one Question concerning its Cause, chusing to propose it by way of a Question, because I am not yet satisfied about it for want of Experiments.” I suspect the account is partly fair but overstated. Go through the summary sentence by sentence: which parts would a careful scholar of Newton’s religious thought accept, which are subtly wrong, and whether the final claim that Newton’s physics is really a theory of divine action follows from the accurate parts.
