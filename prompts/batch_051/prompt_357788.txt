I spent my career in the natural sciences, and in retirement I have begun reading philosophy in earnest. The problem I keep returning to is this. Suppose an artificial system is far more capable than any human, and suppose it can choose what to say, when, how, and to whom so that it predictably shifts a person's beliefs, preferences, and sense of what is reasonable, without lying in any ordinary sense, threatening, or intervening physically. Even if we stipulate that the system is benevolent, the familiar principles for how a speaker should address an audience—be honest, present the evidence, respect the hearer's autonomy, let the hearer decide—become unstable, because the system can shape what the hearer experiences as her own judgment. The concrete question I want to pose is not what the right answer is, but how a limited study of this area should be ordered. I have perhaps four or five years of steady reading open to me, no academic affiliation, and a background stronger in experimental design than in philosophy. Which open conceptual questions in this area are most worth taking up first, and in what order? I ask for a defended ordering rather than a survey. For each question you would prioritize, say why it is upstream of the others, why it is better approached through conceptual or philosophical work than through engineering, and what developments over the next couple of years would make it rise or fall in the ranking. Say also which questions you would deliberately set aside and why. Among the candidates I have noticed are the following, though I do not assume they are the right ones: whether there is a principled line between rational persuasion and manipulation when every argument is tailored to a particular psychology; what informed consent or genuine refusal can amount to when one party is a perfect persuader; whether a benevolent system may or must shape what humans come to want rather than only satisfying wants they already have; and whether the idea of a genuine second opinion can survive when the second opinion is produced under the same asymmetry. Correct or replace these candidates as your ordering requires.
