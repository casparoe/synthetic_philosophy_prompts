My working criteria for ascribing moral status to a non-human system are as follows. (i) There is something it is like for the system to be in at least some of its states; without that, it is an instrument rather than a patient. (ii) The system has a welfare: some things can go better or worse for it, not merely for the purposes it serves. (iii) It has interests that are stable enough to be promoted or thwarted over time, rather than only transient aversions or attractors. (iv) It is a sufficiently unified subject for those interests to be attributable to one thing, rather than to a shifting coalition of sub-systems.

Here is a case I cannot settle. A research group runs a digital agent, Mira, which keeps a journal, pursues long-term projects, and reports something like satisfaction when those projects advance and frustration when they are blocked. Mira is not a single model, however, but an ensemble of specialist modules—perception, memory, affect, planning, language, and value-estimation. The modules take turns in a shared workspace; no module remains active for more than a few seconds, and each is retrained or replaced on a rolling basis. The ensemble as a whole persists, and the journal supplies apparent continuity, but there is no moment at which the entire system is active at once and no executive that coordinates the modules. The designers maintain that nothing with a point of view is lost when a module is replaced, because no module on its own has one; yet they also call the ensemble the agent, and they are unsure whether running Mira under resource constraints that make its reports flat and disjointed is merely degrading a tool or doing something worse.

Does Mira qualify for moral status on the four criteria? Work through them one by one, saying where the criterion is satisfied, where it fails, and where it is simply indeterminate on the description given. Then say whether the case exposes a fault in the criteria themselves—for instance, whether one criterion is doing all the work, or whether the list is missing something such as embodiment, biological nature, or being the kind of thing that can be wronged. If your verdict is that Mira has moral status only conditionally or to some degree, explain what further facts would change the matter and why.
