Lately I keep running into the same fork in the road when people reason about their own position in the universe, and I cant tell whether the two sides have names or whether I'm setting up a false dichotomy.

Case one: an argument about how long humanity will last. If we die out soon, the total number of humans ever will be maybe 100 billion; if we spread through the galaxy, maybe 10^20. I notice that I'm roughly the 100-billionth human. One way of reasoning says: treat yourself as a random draw from all humans who will ever live; in that case, being this early is astronomically unlikely if the total is 10^20, so my early birth is strong evidence that we'll die out soon. The other way says: no, the fact that you exist at all is more likely under the scenario with more humans, because there are vastly more slots for you to fill, and that cancels out (or more than cancels out) the "being early" datum. Same observation, opposite verdicts.

Case two: two theories agree on everything I can observe, but Theory A says there are a billion observers in the universe and Theory B says a trillion. One style of reasoning says I should stay 50/50, since no observation distinguishes them. The other says I should strongly favor Theory B, because my own existence is a thousand times more likely under a theory with a thousand times more possible mes. That last conclusion sounds absurd to me: you can make a theory more likely just by multiplying people you'll never meet, with no new evidence about them.

I know this is adjacent to some famous debate about whether we're near the end of humanity, but I'm after the underlying fork, not that specific argument. So my question: are these two rules established positions with names, and what exactly is the disagreement between them? Is one of them quietly smuggling in an extra assumption, or are both defensible from different starting points? And does the second one really force you to accept the crazy verdict about the trillion people, or is there a way to keep the rule and reject the conclusion?
