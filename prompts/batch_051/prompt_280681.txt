I have been turning over the idea, sometimes labeled panpsychism, that the fundamental constituents of the physical world have some form of experience. What bothers me is less the bare claim that such entities have an inner life than the thought that their experience might include suffering, since suffering would be what carries moral weight. But I have realized I lack a definition of suffering sharp enough to sort the cases that matter to me. Please propose a working definition of suffering that classifies these nine cases: a person enduring a migraine; a dog recovering from surgery; an octopus kept in a barren tank; a plant under severe drought stress; a bacterium exposed to an antibiotic; a thermostat straining near the limit of its operating range; an electron disturbed during a measurement; a computer program that emits pain reports because it was designed to; and a person in dreamless sleep. I am confident the first three are cases of suffering and the thermostat and the sleeping person are not, and I am genuinely undecided about the plant, the bacterium, the electron, and the program. Run your definition through all nine cases, and then tell me where it returns a verdict you find surprising or least able to defend.
