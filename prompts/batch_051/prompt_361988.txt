I host a tiny philosophy podcast for people who never studied philosophy in school, and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends hedonism and what she calls the common core view of pleasure. Her line is that hedonism only works if pleasures add up to something, and they can only add up if beneath all the variety — a hot shower, finally cracking a puzzle, the relief when pain stops, being told your kid is safe — there is one shared felt quality, a kind of glow or tone every pleasant moment has in common. The standard objection people throw at her is the heterogeneity problem: these experiences feel nothing alike, and what we call pleasure is just a grab bag of different states we happen to want or approve of. Her stock reply is that the common core isn't a separate sensation you can pick out, its a dimension any experience can vary along, the way a light can get brighter without changing color.

I want the interview to stress test this rather than let her restate it, and I have some angles I've been turning over on my own. Could you write eight to ten questions with follow-ups? In particular: at least two on the introspection worry (if the core is real, why do so many careful people report not finding it, and does she have to admit you only see it after training yourself to look?); at least two on mixed or borderline cases (do bitter-sweet moments or pain you enjoy like the burn of a hard workout force her to say opposing tones coexist in one experience, and what does coexistence even mean on the dimension view?); one on the difference between enjoying an activity and being pleased that something happened; one on flatness and malaise, where life doesn't hurt exactly but feels gray — is that a third quality, or maybe the absence of the common core, and either way what does it do to her picture; and a closing one on what she would count as evidence against the view — but only after pushing past her usual reply, which is that critics are just attending to the wrong thing and confusing the object of pleasure with the pleasure itself. For each question, write the follow-up you'd press with after the reply she's most likely to give.
