A friend of mine has gotten really into costly signaling and now he defends the whole expensive engagement ring business like this: the ring is just a peacock's tail. A peacock's tail is a handicap — it's costly and dangerous to grow, and that's precisely why it's an honest signal; a sickly or low-quality bird can't afford to display one, so peahens can treat it as a reliable ad. Same, he says, with the ring: it costs a real chunk of money (traditionally the guy had to save for months), so it honestly signals commitment, because a man who wasn't serious wouldn't sink that much into a promise. So the custom isn't wasteful or irrational; it's just the handicap principle doing what it does.
