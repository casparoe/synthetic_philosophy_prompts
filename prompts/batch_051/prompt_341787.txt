Something I keep going back and forth on: we treat the sadistic guard who enjoyed hurting prisoners as a monster, but the desk officer who spent his days organizing the deportation schedules that made the killings possible—the Eichmann type—we tend to think of as a gray, thoughtless functionary, as if he's somehow less evil. Reading Arendt on Eichmann doesn't exactly settle it either: her view seems to be that the thoughtlessness itself matters, that his kind of evil is cliché-bound and banal rather than deep. But is our ordinary differential reaction defensible? The functionary enables far more death than the sadist ever directly inflicts. Are we tracking something real about culpability, character, or the kind of wrong involved, or is it just the imagery of hands-on violence doing the work? In other words, should we keep the asymmetry, reverse it, or give it up—and what would the best defense of it actually be?
