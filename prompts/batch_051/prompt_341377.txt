i'm a software engineer and i've been going back and forth on something. my understanding of hume is roughly this: when you actually look at a cause and an effect, you never see any necessary connection, any real power making the one produce the other. all you see is one event following another, with contiguity and regular succession — constant conjunction, i guess. the feeling that the cause must produce the effect is just a habit of expectation, and we project that necessity onto the world. so out in the world there is only regularity; the necessary connection is something we add.

but the causal discovery stuff i use at work seems to pull more out of observational data than just sequences. take an unshielded collider: x and y are independent on their own, but they become dependent once you condition on z. that pattern of conditional dependence alone lets a discovery algorithm orient x -> z <- y, with no time-order information needed. with assumptions like acyclicity, faithfulness, and no hidden confounders, algorithms like PC can sometimes learn which way the arrows point from a joint distribution, not merely that things are correlated. that feels like the data is carrying a structural asymmetry that isnt just x came before y.

so here's what im stuck on. does this break hume's argument? his premise seems to be that experience gives you nothing beyond regular co-occurrence, so any idea of a real causal tie is something we add from the mind. but if a probability distribution already contains orientation information, then experience gives more than he thought. does his argument survive by saying the orientation information is just another pattern of regularity, and still no necessary connection is observed? or does it put pressure on the regularity reading and make the new hume-style view (hidden causal powers are real, we just can't directly observe them) more attractive? or does causal discovery not touch the philosophical point at all, because it still never shows a necessary connection and the algorithms are only as good as their assumptions? which premise of humes argument is actually under pressure, and is there a repaired version of the regularity view that can absorb what causal discovery finds?
