Her line, roughly: moral sentences conventionally express a disapproval-like attitude plus a belief with deflated, non-robust truth-apt content, and the belief component supposedly does the compositional work in embedded contexts while the attitude component keeps moral judgment practical. My audience is mostly the sort of person who read the SEP entry and then argued about it on LessWrong, so I dont want any 'what is expressivism' filler. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press where the position actually creaks, including at least one on each of: whether the view has just smuggled truth conditions back in through the belief component; what modus ponens with moral premises is even supposed to be valid in virtue of, if the expressed states are these mixed belief-attitude pairs; how the view handles negation when it isnt clear what the contradictory of a disapproval attitude is; and whether the belief component ends up being a moral belief, which looks like realism sneaking back in, or a merely descriptive belief, which looks like the view collapsing into naturalist cognitivism. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give. You're a metaethicist who knows the Frege-Geach dialectic cold: start by brainstorming and steelmanning her three or four standard defensive moves, then write questions that cut them off in advance.
