I've been teaching myself to code little reinforcement-learning bots — the grid-world kind that get +1 for food and −1 for a hot tile. One of them did something that's been bugging me: after getting burned a few times it started avoiding the whole corridor near the hot tile, even after I turned the penalty down to almost nothing, and it would slow down one square before the corner like it was dreading the place. I know that's just the value estimate propagating backwards through time, but it made me wonder. A friend said the negative reward is basically a valenced state for the agent — that it doesn't just shape behavior, it functions like pain or fear. Another friend said that's nonsence, a reward is just a number in an update rule, and the whole comparison is a category error because the bot has no body, no nociceptors, no self for the pain to belong to.

I want to get past the vibes. Suppose someone claims that a sufficiently rich RL agent's negative reward signal can constitute something like suffering, not merely a learned tendency to avoid. How would you actually test that? Give me a concrete protocol: what kind of agent and environment, what you'd manipulate, what behavioral or internal measurements you'd take, and exactly which outcomes would push toward "it's genuinely valenced/suffering" and which toward "it's just shaped behavior." Then be honest about the residue — wether the cleanest positive result would still leave a skeptic a way out, and whether the word "suffering" would mean the same thing at the end of the test as it did at the start.
