So I've been turning over an essay idea after the fourty-somethingth time someone on a rationalist forum argued about whether meritocracy is "real" or a myth, and I want people to kick at it before I write it up properly. The central claim is that meritocratic selection and desert are two different things everyone keeps treating as one. Meritocratic selection answers a sorting question: we've already decided this slot exists with these rewards attached, now who gets it. Desert answers a backward-looking moral question: given what this person did or is, what do they have coming. A perfectly fair sorting procedure can produce winners who do not deserve the prize, not because the sorting was secretly biased, but because the prize structure itself—that there is this slot, with this much money, status, and power attached—is something the sorting never actually justifies.

The essay would argue that most arguments about meritocracy (legacy admissions, test bias, network effects, inherited advantage) stay entirely inside the sorting frame: they only contest whether the sorting was clean. But even if you clean it up completely, the problem just moves. In a tournament with ten spots and a hundred equally capable, equally hardworking applicants, the winners are the output of a lottery, and calling the winners "deserving" is laundry for the arbitrariness of the scarcity. At the same time, people can obviously deserve things—a promised reward for a finished job, say—with no meritocratic tournament anywhere in sight, so the two really do come apart.

I'd especially like comments on: (1) whether the sorting-vs-prize-structure distinction is actually load-bearing or just collapses into the seperate boring point that fair procedures don't guarantee just outcomes; (2) the strongest version you can give of the view that winners of a fair tournament do thereby deserve the prize, and whether my essay actually engages it; (3) where I'm overclaiming or where the desert side needs more steelmanning. Also happy to hear if you think the framing is just a subtle terminological point with no payoff.
