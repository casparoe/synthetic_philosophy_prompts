I'm in a pragmatics seminar, and a friend has gradually convinced me that the standard story about presupposition accommodation papers over a distinction that matters. Take a sentence like "Even Maria passed," uttered to someone who did not previously believe that Maria was among the least likely to pass. The standard story says the hearer accommodates the presupposition and it goes into the common ground. My friend's view is that this is ambiguous between two very different operations: either (a) the hearer comes to share the presupposed content, so it enters something like mutual belief, or (b) the presupposed content is merely written onto the conversational scoreboard as a claim that nobody has yet challenged. On the scoreboard-only view, accommodation is less like coming to believe something and more like a committee minute that records a remark without recording that anyone actually assented to it.

These two accounts predict different things about what later speakers may properly do. If "Even Maria passed" genuinely puts the presupposition into the common ground, then a later speaker should be able to build on it freely — treating it as a premise for an inference, say, or referring back to it without reasserting it — and reopening it later ("wait, was she really among the least likely?") should be a costly conversational repair, like discovering a shared assumption was false. If accommodation only creates an unchallenged scoreboard entry, then a later speaker should be able to reopen the matter fairly cheaply, and building on it should feel more like leaning on an assumption nobody granted than on one everybody accepted. My friend's stronger claim is that most everyday accommodation is of the second, scoreboard-only kind, with the common-ground kind reserved for cases where the presupposition was already independently available in the context.
