Just spent two hours at a bar arguing with a friend about whether there's a sharp line between 'tall' and 'not tall,' and it was basically a preview of the talk I have to give next week, which I'm now dreading. I'm defending epistemicism about vagueness: there is a precise cutoff between tall and not tall, but we can't know where it is. The room will be mostly philosophers of language and linguists who work on gradable adjectives, and they lean supervaluationist — the view that a vague sentence is true if it's true on every admissible precisification, false if it's false on every one, and neither otherwise. My argument in one line: supervaluationism just moves the sharp cutoff into the space of admissible precisifications, since the boundary between admissible and inadmissible sharpenings is itself sharp and unexplained, while epistemicism at least puts the hidden cutoff where we already thought there was a fact of the matter.
