Two years ago I went vegan, and last month I began volunteering at an animal sanctuary. The residents include hens rescued from egg production; they still lay, and there is no rooster. The sanctuary does not sell or give away the eggs—some are fed back to the hens and the rest are composted—but my supervisor has said I am welcome to take some home rather than see them composted. I keep almost doing it, and I am genuinely unsure whether it would be wrong. I would like your credence, as a number, that it is morally permissible for me to take and eat those eggs under the following conditions: the eggs would otherwise be composted, taking them involves no purchase, no one changes how many hens are kept or how they are cared for because I take them, and no one expects me to take them. I do not want a survey of the debate; I want your number and your derivation of it. Decompose the question into the sub-claims your credence rests on—for instance, whether the egg should be fed back to the hen or simply left with her rather than treated as available to me; whether using the bodily products of a rescued animal as food wrongs her even when it causes no further harm; whether an individual act like this could reinforce demand, normalize egg-eating among the volunteers, or weaken the social meaning of my veganism; and whether there is a distinct expressive or virtue-based reason to abstain from using animals as resources even in a case like this. Assign each sub-claim a rough probability with one line of justification, explain how you combine them, state which sub-claim your final number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would move that number most.
