Exam question: "According to Lewis's scorekeeping model of conversation, presuppositions are among the parameters of the conversational score and are updated by a rule of accommodation. Explain what the rule of accommodation says, illustrate it with an example, and then critically assess the following worry: because presuppositions are silently added to the score whenever a speaker needs them, accommodation makes presupposition too easy and deprives the notion of presupposition failure of its explanatory interest. Is the worry well founded?"

Student's answer: "Presuppositions are pieces of information a speaker treats as already given, rather than as the main point of the utterance. The standard diagnostic is that they survive embedding under negation and questions: both 'Mary has stopped eating meat' and 'Has Mary stopped eating meat?' presuppose that Mary previously ate meat, even though only the first asserts it. Lewis's scorekeeping model treats conversation as a game whose score tracks many parameters at once. One parameter of the score is the set of presupposed propositions, which in his framework is the common ground, understood as the propositions the interlocutors mutually accept or believe. The score changes by rules of accommodation. The rule for presupposition says that when an utterance requires a presupposition P for its acceptability and P is not already in the score, P is written into the score, provided nothing about the conversational situation blocks this. Thus a speaker who says 'I have to pick up my brother from the station' manages to introduce the presupposition that she has a brother, and the audience accommodates it without needing any explicit comment.

Accommodation is not, on this view, entirely unconstrained. It operates only within limits, and a hearer can refuse or question a presupposition, for instance by asking what the speaker means. But a serious problem remains. On this account, presupposition failure is ultimately just a matter of whether the hearers choose to object. If no one objects, the presupposition is added to the common ground, and each participant ends up believing the presupposed content just as if it had been asserted. The boundary between presupposing and asserting therefore collapses: a speaker can get any content accepted simply by packaging it as a presupposition. Since the model lets the score automatically absorb whatever presupposition is needed, it cannot explain why some such utterances are defective rather than merely unexpected, and presupposition failure ceases to be the explanatory notion it was introduced to be."
