I'm in a philosophy of cognitive science / AI explainability course and we have to hand in a term paper proposal (about six pages, one undergrad semester) on whether language models can actually report their own reasoning, i.e. whether chain-of-thought is faithful. My half formed angle: the debate keeps running together two senses of "faithful". One is that the CoT tokens literally caused the answer. The other is that the CoT is an faithful reconstruction or simulation of how the model would have reasoned, even if the answer was fixed before the CoT got written. I think a lot of the skeptical results (CoT not matching the model's actual token probabilities, explanations shifting when you push the model, etc.) only undermine the first but get treated as if they undermine both.
