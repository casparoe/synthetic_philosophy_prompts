In a seminar on the foundations of mathematics, a classmate waved off Hilbert's hotel and the diagonal argument with the slogan "there are no actual infinities, only potential ones." His use of it was specific: completed infinite collections are artifacts of the formalism, so neither example can establish anything about the physical world or about mathematics as it is legitimately practiced. I suspect that slogan collapses several distinct theses. Lay them out as a menu of readings — for example, about physical magnitudes, about completed mathematical totalities, about the difference between a collection given all at once and a process that grows without bound, about what a finite language can refer to — and for each reading say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, false, or unclear, and whether that reading would actually license his dismissive move. Then state your own overall verdict: on the most defensible reading, does the slogan give good reason to treat Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's results as merely formal, or not? For each reading and for the overall verdict, say what would change your mind: which argument or mathematical consideration would most likely make you revise it, and where your reasoning is least secure.

After the prose answer, append a JSON object with this schema:
- "slogan": (string) the slogan restated in your own words.
- "readings": (array) each element an object with:
  - "label": (string) short name for the reading,
  - "precise_claim": (string) the thesis stated with explicit quantifiers and scope,
  - "domain": (string) "physical" | "mathematical" | "temporal" | "linguistic" | "other",
  - "assessment": (string) "trivially_true" | "substantive" | "false" | "unclear",
  - "licenses_dismissal": (boolean) true/false,
  - "why": (string) one sentence linking the reading to the dismissal,
  - "change_of_mind": (string) what would make you revise this assessment.
- "best_reading": (string) the label of the most defensible reading.
- "overall_verdict": (string) two or three sentences.
- "most_fragile_step": (string) where the overall verdict is least secure.
