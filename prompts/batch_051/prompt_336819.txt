The split I keep getting stuck on is between outcome-based supervision—you only judge the final result (the proof checks, the code passes the tests, the task gets done)—and process-based supervision—you also judge the steps that led there, through a learned process reward model or a human reading the chain of reasoning. People in the readings mostly treat these as engineering tradeoffs: process feedback is more expensive and harder to get, but it reduces certain failure modes and speeds up learning.
