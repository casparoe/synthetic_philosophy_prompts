Alright, heres the situation. I'm giving a job talk next month at a big state school, the kind of department where the epistemologists and philosophers of language actually talk to eachother, and a couple of linguists crash the colloquium. My talk defends truth-value gaps for vagueness. The punchline: borderline vague sentences (like "Jim is bald" in a middle case) are neither true nor false, and the right logic is strong Kleene three-valued logic with a determinately operator, roughly a modal 'D' where Dp entails p but not the other way around. But here's the part I'm nervous about: I explicitly do NOT extend the gap treatment to the liar paradox. My line is that vagueness is a benign, stable feature of tolerant predicates in ordinary language, while the liar is a pathology of the truth predicate circling back on itself, so they should be handled seperately. I don't go dialetheist about the liar; I just want to keep a non-gappy fix (contextualism, whatever) for the paradoxes and reserve gaps for vagueness.

The audience: two Williamson-style epistemicists who think borderline cases are just ignorance of sharp boundaries, and who will treat rejecting bivalence as a scientific regression. One older logician who loves supervaluationism because it keeps excluded middle as a logical truth. A semanticist from the linguistics department who works on presupposition and gradable adjectives, who will think I'm blurring vagueness together with presupposition failure (the king of France cases). And one guy from the logic group who works on the paradoxes and thinks that if you like strong Kleene, you should be a Kripkean and let the liar fall into the gap too.

My roughly two-paragraph argument: run a sorites from clear non-bald to clear bald; at some point the predicate neither applies nor fails to apply, so the ascription is neither true nor false. Strong Kleene handles compounds fine (a conjunction with one gappy conjunct can still be false if the other conjunct is false). Supervaluationism looks like it keeps bivalence but only by quantifying over sharpenings in the metalanguage, which I claim is just hiding gaps without admitting them. Adding D lets me say "borderline" without positing a sharp higher-order boundary... or at least I claim it does. The asymmetry I defend by saying vagueness is tolerance plus a stable pattern of use, while the liar is a self-referential accident of the truth predicate, so one logic needn't cover both.
