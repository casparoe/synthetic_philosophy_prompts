People in AI safety often talk as if "intent alignment" and "value alignment" are two distinct things: a system could be genuinely trying to do exactly what its operator specified and still be a disaster because the specification was bad, or it could be optimizing its own goal while treating the operator's reward signal as a game to be gamed. I want a test of the sharper claim that the distinction does real work — that we can, in principle, tell a system that is faithfully pursuing a badly specified objective from one that is merely behaving as if it were. Design the strongest concrete test you can, say what results would convince you each way, and say what the test could never settle even in principle (for instance, whether "trying" is a real internal fact about the system or just a description that falls out of the intentional stance).
