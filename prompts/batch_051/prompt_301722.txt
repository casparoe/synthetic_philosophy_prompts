I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about the difference between lying, bullshitting, and just saying something true that you had no right to be sure of. I want a position section in the piece instead of the usual "experts disagree" parade, so I've been trying to pin down where I stand and I'd like a consistency check before I build the essay around it. Here's what I've got:

(1) The rule for assertion is knowledge: you're only allowed to flat-out assert that p if you know that p. Saying something you dont know is out of bounds, even when it turns out true.

(2) If a friend buys a lottery ticket with 1-in-300-million odds, I think it's fine to tell them before the drawing that the ticket lost. A colleague did this to me as a joke and I thought it was fine. So I allow assertions of "your ticket lost" based purely on the odds.

(3) Moore-paradoxical assertions like "it's raining, but I don't believe it's raining" or "the meeting is at 2, but I don't know it's at 2" strike me as not merely weird—they're rule violations, of the same broad family as a lie but notably different. What I mainly mean is: you can't properly say them.

(4) There's a case in the literature about a creationist teacher who teaches evolution because it's the settled science and the curriculum requires it, even though she personally doesn't believe it. I think her assertion to the class—presenting the standard evolutionary account as true—is fine. Her institutional role and the state of the science give her the standing; she doesnt need her private belief to line up.

(5) I think you can violate a rule of assertion and still deserve no blame. A person who grew up in an epistemically terrible environment, or who has completely misleading evidence, might say something they shouldn't have, and yet it would be wrong to come down hard on them. So "was the assertion proper?" and "should we blame the speaker?" are different questions.

Does this list hang together? I'd like the audit: which commitments actually pull against each other, and rank those tensions by how severe they are. Also flag any that only look like they conflict but don't, since I suspect something in here is less of a problem than it seems. For each real tension, tell me what has to give and what the cheapest repair is.
