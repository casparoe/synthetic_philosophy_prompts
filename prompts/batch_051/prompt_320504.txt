I have been trying to think through how much confidence the standard foundational consensus actually warrants, rather than simply leaning on the fact that almost everyone in mathematics repeats it. Consider this precise claim: there exists a finite derivation of a contradiction from the usual first-order axioms of Peano arithmetic, meaning the successor axioms, the recursion equations for addition and multiplication, and the induction schema, in a standard deductive system for first-order logic with equality. I want your actual credence that this claim is true, where credence means the probability you would assign, before any proof of inconsistency is found, to the underlying factual assertion that PA is inconsistent. I am not asking for the verdict of the incompleteness theorems, since those by themselves only show that if PA is consistent it cannot establish its own consistency; I am asking for the number you would put on the underlying fact of the matter.

Please give me that number and then open up the reasoning behind it. Decompose your credence into the main considerations it depends on, assigning a rough probability or qualitative strength to each and showing how they combine into your final figure. I would expect at least the following to appear, though you should add, drop, or reweight as you see fit: the inductive success and internal coherence of ordinary arithmetic and the number theory built on it; the fact that no contradiction has surfaced despite more than a century of intense formal scrutiny, much of it explicitly aimed at testing the foundations, and recent decades of computer-assisted proof search; the proof-theoretic situation, including consistency proofs that only go through by means that outrun the theory itself; the historical fact that apparently secure axiomatic systems have collapsed before; and any specific doubts you have about the induction schema, about unrestricted quantification over the natural numbers, or about the very idea of assigning a probability to consistency claims of this sort. Then identify the factor to which your overall credence is most sensitive, the one where new evidence or argument would move your number the most, and describe what that evidence or argument would have to look like.
