I'm taking a semantics class this term and now I can't stop noticing conditionals, which is how I ended up in a genuinely dumb argument about my birthday cake. Last week I came home to find half the cake gone from the counter, and the only animals that had been loose in the flat were our cat and our dog. I said, "If the cat didn't eat it, the dog did," and my roommate said that was obviously right — the cake was gone and those were the only two candidates. So far, settled. Then we checked the pet camera and saw that the dog had been shut in the bedroom the whole time, while the cat sat on the counter eating exactly that half of cake.

Now my roommate says the first one (an indicative conditional) was true at the time, but the matching counterfactual, "If the cat hadn't eaten it, the dog would have," is false; the dog was locked away and never had a shot. I've got an argument that he can't say both of those things:

(1) Both sentences are built from the same two ingredients: the cat not eating the cake, and the dog eating the cake.
(2) The only difference between "If the cat didn't eat it, the dog did" and "If the cat hadn't eaten it, the dog would have" is a bit of tense/mood marking whose job is to register whether the cat-not-eating option is being treated as a live possibility.
(3) Whether a speaker treats the antecedent as a live possibility is a fact about the speaker's information at the moment, not a fact about how the cat-eating and dog-eating are hooked up in the world.
(4) So the two sentences make the same demand on the world — they have the same truth conditions.
(5) Therefore they can't come apart in truth value, the way my roommate says they do. Either the first sentence was never strictly true, or the counterfactual is true, or he's just wavering.

I keep feeling like theres a slide somewhere around (2) or (4), but I can't make it precise. Is my argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails and what would a corrected version say? And underneath that: is my roommate actually justified in treating the indicative and the counterfactual differently here, or is he just being inconsistent?
