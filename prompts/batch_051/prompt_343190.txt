I've been turning over a parallel and I cant tell if its a real structural fact or just a loose analogy. In differential geometry, and especially in general relativity, we keep being told that the important objects — vectors, tensors, the Riemann curvature — are "coordinate-free." That doesn't mean coordinates are useless: you need to pick a chart or a gauge to compute anything, and then you check that what you said is invariant under changing charts, so the choice of origin or frame cancels out. Let me call that the invariance side.

Now the language side. People have argued that indexicals like "I," "here," and "now" cannot be eliminated from thought or speech without losing something. Someone can believe that Jana is bleeding and still fail to do anything until she also believes that she is Jana; a list of names, dates, and GPS coordinates does not by itself tell her where she is located in that list. My hunch is that indexicals are to natural language what coordinate charts are to differential geometry: local, context-bound labels that you can in principle translate out of ("I am bleeding" becomes a proposition about Jana at time t), but the translation leaves behind a hidden choice of "origin" — a speaker, a place, a now — much like picking a chart. So maybe a language without pronouns or indexicals is like physics with no allowed coordinate systems: not flat-out impossible, but shaped so that no actual assertion can be made without smuggling an anchor back in.

The thing that makes me suspicious: in geometry the coordinate-free and coordinate-dependent formulations are equivalent, so dropping coordinates is costless; in language the standard thought experiments suggest dropping indexicals is not costless, because self-location is doing real work. Is this a deep structural analogy about invariance under change of frame, or does it break exactly where it matters?
