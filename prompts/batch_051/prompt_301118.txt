I got into an argument with friends about independence referendums a while ago, and when I actually wrote down my gut verdicts afterwards they looked kind of inconsistent, and I'm trying to figure out whether there's a principle underneath them or whether I'm just deciding case by case on vibes.

If Scotland had voted Yes in 2014, I'd have said: fine, that's how it works—the terms were agreed with London beforehand, the campaign was free and fair, and both sides had basically accepted the rules of the game. Same for Quebec: if the 1995 referendum had gone the other way, I'd have accepted it too; it was held in a legal framework everyone understood. But I was much less comfortable with Catalonia in 2017: the Spanish court said the vote was unconstitutional, the police tried to shut it down, turnout was a mess, and then the Catalan government declared independence unilaterally. My gut says that wasn't a legitimate way to do it, even though I don't doubt that a large chunk of the Catalan public genuinely wants out. So far I thought maybe I just care about legality and the parent state's consent. But then I think about the wealthy borough that votes to secede from its city so it can stop paying into the common tax pool, and I'd want to block that no matter how clean the vote was. And I'd think a territory that was conquered a few generations ago has a pretty strong claim to leave its current state, even over that state's objection. The case that really ties me in knots: if Shetland and Orkney responded to a Scottish Yes by saying they want their own referendum on staying in the UK or going it alone, I'd instinctively grant them one—but that seems to cut against why I let Scotland go as a single unit.

My one friend keeps offering a one-liner: any territorially concentrated community that votes for independence in a genuinely free and fair referendum may secede, full stop. I can't live with that in the rich-borough case, and I don't know what it says about Shetland after Scotland. Is there a principle—or a small set of principles—that captures most of these verdicts, and which of my verdicts has to give? And what does your best candidate say about a minority inside a seceding region that wants to carve out its own separate state at the same time?
