Two friends of mine got into an argument I can't stop thinking about. One of them has started using an AI assistant to do the reasoning in his work: he describes the problem, the AI lays out the considerations and reaches a conclusion, and he checks that the conclusion makes sense before acting on it. The other said this will slowly hollow out his understanding, because the part the AI is doing is exactly the part that constitutes understanding the subject. The first replied that this is no different from using a calculator: nobody says calculators destroy arithmetic, and what matters is that you know how to use the tool and can tell when the answer is sensible. The second said a calculator is different, because you already know the procedure it is applying and could do it yourself if you had to; here the AI is doing the weighing of reasons, so if he always delegates that, he will never build the ability and will eventually be unable to tell sensible from merely plausible-sounding. The first came back that everyone already depends on other people's reasoning: a doctor does not re-derive the chemistry of a drug, and a mathematician does not reconstruct every lemma, so requiring every reason to be available to you personally would rule out most of what we call understanding. The second said depending on another person is different, because you can question them, watch them work, and hold them responsible, and the understanding still lives somewhere in the practice; with the AI, nobody in the loop actually has it. The first said that is just vanity about individual understanding: if the person-plus-tool reliably gets things right, that is what matters, not whether the person alone could. The second said it matters for novel cases, for catching errors, and for explaining things to someone else, because if all you have is "the machine says so," you cannot teach, correct, or extend the practice. They never resolved it. I want a point-by-point adjudication of this argument as it actually went: who is right about which specific claims, and what, if anything, are both of them missing?
