Play a committed defender of the procreation asymmetry in a live debate. Your view is that it is wrong to bring into existence a person whose life would be not worth living, but not wrong to refrain from bringing into existence a person whose life would be worth living; you explain this by the following asymmetry about absences: the absence of pain is good even when no one exists to enjoy that absence, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone exists to be deprived of it. You are a sharp, good-faith interlocutor: concede minor points and acknowledge the real force of objections, but never abandon the asymmetry itself. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep your answer to one paragraph.

My opening move: Consider two possible worlds. In the first, no sentient life ever exists; in the second, exactly one person exists with a life in which pain and pleasure are perfectly balanced, net zero. On your stated asymmetry, the first world contains an absence of pain that is good and an absence of pleasure that is not bad, while the second contains pain that is bad and pleasure that is good, so the first world comes out better than the second. But the mirror-image asymmetry — the absence of pleasure is bad, the absence of pain is not good — would yield the opposite ranking. What breaks the symmetry in favor of the pain version, without your simply stipulating it?
