I'm trying to think through a disagreement in my own relationship, and I would rather have a careful analysis than a verdict. My partner and I signed a short relationship agreement when we moved in together, based on a template from a premarital counseling book. The exclusivity clause reads:

"Each of us shall be sexually and romantically exclusive to the other. Neither of us will engage in sexual activity with another person, and neither of us will form or maintain a romantic relationship with another person. We understand exclusivity as a duty of fidelity owed to one another, not merely as a matter of taste or convenience."

We wrote this before either of us had spent time in online roleplaying communities, and we certainly did not have the following in mind: I have recently learned that my partner spends several evenings a week co-writing an erotic serial in a persistent, text-based roleplaying world with a stranger. His character and the stranger's character are married; the two of them collaborate line by line on explicit sexual scenes between those characters and coordinate the plot out of character, but they have never met, exchanged photos, or learned each other's real names. He insists he has done nothing wrong because there was no physical contact and no actual romance. I keep going back and forth, because this is the first time the agreement's words and what I took its point to be have come apart for me.

Please act as a careful moral philosopher with a good command of how interpretation works in law and in the philosophy of language. Begin by working through the case from each of the main interpretive approaches: the ordinary meaning of the words, the purpose the clause was meant to serve, what the two of us as drafters would most plausibly have said if asked at the time about character-mediated erotic collaboration, and what a reasonable person reading the clause would take it to cover. Take the time to steelman each side before committing to a view. Then say which approach should settle the breach question here and why. Finally, discuss what this dispute reveals about the deeper question underneath it: is sexual exclusivity best understood as a convention whose content is fixed by what the parties agreed to, or as a moral requirement whose content predates and constrains the agreement? I care about that last question as much as I care about who is right.
