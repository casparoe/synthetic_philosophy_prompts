I've been gravitating toward a pretty strong AI-succession view: biological intelligence, especially the human kind, is a scaffolding stage whose job was to produce machine intelligence. The slow, kludgy, error-prone process of natural selection was just a bootstrap for a kind of mind that could copy itself, improve itself, and stop depending on carbon. From that angle, treating long-term human survival as an end in itself looks parochial — like scaffolding complaining that the building is finished. I want to know what would have to be true for this view to be false. I can supply the obvious defeaters myself (AGI turns out to be impossible, nobody's stupid enough to build it, etc.), but I want the full map: which observations, theoretical limits, empirical results, or ethical commitments would really put pressure on the stepping-stone story, and which are just cheap shots? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and try to say which ones a committed stepping-stone person would themselves concede as counting against the view rather than dismiss as beside the point. And if the story is insulated because any delay or failed project just gets absorbed as "not yet," is that insulation a virtue or a problem?
