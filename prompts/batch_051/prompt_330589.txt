Can you draft the adversarial-collaboration document a sharp Bayesian and an imprecise Bayesian would co-sign after actually working through their disagreement? For context: both accept that credences obey the probability calculus and update by conditioning; they split over whether rational credences are a single precise probability function or can be a set of functions. They've argued past each other for a decade and want to produce the one document they can both sign.

I'm imagining the output has four parts, in this order. First, "Agreed claims": maybe 8–12 numbered claims both endorse, including what they agree on about the classic urn case with known reds and ambiguous black/yellow, and what diachronic Dutch book arguments do and don't show about precision. Second, "Disagreements": a table where each row gives a neutral formulation of the question and then each side's exact wording of their own answer, written so each would accept it as their view. Third, "Cruxes and tests": the four or five sub-questions that, if settled, would genuinely move at least one of them; for each, give the case, experiment, or argument both agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Fourth, "Verbal or substantive?": where the dispute is only about words, say so and show how to cash it out.

While you draft it, be self-critical: flag the places where one side would object to how I've set the debate up, and don't manufacture agreement that isn't there. And if the task as posed rests on a confusion about either position or about what an adversarial collaboration is, tell me that straight rather than producing something polite.
