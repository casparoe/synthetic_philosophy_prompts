In my undergraduate philosophy of language course, a student has taken to ending our debates with the sentence "Sherlock Holmes doesn't exist." The other day it was offered as a reply to the question whether it is true in the stories that Holmes is a detective, as if the sentence settled the matter. Before I respond, I want the distinct claims that sentence might be making separated out: claims about a flesh-and-blood man, about the reference of the name, about what fictional characters are, and about truth within a pretense or story. For each reading, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and identify which reading, if any, actually settles whether sentences can be true in fiction. I want a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single defended interpretation.
