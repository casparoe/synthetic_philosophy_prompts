Our school gave everyone a Chromebook a couple years ago, and the district put monitoring software on them that can see our screens, read our school email, and even turn the camera on during remote tests. The official line is that they are school devices, so we shouldn't expect privacy on them, and that if we aren't doing anything wrong we don't have anything to worry about. Fair enough, I guess.

But the same school is super intense about privacy in other places. Last semester a kid got suspended for reading someone's texts over her shoulder without permission, and in health class they literally teach us that privacy is part of human dignity and that people need private space to figure out who they are; there are even whole assemblies about not sharing each other's photos or doxxing people. So I can't tell weather my school thinks privacy is a big deal or not, and I don't just mean that the adults are being hypocrites. I want to know what picture of what privacy is for would make both halves of how the school acts make sense together. Is the school assuming privacy only matters when you have something to hide, or only when a person controls access to their own stuff, or something else? Is that view actually coherent? Is there one view the school has to be assuming, or are the two halves each compatible with a bunch of different views of privacy, so the real tension is coming from somewhere else? And what would have to change in how they treat us if they really believed the health class stuff all the way down, versus if they really believed the nothing-to-worry-about line all the way down?
