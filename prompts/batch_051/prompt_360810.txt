Please give me your current credence in the following proposition, decomposed so that the reasoning behind the number is fully visible rather than collapsed into a bare estimate.

Proposition P: For any actual human person who began from a particular sperm S and a particular egg E, that person could not have existed had a different sperm from their actual father fertilized E, or had S fertilized a different egg released by their actual mother. The claim is not that the resulting child would have had different traits; it is that the actual person would have been absent from that world, and the child who existed instead would have been a numerically different person.

I currently put my credence in P at roughly 0.7; a colleague who works on the metaphysics of personal identity puts hers at roughly 0.25. I am less interested in the headline numbers than in locating where that disagreement lives.

Please structure your answer as follows:

(1) State your own credence in P as a single number.

(2) Decompose P into the sub-propositions your credence is most sensitive to. Include at least these: (a) that origin essentialism of this kind holds for nonhuman organisms; (b) that the relevant sense of "could not have" is metaphysical necessity rather than merely physical, practical, or legal impossibility; (c) that the link between biological origin and personal identity is tight enough for the necessity to transfer from organisms to persons; and (d) that monozygotic twinning, in which two distinct persons trace back to one zygote, does not undermine gamete-level origin essentialism. Add any further sub-propositions that do real work in your number.

(3) Assign each sub-proposition a rough probability and a one-sentence justification.

(4) Show how those probabilities combine to yield your overall credence in P, and say whether the combination is strictly multiplicative or involves dependencies among the sub-propositions.

(5) Identify the sub-proposition doing the most work in the gap between a person at 0.7 and a person at 0.25, and state what evidence, case, or argument would most move your own credence over the next year.
