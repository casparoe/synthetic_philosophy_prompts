I keep noticing a ladder in how people talk about minds, and I want to know whether it's a real framework or just a loose analogy. At the bottom are creatures whose good behaviors are only there because natural selection killed off the alternatives over generations; the individual never learns. One rung up are creatures that learn through reward and punishment in their own lifetime; the actions they try are still basically blind, but selection now happens inside the individual instead of across generations. Next are creatures that can try out candidates internally and reject the dumb ones before acting, so the environment doesn't have to punish every mistake. Above that there's something that uses language, diagrams, or other public tools to evaluate possibilities the creature couldn't represent on its own. I'm not sure the top rung is the same kind of thing as the others, and I'm also not sure where imitation fits. Some cases seem to straddle rungs: an octopus may do a lot of internal trial-and-error without anything like public language, and a parrot can use words without it being obvious the words restructure its thinking. Does this ladder have a recognized name? More importantly, is it supposed to be a strict hierarchy or just a useful ordering? Which transition does the most conceptual work, and can a single mind sit on different rungs for different tasks? If there is no established name, I'd want a definition and the boundary cases spelled out, not just a label.
