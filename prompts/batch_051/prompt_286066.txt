I am a retired natural scientist who has recently become absorbed in moral philosophy, and I would like your help thinking through a problem where a piece of law and a piece of philosophy pull in different directions.

Consider this provision, written in the register of a typical filial-support statute:

An adult child having sufficient means shall, upon demand, provide reasonable support for an indigent parent, including necessary medical or institutional care. No child shall be liable under this section, however, if the parent, while the child was a minor, abandoned the child or failed substantially to provide for the child's care and support.

Now suppose the following case, which the provision's drafters plainly never had in view. A mother left her son when he was four years old and moved to the other side of the country. For two years she sent small, irregular sums to the grandparents who raised him. After that she sent nothing and made no contact for twenty-eight years. The son is now thirty-four, financially secure, and has just learned that his mother is indigent and living in a memory-care facility. The state demands reimbursement from him under the provision above. The son argues that the exception applies because the mother abandoned him; the state replies that the exception is not satisfied because she sent payments during part of his minority. The son further argues, drawing on Jane English's friendship model, that whatever the statute says, he has no moral obligation to support a parent who never performed the acts of friendship and mutuality that alone ground such obligations.

I would like you to do three things. First, interpret the provision: what do its plain words require, what does its evident purpose suggest, what would its drafters most likely have said about two years of small payments after leaving a four-year-old, and what would a reasonable reader understand it to mean? Second, assess the son's appeal to English's friendship model and say whether the legal and moral questions should be kept separate or allowed to inform one another here. Third, tell me honestly where the statute, read on the most defensible approach, leaves room for English's model and where it shuts it out.

Throughout, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
