On the other hand, I can't shake the intuition that when a central bank buys government bonds during a recession, something real is created — spending happens that wouldn't have otherwise, idle resources get mobilised, and the economy ends up larger than it would have without the money creation. Act as a monetary economist with a philosopher's feel for conceptual distinctions, and please think carefully rather than giving a textbook answer. Start by steelmanning both sides. Then tell me what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that gives, and whether there's a reading of 'creates' on which both can survive. Keep the concepts seperate: wealth, output, liquidity, and the difference between one-off effects and changes to the economy's path.
