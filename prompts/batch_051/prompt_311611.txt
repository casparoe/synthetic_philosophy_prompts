Ok so I've gone down a math-y philosophy rabbit hole lately—structuralism, the old multiple-reductions problem for what numbers are—and I've ended up with a cluster of views I want pressure-tested. I'm not looking for a lecture on the standard positions; I want an audit of whether *my* set hangs together.

(1) Numbers exist, but they're not objects with an inner nature. Being 2 is just occupying the 2-position in the natural-number structure; there's nothing more to 2 than its relations to all the other positions.
(2) So I take the natural numbers to be the structure of an endless well-ordered sequence with a first element and a unique successor for everything, and any system isomorphic to that just is the natural numbers. There's no further fact about what the elements "really are."
(3) The von Neumann and Zermelo set-theoretic reductions are both perfectly good models. Neither is what numbers really are, and treating either as the definition would be a category mistake.
(4) But I'm not a relativist about arithmetic: "3 is prime" is just plain true, not true-in-some-models-but-not-others.
(5) So I also want to say "is 2 an element of 4?" is a pseudo-question: it only has an answer once you've brought in a reduction that (3) says isn't privileged.
(6) Seperately, I think set theory isn't just another structure in the same boat. The cumulative hierarchy is the background framework in which structures get instantiated, not merely one more structure that could be swapped for an isomorphic copy.
(7) And I don't want an account that makes ordinary mathematical talk come out confused. When number theorists ask whether 2 is less than 4, or group theorists treat a subgroup as a subset, they're not making category mistakes.

Does this package actually survive contact with someone pushing on it? I can't tell which of these are load-bearing and which are throwing the whole cluster off, or whether the tensions I half-sense are real or just artifacts of my being sloppy. If there are real conflicts, I'd want them ordered by severity, with what has to give and what the cheapest repair costs.
