A scientific society, sick of eponyms misatributing credit, adopts a rule: any new law, effect, or constant that gets a person's name attached must be named after a randomly selected researcher in the same field who had nothing to do with the discovery. The point is to make eponyms honest by making them openly arbitrary labels, so nobody mistakes the name for credit. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position: what does it do to how scientists talk and cite, who ends up with prestige, and how does the lottery get gamed first? Where does the scheme break, and does it actually fix the problem Stigler's law describes or just make the misattribution louder?
