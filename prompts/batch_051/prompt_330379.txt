Something I've never understood about the moral realism/anti-realism debate is why the argument from queerness is still, after all these decades, functioning as a live move on one side and a live response on the other. The anti-realist says: if stance-independent moral facts existed, they'd be unlike anything else in the natural world—we'd need a special faculty to detect them, and their built-in motivational pull would be inexplicable. The realist replies that epistemic reasons and mathematical facts look just as "queer" by that standard, and we don't throw those out, so the argument proves too much. At that point both sides seem to accept the same argumentative structure—something like "if X is queer in way W, don't believe in X; morality is/isn't relevantly like math"—and then they just dig in on the comparison. And nobody has a test for what counts as relevantly like math.

My working theory is that this is not really a disagreement about what exists. It's a disagreement about which comparisons are allowed to do argumentative work—about how close an analogy has to be before it counts as a reductio. Realists treat morality's practical indispensability as giving it the benefit of the doubt; anti-realists point out that practical indispensability is exactly what you'd predict if evolution or upbringing had wired the conviction into us, so it can't do any justificatory work. If that's right, the debate has stalled becuase each side is using a different standard of evidence while presenting it as a discovery about the same world.

Is that diagnosis correct, or is there a hidden factual question in there (about what evolution actually gives us, or how much moral disagreement there really is), or is it just a verbal dispute over "queer" and "mind-independent"? I want a real diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is—not a survey of positions and not a verdict on who's right—plus an honest answer about whether anything could actually move it: a conceptual distinction, a new empirical result, a formal theorem, or whether we should just expect permanent stalemate and rephrase the question.
