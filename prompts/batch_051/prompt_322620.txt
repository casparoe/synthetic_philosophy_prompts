ok so in my philosophy club we've been going over the symbol grounding problem and at the same time my english teacher is making us write villanelles, and it hit me that a villanelle is kind of the symbol grounding problem in poem form. the idea is that symbols only get their meaning from other symbols, like if you tried to learn a language from a dictionary written entirely in that same language: every definition just points you to more words, and you'd never actually touch the colors or the sounds or the stubbed toes that the words are supposed to be about. searle's chinese room is the same thing — there's a guy in a room with a giant rulebook, he gets chinese characters slipped under the door, looks up which characters to push back out, and does it perfectly, and from outside it looks like he understands chinese but inside nothing is going on except syntax. no meaning ever shows up. the rule-following is flawless and the understanding is zero, and that's the whole problem.

so I want a villanelle about that. please make the two refrains do the philosophical work: one refrain should voice the side where everything is running correctly — the rulebook, the dictionary, the next symbol — and the other refrain should voice the side where nothing is actually being understood. the refrains in a villanelle keep coming back and swapping places, which is exaclty like how the symbol system can recombine itself any number of times and still never get grounded. someone who knows the problem should be able to see that the form is making the argument, not just decorating it; someone who doesn't know the problem should still get a good poem with that slightly eerie stuck-in-a-loop feeling. and plz don't have the poem literally mention searle or harnad or "symbol grounding problem" — it should earn it through the form.
