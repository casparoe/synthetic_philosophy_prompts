I'm running a one-off workshop on forecasting for a mixed group of about 25 grad students and policy wonks, 80 minutes, and I want to build it around a passage from Edward Gibbon's General Observations on the Fall of the Roman Empire in the West, chapter 38 of Decline and Fall. I'll have the passage in front of me to read aloud, but the gist is this: Gibbon says the fall of Rome was the natural and inevitable effect of immoderate greatness, that prosperity ripened into decay, that the causes of destruction multiplied with the extent of conquest, and that the stupendous fabric eventually sank under its own weight. The thing I want them to feel is not the truth or falsity of that specific claim, but the force of the outside-view move: treating Rome as one member of a class of great powers rather than as a unique drama, and letting the base rates of that class do the forecasting work.

Design a classroom activity where the participants have to make survival forecasts for a handful of present-day polities or institutions (say the Chinese Communist Party, the United States as a global hegemon, the European Union, and one or two more). The game should first get them to make intuitive inside-view forecasts, then force them to work out reference-class base rates from cards or handouts with historical empires, dynasties, and hegemonic orders, and then produce a second forecast using only those base rates. I don't want any lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the phrase outside view until the debrief. Include the rules, the exact materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't require real money, a timeline for the 80 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the distinction between inside and outside view, reference class selection, and base rate neglect. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, especially around people objecting that their own case is special. Be honest about which part of Gibbon's argument the activity can't capture, and where the facilitator has to supply it.
