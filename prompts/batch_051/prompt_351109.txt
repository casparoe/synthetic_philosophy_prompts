ok so for a reading group I've been asked to write out where I've landed on respect and blame, and I want to pressure-test whether these all fit together before I show it to anyone. Here's the list:

1. The basic kind of respect — recognizing someone as a person — is owed to every person just in virtue of their capacity to grasp and answer to reasons. It isn't earned and it can't be lost by being bad at things or even being a horrible person.
2. At the same time I want to say that contempt, the attitude of treating someone as beneath consideration (not just acting mean), is never a fitting attitude to have toward another human being, no matter what they've done.
3. I'm fairly convinced you can't be under a moral obligation to feel a particular feeling, since you can't feel on command and ought implies can. So 'you ought to be more grateful' or 'you should respect him' are, taken literally, confused demands.
4. But then I also hold people responsible mainly for their attitudes — resentment, trust, gratitude, condescension — rather than just their outward actions. If someone treated me fine but privately held me in contempt, I'd count that against them morally, maybe more than a clumsy action.
5. I think recognition and esteem are genuinely different: recognition of someone as an equal is owed to everyone, while esteem — admiring them, looking up to them — has to be earned. Lots of ordinary talk about 'respect' runs these together.
6. And I think the wrong in an insult is mostly the attitude it expresses, not the words. When we resent an insult we're resenting the displayed contempt, which is why the same words can be wounding or not depending on what they express.

Could you go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only seam to be? I'd like the tensions ranked by how much they actually force a change, with the cheapest fix for each and what it would cost. Then tell me whether there's a single revision that would clean up the most. I genuinly can't tell where the problem is, so don't just defend the package — audit it.
