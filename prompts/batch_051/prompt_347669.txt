On a rationalist forum I read, a commenter recently dismissed a proposal to reduce a particular kind of far-future suffering by saying the thinking behind it was just "the best of all possible worlds" again, and that this idea has been discredited for centuries. The phrase was doing real argumentative work, but I cannot tell exactly what claim was being made. It could be a claim about global optimality over all possible worlds, a claim about constrained optimality among worlds some agent could actually bring about, a claim that every apparent evil is compensated by a greater good, or an epistemic claim that we cannot demonstrate that any alternative is better. Those are different theses with different verdicts. Take the phrase "the best of all possible worlds" as it functions in arguments of that sort, and set out the distinct precise claims it might express. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then say which reading actually supports the dismissal of the proposal, and which reading is usually meant by people who say the phrase is obviously discredited.
