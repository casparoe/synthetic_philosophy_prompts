I have been puzzling over an inconsistency in the way public life and ordinary conversation treat the size of a population, and I would like help working out what it presupposes.

When we assess how well a country is doing, the measure everyone reaches for is some kind of average: GDP per capita, median income, average life expectancy, average reported life satisfaction. The implication of these measures seems to be that what matters is how well the typical person is doing. On that view, adding a new person who is merely average would leave the country exactly as well off as before, and adding a person below the current average would make the country worse.

Yet almost everywhere else, we treat more people as a good thing. Governments describe falling birth rates as a looming crisis, even when the fall comes from people freely choosing smaller families. A growing city is called thriving even if its income per resident is flat or declining. In ordinary life, most people would say that a couple who have a third child they are able to raise well have added something to the world, not that they have left its value unchanged. And in conservation, a species with many members is considered to be doing well, while a species reduced to a few hundred is called endangered no matter how healthy those remaining individuals are.

I am trying to understand what view of value, if any, would make this pattern consistent. Does treating population decline as an emergency commit us to the idea that a larger number of good lives is better than a smaller number, even when the average life is no better? Or do the per-person measures reveal what we actually believe, with the rest being loose talk? Which parts of this pattern strictly presuppose a particular view of what makes the world better, which parts merely fit one view among several, and where is the practice simply underdetermined? And what would have to change in national statistics, in family policy, and in how we assess endangered species if we settled firmly on one of these views rather than the other?
