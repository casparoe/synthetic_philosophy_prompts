I'm currently taking a course on the philosophy of extraterrestrial life, and we've spent several sessions on how to frame "advancement" in a cosmic context. The standard move is the Kardashev scale, which ranks civilizations by total energy use; John D. Barrow proposed an alternative that ranks civilizations by the smallest scale of matter they can deliberately engineer, from human-sized artifacts down through molecular, atomic, and nuclear scales. I'd like you to lay out the most important implications of taking Barrow's amendment seriously as a measure of civilizational advancement—particularly for what we should expect a very advanced civilization to be doing, which technosignatures would and would not make sense to search for, and whether energy consumption remains a reasonable proxy for capability or intelligence.
