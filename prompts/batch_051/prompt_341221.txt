Defenders of direct democracy often appeal to the Condorcet jury theorem: if a large electorate votes independently and each voter is more likely than not to pick the better of two options, the majority's probability of choosing correctly approaches certainty as the electorate grows. Now take a two-option referendum and break only the independence assumption. A fraction α of the electorate consists of independent voters, each of whom votes for the better option with probability p = 0.6; the remaining fraction votes as a single correlated bloc, following a shared media signal that is correct with probability q = 0.53, so all of them err together. Holding α and q fixed as the total number of voters grows, analyze what happens to the probability that the majority picks the better option. Does the theorem's conclusion survive, fail, or survive only under a threshold condition on α? Work out that condition, and then say whether the result shows that the epistemic case for referendums depends on independence rather than on the sheer size of the electorate.
