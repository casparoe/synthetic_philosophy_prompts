We read a chunk of Abelard in my medieval philosophy class and I'm stuck on the consent thing. The teacher kept saying his position is that the sin is in the consent, not in the act — and I think she also said it isn't in the wanting either, but maybe I wrote that down wrong. So the part that makes something a sin is the inner yes, and you can apparently sin fully without ever doing the outward thing. But "consent" here can't just mean saying yes out loud or signing something, right? I need to give a working definition of consent in Abelard's sense, the kind that's enough for a sin all by itself, and I have six cases that keep breaking every definition I try:

1. Emma plans to steal from the cash register at her job, texts her friend the whole plan, and is about to go through with it, but when she gets to work she sees a cop car outside and doesn't do it. I'd say she consented even though nothing happened.
2. Marcus is physically forced — someone grabs his hand and makes him sign his brother out of the will — and the whole time he's fighting it and thinking "this is wrong, I don't agree to this." I'd say he did not consent even though the signature is real.
3. Lina fantasizes about poisoning her awful boss. She enjoys the fantasy a lot, but she's never once decided to actually do it, and she's pretty sure its never leaving her head. I have no idea whether this is Abelard-style consent or just the wanting that supposedly isn't sin.
4. Jonah firmly decides one night, in his own head, that he'll sleep with his friend's partner. He goes to their apartment, but nobody's home, so nothing ever happens. This feels like the consent-without-the-act case, but I can't tell if Abelard would say he's sinned as much as if he'd done it.
5. Priya, in a flash of anger, mentally says "yes, I'll tell everyone her secret." When she gets to school she panics and ends up saying something totally harmless. I want to say she consented in that moment, but calling it an intention feels off because she never followed through. No idea.
6. Diego's cousin says he'll beat him up unless Diego helps steal a bike. Diego helps, but the whole time his internal monologue is "I don't want this, I'm only doing it because I'm scared." There's some kind of yes to stealing to avoid the beating, but it doesn't feel like the yes Abelard has in mind. Is that consent?

Give me a definition of consent in Abelard's sense that gets the clear cases right and tells me what to say about the weird ones. Test it against all six and say where it gives a strange answer — if it breaks on the fantasy case or the fear case, either fix it or defend the weird answer.
