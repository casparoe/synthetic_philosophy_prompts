I want the first session to make people feel the difference between three things that get thrown around as if they're the same: gaming a proxy reward, tampering with the reward signal itself, and wireheading. No lecture up front, no slides, and I don't want to name the terms until the debrief. I want a game where the scoring rules change between rounds and the players discover the exploits themselves, then have to own what they did when we map it onto the jargon.

Practical constraints: normal classroom, cheap materials, no computers, nobody needs ML background. Small stakes are fine — candy, leaderboard bragging rights. The game should run in about 55 minutes, leaving 20-ish for debrief, so I'd say four rounds max. Round-by-round, the scoring should get progressivly easier to game, so by the last round at least one team either tampers with the scoring mechanism or is visibly tempted to. If you can build in a wireheading move — where someone can get the reward while the task becomes irrelevant, and it's legal under the rules rather than just plain cheating — that's the ideal; if that can't be done honestly, say so and give me the next best thing.

Structure the writeup with these exact sections and approximate lengths:
