I've been teaching Arendt on lying in politics for a long time, and I keep coming back to the argument in Truth and Politics. As I read her, the real danger isn't that politicians occassionally say false things and some people get fooled. The deeper worry is that a certain kind of political lie tries to dissolve the line between fact and fiction altogether. Factual truth, she thinks, is fragile and contingent, and once a state or movement can spin a fully coherent counter-reality, the shared factual world that politics depends on starts to disintegrate. So the threat is less any individual falsehood and more the unmooring of the whole background of factuality.

Here's what I've been stuck on. Arendt was writing in a world where the big political lie was basically a centralized operation: a propaganda ministry, a party, a small group of image-makers, all pushing one coherent alternative story at a public that could in principle still check it against something. The coherence of the lie mattered; thats part of why it was so dangerous.

Now consider the current media environment. Deepfakes, voice cloning, and LLM-generated text make it possible to mass-produce political falsehood at basically zero marginal cost, and recommender systems optimize for engagement rather than truth. The result is that political lying is no longer centralized or even coherent. Every person can get a slightly different personalized fiction, and the fictions don't have to hang together as a single counter-narrative. There's often no identifiable liar, no propaganda ministry, no airtight alternative world being imposed on everyone.

So my question is whether Arendt's argument survives that shift. Which premise is doing the actual work: the claim that political lying attacks the shared factual world, or the background assumption that lies are crafted centrally and aim at coherence? If the new environment destroys the coherence of the lie, is that an improvement on her terms, or just a worse version of the same problem — no shared world at all, not even a false one? Is there a repaired Arendtian position that says something useful about personalized synthetic falsehood, or does the framework simply stop applying? I care about what happens to the argument, not about policy fixes.
