I read a piece that summarized Malthus as follows: "Malthus claimed that population grows geometrically while the food supply can grow at most arithmetically, so unless people practiced moral restraint, famine and epidemic would inevitably force population back down. He failed to anticipate the Industrial Revolution and the Green Revolution, which raised food output far beyond anything he considered possible, and he also assumed birth rates could never fall voluntarily. That is why his prediction of overpopulation did not come true, and why modern environmentalists who cite him are just recycling a failed forecast." How fair is this summary? Please go claim by claim and separate what Malthus actually argued from what the summary attributes to him. In particular, I want to know whether he treated the arithmetic limit as a fixed prediction of famine or as a standing tendency always held in check by the preventive and positive checks he described; whether he allowed any room for technological improvement in agriculture; and whether he really denied that birth rates could fall voluntarily. If the summary is roughly fair about his predictions, does that settle the question — or could Malthus's underlying principle still be right but early, and how would you distinguish a theory being legitimately updated from one being rescued after every failed prediction?
