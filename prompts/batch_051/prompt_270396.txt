I've been going back and forth with a friend about wu-wei for the last month, partly because I've been trying (there's the problem already) to get somewhere with drawing and piano. The harder I aim at being loose and unforced, the more self-concious and wooden everything comes out, and the moments that actually work are the ones where I wasn't watching myself at all. I've ended up mostly convinced the paradox is real: wu-wei, understood as an effortless, unforced responsiveness, seems to recede exactly when you make it a target. Any deliberate strategy for producing it—formal sitting meditation, drills, tricks, whatever—looks like it gets caught in the same bind, because the effort to install effortlessness is still effort aimed at the wrong thing. The version of the Daoist picture I find most serious treats this not as a practical obstacle that better technique will eventually solve, but as a diagnosis: the grasping itself is part of why the thing is lost.

I want to know what would actually count against this view of mine, not just the stock counterexamples. Please structure the answer in four sections. (1) "Steelmanned position"—restate my view in its strongest, most precise form, including a clear statement of what it predicts about people who go looking for wu-wei; about half a page. (2) "Ranked defeaters"—the main body, roughly 400 to 500 words, listing possible defeaters from genuinely fatal to merely awkward: skill acquisition and deliberate practice research, the fact that some teachers seem to convey the thing by sidestepping or tricking the student, the difference between trying to attend to spontaneity and simply letting trained dispositions run, cases where being told to stop trying actually works, and anything else you think I'm missing. For each, say what it would show if it held up and how much damage it does to the view. (3) "What a committed defender concedes"—which of these I'd have to grant as counting against my position and which I can dismiss, and the principle that separates them. (4) "Insulated or not?"—a short verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether for a view of this kind that's a bug or a feature. I suspect the skill-acquisition angle is the one most likely to actually move me, but be honest about whether it should.
