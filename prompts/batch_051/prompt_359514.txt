Over winter break some friends and I did a time travel marathon — Back to the Future, Avengers: Endgame, and we finally watched Steins;Gate — and theres this thing I kept noticing that I dont have a word for. Whenever someone goes back and changes the past, the story still treats the version of events from before the change as the real one, even after it's supposedly been erased. In Back to the Future, when Marty returns to the improved 1985, the timeline where his parents were miserable isn't just gone; it's still the baseline the whole movie was organized around, and the new 1985 is basically that baseline with a couple of edits. In Steins;Gate, Okabe remembers the old worldline after everyone else's memories have been rewritten, and the show treats his memory as a record of something that actually happened, even though in the current worldline those events never did. The audience is supposed to feel that the erased timeline mattered, but nobody ever says what status it has.

The closest things I know are the butterfly effect and branching timelines, but this isn't quite either. The old timeline isn't still out there somewhere like a branch, and the change isn't just chaotic ripple effects. It's more like the story keeps a single timeline, overwrites it, but then quietly uses the time traveler's memory (and our memory from watching) as a second timeline that gives the original version authority. Does this have a name? If not, what would you call it, and what exactly counts as doing this instead of just having an alternate timeline with a traveler who remembers a different branch? Because I don't just want the label — I want to know what the story is actually committing to about time, like whether it's sneaking in an extra layer of time where the change happens, and where the line is between that and ordinary parallel universe stuff.
