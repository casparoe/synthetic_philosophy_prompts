A friend and I argued late into the night about a mutual friend whose resentment of a far wealthier cousin struck one of us as rank envy and the other as an accurate sense of injustice. We talked in circles because we had no shared standard for telling those readings apart. I want the desiderata — the list of requirements — that a theory of envy and resentment must meet if it is to settle that kind of dispute. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of a particular camp. I am less interested in a scorecard of existing theories than in the requirements themselves and their tensions, including any that cannot be jointly met. The requirements should address at least these points: the distinction between envy and resentment, and whether it tracks the difference between wanting what another has and believing one has been wronged; whether a single episode can be both a moral defect and a reliable signal of injustice; how to handle Rawls's category of excusable envy without treating every envious reaction as a justice claim; how the theory classifies envy provoked by a procedure everyone regards as fair, such as losing a cleanly run lottery; and whether the desiderata shift when the background society itself is deeply unjust. Please write the answer in American English.
