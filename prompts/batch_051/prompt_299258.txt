I have been arguing with a colleague about whether twentieth-century algorithmic information theory really vindicates anything in the history of philosophy, and I would like your best reconstruction of one historical figure's likely verdict. Consider William of Ockham. The maxim now popularly attached to his name says that entities are not to be multiplied beyond necessity, but in his own work parsimony was a tool used inside particular debates — about whether universals are real things, about what God's omnipotence permits us to infer from the created order, about when a distinction in language corresponds to a distinction in reality — rather than a single, quantified rule of inductive inference. Now suppose Ockham were given a patient and rigorous exposition of Solomonoff induction: the theory that assigns each computable hypothesis a prior probability that falls off exponentially in the length of the shortest program that generates it, roughly 2^{-K(h)}, where K(h) is the Kolmogorov complexity of the hypothesis. Solomonoff's framework is often described as a mathematical vindication of Occam's razor, because it says simpler hypotheses are automatically favored before any evidence arrives.

Would Ockham recognize his razor in this? Put aside the question of whether he could follow the mathematics and assume he grants its internal validity. My question has three layers. First, would he accept the identifications that simplicity is compressibility and that parsimony is a matter of distributing prior probability over hypotheses — or would he regard those as category mistakes, since his razor was about which entities a theory posits in a given domain, not about the length of a program? Second, would he accept the presupposition that the world is generated by a computation and that every regularity worth finding is computable, or would that restriction sit badly with his theological commitments? Third, where exactly would he push back — on the prior, on short program length as the measure of simplicity, on the idea that one global bias works across all domains, or on the thought that parsimony can settle ontology? Please answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. One of the five points should state how confident you are in the reconstruction overall and single out the point you are least sure of.
