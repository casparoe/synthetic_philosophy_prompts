I'm a doctoral candidate in philosophy, working on the epistemology of legal proof, and I've been gathering my own verdicts on a set of cases about evidence that is in some sense merely statistical. I would be grateful for help extracting the principle—if there is one—behind the pattern.

Here are the cases. They are versions of familiar ones, but I have written them in my own terms so the reasoning has to come from the structure of the examples, not from authority.

1. The bus. A pedestrian is struck by a bus at night and sues the Blue Bus Company. The only evidence is that Blue Bus operates 80% of the buses on that route at that hour, and the remaining 20% are operated by Red Bus. No witness saw which company's bus hit her. I would not find Blue Bus liable, even though the evidence makes it 80% likely that the bus was a Blue Bus bus—comfortably above the civil preponderance standard.

2. The eyewitness. A pedestrian is struck by a car, and a disinterested witness testifies that she saw the defendant's blue car strike him. The witness is known to identify vehicles correctly about 80% of the time in conditions like these; there is no other evidence. I would find the defendant liable here, even though my confidence that this is the right defendant appears to be the same 80% as in the first case.

3. The gatecrasher. Exactly 1,000 people attend a rodeo; the records establish that 499 paid admission and 501 did not, and the records do not identify which individuals paid. A spectator is selected at random and sued for the price of admission. I would not find him liable, even though it is marginally more likely than not—50.1%—that he did not pay.

4. The flagged claim. An insurance company's audit rule flags any theft claim filed within two days of the policy's effective date. Its internal data show that 95% of claims flagged by this rule are fraudulent. A policyholder's theft claim is flagged by the rule alone, and there is no other evidence against her. In her suit to recover under the policy, I would find for her and not treat the claim as fraudulent. Assume the applicable standard is the ordinary civil preponderance standard, with no heightened burden.

5. The prison yard. A prison guard is injured in a yard containing exactly 100 prisoners. It is established that 99 of them took part in the assault and one did not, but no one can identify who fell into which group. The state charges one prisoner solely on the basis that he was present in the yard. I would acquit, even though the evidence makes it 99% likely that he assaulted the guard.

6. The masked figure. A security video shows a masked man committing a burglary. His height, build, a distinctive limp, and a rare jacket together match the defendant, and a competent expert estimates that fewer than 1 in 500 men in the city would fit that combination. There is no other evidence. On this one I keep reversing myself. Sometimes I think it is just a fancier version of the bus case and should come out the same way; other times I think the joint fit of several features is not different in kind from what an eyewitness does when she recognizes a face.

Is there a principle that captures my verdicts in cases 1–5 and tells me what to say about case 6? Please do three things. First, state the most defensible principle—or a small set of candidate principles—in precise terms, indicating which case motivates each component. Second, apply it to case 6 and to at least one further case I have not listed, and say explicitly how it handles the contrast between cases 1 and 2, where I can feel the difference but cannot articulate it. Third, tell me honestly which of my verdicts in cases 1–5 the principle cannot accommodate without strain, and whether the better option is to abandon that verdict or to complicate the principle.

As you answer, please mark clearly which parts of your account are settled—for example, the formal structure of the burdens of proof—and which parts are genuinely contested among people who work on legal proof. Where you are giving a contested view, present it as one candidate among others, not as the consensus. I am not looking for citations or a literature review; I want the conceptual structure tested against my cases.
