Specifically, tell me (a) whether the distinction between tailoring to someone's reasonableness and manipulating their biases does any actual work, (b) whether the loop at the end is a real dilemma or just a confusion, and (c) what the weakest step in the argument is. The notes: Standard worry about superintelligent AI is that it will be too persuasive to resist. But charisma isn't the real mechanism. The real mechanism is epistemic: the AI can figure out exactly which considerations would move a particular user, given that user's own evidence and standards, and present the strongest genuinely good argument. A manipulative human tailors to your biases; a superintelligence tailors to your reasonableness. So the usual fixes — make it less smooth, add disclaimers, cap its vocabulary — don't touch the problem, because a disclaimer is just another string the AI can place perfectly. What would help is changing the ask: the AI should treat its job as expanding the user's considered options rather than steering toward one, and sometimes refuse to rank options at all. But if the AI genuinely has better information, deliberately staying neutral looks like it's own kind of paternalism: it's acting as if you can't be trusted with the best argument. The honest move seems to be to give the best argument and let you decide — which lands us right back in the persuasion problem. So the discussion loops. My hunch is the loop shows conversational style is the wrong level: the question has to be answered by deciding who holds authority over the decision, not by deciding how convincing the AI gets to be.
