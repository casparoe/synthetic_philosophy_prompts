Jeffrey conditionalization is the standard rule for revising a rational agent's degrees of belief when the evidence is not a proposition learned with certainty. Suppose {E_1, ..., E_n} is a partition of possibilities and the agent's evidence does nothing except move her probabilities over the cells of the partition from old values to new ones. The rule says she should keep every conditional probability of the form P(H | E_i) fixed and recompute each unconditional probability as the weighted average of those conditional probabilities, with the new cell probabilities as weights. The rule's justification presupposes three things: that the evidence can be represented as a new probability distribution over a partition; that the agent already possesses the relevant partition; and that the conditional probabilities given the partition are rigid, that is, unaffected by the very change that moves the cell probabilities.

Now consider a development that postdates the rule's canonical motivations. Increasingly, the evidence an agent actually receives arrives as an opaque calibrated probability report rather than as a visible redistribution over a partition the agent antecedently had. A forecaster or prediction market returns a single number for an event; a machine-learning system emits a confidence score; an expert announces that she has moved to seventy percent on some claim. The agent gets the number without getting the partition over which the reporter redistributed probability, without getting the reporter's likelihoods, and often without any assurance that the reported number is defined over the same partition the agent uses. Sometimes the report is a constraint rather than a point value, such as a lower bound or an interval, and sometimes several reports arrive that are jointly inconsistent.

Does the argument for Jeffrey conditionalization survive this development, or does it depend on an idealization of transparent, partition-based evidence that the new setting breaks? If it survives, show how an agent is supposed to locate the partition and justify the rigidity condition when all she receives is a bare number. If it needs repair, identify exactly which premise is under pressure — the representation of evidence as a distribution over a fixed partition, the antecedent availability of the partition, or rigidity — and state the repaired rule, for instance an information-theoretic update that minimizes divergence from the old credences subject to the reported constraints, together with what it gives up relative to the original rule. If the argument is dead, say what should replace it. The question is about the fate of the rule itself, not about how platforms ought to report probabilities.
