You're a philosopher of physics who works on symmetry and explanation, and I want you to think carefully before answering, maybe start by brainstorming a few candidate diagnoses. I keep noticing a pattern in these debates, and I can't tell whether it has a name. The setup: someone says electric charge is conserved because the laws have a certain symmetry, and that this symmetry is what explains the conservation. Then a critic rewrites the exact same physics in different variables, or writes down a different action that gives the identical equations of motion, and the symmetry that was doing the explaining is no longer there — even though the observational predictions and the conservation law are all unchanged. So the explanation seems to track a artifact of how the theory is written down, not a difference in the world. I've seen the same shape of move outside physics too: someone explains an outcome by pointing to a symmetry of their diagram or setup, but a redescription everyone agrees is equivalent removes the symmetry while leaving the outcome fixed. My question is whether this specific failure mode has an established name. If it does, I need to know how it differs from the generic point that the same facts can be represented many ways, and from the point that gauge symmetries are just bookkeeping. If it doesn't have a name, coin one, define it precisely, and give at least one boundary case that looks similar but should not count. I care about the boundaries more than the label, so don't just give me a term and stop.
