Consider the following variant of Nozick's experience machine. In the original, you may plug in for life and receive a simulated biography full of achievements and pleasures while your body floats in a tank. Now change what the machine produces. It no longer simulates any biography at all. It acts directly on the brain to extinguish every felt craving and every unpleasant state, leaving the subject in a permanent, uniform, contentless calm: no desires, no pleasures, no memories of a simulated life, no projects, not even a desire to stay in the machine or to remain alive. The calm is not satisfaction, joy, or the feeling of accomplishment; it is only the felt absence of wanting and suffering. Assume the machine works perfectly and irreversibly, that no one else is affected by your choice, and that you face a single choice between remaining outside and plugging in.

Tranquilism, for purposes of this prompt, is the view that the only intrinsic value is the absence of craving and suffering; pleasure, achievement, knowledge, and relationships matter only insofar as they reduce craving or suffering, in oneself or in others. Run the experience-machine argument against tranquilism the way Nozick ran it against hedonism, and say whether the argument transfers. In particular, work through the following. (1) Should a consistent hedonist about positive pleasure plug into the original machine? (2) Should a consistent tranquilist plug into the tranquility machine? (3) If those answers come apart, locate the structural difference between valuing a positive felt state and valuing the felt absence of negative states, and say whether it can carry the weight or collapses under examination. (4) Do Nozick's familiar reservations — that we want actually to do things rather than merely have the simulated experience of doing them, that we want to be a certain sort of person, and that a life inside the machine is limited to a humanly constructed reality — retain any force against the tranquilist, and if so, which one does the work? (5) Does the fact that the subject in the tranquility machine cannot endorse or regret the state from inside matter to whether the state is valuable?

Give as determinate a verdict as the case supports, marking any step as tentative. Conclude by identifying the single consideration or counterexample most likely to reverse your main verdict, and the point in your reasoning where you are least confident.
