I've been thinking about the units of selection debate on my own, mostly from popular science reading, and I'd like you to help me work through a concrete version of it. Before ranking, state in one sentence the criterion you are using for what makes something a unit of selection. Then rank the following candidates by how confident you would be in treating each as a unit of selection — that is, as a thing whose differential survival and reproduction can, by itself, drive evolutionary change — from strongest to weakest. For each candidate, give one sentence explaining why it lands where it does.

The candidates are: a single gene; an individual organism; a kin group (a set of related organisms whose members affect one another's survival and reproduction); a eusocial insect colony; a transposable element (a DNA sequence that can copy itself within a genome); a cancer cell lineage within a patient; a holobiont (a host organism together with its resident microbes, treated as one evolving unit); a viral quasispecies (a cloud of closely related viral variants within a single host that evolves as a population); a whole biological species, treated as a unit in macroevolution; and a plasmid.

After the ranking, say which candidate you found the hardest borderline case, and explain what fact about the world would most change your mind about where it belongs.
