Write a deadpan internal policy memo, around 650 words, from the "Office of Settled Findings" at a mid-sized policy institute that has quietly adopted the dogmatism paradox as its evidence-handling policy. For background if you need it: the paradox is roughly this—once you genuinely know something, any piece of evidence that seems to count against it must be misleading, since if it weren't misleading you wouldn't have known the thing in the first place—so knowledge appears to give you a permanent license to dismiss future counterevidence. The memo should announce, as a routine housekeeping update, that any finding that has passed review and been stamped "known" is no longer open to counterevidence; material that conflicts with a known finding gets logged under "misleading evidence" without being read. Include a short FAQ for staff: What if the new evidence looks really rigorous? What if I suspect the original review made an error? How do I open an appeal? The answers should make it quietly impossible for any appeal to ever get off the ground, because in order to challenge a known finding you would first have to stop knowing it—but the policy defines knowing as a one-way status, not a revisable one. Close with a cheerful note about how much staff time this will save and how much more confidently everyone can now act. The humor has to come from getting the epistemology right, so a formal epistemologist laughs rather than winces: don't just write the office as stubborn or arrogant, make every clause follow from the view's own logic, and make the trap legible without ever naming it. Around 650 words.
