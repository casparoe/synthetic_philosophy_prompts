Take the classic split-brain confabulation setup: the word walk is shown only to the right hemisphere, the patient gets up, and when the left hemisphere is asked why, it says something like it was thirsty or needed to stretch. The usual reading is that the left hemisphere's interpreter makes up a story because it can't access the real cause of the action.

Now flip the asymmetry. Suppose you show a command only to the left hemisphere, and then ask the right hemisphere—which also didn't see the command—to point to a card from a set of likely motives: thirst, boredom, an instruction from the experimenter, a guess, I don't know. Does the standard analysis predict that the right hemisphere will confabulate in its own nonverbal channel? If it does, does the interpreter account become less about language and more about any reporting system that has to explain causes it can't see? If it doesn't, is that because the right hemsiphere lacks an interpreter, or just because the card setup makes an I-don't-know answer available in a way the standard verbal question doesn't? And would either outcome move the one-person-versus-two-person debate about split-brain consciousness?
