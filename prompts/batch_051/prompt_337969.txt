I am preparing a single fifty-minute class for second-year philosophy undergraduates, and I want it to deliver one idea: the difference between Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts of the colour-phi phenomenon. I am not asking you to explain the distinction to me; I am asking you to plan how I should explain it.

The class will be built around this demonstration. A red disk is flashed briefly on one side of a screen, then after a short interval a green disk is flashed briefly on the other side. With suitable timing, observers report a single disk moving across the gap and changing colour near the midpoint, and some report having seen the disk turn green before the second flash was physically presented. The two families of account are often labelled Orwellian and Stalinesque: roughly, that an initial experience is first produced and afterwards revised in memory, or that the experience itself is edited before it is allowed to become conscious.

Set out the sequence of the class, with attention to the following.

First, what has to be in place before the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction can even be posed, and what depends on what within that preparatory sequence. One thing I suspect must come early is the distinction between the time at which a stimulus occurs and the time at which it is consciously represented, together with the point that neural processing time alone does not decide between an account that revises the record afterwards and one that edits experience beforehand.

Second, what the carrying demonstration must have and what would spoil it.

Third, the misconceptions I should expect and the order in which they will come up. I am particularly worried about three: that the subject is merely confabulating; that the two accounts collapse into a choice between delaying early and delaying late and therefore cannot genuinely differ; and that the case shows introspection is globally unreliable. Tell me at which point in the sequence each should be headed off.

Fourth, what I should deliberately set aside at this level and say so.

Fifth, one closing question or exercise that would reveal whether the class has grasped the difference between altering the record of an experience after it has occurred and altering the experience before it is laid down in the record.

Please write your answer in British English. End with a last line of the form Summary: followed by a one-sentence summary of the central idea the class should leave with.
