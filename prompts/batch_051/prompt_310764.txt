I'm giving a talk next month defending the claim that the seperateness of persons is a much weaker objection to utilitarianism than people make it out to be. The line I'm pushing: pressed hard, the objection either proves too much (it would make ordinary prudential trade-offs across time just as objectionable, since the cost and the benefit fall on different temporal parts of a single life) or it collapses into a separate objection about consent and rights; so the real work against utilitarianism is being done elsewhere, and the bare fact that there are distinct persons doesn't do what people think it does. The audience is a philosophy department workshop: three contractualists who treat the point about aggregating small harms to many as the decisive argument, two Kantians who think the deeper issue is respect for persons as ends, and a welfare economist who suspects the whole debate is mostly confusion about interpersonal comparability. I can already see the first two objections coming, but I want the actual prep sheet: what they'll say, in their vocabulary, ordered by how likely and how damaging it is, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

Give me the prep sheet as a JSON object with this schema:
- "briefing": one paragraph on the two or three themes the Q&A will actually target, and the overall strategy for the room.
- "objections": an array ordered by likelihood-times-damage, most dangerous first, each with:
  - "order": number
  - "objection": the objection phrased the way this audience member would actually say it
  - "raiser": which camp (contractualist, Kantian, economist)
  - "likelihood": "high" / "medium" / "low"
  - "damage": "high" / "medium" / "low"
  - "concede_or_hold": what to grant vs. what to refuse to give up
  - "reply": a two-minute reply, the thing that has to land
  - "weakest_point": where that reply is soft and what would pressure it
- "hoped_nobody_asks": an object with "question", "why_damaging", and "prepared_response".
