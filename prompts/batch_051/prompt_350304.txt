The culture-industry critique associated with Adorno and Horkheimer argues that mass-produced entertainment standardizes culture: apparent differences among products are mostly cosmetic variations on interchangeable formulas, and this standardization habituates audiences to passive, administered consumption. Now consider a development that seems to break the standardization premise. Streaming services and recommendation systems can produce fine-grained personalization, adapting playlists, thumbnails, and even versions of films or songs to individual users, and sorting audiences into micro-genres so narrow that no two users encounter quite the same product. Does the culture-industry argument survive this development? Which specific premise does algorithmic personalization put under pressure, and can the argument be repaired in a weaker form, for instance by relocating the problem from standardization to the commercial prediction and shaping of taste? Or does the original argument simply become inapplicable to this environment? I am asking what happens to the argument itself, not what the platform should do.
