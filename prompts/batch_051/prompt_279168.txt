I keep going in circles about weakness of will versus hyperbolic discounting. Say someone plans to wait for a larger later reward, then caves and takes a smaller reward now. One reading: they're akratic—they still judge the larger reward as better and are failing to act on that judgment. The other reading: their evaluative judgment itself changed as the temptation got close, exactly like hyperbolic discounting predicts, so at the moment of choice the smaller-sooner reward genuinely looks better to them. Those feel like different psychological claims, but I'm not sure they make different predictions. Design an experiment (or a set of measures) that could seperate them: what would you measure at the moment of temptation, what pattern would count as evidence for a stable judgment plus a failure of self-control versus a genuine shift in the evaluation, and what would even a clean result still leave open? Is there any result that would show the akrasia/hyperbolic distinction is more than two words for the same discount curve?
