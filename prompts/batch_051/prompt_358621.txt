I'm on the compensation committee of a mid-sized co-op, and we have a problem. We agreed years ago that pay and promotions should track merit, not seniority, social background, or office politics. We also agreed that merit isn't just results: someone who got lucky, inherited a great account, or was handed easy territory shouldn't be credited as if they deserved it. But we've got to actually run this every year for about 85 people, and most of us have no philosophy background. Turn the desert-based idea of merit into a reusable procedure for us: a checklist or ordered set of questions to run through for each person, that seperates desert from brute luck, effort from talent, and contribution from windfall. Work through three cases: one person who hit their target because of a freak shift in the market, one who missed their target because the market collapsed, and one whose numbers were great but largely because they inherited a client book. Flag the points where judgment cannot be removed, and say honestly what the procedure will not catch. Answer in British English.
