I am commissioning a special issue on consciousness studies, and I want the centerpiece to be a document that could plausibly have been co-signed by two contributors who have been arguing past each other for years. One works in the global workspace tradition and holds that consciousness in the access sense is the core phenomenon, with reportability, broadcast to frontal-parietal systems, and availability for reasoning and control as its defining features; on this view, global workspace theory gives us the architecture of consciousness itself, and purported phenomenal residue beyond access is either pre-conscious processing or a verbal shadow. The other defends a phenomenal-first, phenomenal-overflow position: what-it-is-like is the datum to be explained, it outruns cognitive access, and global workspace theory at best captures the machinery of report, not the qualitative character that overflows it. I want the joint statement a good adversarial collaboration between these two would produce, not a victory for either side.

Please draft the statement as follows. First, a numbered agreed-claims section containing claims both sides can sign: claims about the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness itself, about what the partial-report (Sperling-style), change blindness, inattentional blindness, and no-report paradigm literatures show operationally, and about what global workspace theory is and is not committed to. Second, a disagreements table with these columns: Issue; Access-first wording; Phenomenal-first wording; Substantive or verbal?; and Crux it bears on. Each residual disagreement must be stated twice, once in wording the access-first side would accept as its own and once in wording the phenomenal-first side would accept, with no strawmanning of either. Third, a cruxes table with these columns: Crux; Case or experiment both accept as bearing on it; Access-first prediction; Phenomenal-first prediction; and What would be learned. For each crux the predictions must be recorded in advance, so that a reader can see what each side expects and what would move at least one of them.

I suspect the cruxes will include issues like whether the unreportable items in partial-report paradigms are consciously seen but inaccessible or are held in a high-capacity pre-conscious buffer, and whether removing report in no-report paradigms removes the phenomenon under study or merely removes one assay of it, but do not feel bound by my guesses. Describe the paradigms operationally rather than citing papers. After the tables, append a JSON object with the same content, using these fields: agreed_claims (array of strings), disagreements (array of objects with keys issue, access_first_wording, phenomenal_first_wording, substantive_or_verbal, and crux_it_bears_on), cruxes (array of objects with keys crux, case_or_experiment, access_first_prediction, phenomenal_first_prediction, and what_would_be_learned), and verbal_disputes (array of strings naming places where the two camps were using one term in different senses).
