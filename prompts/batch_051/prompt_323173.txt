You are a philosopher of science who works on the methodology of long-run predictions and on the history of political economy. Think through the problem carefully, and do not settle on the first interpretation that occurs to you.

I am a philosophy professor preparing a seminar on whether Malthus's account of population has been refuted by the subsequent historical record. In a planning meeting, an economist colleague waved away every historical counterexample with the same line: "Malthus was right — he was just early." The work the phrase was doing was clear enough in context: he conceded that the subsistence crises Malthus led his readers to expect did not arrive on Malthus's schedule, but he insisted that nothing in the last two centuries actually counts against the underlying theory.

I want you to disambiguate that slogan. What distinct precise claims could "Malthus was right, he was just early" be used to assert? Begin by brainstorming as many readings as you can, from the harmless to the radical, and then lay them out as a menu. For each reading, state exactly what the claim is, what would count as evidence against it, and whether it is trivially true, substantively true but in need of support, or false. Make sure your menu includes at least the following families of reading, and add any further ones you think are genuinely distinct: a claim about literal growth ratios for population and food; a claim about a persistent tendency of population to press against available resources; a claim about ultimate physical limits on a finite planet; a claim that Malthus's positive and preventive checks operate but through channels he would not have recognized; a claim that Malthus correctly described stagnant-technology or pre-industrial regimes only; and a methodological claim about when a long-run prediction may be postponed rather than treated as refuted.

Then take the further step I actually care about. When the slogan is used as my colleague used it, what kind of move is it? I am not asking for a neutral survey: after laying out the readings, commit firmly. Tell me which one or two readings you think are doing the real work in that dialectical context, and give your definite verdict on whether, on its best reading, the slogan rescues Malthus's theory, leaves it falsified, or renders it unfalsifiable. Give your reasons, and say what evidence you would accept as finally counting against the theory on that best reading.
