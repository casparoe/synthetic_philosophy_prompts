I am a novelist doing background reading on early twentieth-century philosophy of mathematics for a book built around a rivalry between two mathematicians, and I recently came across a synopsis that summarizes the period like this:

'In the early twentieth century, three schools fought over the foundations of mathematics. Logicism, associated with Frege and Russell, held that mathematics is nothing but logic, so every mathematical truth is a logical truth. Formalism, associated with Hilbert, held that mathematics is best understood as the manipulation of symbols according to explicit rules, so the question of mathematical truth reduces to the question of what is derivable in a consistent system. Intuitionism, associated with Brouwer, held that mathematics is a product of the human mind, and therefore rejects the law of excluded middle across the board because truth is identified with proof.'

I suspect the summary is mostly right but compressed in a way that may distort at least one of the schools, especially the formalism section; I want to know how much I can safely have a character endorse without making her sound like she is reciting a caricature. Please go through the summary claim by claim and tell me what a careful philosopher of mathematics would accept, what is a helpful simplification, and what is misleading or wrong, and say whether the three schools are properly understood as rivals answering one shared question. Please write your answer in American English.
