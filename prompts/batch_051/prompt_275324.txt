I run a small monthly reading group that pairs short fiction with issues in the philosophy of mind, and our next story is Borges's 'Funes the Memorious.' I chose it because it seems to put pressure on something the literature on concepts says, but I do not want the discussion to settle for flattening the story into a neat illustration of a theory. Read the story through the modern debate among prototype, exemplar, and theory-theory accounts of what concepts are and how they work. After his fall, Funes can no longer forget or coarse-grain his experience; each leaf, each glimpse of an animal, each perceptual instant stays distinct, and he is disturbed that a dog seen in profile and the same dog seen from the front should fall under one name. He even builds a private numeral system in which every number has a unique proper name rather than a reusable position in a series. The narrator's diagnosis is that this total recall destroys thought, since abstraction depends on refusing to let every particular weigh equally and on letting one instance stand for another. I want a reading that is faithful in both directions.

I am especially interested in this question: is Funes best read as a limit case of an exemplar-based mind, a memory storing maximally rich traces with no summary representation at all? If so, why does his mind fail at precisely the thing exemplar models are built to handle, namely grouping new instances by their similarity to stored ones — and does that failure reveal that even similarity judgments require an abstraction the models take for granted, some weighting of which respects matter and which do not? Does a prototype account have a cleaner diagnosis, that Funes lacks the smoothed typical structure that lets people ignore individual detail, while a theory-theory reading locates the deficit deeper, in the loss of the causal-explanatory kind knowledge that makes two appearances count as one enduring thing? I would also like to know where the story quietly imports assumptions the empirical theories would contest, and where Borges may be pointing at something the debate underplays — perhaps that conceptual thought just is organized forgetting. What does the figure of Funes add to the quarrel among prototype, exemplar, and theory-theory views that those views cannot already say on their own?
