I'm a software engineer TAing for an intro class that's mostly AI but touches some philosophy of mind. One of the readings was Sutton and Barto's Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (second edition), where they say the reward signal communicates what you want the agent to achieve rather than how to achieve it. The exam question was:

"Using that reward-signal point, evaluate this claim: if a robot is trained with positive reward for keeping its battery charged and negative reward for low battery, then the robot can literally suffer from battery anxiety. Answer yes/no and defend."

A student wrote:

"Sutton and Barto are clear that the reward signal is not a subjective feeling; it's just a scalar number the environment gives to tell the agent if a state is good or bad. Since suffering requires consciousness, a robot with low-battery negative reward isn't literally suffering. But anxiety is just the disposition to predict and avoid negative states, so if the agent has a learned internal model that predicts low battery and triggers avoidance, that is functionally the same as anxiety. The negative reward prediction error it gets when it expects high battery and gets low battery is exactly what valence is in humans. So I'd say the robot doesn't literally suffer but it does have battery anxiety in a functional sense."

I need a grade out of 10 and point-by-point comments, because I have to defend it to the student. I don't have philosophy background, so I'd like to know wether the answer is actually mixing something up or whether it's fine.
