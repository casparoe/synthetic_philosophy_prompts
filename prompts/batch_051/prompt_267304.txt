Here are the criteria I work with for deciding when a state may legitimately apply its speech restrictions to online expression that originates or is hosted abroad: (i) fair targeting — the speaker could reasonably foresee the expression as aimed at the state's residents rather than at a global audience; (ii) substantial effects — the alleged local harm is serious and suitably connected to the state; (iii) feasibility without collateral censorship — the order can be complied with by narrower means such as geoblocking rather than forcing the platform to adopt the strictest applicable speech code as its global default; and (iv) comity — the order does not require the platform to violate or abandon strong protections in its home state, and can realistically be enforced. Now consider this case. A small video platform incorporated in State A, where its content is lawful and strongly protected by A's constitution, carries a short film denying a historical atrocity. The platform does not target State B and has no staff, servers, or assets there. State B criminalizes public denial of that atrocity. A court in B finds that significant numbers of B residents have accessed the film and orders the platform to make it inaccessible to users in B and to pay a civil penalty, enforceable only if A's courts recognize the judgment. The platform can geoblock B at low cost, though the film remains available there through VPNs and screenshots circulated in encrypted groups. Does State B's assertion of jurisdiction qualify under my criteria? Go criterion by criterion; say where the criteria underdetermine the answer; and say whether the case shows that any criterion needs to be revised — for example, whether feasibility should be read in light of widespread circumvention tools, or whether a platform's failure to target the state should matter less than the audience's actual location. Throughout, distinguish what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on jurisdiction and online speech, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
