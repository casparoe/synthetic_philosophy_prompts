In a recent discussion about whether a just God could condemn a sincere Muslim or someone who never heard the gospel, a participant ended the argument by invoking the old maxim Extra ecclesiam nulla salus — outside the Church there is no salvation — as though it settled the question against every form of religious pluralism. I suspect the maxim can be made to assert several quite different things. It might be read as a claim about where salvation is ordinarily available, as a claim about the visible boundaries of the Church considered as an institution, as a claim about who is in fact saved, as a claim about culpability and ignorance, or as a sweeping claim that non-membership is always and everywhere damning; these readings differ in scope and severity. I would like a careful disambiguation rather than a single interpretation. Lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could be used to assert, from the most minimal or platitudinous to the most radical, and for each reading say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or implausible or false, and whether it would actually support using the slogan to dismiss pluralism as this participant did. Then identify which readings are expressions of exclusivism, which are compatible with inclusivism, and which, if any, a pluralist could accept; and explain what further premises, beyond the slogan itself, would be needed for the maxim to rule out pluralism on its own.
