In my upper-division course on free will, we have been discussing sphexishness: the pattern of mechanical rigidity, named after the Sphex wasp, in which an organism carries out a complex sequence of actions successfully while conditions remain exactly as expected, but then repeats a fixed sub-routine pointlessly if some small feature of the situation is perturbed. On one interpretation of the example, the wasp’s failure is not a lack of intelligence but a lack of the kind of flexibility that matters for free agency: the wasp cannot step back, notice that the situation has changed, and revise its routine accordingly.

For my creative final, I need a short story of around 800 words that dramatizes this distinction between sphexish rigidity and genuinely flexible, self-correcting agency, and I would like you to write the story for me. The story should transpose the idea into a fresh setting rather than simply retelling the wasp example; possible settings might include an office, a household, a bureaucracy, an autonomous system, or an imaginary creature. A reader who knows the philosophical background should be able to recognize that the story is about sphexishness, but the story should not lecture or define the term. It should make the reader feel why the difference between running through a complex but brittle routine and actually adjusting to novelty matters for freedom. Please ensure that the central case is not merely ordinary habit, compulsion, or laziness: it should involve behavior that looks competent and purposeful, yet collapses into rigid repetition the moment the environment withholds its usual cues. It would be especially good if the story contains a moment in which a character either achieves or conspicuously fails to achieve the step-back-and-revise move that sphexishness rules out.
