i’ve been trying to figure out whether Mill and Bentham actually disagree about pleasure, or whether Mill is just sneaking in a qualitative dimension that Bentham could have accepted. In chapter 4 of An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation, Bentham says:

“To a person considered by himself, the value of a pleasure or pain considered by itself, will be greater or less, according to the four following circumstances:
- Its intensity.
- Its duration.
- Its certainty or uncertainty.
- Its propinquity or remoteness.”

In chapter 2 of Utilitarianism, Mill says:

“It is quite compatible with the principle of utility to recognise the fact, that some kinds of pleasure are more desirable and more valuable than others.”

These seem to be in tension: Bentham treats pleasures as measurable along a single quantitative scale, while Mill says a pleasure’s kind can make it more valuable. But maybe they can be reconciled if Mill’s “more valuable” just means greater in one of Bentham’s dimensions, or if Bentham’s list wasn’t meant to be exhaustive.

Do these two passages actually conflict? If they do conflict, is it a disagreement inside hedonism, or is Mill drifting toward a non-hedonist view of value? And if they can be reconciled, what exactly is the reconciliation and what price does it exact?
