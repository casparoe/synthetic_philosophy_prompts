So I cover science and ideas for a living, and lately I keep getting stuck on a thought experiment about two dead pragmatists. Peirce thought truth is where a community of inquirers would eventually end up if they kept investigating long enough, were honest about it, and didn't get derailed. James was more loosey-goosey: whatever works, whatever has cash value in experience, that's what we're entitiled to call true. Suppose we drop both of them into a meeting at a big social media company in 2025, where a product manager is explaining that they A/B test everything—headlines, notifications, even whether a post gets flagged—and that whatever maximizes engagement wins. The PM calls this "letting the data decide what's true for our users." Walk me through, in as much detail as you can, what you think Peirce would actually say to that room. Where exactly would he locate the mistake, and would any part of him see A/B testing as a debased cousin of his own convergence idea? And if you want, tell me whether James would secretly nod along or whether he'd be just as appalled.
