I'm trying to get the finks/masks distinction straight for a paper I'm writing, and I keep getting hung up on one thing. As I understand it, both are counterexamples to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions. A fink makes the disposition vanish when the stimulus shows up, so the object doesn't manifest. A mask, as far as I can tell, stops the stimulus from showing up in the first place. But if the stimulus never occurs, then the counterfactual 'if it were stimulated, it would manifest' never gets tested—the antecedent just isnt met. So why are masked dispositions treated as a separate problem for the conditional analysis, rather than cases where the analysis is simply silent? Have I got the definition of a mask wrong, or is there something deeper going on?
