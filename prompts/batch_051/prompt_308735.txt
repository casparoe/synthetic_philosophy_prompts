I am a novelist, and while researching the predictive processing and free energy principle literature for a novel about a woman who can no longer tell whether her perceptions are her own, I have noticed that a dispute in that literature has persisted for years without much movement. One side writes as if the free energy principle is an empirical discovery about how biological systems must work; the other side insists that, as usually stated, it is close to tautological or unfalsifiable, because any system that persists can always be redescribed, with enough ingenuity, as minimizing free energy under some generative model. Both sides appear to understand the mathematics, so it is hard for an outsider to tell where the disagreement lives. My working hypothesis is that the real dispute is not about the equations or the neurobiology but about what a scientific explanation has to do, and specifically about whether a principle that applies to anything self-sustaining can explain the particular architecture of the brain. I would like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict for one side. Is it a substantive empirical question in disguise, a verbal dispute over terms such as model, principle, or explanation, two groups answering different questions under one heading, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? Please be explicit throughout about which parts of the free energy formalism are technically settled and which parts are interpretive or contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
