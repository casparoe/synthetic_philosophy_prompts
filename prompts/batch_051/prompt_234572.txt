I'm putting together a short presentation for my philosophy of religion reading group on Boethius, Book Five of the *Consolation*, and I've realised my own position may be a bit of a patchwork. I want an audit before I go in. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I think Boethius's basic move is right: divine foreknowledge isn't really fore-knowledge because God is outside time, and his knowledge of future contingents is more like an eternal present to which all times are simultaneous. So the problem of foreknowledge is really a problem about what an atemporal knower would be like, not about infallible prediction.

(2) I'm a libertarian about free will: a free action must involve an ability to do otherwise, holding fixed the past and the natural laws (or at least the relevant causal history).

(3) I accept bivalence for future contingents: for every future-tensed proposition about a free human act, it is already either true or false, even if nobody knows which.

(4) I also think God's knowledge is infallible in the strongest sense: if God knows that I will decide on Tuesday to skip the meeting, then it is not possible that I decide otherwise.

(5) I accept the fixity of the past: what has already happened or already been true at a past time is now necessary in a way that makes it out of my control.

(6) However, I think Boethius is right that merely being known by God doesn't add a new kind of necessity to future events; the alleged conflict comes from confusing the certainty of the knower with the necessity of the thing known.

(7) Finally, I think the timelessness of God is independently motivated by perfect being theology: a perfect being can't be subject to temporal succession because that would be a form of dependence or potentiality.

I don't want a generic 'Boethius solved it' or 'Boethius failed'. I want you to go through these commitments pairwise, flag which are in genuine tension, which only look in tension, and rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single change to (1)–(7) would make the whole set hang together best. Please answer in British English.
