I've been trying to articulate my views on what a self is, and I would appreciate a consistency audit before I have to defend them. I am drawn to the broadly Hofstadterian picture on which a self is not a fixed organ or a soul but a pattern — an abstract, multiply realizable structure of perceiving, remembering, and deciding — and on which such a pattern can be copied, blurred, and spread across more than one head. Here is the set of claims I currently want to stand by:

(1) The self just is that kind of abstract pattern of mental activity, not any special substance or particular chunk of matter.
(2) Because it is a pattern, it could in principle be realized in a different substrate, duplicated, or run in parallel.
(3) Sameness of self is a matter of degree: a future person, or indeed another person now, can be more or less me rather than simply me or not-me.
(4) Yet the unity of my consciousness is real rather than merely apparent: there is exactly one stream of experience that is this one, and from the inside its boundaries feel sharp.
(5) In actual life, self-boundaries are fuzzy. People close to me have shaped my perceptions, memories, and reactions so deeply that parts of their patterns are now functionally parts of mine, and vice versa.
(6) This is why I suspect that when I die, fragments of my pattern will continue in other people — not merely as memories about me, but as working fragments still doing some of the cognitive work that was me — and that this is a literal, not a metaphorical, continuation of me.
(7) But if an exact copy of my brain-state were made tomorrow, I do not think I would wake up in two places: there would be two separate streams of consciousness, though I ought to regard the copy's welfare as my own.

My difficulty is that I cannot tell where the package strains or, more importantly, where it actually breaks. I want you to go through the commitments and distinguish the pairs that are genuinely in tension from the pairs that only appear to be, order the genuine tensions by severity, say what would have to give for each and what the cheapest repair would be, and then tell me which single revision would do the most overall work. If a pair only looks inconsistent because two senses of a term are being run together, please name the distinction rather than flagging a tension.
