Here is my opening. Your view allows the higher-order representation to misrepresent its target — indeed, it seems committed to the possibility of a higher-order state that fires while the first-order state it purports to be about is absent. In such a case you would be aware of yourself as having, say, a vivid visual experience of a red patch, even though no corresponding red-perceiving state occurred. But then the first-order state appears to be doing no explanatory work: the conscious episode is wholly accounted for by the higher-order state alone. Why is that not a reductio of the claim that being the target of a higher-order state is what makes a state conscious?
