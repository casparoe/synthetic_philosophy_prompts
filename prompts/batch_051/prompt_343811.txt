I came across the MU-puzzle a while ago and have been turning it over in my head ever since. The setup, as I understand it, is this. You begin with the string MI and try to reach MU using four rules: (1) if a string ends in I, you may append a U; (2) if a string begins with M, you may double whatever comes after the M; (3) if III occurs anywhere, you may replace that III with a single U; and (4) if UU occurs anywhere, you may delete that UU. The standard solution jumps out of the system rather than grinding through moves inside it: count the I's. MI begins with one I. Rules 1 and 4 leave the I-count alone; rule 2 doubles it; and rule 3 removes three I's. Since doubling a non-multiple of 3 gives another non-multiple of 3, and removing three I's leaves divisibility by 3 untouched, no reachable string has a number of I's divisible by 3. MU has zero I's, so it cannot be reached.

Now modify one thing. Suppose rule 3 says instead that any occurrence of five consecutive I's may be replaced by a single U, with the other three rules left exactly as they are.

Does the old divisibility-by-3 argument still work in this modified game? If it breaks, don't just say so: exhibit an actual sequence of moves that reaches a string with a number of I's divisible by 3, so I can see exactly where the argument gives out. Then take up the real question: is MU reachable now? If it is, give the derivation; if it isn't, give the invariant or outside-the-system argument that shows it can't be reached. Finally, step back and tell me whether the jump out of the system you use here is the same jump as in the original puzzle, with the same kind of justification, or a different move wearing the old one's clothes. The underlying question I'm after is: when 3 becomes 5, what is preserved in the JOOTSing idea and what is given up?

Throughout, please keep two registers separate. The derivations and invariants in this string game are settled: they're just arithmetic, and should be presented as forced. The lesson about jumping out of systems—what it shows, how far it generalizes, and what it should make me believe about reasoning in general—is the contested part. If you take a particular reading of that lesson, flag it as your reading rather than presenting it as what everyone who thinks about this agrees on.
