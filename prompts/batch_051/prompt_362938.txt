In Book I, Chapter 6 of The Social Contract, Rousseau describes the social compact as one in which each associate places his person and all his power in common under the supreme direction of the general will, and in return is received as an indivisible part of the whole. In Book I, Chapter 7, he says that a person who refuses to obey the general will may be compelled by the whole body, and he calls this being forced to be free. Treat that two-part description as a set of criteria for being bound by the general will. Consider a borderline case: a permanent resident who has lived in a state for decades, pays taxes, obeys its laws, and relies on its protection, but is excluded from voting and from membership in the sovereign body because the state reserves citizenship for a narrower class. Is this person bound by the general will, and may the state legitimately compel him to obey laws he had no part in forming? Go through the criteria, note where they underdetermine the result, and say whether the case shows that Rousseau's account of being forced to be free needs amendment.
