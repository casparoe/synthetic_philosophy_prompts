I am a postgraduate tutor running a one-off fifty-minute seminar for second-year economics undergraduates on the socialist calculation debate. The students can work with basic supply and demand but have not read the primary texts, and they will arrive thinking the debate is only about whether central planners are smart enough or have enough computational power. Plan how I should teach the distinction between Mises’s calculation argument and Hayek’s knowledge argument, and why Lange’s market socialism is usually taken to answer the first more successfully than the second. I need the explanatory arc: the components that must be in place before the distinction can be drawn and what depends on what; the single running example that should carry the session and the features it needs and must avoid; the misconceptions to expect—at minimum, that prices are only a unit of account, that Hayek is merely restating Mises in milder language, that the difference between them is one of degree of information rather than kind, and that Lange’s trial-and-error procedure is being proposed as an institutional blueprint—and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off, with how much time each deserves; a final question that would show whether students can hold the two arguments apart rather than just reporting the standard contrast; and what I should explicitly leave out at this level and how to mark it as omitted.
