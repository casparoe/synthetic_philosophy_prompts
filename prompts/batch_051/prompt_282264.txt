I am assembling a small pamphlet of satirical bureaucratic documents for a course on distributive justice, and I would like a piece about luck egalitarianism. As I understand the view, it holds that inequalities are unjust when they flow from brute luck — unchosen circumstance such as a congenital disability or a tree falling on one's house — but may be just when they flow from option luck, the outcome of a deliberate gamble such as taking an insecure job, declining insurance, or sinking one's savings into a start-up. The satire should come from inside the position: write as a sincere and intelligent administrator who genuinely believes that sorting misfortune into brute luck and option luck is a moral improvement, and who is simply applying the distinction with a clean conscience. The comedy should be produced by the remorseless consistency of the application, not by making the administrator a cynic or a buffoon.

Please write a Notice of Determination letter from a fictional public body — call it the Bureau of Compensatory Justice — to a claimant whose application for compensation for a serious shortfall has just been denied. The letter should subject the claimant's situation to an exacting audit that assigns each relevant fact to brute luck or option luck, and should carry the logic far enough that a reader begins to recoil: for example, by treating unchosen features of temperament, upbringing, or the options one happened to face as if they were ordinary gambles. Keep the register deadpan officialese, and do not wink or break character. The target should be the administrative discourse that grows up around the brute/option distinction, more than the bare idea that choices can affect entitlement, and a specialist in the literature should find the parody fair rather than a strawman. Write the piece as exactly five numbered sections, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, around 150 to 250 words in total.
