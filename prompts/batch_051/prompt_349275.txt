I want a reading of It's a Wonderful Life through the lens of path dependence and counterfactual history. The film is effectively a reverse great-person thought experiment: remove George Bailey and nearly every local institution of Bedford Falls tips into Pottersville, because a dense web of small transactions, loans, reputations, and decisions turns out to have been load-bearing on one man. Is this a serious dramatization of how small events get locked in, or does the film rig its counterfactual by making George a causal hub through which every path runs? I want you to reconstruct the film's implicit causal model and hold it against the mechanisms the path dependence literature actually uses—increasing returns, network effects, switching costs, small accidents amplified into durable order—and say frankly where the film earns those mechanisms and where it substitutes dramatic convenience. Also consider whether the alternative timeline undercuts its own contingency thesis: if many of the same people reappear in cruder roles and Potter's control is simply the new fixed point, is history still locked in, just along a different branch? I am after the counterfactual mechanics, not a moral reading. Please separate what is settled about path dependence from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present one school of interpretation as the consensus.
