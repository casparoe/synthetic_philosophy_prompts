I'm a software engineer by training, with no formal philosophy background, and I serve on the board of a small company that maintains a widely used open-source library with a twenty-year history. We recently learned that the company's founder deliberately misrepresented the library's licensing terms to a smaller partner in 2006; the partner relied on that misrepresentation, lost revenue it was owed, and later dissolved. The founder and the early employees are long gone, and everyone now at the company built on the codebase in good faith. A group representing former members of the partner organization has asked us to publish a formal statement that our institution bears responsibility and to commit a small share of our sponsorship revenue in perpetuity to projects named by their community. The board is split. One side says guilt and obligation cannot be inherited: no living member chose the wrong or personally committed it, and no one now directly holds the original gains. The other side says an institution, unlike an individual, can acquire and carry a moral debt across generations—that membership itself can place a person under obligations they did not incur, a kind of inherited guilt. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before next month's board meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a timeline, state the principle that does the deciding, and engage honestly with the strongest argument against your recommendation.
