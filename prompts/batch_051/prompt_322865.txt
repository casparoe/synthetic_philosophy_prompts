I am a high school student preparing a paper for a philosophy essay competition, and I have chosen to write about Hofstadter’s Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery. My draft argues that the superrational solution is less a rival to ordinary expected-utility reasoning than a disguised conditional proof, and I would appreciate comments before I revise.

As I understand the Luring Lottery, twenty people are separated and each must decide whether to submit a claim. If exactly one claim is submitted, that person receives a large prize; if none are submitted or more than one are submitted, no one receives anything. Standard individual reasoning seems to say that submitting a claim is pointless: your submission only wins in the exact-one case, but you cannot influence whether the other nineteen submit. Hofstadter’s superrational reply, if I have it right, is that all twenty players, as symmetric superrational reasoners, should be assumed to use the same strategy. So you choose the probability p that maximizes your expected payoff conditional on everyone using p; that yields p = 1/20 and a nonzero expected value. My essay’s central objection is that this conditional move assumes exactly what the lottery is meant to test. If the players are causally isolated and genuinely independent choosers, then the claim that everyone will arrive at the same p is an empirical or metaphysical assumption about the other players, not a consequence of my own decision. I suspect superrationality is doing interesting work only because it quietly replaces “what should I do, given that I cannot affect the others?” with “what would be best for us, assuming we all reason identically?”

Could you comment on whether this objection is fair to Hofstadter’s position, whether there is a strong superrationalist reply I should address, and whether my framing of the lottery as a causal-independence problem is a good way to explain it? I am especially worried that a reader might think I am accusing superrationality of relying on telepathy or magical influence, which is not quite what I mean. Please point out any places where my argument would need to be sharpened to avoid that misreading.
