Ok so in my world history class we're doing the Islamic Golden Age and we read excerpts from Ibn Rushd's The Decisive Treatise (the one where he's basically arguing that religion and philosophy don't have to be enemies). Two things he says are driving me crazy because they seem like they can't both be true. Early on he says revealed law actually requires people who are capable of it to study what exists by demonstrative reasoning: philosophy isn't just allowed, it's kind of commanded, because scripture keeps telling people to reflect on creation. But later he says you absolutely should not share the conclusions of this demonstrative reasoning, especially allegorical interpretations of scripture, with the masses. He even divides people into three types, demonstrative, dialectical, and rhetorical, something like that, and says most people can only handle the plain surface meaning, and that putting the philosophers' interpretations out there for everyone just causes unbelief and sectarian fighting.

My friend says this is a flat contradiction: how can God comand philosophy and then forbid telling almost everyone what philosophy concludes? I feel like theres a way to read it as consistent, but maybe that's just me wanting the text to be smarter than it is. Can these two passages be reconciled? What are the best candidate readings, and which one is the least like special pleading? Also, if the best reading makes him consistent, does that make the whole religion-and-philosophy harmony thing kind of hollow for most people?
