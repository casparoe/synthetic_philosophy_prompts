I am preparing a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher of language who defends a scorekeeping account of presupposition. On her view, presuppositions are not first and foremost properties of sentences but requirements on the conversational score—a cluster of parameters that includes the common ground, standards of precision, and salient referents—and accommodation is the rule-governed process by which the score is adjusted when an utterance imposes a requirement the score does not already satisfy. Draft eight to ten interview questions that push beyond having her restate this framework. I want at least one question on each of the following pressure points, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: (i) whether the account is best understood as a semantic or a pragmatic theory of presupposition, or as a way of dissolving that distinction; (ii) the proviso problem, in which some conditionals with presuppositional consequents lead hearers to accommodate the unconditional presupposition rather than the weaker conditionalized one that projection rules would predict, and how she explains the asymmetry with conditionals where the weaker reading survives; (iii) local versus global accommodation, as in a conditional such as If Ana has children, her children attend the local school, where the presupposition triggered in the consequent may be satisfied within the antecedent rather than added to the common ground; (iv) informative presuppositions, where new information is introduced as if it were already taken for granted, and how the scoreboard can be updated by content that is not at issue; (v) the limits of accommodation, including why some presuppositional demands cannot be quietly absorbed and instead elicit an explicit repair challenge from the hearer; and (vi) how her view handles presuppositions triggered under embeddings other than conditionals, such as negation, questions, and modals. The questions should press where the literature presses: anticipate the reply the guest is most likely to give and build each follow-up around it, rather than inviting her merely to restate the position.
