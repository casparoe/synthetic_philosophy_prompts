Someone I know through the rationalist/EA world is about to run a small grants call — enough to fund two or three theory postdocs for maybe three years, definitely not an empirical ML shops — and they've scoped it to "low-impact AI": impact measures, avoiding side effects, agents that don't knock over everything outside their assigned task. They asked a few of us what the call should actually ask for, and I'm realizing I don't have a settled view on which question in that cluster is the one worth paying someone to sit with for three years. So I want a defended priority ordering, not a survey of the area.

Some candidate problems I keep flipping between (add or subtract freely): (a) whether "side effect" is even coherent without a prior notion of "intended effect," and whether low-impact AI is secretly a theory of intentions wearing a consequence-based costume; (b) the baseline problem — impact relative to what: no agent at all, a competent human doing the same task, the cheapest safe-ish alternative? Each baseline smuggles in a bit of value, but differently; (c) whether impact should be a penalty term in the objective or a hard constraint on the agent's option set, and whether that distinction collapses once you think about learning what the task actually is; (d) whether corrigibility/shutdown is just low-impact with the protected quantity being "the off switch keeps existing"; (e) whether the existing measures — attainable-utility/option-preservation style and relative-reachability/capacity style — are one idea with different distance metrics or genuinely different things, and how you'd tell from the behavior of an agent rather than from how the approaches are usually packaged; (f) the trajectory problem: an agent can be low-impact for a long time and then make its high-impact move from a prepared position, so what notion of "impact over a whole trajectory" solves that without already solving alignment. For each one you'd fund, I want to know why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for a philosophy-ish theory person rather than someone with a GPU budget, what two or three years of progress would concretely look like, and what evidence would knock it down your list. Also name the question a bad call would omit but shouldn't, and the one that sounds deep but is actually a trap that would burn two postdoc-years.
