I've realised I follow a fairly consistent policy in arguments about automating jobs, and I can't tell whether it's sensible or just a bias with a rule's costume on. When someone claims that a particular job is so meaningless, demeaning, or socially dead that automating it out of existence is basically a pure win for the people who do it, I check whether they've ever done the job. If they haven't, I treat their claim about the job's felt worth as low-grade evidence - no matter how many satisfaction surveys or turnover statistics they cite. If they have done it and say it had hidden goods to it, the small talk, the rhythm of a shift, a modest sense of being useful, I weight that heavily even when it's one voice against a pile of data. Last week this came up about supermarket checkout work. A friend insisted self-checkout was an obvious improvement because scanning items is repetitive and socially dead; he's never worked a till. I caught myself mostly discounting him and siding with a retired cashier in the comments who said she'd genuinely miss the rhythm and her regular customers. She offered no data at all.

To be fair to myself, I don't apply this to every claim. If an economist says automating the tills will cut wages, I'll believe them without them ever having scanned a barcode. The policy is only for claims about whether a job is meaningful or demeaning from the inside. But that's the part I want pressure-tested. Is the felt meaningfulness of work the kind of thing only insiders can report on, so that an outsider's studies can't easily overrule an insider's testimony? Or am I priviledging vivid first-person stories over base rates - trusting one decent anecdote over a hundred questionnaires? I can see a middle position where I keep the policy for what a job feels like from inside but drop it for whether the job should be automated; except I worry that middle collapses, because deciding whether to automate a job is partly deciding whose report of the job's value gets to count. So: is this policy defensible? If it is, where does it break down, can it be restated to keep the good parts without the costs, and am I entitled to it given that I can't go off and do every job before forming a view?
