In my world history class we just finished the British Raj, and my teacher made us read a defender like Niall Ferguson (railways, common law, a unified administration, english education) and a critic like Shashi Tharoor (famines, deindustrialization, extraction). I dont care who wins the argument right now. What I actually want is the thing underneath: the rules for keeping score. Our class discussion just turned into people throwing facts at each other, and nobody could explain what would even count as a point for either side.

So write me a list of requirements that a fair balance sheet of the British Raj (or any empire honestly) would have to meet before we trust its bottom line. For each requirement, give the case or example from the Raj that motivates it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable (if you drop them the whole balance sheet becomes worthless) and which are just preferences that one camp insists on. Some specific things I want you to deal with: do we score the Raj against a counterfactual India without the British, and how do you do that without just making stuff up? Do railways and law courts count as benefits if they were mostly built to extract wealth or control people? Does early suffering get weighted the same as later benefits, or is that secretly a moral choice? Can you put peace and order in the plus column if it was imposed and maintained by force? And is there a point where the requirements themselves start to conflict — like, being totally rigorous about the counterfactual might make the balance sheet impossible to actually compute, so maybe the list itself is a kind of argument? That last part is what I most want to see. Skip the actual verdict on Ferguson vs Tharoor; I'll do that part myself.
