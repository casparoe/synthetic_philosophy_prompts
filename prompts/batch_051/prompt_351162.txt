I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm writing a reported essay on the way arguments from nature keep resurfacing in debates over sex roles. To open one section, I'd like a short poem. Please write a villanelle in the standard 19-line form that works through the critique of the appeal to nature developed in John Stuart Mill's The Subjection of Women. Rather than name-dropping Mill, the poem should dramatize the argument: it should show the word nature doing double duty, describing what women supposedly are while quietly prescribing what they ought to be, and it should carry the point that we cannot read women's nature off existing arrangements, because custom and power have shaped those arrangements from the outset. Use the villanelle's two recurring refrains as the engine of the idea—let one refrain voice the appeal to nature and the other voice the objection that the untouched nature being invoked has never actually been observed—so that the two claims keep returning in unresolved tension instead of ending in a lesson. The tone should suit the essay: dry and ironic rather than sermonizing. The poem need not mention Mill or the book.
