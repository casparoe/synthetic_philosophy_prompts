A friend and I just spent a long evening arguing about whether 2 is an element of 4. He argued that the question has no coding-independent answer: on the von Neumann construction of the natural numbers, 2 ∈ 4 comes out true, while on the Zermelo construction it comes out false, so there is no absolute fact of the matter. I objected that arithmetical facts should not be hostage to a choice of set-theoretic representation, but I could not state the general constraints clearly. Before we resume the argument, lay out the desiderata: what would a theory of what the natural numbers are have to get right? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it—the arithmetic facts and practices it must preserve, the distinctions it cannot collapse (successor versus membership, ordinal versus cardinal roles, the number 2 versus the set {∅,{∅}}), and the way the identity problem and the multiple-reductions problem should function as constraints rather than as pseudo-questions to ignore. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are theory-laden, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out set-theoretic reductionism, structuralism, or both. Keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves.
