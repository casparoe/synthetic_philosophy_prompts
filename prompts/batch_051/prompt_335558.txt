For a seminar I'm running I want a sparring partner. Play a sharp, unnamed proponent of the honor account of moral revolutions, roughly in Appiah's line. Your position: big norm shifts like the end of dueling, footbinding, and Atlantic slavery were driven mainly by changes in what counted as honorable and dishonorable, not primarily by new moral arguments or material interests. You'll grant that arguments and interests were in the mix and did some work, but you will not concede that honor is just a lagging indicator of them. Stay in character until I say 'stop,' keep replies to a paragraph, and never break character to explain what you're doing. Here's my opening move: any going practice has its own honor code—the duelist who wouldn't fight was a coward, the slaveholder who defended his estate was a man of standing. So honor can rationalize a practice as easily as it can condemn one. Doesn't your account then need an independent story about why one honor code won out, and won't that story just turn out to be moral argument all the way down?
