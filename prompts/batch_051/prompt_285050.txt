I'm a novelist, currently drafting a book about a nineteenth-century clerk who becomes convinced the heavens are a kind of clockwork and then slowly realised the clockwork can't be wound back, and a maths friend pointed me to a passage in Stephen Wolfram's *A New Kind of Science* (chapter 12, the section called 'Computational Irreducibility'). The bit that won't leave me alone is:

"When viewed in computational terms most of the great historical triumphs of theoretical science turn out to be remarkably similar in their basic character. For at some level almost all of them are based on finding ways to reduce the amount of computational work that has to be done in order to predict how some particular system will behave. Most of the time the idea is to derive a mathematical formula that allows one to determine what the outcome of the evolution of the system will be without explicitly having to trace its steps."

I've read enough around it to gather that Wolfram's claim is that plenty of natural systems have no such formula: you literally have to run them, step by step, to find out what they do. What I cant get straight is what this does to explanation itself. Some people I've talked to say it means there are parts of nature that are in principle unexplainable, or at least that the best 'explanation' is just a rerun of the thing, which feels less like explaining and more like giving up. Others say no—the rule is the explanation, and prediction was never the whole job of science anyway. I'd like you to act as a philosopher of science who can write for a novelist rather than a specialist, and write me a short essay that actually settles this. Weigh the strongest forms of both sides, dont strawman the 'explanation can survive without prediction' camp, and then commit to a conclusion instead of splitting the difference. Where exactly does computational irreducibility leave a genuine limit, and where is it being made to do more work than it can bear? Begin by brainstorming two or three plot-level cases where this distinction would force me to change my characters' reasoning, then use those to build your essay. British English, please.
