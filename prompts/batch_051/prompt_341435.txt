I'm a novelist. The stuff I actually work with is point of view: close third, omniscient narration, free indirect discourse, interiority, the gap between what a narrator knows and what a reader is made to feel. A philosopher friend keeps going on about Mary's room — Mary is raised seeing only black and white, learns every physical fact about human color vision from books, then steps outside and sees red for the first time and (supposedly) learns something new; therefore the physical facts aren't everything. I keep thinking the whole argument is just a dramatization of a craft distinction I already use, but I'm not sure.

Restate the knowledge argument — the Mary's-room argument that there are facts physical description leaves out — in the vocabulary of narrative point of view, as faithfully as you can. Let physical facts play the role of what an omniscient narrator can state; let Mary's pre-release knowledge be the narrator's or reader's knowledge under pure telling; let her seeing red play the role of a reader or character getting something only through direct experience / showing. Say where free indirect discourse and close third person fit into the argument, and what role the ordinary reader's limited access to a characters experience plays.

Then give me the leak report: which claims in the knowledge argument translate cleanly, which only translate by stretching the craft terms, and which are untranslatable into the way novelists talk about point of view. And settle my suspicion: is the philosopher's claim just the old showing-versus-telling rule dressed up as a mind/body thesis, or does something survive my translation that my own craft vocabulary can't capture? I'd rather you tell me wether the translation fails at an interesting place than give me a neat set of equivalences.
