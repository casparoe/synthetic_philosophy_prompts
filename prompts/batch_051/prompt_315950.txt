I'm a control systems engineer — I live in setpoints, plants, error signals, disturbance rejection, model mismatch, the whole lot — and I keep running into this "teleosemantics" idea for naturalizing mental content. As I understand it, the story goes: what a mental state means is fixed by what it is for, where "what it is for" gets cashed out by selection history. A state misrepresents when it fails to do what its type was selected to do under normal conditions. The frog's bug-detector means roughly small-dark-moving-thing because froggy ancestors with that wiring got fed snapping at buggy things; a frog snapping at a black dot that isn't a bug is not hallucinating a bug, its malfunctioning. Consumer systems, not the producer systems, set the normal conditions and the success standard.

Restate this whole position in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, without secretly importing a designer into the middle of it. Is content just the setpoint? Is truth zero tracking error under nominal conditions? Are normal conditions a bounded operating envelope around the niche the plant was selected for? Is misrepresentation ordinary tracking error, or is it a fault condition, or model mismatch?

After that, commit: is the untranslatable residue showing that control theory is missing something normativity-shaped, or is it showing that the teleosemantics crowd was quietly borrowing an intentional designer all along? Take a definite line and argue it; don't just lay the options out for me. British English please.
