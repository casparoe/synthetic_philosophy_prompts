Write the adversarial-collaboration statement that two people in my reading group should have produced after a year of arguing about what death is. One is a whole-brain-death person: death is the irreversible loss of all brain function, and a ventilator can keep a body going but not a person. The other is a circulatory-death person: death is the irreversible loss of circulation and respiration, and 'brain death' is just a workable legal proxy for the same underlying fact. First give a numbered agreed-claims section: what both can sign about irreversibility, about death being a biological event rather than a mere legal line, and about some cells and tissues surviving after death. Then give a disagreements table, with each side stated in wording that side would accept. Then give the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether the brain is the organism's integrator or just one important organ among others, and another is what 'irreversible' means — cannot be reversed under any circumstances, or will not be reversed because nobody ought to try. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it (brain-dead bodies on ventilators whose hearts still beat and whose wounds still heal, autoresuscitation data after cardiac arrest, DCD organ retrieval under the dead donor rule), and say what each side predicts about it. If any part of the fight is verbal, say which part. Write the whole thing in American English.
