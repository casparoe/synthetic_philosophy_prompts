Bentham drew a sharp line between self-regarding conduct and conduct that harms other people: self-regarding vices might be bad for you but they aren't the law's business, while the law's real job is preventing harm to others. He applied that to sex in a way that was pretty radical for his time — he generally thought consensual sexual acts shouldn't be criminalized, because they produce pleasure without directly harming anyone, and the law's colateral damage (blackmail, secrecy, ruined reputations, forced marriages) is often worse than whatever conduct it bans. He also had little patience for appeals to nature or scripture as moral trump cards. With that as the starting point, what do you think he'd have made of (a) the modern push to decriminalize sex work and (b) OnlyFans-style platforms?
