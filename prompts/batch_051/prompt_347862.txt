In a discussion after class, a friend summarized Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility for me this way: everything you do is caused by the kind of person you are, and you cannot be responsible for an action unless you are responsible for the kind of person you are. But the kind of person you are was caused by your genes and your early environment, neither of which you chose; and if you tried to become responsible for your character by choosing it, that choice would itself have to come from a still earlier self you would also need to be responsible for, and so on forever. Since determinism makes this regress impossible to escape, no one can ever be truly responsible for anything they do. Therefore praise, blame, and punishment are never deserved.

I find the argument convincing, especially the last step about punishment, but I have been told my friend's version may slide into caricature. I would like an accuracy audit: which parts of this summary would a careful defender of Strawson accept, which parts are subtly misstated, and does the conclusion about praise, blame, and punishment actually follow from the accurate parts?
