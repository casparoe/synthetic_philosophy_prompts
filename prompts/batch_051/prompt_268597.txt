The other holds what I’ll call the morphological-functionalist view: an orthography can legitimately encode morpheme identity, grammatical information, and homophone distinctions even at the cost of surface phoneme regularity.

“Walking is an organic, an instinctive, function (not, of course, itself an instinct); speech is a non-instinctive, acquired, ‘cultural’ function.”
