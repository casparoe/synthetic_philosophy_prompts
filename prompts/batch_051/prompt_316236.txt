I am a philosophy PhD student working on moral responsibility, and I want to use Macbeth as an extended test case for the debate around the principle of alternative possibilities, but I have not found a reading that holds the philosophy and the play equally in focus. Please write that reading rather than a survey of the literature.

To fix terms briefly: the principle of alternative possibilities says an agent is morally responsible for an action only if she could have done otherwise. Frankfurt-style intervention cases are the standard challenge: an agent decides and acts from her own motives, while a hidden intervener stands ready to make her decide and act in that same way if she wavers, so she could not have done otherwise, yet the intervener never steps in and the action is intuitively hers; she seems responsible anyway.

The Weird Sisters' prophecy that Macbeth will become king looks like the play's version of such a guarantee: if the prophecy is genuine, the outcome is fixed, while the play still treats the murder of Duncan as something Macbeth chooses, hesitates over, and is driven to by his own ambition and by Lady Macbeth's pressure. I want a reading that works through three questions. First, where exactly does the prophecy function like a Frankfurt intervener, and where does it not; is Lady Macbeth better read as an external intervener or as part of Macbeth's own motivational economy; and what does the difference do to the principle of alternative possibilities in the scenes you discuss? Second, is Banquo a kind of control case—he receives a prophecy too but does not murder, at least not as Macbeth does—and does that contrast show the play marking a distinction between a fate that foretells and a fate that forces? Does that distinction correspond to anything in the philosophical debate? Third, does the play's treatment of Macbeth's guilt, self-deception, and damnation commit it to holding that he could have done otherwise, or is its psychology compatible with responsibility without alternative possibilities of the Frankfurt sort? Be explicit about the places where the text resists the framework: the play may simply lack a worked-out metaphysics of agency, and the tragic form may put pressure on both the principle and its denial. End by saying what the play adds to the philosophical debate that bare intervention cases miss.

Please keep your response to at most 1,200 words.
