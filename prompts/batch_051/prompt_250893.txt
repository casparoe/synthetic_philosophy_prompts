I am taking a course on the epistemology of religious belief, and we have been treating Huxley’s 1889 essay “Agnosticism” as a primary text on the burden of proof. I am trying to build a taxonomy of the distinct argumentative moves by which an agnostic or atheist can refuse to shoulder the burden of proof when challenged to justify disbelief. Our professor assigned this passage and asked us to treat it as one item in the taxonomy:

> “The agnostic says, ‘I can not find good evidence that so and so is true.’ ‘Ah,’ says his adversary, seizing his opportunity, ‘then you declare that Jesus Christ was untruthful, for he said so and so’; a very telling method of rousing prejudice. But suppose that the value of the evidence as to what Jesus may have said and done, and as to the exact nature and scope of his authority, is just that which the agnostic finds it most difficult to determine? If I venture to doubt that the Duke of Wellington gave the command, ‘Up, Guards, and at ’em!’ at Waterloo, I do not think that even Dr. Wace would accuse me of disbelieving the duke. Yet it would be just as reasonable to do this as to accuse any one of denying what Jesus said before the preliminary question as to what he did say is settled.”

So far my list includes: (i) the methodological point that agnosticism is a policy about evidence rather than a positive proposition about the divine; (ii) the historical-critical point in the passage, namely that doubts about the reliability of reports about Jesus are not equivalent to the assertion that Jesus was untruthful; (iii) the symmetry argument that trust in Mohammed and trust in Jesus have the same evidential structure; and (iv) the argument from the fact that theists themselves reject countless religious traditions without treating that rejection as a claim requiring proof. I want one further move in this dialectic that is not on the list and not a restatement of any of these—in particular, a move that works even when the opponent denies that burden of proof is a useful category at all. Then tell me honestly whether that further move is any good, or whether it just relocates the burden. Keep your answer to at most 250 words, not counting any quotation of the passage.
