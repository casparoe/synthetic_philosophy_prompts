I'm on my hospital's critical-care triage commitee. We're rewriting our ventilator-allocation protocol for the next bad flu/RSV season, and the draft says that when every ventilator is in use we can withdraw a ventilator from a patient with a much worse expected outcome and reassign it to a newly admitted patient with a much better one, provided two attending physicians independently confirm the prognosis gap. The committee is split down the middle. One camp says withdrawing the vent in order to give it to someone else is an action that kills the first patient, and that writing this into policy turns the hospital into a place that kills people on purpose. The other camp says the first patient dies of their underlying illness, not of the withdrawal, and that refusing the vent to the second patient is just letting that person die, so either way we're letting someone die and we should save the person more likely to survive. The chair asked me to draft the decision memo: approve the reallocation protocol, reject it, or approve it with conditions. Give me a clear recommendation, apply a stated principle to this hard case, and address the strongest argument for the option you reject without using "it's just letting die" as if that ended the discussion.
