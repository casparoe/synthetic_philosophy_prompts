So I just got out of an hour-long argument with a friend about whether clicking "I agree" on a website counts as actually agreeing to anything, and I'm still annoyed because neither of us could state what agreement even is. His line was that these click-to-accept terms are basically a sham: the company writes forty pages nobody will read, makes sure you can't negotiate any of it, and then treats a button-press like it was a handshake. He thinks the click has zero moral force and maybe no real assent behind it either. I half-agree, but I don't want to go all the way to saying someone who clicked is in exactly the same position as someone who never even saw the prompt. That can't be right, and it's not to different from saying any contract you signed without reading every line is void.

So I'm looking for a definition of "agreement" that sorts these cases properly:

1. Signing a lease after reading it carefully. (Obvious agreement.)
2. Clicking "I agree" to a forty-page terms of service you never open. (My gut says this is agreement of some kind, even if it's thin.)
3. Buying software where the license terms are printed inside the box and the outside says opening the box means you accept them. (Genuinely unsure.)
4. Using a website whose footer says your continued use of the site counts as acceptance of its terms. (My friend says obviously not; I'm not so sure.)
5. Saying "fine, okay" to end a long argument. (Publicly it looks like agreement, but what did you actually agree to?)
6. Signing a contract with a gun to your head. (Obviously not agreement.)
7. An app popping up a notice that the terms changed, and you keep using the app. (No idea.)
8. A twelve-year-old clicking "I agree" to an online game's data-sharing terms. (Also no idea.)

Give me a definition that isn't just a vibe. State it clearly, apply it to each case in order, and tell me which of my starting instincts you'd reverse. Then say which of your own applications is least secure. If you think "agreement" needs to be split up — say, a private mental act versus a public signal, or a morally binding agreement versus a legally effective one — split it explicitly and tell me which cases move between the two.
