I'm taking a decision theory course this term and we've spent the last two sessions on expected value fanaticism, and it's honestly been messing with my head. The basic problem is that if you just maximize expected value, a sufficiently tiny probability of a sufficiently huge payoff will dominate every ordinary choice — like a 1-in-10^20 chance of producing some astronomically large amount of value, or the Pascal's-mugging-style case where a guy says that if you don't hand over your wallet he'll simulate and torture 3^^^^3 people. For a short paper I'm collecting the standard escape routes people use to avoid the fanatical conclusion, and so far I've got five: (1) bounding the utility function, so there's a finite ceiling on how good an outcome can be and nothing gets infinite expected value (though this only helps if the ceiling isn't itself astronomically huge); (2) imposing a de minimis probability cutoff and treating everything below it as zero; (3) switching to a risk-sensitive or risk-averse decision rule — maximin, a quantile rule, Buchak-style risk-weighted expected utility, that family — so a tiny chance of a giant payoff doesn't mechanically outweigh near-certain moderate outcomes; (4) the model-error move, where you say probabilities below some level are really noise in your own estimate of the world rather than features of the world, so they shouldn't guide action; and (5) the self-defeat objection, which says fanatical bets are so cheap to invent that you can always manufacture a tiny-chance-huge-payoff bet on the other side, so fanatical reasoning doesn't even give you a stable plan.

What I want is one more way of resisting the fanatical conclusion that is genuinely different in structure from those five — not a different choice of bound, not a combination of the cutoff and the model-error point, not a special case or rewording of any of them. It can be a theoretical fix, an empirical pushback, or a different way of setting up the decision problem, as long as it's a distinct move. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good or whether it secretly collapses into one of the five. I have a feeling theres a sixth move im missing and it's going to be something obvious.
