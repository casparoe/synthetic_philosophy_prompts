I keep running into a thing I dont have a word for, and I'd like to know if there's one. As far as I can tell there are two normal ways someone ends up saying something that isn't true. One, they're lying: they know it's false and they want you to believe it. Two, they're just wrong: they tried to get it right and missed. But I've started noticing a third case that doesn't seem to fit either box. It's when someone says something false and they're not trying to fool you, but it's also not really a mistake, because at no point did they check or care whether it was true. The truth just isn't in the picture. A salesman tells you this is the most reliable car on the lot, not because he believes it and not exactly to trick you, but because that's what the moment seems to call for; he's just keeping the conversation moving toward a sale. Or one of those AI chatbots that will tell you, with total confidence, that some historical figure did such-and-such, and it's wrong. The machine isn't trying to deceive you, but "mistake" feels off too, since a mistake is what happens when you were aiming at the truth and missed. The chatbot wasn't aiming at the truth at all; it was aiming to produce something that sounded like an answer.

Is there an established name for this third thing, saying false stuff where neither the truth nor the listener's belief is the point? And if there is, I want more than just the word: how exactly is it different from lying on one side and being wrong on the other, and does it even make sense to apply it to a machine that doesn't have intentions? If there isn't a name, I'd like a definition with some edge cases, and a name if you can come up with one. I'm less interested in the label itself then in getting the boundaries right, because I keep finding cases that seem to sit halfway between this and ordinary error, and I want to know what the test is supposed to be.
