I'm a software engineer on a platform team and I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have ended up with a question that seems philosophical. My team runs a few hundred scheduled jobs, and hardly a week passes without someone reporting an eerie pattern: a test that fails only on one person's machine, a cache that goes stale exactly every 13 days, two customers filing unrelated bugs in the same hour. I know enough about the birthday paradox to appreciate the obvious objection — in a system this large, with this many events and this many ways of slicing the data, someone is almost guaranteed to have a story like that, and the story itself may be evidence of nothing. Yet some of these stories do turn out to track real dependencies, and I would like a principled filter rather than a reflex.

Here is the filter I have drafted. A coincidence should be treated as evidence of a non-chance process only if it satisfies four conditions. First, specificity: the surprising pattern could have been stated in advance, rather than being selected from many possible patterns after looking at the data. Second, single-shot improbability: for the particular entity in question, the pattern is genuinely unlikely under the hypothesis that the events are independent. Third, mechanism: there is at least one plausible process by which a common cause could have produced it. Fourth, ensemble correction: the surprise survives an honest birthday-paradox calculation — that is, after multiplying the possibilities by the number of entities, time windows, observers, and alternative descriptions, the pattern would still not be commonplace. I would like you to pressure-test this filter against the following case.

A colleague on a different team comes to us with a story. One of our jobs, `ledger_export`, which normally fails a few times a month, failed at 03:04:11 one Monday and at 03:04:04 the following Monday. She wants us to treat the Monday 03:00–03:10 window as genuinely hazardous and delay a dependent job until 03:15. Her reason is the billing team's maintenance job, which runs Monday at 03:00 UTC and could plausibly contend for a lock. Another teammate dismisses this as a birthday-paradox artifact: we run about 400 jobs on tight schedules, we have a year of logs, and it would be strange if no pair of failures landed within seconds of each other; the job and the precise window were only singled out because the pair was visible. Does this case qualify under the four criteria? Work through the criteria one by one, say which are doing genuine work and which leave the verdict underdetermined, and tell me whether the case shows that the filter itself needs tightening.
