Now, here’s the passage from your own text I want to push on:

“So it is said: the ruler must not show what he wants. Show what you want, and the ministers will carve and polish themselves to fit it. He must not show what he thinks. Show what you think, and they will paint themselves up to stand out for it.”

Here’s my opening challenge, playing a Confucian minister who’s read your chapter and is, let’s say, not entirely won over. You say the ruler must hide what he wants and thinks. Fine. But your whole system also says the ruler should have fixed, public laws with clear rewards and punishments. Isn’t posting a clear schedule of rewards and penalties just a roundabout way of announcing what the ruler wants and doesn’t want? And if the ministers know the reward schedule, won’t they carve and polish themselves to hit the quota, game the metrics, and produce exactly the appearance the ruler can’t see through? I don't get how the two handles don’t become the very display that ruins you. Defend yourself.
