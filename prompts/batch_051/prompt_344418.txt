So I'm on the board of a small library district and we're now in a standoff with the state health department, and I need a careful read. Our state's public health law has a section that hasn't been amended since 1947. It says: "Whenever the governor declares a public health emergency, the department of health may take such measures as are reasonably necessary to prevent the spread of communicable disease, including the temporary isolation or quarantine of persons and the exclusion of persons from public places." Last year the governor declared an emergency for the respiratory outbreak, and the department issued an order requiring every employer to make each employee run a Bluetooth contact-tracing app on their personal phone. The app logs when someone has been within six feet of someone else for fifteen minutes, sends those contacts to the department, and when it flags someone the department texts that person an automated isolation order directing them to stay home for ten days. The order says an employer is responsible if an employee refuses or simply doesn't have a phone.

We have one shelver with a flip phone who refuses to install anything, and last week a colleague tested positive, so the app flagged three other employees and the department ordered them to isolate even though none of them has a symptom or even got tested. The department says this is plainly authorized because it is "reasonably necessary to prevent the spread" and is just a modern form of excluding people from public places. I'm not so sure a 1947 clause about quarantining visibly ill people and keeping them out of buildings was meant to let the state draft every phone in town into a location-tracking system and write isolation orders by algorithm.

Walk me through what the words themselves cover and don't cover, what the purpose of the 1947 law says, what the people who actually wrote the clause would have said if someone had handed them a smartphone, and what a reasonable reader today takes "such measures as are reasonably necessary" to allow. Then tell me which approach should actually decide this and whether the department's order is authorized.
