I work as a software engineer and have no formal background in philosophy. I keep encountering the idea of a "Ulysses contract" and the claim that a constitution is a form of precommitment: a person or community binds its future self, as Ulysses had himself tied to the mast, so that a later burst of temptation, fear, or short-sightedness cannot move it. I'd like you to restate this debate in the working vocabulary I actually use, and then report what does not survive the translation.

My vocabulary, which you should treat as the target and not stretch with unexplained philosophical terminology, includes: Git branches and protected branches, code review and merge approvals, CI/CD pipelines and required checks, role-based access control and break-glass accounts, feature flags and kill switches, read-only mode and deployment freezes, database migrations (backward-compatible, expand/contract, irreversible), deprecation and tombstoning of APIs, lockfiles and pinned dependencies, immutable infrastructure, circuit breakers, and the difference between making an action impossible, making it expensive, and making it not the default.

Please translate each of the following strands of the debate, one at a time and as faithfully as you can:

1. The claim that precommitment is rational: a calm, better-positioned self can foresee that a future self will be weak, panicked, or reckless, and therefore has reason to constrain that future self in advance.
2. The objection that this is an abdication of autonomy or responsibility: a person, or a political community, that cannot reconsider its earlier choice is not fully free, and a founding generation that binds later ones can become a "dead hand."
3. The distinction between binding yourself and being bound by another party, and the related distinction between a Ulysses contract and an ordinary contract with someone else.
4. The reply that a good precommitment device does not foreclose all judgment, because constitutions include amendment procedures and courts that interpret them — in the same way that Ulysses could still listen to the song even though he could not act on it.

For each strand, after translating it, give me a residue report. Say what maps cleanly onto things like release freezes, protected branches, or required checks; what maps only with distortion, and exactly where the distortion enters; and what has no natural expression in my vocabulary at all. Then close with your honest assessment: where something fails to survive the translation, is that because my vocabulary is genuinely missing a concept I should care about, or because the underlying philosophical claim was less sharp than it first appears? Please write the answer in American English.
