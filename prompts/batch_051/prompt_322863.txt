In my free will seminar we have been reconstructing Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, and I would like help seeing where it goes wrong, if it does. Consider this version. (1) What you do on any occasion issues from the way you are at that time—your beliefs, desires, values, and dispositions—together with the situation. (2) To be truly responsible for what you do, you must be truly responsible for the way you are that issues in the action. (3) You are truly responsible for the way you are only if you chose to be that way. (4) But any such choice would itself have issued from some earlier way you were, so the same requirement applies to that earlier constitution, and a regress begins that never reaches a point at which you formed yourself from no prior character at all. (5) Therefore, no one is ever truly responsible for what they do. Is the argument valid? If not, identify the exact step that fails and the reading of it that fails. If it is valid, is the best compatibilist response to reject step (2) as demanding more than ordinary moral responsibility requires, or can a compatibilist reject some other step? Please be specific about the step and the interpretation.
