Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Do not flag pairs merely because they are vague; precisify first. My commitments are:

1. The birthday paradox is the master lesson: an event can be extraordinarily improbable for a specified person–time pair while being nearly certain to happen somewhere once all the person–time pairs are counted. Low probability by itself therefore never justifies positing a non-chance cause.

2. Consequently, how much a surprising event should move me depends on the size of the relevant opportunity space — the prior people, days, trials, patterns, and predictions that could have produced something like it.

3. But a coincidence that was fixed in advance with unguessable specificity (for example, writing a particular name in my journal the night before receiving an unexpected call from that person the next day) is far better evidence for a non-chance connection than a resemblance noticed only after the fact, because it is not vulnerable to the objection that I would eventually have found something to match.

4. Littlewood-style reasoning (a one-in-a-million event should happen to each of us roughly once a month, given how many events we live through) should make me nearly immune to being moved by personal reports of miracles or premonitions.

5. Still, some personal coincidence reports are so well documented, specific, and free of obvious selection effects that they ought to raise my confidence in a non-chance explanation even when the relevant opportunity space cannot be quantified.
