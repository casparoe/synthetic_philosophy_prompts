I'm after a new kind of counterexample to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions: x is disposed to manifest M in response to stimulus S iff, were x to undergo S, x would M. The ones I already know and don't need repeated (and I think these are all the standard ones) are: finkish cases, where the stimulus removes the disposition before it can manifest; masking cases, where the disposition remains but something external blocks its manifestation; and mimic cases, where the object lacks the disposition but the stimulus would produce the manifestation anyway. Give me a counterexample that is not in that list and is not just a rewording, special case, or combination of them. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good, and whether it collapses back into a fink, mask, or mimic on the most natural description.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion, in British English: (1) the new counterexample, or a straight statement that you couldn't find one; (2) which direction of the biconditional it breaks, if any; (3) whether it is genuinely distinct from a fink, mask, or mimic, and why; (4) your honest verdict on whether it is any good; (5) what in this area is settled and what is genuinely contested, without presenting a contested view as the consensus.
