My friend Aisha curates a collection of optical devices and early photographs at a small museum, and she has unusually firm, carefully defended views about what we literally see when we look at images. I've been collecting her verdicts because she's so consistant, and now I want to predict what she'll say about a new case. Her track record, as best I can paraphrase it: (1) In an ordinary flat mirror you see your own face, not a picture of your face. (2) Through a telescope aimed at a boat in the harbor, you see the boat. (3) In a photorealistic oil painting of that same boat, you do not see the boat — you see a painted canvas. (4) In a standard photograph of the boat, you do see the boat. (5) On a live video feed of the boat from a pier camera, you see the boat. (6) In a careful hand-drawn copy of a photograph of the boat, you do not see the boat. (7) When I asked about a photograph of a photograph of the boat, she said you still see the boat, just through an extra link.

The new case: a snapshot of Aisha's great-grandmother, taken in 1965 and printed at the time, showing her as a young woman. The great-grandmother died in 1995. Looking at that print today, does Aisha see her great-grandmother, or does she see only a picture of her?

I want you to answer as someone who works on philosophy of perception and the transparency debate, but don't just recite the literature — reason from the verdicts themselves. Please think carefully; maybe start by brainstorming two or three different principles that would generate all seven verdicts. Then: (a) state the most plausible candidate rule or rules that fit her track record; (b) say what each candidate rule predicts about the 1965 snapshot; (c) give your best guess about what Aisha would say, with the reasons you prefer one rule over the others; and (d) give one further case that would cleanly seperate the surviving candidate rules or test the rule you've attributed to her. Finally, tell me how confident you are in your prediction, and single out the point you're least sure about.
