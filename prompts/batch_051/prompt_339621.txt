I'm putting together a one-year dissertation fellowship proposal and I keep going in circles on how to frame it. My half-formed idea is that the whole debate about whether consequentialism gives virtues and duties the right place keeps assuming there are only two options — instrumental or independent — and Mill actually floats a third one that nobody presses. The passage I cant stop thinking about is from Mill's Utilitarianism, chapter 4, where he writes:

"Virtue, according to the utilitarian doctrine, is not naturally and originally part of the end, but it is capable of becoming so; and in those who love it disinterestedly it has become so, and is desired and cherished, not as a means to happiness, but as a part of their happiness."

The phrase that does the work for me is "not as a means to happiness, but as a part of their happiness." That's not instrumental, but it's also not making virtue an independent source of value outside happiness. I want the proposal built around that distinction: can we read Mill's "part of the end" move as a distinct axiological category — something originally instrumental that becomes constitutive of the end — and would treating virtues (and maybe duties) that way dissolve the standard objection that consequentialism can't take character seriously, or does it just restate it? Turn this into a two-page proposal with a precise statement of the question, why the existing instrumentalist and independent-value readings leave it open, the method (what I'd actually do, month by month), what a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing.
