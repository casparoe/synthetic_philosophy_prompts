Give me the list of desiderata. For each one, give me the case or intuition that puts it on the list, and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of one side of the argument. If some subset of the list can't be jointly satisfied, flag that explicitly — that's the part Id actually want to see, because it would tell me where the real disagreement is. The purpose is fixed: I'm evaluating whether DNA is a code in a literal-ish sense, not whether 'life is information' is a nice slogan. Coming at it from software, my naive checklist would be something like: a symbol set, a mapping from symbols to meanings or instructions, an encoder, a decoder, and a mapping that is arbitrary or stipulated rather than forced by the underlying physics — nothing about 01100001 forces it to mean 'a'. I already suspect the encoder part is wrong for biology unless transcription counts, and I dont know whether the arbitrariness part is even load-bearing. So tell me which of my programmer intuitions survive and which are just parochial. Does an adequate account have to say where the meaning lives?
