I am teaching Shirley Jackson's "The Lottery" in a course that has been discussing expiatory sacrifice, scapegoating, and the transfer of guilt. I want to use the story to test the standard model on which a scapegoat is someone onto whom a community shifts its collective sin or impurity, so that the killing or expulsion discharges what would otherwise fall on the group. Jackson's village seems to do something stranger: no one accuses Tessie Hutchinson of anything; no ritual specialist lays the community's guilt onto her; the lottery appears to have lost whatever theology once explained it and is kept up as inherited civic routine. The victim is chosen by lot rather than by accusation or desert, and the hints of a rationale are a vague association with the harvest and the claim that the village has always done this.

I would like a reading of the story as a contribution to philosophical and anthropological thinking about ritual substitution. In what sense, if any, is Tessie a scapegoat rather than merely an arbitrarily selected victim? Does scapegoating require that at least some participants believe the victim is guilty or that her death transfers something—sin, blame, bad luck, or future misfortune—from the group to her? Or is it enough that one person's suffering is ritually marked as standing in for what the community would otherwise have to bear? Does the story show that the guilt-transfer apparatus is dispensable and that ritual killing persists on convention and fear alone, or does it show that once the apparatus is hollowed out we are left with a different concept? Consider also the fact that the victim is not a marginal outsider but a longstanding neighbor, and that her own family participates in the closing-in. Does that strengthen or undermine the analogy with classical scapegoating? I would like a reading that is faithful to the story's details, that distinguishes what the story shows from what it leaves unexplained, and that says what it adds to the theoretical debate that a bare argument about scapegoats would miss.
