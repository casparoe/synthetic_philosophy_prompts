I am preparing a seminar on the mental imagery debate between Kosslyn and Pylyshyn, and I keep running into the same difficulty: the two sides seem to presuppose different accounts of what mental imagery is, so before one can assess whether imagery is depictive or propositional one needs a workable definition of the term itself. I would like you to propose a definition of mental imagery that can classify the following cases. Picturing a friend's face with one's eyes closed, and hearing a tune playing unbidden in one's head, strike me as clear instances of imagery. An afterimage left by a bright lamp strikes me as clearly not imagery, because it is a perceptual after-effect rather than something produced in thought. The cases that leave me genuinely uncertain are: mentally rotating an unfamiliar shape to decide whether two drawings show the same object; a person with aphantasia who reports no quasi-perceptual experience at all but can still tell one accurately that the flat has two windows and the sofa is against the far wall; a blind person navigating a familiar room by a felt spatial layout with no visual qualities; dreaming of a landscape; and the sense of seeing a scene whilst deeply absorbed in a novel. Please run your definition through all eight cases, flag any that produce the wrong verdict or no verdict, and revise the definition or defend the surprising classifications accordingly. Then say whether the definition you end up with pushes you towards Kosslyn's quasi-pictorial account or Pylyshyn's propositional account, and whether any single disputed case does most of the work in forcing that choice.
