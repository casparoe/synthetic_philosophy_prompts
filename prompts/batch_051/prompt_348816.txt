I have come to hold a Humean view of laws of nature in something like the Lewisian best-system sense: laws are not an extra ontological force that governs the world's unfolding; they are the axioms of the true system that best balances simplicity and strength over the whole distribution of local, categorical facts. The heart of the position, for me, is a passage in David Hume's *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, Section VII, "Of the Idea of Necessary Connexion." Hume writes:

"When we look about us towards external objects, and consider the operation of causes, we are never able, in a single instance, to discover any power or necessary connexion; any quality, which binds the effect to the cause, and renders the one an infallible consequence of the other. We only find, that the one does actually, in fact, follow the other."

I read this as making a metaphysical point, not merely an epistemological one: there is nothing in the world over and above the actual pattern of events that makes something a law. I want to know how exposed this position really is. What would have to be true for the Humean regularist view of laws to be false? I am not asking for the standard objections as labels, but for a map of the live defeaters: which scientific developments—in how physicists treat initial conditions, phase space, conserved quantities, symmetries, or the explanatory use of laws—would count as evidence against the view; which formal or conceptual results, such as problems about chances, counterfactual dependence, or the gap between mere regularities and laws, would put real pressure on it; and which intuitions about possible worlds or explanation would a committed Humean still feel as genuine costs rather than merely external challenges. Please distinguish fatal defeaters from those that are only awkward, say which ones the view's defenders would themselves concede as counting against it, and tell me whether the view has been qualified enough that it risks nothing—and if it has, whether that insulation is a problem for a metaphysical position or just what should be expected of an account of this kind. What would move me most is either a concrete case in which two worlds agree on all non-nomic facts but differ in their laws, or a scientific practice whose success depends on treating laws as more than concise summaries. Are either of those actually live, and if not, what is the strongest available version of a defeater?
