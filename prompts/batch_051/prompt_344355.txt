In my environmental ethics course we keep circling around one pattern: the same person who will stop to free a raccoon caught in a fence, and who pays extra for cage-free eggs, will also eat chicken without asking how the bird lived and will give no thought to the mice, voles, and insects killed when grain is harvested for bread or those eggs. That person would also find it strange, maybe even wrong, to intervene to reduce starvation or predation among wild animals. I want to know whether these judgments share one underlying asymmetry, and whether it can be justified rather than explained away as a psychological fact about what is visible and what is not. Pick the strongest justification you can find for the asymmetry, test it against the hardest version of the pattern, and tell me whether you would keep, revise, or abandon it.
