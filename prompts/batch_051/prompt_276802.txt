His line is that ML already has the whole toolbox for this — priors, regularization, Occam's razor, generalization error, model selection, the bias-variance tradeoff, the no free lunch theorem — and that calling beauty an inductive bias pretty much settles the matter. I don't think it does, but I'm not looking to argue with him directly; I want a translation good enough that he can see, in his own terms, exactly where the ML picture runs out.

So here's the assignment. Restate the debate honestly in his vocabulary. Take at least three positions: (1) the optimist who says elegance is a genuine sign of truth, pointing to the track record of simple, symmetric, unifying theories in physics and the way physicists kept betting on mathematical beauty before the data was in; (2) the skeptic who says elegance is aesthetic fashion, that the beautiful-looking theory has often lost, and that standards of beauty shift; (3) the subtler view that elegance is a non-empirical virtue — something that rightly guides theory choice without itself being evidence. For each, say what maps cleanly onto a prior, a regularizer, a claim about the data-generating process, or a meta-learning move, and what you had to fudge. Then give me a residue report: what in the philosophical debate has no counterpart in his vocabulary at all? Is the residue just philosophers being sloppy, or is it something ML genuinely can't say — in particular, does the ML framing settle anything about whether the actual world is biased toward simple hypotheses, or does it just relocate the question? He's fluent in ML and probability but has no philosophy background, so spell out any philosophical terms you need to use.
