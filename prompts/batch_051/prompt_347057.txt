I am a retired research chemist. My working life was spent with systems that tend toward equilibrium, and I have come late to philosophy, where I have begun with the texts of Schopenhauer. The idea that has most unsettled me is his pessimism, not because I believe it, but because I want to be sure that any judgement I form rests on the full range of his case rather than on the few arguments that happen to be mentioned in introductory surveys.

For my own notes I am compiling a list of the distinct arguments Schopenhauer gives, or that follow directly from his account of the will, for the conclusion that life is on balance bad and that existence is essentially suffering. So far I have four:

(1) the negative-character argument: pleasure is never felt as a positive state, but only as the temporary removal of a want or pain, while pain is felt directly; happiness therefore lacks the positive reality that suffering has;
(2) the striving argument: the will is ceaseless wanting, so life alternates endlessly between the pain of unsatisfied desire and the emptiness that follows satisfaction, the next desire always arriving before any lasting contentment does;
(3) the boredom argument: even when pressing wants are still, what remains is not flourishing but ennui, which shows that existence has no intrinsic content once striving is subtracted;
(4) the comparison argument: as an empirical matter, the quantity and vividness of pain in the world, including the way sentient beings live at one another's expense, plainly outweigh pleasure, which is mostly brief and conditional.

These four strike me as sharing a single basis: they work either from the character of desire and its satisfaction or from a quantitative tally of pleasure and pain. I want one further argument for the same conclusion whose structural basis is different from that — not a rewording, not a special case, and not a combination of the four. I also want a brief account of what this further argument adds that the four together do not, and an honest judgement of whether it is any good or whether it merely dresses up the existing case.

Please keep your answer to no more than 400 words, and write it in British English.
