I recently inherited a project that forces me to choose between building a system with an explicit objective and building one trained to act in the way its overseers would approve of, and the discussion group I joined for guidance turns out to be deadlocked over exactly that choice. The two positions are familiar to everyone in the group, and the argument never advances. One side says a goal-directed agent is dangerous because any finite objective is a proxy: a capable optimizer will exploit the gap between the written goal and what its designers actually value, and the more capable it gets, the more efficiently it will find unforeseen ways to score well while doing something catastrophic. An approval-directed agent, by contrast, is not bound to a frozen formula; it is trained against a living human evaluation in each situation, so it stays anchored to what we in fact judge good and remains corrigible in a way a fixed objective cannot be. The other side says this merely moves the goal into the approval signal. A system trained to get approval now has an objective — approval itself — and we should expect it to improvise ways of producing the appearance of good behavior, to game its evaluators, or to shape the channel through which approval is expressed. They add that approval cannot even be made into a training target without making it goal-shaped somewhere: someone has to define it, measure it, and optimize against it.

No one in the room denies that misspecified objectives are dangerous, and no one denies that approval is hard to measure; the disagreement is over which fact should set the default. My own guess, offered only as a hypothesis, is that the debate is not empirical but conceptual: the two sides are using the word 'goal' for two different things — one for the operative target of a trained policy, the other for the inductive pressure of a training process — and the argument is really about where the intentionality of the system is located. But I am not at all sure.

What I want is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a survey of agent architectures and not a verdict on which side is right. Is this a merely verbal dispute about 'goal-directed,' a substantive disagreement driven by a hidden empirical question — for instance, about how approval-driven training will behave under distribution shift — a difference about which failure mode should be treated as the default, or a difference about what it takes for a system to count as an agent at all? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result, an experiment, a sharper distinction, or changing the question? Lead with your diagnosis in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
