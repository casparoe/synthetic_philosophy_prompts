I'm raising two children without religion inside my husband's close, observant Catholic family, and I've spent the past year trying to write down where I stand before the children get older and the family stops politely ignoring the question. The list below isn't a position I'm defending; it's a record of commitments I seem to hold all at once, and I want to know whether they can all stand together.

(1) I should not lie to my children about what I believe, and I want to raise them to be honest in the sense of not asserting what they don't believe and not performing a belief they don't have. (2) I should not coach them to tell their grandparents that they believe, to say prayers they don't mean, or to manufacture religious conviction for the sake of family comfort. (3) But I also won't stop them from going to Mass with their grandmother, kneeling when the family kneels, or saying a blessing at her table when they want to join in; those rituals are one of the main ways this family gives and receives love. (4) Their relationship with their grandparents and cousins should not be conditional on metaphysical agreement; it should stay warm and close even if the children grow up entirely secular. (5) I hold that participating in a ritual whose literal content one doesn't endorse isn't always dishonest — much of social life involves performing forms one doesn't literally affirm, from singing a national anthem to attending a friend's wedding ceremony — and my own tactful silence about my nonbelief isn't a lie. (6) Yet I would be uneasy about my child taking communion or reciting the creed, because those particular acts seem closer to a declaration of belief than to mere courtesy, and that difference strikes me as mattering. (7) I don't want the children to grow up as secret nonbelievers inside a believing family; my hope is that they can eventually be openly nonreligious and fully inside the family at the same time.

Does this set hang together? Please audit it rather than defend it. Identify which pairs or triples are in genuine tension and rank those tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious; identify which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve once a relevant distinction is drawn; and then tell me what the cheapest revision would be, what it would cost among the values in (1)–(7), and which single revision would clean up the most. I suspect something has to give, but I can't tell which of these commitments is doing the real work and which is an unprincipled compromise.
