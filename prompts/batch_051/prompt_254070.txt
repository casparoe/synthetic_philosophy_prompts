I've come around to a fairly strong version of objective Bayesianism, roughly the Jaynes picture: for a well-posed assignment of a prior under partial knowledge there's a uniquely rational choice — maximize Shannon entropy subject to the constraints that encode what you actually know, and where that alone doesn't pin a density down, let invariance under the relevant transformation group break the tie. I find it compelling because the alternatives feel like an arbitary punt: subjective Bayesianism lets any coherent prior count as rational, and "just don't assign a prior" dodges the question.
