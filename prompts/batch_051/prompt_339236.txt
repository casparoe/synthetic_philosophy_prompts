I'm trying to get my philosophy of education straight before I submit a homeschool co-op proposal. I basically hold these Locke-inspired committments: (1) kids come into the world as blank slates, so their bad behavior is mostly from bad examples and habits rather than any built-in depravity; (2) the main end of education is virtue and a sound mind, so academic content matters mostly as it serves character; (3) learning should be made a game or free play wherever possible, never a forced task, because Locke says in Some Thoughts Concerning Education that children should be treated as rational creatures and that their learning should feel like play rather than work; (4) habits and practice matter more than rules and precepts; (5) shame and love of reputation, not corporal punishment, should be the main disciplinary tools. But I also believe (6) education should respect the child's autonomy and consent, so I'm uneasy about using shame and adult-manipulated play to steer them. I can't tell which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest revision would be. Please rank the tensions by severity and say what would have to give.
