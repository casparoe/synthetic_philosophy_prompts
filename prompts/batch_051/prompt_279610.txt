my burkean-leaning colleagues lean on Chesterton's fence at every faculty meeting: before you pull down some old fence, you'd better know why it was put up; institutions encode the accumulated wisdom of the dead even when nobody in the room can state the reason. I keep wanting to press on the analogy itself instead of the specific proposal. If the institution is the fence, whats the builder, the original reason, the person walking past who wants it gone, and the cost of keeping it? give me the correspondence table first, then the places the analogy breaks in ways that actually matter for the conservative use. In particular, the analogy seems to quietly import that a fence had a reason that still applies and that leaving it up is cheap — but those are exactly the contested claims. how much of the 'just leave it alone' conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and is there a better analogy that does the same work honestly, or was the fence carrying the whole thing?
