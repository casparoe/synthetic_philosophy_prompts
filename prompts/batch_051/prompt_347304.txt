I retired recently from a career in the natural sciences, and I have since been reading philosophy; my present focus is social choice theory. I have worked my way into the following set of commitments, and I would like an impartial consistency check before I present them to my reading group.

(1) When exactly two alternatives are before the voters, majority rule is the uniquely correct way to aggregate preferences; I accept the usual characterization of it as the only rule for the two-alternative case that is anonymous, neutral, and positively responsive.

(2) For elections with more than two candidates, I favor instant-runoff voting. Plurality's willingness to elect a candidate with only a minority of first-choice support seems indefensible to me, and I take IRV's sequential majorities to give the eventual winner a genuine majority mandate.

(3) I accept Arrow's impossibility theorem as correct and regard it as revealing something deep about preference aggregation, not a mere technical curiosity.

(4) I hold that any acceptable method should elect a Condorcet winner whenever one exists: if a candidate would defeat every rival in a one-on-one majority vote, electing someone else strikes me as perverse.

(5) I regard monotonicity as a hard requirement. A method under which a candidate can go from winning to losing because some voters rank that candidate higher is, in my view, fundamentally defective.

(6) I do not think independence of irrelevant alternatives is a reasonable constraint on voting methods; the presence or absence of a third alternative can legitimately affect how two others are compared.

I would like an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, and how severe is each tension? In each case, what would have to give, and what would be the cheapest revision? Also flag any pair that only appears to be in tension, with an explanation of why the appearance dissolves. Finally, if there is a single revision to my list that would remove the most problems at the least cost, identify it. I would prefer not to assume in advance which commitment will have to go.
