so i just got back from a three-hour arguement with a freind about sweatshops and i need someone to tell me whether the thing i kept reaching for is actually a thing or just me being annoyed. his line was: if a worker voluntarily takes a job, and the job is better than any of her other options, then the exchange is mutually beneficial, and beneficial exploitation is basically an oxymoron. i kept saying he was laundering the baseline, and i want to check my own idea before i ever use the phrase in public again.

here's the concept, in two pieces. a 'pareto defense' of a transaction is the move where you say it can't be wrongful (exploitative) because the weaker party gains relative to her alternatives. piece one, which i'm calling the dirty baseline principle: a pareto defense only works when the baseline you measure the gain against is not itself produced, or held in place, by wrongdoing that the benefiting side is entangled in. piece two, 'baseline laundering,' is the rhetorical move of pointing at the gain while quietly treating that rigged baseline as a natural fact.

the stock case: a garment worker takes a factory job at $2.50 a day because her alternatives are worse. the defender says she chose it and she's better off, so where's the objection? i want to say the comparison is rigged when her alternatives are worse because of things like land grabs, tariffs, migration restrictions, and the purchasing practices of the same supply chains the consumer is plugging into. the gain is real, but it's measured against a floor that the same system dug. calling the deal 'mutually beneficial' then launders the system's own contribution into a point in its favor.

my shaky spot: does it matter who dug the floor? take a flood that wipes out someone's field, and a merchant who then offers a miserable wage while clearly benefiting from her bad luck. he didn't dig anything. is the pareto defense sound there, or is it still laundering? my friend says that if the principle applies whenever there's any background injustice anywhere, then no actual exchange is ever clean, which is absurd. i think that's his best point and i don't have a good answer other than 'the two cases feel different.'

so, straight up: (i) is this already a named view or distinction? (ii) reconstruct my idea in its strongest form even if it means fixing it for me, and (iii) give me the three hardest objections and say which, if any, are fatal rather than just uncomfortable. don't be nice.
