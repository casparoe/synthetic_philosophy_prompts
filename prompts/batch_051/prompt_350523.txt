My friend has become a bit famous in our group for how she handles disagreements, so I've been keeping a record. Here are the cases so far:

- She and a co-worker studied the same sales figures and guessed next quarter's revenue. She said 420k, he said 380k, and she immediately moved to 400k.
- Her partner said the bedroom was too cold. She said it felt fine to her and held her ground.
- A friend with pretty similar movie taste said a film they'd both just seen was shallow. She kept insisting it was deeply moving; no shift.
- A fellow doctor she considers her equal disagreed with her diagnosis from the same test results. She deferred hard and more or less adopted his reading.
- A friend who follows politics about as closely as she does disagreed about whether a given policy would reduce violent crime. She came away saying she genuinely didn't know either way.
- The same friend said eating octopus is obviously wrong. She said she still didn't think so, and that her confidence hadn't moved at all.

Now her brother, a trained wine judge, tastes the same bottle she'd just called excellent and says it's technically unbalanced. Predict whether she changes her mind, and roughly how much. I care less about the particular answer than about the rule that best explains her track record, the other rules that fit it about equally well, and which one future case would cleanly seperate those candidate rules.
