Suppose a country sets itself a goal of rising ten places in the World Happiness Report within four years. It creates a Life Evaluation Unit whose budget depends on its own predictions: for every major regulation, the unit must pre-register how much it expects the next year's average Cantril ladder score (the 0–10 life-evaluation question Gallup asks, where 0 is the worst possible life and 10 the best) to move, and next year's budget scales with how accurate those pre-registered predictions were. The unit cant touch Gallup's sampling or the questionnaire itself, but it can recommend policies, translations, public information campaigns, and the timing of major announcements. Treat it as a mechanism, not a moral position. Walk through what it incentivizes among the unit, other ministries, local politicians, and ordinary residents once everyone understands the scoring rule. Where does the number get gamed first, how quickly does the ladder score decouple from whatever it was originally measuring, and is any of this fixable without dropping the metric entirely?
