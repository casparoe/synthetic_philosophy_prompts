I've been trying to get clear on the actual argumentative machinery behind the Eleatic denial of plurality and change, and the whole thing gets confusing real fast because Parmenides writes in verse and most of the fragments come down through later commentators who have their own agendas. So far the standard moves I can reconstruct are these. There is Parmenides' not-being route: you cannot think or speak what is not, so any talk of something coming to be or passing away is impossible, since both would require thinking or saying of what is not. There is also his ungeneratedness move, the one about what necessity would have driven it to be born later rather than earlier, so there is no reason for it to come to be at any time and therefore it did not come to be. Zeno's plurality paradox argues that if there are many things, they are both limited, since they are as many as they are, and unlimited, since there is always something between any two things, so plurality is contradictory. Melissus adds that what is is unlimited, and what is unlimited must be one, because two things would each limit the other. Then there is the void argument: motion would require empty space for a thing to move into, but the void is nothing, and nothing cannot be, so motion is impossible. I'm also setting aside Zeno's more local paradoxes of motion, like the dichotomy and the arrow, since those feel like a different kind of argument aimed at a particular analysis of motion rather than at plurality or change as such.
