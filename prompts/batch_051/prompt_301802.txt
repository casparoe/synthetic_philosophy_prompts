I've been trying to pin down my views on effort, spontaneity, and skilled performance, and I'd like a consistency audit before I write them up for a reading group. Here is roughly where I've landed.

1. In the early and middle stages of acquiring a skill, improvement comes mostly from deliberate practice: isolating components, drilling them at the edge of one's ability, and error-correcting. Self-monitoring and felt effort are not incidental to this process; they are its engine.
2. In performance, the same self-monitoring becomes a liability. At their best, performers in conversation, improvisation, sport, and similar activities report that the explicit controller goes quiet; behavior remains fluent and self-adjusting, but there is little felt sense of a self issuing commands. I'll use wu-wei to name this state, intending it as a psychological description rather than a mystical one.
3. Wu-wei is a real, causally describable state, roughly well-automatized skill plus the absence of top-down interference, not merely a flattering story people tell after the fact about having been in flow.
4. You cannot enter wu-wei by deciding to enter it. Forming the intention to be spontaneous now, or trying directly to stop trying, tends to reinstall exactly the monitoring that blocks the state.
5. Nevertheless, wu-wei is cultivable in an indirect way: you drill the component skills effortfully outside performance, then design environments, routines, and warm-ups that make it more likely that the components run without a supervisor. You cannot aim at wu-wei directly, but you can aim at its preconditions.
6. A good deliberator should sometimes prefer an option that scores lower on their explicit expected-value calculation because that option leaves more room for wu-wei than the higher-scoring one does.

I'd like to know whether this set hangs together. Go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only look as though they are, and how severe each real tension is; for each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. In particular, is there a hidden contradiction between 4 and 5, or does the word cultivable do more work than it appears to? Does 6 actually cohere with the instrumental-rationality picture behind 1, or does deliberately preferring the lower-scoring option commit me to something strange? And if exactly one of these six had to go, which removal would leave the most stable package?
