I've been going back and forth over epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment, and half the time I'm convinced they're just notational variants: both say high stakes raise the bar on 'knows', except one locates the raising in the word and the other locates it in the world. I'd like a case that would actually seperate them, because I'm not sure I'd recognise the difference in real life.

Here's the spec. Build me a realistic third-person case where the two views come apart in the direction that gets less attention: the attributor has a lot riding on whether p is true, while the subject has almost nothing riding on it. The proposition and the subject's evidence are held fixed. I want the design to push the verdicts to these: the pragmatic encroacher says the sentence 'Priya knows that p', as uttered by Dana, is true (because Priya's stakes are low), and the contextualist says that same sentence is false in Dana's mouth (because Dana's high-stakes situaton raises the conversational standard). Use an ordinary workplace or service setting — no banks, no airports, no zebras or fake barns, no sceptical scenarios — and make the evidence genuinely moderate: good enough that a low-stakes Priya would count as knowing, but not so strong that it obviously meets any standard. The attribution has to be in the third person, present tense, and about something Dana can act on later that day.
