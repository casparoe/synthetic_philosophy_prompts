I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I want a careful analysis of an asymmetry I have noticed in how my colleagues and I reason about biological versus cultural change.

When a biological trait, such as lactose tolerance, spreads through a human population, we treat that spread as a purely causal process: the trait became common because it increased survival or reproduction, or because of drift, migration, or linkage. We do not treat its prevalence as evidence that the trait is good, correct, or in any sense merited. But when a cultural item—say a programming language, an architecture pattern, or an engineering practice—spreads and becomes dominant, we often do treat its prevalence as evidence for its merit. People say things like “there must be a reason everyone uses X,” or “X won out for a reason,” and we invoke the size of a community as if it were a quality signal.

A Darwinian theory of culture, and the memetic tradition that grows out of it, says cultural variants spread by imitation, differential retention in memory and institutions, prestige bias, conformist bias, and network effects. On that view, the spread of an idea and the truth or usefulness of an idea are different properties. That would make the asymmetry above look unjustified. But maybe there is a relevant difference between genes and ideas: ideas are evaluated by minds, can be deliberately improved, and their spread often depends on whether they solve a problem for their users. So perhaps prevalence is weaker evidence for merit in the cultural case, but still some evidence. I want to know whether the asymmetry can be defended on those or other grounds.

Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in cultural evolution and social epistemology. Start by brainstorming the strongest candidate justifications for treating cultural prevalence as evidence of merit, then test them against nearby cases from both biology and technology. Finally, give a reasoned verdict: should we drop the practice of treating dominance as a quality signal for cultural variants, keep it, or keep it only under carefully specified conditions? Be precise about where the line should be drawn, if anywhere.
