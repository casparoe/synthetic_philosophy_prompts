A colleague of mine has been defending a broadly Rortyan antirepresentationalism about the mind. On her view, to say that an organism or a device represents its environment is not to report a discoverable relation between inner states and the world; it is to adopt a useful vocabulary, and there is no further fact of the matter answering to it. I am not convinced the view is testable, but I also cannot see how to test it without begging the question. Take a rat that has learned a maze. Design the strongest experiment you can for deciding whether, when its usual route is blocked and it takes a novel shortcut, the rat is representing the layout of the maze or merely running off acquired dispositions. Say which result patterns would favor the representational reading and which would favor the antirepresentationalist reading, and then say precisely what the experiment would and would not settle. In particular, would a successful shortcut establish representation, or would it merely force the antirepresentationalist to redescribe the dispositions, and is there a residue that no behavioral result could reach?
