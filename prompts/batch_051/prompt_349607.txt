Retired physicist here — thirty years babysitting scintillators and drift chambers, and now I've got the philosophy bug, mostly epistemology. There's this business about "easy knowledge" and bootstrapping that I keep going in circles on, so let me lay out the argument the way I'd set it up in the lab and ask you to poke holes in it.

Take an old muon detector in my shed that hasn't been calibrated in years. I switch it on and it logs, say, 500 "muon" events over a month. Here's the reasoning I find myself sliding into:

(1) On each occasion, when the detector reads "muon," a muon (probably) passed through it. I don't think I need an independent calibration run to be justified in that particular belief — if I did, no instrument could ever get its evidential bearings, since every calibration appeals to some other instrument, and so on.
(2) So on occasion 1, the detector said "muon" and a muon passed through. Which is just to say: the detector got occasion 1 right.
(3) By the same logic, it got occasions 2 through 500 right too.
(4) So I've assembled a track record — 500 out of 500 correct — and by induction I can conclude the detector is reliable: its "muon" readings track muons.
(5) Therefore I can become justified in believing the detector is reliable merely by running it and taking its word for it on each seperate occasion.

But that seems too cheap. A detector with a stuck "muon" output would generate the same apparent track record, and I'd be proving a broken instrument reliable by trusting it. Every step looked fine individually, so I half-suspect (1) is doing something sneaky — but if I drop (1), I'm not sure how empirical knowledge ever gets off the ground.

So: is the argument valid? Are the premises true? If it fails, which step fails, and on what reading of (1)? And be clear about your own confidence here: what would change your mind about where the problem is located — say, an argument that closure or evidential transmission works differently than you assumed, or that rule-circular track-record arguments can actually confer justification?
