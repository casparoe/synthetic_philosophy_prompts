Two philosophers have completed a six-month adversarial collaboration about the conditions of salvation — one a soteriological exclusivist (holding that salvation is available only through one religious tradition) and the other a soteriological pluralist (holding that it is available through many). Write the joint statement they could both sign. It should not be a lowest-common-denominator summary: identify the claims both sides can genuinely accept once the dispute is refined by separating doxastic exclusivism from soteriological exclusivism and by separating the bare possibility of salvation outside a given tradition from claims about actual instances of it; state the residual disagreement, phrased so that each side accepts the wording of its own view; identify one crux, a sub-question whose resolution would move at least one side; and specify a concrete kind of case both parties accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format the statement as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The five items should cover, in order: the jointly accepted framing; a second jointly accepted claim; the residual disagreement in each side's accepted wording; the crux; and the probative case with each side's prediction.
