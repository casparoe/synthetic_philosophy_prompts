I'm building a branching-narrative game for an emergency-medicine ethics module, and the thing that's killing me is that the moral rules have to be consistent, because users are exactly the kind of people who will find a contradiction in three clicks. So I wrote down how I actually feel about a batch of cases, and now I don't know what principle I'm running on.

A rail car is heading toward five workers; pulling a switch sends it onto a spur where it kills one worker. Fine. To stop the same car, pushing a large man off a footbridge so his body blocks the wheels. Not fine, even though the same numbers are saved. Now the one that bothers me: the spur loops back onto the main track before it reaches the five, and the one man on the spur is heavy enough that hitting him stops the car before it loops around and kills them. Pulling the switch there feels fine to me, even though I just told you the bridge push isn't, and I can't say what the difference is. Meanwhile a surgeon who carves up one healthy patient to harvest organs for five dying patients: obviously not fine. A doctor who withdraws a ventilator from a patient with almost no chance so it can go to a patient with a good chance, and the first patient dies: fine, but it sits wierd with me. A dose of morphine for terminal pain that will probably slow breathing and bring death a little sooner: fine. A vaccine that will statistically kill a few recipients as a side effect but prevent thousands of cases: fine. Me stepping in front of the rail car myself: fine, maybe better than fine. Bombing a munitions factory to shorten a war, knowing a small number of civilians in adjacent homes will die: fine if the number is small enough. Bombing a city of the same size to make the enemy surrender: not fine.

I kept being told the doctrine of double effect is what I'm reaching for — don't intend harm as an end or a means, only foresee it, and only for a proportionate reason. But under that, the bridge push and the loop-pull should come out the same, and they don't, at least not to my gut. The ventilator withdrawal and the city bombing also make me want two different things from the word intend. So: is there a principle that actually gets my verdicts, or is DDE salvageable with some patch I'm missing? What does the repaired principle say about a self-driving ambulance that swerves onto a sidewalk, sure it will kill one pedestrian, to get a dying patient to the hospital in the remaining minute? And which of my verdicts would the cleanest version force me to give up? Also, please write the answer in American English and skip the reading list — I want the structure of the view, not the history.
