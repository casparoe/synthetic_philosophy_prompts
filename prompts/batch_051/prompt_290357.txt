I am a university student in an epistemology course that recently covered self-locating belief and the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I have been trying to work out a view I would like an honest assessment of.

For context, the version of the problem we covered is this. Beauty is told that a fair coin will be flipped on Sunday night. If it lands heads, she will be woken on Monday, interviewed, and then put back to sleep until Wednesday. If it lands tails, she will be woken on Monday and Tuesday, with a drug erasing her memory of the Monday waking before Tuesday, and then put to sleep until Wednesday. On waking, she knows the whole setup, but cannot tell which day it is or whether she has already been woken. The question is what credence she should assign to the proposition that the coin landed heads: halfers say one half, thirders say one third.

The view I have been developing, which I call the two-index account, is that this question is ambiguous between two different indices. On the first reading, Beauty is asking a world-index question: which coin-outcome world she is in. Her prior over actual worlds is split evenly between heads and tails, and her waking evidence, though it is self-locating, does not discriminate between heads-worlds and tails-worlds considered as worlds; so the correct world-index credence in heads remains one half. On the second reading, she is asking a state-index question: among the protocol's subjectively indistinguishable waking states — Monday-heads, Monday-tails, and Tuesday-tails — which is she currently occupying? Since two of those three states belong to tails-worlds, the correct state-index credence in heads is one third.

My central claim is that halfers and thirders are both right, because they are answering different questions, and that there is no single well-formed quantity picked out by the phrase her credence in heads until an index is specified. I think the ambiguity is easy to miss because for ordinary, non-self-locating propositions the two indices collapse into one. The way I test the idea is with a counting policy: if Beauty adopts the policy of answering tails at every waking, that policy is correct in one half of all experiments but in two thirds of all awakenings. I take both frequencies to be accurate descriptions of her performance over two different partitions, not rivals.

I realize this will sound close to the contextualist or two-question responses to the problem, but I have not found the exact index distinction stated this way. What I would like from you is a candid assessment. Is the world-index/state-index distinction coherent, or does it collapse under pressure? Has someone already defended essentially this view under another name? Most importantly, where does it break? In particular, I worry that my uniform weighting of the three states is simply the thirder's answer imported as an assumption, and that there may be variants of Sleeping Beauty in which the two indices come apart so badly that my account stops being tenable. Am I incoherent anywhere, and what is the strongest objection I am not seeing?
