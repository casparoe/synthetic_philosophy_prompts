I work on a product policy team at a mid-size software company and I review new user-facing features before they ship. The company has a vague rule that any feature has to be one the team could will as a universal law for all similar products — our founder's "Kant rule," which he announced after one philosophy class and never explained. I see about five features a week, I have no philosophy background, the engineers want a yes/no with a written rationale in roughly thirty minutes, and the stakes are medium-high: if I wave through something manipulative it ends up in the news, and if I'm too strict we never ship.

I want you to turn Kant's universalizability test into a procedure I can actually run. I understand there are two parts — one where universalizing the maxim makes the whole practice inconceivable or self-undermining, and one where the universalized world is conceivable but you couldn't rationally will it — and that the hardest part is stating the maxim without cheating. Write me the checklist: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a human because it's a judgment call. Include rules for how to get a maxim out of a product spec — what has to go in it, what level of generality to use, and how to catch the team or me restating the maxim narrower or nobler than the feature actually is, since everyone games the test that way. Then run the procedure on three features: (1) a fake "3 people are viewing this right now" urgency label in a booking flow; (2) a default-on public profile setting with clear disclosure but an easy opt-out buried in settings; and (3) an A/B test where some users get a layout the team already believes is worse, purely to measure how much worse it is. Tell me where the procedure misfires, be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as mechanics, and say what argument or counterexample would make you revise the procedure itself and where you're least sure you've got Kant right.
