I teach a world history survey course and I keep getting stuck on how the syllabus is organized. The course is called world history, but the units run ancient, medieval, early modern, modern — which are basically Europe's own turning points (fall of Rome, Reformation, industrialization) — and the rest of the world gets slotted in around them: Africa before 1500, pre-Columbian America, early modern Japan. If we aren't actually claiming that Europe is the motor of everyone else's history, what are we claiming? Reconstruct the implicit theory of historical time and agency that this organization presupposes. Be precise about what it strictly presupposes versus what it merely fits with, and where it's underdetermined such that several philosophies of history would license it. Then say what would have to change for the course to be told from everywhere at once, or whether that ideal is even coherent. Also be explicit about which consideration would most likely change your verdict about the presuppositions, or where your reconstruction is most fragile.
