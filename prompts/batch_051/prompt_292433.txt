Here's one I want you to think through. It's 2040, and a non-invasive headset can reconstruct silent inner speech at about 90% accuracy. Its cheap (about $150), comfortable enough to wear all day, and a lot of people do, because the same device handles notifications and memory logging. It only records when the wearer switches it on, and it only picks up the running verbal monologue people already put into words in their heads -- not images, not unverbalized intentions. The two companies that make it keep the raw neural data in the cloud and sell 'cognitive analytics' to advertisers, insurers, and police. No country has passed a neurorights law yet, and courts are still treating the data like ordinary consumer data. Nothing else about the world has changed.

Walk me through the next 15 years, but I want mechanisms, not a story. Which developments come first, and why: a black market in stolen thought logs, employers requiring the headset for 'focus monitoring,' insurers pricing premiums off inner-speech data, police trying to use a suspect's silent rehearsing as evidence, something else? For each one, say who has the incentive to push it, what existing law or friction slows it down, and how confident you are in the timing and direction. Then step back: which of these effects are robust across most plausible worlds, and which depend on thin assumptions about adoption rates or early court rulings? What's the single change to the setup that would most alter the trajectory -- on-device processing so the data never leaves the device, a strong legal privilege for inner speech from day one, or the headset never becoming socially normal?

Finally, zoom out. What would 'cognitive liberty' have to mean to do any work that ordinary privacy, free speech, and bodily integrity don't already do? Is it a genuinely new right, or just privacy with a sci-fi sheen?
