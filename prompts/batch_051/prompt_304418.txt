After a long arguement with my friend about why there's something rather than nothing, I want a poem to send to him. He kept offering causes—God, the big bang, the laws of physics—and I kept saying those are all already things, part of the something we were asking about, so the question just moves up a level. He said maybe the question only feels deep because 'nothing' isn't a real alternative. I don't think that's right, but i couldn't say exactly what the question is after. Write a sonnet where the octave is the search for a first cause or first event, the volta is the moment the reader sees that any such thing is itself part of the something, and the closing couplet says what the question has to be asking to keep its grip—not "what made it?" but why there's any contingent thing at all. It should be accurate enough that someone who works on this stuff recognizes the distinction between explaining an item inside the world and asking about the totality, not just a poem that name-drops the question.
