I've sunk a bunch of evenings into a forum argument about group selection that keeps looping: everyone seems to agree about what happens in the models, but they still argue over whether "group selection" is doing any real causal work or is just a redescription. I want to turn this into one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes for my blog, where readers answer questions about cases and get told what they are, without ever checking a box next to a position's name. The existing quizzes I've seen all just ask "are you on Team Dawkins or Team Sober and Wilson?", which sorts by tribal affiliation, not by what people would say.

Could you build me the actual instrument? Eight to ten forced-choice vignettes, each a concrete scenario with two or three answers, phrased so a smart layperson can answer without knowing the jargon. The leaves I want it to sort people into are: a gene's-eye view in the Dawkins/Hull register; a Williams-style individualist who thinks group-level talk is at best harmless shorthand and at worst a confusion; a Sober-and-Wilson multi-level selectionist; a Gould-and-Eldredge species-selectionist; and a deflationary "the whole fight is bookkeeping, replicators and interactors are just two levels of description" position.

The cases should include at minimum: a segregation distorter allele that spreads by biasing meiosis even though it hurts the organism; worker sterility in ants and other eusocial insects; a patch-structured population where cooperative groups export more migrants than selfish groups even though selfishness wins within every patch; and a trait that persists only because species that have it speciate at a higher rate. Add whatever else you need to force the replicator/interactor distinction and the question of whether genuine group-level adaptation—not just group-level differences in output—has to be present before "group selection" is warranted. Work through the logical space first: the claims someone can accept or deny, and which combinations are coherent. Only then place the named positions in that space. Note any empty regions and diagnose each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves your vignettes can't seperate, and say what additional case would split them.

One more constraint: no math beyond maybe an intuitive gloss on fitness as covariance, and the phrase "which position do you hold?" may not appear anywhere in the quiz options.
