Just got back from a long argument with a friend about the paradox of tragedy, and I realized my own verdicts dont actually fit together. Here are the cases as I see them:

- I'll watch a well-made fictional tragedy where a sympathetic character gets destroyed, feel genuinely miserable at the end, and still say I loved it and would rewatch it.
- But I wont voluntarily watch an equally well-made documentary about a real person dying, and if I do, I don't enjoy it, I just feel bad. I don't rewatch footage of real disasters either.
- I sometimes put on a song specifically because it makes me sad, and the sadness feels good. But if I'm already grieving, the same song makes it worse and I skip it.
- I hated a movie where the protagonist suffers for no reason and the ending is just grim and pointless. If the sadness feels meaningful or somehow earned, I can enjoy it; if it feels arbitrary and cruel, I can't.
- A sentimental tearjerker can make me cry more than the subtle tragedy, but I'll say the subtle one was the one I actually enjoyed. So tears don't track enjoyment.

My friend thinks it's all about safety and distance: it's not real, so we get to exercise unpleasant emotions without paying the cost. That fits the first two cases, but not the song case or the arbitrary-ending case, at least to me.

Is there one principle that delivers all these verdicts? If not, which verdicts should I give up or revise, and what does the best principle predict about a fictional tragedy that closely mirrors a painful thing I'm currently going through?
