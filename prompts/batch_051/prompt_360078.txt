I'm helping run a small reading group that's going through the philosophy of colour and someone suggested a session on brain-machine interfaces and "impossible" colour experience. I keep running into the same canned arguments — representationalism, functionalism, the lot — and I'd like a handout that sorts people by what they'll actually say about cases, rather than which label they like.

Could you build me a taxonomy/decision tree for the question: can a direct neural interface produce a phenomenal colour that no ordinary human has ever experienced? Use as the root case a "qualia implant": a device that drives visual cortex in a pattern the eyes and optic nerve can't naturally produce — maybe directly stimulating the opponent-process machinery so the subject swears she's seeing a reddish green, maybe adding a fourth cone-like input channel into the cortical colour system. Each branch should be a claim or case a person can answer without knowing the jargon (would drug-induced colours count as new? if she can't remember or recognise the colour later, did she see it? is her insisting it's reddish green enough?). The leaves should include at least: a no-new-qualia-ever position; a recombination-only position; a forbidden-colours-are-real-but-just-unstable-cancellation-glitches position; a genuinely-new-qualia-are-possible-and-the-implant-would-do-it position; plus whatever else you think is actually occupied. For any combination nobody occupies, give a one-line diagnosis: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Keep it to about a page and structure it like this: (1) The root question and a short audience note; (2) The branch questions, with a sentance each on why they're diagnostic (roughly a third of the page); (3) The tree itself, leaves named or marked "unnamed"; (4) Empty cells with their one-line diagnoses; (5) Where the tree cannot actually separate positions, flagged honestly; (6) A note separating what's settled (say, the basic anatomy of opponent processing) from what's genuinely contested (most of the modal stuff about qualia) — please don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus. Also flag which leaf names are just labels of convenience rather than established positions; I'd rather have that than a fake taxonomy.
