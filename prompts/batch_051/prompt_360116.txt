Suppose that, starting tomorrow, a company starts selling a cheap over-the-counter pill in the United States. Call it Cog. It improves working memory and sustained attention in healthy adults by roughly one standard deviation on standard cognitive tests, its side effects are about as serious as a strong cup of coffee, and — this is the weird part — it does nothing at all for people with ADHD, dementia, or other diagnosed cognitive deficits; nobody understands why. Nothing else changes. Predict, with reasoning, how the therapy/enhancement distinction in medicine and law gets renegotiated over the next twenty years. Which institutions move first (drug regulators, insurers, schools, employers, the military, sports bodies), what arguments do they reach for, and where does the line between treating a deficit and improving the healthy end up? I want the causal chain, not just a story: which pressures push toward treating Cog as medicine, which push toward treating it as a consumer product, and how does the fact that it provably doesn't help sick people change the usual debate? Tell me which predictions you're most and least confident in, and what would have to be true for the distinction to quietly collapse rather than harden.
