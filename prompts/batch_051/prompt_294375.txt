I've been reading Augustine on lying, especially the part in the Enchiridion where he says no lie is ever allowable, even to save another person from injury, but that lies differ a lot in guilt depending on the intention and the subject matter. I half-buy the absolutism and half-suspect it's a definitional trick. So here's the position I want pressure-tested: every knowingly false assertion made with intent to deceive is a sin, full stop; some are much graver than others, but none is morally indifferent or permissible, even the lie told to a would-be murderer at the door. What would count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, including cases that have arguably already happened or that a defender of this view would themselves concede as awkward, plus an honest assessment of whether the view has been insulated by qualifications like "you can evade without properly lying" or "that's not a lie, just a false signification." If it has been insulated that way, is that a problem for a moral view of this kind?
