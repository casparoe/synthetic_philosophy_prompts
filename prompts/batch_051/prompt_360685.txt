I have been arguing with people in my reading group about actual infinities, and I have noticed an asymmetry in how many of us handle the same concept across domains. Most of the group is happy to do standard set theory: they agree that the natural numbers form a completed set, that Cantor's diagonal argument shows there are more reals than naturals, and that Hilbert's hotel is a valid thought experiment about denumerable infinity. But when the conversation turns to whether the physical universe could contain an actually infinite number of past events, or whether an actually infinite collection of concrete objects could exist, several of the same people suddenly become finitists. They say completed infinities are legitimate idealizations in mathematics but physically impossible, or at least deeply suspicious. The asymmetry is this: we are willing to treat an actually infinite collection as a well-defined object for mathematical purposes, but we refuse to accept that the same kind of collection could be instantiated outside mathematics, even though the mathematical theory is supposed to constrain what is possible in any domain where it applies.

I want to know whether anything justifies that split, or whether it is just sociological comfort with textbook objects. Some candidate justifications I have considered: first, physical actual infinities would violate the principle that every physical state must be reachable by finite steps; second, mathematics does not assert existence in the same sense as physics, so no asymmetry needs defending; third, the paradoxes of infinitary reasoning are tolerable in mathematics but reveal genuine incoherence in concrete actuality. The candidate I find hardest to dismiss is that our confidence in infinite mathematics is really about formal manipulation of symbols, whereas physical actual infinities would force us to make sense of completed totalities in nature. But I am not sure that is a principle rather than a restatement of the asymmetry.

Please give me the strongest principle that could justify treating completed infinities as permissible in mathematics while impossible or highly suspect in concrete reality, then test it against nearby cases: an actually infinite past, an infinite spatial lattice, a universe with infinitely many planets, an infinitely divisible object. Tell me whether the asymmetry should be kept, given up, or reversed, and what would make you change your mind.
