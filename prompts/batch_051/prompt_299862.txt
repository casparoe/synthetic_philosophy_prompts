What is pseudohistory? The label gets applied to a varied set of works, and I would like a working definition precise enough to sort the following cases. I am fairly confident that a scholarly monograph that turns out to be wrong is not, merely for that reason, pseudohistory, and that a novel set in the past is not pseudohistory merely because it invents dialogue; but I cannot say where the boundary falls or which criterion does the real work.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

1. A bestseller that argues the pyramids, Stonehenge and the Nazca Lines were built with extraterrestrial help, treating ancient myth and the scale of the monuments as evidence while ignoring or dismissing the peer-reviewed archaeological literature.

2. The New Chronology associated with Anatoly Fomenko, which holds that most of ancient and medieval history was fabricated or misplaced, that written sources were forged on a vast scale, and that the standard timeline survives only because of institutional inertia; its proponents carry out quantitative analyses of astronomical references, but respond to contrary evidence by claiming the evidence itself was forged.

3. A professionally curated foundation gallery in a national museum that tells the nation's origin story by merging legend with documented history and omitting the displacement of neighbouring peoples, though the curators know the fuller record.

4. A historical novel that invents a speech for a real king at a real battle, while preserving the main documented events.

5. An amateur local historian who argues against expert consensus that Romans reached a particular island, citing a handful of anomalous finds; she publishes her measurements, acknowledges the standard objections, and proposes an excavation that could falsify her claim.

6. A television programme that presents the Atlantis story as a memory of the Bronze Age island of Thera and the Minoan civilisation, while explicitly noting that this is one hypothesis among several and that most specialists doubt it.

7. A counterfactual history that asks what would have happened if the Spanish Armada had landed, and labels itself as speculation throughout.

8. A propaganda pamphlet that recounts a recent massacre by quoting authentic documents selectively and out of context so that one side appears blameless.

I suspect a good definition will have to separate at least three things: whether the thesis is false, whether the method is defective, and whether the author is sincere; but I am not sure these can be cleanly pulled apart. Please propose the definition, run it through all eight cases, state where it gives a verdict you did not expect or no verdict at all, and then revise or defend it accordingly.
