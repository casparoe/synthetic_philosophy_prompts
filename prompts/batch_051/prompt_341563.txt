I'm a portfolio manager at a small pension fund and I've been going down a rabbit hole on Keynes's writing about probability. The way I read him, a lot of what finance treats as measurable risk isn't really risk in his sense, because there's no stable distribution to measure against — it's uncertainty. And seperately, a probability can look high while the weight of evidence behind it is thin, so you should be less willing to act on it than the number suggests. I want to turn this into a pre-trade checklist my analysts can run. We make maybe ten allocation decisions a week, each gets about 20 minutes of scrutiny, none of the analysts have a philosophy background, the CIO wants a written escalation trail, and it's retirees' money, so I need something they'll actually use.

The checklist should force them to classify each input number: is this a well-grounded probability or a dressed-up guess, and is the weight of evidence enough to act on even when the point estimate looks good. It should also flag where the procedure stops and a human has to judge. Work through two examples: one where a model says 85% confidence in a trade but the checklist says to cut the position, and one where a number is basically unmeasurable but the checklist still has to let us act anyway. Then give the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields: `steps` (an ordered array of objects, each with `question` for what the analyst asks, `decision_rule` for what to do with the answer, and `judgment_call` which is true if the step can't be fully mechanized), `common_failure_modes` (an array of strings about where the checklist will misfire), `worked_examples` (an array of two objects, each with `case` and `output`), and `limitations` (an array of strings for what the procedure cannot decide).
