Recently my partner and I went through IVF, and the arguments we had about which embryos to transfer—and later what to do with the ones we did not use—reopened an old metaphysics question I could not let go of: could a given person have come from a different sperm and egg? I run a small philosophy blog whose readers have usually taken one introductory metaphysics course but are not specialists, and I want to turn this into a quiz. Please write a "which view of origin essentialism do you actually hold?" quiz for me, with eight to ten questions and a scoring key. Approach it as a professional metaphysician working on modality and the metaphysics of persons. Think carefully about the distinctions first, brainstorm several candidate diagnostic questions, and keep the ones that most sharply separate the positions, so the quiz sorts people by what they say about cases rather than by which label they like. Each question should give a concrete case with two or three answer options and no jargon. At least the following cases must appear: the same egg fertilized by a different sperm from the same father; monozygotic twinning, where one zygote splits into two embryos; a clone produced by somatic cell nuclear transfer from the quiz-taker's own cell; the quiz-taker's original gametes combined in vitro twenty years earlier than they actually were; and an IVF embryo frozen for ten years before transfer. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these leaves: Kripkean gamete essentialism (the very sperm and egg are necessary), zygote essentialism (the zygote, not merely its component gametes, is necessary), a genome-based view (what matters is the genetic sequence), parental essentialism (only one's parents are necessary), a dualist view on which the same soul could animate a different body, an anti-essentialist counterpart view with no strict origin requirement, and a view on which you begin only after the embryo can no longer split into twins. After the key, note any answer combinations that no named position occupies and say briefly whether each is incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored. Finally, flag any two leaves the quiz cannot really separate and explain why.
