I've been reading lately about how you could study consciousness scientifically, and I keep coming back to heterophenomenology: the idea that scientists should treat what subjects say about their experience as reports to be explained, not as automatically true descriptions of a private inner world. I'm drawn to it because it gives you public data and a clean method, and it stops you from just taking people's word for what their minds are like, which would make the whole thing unscientific. But I also have an intuition I can't shake. When I burn my hand, my pain is not just the fact that I would report feeling pain. It seems like I know my own experience in a way no outside observer ever could, and the method asks us to treat everyone, including ourselves, as if we could only ever study the outside of there own minds. I don't know which one has to go. Is the intuition that we have direct knowledge of our own experience just a leftover from a bad picture of the mind that I should drop? Or does heterophenomenology quietly deny something it claimed to stay neutral about? Or are they actually compatible because the method is just about collecting data, not about what experience really is?
