I run a small philosophy discussion group made up mostly of people who came to the subject through rationalist and LessWrong-adjacent blogs, and over the past few weeks we have been reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty. The live argument among us is whether the commitments the book keeps circling — that the world is more than five minutes old, that memory is broadly reliable, that Moore's gesture of holding up a hand displays knowledge — are a different kind of thing from ordinary well-supported belief, or just very stubborn ordinary beliefs. It is the sort of debate in which people talk past each other because they are answering different underlying questions, and I would like to build a sorting quiz for the group blog: 'which hinge epistemology do you actually hold?' It should sort readers by what they say about cases, never by which label or author they prefer.

Please make it either a decision tree or a quiz of seven to nine concrete forced-choice vignettes with a scoring key. Each diagnostic question should be a case in which the positions genuinely come apart: being challenged to prove you have hands; a friend who denies that the future will resemble the past and asks what grounds your confidence; waking from a fever dream and deciding whether to trust a vivid memory that would be convenient as an alibi; an experimental result that conflicts with a well-established theory, where you must choose between treating it as measurement error and revising the theory; someone who denies modus ponens and invites you to argue with them without using it; a flat-earth-style disagreement in which there is no shared base of evidence. The answer options should be phrased so a reader can choose without knowing the positions, but so the answers reveal the underlying commitments: whether a hinge is truth-evaluable, whether evidence could support or undermine it or whether it is presupposed by anything that counts as evidence, whether genuine doubt of it is a possible state or only a verbal performance, and whether it holds universally or only within a world-picture. Build the branches from those questions first, then locate the familiar positions as occupied leaves.

The leaves I want included should be stated through their commitments, with standard labels only where they are stable: hinges as animal or action-based certainties rather than believed propositions; hinges as propositions we are rationally entitled to trust without evidence; hinges as rules or norms that set standards of evidence rather than claims about the world; hinges as empirical certainties that simply have overwhelming justification; and hinges as framework-relative commitments that cannot be questioned from outside a world-picture. For combinations that nobody occupies, say whether they are empty because they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two positions the instrument cannot separate with cases alone. Then commit: choose the final question set, the wording of the options, the scoring scheme, and the placement of the leaves, and give your reasons for those choices. Be explicit about which distinctions the quiz is too coarse to capture and where a short debrief note on the group blog should correct for that.
