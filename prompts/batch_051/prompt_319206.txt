I'm in a course on Confucian thought, and one view keeps following me around outside class. The version of Confucian role ethics we read says there isn't a "me" underneith my roles — no chooser who exists first and then picks up relationships; I just am my roles and relationships (daughter, sister, student, friend, neighbor), and living well is about cultivating and fulfilling them well. This started feeling true this year, when my mom got sick and "being a good daughter" felt less like a decision I make and more like something I already am, with claims on me I never chose.

But when I tried to press the view, I ended up with an argument that seems to prove too much:

1. There is no self behind my roles; I just am the roles I inhabit.
2. If there is no self behind the roles, then there's no position outside them from which I can step back and assess them.
3. If there's no position outside them, then questions like "should I stay in this role?" or "does this role ask too much of me?" can't really be asked — the role is the frame within which any questioning happens.
4. So someone who cuts off an abusive parent, or leaves a toxic friendship, or walks away from a community that demanded too much, could never be responding to the genuine merits of the question "should I end this?"; at best they'd be acting from some other role or impulse, not from any judgment that the role should end.
5. But that can't be right; people sometimes correctly judge that a role should end, and that judgment is exactly what guides them.
6. So either the picture in 1 is false, or one of the moves from 1 to 4 fails.

I'd really like to keep 1, because it explains the un-chosen, binding quality of what I owe my mom better than any "I picked these values" story I've ever heard. But I can't see how to keep 1 without ending up at 5. Where does the argument go wrong, if it does? If I have to reject a step, which one should it be, and what do I lose by rejecting it?
