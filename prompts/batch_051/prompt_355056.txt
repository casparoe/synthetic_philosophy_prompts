I am a causal-inference researcher and I think most naturally in the vocabulary of structural causal models: Pearl's three rungs of association, intervention, and counterfactual; modular structural equations; the do-operator; exogenous noise; and the fact that many distinct SCMs are observationally equivalent. My knowledge of Hume is secondhand, so treat me as fluent in the target vocabulary but only roughly familiar with the source material.

Please restate the following three readings of Hume on causation, one by one, in that vocabulary:
(1) the classical regularity reading, on which causation is nothing more than constant conjunction plus the mind's felt determination to pass from one idea to another;
(2) the projectivist reading, on which necessary connection is a projection of our inferential habits onto a world that contains only regularities;
(3) the New Hume, or skeptical-realist, reading, on which Hume accepted that there are real necessary connections among objects, or hidden powers, while denying that we can know their nature in any way beyond their observed effects.

For each reading, I want a translation that does not flatter the target vocabulary: say what corresponds in SCM terms to constant conjunction, necessary connection, hidden powers, and the contrast between connection in the objects and connection in the mind. Mark where the translation is exact, where it distorts the source, and where the source claim simply has no expression in SCM terms. Be especially precise about two things. First, whether the best SCM analogue of a necessary connection is the structural equation itself, its invariance under intervention, or something in the exogenous variables. Second, whether observational equivalence among SCMs strengthens or weakens the New Hume claim that necessity is real but unknowable.

Then give the residue report. What is left over in each of the three readings after the translation, and does that residue indicate a genuine question my vocabulary cannot pose, or does it indicate that some part of the Humean dispute is less substantive than it appears? I am not asking for an introductory lecture; I want the translation and the residue, kept honest.
