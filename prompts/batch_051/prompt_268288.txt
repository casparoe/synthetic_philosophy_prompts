I spent thirty years in a physical chemistry group, so I've watched my own corner of science go from "why would anyone study that" to standard industrial practice. Since I retired I've been reading philosophy of science and the debates about whether universities should keep funding "useless" fields—philology, pure set theory, historical musicology, taxonomy of orchid bees, that sort of thing. I keep running into the same few moves: the lottery-ticket argument (number theory looked useless until cryptography needed it) and the line that knowledge is valuable for its own sake, which doesn't say which knowledge and doesn't justify keeping a field going on it's own terms.

I don't want a position yet. I want the spec first. Specifically: what does a theory of the value of unusual scholarship have to get right to count as adequate? Adequate for what: for a university deciding where cuts should land when money is tight—not for answering whether there should be ideas at all.

To keep this from getting abstract, the list should be tested against six cases I suspect will pull theories apart: taxonomy of orchid bees; Hittite phonology; pure set theory (large cardinals); medieval Icelandic scribal practice; the luminiferous ether as a research program after 1905; and a currently useful lab with no doctoral pipeline yet. Add a case or two of your own if they'd force a requirement the list otherwise misses.

Structure the response in these sections, in this order, with rough lengths:

1. Ground rules (about 150 words): fix what counts as "useless" for this purpose, and restate the purpose in your own words.
2. The requirements (about 500 words): a numbered list; for each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it.
3. Negotiable vs. non-negotiable (about 200 words): sort the list into criteria any adequate theory must respect, versus criteria that only one camp cares about.
4. Tensions (about 250 words): identify pairs or larger subsets of requirements that pull against each other; say whether you think any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and why.
5. Most discriminating requirement (about 100 words): name the single criterion that does the most to knock out the defenses people actually give.

And please don't turn this into a scorecard for the existing defenses—I'll do that myself later. I want the criteria and the reasoning behind them.
