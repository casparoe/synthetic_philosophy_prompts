A small city's cultural commission makes grants under a bylaw adopted in 1798 by a committee that expressly modelled its policy on Rousseau's Letter to M. d'Alembert on the Theatre. The committee understood Rousseau to have argued that the moral improvement theatre appears to offer is largely illusory: the pity or admiration stirred in a spectator is a transient and self-regarding passion, spent during the performance and accepted in place of the active duties one owes to real neighbours, so that a sound republic should prefer public festivals in which citizens are themselves the spectacle. The bylaw's operative clause reads:

"No grant of public funds shall be made to any spectacle that assembles citizens as passive spectators and excites in them a fleeting pity or admiration, which they accept in place of the active duties they owe to one another, or that makes private vice pleasing to its audience."

The commission must now decide an application for a grant to an installation of a kind the drafters did not imagine. Visitors enter singly, put on a headset that plays filmed, artfully edited testimonies of local residents recovering from addiction, and are then brought together with those residents for a facilitated discussion. The applicants argue that there is no stage, no dramatic performance, and no fixed passive audience, and that the subsequent discussion turns the visitor into a participant. Opponents reply that the film is constructed to wring pity from each visitor, that the headset isolates visitors rather than bringing citizens together, and that the discussion does not undo the primary experience.

Does the clause, read in light of its Rousseauian purpose, forbid this grant? Argue it (i) from the wording of the clause, (ii) from the purpose of the clause as an enactment of Rousseau's critique of theatre, and (iii) from what the 1798 committee would most plausibly have said if asked about recorded moving images and facilitated post-viewing discussion. Then say which of these approaches should control the decision, and why.

Limit your answer to 500 words.
