In personal life, self-sufficiency is usually praised as the condition of not depending on other people's choices for what one needs; autarky is supposed to be the same ideal scaled up to states, and I have been trying to determine whether it can be stated precisely enough to be a serious political ideal rather than a slogan. For the sake of argument, I have settled on four criteria. A country is genuinely autarkic only if it satisfies all of the following. First, it can supply from domestic production the goods its population needs to survive at a tolerable standard of living: basic food, water, energy, and basic medicines. Second, it can keep doing so for an extended period if ordinary international trade were disrupted. Third, it is not vulnerable to coercive pressure from other states through their control of goods that are essential to its economy or its security. Fourth, it possesses, and can continue to reproduce, the domestic skills, institutions, and infrastructure on which that production depends, rather than merely holding stockpiles against an emergency.

Now consider a mid-sized, wealthy island nation that is clearly self-sufficient in staple food and electricity, holds large reserves of fuel and grain, and manufactures most of its own generic medicines, so it plainly satisfies the first and second criteria. But its advanced economy, military, hospitals, and communications networks depend almost entirely on imported advanced semiconductors, rare-earth components, and a small number of specialized biologics. The country operates fabrication plants that produce older chip generations, but no domestic firm designs the most advanced chips it uses, and it is technically disputed whether its air-defense and financial systems could run, even in degraded form, on the older chips in a crisis. In a prolonged embargo, the country would not starve or freeze, but many of its systems would work poorly or not at all, its standard of living would fall, and it might be unable to defend itself as it currently does. Is this country autarkic by the four criteria? Work through the four criteria one by one; where a criterion does not settle the verdict, say whether the difficulty lies in the criterion or in the case. Conclude by saying whether the case shows that any of the criteria should be revised — for example, whether the third criterion's reference to goods essential to its economy or security reaches too far or not far enough, or whether autarky should be understood as a matter of degree rather than a threshold. Please answer in American English.
