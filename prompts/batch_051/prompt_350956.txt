Here is the practice I want you to unpack. In wetland mitigation banking, which exists in many jurisdictions, a developer who destroys a wetland can satisfy a legal requirement of no net loss by purchasing credits from a bank that restored or created a wetland somewhere else, sometimes far away. The restored site is then managed long-term, and the credits are treated as offsetting the original loss. Carbon offsets work in a broadly similar way: an emitter pays for a project elsewhere that avoids or stores carbon, and the avoided or stored tonnage is counted against its own emissions. In both cases, for legal and regulatory purposes, a harm in one place and time is canceled by a benefit of a roughly similar kind in another place and time, produced by a different party.

What theory of value and wrongness does this practice presuppose? More specifically: does it strictly presuppose that the goods in question are fungible, or can it be reconstructed on a view according to which a particular wetland, with its place in a watershed and its history, is non-substitutable? Does it presuppose a consequentialist picture on which harms and benefits are entries in a ledger that can be summed to neutrality, or does it require only a weaker form of comparability? Is the practice consistent with the same societies' refusal to extend offsetting logic to other domains, such as workplace deaths, bodily injury, or discrimination, where paying someone else to do good is not treated as canceling the wrong? Reconstruct the implicit axiology as precisely as the practice supports, distinguish what it strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several rival theories could license it. Then consider the safeguards built into these systems, such as requirements for additionality, permanence, and some degree of ecological similarity or geographic proximity; do those safeguards quietly betray a rival theory, one on which the two sites are not straightforwardly interchangeable after all?
