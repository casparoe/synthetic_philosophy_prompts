So here's a counterfactual I keep turning over. In 2035 a lab has what they call total interpretability: a transformer that, given the weights and a specific input of any other model up to its own parameter count, produces a correct, human-auditable explanation of why that model output what it did. It's been verified on thousands of smaller models where the true mechanism was known, and it holds up. Mostly as a stress test, they point it at itself: same weights, same input format, explaining its own explanation-producing computation token by token. Give me your reasoned prediction of how that plays out. Does the recursion bottom out, or does "explaining itself" collapse into something circular, like printing a faithful-but-unauditable trace of its own weights? Is the self-explanation something you should trust the same way you trust its explanations of smaller models? What breaks first when they aim it at slightly larger models, or at a copy fine-tuned to be a slightly better interpreter? I want the mechanism chain, not a story: which constraints do the real work — information-theoretic, computational, self-referential, or just our inability to check the output — and what would have to be true for it to go better than you expect. Tell me which of your predictions you're most confident about, too.
