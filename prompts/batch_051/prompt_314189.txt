I've been going back and forth on what "fitness" even means in evolutionary biology, and I keep hitting the tautology problem: if fitness just is actual reproductive success, then "the fittest survive" is a definition, not a claim about the world. The usual fix is to make fitness a probabilistic propensity — expected offspring, a disposition to survive and reproduce — but then it starts sounding like a property of our model rather then the organism. I want a working definition that threads that needle. It shouldn't be tautological; it should stay a property of the organism (or genotype or trait), not just the modeler's uncertainty; and it should give a definite verdict on each of these: (1) a cheetah genotype whose only bearers all die in a mudslide before reproducing; (2) two clonal fish with the same genome, one of which gets lucky and leaves ten offspring and the other none; (3) a flashier male-bird trait that raises matings but also predation; (4) a neutral allele drifting up in frequency in a small population; (5) a sterile worker ant. Give the definition, run it through all five, and flag the places where it gives a surprising answer or where you had to quietly redefine the word.
