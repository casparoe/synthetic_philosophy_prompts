After forty years working in evolutionary ecology I've drifted into philosophy of mind, and the meme side of things keeps bothering me. People write as if a meme is a thing that sits inside a host — "the birthday tune is in your head," that sort of talk — and gets passed along when the host performs it. But when I look at what is actually going on, I can't find the meme stored anywhere. The tune isn't in my head as a tune; what's in their is some sort of neural disposition or trace, and the tune itself only exists during the moments somebody hums or whistles it. So I've started thinking of culture as made of what I call pulses. A pulse is an event in which a meme is actually realized — the joke gets told, the hand gesture gets made, the tune gets whistled — and the "meme" is just the type under which all these pulses fall. Nothing is copied, strictly speaking, except one pulse causing a later pulse of the same type through perception, memory, and motor routines. Between pulses there is no meme, only a stored disposition that I'd compare to a seed bank or a dormant virus rather than a living population.

I call this the pulse account of memes, and it seems to earn its keep: it predicts that a meme with long dormant periods should accumulate fewer copying errors than one that cycles constantly, since the stored trace isn't doing much while it sits there; it predicts that transmission fidelity should track the pulse-to-pulse interval rather than total time "inside a host"; and it says a meme that is only remembered but never performed is already extinct, even if the host still carries it. But I'm also aware I may have just announced a relabeling and dressed it up as science. Give me an honest assessment. Does this collapse into the ordinary host-centered view with extra jargon, or does it split off real phenomena? What would the standard memetic picture say back to me? And give me one cultural change my account handles worse than the usual one, and one it handles better — I want to see where it actually breaks down rather than just hearing that it's unorthodox.
