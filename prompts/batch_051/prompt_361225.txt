I am attracted to the view that epistemic dependence on AI is just a further stage of ordinary cognitive offloading: if the human-plus-tool system is reliable and the human is competent in its use, then the resulting beliefs can count as knowledge, and it is a mistake to assess the human's cognitive standing apart from the system. This view is appealing because it gives a unified treatment of testimony, calculators, written records, and machine learning systems, and avoids romanticising unaided cognition. Yet I also have a firm intuition that a scientist who obtains a correct, publishable result by querying an opaque model, and who cannot explain why the result holds even after the fact, does not understand the relevant phenomenon, no matter how skilfully she frames the query and screens the output. I do not want to give up either judgement. Please lay out the strongest version of each side and then give me a negotiated verdict: should the reliabilist picture be amended by an explicit understanding condition, should the intuition be debunked as failing to track anything epistemically important, or is there a reading on which knowledge without understanding is the right description, and if so why should we care? Say what would justify each move rather than simply picking one. Answer in British English.
