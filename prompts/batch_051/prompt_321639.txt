I help run a small online philosophy discussion group, and we have a recurring problem: a flood of extremely confident, low-effort posts from people who have clearly read half a Wikipedia article and now want to refute some position they only just heard of. I recently read about the handicap principle — the idea that animal signals stay honest because they are expensive to fake (costly signaling), like a peacock's tail or a gazelle stotting — and it gave me an idea for a moderation rule.

The proposal, which we are calling the ornament rule, works like this. Any top-level post that claims to refute or debunk a named view must be accompanied by a handwritten transcription (minimum 500 words) of the exact passage from the primary text that the post is criticizing. The poster uploads a photo of the handwritten pages, but the transcription itself is never shown in the thread: it is purely a costly hoop, not part of the argument. Moderators spot-check the transcription against a digital copy and delete the post if it is fake or looks machine-generated. The rationale is that only people who genuinely have something serious to say will pay the cost, the same way only a healthy peacock can afford to grow a huge tail. We expect it to filter out trolls and drive-by hot takes without actually censoring any substantive argument. My co-moderator thinks it is just a poll tax on participation and will select for people with to much free time, but she couldn't say exactly what it would do to the community, so we are going back and forth.

Act as an expert on signaling theory and the design of online institutions, and think carefully before answering. Start by laying out what this rule actually selects for, and whether the peacock analogy is doing real work or just decorating the proposal. Then walk me through who games it first, what the forum looks like after six months (who stays, who leaves, what kind of posts dominate), and whether the failure modes could be fixed with tweaks like variable transcription lengths, requiring the transcription to be done in one sitting on video, or making the transcription part of the actual post. Be specific; I care less about whether the rule is morally fair than about how it would actually behave in practice.
