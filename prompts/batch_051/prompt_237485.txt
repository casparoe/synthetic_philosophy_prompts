I am a graduate student in formal epistemology, and I have built an argument for a seminar that now strikes me as suspicious because its conclusion is stronger than the mathematics seems to warrant.

(1) The standard Bayesian convergence results say that, for two agents who share a hypothesis space, whose priors are mutually absolutely continuous (that is, they assign zero prior probability to exactly the same hypotheses), and who observe an indefinitely long sequence of identifiable, independent data from the true hypothesis, the agents' posterior distributions both converge to the truth. In short, priors wash out.

(2) But substantive disagreements are rarely of that form. Take two agents whose models have different support. Agent A puts all prior mass on a low-dimensional family of hypotheses, say linear models. Agent B puts positive prior mass on a much larger space that contains A's family and much more, say all smooth functions. The truth lies outside A's family. Then their priors are mutually singular, not mutually absolutely continuous.

(3) By the conditions in (1), the convergence theorem does not apply to A and B.

(4) So the theorem gives no guarantee that A and B will come to agree. And there is reason to think they will not: A will concentrate on the best linear approximation to the truth, while B can converge to the truth itself, leaving A wrong indefinitely no matter how much data arrives.

(5) Therefore the convergence results cover only cases in which the agents already agree about which possibilities have positive prior probability, which is exactly the hard part of most substantive disagreement.

(6) So the usual gloss that Bayesian priors wash out and that rational inquirers will eventually converge is empty in the cases that matter.

I would like to keep (1) as a faithful statement of the theorems, and I do not see how to deny (2) as a description of many real disputes. But (6) feels like it overstates. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If no single step is false, tell me exactly what the argument does establish and what weaker claim survives. Please write your answer in American English.
