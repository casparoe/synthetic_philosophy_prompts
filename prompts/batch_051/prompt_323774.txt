Consider the standard argument that cooperation can evolve in the iterated prisoner's dilemma through reciprocal strategies such as tit-for-tat, and that this shows how cooperation can emerge without central enforcement. The argument rests on the assumption that a player can recognize a past co-player and condition on the memory of that co-player's previous cooperation or defection. Now embed the same strategic situation in online platforms, where each user carries a cheap, public, persistent reputation score generated by other users' ratings—ratings that are noisy, inflated by reciprocal five-star norms, and occasionally fabricated by interested parties. Does the argument still establish what it claimed to establish? Identify which premise the new setting bears on, and state what, if anything, survives in a repaired version.
