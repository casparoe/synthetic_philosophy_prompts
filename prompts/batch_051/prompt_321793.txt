for the end of a unit on burkean conservatism and chesterton's fence, i want a closing poem, a sonnet (any rhyme scheme you like, but it needs a genuine volta). the octave should be a moderniser's voice: they come on an old fence across a path, it looks arbitrary, it slows everyone down, no one remembers what it was for, and pulling it down feels like progress not vandalism. the sestet should turn on the idea that this very impression of pointlessness is doing too much work — that not knowing the reason is not the same as there being no reason, and that the fence is a sort of stored prudence, a burkean arangement that can be wiser than any of the people now looking at it. don't quote chesterton, but work in the principle that you shouldn't remove a fence until you know why it went up, and also complicate it: some fences really are just fences and should come down, so the principle has to be about the order of justification, not about keeping everything. end with the moderniser paused, not converted.
