Draft the joint statement of an adverserial collaboration between two people who disagree about what makes someone complicit in another's wrongdoing. Call them the causalist and the participationist. The causalist holds that you're only complicit if your act made some difference to the principal wrong (perhaps only by raising its probability); the participationist holds that what matters is deliberately joining in, lending your will, or endorsing the enterprise, even if your contribution was never used. Structure the statement in three parts: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, non-vacuous and unslanted; (2) a disagreements table, each row stating a contested claim in wording each side would accept for their own view; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that answering them would move at least one side — plus the cases both agree bear on them. Cover at least these four: a lookout whose warning is never needed; a second getaway driver who turns up after an already-clean escape; a shareholder whose one vote couldn't have changed a firm's toxic-dumping decision; and an encourager shouting from a hiding place so the attacker never hears. Flag any disagreements you judge to be partly verbal, and note the largest concession each side must make to keep the document honest.
