For a while now I've been sitting in on a running argument in our department between the microhistory and history-from-below people on one side and the people who work on states, institutions, elites, and long-run structures on the other, and its starting to feel like the debate has settled into a permanent stalemate. The microhistory side says the right single case, a sixteenth-century miller whose strange cosmology survives only because an Inquisition notary wrote it down, can open up a whole mental world that serial data would never give you. The structural side says that is precisely the problem: one unusual person, preserved by a repressive institution because he was being prosecuted, tells you nothing about the millions who left no trace, and treating him as evidence about a population is not just unrepresentative but something like a category mistake. They also add that the archive itself is a state artifact, so the history-from-below project is always reading through the state's categories even when it tries to read against the grain. Both sides agree the miller was unusual. Both agree the source was produced by power. Both agree no aggregate record could have captured him. But one side says the exception shows the limits of what was thinkable, and the other says an exception without a denominator is just an anecdote, and the conversation ends there every time. I suspect this is not really a disagreement about evidence in the ordinary sense, because I cannot imagine what document would change either side's mind. But I can't tell wether it is a disagreement about what history is for, about what counts as an explanation, about representativeness as a standard of proof, or whether the two sides are just answering different questions under the same word, history. What kind of disagreement is this actually? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress, or should we expect the standoff to persist because the two projects are both legitimate and not measuring themselves against the same goal? I'd rather not get a verdict on which side is right; I want the anatomy of the standoff. And please no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just write it through in continuous prose like an email.
