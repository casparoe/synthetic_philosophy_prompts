Student answer: "Mises argued that without factor markets there are no prices for capital goods, so planners cannot rationally compare different methods of production. He is usually taken to be making an information-processing point: the planner would need to solve an enormous system of simultaneous equations describing all production possibilities, and this cannot be done in practice, so socialism is impossible. Hayek's contribution was to shift the issue from computation to knowledge. The data needed for planning exists only in dispersed, local form across millions of agents, and no central board can gather it. Lange's market socialism responds to Mises directly. The Central Planning Board sets prices for producer goods by trial and error, raising them when there are shortages and lowering them when there are surpluses, while managers follow two rules: minimize average cost and produce where marginal cost equals price. If the trial-and-error process converges, it supplies the scarcity values planners need without actual markets in capital goods. Hayek's remaining objection is that the board still has to know the local circumstances and production possibilities in order to run the rules properly, so the dispersed-knowledge point still cuts. Thus Lange meets Mises's challenge but not Hayek's."
