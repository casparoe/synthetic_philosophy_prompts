Two philosphers who've been talking past each other for a decade have agreed to write up an adversarial collaboration instead of another public debate. One holds the Kantian line on human dignity: every person has an unconditional worth that isn't reducible to their capacities, interests, or usefulness, and this worth is what grounds both the equal status of persons and the shape of their rights. The other holds the skeptical bioethics line: dignity does no independent normative work, and where it seems to it's a vauge stand-in for respect for autonomy or a cover for some other commitment nobody wants to defend out loud. They've promised a single document, not a debate: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims—substantive enough that neither would call it a word-cloud, taking in what they both think dignity-talk actually does in arguments and which cases they handle the same way; then (2) a disagreements table, where each row gives the exact residual disagreement, with each side's own position worded so that side would sign it; the crux, meaning the sub-question whose answer would make at least one of them change a substantive view; and where possible a test, case, or argument both accept as bearing on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded before the result is in. Anchor everything in two cases they've both agreed are live: a competent adult who sells the right to be humiliated in private (spat on, insulted, made to crawl), and a poor but competent adult who wants to sell a kidney. If they don't actually part ways on those cases, the document has to say where they would part ways and why these don't do it. If some chunk of their fight was only ever verbal, say which chunk and why.
