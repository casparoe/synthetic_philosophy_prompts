Something about the old sola scriptura disputes has been bugging me since a seminar on Reformation theology, and I'm after a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a ruling on the doctrine. The way I understand it, one side says Scripture is perspicuous: whatever you actually need for salvation is stated clearly enough that an ordinary reader can get it, and the obscure passages should be read in the light of the clear ones. So no external judge is required; the text, read as a whole, interprets itself. The other side says this is exactly the inference you can't draw from the fact that the text exists. Perspicuity is supposed to settle interpretation, but the historical record is endless division among people who all accept it — over infant baptism, the real presence, predestination, church governance. So something has to break the tie, and that something looks an awful lot like a living interpretive authority; otherwise you're left with everyone having a private opinion, Bible in hand but no shared reading. Then the first side turns around and says this just moves the regress up a level: the authority's rulings also have to be interpreted and applied, so deciding whether you're following that authority correctly is just another private judgment. At that point each side is wielding the identical regress argument against the other, and neither can explain why the other's stopping-rule isn't arbitrary.

What I'm after is a structural account of why this debate has stalled. Is it a substantive disagreement about what a text plus good hermeneutics can actually pin down, or is it mostly verbal, hinging on what 'clear' or 'interpretation' or 'authority' are being asked to do? Are the two sides answering different questions — one about semantic content and one about social coordination, say — under the single heading of 'who decides what the text means?' Or is it a clash of starting intuitions about what counts as a legitimate place to stop asking for reasons? My hunch is that the disagreement isn't really about the clarity of the text but about whether clarity has to produce convergence, and that each side has smuggled in a different criterion for what successfully deciding a text would look like. But I'd like someone to test that hunch rather than just tell me who's right. Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a change of question, a certain kind of empirical or formal result — or is this the sort of impasse where no progress should be expected because the starting-points aren't mutually argueable?
