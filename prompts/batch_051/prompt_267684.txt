Last week I rolled through a stop sign on my way home — well, I did stop, technically, but I was looking at my phone and only half-glanced both ways, and a kid on a bike shot out right after I'd passed. Nothing happened, but the timing was close enough that I keep thinking: if the kid had left his driveway two seconds earlier, my whole life would look completely different to everyone around me, and yet the part of me that made the choice would of been exactly identical. A friend of mine says this is basically the old moral luck case with the two careless drivers: both run a red light, one hits a pedestrian and kills them, the other makes it through because no one happened to be crossing, and the question is whether the unlucky one deserves more blame for the same decision. I get why that case is supposed to bother people, but it's started to feel too clean to me, because in that version one driver has a victim and the other dosen't, so the asymmetry is between something happening and nothing happening.

Here's the variant I can't shake. Suppose both drivers run the red light at the same speed, both are equally distracted, and both actually hit a pedestrian. The only difference is that one pedestrian is an elderly man who dies on impact, and the other is a healthy twenty-something who ends up with a broken wrist. Same decision, same recklessness, same information, and each driver has a victim — the difference in outcome comes entirely from the body of the person they happened to hit, not from anything the drivers did differently. Does the standard moral-luck analysis of the original case — the thought that how things actually turned out can itself ground extra blame — still say the first driver deserves more blame? If yes, is that still moral luck about the driver, or has it quietly become about the victim? If no — if we now want to say they're equally blameworthy — then what exactly changed from the original case, because the original two-driver case seems to push a lot of people toward saying the fatal driver is worse. Or does this version just expose that the original was tracking our horror at the death, rather than any real view about blame?
