I keep coming back to the dignity debate in bioethics and I genuinely can't figure out why it never moves. On one side you've got the Kantian-flavored view that human dignity is the actual foundation of rights—like, rights exist to protect dignity, and things such as organ markets, dwarf tossing, paid research subjects, or cloning are wrong because they violate dignity even when everyone autonomously consents. On the other side there's the Macklin line: dignity is a useless concept because in medical ethics it either just means respect for autonomy, or it's doing this vague sanctity-of-life work nobody can pin down, and either way it earns no keep. This has been going for something like twenty-plus years, and the same moves keep being made. The dignity side says well, what about [x] where autonomy plus welfare doesn't capture the wrong; the skeptic side says that residual bit is just unargued intuition or religion wearing secular clothes. Nobody seems to shift.

What I want is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive fight over whether there's a distinct value over and above autonomy and welfare, or is it a verbal fight about the word 'dignity' that would dissolve if people said 'inviolability' or 'non-instrumentalization' instead? Are they disagreeing about the cases, about standards of evidence in ethics, or about starting intuitions neither side can argue for? Or is the question ill-posed because 'dignity' is being asked to name something with no clear functional role?

My own hunch, presented as a hunch, is that the two camps are using the concept to do different jobs: one side uses 'dignity' as a constraint-generator, a placeholder for whatever makes certain ways of treating persons wrong, while the other side evaluates it as if it were supposed to be a mid-level principle that directly guides practical deliberation. If that's right, maybe progress would look like a taxonomy of the different roles a moral concept can play, rather than another paper arguing over examples. But I don't want my hunch just confirmed—I want you to tell me, based on what each side actually says, what kind of disagreement this is and what (if anything) woud count as progress.
