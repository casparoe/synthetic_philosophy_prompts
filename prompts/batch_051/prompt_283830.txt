I work on the problem of inferring, from a model's behavior, what objective it is actually optimizing — in practice, trying to reconstruct the effective reward or search space from rollouts, learned proxies, and the failure modes that appear once a proxy is optimized hard. A collaborator keeps compressing a lot of those results into one proverb, Goodhart's law, in the form people usually repeat: "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." His claim is that proxy optimization is all we ever get to observe, so the project of reading the true objective off observed behavior is fundamentally confused. When I asked him to say exactly what the law asserts, he offered a formulation about statistical regularities coming apart once they are put to use for control, and moved on.

I would like to treat that proverb as a family of distinct precise claims rather than a single proposition. Please lay the readings out as a menu, including at least the following where they are genuinely distinct: (a) the passive or statistical reading, on which a proxy is correlated with the target in ordinary data but can diverge, saturate, or invert at the extremes where an optimizer does its work; (b) the active or incentive reading, on which an agent who knows the proxy is being used changes its behavior, so the proxy-target relationship degrades in response to the measurement itself; (c) something in the vicinity of Campbell's law, a claim about social indicators and what attaching high stakes to a metric does to the process being measured; and (d) any further reading that deserves its own entry — for example a claim about mechanism design, about adversarial evaluation, or about the impossibility of ever recovering an objective from behavior. For each reading, say what must be true of the optimizer and its environment for the claim to hold, whether it is a near-tautology or a substantive empirical prediction, whether it applies to a learned reward model, and how much of my collaborator's pessimism it actually underwrites. I want to come away with a clear sense of where "Goodhart's law" names something structural about optimization under a proxy, and where it is just a dramatic way of saying that someone chose a bad proxy and failed to validate it off distribution.
