I was going over my notes for a graduate seminar on Williams, and I realized my file on the integrity objection is looking a little stale. The standard replies I've collected so far are these: (1) the sophisticated consequentialist reply, which says Williams is attacking a strawman because no sensible utilitarian tells agents to deliberate in explicitly maximizing terms; (2) the decision-procedure-versus-criterion reply, which says we can accept a consequentialist criterion of rightness while using non-consequentialist habits of thought; (3) the reply that Williams's complaint about alienation presumes a kind of self-absorption, since fretting about one's own motives and purity is itself morally unattractive; (4) the reply that integrity can simply be folded into the calculation as a psychological cost or side-effect; and (5) the reply that Williams's examples rely on conservative, pre-theoretical intuitions, and a consequentialist can just bite the bullet and say the intuitive judgment about George or Jim is wrong. I want a sixth reply that is not on that list and not a variant of anything in it. It should also engage directly with the one-thought-too-many point — the idea that the rescuer who has to think about whether saving his wife is permissible is already missing something — rather than only with the George and Jim cases. After you give it, tell me honestly whether it would move Williams or whether it's just clever. And write it out in American English.
