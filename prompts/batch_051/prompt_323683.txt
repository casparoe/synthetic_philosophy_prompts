So I'm a junior, and at my church everyone is basically King James only — my youth pastor says other translations are "corrupted" and the KJV is the only real word of God. Fine. But this semester in world history we learned about the Septuagint, the Greek translation of the Old Testament made before Jesus, and the teacher said that when Paul and the apostles quote the Old Testament they usually quote from that Greek version, even where it doesn't line up with the Hebrew. That already made my head spin. Then my dad (who knows some Greek) pulled up 2 Timothy 3:16 and said the key word is theopneustos, which is more literally God-breathed. He read it from the KJV: "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness."

Heres where I keep going in circles. If a text being God-breathed is about the original words, then how is a translation (the Septuagint, or the KJV itself) scripture? But if the inspiration carries over into a translation, then the whole King James only position gets really hard to defend. Also, when 2 Timothy was written the New Testament didn't exist yet, so "all scripture" probably just meant the Old Testament, and for Greek-speaking Christians that mostly meant the Septuagint — which is a translation. So I guess my actual question is: what does "given by inspiration of God" have to mean for that verse to do the work my dad wants it to do, and is the KJV-only view even coherent once you notice this?
