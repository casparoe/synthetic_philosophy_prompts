I would like a working definition of liberty — or of freedom, if you judge the two terms not to be interchangeable; say which term you are defining and why. The definition should classify each of the following cases as free or unfree, with a definite answer for each and a sentence explaining the classification:

(a) a prisoner in a well-run prison whose routine is predictable and whose guards do not abuse their powers;
(b) a motorist who stops at a red light on an empty road at three in the morning;
(c) a slave whose owner happens never to interfere or threaten, but who could do so at any time with impunity;
(d) an employee who may resign but has no savings, no alternative income, and no public assistance to fall back on if she does;
(e) a citizen who voted for a law that now constrains her, and who regrets that vote;
(f) a person with a compulsion she disavows — say, an addiction — who acts on it;
(g) a novice in a religious order who freely took a vow of obedience and is now ordered to undertake work she hates.

I am confident that (a) counts as unfree and (b) counts as free in the relevant sense. I have no settled pre-theoretical view about (c) through (g); part of the exercise is to see which classifications a definition forces.

Please proceed in this order: propose the definition; run it through all seven cases; say explicitly where it returns a verdict you would not want to defend, if anywhere; and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Conclude by saying whether your definition is best classified as negative, positive, republican, or some hybrid, and note which case does most work in forcing that classification.
