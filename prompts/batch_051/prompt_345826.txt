Heres the setup. I'm hosting a recorded panel Q&A with a guy I'll call Michael — a donor advisor who spent about 15 years directing money for a few large family foundations. His whole thing is marginalism about doing good: you should buy the most cost-effective, legible, countable unit of improvement you can find — bednets, cash transfers, deworming, that kind of stuff — and basically never fund "systems change," which he calls speculative philanthropy. He'll admit systems matter, but he says they mostly change through the slow accumulation of marginal fixes or through forces nobody can steer, so a donor acting as a donor should ignore them. He also has a rule he calls the two-account rule: he'll only recommend a grant if he can draw a causal line from the money to a counted outcome within about five years, otherwise he files it under "activist speculation," which he doesn't count as giving.

I want questions that really get inside this, not the standard "how can you be so sure systemic change fails" or "isn't this just the status quo." Draft me 8 questions, and for each one give the follow-up you'd use if he gives the answer he usually gives. I want you to commit to a definite read on where this view is weakest — pick the two or three pressure points you think are most damaging and make the sequence build toward them; don't give me a menu of alternative questions to pick from, give me the questions you'd actually ask. For each, a sentence on why it bites. I especially want pressure on: (1) the legibility problem — his method can't see the things it's worst at measuring, and that's a bias, not a neutral filter; (2) his claim that systems change by accumulating marginal fixes — where coordination problems, thresholds, and public goods break that story; (3) what happens to the plumbing if every large donor thought like him; and (4) how someone who says he's an expected-value guy deals with low-probability, high-upside systemic bets. No citations or reading-list questions, I want it all answerable from the view itself.
