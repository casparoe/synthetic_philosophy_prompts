Suppose a city, instead of ordinary rent control, tries this. Existing tenants' rents can rise at most with inflation plus one percent, but a tenant who moves out may sell their tenancy to anyone they like: the incoming tentant takes over the capped rent, and the outgoing tenant can charge them whatever one-time price the two agree on. The landlord can't refuse the transfer except for the usual credit and background checks, and can't raise the rent at turnover. Newly built units are exempt for their first fifteen years. Walk me through how this actually plays out: who captures the gap between the capped rent and what a vacant unit would fetch, who stays and who moves, what landlords do to maintenance and construction, how people game it (sublets, shell relatives, fake exits?), and whether the fifteen-year exemption repairs the rot or just moves the boundary. Where does it break first, and can the failure modes be fixed without collapsing into either full rent control or no rent control?
