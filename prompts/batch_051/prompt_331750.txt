I work on a grocery delivery app. Our city health department is proposing a rule that any grocery app operating here has to make the default product ranking a “health score” — a composite of calories, sodium, fiber, protein, etc. Users can switch to sorting by price or brand or favorites, but the default resets to health score every time they open the app. The city calls it a nudge, not a restriction. I’m a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I might butcher this, but I’ve been reading John Stuart Mill’s On Liberty, and there’s a passage in Chapter V where he says trade is a social act and so it falls within society’s jurisdiction, but then he says freedom of trade rests on different grounds than the principle of individual liberty he has been defending. I keep wondering whether this grocery app rule is just an ordinary regulation of trade, or whether it’s using a trade platform to shape self-regarding choices about what I eat, and whether Mill’s distinction would make those two things different in practice. Can you walk me through how this rule would actually play out: what it incentivizes for shoppers, for the app’s ranking engineers, and for food manufacturers, where it gets gamed first, and what second-order effects I should expect? And, given that Mill passage, is the nudge mostly harmless trade regulation, mostly paternalism in disguise, or does the distinction break down when a private app is the one doing the defaults?
