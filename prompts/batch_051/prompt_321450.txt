I am currently taking a course in moral psychology and the virtues, and we have been discussing whether a virtue such as temperance should be understood as a single underlying trait of character or as a convenient label for a range of domain-specific abilities. I would like to probe this question in a way that is concrete rather than merely definitional.

Take the following claim: temperance — the settled disposition to regulate appetites and desires such as those for food, drink, pleasure, spending, or retaliation — is a genuine psychological kind, distinct both from mere prudence (restraining oneself because restraint pays off later) and from repression or conformist self-policing (restraining oneself because one has internalised an external demand without endorsing it). Suppose a person displays what looks like temperance across several domains: they eat moderately, drink moderately, rarely lose their temper, and do not overspend. How might one test whether this reflects one underlying trait of self-governance, as opposed to a bundle of domain-specific skills that happen to travel together in this person, or a high-functioning habit that simply mimics the virtue?

I would like a concrete research design — for instance, experience-sampling across domains, behavioural choice tasks, or training/intervention studies — together with a statement of which results would favour each reading. In particular, what evidence would favour the single-trait view, what evidence would favour the domain-specific skills view, and what evidence would suggest that what we are calling temperance is really prudential restraint or conformist suppression? I would also like you to be explicit about what even an otherwise favourable study could not settle: can any behavioural evidence distinguish the virtue itself — restraint done for the right sorts of reasons and integrated with the rest of the agent's character — from a stable strategy or habit that produces the same outward behaviour? Or is that distinction one that no empirical test can reach? Please answer in British English.
