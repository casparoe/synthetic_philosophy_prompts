Here's where I'm at. A family clears and farms a plot of genuinely unowned land and eventually sells it to a developer and gets rich: fine. A person invents something in their garage and makes billions licensing it: fine. A basketball player gets a million fans to each voluntarily pay a dollar to watch him play, and he ends up with a fortune, and the resulting inequality seems enormous: fine, it actually doesn't bother me at all. A person buys a used car in good faith, then learns it was stolen and the original owner wants it back: not fine for them to just keep it, though I also think the buyer was wronged and the thief owes him the money. My uncle's money, which traces back to the expropriation: I don't feel fully entitled to it, even though I personally did nothing wrong. A guy buys up bottled water before a hurricane and sells it afterward for thirty dollars a bottle to desperate people: not fine, even though no one is forcing anyone at gunpoint. The rough principle I keep muttering to myself is something like the Nozick line I half-remember from undergrad: you're entitled to a holding if you originally got it from the unowned world without making others worse off, or you got it through a free exchange or gift from someone who was entitled, and if there was theft or force somewhere upstream then that has to be fixed. But when I run that, it seems to bless the hurricane guy, and I really dont want to bless the hurricane guy.
