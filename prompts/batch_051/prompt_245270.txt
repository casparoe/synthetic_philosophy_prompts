I am a student currently taking a seminar on how moral revolutions happen, and I am trying to build a study aid for the central question of the course: when a deeply entrenched practice such as dueling, chattel slavery, or footbinding collapses, is the primary engine of change rational argument, material interest, or shifts in honor and status? The course has made clear that these three mechanisms are easy to distinguish in the abstract and extremely hard to tell apart in actual cases, so I would like you to write a decision tree that a reader can use to classify an episode of norm collapse without already knowing which theoretical label each branch leads toward.

Please build the decision tree to the following specifications.

Leaves and logical space. The terminal classifications should be: argument-led change, interest-led change, honor-led change, coercion-led change, generational replacement, and a mixed or feedback leaf for episodes in which no single mechanism does the primary work. Before attaching these labels, build the instrument up from the underlying diagnostic dimensions rather than from the list of names. Useful dimensions include the temporal order of belief change and behavior change, whether compliance tracks material incentives, whether defection from the practice is coordinated or cascade-like, whether esteem and ridicule do the work, whether state force precedes the behavioral shift, and whether the practice dies out chiefly because a new generation never adopts it. Locate the named positions within that space, and briefly note any logically coherent combinations of answers that are probably unoccupied or unattractive, with a short diagnosis of why.

Diagnostic questions. The substance of the tree should be in the nodes. Each node should pose a concrete question that a student could answer from a case description without knowing any of the theoretical labels: for instance, whether people who gave up the practice had already ceased to believe it was acceptable, whether the practice became materially costly or technologically obsolete around the time of its decline, whether public ridicule preceded elite defection, whether law or force changed behavior before the behavior itself had begun to shift, and whether those who abandoned the practice were disproportionately young people who had never fully entered it. Do not phrase any node as a question about which mechanism was operating.

Separability. Where the tree cannot cleanly separate two leaves, flag it rather than papering it over. For example, honor campaigns often succeed by making a previously suppressed moral argument socially safe to voice, which can make argument-led and honor-led change hard to disentangle; material interest and status are entangled when a practice is an elite marker whose economic underpinnings shift; and coercion is often the instrument through which an interest-based coalition acts. Please state whether the tree should output a single primary leaf or a primary-plus-secondary profile in such cases, and justify that choice.

Test cases. Validate the tree against the following three case capsules, exactly as our instructor distributed them, using only the information given. For each case, report the leaf or profile the tree assigns and identify the least ambiguous piece of evidence that drove the classification. If a decision would require a fact the capsule does not state, say which missing fact would matter and how it would move the classification.

Dueling in Britain. A long-established upper-class practice of settling private affronts with pistols, tolerated in elite culture for generations and then abandoned over the early nineteenth century. The explanations floated in class are: (a) an argument-based explanation, on which religious and moral criticism increasingly held that a duel settled nothing and was incompatible with Christian conduct; (b) an interest-based explanation, on which improvements in pistol design made dueling more likely to be fatal, raising the personal cost of defending one's honor; and (c) an honor-based explanation, on which satire and ridicule made the duel look absurd rather than dignified, so that refusing a challenge eventually read as self-respect rather than cowardice.

Footbinding in China. A family practice that shaped the marriage prospects of Chinese women, followed for centuries across much of society and then abandoned within roughly a generation around the turn of the twentieth century. The explanations floated in class are: (a) argument, on which reformers' humanitarian and nationalist claims about health, cruelty, and national weakness persuaded families to stop; (b) interest, on which new economic opportunities for women's labor made bound feet a practical liability; and (c) honor, on which anti-footbinding societies and public pledge campaigns made natural feet a sign of modern respectability and bound feet a mark of backwardness, flipping what parents took to be best for a daughter's marriageability without necessarily converting them to the reformers' moral outlook.

The British slave trade. A state-backed trade in enslaved Africans, carried on for more than a century and then abolished after a lengthy campaign of mass petitioning, consumer boycott, and parliamentary action. The explanations floated in class are: (a) argument, on which religious and humanitarian claims about the humanity of the enslaved and the sinfulness of the trade progressively changed public opinion; (b) interest, on which a coalition of economic interests that no longer depended on the trade outmobilized those that did; and (c) honor, on which the campaign made participation in the trade a stain on national character, so that continuing it came to seem incompatible with the self-image of a civilized nation.

Finally, present the tree as a sequence of numbered questions with explicit branch instructions so that it can be used during discussion without reconstruction. Keep it to no more than ten decision nodes and no more than six leaves, define any technical terms the first time they appear, and do not include citations or a reading list.
