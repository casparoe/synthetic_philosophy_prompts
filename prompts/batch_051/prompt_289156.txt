I'm working on a piece about how an old line from W. E. B. Du Bois has aged now that the distribution system for Black art has completely flipped. The line, roughly, is that no work of art is ever pure; every work does propaganda work weather its maker wants it to or not, so a Black artist chasing art for its own sake while his people are still fighting for recognition is just checking out of the fight. The force of that came partly from the world Du Bois was looking at: a small set of white editors, publishers, and patrons decided what Black art would reach an audience, and the audience itself had been soaked in stereotypes. Now those scarce gatekeepers are mostly gone, but the replacement is a feed ranked for engagement, where anger, stereotype-bait, and trauma are what tend to get amplified. So does Du Bois's argument survive that change, or does it only go through in a world of scarce curators? I want the specifics: say what the argument needs to be true, which premise the new setting actually pressures, and whether there is a repaired version that keeps the kernel of the position without the parts tied to his distribution landscape.
