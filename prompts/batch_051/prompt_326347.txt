I'm a PhD student working on blame, and I'm drafting a paper on the standing to blame — I need a case for the last section. Could you build me a scenario to spec? I want it to force a clean split between two ways people think about standing, so I can show the contrast to some undergrads.

The two views, roughly:

(1) The clean-hands or hypocrisy view: standing is a matter of whether the blamer is in a position to press the norm they're invoking. You lose it by being a hypocrite or complicit in the same wrong, or by failing to take yourself to be bound by the norm. What doesn't matter is whether you're the victim.

(2) The stakeholder or grievance view: standing is like having a complaint to press. It belongs to the person who was wronged, or to people the victim has authorised. A stranger with no stake and no invitation is meddling, even if their hands are clean.

Specs for the case:

- P blames Q, openly and sincerely, for Q's wrong φ.
- φ is a private wrong against R — say, Q betrayed a confidence R placed in them, with no wider public harm.
- P is a stranger to R and Q: no friendship, family, professional role, or special community tie making it P's business.
- P has genuinely clean hands: never done anything like φ, never complicit in anyone else doing it, and once paid a real cost for refusing to do it.
- R has repeatedly asked P to stop moralising, saying R has forgiven Q and it's between the two of them.

Make the case come out so that view (1) says P retains standing — clean hands pass the test and R's refusal doesn't affect P's standing — while view (2) says P lacks standing, because R is the only holder of the grievance and R explicitly hasn't authorised P to press it.

Then do the checks for me. Give the reasoning each view uses to get its verdict, identify which structural feature of the case makes them peel apart, and say what happens if you remove that feature — e.g. if R actually invites P to speak on R's behalf, do the two views collapse back together? Also tell me honestly where a defender of the clean-hands view would attack the case: which premise would they try to resist, and what would their reply be?
