I'm a journalist working on an explainer about Brandom's inferentialism, and there's one point I can't get past. His slogan is that a concept's content just is its inferential role. But on that picture, my inferential role for "gene" is bound to be differnt from a molecular geneticist's: I've got some of the same inferences, plus a few shaky ones, and she's got vastly more and better ones. If content is inferential role, and our inferential roles differ, doesn't it follow that we're expressing different concepts, so when I ask her a question about genes she isn't really answering about the same thing? That can't be right—she can correct my mistaken inferences about genes, which seems to require a shared subject matter. What am I missing? Does Brandom make the inferential role social or normative in a way that lets two people with non-identical inference sets still share one concept, or does he bite the bullet here?
