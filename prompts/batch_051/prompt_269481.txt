I keep going back and forth on van Fraassen's Reflection principle from his 1984 paper Belief and the Will. The way i understand him, the principle says roughly this: my current credence in A, conditional on the supposition that my future credence in A will be x, ought to just be x itself. And the Ulysses point is that you can rationally bind yourself against your own future judgment only because you expect the future self to be epistemically compromised, not merely because you expect a preference shift. I'm tempted to treat Reflection as a genuine norm of rational credence, not just a bookkeeping constraint. So I want to know: what would count against it? I mean concrete formal results, cases, or arguments that should actually move me off it, sorted by how decisive they'd be, and which objections are merely awkward. Also, has the principle been qualified so much—like "provided the future self is unimpaired and has no less evidence"—that it no longer risks anything? Thats the part I really want: does Reflection still have real exposure, and if not, is that a problem for a norm of this kind? And tell me where your answer is most likely to fail: which further argument or case would make you give a different verdict.
