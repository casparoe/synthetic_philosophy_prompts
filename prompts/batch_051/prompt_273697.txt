In my aesthetics course we've been doing Schopenhauer on music and I keep going back and forth between two things that both feel right, so I want a negotiated verdict rather than a winner. On the theory side: Schopenhauer thinks all the other arts present the Platonic Ideas, but music stands apart — it gives us the will itself directly, not an image of it. That would explain a lot: why music feels immediate and bodily, why it doesn't need words or concepts, why it seems universal. But I also have a pretty firm intuition: when I actually listen to, say, late Mahler or Billie Holiday, I don't hear a metaphysical will; I hear somthing determinate — grief, longing, resolution, sometimes almost a story. And if music really is the will itself, shouldn't there be something strange about how much calm or reconciled music works? What I want is a map of the moves: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what would it take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and is there a reading of Schopenhauer — or of what music expresses — on which both survive without one side being reinterpreted into triviality? Also please separate what's actually settled about his view from what's genuinely contested among people who know the material, and flag if a reading you're leaning on is contested rather than consensus.
