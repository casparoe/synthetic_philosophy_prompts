What is it to rationally update your credences when you learn a conditional — not "A happened" but "if A, then probably B"? I want a rule that handles these cases: (a) Judy Benjamin: a paratrooper dropped at night into a region that's 75% Red and 25% Blue; Red has two companies, Headquarters and Second, Blue has one; her prior is 3/8 Headquarters, 3/8 Second, and 1/4 Blue. Radio tells her: if you're in Red, the odds are 3:1 you're in Headquarters rather than Second. Intuitivly that message only reshuffles probability inside Red, so P(Red) should stay 3/4 — does your rule deliver that, and if not, which should give way, the rule or the intuition? (b) Learning "if A then B with probability 0.8" and then learning A, versus learning A first and then the conditional — should the order matter? (c) A conditional whose antecedent you currently assign probability zero, like "if you had taken the drug, you'd very likely have recovered." (d) Being told a conditional that directly contradicts one you already hold, say you're certain P(B|A)=0.9 and a source you trust tells you P(B|A)=0.2. Propose the rule, run it through all four, and say which verdict you'd least want to defend.
