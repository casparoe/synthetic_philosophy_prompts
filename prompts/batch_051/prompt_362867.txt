I keep running into the claim, in various strengths, that conscious experience is a kind of user interface: the brain doesn't hand us the full data, it hands us a simplified, useful summary and then adds a bit of felt confidence that we're seeing more than we are. One specific version: when you look at a scene you have a strong impression of a rich, stable visual field extending into the periphery, but actually most of that periphery is not represented in any detail; you've just got gist plus a feeling of detail that doesn't correspond to anything beneath it. I want to know how one could actually test the stronger version of that claim rather than just argue about it. Design the best experiment or observation you can that would bear on whether the felt peripheral detail is backed by a real visual representation or is a sort of confabulated feeling about one's own vision. Say what result would favour each side. Then be precise about what even a clean result would and would not settle, especially whether it tells us anything about consciousness itself as opposed to only about what information the visual system has access to. Please clearly separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus. Continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, the way you'd write me an email.
