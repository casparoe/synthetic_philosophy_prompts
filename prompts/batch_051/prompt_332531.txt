I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, so keep the jargon light. I've been going down a rabbit hole on the Allais paradox. The usual line is that the independence axiom is a requirement of rationality, but in Allais-type choices a lot of people—even people comfortable with probability—pick the 'irrational' gamble. Some folks say that just means the axiom is wrong about what should be chosen, not just wrong about what people actually do. How would you test that? Give me a concrete experiment: what you'd vary, what you'd measure, and what result would make you think the Allais choosers are making a correctable mistake versus a stable, defensable preference. Say whether having people re-choose after the gambles are rewritten so their shared part is obvious counts as real evidence, and if not, what would. Then be honest about what even the best outcome of the test leaves unsettled—where we stop measuring anything and are just stipulating what 'rational' means.
