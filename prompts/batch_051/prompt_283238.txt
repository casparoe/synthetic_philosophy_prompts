I am preparing an undergraduate tutorial on the golden rule, and I keep running into the same difficulty. The positive formulation says, roughly, that you should treat others as you would wish to be treated yourself, while the platinum rule says you should treat others as they would wish to be treated. In easy cases, where people happen to share preferences, the two rules give the same advice, and in the usual counterexamples one party has a preference that students dismiss as contrived, such as wanting to be hurt, flattered, or told uncomfortable truths; the underlying problem never gets a fair hearing.

I would like you to construct a thought experiment to specification that makes the problem bite. Build a realistic, one-paragraph scenario, set in an ordinary workplace, shared household, or neighbourhood, in which a thoughtful and well-intentioned agent sincerely applies the positive golden rule on its everyday reading (asking how they themselves would wish to be treated in the situation, rather than what the other person would prefer) and is thereby led either to do something that the platinum rule clearly forbids or to refrain from something the platinum rule clearly requires. The case must not involve anyone wishing to be harmed, deceived, humiliated, or manipulated; both parties must have accurate factual beliefs and their preferences must be stable under reflection and within the normal human range; and the agent must not be applying the rule mechanically or self-servingly. After presenting the scenario, show your working: state the act in question, the verdict each rule delivers and why, and also say what the negative or silver formulation, which forbids doing to others what you would not want done to yourself, says about that same act, even if it falls silent. Then identify the single feature of the case that does the work, explain what would happen if that feature were removed, and give the strongest defence a proponent of the positive formulation could offer to stop the case from counting against it. The scenario should be one from which those verdicts can be derived by applying each rule as stated, rather than one whose description already stipulates the answers.
