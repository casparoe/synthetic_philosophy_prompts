I want the two positions pinned down much more precisely than I have been managing.

Please draft the joint statement that two good-faith, well-informed parties could sign after an adversarial collaboration: a committed proponent of the kalam argument and a committed proponent of the contingency argument, each having tried to state the other's view as charitably as possible. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section on what both accept about causation, explanation, limits on regress, and what would count as a successful first-cause argument; (2) a disagreements table that is exact rather than rhetorical — covering, for example, whether the universe's having begun to exist is genuinely load-bearing, whether an infinite causal regress is ruled out only for essentially ordered series or also for accidentally ordered ones, and whether either argument can deliver a personal first cause — with each position worded so that its own proponent would sign it; (3) for each row, the sub-question that is the actual crux; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test or case both sides would agree bears on it (such as a settled cyclic cosmology, a demonstration that the past is actually infinite, or a deflationary account on which the universe's existence is a brute fact), with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. Format the statement as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table.
