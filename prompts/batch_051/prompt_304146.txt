For a symposium on mind uploading I have been asked to state my position compactly before the discussion, and I would like an audit of whether the position hangs together before I defend it. My considered commitments are these.

1. I am not an immaterial substance. What I am, most fundamentally, is a certain causally integrated pattern of information processing, and that pattern could in principle be realized in a substrate other than my present biology.
2. Survival over time consists in psychological continuity: chains of overlapping memory, intention, standing character, and similar psychological connections, appropriately caused across time.
3. Numerical identity is not what ultimately matters in survival. What matters is whether some future entity stands in the right kind of continuity relation to me.
4. If my neurons were replaced one at a time over several months by functionally equivalent artificial units, while I remained conscious and able to report on the process throughout, I would regard the resulting person as me, not as a copy.
5. I would refuse destructive whole-brain scanning followed by immediate instantiation of the scanned model in a digital substrate, because I think the procedure would kill me. The resulting entity would be a new person who inherits my mental life.
6. If the scan were non-destructive and the digital model were instantiated while my biological body continued to live, I would call the digital entity a copy of me, not a second survivor, and I would not say my survival had been doubled.
7. I think personal identity over time can be vague: there can be cases in which there is no determinate fact whether a later entity is me, and the vagueness is a feature of the world rather than merely of our words.
8. I think the difference between my verdicts in (4) and (5) is not a brute preference or a failure of nerve. It tracks a real difference in what the two procedures do to me.

Work through this set as a coherence audit. Identify the pairs of commitments that are genuinely in tension and rank them by severity. Identify any apparent tensions that dissolve under closer inspection. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent while preserving as much of it as possible.
