My building's noise policy, written in 1987, reads: 'Residents shall not operate any sound-producing device at a volume that is unreasonably disturbing to other residents between the hours of 10 p.m. and 8 a.m.' I fall asleep to a white-noise machine. I started at notch 1, which the manual says is barely audible from two feet away, and I've been turning it up one notch a week because I've gotten use to it; I'm at notch 9 now and my downstairs neighbor has finally complained. The rule was clearly aimed at parties and stereos, so nobody in 1987 was picturing someone creeping a white-noise machine up its dial notch by notch. The committee chair told me: 'There's no single notch where it becomes unreasonable, but that doesn't mean no notch matters—that's just the old sorites trick.' Is the chair's reply coherent? What does the policy actually require of me at notch 9, and how should the committee decide? Walk through the words, the purpose of the rule, what the drafters would probably say if asked, and what a reasonable resident reading it today would take it to mean—and say where the vagueness of 'unreasonably disturbing' leaves each approach.
