The graduate handbook in my history department requires each doctoral student to pass a field examination in "one major chronological field: medieval (c. 500–1500), early modern (c. 1500–1800), or modern (c. 1800–present)." I want to take my field on the construction and contestation of those very categories, organized around the medieval–Renaissance boundary, c. 1350–1700. My director of graduate studies says this does not satisfy the rule because it is not one of the three listed fields and straddles two of them. I care less about the administrative outcome than the principle: can a field whose subject is the critique of period categories fall under one of those categories, or does the rule implicitly require accepting the categories it enforces? How would the plain words, the purpose of the requirement, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable student would understand each treat this case, and what would that reveal about what the requirement is actually protecting?
