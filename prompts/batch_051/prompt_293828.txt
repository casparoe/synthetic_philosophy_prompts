Analyze the debate over smoking and lung cancer from the 1950s and 1960s — the early Doll and Hill observational studies, R. A. Fisher's objection that a genetic confounder might cause both the habit and the disease, and the 1964 Surgeon General's report — as a case study in the difference between counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation. I want a disciplined mapping: what each theory treats as the relevant causal claim, what evidence would settle that claim, and what each theory says the disputants were actually entitled to conclude at each stage, given that no randomized experiment on smoking could be performed. Then say where each theory misdescribes or is silent about the episode, and what the episode shows about which theory better captures how causal claims are established in epidemiology.
