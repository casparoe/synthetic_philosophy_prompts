What is a generation, in the sense in which Baby Boomers, Millennials, and Generation Z are generations? I want a working definition precise enough to tell when a named cohort is a real social kind rather than a label of convenience. It should clearly count the post–World War II baby-boom cohort as a generation, and it should clearly not count a purely arbitrary interval such as everyone born in 1990. Now test the definition, revising or defending it where needed, against these four hard cases: (a) a cusp micro-cohort such as Xennials; (b) a cohort defined solely by a single shared event, such as the children who were in school on September 11, 2001; (c) someone who emigrated in childhood from a country whose cohort-defining events differ sharply from the destination country's; and (d) a family generation in the parent–child lineage sense. Where the definition yields no answer or clashes with ordinary use, say so, and for each case note whether it survives as a real kind or only as a label. Respond with exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 states the definition and the two anchor verdicts; items 2–5 address the four hard cases in order, one per item.
