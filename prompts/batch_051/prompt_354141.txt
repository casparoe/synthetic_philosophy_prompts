I'm drafting a short document stating where I stand for a workshop on path dependence and historical contingency, and I'd like an audit of the package before I circulate it. The workshop's organizing question is whether history is locked in, or whether the same broad outcomes would have turned up even if earlier accidents had been different, and I've found that I hold a set of views that each strike me as right while never having checked whether they all pull in the same direction.

The commitments I'm planning to circulate are these:

1. Path dependence is fundamentally a counterfactual property: an outcome is path-dependent iff a small change in an earlier state of the process would have produced a large change in the later outcome.
2. This is the normal condition for major historical outcomes — constitutional forms, patterns of economic leadership, dominant technologies, and the organizational shape of large institutions are usually path-dependent in that sense.
3. For path dependence to be explanatory rather than merely descriptive, some self-reinforcing mechanism must be at work: increasing returns, switching costs, learning-by-using, network externalities, or complementarities that make the existing path progressively cheaper to continue than to abandon.
4. At the same time, I want to count political sequences that run through shifting balances of power and legitimacy — one event reorders which actors can act, enabling the next event, without any self-reinforcing mechanism attaching to the institution itself — as path-dependent, not just as ordinary causal chains.
5. I'm also deflationary about the standard exhibits: once contemporaneous costs and switching costs are fully counted, many QWERTY-style stories of an inferior alternative winning and then persisting dissolve, and the supposed survivor was often not clearly inferior at the time.
6. I accept a long-run selection principle: severely inefficient institutions and technologies tend, over time, to be reformed, replaced, or outcompeted, because persistent inefficiency creates gains that some coalition can eventually capture.
7. But the independent emergence of states, writing, and markets across unrelated regions suggests that certain outcomes are robust: once enabling conditions are present, something in that neighborhood was highly likely, not just a lucky accident.

Please audit this set for internal coherence. Tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to, and which commitments actually support one another. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and say what would have to give for the set to become coherent, and what accepting the cheapest repair would cost. Don't rewrite the list; just locate the trouble. Keep the audit to at most 800 words.
