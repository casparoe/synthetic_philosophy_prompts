So I'm a novelist, and I've been reading Spinoza's Ethics because my next book has a character who's a lens grinder and I wanted to make his determinism real in the prose. The passage that got me is from Part One, Definition VI: "By God, I mean a being absolutely infinite—that is, a substance consisting in infinite attributes, of which each expresses eternal and infinite essentiality." I took that seriously as the starting point and built an argument that now seems to prove too much. Tell me where it goes wrong, because I can't find the exit.

Here's the argument as I've been carrying it around:

(1) God is the one absolutely infinite substance, and by that definition God's essence involves existence, so God exists necessarily.
(2) Whatever follows from God's nature does so with the same necessity; there are no loose parts, no unrealized alternatives.
(3) Every finite thing—including me, my drafts, my indecision about whether a character would say a line—is a mode of God and so falls under that necessity.
(4) Therefore, my sense that I could have written yesterday's chapter otherwise is an illusion; there was no other way the chapter could have been produced.
(5) So regret, revision notes, and the whole feeling of "this draft is wrong and I need to fix it" are all based on a false picture of possibility.
(6) But Spinoza also calls the highest state the intellectual love of God, which involves understanding things as necessary. If I genuinely understood my own novel as necessary, I'd have to love the bad draft exactly as much as the good one, because both are equally necessary.
(7) That seems to swallow the distinction between a writer and a passive evening reader, and it makes the act of choosing a word feel fraudulent.

i'd really like to keep (1) and (2), because I think the metaphysics is the whole point of the book I'm writing. But I can't see how to escape (4) without either making human actions an exception to God's necessity or making them not real modes. And if I give up (6), I don't know what's left of the intellectual love of God that isn't just a fancy way of saying "stop complaining." Which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
