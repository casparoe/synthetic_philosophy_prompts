I need a workable definition of exploitation — the ordinary moral concept people are reaching for when they call a job, a price, or a relationship exploitative, not the technical Marxist sense. I keep finding cases where my gut and my theory disagree, which is annoying becuase I thought I knew what the word meant.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

- A landlord raises the rent sharply because he knows the tenant can't afford to move and won't fight it. I'm sure this is exploitation.
- A factory in a poor country pays workers around $2 a day in bad conditions, but the workers line up for the jobs because every alternative is worse; the company could easily pay more and still turn a profit. I think this is exploitation, but on paper it's a win-win and the workers consent, so I can't identify the wrong-making feature.
- An unpaid internship at a magazine where interns do the work of entry-level employees but get a resume line and networking in return. I say exploitation; a freind says no, because the interns aren't coerced and they do gain something from it.
- A company buys from small coffee farmers at a price that barely covers their costs, though the farmers are free to refuse and sometimes do. I genuinely don't know.
- A guy selling $20 bottled water after a hurricane when the stores are closed. My gut says exploitative, but I can't tell whether that's just price gouging / supply and demand rather than exploitation proper.
- A PhD advisor listed as coauthor on a paper the student basically wrote. I'm sure it's exploitation, but it's awkward because the student also benefits from having the advisor's name on it.
- A plumber who charges $150/hour to fix a leak on a holiday because he's the only one available. I lean not exploitation — expensive and annoying, but not the same moral wrong as the landlord.
- Two friends splitting a dinner bill evenly when one ordered far more food than the other. Not exploitation, just mildly unfair, but I don't know why my definition wouldn't count it.

I keep wanting to define it as taking unfair advantage of someone's vulnerability, but then the plumber (taking advantage of my emergency) counts, and so does the dinner friend (taking advantage of my inattention), and I don't want either of those. Your definition has to explain what filters them out.

The sweatshop and the internship are both cases of what people sometimes call mutually beneficial exploitation, which sounds almost like a contradiction, and that's exactly the knot I want the definition to untangle.

Please propose a definition, run it through all eight cases, and say where it gives a verdict I didn't expect. If you have to bite a bullet — for example, deciding that the plumber really is exploiting me — defend it. And answer this directly: can a transaction be exploitative even when both sides benefit and both consent? If your definition says no, you'll need to explain away the sweatshop and internship cases; if yes, you'll need to say what's still wrong with them.
