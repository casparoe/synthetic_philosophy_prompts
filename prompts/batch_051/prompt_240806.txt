I am a doctoral student working on the methodology of normative theory, and I would like your help thinking through a recurring conflict among kinds of evidence. Suppose I encounter three pairwise comparative judgments about a single evaluative dimension, each backed by its own locally compelling principle: A over B, B over C, and C over A. The cycle feels wrong, but each pairwise judgment feels right on its own. A scalar principle that ranks outcomes by a single index, by contrast, is simple and strong and would rule such a cycle impossible. I would like you to rank the following in order of how much authority you think each should carry in settling this conflict, and to give each a rough weight as a fraction of the total: (a) firm considered judgments about particular cases, (b) formal requirements such as transitivity or acyclicity, (c) theoretical virtues such as simplicity and unification, and (d) any further source of evidence you think belongs on the list. Then say, in a sentence or two per item, why each lands where it does. In particular, state under what conditions you would let the formal requirement overturn a persistent case intuition, and under what conditions you would do the reverse.
