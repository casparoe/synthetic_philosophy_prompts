Two engineers on my team argued at lunch about whether we should take on a speculative project: a static analyzer intended to catch every data race in our codebase. I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and the argument ended up on ground I've never sorted out, so I'd like a neutral, stage-by-stage ruling. Their exchange went roughly like this:

(1) Mina said we should take on the project because she can picture, in real detail, how the finished tool would work — the query surface, the internal representation, the reports — and that clearly conceiving a working system is good evidence the system can be built. Engineering estimates, she said, rest on exactly that.

(2) Jordan replied that conceivability is too cheap to be evidence. People have clearly conceived of perpetual motion machines and of programs that decide the halting problem, and those are impossible. A vivid mental model can hide a contradiction.

(3) Mina answered that those examples cut the other way: in each case the impossibility was exposed by later reasoning about the conceived thing, so the conception was the object on which that reasoning operated. When a conception survives serious attempts to find an inconsistency, she argued, that is a fallible but real reason to raise confidence.

(4) Jordan said that move changes the subject. Once you say a conception survives attempts to break it, the work is being done by the absence of a found impossibility — which tracks our ignorance, not any positive bridge from conceivable to possible.

(5) Mina finished that if she refused to treat conceivability as a guide to possibility, she could not estimate the feasibility of any project, because almost no project arrives with a proof of feasibility before work begins.

Please give your response as exactly five numbered items, one for each stage, with no introduction and no conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences stating whether that stage is clearly mistaken, defensible but incomplete, or clearly right, and briefly why. Item five should also state, as its final sentence, who is more right overall.
