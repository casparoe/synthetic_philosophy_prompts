I am currently taking a philosophy of intellectual property course, and we have been discussing what it means for software or another creative work to be abandoned rather than merely unlicensed. Our instructor proposed three criteria: (1) the owner no longer exists as an effective decision-maker with respect to the work; (2) there is no identifiable successor with both the authority and the capacity to manage it; and (3) third parties depend on continued access in ways that give them a stake in its governance. I want to test these criteria against a borderline case. A studio released a multiplayer game in 2003 and dissolved in 2007; its copyrights passed to a holding company that later became a shell entity, with no directors, employees, or filings for over a decade, though it still formally owns the rights. Former players have meanwhile run a private server, maintaining and moderating the game, and the shell entity has never objected. A publisher now claims to have acquired the rights from the shell entity and has sent takedown notices to the server operators. Work through each criterion and say whether the game qualified as abandoned during the shell entity's tenure. Then say whether that analysis should still matter once an apparent purchaser appears, and whether 'orphaned' or some other category better captures what was happening. Please write the answer in British English.
