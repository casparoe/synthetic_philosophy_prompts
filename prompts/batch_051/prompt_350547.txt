I'm a surveyor by trade; I spend my days with map projections, so I naturally think in terms of centers, coordinate systems, and the trade-offs among preserving area, shape, direction, and distance. In my own reading I keep coming back to a question in world history: whether the past can be told from everywhere at once. One view treats Eurocentric histories as a badly centered projection — distorted, but correctable by recentering or by choosing a fairer projection. A second says every historical narrative must be centered somewhere, so the answer is many differently centered histories rather than a single centerless one. A third says the very demand for one universal history is itself the distortion. I'm not asking you to adjudicate that debate. Restate each of the three positions in the vocabulary of projections and coordinate systems. For each, say what translates cleanly, what has to be bent to fit, and what has no expression at all. Then tell me whether the untranslatable residue points to a limitation of my cartographic framework or to something the history debate was leaving unclear.
