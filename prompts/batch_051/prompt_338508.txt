I keep going back and forth on a parallel and I want someone to check me on it. Hacking's looping effects in psychiatry say that when you classify people, the classification isnt a neutral label. The people classified find out about the category, react to it, identify with it or reject it, doctors and schools and courts reorganize around it, and eventually the category itself starts to carve out a different kind of person than it did at first. The loop changes the looped: the classification alters the thing it was supposed to classify. This remided me of Goodhart's law, the idea in economics and management that when you take a measure and make it a target, people game the measure, so it stops tracking the underlying thing it was supposed to track — test scores, sales numbers, hospital wait times, whatever.

The obvious shape is the same: a representation is introduced to track some underlying reality; the represented people respond to the representation; the representation therefore stops being a faithful description. But I cant tell whether that shared shape is deep or whether I'm just pattern-matching on the phrase feedback loop. So: give me a precise mapping between the two if you think one exists. What would have to be true on each side for results to transfer from one to the other? And where does the mapping actually break? For instance, does Goodhart require an explicit incentive attached to the metric, while looping works through identity and self-understanding with no reward at all? Or does looping require the classified kind to be part of a persons self-concept, whereas Goodhart cases can involve people who dont care about the metric, only the reward? Are those real differences or just two descriptions of the same mechanism from different angles? Tell me honestly if the analogy is shallow.
