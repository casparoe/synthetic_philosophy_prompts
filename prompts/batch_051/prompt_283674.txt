I got into an argument with a grad student about the old stone question — weather God could make a stone so heavy he couldn't lift it — and I noticed every time I shored up one part of what I think, another part went soft. So I'm writing the whole position down and asking someone to tell me where the actual cracks are.

My commitments, as best I can state them: (1) omnipotence means God can do anything that is logically possible — not absolutely anything (so no making 2+2=5), but the full range of actions logic permits. (2) The action type making a stone too heavy for the maker to lift is a logically possible action, since plenty of limited beings in other possible worlds could perform it and the description has no internal contradiction. (3) But God cannot perform that action, because God is omnipotent, and performing it would mean God could no longer lift some stone God previously could — a loss of power, not a power. (4) The way to dissolve the apparent paradox is to notice that an action can be logically possible for a limited agent without being logically possible for an omnipotent agent; what counts as a possible action depends on who is acting. (5) The paradox only has teeth if someone defines omnipotence as the power to actualize any action-description whatsoever, and I don't think anyone has to accept that definition.

I'd like an audit of this exact package, not a general primer on omnipotence. For each real tension, say how severe it is and what would have to give; if something only looks like a tension, say why it dissolves; and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Format: exactly five numbered findings, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion, ordered from the biggest problem to the smallest.
