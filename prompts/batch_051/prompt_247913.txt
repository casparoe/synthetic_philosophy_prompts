The image of two hands each drawing the other is easy to gesture at, but the hard question is what it looks like in an actual rule-governed system: what keeps a tangled hierarchy from collapsing into a single level that talks about itself, and what makes an inviolate level genuinely inviolate rather than merely the unspoken next level up. I want a case built to answer that.

Construct a small, concrete, non-mathematical system — invented rather than an existing body — drawn from ordinary institutional or software practice: version control, a contract between two processes, a standards body, a modular build system, or something similar. The system has two levels, L1 and L2, and must meet these specifications:

1. L1 can change the rules under which L2 operates.
2. L2 can change the rules under which L1 operates.
3. Neither L1 nor L2 can change a third body of rules, R.
4. R is what prevents the mutual ability to change each other's rules from degrading into infinite renegotiation or a unilateral takeover.
5. R is not a higher court that outranks L1 and L2. Its inviolability must come from something other than rank — for example, because it lies outside the ordering altogether, or because each level can recognize the other's revisions as revisions only by presupposing R.

Constraints: no sentence that directly refers to itself, no Gödel numbering, no science fiction, and the setup should fit in a single paragraph.

After presenting the scenario, do the following. Trace one complete cycle in which L1 changes L2's rules and L2 changes L1's, and identify the exact point at which R does its work. Show what would happen if R became revisable — would the system regress, collapse, or continue silently? Then answer the two dismissive readings of the case: the reductionist who says R is really a hidden top level with cleaner terminology, and the skeptic who says R does no independent work because the stability is already explained by the participants' incentives or habits. Finally, name the single structural feature that makes R inviolate in a way those two objections miss, and say how far that feature generalizes to other tangled hierarchies.
