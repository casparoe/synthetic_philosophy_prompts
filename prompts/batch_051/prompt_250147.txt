I work on an infrastructure team and also sit in on a political-theory reading group, and over the past month the same sentence has been used to settle arguments in both rooms: "Chesterton's fence" — don't tear down a fence until you know why it was put up. In one case it stopped a proposal to delete a legacy software module that everyone agreed was poorly written; in the other it was aimed at a plan to restructure a small academic department, on the grounds that Burkean caution about received institutions should make us presume the structure exists for reasons we haven't yet recovered. I found myself unsettled both times, not because the point was obviously wrong, but because it seemed to do different work in each case — sometimes a modest counsel to investigate before acting, sometimes a heavy presumption in favor of whatever already exists.
