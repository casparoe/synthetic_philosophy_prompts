I'm putting together a thought experiment for my high school's philosophy journal and want a careful, mechanism-level prediction rather than a summary of positions. Consider a world otherwise like ours in which, starting this year, every ten-year-old must take a cheap brain-scan that classifies people according to a trait researchers call sphexishness. The name comes from the often-repeated story of a digger wasp that, when an experimenter moves its paralyzed cricket a few inches from the burrow, repeats its nest-check routine instead of simply dragging the cricket in. In the thought experiment, a person is classified as sphexish if their learned abilities run as fixed programs: they execute complicated routines smoothly when the present situation matches a stored pattern, but when novelty or a small mismatch arrives they cannot recombine parts of old skills, so they restart the routine, follow it literally, or freeze. A flexible generalist can take a skill apart and reassemble it for a new problem. These categories are not new; people have always been distributed this way, and both types can be articulate, intelligent, and fully convinced that they are choosing freely. What is new is that everyone's score is public, exact, and treated as an official fact about each person. Predict the next thirty years. Write your answer as a bare numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction, conclusion, or extra commentary. Cover these five areas, one per item: (1) how the philosophical debate about whether free will is compatible with determinism shifts; (2) how ordinary practices of praise, blame, and promising change; (3) how schools and employers handle the classification; (4) how the two groups come to regard and treat each other; and (5) how a person's sense of their own decisions changes after learning their score. For each item, make the prediction concrete, name the mechanism or incentive driving it, and include a short clause stating how confident you are and what would have to be true for the prediction to be wrong. Please write in American English.
