I am a novelist, and I want a thought experiment built to my specifications. I have been rereading W. E. B. Du Bois’s 1926 essay Criteria of Negro Art. Suppose I accept his central claim that all art is propaganda, and also his further claim that the proper propaganda for Black art is not flattering idealization but the truthful representation of Black life in its full range—its suffering, its anger, its beauty, its contradictions. Now construct a case where an artist who fully accepts this view must, precisely in order to make the most politically effective propaganda, create a scene or image that a simpler reader of Du Bois would denounce as harmful to racial uplift because it appears to reinforce a familiar racist stereotype. Constraints: the medium is novel-writing, playwriting, painting, or song; the scenario must be historically plausible and realistic, not science-fictional or allegorical; the artist’s choice must flow from Du Bois’s own principle rather than from cynicism, self-indulgence, or error; and the scenario should isolate the feature that separates truthful propaganda from uplift propaganda. After setting out the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each of these specifications: state exactly what the Du Boisian principle requires in the case and why, what the uplift reading would require, and which specific feature of the scenario is doing the work. Then tell me what happens if that feature is removed, and how a defender of the uplift reading would attack the scenario.
