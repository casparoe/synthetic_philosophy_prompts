I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and two engineers on my team got into it in code review over whether the order of the roots returned by a quadratic solver is arbitrary. The function returns `[(-b + sqrt(D))/(2a), (-b - sqrt(D))/(2a)]`; for x^2 - 2 = 0 it came back as [1.414..., -1.414...] on our machines but reversed on a different OS, and a unit test that asserted the first root was positive broke.

(1) Priya said the test was wrong to require the positive root first, because the two roots are algabraically indistinguishable over Q: any polynomial over Q that has one as a root also has the other. So "the positive root" is extra structure, not something given by the equation.

(2) Marcus said that's overdramatizing. The two roots aren't indistinguishable in the real world: one is bigger than zero and one is smaller. sqrt(2) is by definition the positive one, so the test is just checking that the solver returns what every math textbook means by sqrt. If a platform hands them back reversed, sort them.

(3) Priya said sorting works only because the reals are ordered. In the field Q(√2) the two roots are completely symmetric: a + b√2 ↦ a - b√2 is an automorphism fixing Q, so any algebraic statement true of one is true of the other. Which root gets to be "positive" is decided by which of the two embeddings into R you choose; the equation itself doesn't force it.

(4) Marcus said that only matters if you insist on doing field theory. For x^2 + 1 = 0 you can also separate the roots by the sign of the imaginary part: i is above the real line, -i is below. We're writing numerical code, not a Galois theory textbook.

(5) Priya said the i/-i case proves her point: conjugation swaps them and fixes everything real, so "above" vs. "below" just assumes we've already oriented the plane. There's no algebraic property that picks out one of the two; the labels are convention.

(6) Marcus said fine, but the test was about real roots, and there the positive root is a genuine canonical choice. He said Priya was moving to a higher level of abstraction and then complaining that the canonical choice doesn't survive the move.

I didn't referee it then, but I keep turning it over. Please adjudicate this move by move: who's right about which point, where does each overstate, and what, if anything, are they both missing? Also, is there a single fact about what "canonical" means here that would settle most of it?
