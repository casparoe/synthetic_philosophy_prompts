I'm in an ethics of migration course and last week we spent the whole seminar on the word "refugee" without actually landing anywhere. People had super quick intuitions about the stock examples but the second someone asked why, they all unraveled. I want a working definition of "refugee," not a dictionary gloss. It obviously has to count a journalist who left after her paper was shut down and her colleagues were arrested, and a family from an ethnic minority being rounded up by their own government. It obviously shouldn't count an engineer who moves abroad because the pay is way better, or a tourist who overstays quietly to take a job. But it also has to produce a definate verdict on the cases that actually came up in class and derailed us:

a farming village on a coast that's slowly going underwater from sea-level rise, whose own government is too broke to move them; a teenager fleeing a cartel that runs her town because the police are too corrupt or intimidated to protect her; a gay man from a country where same-sex conduct is technically illegal but the law is almost never enforced, who says being outed would get him killed by neighbors; somebody crossing a border during an all-out civil war where no side is singling him out personally; and a family walking out of a famine after the state has collapsed and there just isn't food.

I'm confident on the obvious ones and genuinely lost on the middle five. Part of me suspects the word is doing two jobs at once — a descriptive-legal one and a moral one about who we owe protection to — and getting a definition means choosing which job you're doing or admitting they split. So propose the definition, run it through all the cases, flag anywhere it gives the wrong answer or no answer at all, then either fix it or defend the surprising verdict. If the two jobs do come apart, say which cases land in the moral category but not the legal one or vice versa, and whether a rich country is still obliged to take in people your definition excludes. Then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure.
