So I just got home from a long argument with a friend about whether there's any real difference between manipulation and legitimate persuasion. He thinks the whole distinction is just status games, and he left me with an argument I couldn't answer. I cant shake the sense that something goes wrong in the middle but I couldn't say where in the moment.

(1) An influence is manipulation when it works by bypassing or subverting the target's rational capacities; it's legitimate persuasion when the target's rational capacities are the thing doing the work.

(2) In any actual case of persuasion, I don't neutrally dump every relevant consideration on the person. I choose which reasons to mention, in what order, with what emphasis, precisely because I want them to arrive at a certain view.

(3) So the factors that actually determine what the target ends up believing are factors I selected — my choice of reasons, ordering, and framing — not the target's own assessment of the merits.

(4) If someone's rational capacities are working on a slate of considerations selected and framed by someone else for the purpose of producing a particular belief, then those capacities aren't really doing the work; the selector is.

(5) Therefore even respectful, reason-giving persuasion works by bypassing or subverting the target's rational capacities.

(6) So legitimate persuasion is just manipulation that we happen to find socially acceptable.

Tell me whether this argument is valid, and if not, which numbered step is the first one that fails and why. If it is valid, which premise should I reject? I half-suspect step 3 or 4 is sliding on "the thing doing the work," but I can't make the objection precise.
