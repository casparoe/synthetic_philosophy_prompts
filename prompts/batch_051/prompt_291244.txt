After retiring from a career in the natural sciences, I have been reading cognitive science and the philosophy of concepts, and one thing puzzles me: the dispute among prototype theorists, exemplar theorists, and theory theorists has run for decades without anyone seeming to change their mind. Prototype theorists take a concept to be a summary representation of the central tendency of a category, and they point to graded typicality effects, such as a robin being judged a better bird than a penguin. Exemplar theorists deny the summary and say categorization works by retrieving particular stored instances and comparing new items to them, which they claim better explains our use of idiosyncratic information about individual cases. Theory theorists say neither account captures how concepts are embedded in causal-explanatory knowledge, so that something like BIRD is really a small theory about what birds are, how they live, and what follows from being one. All three camps draw on overlapping behavioral data, and the literature now seems to be in a stable equilibrium of acknowledged mixed evidence.

My working hypothesis, formed from outside the field, is that this is not one debate but three, and that it has stalled because each side is answering a different question under one label: one is explaining the structure of category judgments, one the content and use of memory, and one the role of background knowledge in reasoning. I further suspect that all three share an undefended assumption, namely that a concept is a stored, stable, context-independent representation. If that assumption were relaxed, and concepts were treated as assemblies built in working memory from long-term resources as a situation demands, the three views would no longer be rivals but accounts of different components of a larger process.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a survey of the positions. Is the disagreement substantive, or is it largely an artifact of a shared framing, as I hypothesize? If substantive, is it a dispute about facts, about standards of evidence, about which phenomena need explaining, or about starting intuitions? And what would actually count as progress: a decisive experiment, a formal result, a sharper distinction, or a change of question? Please argue for your diagnosis, and tell me plainly if my own hypothesis is wrong or question-begging.
