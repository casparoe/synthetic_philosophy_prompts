Write a villanelle about Hofstadter's idea that the self is a strange loop—an abstract pattern that exists by referring back to itself, not a thing located anywhere in the brain. heres the constraint: the two refrains should function as the two halves of the loop, one of them a line in the voice of the 'I' making its claim and the other a line about the machinery that produces that claim, and the refrains should shift in felt meaning each time they come back as the surrounding stanzas force them into new contexts. The form should do the argumentative work: by the final couplet a reader should feel why the self can't be pinned down to either the pronoun or the process, only to the way they keep circling through each other. Avoid just versifying Gödel, Escher, Bach chapter summaries, and try not to name-drop Hofstadter, 'strange loop,' 'Gödel,' or 'Escher' in the poem itself. It should read as a real poem, but a philosopher of mind who knows Hofstadter should recognize exactly what it's doing.
