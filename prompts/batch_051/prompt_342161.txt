I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I've been reading around the idea that an action can be tested not by its own consequences but by asking whether the rule behind it could be accepted or prescribed for everyone in relevantly similar circumstances. Our platform team is proposing the following mechanism for the shared release infrastructure, and I'd like a walk-through of how it would actually behave.

Before anyone may consume more than their baseline share of the merge queue, the build farm, or the single staging environment, they must submit a sentence of the form 'Everyone may do this sort of thing when ______.' An automated generalisation check then simulates, as best it can, what would happen if every applicable user acted on that sentence; if the simulated outcome would push the shared resource past agreed failure thresholds or damage the release process, the action is refused. Users may retry with a narrower condition, but they cannot exempt themselves by name or by any predicate that only their own circumstances could satisfy.

Walk through what this would actually do in practice: the kinds of conditions people would write, how they would game it first, how the simulator's approximations and thresholds would be exploited, what second-order effects it would have on who gets the resource and on what gets shipped, where the rule would break, and whether the failure modes are fixable by adjusting it.
