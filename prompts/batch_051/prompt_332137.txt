Ordinary and legal practice treats a harmful output very differently depending on whether the system that produced it is characterized as a tool or as an agent. A clinician who follows a dosing calculator's recommendation and harms a patient is blamed as the decision-maker, while a hospital deploying an autonomous infusion system finds blame directed at the vendor or the system itself, even when the data supplied, the action taken, and the available warning signs are identical. Likewise, a driver is told to remain responsible while using a lane-keeping assistance feature but is assured that an autonomous driving system takes over responsibility. What, if anything, justifies this asymmetry? Is being an agent a real property of the system, or a stance we adopt for reasons of liability, marketing, or social coordination? Give the strongest justification you can for the practice, test it against the hard case where a tool and an agent are behaviorally indistinguishable and differ only in their label or interface, and say whether the asymmetry should be kept, narrowed, or abandoned.
