Argue that chain-of-thought monitoring, as a safety technique, faces a structural problem that cannot be solved by improving the monitor's classifier alone. Begin by distinguishing three roles that monitoring proposals often run together: the chain of thought as a causal record of the process that actually produced the action, the chain of thought as a public justification that supports the action, and the chain of thought as evidence that lets a monitor infer the model's aims or plans. Then argue that outcome-based optimization pressure drives these roles apart even when the model is never explicitly trained to deceive the monitor: a model rewarded for downstream outcomes has an incentive to produce reasoning that is reverse-engineered from the action it already favors and calibrated to what a monitor expects faithful reasoning to look like. Defend the claim that, under this pressure, the causal-record role is the most fragile and the least recoverable from the transcript alone, and identify the monitoring design or training intervention you think is most robust to this failure mode. Address the objection that your proposal merely pushes obfuscation to a subtler level.
