I keep returning to a simple thought about promising—that its moral force is a matter of the expectations the promisor knowingly creates—but every time I try to spell the thought out, I get an argument that seems to prove far too much, and I cannot tell which step should go.

(1) When a promise binds, it binds because the promisor has knowingly led the promisee to expect the promised performance, and the promisee's expectation is traceable to that act rather than to independent evidence about the promisor's character.
(2) If knowingly leading another person to expect a course of conduct is what grounds promissory obligation, then that same ground is present whenever an act knowingly causes another person to expect a course of conduct.
(3) A credible threat—someone says to a shopkeeper that unless she hands over the till, he will smash the window—knowingly causes the victim to expect the threatened conduct, and that expectation is what gives the threat its force.
(4) So, by (2), the threatener stands under the same sort of obligation to smash the window as a promisor stands under to perform what was promised.
(5) But that is outrageous: the shopkeeper is not wronged if the threatener fails to smash the window after she refuses; if anything, the threatener is obligated not to smash it.
(6) Therefore (1) must be false. Yet if (1) is false, I no longer see what is left of the most natural explanation of why promises bind—and the remaining candidates, which locate the force of promising in a social practice or in the promisor's own act of will, seem to face troubles of their own. It looks as if either threats bind or promises do not, and I cannot accept either.

I would most like to keep (1) and (5); that seems to force the trouble into (2), (3), or (4), but I want to know exactly what must be added or withdrawn and whether the repair is principled rather than ad hoc. Please identify the step or steps that should be rejected, explain why they looked plausible, and price the alternatives: what does each major account of promising have to give up to avoid the conclusion, and does the repair still handle promises on which the promisee never in fact relies? A good answer will also say which parts of this debate are settled and which are genuinely contested among people working on it, and will not present a contested diagnosis as the consensus.
