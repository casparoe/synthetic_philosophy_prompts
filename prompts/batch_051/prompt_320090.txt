In the Jäsche Logic, Kant marks the distinction between intuitions and concepts by their form: an intuition is a singular representation, while a concept is a general representation, a representation of what is common to several objects. Something about that division reminds me of the difference between a fitted classifier and a sample in statistical learning. A trained classifier is general in almost exactly this sense: it represents what many training cases have in common, through its parameters, rather than any one case; an input feature vector is singular. But Kant does not take concepts to be mere summaries of shared properties; he takes them to be spontaneous rules for unifying a multiplicity of given intuitions. A classifier likewise functions as a rule for sorting new inputs, not as a memory of old ones. Is this parallel deep enough to be precise, or is it only a family resemblance? If it is precise, what does the Kantian claim that concepts are rules for the unity of intuitions become on the learning-theoretic side, and what would a case have to look like for the mapping to break?
