I'm outlining a novel and I've been reading way to much fringe history for it — Atlantis, ancient aliens, the "everything you know about the timeline is wrong" crowd — and I keep noticing a move I don't have a word for. Someone will make a very concrete claim: these blocks couldn't have been cut with the tools available, a 16th-century map shows an ice-free Antarctic coast. When you point out that the claim is wrong or at least not established, the big conclusion — lost high-tech civilization, alien contact, whatever — doesn't lose anything. It just slides to a different block or a different map. It's like the conclusion isn't supported by the evidence so much as spread across it, so no single failure ever counts against it. Worse, the same person treats every unexplained gap in the mainstream account as fatal, while every unexplained gap in their own account gets treated as another clue in its favor. Is there a name for this? I keep wanting to say moving the goalposts, but there the standard for success keeps moving; here the support keeps moving while the claim is never really exposed. If theres an established concept that captures it, I want to know how it differs from the obvious neighbors and which fits best; if not, name it and define it so the boundaries are clear. Also tell me how confident you are in what you claim and which point you're least sure about.
