I'm preparing a seminar handout on criticisms of the Darwinian theory of culture — the family of views, associated with Dawkins, Dennett, and Blackmore, on which cultural items ('memes') replicate, vary, and undergo selection in a way sufficiently gene-like to support Darwinian explanation. The serious objections I have collected so far are these: (1) the unit problem, that memes have no principled boundaries and can be individuated at nested levels from a four-note hook to an entire symphony, with no cultural analogue of the gene as a privileged replicator; (2) the fidelity problem, that cultural transmission is too error-prone and reconstructive to supply the high-fidelity copying that cumulative selection appears to require; (3) the Lamarckian problem, that modifications a meme acquires in one host are typically transmitted to the next, so variation and inheritance are not kept separate in the way the Darwinian model demands; (4) the missing genotype–phenotype distinction, that there seems to be no meme analogue of a replicator sheltered from its vehicle, so selection lacks a stable underlying unit to act on; and (5) the reticulation problem, that cultural items are copied from many sources and recombined, so culture does not form the branching, single-parent lineages a Darwinian tree requires. I would like a sixth objection that is not on this list and is not merely a special case, restatement, or combination of the five. It should identify a difficulty the others do not already cover. State the objection precisely, explain why it is a problem for the memetic program in particular, and say whether you regard it as stronger or weaker than the five above, and why.
