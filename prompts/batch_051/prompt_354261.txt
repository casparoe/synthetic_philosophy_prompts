A colleague defends geometric rationality as a theory of how to act under repeated risk. On her view, an agent who maximises expected value across a sequence of favourable but risky gambles is making a mistake, because expected value tracks the ensemble average of possible outcomes, whereas the quantity that governs what actually happens to a single agent over repeated gambles is the time-average growth rate; the rational policy is therefore to maximise expected log wealth, i.e. to bet Kelly. It seems to me that this dispute may be only partly empirical: it could turn on a mathematical point, on which averaging procedure correctly represents a single agent's situation, or on what 'rationality' means in the first place. Design the strongest test or argument one could use to determine how much of this dispute is substantive. Be concrete about the conditions under which expected-value maximising and Kelly betting genuinely diverge, say what a formal or empirical result would and would not show, and identify the part of the disagreement that no test could settle in principle.
