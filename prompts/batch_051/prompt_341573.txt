I run a small grant program for formal social epistemology, and next cycle I can fund two theorists for a year on the epistemic-voting side. The board is mostly practitioners from deliberative minipublics, and the same thing happens every meeting: someone waves at the Condorcet jury theorem to justify majority rule or scaling up group size, and nobody in the room can actually say how much work the competence or independence assumptions are doing. So I need to write a call that tells applicants which open questions in this area matter, not a survey of every extension anyone has tried. My own rough list: how sensitive the result is to all voters sharing the same competence p; whether correlation between votes is always fatal or can sometimes help; strategic voting when voters care about winning rather than truth; multi-option or structured decisions where the theorem doesn't even apply as stated; and whether the asymptotic results mean anything at all for a real jury of 12 or an assembly of 100. I want you to rank the two or three you'd put in the call and defend the ordering: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractible with probability and logic rather than data, and which would actually change how practitioners run these bodies. Then tell me which of the others you'd leave out and why.
