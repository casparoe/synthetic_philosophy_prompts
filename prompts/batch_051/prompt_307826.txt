We have an odd asymmetry about small broken commitments. If a friend says she'll call at three and doesn't call until 3:40, I'd say she broke a promise and should apologize. If the same friend says she'll meet me at the café at three and walks in at 3:40, I'd say she was late—I probably wouldn't call it a broken promise at all, even though the commitment looks the same: a person, an agreed time, an agreed action. Both leave me waiting forty minutes, and I don't think the waiting feels clearly worse in one case than the other. What distinction am I actually tracking? Could be that showing up has softer temporal edges than a phone call, or that seeing her at all makes the lateness retroactively fine, or just that I care less about one of them. Give me the strongest principle that would justify treating these two cases differently, test it against nearby cases (a film with a hard start time, a call that was explicitly open-ended), and tell me whether the asymmetry is worth keeping or is just social fiction.
