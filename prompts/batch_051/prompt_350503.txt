A forum is considering the following reputation reform, and I want to analyze it for a piece on platform design. Every user gets one upvote or downvote per post, as before, but each user also accumulates a public taste score based only on early votes: votes cast before a post reaches ten net upvotes or is two hours old. An early upvote on a post that later reaches the front page raises the score; an early upvote on a post that later sinks lowers it; the reverse holds for early downvotes. The score does not make a user's votes count for more. Instead, it determines how prominently the user's own posts are shown to others, so that reliable early spotters get more initial visibility for what they write. The stated rationale is that users who can identify quality ahead of the crowd are likely to produce quality themselves. Walk through how this would actually behave: what it incentivizes among ordinary users, power users, and moderators; how it would be gamed; what it does to early, risky, or niche content; and whether its failure modes can be fixed without giving up on ranking contributors by their early-vote record. Please separate what follows fairly directly from the mechanism from what is genuinely contested among people who study reputation systems, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
