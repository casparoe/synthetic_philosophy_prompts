I'm trying to settle a case about harm, and I keep going back and forth. Here are three criteria for when an act harms a person that I've seen floated, maybe a little loosely:

1. Comparative harm: an act harms a person only if it leaves them worse off than they would have been if the act hadn't been performed.

2. Threshold harm: an act harms a person if it causes them to be in a state below some threshold of well-being — but I don't know whether the threshold should be a life not worth living, a minimally decent life, or a typical life for their society. Depending on where the threshold sits, this case comes out differently.

3. Existence-sensitive comparative harm: same as 1, except the comparison is only defined when that same person exists in the alternative. If the person wouldn't have existed at all had the act not been performed, the question of whether they're worse off has no well-defined answer — so a harm claim would be undefined, not false.

Now the borderline case. Maya has a medical condition her doctors understand completely: if she conceives now, any child she has will be born with a particular congenital impairment. The impairment is not catastrophic. The child's life would be worth living overall — loving relationships, meaningful projects, real pleasures — but with chronic pain, reduced mobility, and steady social exclusion, so the child's expected well-being is clearly below a typical life. If Maya waits three months, the risk of this impairment drops to the population baseline, and the child would almost certainly be healthy. But if she waits, it will be a different child — different egg, different sperm. Maya decides not to wait, and her daughter Leah is born with the impairment.

So: does Maya's decision harm Leah? Go through the criteria honestly. Where a criterion gives a definite answer, say whether you actually endorse it or whether the criterion just happens to give the intuitive result here. Where a criterion doesn't give a definite answer, say whether that's a problem with the criterion or with the case. And say whether the case shows we need a fourth criterion, or a distinction between harming and wronging, or weather we should just accept that the decision doesn't harm Leah even if it might be criticizable some other way.
