I've been trying to get my head around Gould and Lewontin's Spandrels paper and keep getting stuck on a particular move they make. In the opening pages they describe the central dome of San Marco, the four arches that support it, and the tapering triangular spaces left over where the dome meets the arches — the actual spandrels. They point out the mosaics in those spaces are elaborate, but the spaces themselves weren't designed to hold the mosaics; they're just geometrically necessary once you've chosen that dome-on-arches construction. Then they say something like a biologist who sees a trait and immediately asks "what is it for?" is like an observer looking at those mosaics and treating the spandrels as if they were built for the purposes of the mosaic work.

What I want to understand is what exactly this analogy is supposed to establish. Is it a claim about the cognitive bias of adaptationist researchers, or is it a claim about the evidential standards needed to infer adaptation? Like, does the critique actually challenge the adaptionist research programme, or does it just demand more rigorous evidence before saying "trait X was selected for function Y"? Some people on the evo psych side of the rationalist blogosphere have said the paper is overrated because it conflates "not yet proven adaptive" with "not adaptive." I want to know if that's a fair reading.

Can you unpack the structure of the argument, being specific about what Gould and Lewontin mean by the "adaptationist programme," and whether the spandrels analogy shows anything stronger than the obvious point that current utility doesn't automatically reveal historical origin? Try to steelman both the original critique and the standard rebuttal, but I'm mainly interested in whether the critique has a valid core that survives once you grant the obvious points about needing evidence. Also, is there something specific about the architectural analogy that does or doesn't carry over to organisms, given that organisms aren't designed by a conscious architect choosing among competing structural plans?
