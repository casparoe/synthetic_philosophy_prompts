I'm a programming languages engineer — I live in types, effect systems, invariants, preconditions and postconditions, exceptions, and the odd monad. A philosopher friend keeps trying to explain J. L. Austin's speech act theory to me, and I keep telling him it sounds like effectful function calls with preconditions. He says I'm flattening it, so I'd like you to actually do the translation properly and then tell me what didn't survive.

Here's the picture as I understand it. Austin starts from the observation that some utterances dont describe the world so much as do something in being said: promising, ordering, christening, marrying, betting. He calls these performative, in contrast to constative utterances that just describe. He also carves every speech act into three: the locutionary act (the saying of something), the illocutionary act (the act done in saying it, e.g. promising or ordering), and the perlocutionary act (what it produces in the hearer, e.g. persuading or alarming). A performative goes wrong not by being false but by being infelicitous: the procedure may not exist, the speaker may lack the standing, the circumstances may not cooperate, and so on, which he splits into misfires and abuses. Eventually he seems to drop the performative/constative split and treat all utterances as acts.

Restate this in the vocabulary of programming language semantics as faithfully as you can — types for the different act kinds, preconditions and postconditions for felicity conditions, exceptions or failed preconditions for misfires, state transitions for the social facts created, effects or monads for sequencing, whatever is genuinely apt — and then give me a residue report. Which distinctions carry over cleanly, and how? Which did you have to fudge, and which have no counterpart at all? I especially want to know where the social bit lands: authority, uptake by the hearer, the commitment the speaker takes on. Does it correspond to capabilities, permissions, session types, transaction semantics, or does it need something my vocabulary genuinely can't express? And the punchline: is the residue evidence that my vocabulary is missing something important, or that Austin's picture is less precise than it pretends to be?
