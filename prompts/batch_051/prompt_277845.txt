I'm a law and philosophy PhD student, and I'm TA-ing the discussion section for an undergrad Philosophy of Law course (16 students, 80 minutes). We spent the last two weeks on corporate personhood, and my students keep coming back to the same question: "ok but is the AI a person or not?" I want a session that breaks that binary. The thing I want them to feel their way into is that legal personhood isn't a single on/off status — it's a bundle of seperate legal capacities (standing to sue, being held liable, owning property, entering contracts, constitutional rights, etc.), and corporations got different pieces of the bundle at different times, for different reasons. So I'd like an activity where the group has to decide, capacity by capacity, which pieces of the bundle they'd extend to an autonomous AI system, and on what grounds — not "is it a person" in general. Can you design that as a structured role-play or mock committee, with roles, a rough timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the bundle view and the corporate-person analogy? Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of the analogy the activity can't reproduce (my worry: corporations are ultimately groups of humans, and an AI isn't). No lecture up front, and keep the term "bundle theory" for the debrief.
