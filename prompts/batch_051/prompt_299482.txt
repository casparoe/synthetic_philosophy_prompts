I've been thinking on my own for a while about the old dilemma that asks whether things are good because God commands them, or whether God commands them because they are good. I've come to rely on a distinction I call the difference between a fact's source and a fact's test, and I'd like an honest assessment of it. A thermometer does not make a room hot; it tells you the temperature. I want to say the same about God's commands and morality: God's commands are not what makes moral facts true — goodness stands on its own, so God commands what he commands because it is good — but God's commands are the test of moral facts, the way goodness is made known to us, and in fact the only fully reliable test we have. That means both horns of the dilemma capture something and both overreach: one side is right that God answers to an independent standard, and the other side is right that God's commanding is central to how we come to know right from wrong, but neither relation can do the other's job. Does this hold together, or does it collapse once you press on it? Has someone already worked it out under another name? And give me the strongest objections, including at least one from an ordinary religious believer and one from an atheist — is any of them fatal rather than just uncomfortable?
