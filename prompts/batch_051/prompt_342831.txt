I have ended up with the following package of views, and I would like a consistency audit before I have to defend it. I am interested in whether the claims hang together on their own, not in whether they match some historical figure's system.

(1) The world as I encounter it is a structured representation, while the underlying reality is a blind, aimless striving that is also my own inner nature.
(2) Because willing always arises from a felt lack, and because any satisfaction is temporary, suffering is the default condition of conscious life, not a contingent result of bad circumstances.
(3) Aesthetic experience is valuable because it briefly quiets willing and allows a will-less contemplation of things.
(4) Compassion is the only genuinely moral motive, because in it the apparent separation between oneself and another is recognized as a feature of the representation rather than of the underlying reality.
(5) The highest response to the human condition is the denial of the will: an ascetic renunciation in which the individual's willing is extinguished.
(6) Nevertheless, the will is the sole underlying reality and cannot be destroyed.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance dissolves, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify the cheapest repair and what that repair costs elsewhere in the package.
