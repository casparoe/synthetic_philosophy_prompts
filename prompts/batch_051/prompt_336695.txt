I work for a humanitarian organization that produces visual instructions for people who may not share a common language or have high literacy — things like how to purify water, how to recognize unexploded ordnance, how to take a child's malaria medication. We commission illustrators, but their drafts keep coming back beautiful and almost completely unreadable: perspective views, three-quarter angles, decorative backgrounds, tiny little details. I've been reading Otto Neurath's International Picture Language (1936), and there's a passage where he says, roughly, that a picture language has to be built up from a fixed set of signs so that a statement can be read directly from the picture without a key, the way words are read; realistic detail and perspective actually work against this because the eye gets caught by accidental features instead of the sign. I find it convincing but I don't know how to turn it into something I can actually use when I'm looking at a draft. Please write a decision procedure my team could actually run on a proposed pictogram before we send it to field testing. The questions to ask in order, what counts as passing each one, where the procedure stops with a "no, redraw," and where it hands the decision to a person because it's a judgment call about meaning rather than a mechanical check. We review dozens a week, and the person doing the initial screen has maybe ten minutes per symbol and no formal training in semiotics. Then run the procedure on three examples: a pictogram showing a person pouring water from a jerry can at a 45-degree angle with realistic shadows; a pictogram showing a stick figure with a square body and no face next to a crossed-out artillery shell; and a pictogram showing a mother smiling while spooning medicine into a baby's mouth. I suspect one of these will pass under the procedure even though our field staff would call it to abstract. Flag the cases the procedure can't settle, and be honest about where Neurath's assumptions about universality might be doing more work than the checklist itself. Also, please say how confident you are in the specific recommendations and single out the point you are least sure of.
