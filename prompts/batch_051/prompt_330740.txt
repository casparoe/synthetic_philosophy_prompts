Draft the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration would produce between two camps on non-disclosure agreements in government. The first camp holds that because governmental authority is delegated from the public, NDAs binding public employees, contractors, or officials are presumptively illegitimate except in narrowly specified cases; the second camp holds that confidentiality in government is itself a public good because it enables candid advice, effective negotiation, and the responsible handling of sensitive information. A friend and I have been arguing about this for months—most recently over a city contract that bars a contractor from releasing a consultant's report on a water-utility failure to ratepayers—and we have decided to stop talking past each other and draft a document we could both sign. Please write that document for us.

It should include four parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: about the parties involved, what NDAs actually bind in public and semipublic settings, which governance values are in play, and what standard transparency and accountability arguments do and do not establish; these must be substantive and specific, not platitudes. Second, a disagreements table stating the precise residual disagreements, each formulated so that the side who holds it would accept the wording of its own position. Third, a crux section identifying the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one of us—for example, whether the public's right to know attaches to outcomes, deliberations, or both; whether ex post release of a consultant's report would chill future candor more than it improves accountability; and whether a public-interest override should be applied case by case or as a bright-line rule. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both of us would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts the evidence would show. Fourth, flag any part of the dispute that is partly verbal—for instance, competing uses of "public," "deliberative," or "transparency"—and say which part is verbal and which is not. Do not adjudicate which side is right. Keep the whole document to at most 1,400 words.
