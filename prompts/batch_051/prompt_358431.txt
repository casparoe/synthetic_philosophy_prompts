I recently read a non-technical economics blog's explanation of Douglas Hofstadter's idea of superrationality, and I would like help checking how much of it I can rely on. The blog's account went something like this:

"Standard game theory holds that in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma each player should defect, since defection yields a better outcome than cooperation regardless of what the other player does. Hofstadter's superrationality is meant to overturn this. A superrational player assumes that the other player is just as rational, will run through the same reasoning, and will therefore arrive at the same move. Consequently, the only outcomes worth taking seriously are the symmetric ones: both cooperate or both defect. Because each player prefers mutual cooperation to mutual defection, a superrational player cooperates. Cooperation is thus the dominant strategy for a superrational player, and two superrational players will always cooperate with each other. This, the blog says, is what allows cooperation to emerge in repeated Prisoner's Dilemmas of the sort Robert Axelrod studied in his tournaments. The upshot is that defection is rational only in a narrow and defective sense of 'rational,' and the Prisoner's Dilemma dissolves once the players are genuinely rational."

I find that summary persuasive in broad outline, but I have also seen superrationality described as a subtle idea that exactly this kind of popular gloss badly muddles, and I am not sure where the muddle is. Please give me an accuracy audit of the passage. Go through it claim by claim and separate out: what is accurate; what is a natural but significant simplification; and what a defender of Hofstadter's distinction between standard rationality and superrationality would say has been garbled. In particular, I would like to know whether it is legitimate to call cooperation the dominant strategy for a superrational player, whether superrationality has anything to do with cooperation in repeated games of Axelrod's sort, and whether the final claim that the dilemma dissolves for genuinely rational players follows from the corrected version of the view.
