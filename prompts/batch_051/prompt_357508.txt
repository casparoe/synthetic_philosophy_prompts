Consider two people who treat a colleague identically well in every outward respect: both listen, cooperate, and share credit, and she never learns of any difference between them. The first privately holds her in contempt, convinced she is beneath serious consideration; the second has no contempt, but no attitude of recognition either — she simply never registers to him as someone whose judgment and standing matter. Most of us would judge the first more harshly, though the outward behavior is identical and neither inward state is under direct voluntary control. That looks like an asymmetry in ordinary moral judgment: we condemn the presence of a bad attitude more than the absence of a good one, even when both are unexpressed. Is anything justifying it? Supply the strongest justification you can, test it against nearby cases, and say whether you would keep, reverse, or abandon the asymmetry.
