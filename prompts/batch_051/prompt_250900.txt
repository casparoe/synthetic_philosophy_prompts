Been prepping a lecture about truth in fiction and empty names, and I've noticed my own verdicts on individual sentences dont obviously hang together. I'd like you to find the principle behind them, or tell me which verdicts I should abandon. My gut calls are these.

'Sherlock Holmes does not exist' — true. 'Sherlock Holmes is a famous fictional detective' — true. 'Sherlock Holmes was a detective' — not true, but not false either, whereas 'Sherlock Holmes was a fireman' is just plain false. 'In the Holmes stories, Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street' — true. 'In the Holmes stories, Holmes is a fictional character' — false. 'In the Holmes stories, Holmes has type AB negative blood' — neither true nor false; the stories simply dont settle it. 'Hamlet is more indecisive than Holmes' — true, and I'd assert it without hedging.

Is there one account of what empty names do and how truth-in-fiction works that gives me all seven? And what would it predict about the cases I keep wobbling on: 'In the Holmes stories, London has over a million inhabitants', 'Holmes is self-identical', and 'There is a detective who does not exist'? If no tidy account gets all seven, which of my gut calls goes?
