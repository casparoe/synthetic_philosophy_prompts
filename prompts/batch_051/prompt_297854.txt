Write a parody of the terms-of-service agreement for a direct-to-consumer genetic testing company (invent the name, something like "Codon & Clause"), about 700 words, formatted as a short preamble followed by numbered clauses. The satire should come from watching the company's lawyers use the fine print to settle, mostly by stipulation, whether DNA is literally a code. Work in at least these moves: a clause declaring that your genome is "written in a four-letter code" while disclaiming the existence of any writer, sender, or proofreader; a clause that calls variants "typographical errors" yet denies there is any original or intended text they deviate from; a clause distinguishing causal or statistical information from semantic meaning, deployed in whichever direction protects the company; and a clause on ownership of "your" sequence that quietly exploits the difference between copying a text and instantiating a pattern. Keep the irony deadpan throughout and let the contradictions do the work; a philosopher of biology should find it a fair satire of the genetic-code debate, while an ordinary reader should still find it legible. Do not break character or gloss the jokes.
