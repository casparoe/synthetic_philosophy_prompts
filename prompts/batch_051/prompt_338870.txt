Question: In your own words, reconstruct Pascal's claim that justice and force must be joined together. Why does he think that a merely just order would be powerless, and that a merely forceful order would be unstable or resented? Then explain what, on his view, actually gives positive law its authority, given that he denies we can reach universal agreement about what is just. Finally, classify the resulting position: is Pascal best read as a conventionalist about justice, a normative realist about justice, or neither? Defend your answer with attention to his distinction between what is true and what is useful or necessary for order.

Student answer: Pascal's starting point is that justice is not self-enforcing. People obey mostly out of habit, fear, and opinion, so a rule that is perfectly just but unenforced will be trampled as soon as it inconveniences someone powerful enough to ignore it. Rule by bare force is also brittle, because subjects will see it as nothing but violence and come to hate it, so the strong have to dress their commands up in the forms of law and legitimacy. That is why Pascal says justice and force must be united: on the one hand power should be made to look lawful, and ideally the holders of power should be made genuinely just. He isn't just describing a cosmetic trick; he wants the strong to actually become just, not merely seem just. At the same time, since human beings can never agree about what justice demands, Pascal concludes that there is no true justice, only the various customs that each society happens to have the force to enforce. The authority of actual laws therefore comes entirely from custom plus force, with no standard outside them. That makes him a conventionalist, full stop. He adds that the common people shouldn't be told this, because if the many realized that law is just convention with teeth they would stop respecting it and the whole order would collapse. But that just confirms that for him politics is really about preserving order through useful beliefs, not about realizing justice.
