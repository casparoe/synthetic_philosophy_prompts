I'm a philosophy professor, though my partner works in ML and I've picked up enough of the jargon to be dangerous. I keep hearing from there colleagues that Keynes's thing about risk versus uncertainty is just the aleatoric/epistemic distinction plus model uncertainty, and that his worries about long-term expectations are just out-of-distribution generalisation. I don't buy it, but I'd like the strongest version of their view before I argue with them.

What I want is a systematic translation. Take Keynes's views on probability and uncertainty — mainly from the Treatise on Probability but with an eye on the General Theory on animal spirits and conventions — and restate them in the vocabulary a good ML researcher actually uses: priors, posteriors, likelihoods, model classes, model evidence, calibration, sharpness, epistemic versus aleatoric uncertainty, out-of-distribution generalisation, robust or risk-sensitive optimisation, and credal sets or imprecise probabilities if that's fair game. Make sure you cover five things in particular. First, probability for him is a logical, objective relation of partial entailment, not a frequency and not a subjective degree of belief. Second, he thinks many probabilities are only partially ordered: we can say one belief is more probable than another without putting numbers on either. Third, the weight of argument: a probability of 0.5 can rest on a lot of evidence or on almost none, and that matters even though the number is the same. Fourth, the very strict conditions he put on the principle of indifference, and his worry that people treat it as a free licence to slap flat priors on everything. Fifth, the idea that under genuine uncertainty in his sense — closer to Knight's than to the textbook version — the rational thing is often to fall back on convention rather than optimisation.

Then give me the residue report. Which of those five translate cleanly into the ML vocabulary, which translate only with distortion (and say exactly where the distortion creeps in), and which cannot be expressed in that vocabulary at all. And be honest about whether each bit of residue is a place where the ML vocabulary is missing something Keynes was right about, or a place where Keynes was just less clear than he looked. I want this because I suspect the smooth translation is doing real philosophical work behind my back, and I'd rather see the join.

As for my fluency: I know what all the terms I listed mean roughly, but I won't follow anything that leans on recent papers or notation-heavy derivations. Keep it conceptual but precise.
