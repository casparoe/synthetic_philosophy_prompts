I find myself attracted to prudential hedonism because it promises a single, elegant measure of well-being: pleasure counts in favor of a life, pain against it. But when I try to say what all pleasures have in common, I fail. The relief of a forgiven debt, the satisfaction of finishing a proof, the enjoyment of a cool drink after exertion, taking pleasure in a rival's setback, and simple bodily comfort feel so unlike one another that 'pleasure' begins to look like a loose label rather than the name of one thing. The theory has a natural reply: pleasure need not be a shared felt quality, since it may instead be a single attitude or functional state — for instance, being pleased that something is the case, or being disposed to want an experience to continue — whose objects vary. Both the theory and the intuition exert a genuine pull on me. Which should yield? Should the intuition that pleasure is too heterogeneous to form one kind be debunked or explained away, should hedonism be revised or abandoned, or is there a version of hedonism on which the diversity of pleasures is not an objection at all? Say what would justify each of these responses, rather than merely selecting one.
