An article I read on the free will debate gives the following summary of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. Strawson begins from the idea that nothing can be the cause of itself. To be truly responsible for what one does, one would have to be responsible for the character, desires, and principles from which one's actions flow. But a person's character is the product of their genetic endowment and their environment, neither of which they chose, and any deliberate attempt to change oneself would itself issue from the character one already has. Every step backward in the search for responsibility therefore reveals another layer of character one did not choose, and the demand collapses into an infinite regress. Our actions are thus the causal outcome of factors entirely outside our control, and this shows that, if determinism is true, no one is truly morally responsible for anything. Given what neuroscience and genetics increasingly tell us about the causes of human behavior, the article concludes that Strawson has shown that praise and blame, gratitude and resentment, are never deserved by anyone, and that our ordinary practices of holding one another responsible rest on an illusion. I have a vague memory that Strawson frames the argument in terms of the impossibility of being causa sui, and I believe I have read that he insists the argument does not depend on the truth of determinism, which makes me suspect that the summary has quietly moved the argument onto causal and empirical ground where Strawson would not place it. But I cannot reconstruct the details, and I am unsure how much else in the summary is trustworthy. Is this a fair summary of the Basic Argument? Please go through it claim by claim, distinguish the accurate parts from the subtly distorted ones, say how Strawson himself would respond to any distortions, and indicate which of the concluding claims about praise, blame, gratitude, resentment, and our responsibility practices survive once the summary has been corrected, and in what sense Strawson would or would not accept them.
