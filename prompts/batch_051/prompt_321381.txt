i've been thinking about generics and their exceptions, and I keep using a rough distinction I want checked. 'machinery generics' are true because the property is built into what the kind does or is for: ducks lay eggs, crows build nests, beavers dam streams. 'alarm generics' are true because the property is a danger or risk associated with the kind even when it's rare: mosquitoes carry malaria, sharks bite swimmers. my proposal is that a generic 'Ks are F' is acceptable only when one of these two backing relations holds, and bare statistical prevalence is neither necessary nor sufficient. so 'ducks lay eggs' works though most ducks don't lay eggs, and 'mosquitoes carry malaria' works though almost no individual mosquito does, but 'books are paperbacks' sits wrong because there's commonness with no machinery and no alarm. what I want is an honest assesment: is this coherent, does it already exist under a better name, and where does it break first? i'm not sure what to do with 'people are right-handed' and 'tigers have stripes', which feel true without being machinery or alarm in any obvious way.
