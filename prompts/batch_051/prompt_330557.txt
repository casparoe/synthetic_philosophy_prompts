Ok, here's one for the philosophy of religion unit. I want a dialogue between two sharp, equally matched characters: a Reformed defender of sola scriptura and a Catholic critic who thinks the position collapses into a regress of private interpretation. Don't make either one a strawman. The Protestant should get to run a real argument: the perspicuity of scripture on what's necessary for salvation, the internal testimony of the Holy Spirit, the distinction between scripture's normative authority and the church's merely ministerial role, and the worry that an infallible teaching office has to be established from the very text it's then supposed to interpret. The Catholic should keep pressing the obvious question - who decides what the text means? - not only for live disputes (wether baptism regenerates, or how to read the words of institution at the last supper) but for the prior question of which books belong in the canon at all, since the table of contents isn't itself a verse of scripture. Make them actually answer each other: when the Protestant invokes the Spirit's internal witness, the Catholic should ask what happens when two sincere readers both claim it and end up contradicting each other; when the Catholic points to the magisterium, the Protestant should point out that identifying the true church, and then interpreting the magisterium's own decrees, seems to start the same regress one level up. Leave it a genuine standoff — each can concede the other's best point but neither converts. Around 800 words. The reader should come away feeling why this is a hard problem and not a solved one.
