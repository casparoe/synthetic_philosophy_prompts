In my existentialism seminar I teach bad faith like this: it's a lie to oneself where the distinction between liar and lied-to has collapsed, and the person both knows and hides the same unwelcome truth. The usual mechanism is treating yourself as a fixed object — a nature, a role, a past, a label — in order to disown the freedom of having to choose and keep remaking yourself, while silently leaning on that same freedom to keep the whole performance going. It's not a plain factual error; it's something you maintain against the evidence.

Now take a recovering alcoholic, sober for years, who says he's an alcoholic and always will be. When offered a drink he doesn't experience it as a live choice; he tells himself the question is already causally closed, one drink is not a possiblity for a creature like him. Is this bad faith in Sartre's sense? Go through the criteria one by one and be honest about the borderline; then say whether any mismatch should push us to revise the criteria, not just the verdict.
