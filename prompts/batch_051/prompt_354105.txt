I'm a PhD student in philosophy of biology, and I'm trying to settle what I actually think about the functions debate—specifically the fight between etiological/selected-effects accounts and causal-role accounts. I've noticed that every time I think I've got a refutation of one side, the other side just says I've changed the subject. I've been reading Peter Godfrey-Smith's paper Functions: Consensus Without Unity, and he argues, roughly, that a lot of the apparent disagreement between these camps is overblown: under a shared explanatory schema, both sides agree on more than they admit, and the real disagreement is about which explanatory purposes are central. I don't want to mangle his point, but my sense is he thinks function attributions are answers to different explanatory questions, so asking which account is correct is a bit like asking whether a hammer is more correct than a screwdriver. Anyway, I want an essay that weighs the main arguments for and against the selected-effects account specifically, against causal-role as the default rival, but does not end in a can't-we-all-get-along shrug. It should reach a verdict, even if that verdict is a local one (e.g., selected-effects is the right account for evolutionary biology but not for physiology, or maybe one of them is just more fundamental). In particular, I'd like you to deal with the classic hard cases: spandrels, vestigial organs, and the fact that causal-role functions can be assigned to parts of organisms that have no evolutionary history, like a cirrhotic liver functioning to produce ascites, or a tumor's blood supply functioning to feed the tumor. If those cases force you to abandon selected-effects, say so; if not, explain why they don't. Also, engage with the charge that etiological functions are just causal-role functions with a hidden historical condition attached, and whether that makes the selected-effects account less explanatory. Finally, say something about whether Godfrey-Smith's consensus claim is actually right or whether it papers over a real difference in what the two sides mean by function.
