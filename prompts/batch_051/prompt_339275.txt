Here's an asymmetry in ordinary practice that I keep noticing. If a friend sincerely says they're in agony, I treat the matter as settled: I don't ask how they know or worry they're misreading their own signals. But if the same friend says 'I turned down the job because the commute was too long,' or 'I'm not angry, I'm just tired,' I may accept it and still wonder whether they're kidding themselves. Both are reports about their own mind, reached by introspection. Is there a principled line between the states where introspection is treated as authoritative and the ones where it's treated as fallible — or should we either be more sceptical of the pain report or more trusting of the motive report? If you defend a line, test it against two nearby cases: someone reporting the reason for a choice they made five minutes ago, and someone reporting a low mood that has lasted for months. Please write the answer in British English.
