I keep running into "nothing comes from nothing" doing completely different jobs. A physicist used it the other day to wave off a question about conservation laws; a friend pulled it out while defending a vague cosmological argument; and in a seminar on the Presocratics the professor glided from it straight to Parmenides ruling out all change and plurality — since coming-to-be would have to come from what is not, and what is not is just nothing. Same four words every time, and the slide felt seemless even though the conclusions are miles apart.

Take the saying on its own and pull the precise readings apart. Which of them are platitudinous once unpacked, which are substantive and actually defensible, and which are false or only survive with heavy qualification? Then don't stop at a menu: commit. Which precise reading does the Eleatic denial of change and plurality actually need, and does the saying, on that reading, deliver it? I want your view and the reasons for it, not a survey of possibilties.
