alright so after a year of me and my friend talking past eachother about Mencius vs Xunzi we're finally doing the adversarial-collaboration thing, and i want the document we should have produced. the two camps, steelmanned: (M) human nature is good — we're born with actual contentful sprouts of the virtues (compassion, shame, deference, a sense of right and wrong), the way we're born with a language faculty, and moral failing is mostly a failure to extend those sprouts under stress or scarcity; (X) human nature in the raw is just desires, feelings, and a default bias toward our own kin and comfort — everything moral is artifice (wei), built on top through ritual, training, and reflection, like steaming a straight piece of wood into a wheel. both camps agree humans can end up genuinely good and that no one gets there without a lot of external structure; the whole fight is about the seed, not the grown tree.

write the joint statement with: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about the child-at-the-well case and about what cultivation/education does; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own view (no "one side is a romantic, the other a cynic"); (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions that would actually move at least one of us — and for each crux the case or evidence both sides agree bears on it, with our predictions writen down in advance; and (4) a short section on how much of this is really a verbal dispute about the word "nature" (xing) and how much is a real dispute about development. do not let the joint statement collapse into "humans need both nature and nurture"; if both sides could sign it, it has to be sharper than that. and if either side is running a motte-and-bailey on some subclaim, flag it instead of signing off on it.
