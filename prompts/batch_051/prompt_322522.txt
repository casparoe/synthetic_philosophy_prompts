I am writing a novel in which a critical historian of Mediterranean antiquity and an independent Atlantis researcher are required, as a condition of a foundation grant, to carry out an adversarial collaboration on whether the Atlantis narrative has a recoverable historical core. As an appendix to the novel, I want the document that collaboration would issue.

Please draft that joint statement in full. The two positions are these: (A) a critical historian who holds that the Atlantis story is best explained as a philosophical and political invention, and that literalist reconstructions of it are pseudohistorical; and (B) an Atlantis researcher who holds that the story preserves, however distortedly, a real maritime culture or catastrophic event, and that mainstream scholarship applies inconsistent standards to textual and archaeological evidence.

Format the statement as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be substantive rather than vacuous: they should specify what the ancient textual tradition does and does not say, what physical evidence has and has not been found, and which methodological principles both sides accept. In the table, state each residual disagreement with enough precision that each side would accept the wording of its own view. For each disagreement, articulate the crux, namely the sub-question whose resolution would lead at least one side to revise its position, and describe the evidence, test, or comparative case that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

I am particularly interested in where an apparent disagreement dissolves under precise restatement because it was merely verbal, and where it survives restatement and remains genuine. Do not include citations, footnotes, or a bibliography; the statement should stand alone as a document both parties could sign in good faith.
