Ok so I'm a software engineer on a game localization team, and we keep having the same argument over how faithful a translation should be. I recently read part of that Hofstadter book — Le Ton beau de Marot, I think it's called — the one where he gets a bunch of different people to translate the same tiny French poem and everybody makes different tradeoffs about rhyme and meter and meaning. That is basically our argument in book form, and I want to settle it, or at least sort people.

I'd like a "which translation philosophy do you actually hold?" quiz I can send to my coworkers. Something like eight questions, each one a short concrete mini-case (a few sentences, since these are busy people), with three answer options that force a real choice about the thing being translated. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named positions, plus a couple paragraphs after the key saying which positions the quiz can't actually tell apart, and which combinations of answers nobody landed on and why that might be (incoherent, unattractive, or just a gap).

The positions I definitely want as leaves are roughly: form is part of the meaning (keep rhyme/meter/structure even if you have to fudge content); meaning first with form as a nice-to-have; same effect on the reader/player even if that means rewriting a lot; and "some things genuinely dont translate, so say so instead of pretending." I'm sure there are in-between positions too — add them if they fall out of the questions, but don't force them in just to have more leaves.

Some cases I want used: (1) a pun that is also the clue to a puzzle, so if the pun dies in translation the puzzle becomes unsolvable; (2) a shopkeeper whose lines are all jokes in rhyme — do the target lines need to rhyme?; (3) a short poem carved on a wall in-game that players screenshot and compare across languages; (4) a UI label with a hard character limit where the literal translation is too long; and (5) a character whose dialect marks them as rural in the original. That's five, so add three more with a similar flavor.

Two ground rules: the questions should never ask which label people identify with — they should ask about the cases and let the answers do the sorting. And if two positions would answer every question the same way, flag that rather than pretending they're seperate. Keep it readable for people who've never read any translation theory. Don't worry about citations or a reading list, this is for a team meeting, not a paper.
