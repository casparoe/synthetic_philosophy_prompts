In my philosophy of science course we keep circling a standoff. The no-miracles argument holds that the predictive and technological success of mature scientific theories would be a miracle unless they are at least approximately true. The pessimistic meta-induction replies that the historical record is full of empirically successful theories later discarded, so current successful theories probably share their fate. I want to treat the realist's core claim—that the success of current theories is best explained by their approximate truth—as something evidence could bear on, rather than just an armchair slogan. Act as a philosopher of science with a working knowledge of experimental and historical method, think carefully, and begin by brainstorming several plausible operationalizations of approximate truth and of success. Then pick the strongest and design the best concrete test of the claim you can, whether an experiment, a structured historical comparison, or something else. Say exactly what results would support the claim, what results would undermine it, what your operationalization leaves out or distorts, and what no test of this kind could settle even in principle.
