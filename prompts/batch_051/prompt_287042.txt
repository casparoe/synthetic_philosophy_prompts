Two people each decide, with the same deliberate consent, to burn down a neighbor's shed. One is stopped on the way over because his car breaks down; the other goes through with it. Everyone, me included, judges the second much more harshly, and the law grades attempt and completed arson very differently too. But I keep getting stuck on something. Abelard held that sin is in the consent alone, not in the deed, and that the outward act adds nothing to the guilt, since the internal act of consent already constitutes the sin. If he's right, the whole practice of blaming people less for what didn't happen starts to look like a concession to how visible and dramatic outcomes are, not a real moral difference. Is there something real we're tracking when we treat attempt and completion as different in degree, or is the completed act just the same sin with better PR? Give me the strongest possible justification for keeping the asymmetry, and test it against cases where the attempt and the completed deed flow from exactly the same consenting state of mind. Then say whether you'd keep the asymmetry, ditch it, or revise it. Oh and please write the answer in American English.
