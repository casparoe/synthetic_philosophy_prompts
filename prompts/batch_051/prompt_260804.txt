I work for a state natural-resources agency that manages a shared groundwater basin, and we get a new application to appropriate from the common pool every month or two. Our board keeps falling back on the line that existing users have put their labor into the water, and that Locke says that settles it. Thats not a procedure, and our staff are hydrologists, not philosophers; they need something they can run in a afternoon and the board can defend in one page. Write me a decision procedure—questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it has to stop becuase its a values call rather than a factual one—for deciding whether a proposed appropriation from a common resource is legitimate under a Lockean labour theory with the proviso. Please handle both the acquisition part and the proviso part: what it takes to mix one's labour, and what it takes to leave enough of equal quality for everybody else. Then run the procedure on two cases we actually face: (1) a farmer wants to pump enough groundwater that a neighboring well drops a few feet, and (2) a company wants to fence off a stretch of lakeshore the public has always walked but that no one is actively using right now. Flag any place where the procedure hands back an answer the board will not like. And since our general counsel keeps citing Locke like the proviso has one agreed reading: separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study Locke, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
