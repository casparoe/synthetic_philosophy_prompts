Question

Explain the difference between presupposition and entailment; explain the notion of presupposition accommodation within David Lewis's scorekeeping model of conversation; and discuss, using an example of your own, one case in which a presupposition is not accommodated and what that shows about the state of the conversation.

Student answer

An utterance presupposes a proposition when that proposition must be true, or at least must be treated as true, for the utterance to be evaluable as true or false. An entailment is different: it is a proposition that must be true if the utterance is true, and if it is false the utterance is false rather than truth-valueless. For example, the sentence 'Leila has a brother in York' entails that Leila has a brother, and the sentence 'Leila has stopped cycling to work' presupposes that Leila previously cycled to work. If an entailment is false, the sentence is false; if a presupposition is not met, the sentence is neither true nor false.

Accommodation is needed because speakers constantly produce sentences whose presuppositions are not already in the conversational score. On Lewis's scorekeeping model, conversation is like a game in which various components — what is presupposed, what is salient, the standards of precision, and so on — make up a score that is updated at each move. When a speaker uses a sentence whose presupposition is not part of the current score, the hearer accommodates by quietly adding that proposition to their own beliefs before the conversation continues, provided there is no particular objection. This is why a speaker can say 'Maya's new neighbour has stopped parking across her drive' when it has not yet been established that Maya has a new neighbour or that the neighbour previously parked there; the hearer simply takes both propositions on board.

Accommodation is blocked when the hearer does not already believe the presupposed proposition. For instance, if Alex says to Sam, 'Have you stopped leaving your bicycle in the corridor?', the presupposition that Sam has been leaving his bicycle in the corridor is not accommodated, because Sam does not believe it; Sam's natural reply is 'I never left it there in the first place.' The failure of accommodation shows that the conversational score was not updated as Alex's move would have required, and that Sam has instead challenged the presupposition itself.
