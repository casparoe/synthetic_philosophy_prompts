I am the senior editor of a popular history magazine. Every month my junior editors and I approve, rewrite, or kill dozens of sentences in long-form narrative features that attribute large historical outcomes to non-human causes: a plague that "brought down" an empire, a drought that "made" a revolution inevitable, a volcano that "triggered" a famine. We have no in-house historian; our fact-checkers can verify dates, numbers, and quotations but will not rule on causal claims; and a prompt correction request from an academic historian is costly to our reputation. I need a reusable decision procedure that a junior editor without training in philosophy or historiography can run in about twenty minutes on any sentence of the form "X caused / triggered / made inevitable / helps explain Y," where X is a germ, a climate event, or a geological event.

The procedure should be grounded in what is defensible about causation and causal explanation, and it should encode the distinctions that survive translation into an editorial checklist: necessary versus sufficient conditions; triggers versus standing background conditions; contribution versus explanatory salience; why "without X, no Y" can be true while "X caused Y" still misleads; and why treating a volcano or a pathogen as an actor invites the erasure of human institutions, choices, and variation across similarly affected societies. It should also state explicitly where the procedure stops and a human editor must exercise judgment because two reasonable causal stories can both be true.

Please give me the procedure as an ordered checklist: the questions in the order they should be asked, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure says "rewrite as stated," and where it says "escalate to the senior editor." Then run it on these three manuscript sentences and show what the procedure does to each, including the rewritten form it would require if it does not pass as written:

1. "The 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora caused the 'year without a summer' of 1816."
2. "The Laki fissure eruption of 1783, which darkened European skies and ruined harvests, caused the French Revolution."
3. "Smallpox, not Spanish steel or strategy, conquered the Aztec Empire."

Finally, tell me which kinds of causal claim the procedure cannot settle even when followed faithfully, and say which of the three cases above, if any, forces the procedure to give an answer my editors will not like.
