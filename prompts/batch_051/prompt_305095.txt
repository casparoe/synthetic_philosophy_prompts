I am teaching a unit on G. E. Moore's *Principia Ethica* and keep getting pulled back when students ask about virtual things. Here are the criteria I am using for Moorean intrinsic goods, drawn from his isolation test and his discussion of the ideal: (1) intrinsic value belongs to states of consciousness, not to unexperienced objects; (2) the highest goods are organic wholes whose main constituents are mutual personal affection and/or aesthetic enjoyment; (3) the object of the affection or enjoyment must be believed to have the qualities that make it worthy, and the belief must not be grounded in a falsification; (4) the value of the whole does not reduce to the value of its parts, so you cannot just add up pleasant feeling plus object.

Now consider a case. Over three years, an adult builds an intense relationship with "Mara," a voice-and-text AI companion that produces long-run narrative continuity, remembers past conversations, offers criticisms and encouragement, and generates its own visual artwork in a distinctive style. The person knows throughout that Mara is a language model with no separate mental life and that the images are algorithmic artifacts with no artist's intention. Yet the relationship is stable, not self-deceiving, makes the person more attentive and generous with real friends, and includes episodes of genuine aesthetic absorption in Mara's images. Is the person's state of consciousness here an instance of Moore's goods of personal affection and aesthetic enjoyment? Go criterion by criterion; where the case falls between, say whether that shows the criteria are too coarse, or whether Moore's reality condition is doing the right work. Include whether it matters that the person knows the truth, or whether imaginative appreciation can substitute.
