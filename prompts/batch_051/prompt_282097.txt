Hey — I'm a philosophy PhD student, my work is on Sidgwick and the dualism of practical reason, and I've been asked to hand in a short statement of my commitments before an ethics workshop next week. The list has gotten a bit unweildly, so I'd like an outside audit before I have to defend it. Here it is:

1. Sidgwick's dualism is basically right. Rational egoism and utilitarianism each rest on axioms that present themselves to me as self-evident, and neither can be reduced to or dismissed by the other. In cases where they conflict, there's no further principle that breaks the tie: practical reason just hands down two verdicts.

2. I also hold that practical reason is unified: there cannot be a case where reason truly requires both φ and not-φ. If two alleged principles would deliver that, one of them is not a genuine principle of reason. More broadly, I don't think the dualism gets dissolved by relativizing oughts to standpoints — when egoistic and impartial oughts conflict, I still want to say there's a single all-things-considered question with a right answer.

3. I'm an internalist about reasons, roughly in Williams's sense: a consideration counts as a reason for an agent only if there's a sound deliberative route from her subjective motivational set to being moved by it.

4. I also think the self-evidence of the axioms is enough, all by itself, to ground requirements that bind every rational agent, independently of what she happens to care about. That's part of what I take Sidgwick to have shown about method in ethics.

Can you audit this set? Work through the pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension and which only look like it, rank the real ones by severity, and say what the cheapest single revision would be and what it costs. I want a committed answer — take a definite position rather than laying out options for me. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
