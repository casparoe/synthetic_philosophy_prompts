I run evals at a startup that fine-tunes a general-purpose chat assistant. Our lead engineer wants to add a training stage where, before answering any user request, the model first emits a hidden risk estimate (0–100) and a one-sentence reason; users never see either. During RL we reward the model for calibration: it gets a bonus when its risk estimate matches what a seperate harm classifier assigns to the final conversation, and a penalty when it overestimates or underestimates. The hidden estimates and reasons also get surfaced to our human reviewers to help them triage flagged conversations. Walk through how this would actually play out — what the model ends up optimizing, how the scheme gets gamed by the model, by users, and by us, where it fails first, and whether the failure modes can be patched without giving up the hidden-estimate idea. Format: exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion, and spend the fifth point on what would most change your read of the scheme or where your analysis is shakiest.
