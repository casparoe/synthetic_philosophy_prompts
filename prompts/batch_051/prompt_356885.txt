I keep returning to a disagreement about the so-called DNA–Gödel isomorphism: the idea, developed in Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* through the toy system Typogenetics, that there is a deep structural parallel between Gödelian self-reference and the self-replication of a strand of DNA. In Typogenetics, strands of bases encode enzymes that perform typographical operations (cutting, inserting, deleting, searching, copying) on other strands, and one can construct strands that bring about their own reproduction. The two camps I encounter agree on these formal facts but divide sharply over what to make of them.

One side treats the parallel as deep and explanatory: a string can cause a copy of itself only because a finite string can encode the operations that reproduce that very string, and the reasoning that establishes the existence of such strands is the same diagonal, Gödel-numbering move that yields self-referential sentences in arithmetic — bases are read both as inert sequence and as executable code, just as numbers are read both as arithmetic values and as codes for formulas. The other side treats the parallel as an overextended pun: DNA and enzymes are causal machinery, not a language with truth conditions; nothing is proved about any biochemically real system; any fixed point in a rewriting toy is a generic consequence of self-application and transfers no metamathematical result; and calling it "Gödelian" lends prestige without insight.

I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than an adjudication. Is this best understood as a substantive dispute over whether semantic notions like encoding, reference, or aboutness can legitimately apply to non-interpreted causal processes; a verbal dispute over what "isomorphism" must preserve and at what depth; a dispute about burden of proof, since each side claims the other is importing the conclusion; or a hidden formal question, such as whether there is a nontrivial common fixed-point theorem of which both the Gödel sentence construction and Typogenetics-style self-copying are instances, and what such a theorem would actually entitle one to transfer? Give the diagnosis, defend it using what each side actually claims, and say what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a theorem, a distinction, a change of question, or an argument that the debate is ill-posed.
