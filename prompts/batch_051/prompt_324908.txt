I'm an epistemology PhD student, and I've spent the last term trying to get clear on the dispute between contextualists and pragmatic encroachers about knowledge, and honestly its left me with a weird impression. The original bank cases were supposed to show that whether we'll say someone 'knows' depends on what's at stake: same subject, same evidence, but in the high-stakes case we're reluctant to say he knows the bank is open. Contextualists took this to show that the word 'knows' shifts its extension with the attributor's context, so the same claim can be true in one context and false in another. Pragmatic encroachers (or subject-sensitive invariantists, depending who you ask) took the very same cases to show that knowledge itself is stakes-sensitive: practical facts about the subject's situation partly determine whether they know, even though 'knows' means the same thing across contexts. By now, though, both camps have accommodated pretty much all the same data. They agree about the bank cases, they agree that merely statistical evidence often doesn't get you knowledge, they agree that the practical stakes matter somehow, and they even borrow each other's machinery for how practical factors enter ascriptions. Yet nobody moves. I've started to suspect the disagreement is less about the cases than about what a theory of 'knows' is answerable to: ordinary language, the state itself, or something a psychologist would find under the hood.

So my question isn't who's right, but what kind of impasse this is. Is it a substantive disagreement, a verbal one over whether 'knowledge' picks out a word or a state, or two different projects sharing a label? Is there a hidden empirical or linguistic question—about speaker judgements, or about the psychology of knowledge—that would break the tie? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like from here: a certain experiment, a formal result, a sharper distinction, or an argument that we shouldn't expect progress at all? I have a hunch it's a labelling problem, but I'd rather have the hunch tested than confirmed.
