I have been trying to work out what a future that is actually good to live in would have to be like, and I have landed on an idea I call the sandbox standard. The idea is that a good future would resemble a sandbox more than a hotel. A hotel is designed to take care of you: it removes friction, anticipates your needs, and leaves you with as little to do as possible. A sandbox does the opposite in an important way: it gives you safe boundaries and raw materials, then leaves the playing to you. What makes the play absorbing is that it is open-ended, effortful, and full of small difficulties that are genuinely yours to handle. Applied to the future, the standard says that a good society should guarantee people free time, decent health, and enough material security to take risks, but it should deliberately refuse to smooth everything out. A broken bench, a hard project, or a task that must be done by hand is not always a design failure; sometimes it is the sandbox doing its work. I realize this can sound like a comfortable person romanticizing inconvenience, so I want a candid assessment of the idea rather than of my motives. Is the sandbox standard coherent? Has someone already worked it out under another name? And what is the strongest case in which it would give the wrong advice about how to build a future? Please answer in British English.
