I keep coming back to a structural parallel that I cannot decide whether to trust, and I would like you to press on it for me. On one side is Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals. On the other is a fact every programmer and algebraist knows: you cannot make a partial operation total for free, and the attempt often collapses the structure you were trying to extend.

Let me lay out the philosophical side as I understand it. Adams's Thesis says that, for an indicative conditional, the rational degree of belief in 'if A then B' should equal the conditional probability of B given A, understood as the ratio of the probability of A and B jointly to the probability of A, whenever the probability of A is nonzero. This matches how we actually evaluate ordinary indicatives. But it immediately treats the conditional as a kind of ratio, with the antecedent's probability in the denominator. Now suppose the indicative conditional also expresses an ordinary proposition in the same Boolean algebra as truth-functional compounds of A and B, so that its probability is just the measure of that proposition and it composes under negation, conjunction, and disjunction in the usual way. Lewis showed that these demands cannot all be met on any interesting probability space. The standard line: require Adams's Thesis to hold for every probability function obtained by conditionalization, not just the original one. Then for any A and B such that B and not-B each overlap A with positive probability, the conditional proposition has probability one conditional on B and probability zero conditional on the negation of B, so its unconditional probability collapses to the probability of B itself; the Thesis says it should instead be the probability of B given A; hence B is forced to be probabilistically independent of A. You can then show that no space with three pairwise incompatible positive-probability events can admit such a connective at all. The attempt to make the conditional a first-class citizen of the Boolean algebra trivializes the probabilistic structure.

The other side is the partial operation totalization. Conditional probability is a ratio, and a ratio is undefined where the denominator is zero. In code, you can make division total by returning infinity, NaN, or throwing, but you then lose the algebraic law that dividing x by y and multiplying by y returns x, on exactly the newly added cases. In a commutative ring, the sharper version of the same point: if you add a multiplicative inverse for zero, distributivity forces zero times that inverse to equal both zero and one, so zero equals one, and the entire ring collapses to a single element. Making the partial operation total destroys the very structure that made it useful.

These two situations look to me like the same shape: a partial operation is reified as a first-class element governed by the original structural laws, and the only structures that survive are degenerate.
