I run a monthly discussion group for non-academics — smart people with strong opinions and zero patience for jargon. Next month I want to spend about forty-five minutes on a single idea I think is badly under-discussed: that wealth can have value even when it is never converted into consumption. Not security exactly, not status, not "money buys nice things" — the sheer fact of having options, knowing you could quit the job, retrain, move, survive a bad decision, or bail out someone you love, changes how a life actually goes even when none of those options is ever exercised. I want the group to come away able to hold that thought apart from "a safety net" and apart from "spending power," and then to argue about whether prosperity understood this way — abundance itself, as a stock rather than a flow — has moral worth over and above what it buys.
