I am a retired evolutionary biologist who has recently begun reading deeply in philosophy of biology, and I keep finding myself pulled between the selected-effects (etiological) account of biological function and the causal-role account. Please act as a professional philosopher of biology who knows this debate in detail. I would like you to draft the joint statement that a committed selected-effects theorist and a committed causal-role theorist could both sign at the end of a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Think carefully before writing, and begin by brainstorming the hard cases that most cleanly separate the two positions rather than the familiar central examples.

The document should contain four parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: claims both sides can accept about what function attributions are doing and what an adequate account must accommodate, stated so they are substantive rather than vacuous — touching on clear central cases, on the ordinary distinction between functioning and malfunctioning, and on the explanatory point of saying what something is for. Second, a disagreements table: each row states one disputed point in neutral wording, then gives each side's own formulation of its view in terms that side would accept without hedging. Third, a cruxes section: identify the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side. Fourth, for each crux, give a concrete case both sides agree bears on it — for example, a trait fixed by drift that later becomes useful, a selected trait whose selected effect no longer occurs but that now contributes to a different capacity, a synthetic organism with an engineered part, or a clear case of disease — and record each side's prediction about that case in advance, along with what observed or argued result would lead each side to revise. End with a short assessment of which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal and which are substantive. Please write the entire document in American English.
