I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about imposing risk on other people without their consent, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts:

- Driving to work sober, at the speed limit, in good weather: fine, even though it puts pedestrians at some small risk of being hit.
- Driving the same road after two drinks, just over the legal limit, on empty streets: not fine, even though the absolute risk is still pretty low.
- Burning leaves in the backyard on a windy day with neighbors' houses close by: not fine, even though the chance of an ember starting a roof fire is small.
- Letting my kid play baseball in a public park where a foul ball could hit someone on the path: fine.
- Going to a crowded house party when I know I have the flu: not fine.
- Taking the bus with a common cold and no mask (pre-2020): fine.
- A chemical company running chlorine gas through a city by rail because there's no practical alternative route: fine, but only if they're using the best available tank cars and route, even though a derailment would be catastrophic.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What would it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a self-driving car company pushing an update that has a tiny chance of causing a rare fatal error per trip, but that lowers the overall crash rate compared to not updating? And which of my verdicts, if any, would the principle force me to drop?
