Pain asymbolia gets invoked to show that the badness of pain isnt intrinsic to its felt character: the patients can point to where the pinprick is, say its sharp and hot, and give it a seven out of ten, but they dont find it unpleasant and they dont try to escape it. I suspect the argument secretly bundles three things that can come apart—the sensory-discriminative side of the pain, the negative affect, and the motivational pull to get away—and the philosophical work is in saying which one is doing the work. Turn this into a proposal with a determinate question, an account of why the existing positions leave it open, and a method I could run without a lab. Include what I would do month by month, what a clean result looks like, what a failure looks like, and an honest verdict on whether a semester is enough given that the empirical facts about asymbolia are themselves contested. Also make the proposal face the deflationary line head-on: maybe asymbolia just shows a normal emotional reaction failing to fire, which everyone already believed, and says nothing about whether the badness belongs to the pain itself.
