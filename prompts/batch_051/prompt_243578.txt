Since retiring from the physical sciences, I have been reading the judgment-aggregation literature, and I would like a variation of the discursive dilemma checked. Take the standard three-judge case over the propositions P, Q, and P∧Q, with the usual profile: one judge accepts P and rejects the other two, a second accepts Q and rejects the other two, and a third accepts all three; simple majority voting on each proposition then produces an inconsistent collective set. Now make two changes. Each judge reports a precise credence in each proposition rather than a Yes/No vote (the profile above is entirely 1s and 0s), and the group's credence in a proposition is the straight average of the three reported credences, with the group accepting a proposition just when that average is strictly greater than a threshold t. For t = 0.5, run the numbers: does the group still accept P and Q while rejecting P∧Q? Is the resulting state coherent at the level of the credences and at the level of the accepted propositions? Over what range of t does the original inconsistency reappear? Finally, does the discursive dilemma survive this change, or is it dissolved into an artifact of binarizing graded credence—and what would that show about whether the original paradox is rooted in aggregation, in binarization, or in both?
