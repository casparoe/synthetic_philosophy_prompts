I went down a rabbit hole last year about whether we should use medicine to make people morally better, not just treat them when they're sick. The version that got me is the one that says our moral instincts are tuned for small tribes and immediate dangers, but now we have nukes and a changing climate, so our hearts haven't caught up with our hands, and if we don't chemically or genetically improve our motives we might do ourselves in. So I tried to make my own little checklist for what should count as a moral enhancement rather than just drugging people into compliance. What I came up with: the change has to reach why a person does the right thing, not just clamp the behavior while the ugly part stays in place; it has to make the person more moral by ordinary standards, not just easier to run or convenient for someone; it shouldn't break their capacity to think about right and wrong, because then you've traded morality for something more like a thermostat; and the improvement should be broad, not tied to one narrow setting. Then I heard about—maybe oxytocin, the hormone they used to call the love hormone—where a spray made people nicer to the people they already counted as their own but didn't make them warmer to outsiders, and maybe even made them a touch more defensive around out-groups. I can't swear I got the details right, and it doesn't matter much for the question, because here's the case I want to run by you. Suppose a treatment works exactly like that: give it to someone and they become a better friend, parent, teammate, neighbor so long as the other person is in their circle—real care, real loyalty, real honesty in daily life—but it also sharpens the us/them line and leaves them a little less willing to see outsiders as mattering. On my four criteria, does that treatment count as a moral bioenhancement? Please go through the criteria one at a time and say where the case fits, where it clearly doesn't, and where my criteria are too vague to decide. And then tell me whether this case means I should add a criterion, drop one, or rethink whether moral enhancement is a single thing at all. No headings, bullet points, bold, or tables please, just normal paragraphs like an email.
