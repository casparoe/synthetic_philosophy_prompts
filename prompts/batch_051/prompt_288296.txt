i've been trying to get a handle on the old "death before the Fall" problem without going full young-earth, and I've come up with a distinction I want checked. I call it creational cost vs fallen damage. The claim is that the Genesis judgment that creation was good attaches to the whole process, not to each creature's experience. So predation, parasitism, disease, extinction, the general churn of natural selection — those are creational cost: the price of a world in which finite creatures pursue their own goods and in which life diversifies at all. They are not natural evil, and they need no Fall to explain them. Fallen damage is narrower: suffering that enters through the specifically human moral rupture — cruelty, making an animal suffer for the pleasure of it, the cursing and violence that the Bible attaches to the fall, that kind of thing. I like this because it lets me hold that the pre-human world was good in a systemic sense and that evolution happened, without having to say animal suffering doesn't matter. But I can already see the obvious objection: the line between cost and damage looks like it's drawn wherever I need it. A lion eating a gazelle alive and a human torturing a dog both involve pain and terror; my distinction says one is a feature of a good creation and the other is corruption, and I'm not sure I can say why except that one has a human agent in it. Also I might just be restating the old "the Fall was cosmic, not just human" move, or the idea that creation is a process rather than a finished product. So before you tell me it's bad: what does it get right? Is it a real distinction or just a metaphor? Give me the three strongest objections and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than just uncomfortable.
