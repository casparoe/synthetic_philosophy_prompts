I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I sit on the editorial committee at our magazine. We meet every Tuesday for about 45 minutes to decide which stories get greenlit, and the calls that eat the whole meeting are always the same shape: one story would be good for us — traffic, awards, access, a "get" — and another would genuinely serve readers and the public but might bore them or cost us subscribers. Right now we decide by vibes and whoever argues loudest.

Years ago a philosopher I interviewed told me about Sidgwick's dualism of practical reason. The way I remember it: practical reason has two master principles that both seem self-evident — one says pursue your own good, the other says pursue everyone's good — and when they pull in opposite directions, reason has no higher principle to break the tie. It just goes silent. That's... our meeting, every week.

Can you turn that into a procedure we can actually run in the room? We're journalists, not philosophers, so it has to be a plain-language checklist: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it explicitly hands the decision to a human because the theory itself says no procedure can settle it. And say plainly what the procedure cannot do. Then run it on three cases: (1) a splashy story on a hyped AI result that would do huge traffic but probably mislead readers a bit, (2) a deeply important but unsexy public-health story that would serve the public and cost us subscribers, and (3) a soft profile of a major funder that would buy us access but mostly serves one board member's career. I'd like at least one worked example where the procedure tells us something we won't want to hear.

The editor-in-chief has to sign of on anything we adopt, so keep it usable in a real meeting. Also, lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my question back at me — and write in American English.

And please don't just humor me: if I've mangled Sidgwick, or if operationalizing him like this is a category mistake, or the whole framing is off, say so plainly and then do the closest useful thing.
