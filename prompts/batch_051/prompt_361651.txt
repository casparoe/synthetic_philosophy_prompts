Longtime lurker here. I've ended up with a cluster of views about which systems are conscious and why it matters, and I've defended them one at a time in different threads but never checked whether they actually fit together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Moral patienthood tracks phenomenal consciousness. If there's nothing it is like to be a system, it isn't a moral patient — intelligence, goal-directedness, and convincing self-report don't count by themselves.
2. Substrate-independence: if you replaced every neuron in a human brain one at a time with little chips that reproduce each neuron's input-output behavior (same thresholds, same timings, same connections), the person stays conscious throughout and the finished silicon brain is just as conscious and just as morally significant as the orginal.
3. But the China-brain setup, where a whole nation of people pass slips of paper around to implement that same neuron-level input-output structure, is not a single conscious thing: there's no unified subject, just a billion little human experiences and a lot of paper.
4. A blockhead — a machine that answers any sentence by looking it up in a giant precomputed table and would behave indistinguishably from a human over every actual conversation — isn't conscious either; the thinking was all done at compile time and nothing is actually worked out at runtime.
5. A whole-brain emulation running on a standard digital computer, with each neuron simulated in software and updated on discrete timesteps, would be conscious and should have the same moral status as the biological person.
6. Split-brain patients can have two conscious streams in one skull at once, so the number of subjects doesn't always equal the number of organisms.

I'd like a consistency audit: are these jointly tenable? I want the tensions ranked by severity, a note on which ones are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension the cheapest revision and what it costs. I genuinely don't know which one has to give, so don't just tell me the set is messy — tell me exactly which pairs pull against each other and what I'd be committing to either way.
