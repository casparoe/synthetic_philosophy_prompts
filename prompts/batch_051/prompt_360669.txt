I am a retired physical chemist, and I have lately been reading the philosophy of mind with more care than I once did. The problem I keep returning to is the combination problem for panpsychism, and I would like you to build me a thought experiment to order. I want the case in order to see how much explanatory weight a primitive bonding relation can carry before it becomes unconstrained, which is the point at which my reading currently stalls.

Let me fix the two positions I want the case to separate. The first is constitutive panpsychism that answers the combination problem by positing a primitive phenomenal bonding relation. On this view, the world's fundamental entities are micro-subjects, and a suitably related plurality of them constitutes a further, unified subject of experience; the bonding relation is taken as basic and is not reducible to any causal or functional relation. That relation is what marks the difference between a single large subject and a mere collection of small ones. The second position is panprotopsychism, on which the fundamental entities are not themselves subjects but proto-phenomenal properties—properties that are not experiential but that jointly constitute experience when they are arranged in a suitably integrated physical system.

Here are my specifications. Construct one scenario, involving only ordinary adult human beings and technology no more exotic than a surgically implanted neural link, in which the two positions give the following different verdicts. The constitutive panpsychist is driven by the logic of the bonding relation to say that the composite formed by the two individuals is itself a further, unified subject of experience, over and above the two subjects the individuals already are, even though the composite displays no behavioural or functional unity. Panprotopsychism, by contrast, is not driven to say this: for that view, the case contains at most the two original subjects and whatever ordinary experiences the link causes in them, and no new macro-subject arises. The divergence must fall out of the two positions' own commitments about what it takes for micro-subjects to bond versus what it takes for proto-phenomenal properties to constitute experience; the scenario may not simply stipulate those verdicts, but must generate them from the theories. The factual setup should be one a neuroscientist would recognize: the two people, a bidirectional informational link, no integration of their motor or decision-making systems, and, from a third-person standpoint, no sign that a single new mind has appeared. The scenario itself should be no longer than a paragraph.

After you have presented the scenario, please do the following. First, walk through the reasoning each position gives, so that I can see the specified verdict fall out of it rather than being assumed. Second, identify the single structural feature of the case that is doing all the work. Third, say what would happen if that feature were removed—whether the divergence would collapse into agreement, and, if it does, which position the case then supports. Fourth, give the strongest reply available to the defender of the constitutive panpsychist bonding relation, and explain what theoretical price that reply pays.
