At dinner my cousin reached for Hume's old image to settle an argument about tariffs: money is just the oil that keeps the wheels of commerce turning, not one of the wheels themselves, so a trade deficit is basically sending lubricant abroad and getting real goods back, and worrying about it is like a car worrying about the oil it burned. His conclusion was that only productive capacity makes a country rich, and people who obsess over the balance of trade have confused the lubrication with the engine. I keep thinking of places this sounds wrong — bank runs, liquidity crunches, dollarizing economies — but I can't tell which objections actually bite. Audit the analogy for me: give the correspondence table (what's the wheels, the oil, the engine, the fuel?), then say where the mapping breaks, which disanalogies matter for the trade-deficit conclusion and which are just noise, and whether his conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table.
