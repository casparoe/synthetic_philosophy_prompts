ok so i'm writing this essay for my philosophy class on Nietzsche vs Schopenhauer about pity and i'd really like some comments on my draft, especially whether my central argument actually works or if im just playing word games. here's the idea.

my essay says the whole fight between them isn't really about whether you should help suffering people, its about what pity actually is and what it does in the person who feels it. Schopenhauer thinks compassion is the one real moral motive: when i see someone else in pain, the usual wall between me and them sort of thins out, and i act for their sake rather than my own. its supposed to be the foundation of morality because it directly opposes pure egoism.

Nietzsche thinks this is a nice story but mostly fake and kind of dangerous. on his view what actually happens when people feel pity is they get a little hit of feeling superior, or they use the other persons pain as an excuse to feel their own tender feelings. so pity doesnt just fail to help, it actually adds more suffering to the world, because now two people feel bad and neither is really doing anything useful, and the pitied person often experiences the pity itself as condescending. he also says pity is arrogant because it assumes the pitier already knows whats good for the other person.

my move is to split pity into two things: (a) pity as the felt echo of someone elses pain, and (b) compassion as the actual motive to relieve their suffering. then i argue nietszches attacks mostly hit (a), not (b). but schopenhauer, if you read him carefully, is also mostly talking about (b), active helping, not about feeling bad along with the person on purpose. so part of nietzsches attack is aimed at a sentimentalist caricature schopenhauer didnt exactly hold. but i also think nietzsche has a surviving point: even active compassion can get infected by the same condescension and self-congratulation problems, and schopenhauer's explanation of how this wall-thinning even works is pretty vague.

so my questions are: is my distinction between (a) and (b) actually doing real work or just making the disagreement disappear? does nietzsche anywhere really go after the active-helping version of compassion directly, not just the feeling version? and is the last part about schopenhauer being vague fair, or am i just not getting what he means? also if you have any idea for a stronger ending id appreciate it, right now it just kinda stops.
