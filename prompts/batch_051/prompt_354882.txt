For my reading group I need five specific points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion. We’re going through Schopenhauer’s *The World as Will and Idea*, and I’m stuck on §52 on music. He says music is unlike the other arts because it is not an image or copy of the Forms, but a direct objectification of the will itself. He also says the bass is the lowest grade of the will’s objectification and the melody is the highest, yet the melody alone has significant, deliberate, freely chosen movement, and only it can “sing” the will’s deepest strivings. My commitments are these: (1) Schopenhauer thinks aesthetic experience quiets the will, so pure music should be the least will-quieting art if it is the most directly will-like. (2) If music presents the will itself, then it should feel agitating, not calming. (3) If bass is the lowest grade of the will and melody the highest, then melody should be more will-like than bass, but Schopenhauer also says melody is free while bass is sluggish and insensate. (4) Since millions of people find melancholy music genuinely consoling, Schopenhauer must be wrong that music gives us the will itself rather than a transformed image of it. (5) If music is the most powerful art, as he says, then it should be the most dangerous, not the most beneficial. Which pairs among these five actually conflict, which only seem to, and which single revision would fix the most?
