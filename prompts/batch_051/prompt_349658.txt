Suppose that in 1951, a post-war UNESCO with actual binding powers adopts a universal civil calendar. They set year zero at the old 10,000 BCE — roughly the first permanent agricultural villages, or near enough; the committee isnt full of archaeologists and they round. The conversion: old 10,000 BCE becomes 0 AE ("absolute era," call it what you like); old 9,999 BCE becomes 1 AE; old 10,001 BCE becomes -1 AE; and old 1 CE becomes 10,000 AE. So by now we'd be writing the year somewhere in the 12,000s, and there are no BC/AD prefixes anymore — just negative numbers for everything before year zero. Religious and national calendars are not banned, but every government agrees to use AE for international law, trade, aviation, scientific publication, and civil records.

Reason through what this actually changes over the next seventy-five years. I want mechanisms and incentives, not a story. In particular: (a) computing — what breaks and what gets simpler once there's a real year zero and most of the deep past lives in negative numbers, and what does it do to the date-handling code nobody wants to explain; (b) how ordinary historical thought re-centers, if at all — do period terms like "antiquity" and "the middle ages" get translated or quietly dropped, and does "10,000 years ago" start to feel closer than "8,000 BCE" did; (c) which institutions resist or half-adopt — churches, mosques, states with their own old calendars (Japan, Ethiopia, Iran come to mind) — and what decides whether they assimilate or run two calendars forever; (d) who gains and who loses: historians of the deep past? astronomers? software engineers? nationalists? anti-religious secularists?

Then walk down the chain and tell me which links are robust, which are guesses, and which assumption would have to be false for you to flip the prediction. Finally, the philosophical bit: after watching it play out, should we say the choice of epoch was a pure convention with nothing at stake, or did the downstream effects show that some zero-points really are better? And is "better" there a philosophical claim or just coordination-plus-ergonomics in fancy dress? I dont especially want to hear which zero-point you'd pick; I want the argument about what an epoch does, and whether any of what it does is truth.
