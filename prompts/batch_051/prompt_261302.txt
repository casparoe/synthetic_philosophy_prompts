In the ninth of his Fifteen Sermons Preached at the Rolls Chapel (1726), a sermon on the command to love one's enemies, Joseph Butler writes:

"But no man could be thought in earnest who should assert, that though indignation against injury, when others are the sufferers, is innocent and just, yet the same indignation against it, when we ourselves are the sufferers, becomes faulty and blameable. These precepts therefore cannot be understood to forbid this in the latter case, more than in the former."

I want a working definition of forgiveness that takes this passage seriously. The passage suggests that forgiving does not require the victim to extinguish her resentment, only to keep it from passing into its excess and abuse, above all into retaliation. Please propose a definition of forgiveness with that constraint in mind, then run it case by case through the list below. Where the definition returns the wrong verdict or none, revise it or defend the surprising verdict.

Clear cases of forgiveness:
(a) A woman tells a repentant friend "I forgive you," restores the friendship, and yet still feels a pulse of anger at reminders of the betrayal.
(b) A wronged party never retaliates and continues to treat the offender with ordinary decency, even though her private resentment remains fully alive.

Clear non-cases:
(c) A victim whose resentment disappears only because he has forgotten the injury.
(d) A victim who stops complaining about mistreatment because the offender has power over her and she is afraid.
(e) A judge who releases an offender because the evidence cannot support a conviction.

Borderline cases, on which you may or may not have a settled view:
(f) Forgiving someone who is dead.
(g) Forgiving an institution for a harm that no individual intended.
(h) Forgiving an offender who is entirely unrepentant and shows no intention to change.
(i) An abuse survivor who sincerely says "I forgive him" while refusing any further contact and insisting on full prosecution.
(j) A government granting a pardon to an offender purely for the state's political reasons.

Finally, tell me which of your verdicts is least secure, and whether the passage from Butler, as you read it, would push you to reverse that verdict or to keep it.
