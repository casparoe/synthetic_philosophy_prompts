I've been going back and forth on the Hart–Devlin debate for weeks and I want someone to force me off the fence, not give me a tour of it. On the one hand: the harm principle looks to me like the only account of when the criminal law may coerce adults that isn't just a prettied-up way of saying 'the majority finds this icky.' If the sole ground for criminalizing conduct is that it harms non-consenting others, you get a clean, non-arbitrary rule, you stop having to argue about whether some subculture is corrupting society, and you don't have to pretend that a society's shared morality is the sort of thing that falls apart when the law stops enforcing it — which it very much looks like it doesn't. I also find the Hartian side persuasive: the fact that a lot of people share a moral conviction doesn't make the conviction right, and enforcing the unreflective parts of popular morality is exactly the mechanism that gives you sodomy laws and blasphemy laws.

On the other hand, I have an intuition I can't shake. I think consensual incest between adult siblings should remain criminal even in the cleaned-up case: no third parties, no children, no exploitation. I can't name a non-consenting victim the harm principle would recognize, and I know the standard debunk — it's disgust, it's a purity taboo, the revulsion is doing the work. I've tried the rationalist version where I taboo 'moral fabric' and find nothing but mood affiliation, and the judgment that this should stay illegal still doesn't update. I'd vote to keep the law, and that bothers me more than any clever counterexample.

So what should give? Present both sides at their strongest and then commit. Either tell me the intuition is a culturally installed taboo I should debunk and drop, and what would have to be true for that debunking to actually work; or tell me the harm principle needs amending, and what amendment could capture this case without quietly turning back into legal moralism; or show me that on the right reading the harm principle and the intuition are compatible, and say what that reading commits me to elsewhere. Pick the move you think a careful person should make, defend it, and tell me what evidence or argument would flip you. Don't leave me with the menu.
