Consider two statements a person might be willing to make. A Christian who accepts the standard evolutionary account of life's history might comfortably say that God willed the long process of natural selection, and in doing so willed that predation, parasitism, disease, and starvation would be the means by which living things developed over hundreds of millions of years, long before any human being existed. Yet the same person is likely to refuse to say that God willed that this particular fawn die slowly of starvation, or that this particular caterpillar be eaten alive from within by wasp larvae, even though the process they have just attributed to the divine will is nothing over and above the sum of such particular events. I would like you to examine this asymmetry and ask whether anything justifies it. Is there a principled difference between willing a process and willing its constituent events, or between designing a system and endorsing every concrete act the system predictably produces? Or is the asymmetry simply an inconsistency one has to live with in order to hold together evolutionary history and the doctrine of a good creation? If there is a justification, state it in its strongest form, test it against nearby cases such as a factory owner who commissions a production process known in advance to injure specific workers, and conclude by saying whether the asymmetry should be kept or abandoned. Please write your answer in American English, in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
