I am preparing a short artist’s statement on tragedy for a writers’ fellowship, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency check. I write literary fiction that often ends badly for its characters, and I have defended such endings in interviews and craft talks many times. In trying to write my position down in a more systematic way, I find myself committed to all of the following:

(a) The value of a tragic story is not the sadness the audience feels. It is the interest of watching a bad outcome become intelligible as the product of character and circumstance.

(b) I reject catharsis in the hydraulic sense. Art does not purge or drain emotion. What it does is shape and organize feeling, and I take that shaping to be an aesthetic end rather than a therapeutic one.

(c) Character involvement is a condition of tragedy’s working. If the reader does not care about the agent, the suffering is inert.

(d) I am moved by fictional characters while knowing they do not exist, and I take that movement to be part of why the work is good, not an accidental byproduct of a good plot.

(e) The distinction between earned and gratuitous suffering is real and important in criticism, but I cannot specify its grounds except by pointing to craft and pacing.

(f) Tragic art does no harm to its audience. Its sadness is a cognitive and emotional clarification rather than a cost that has to be justified.

(g) I accept a broadly Humean thought that in tragedy a subordinate painful passion is converted into a dominant movement of pleasure through eloquence and the pleasure of imitation, though I would put less weight on eloquence and more on the reader’s sense of being shown why things had to go as they did.

I suspect not all of these can be held together, but I do not want to guess where the problem is before I present them. Go through the list and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each pair, say whether the tension is genuine or only apparent, rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say what the cheapest fix would be for the worst one. Please write your answer in American English.
