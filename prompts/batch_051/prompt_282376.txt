A friend and I argued late into the night about the film Million Dollar Baby. He insisted the ending is a straightforward brief for a right to assisted dying; I held that it actually complicates a purely autonomy-based justification, because Maggie cannot act herself and Frankie must judge that her suffering has become unbearable. I would like a focused reading of the film through the philosophical distinction between justifications for assisted dying that rest on autonomy and those that rest on mercy or dignity. Which scenes and choices align the film with each rationale? Does the film present Frankie's act as respect for Maggie's self-determination, or as something he does for her—perhaps even something he assumes the authority to decide? Where does the work resist the autonomy reading, and what does it add to the philosophical debate that the standard autonomy-versus-mercy distinction misses? I am less interested in a verdict on assisted dying itself than in what the film reveals about the structure of these justifications.
