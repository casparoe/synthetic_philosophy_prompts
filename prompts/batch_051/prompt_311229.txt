I'm putting together a closing poem for an advanced undergraduate seminar on well-being, to be read after the session on the paradox of hedonism. Write a villanelle whose two refrains are the paradox's two half-truths: one saying that pleasure flees when pursued directly, and the other saying that pleasure arrives only as the byproduct of absorption in something valued for its own sake. The speaker should already know both refrains and should be caught in the second-order bind of monitoring their own enjoyment, aware that the monitoring is itself a form of pursuit.

Let the form carry the philosophical content. The refrains should alternate like two claims that keep passing each other, and when they meet in the final two lines the pairing should show why they cannot be willed together: choosing the absorbing activity for the sake of the byproduct is still aiming at the byproduct, and that aiming is what scares the pleasure off. Leave the tension open rather than resolving it, and make it a real poem — technically sound as a villanelle, with language that has some life in it — rather than a versified slogan. It should be precise enough that someone who thinks about the paradox would recognize its structure, but it need not use technical vocabulary.
