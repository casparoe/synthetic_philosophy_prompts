Construct a decision problem for a donor choosing between two ways to spend the same amount on childhood nutrition in a low-income region, where a marginal cost-effectiveness analysis and a systems/feedback analysis give opposite recommendations even though both accept the same first-order numbers. Constraints: spell out the actual mechanism (the feedback loop, what lags, who is affected), no global catastrophic stakes, no 'emergent' effects that aren't causal through identifiable people, and the marginal estimate has to be genuinely right about the first-order impact. Then show each analysis step by step, say which single structural feature flips the verdict, what happens to the disagreement if you shorten the delay or weaken the loop, and what would change your mind about whether the systems side is doing real causal analysis rather than moving the goalposts after the first-order results come in. The case is for a workshop on whether 'systems change' is a real category or just interventions with longer, lagged effects.
