I am a software engineer who works in a typed functional language, and I am comfortable with algebraic data types, pure versus effectful functions, transparent type aliases versus opaque newtypes, and the habit of reading a type as a specification of the values that inhabit it. I have no formal background in moral philosophy. Recently I have been reading about two metaethical doctrines: the is/ought gap, understood as the claim that no conjunction of purely descriptive premises logically entails a normative conclusion unless the argument already contains a normative premise; and the naturalistic fallacy, understood as the claim that normative concepts such as good cannot be defined in purely descriptive terms, because any proposed definition leaves open the further question whether that descriptive property is good. My engineering intuition is that both doctrines are really claims about type discipline. Descriptive sentences inhabit a type Fact; normative sentences inhabit a type Ought; the first doctrine says there is no total function from Fact to Ought, so any derivation must either take an Ought as an argument or invoke an effect that produces one. The second doctrine says that defining Ought as a transparent type alias for some descriptive type, such as Pleasure, is a category mistake, and that morality needs an opaque newtype rather than a synonym.

Please restate the is/ought gap and the naturalistic fallacy in this vocabulary as systematically as you can, keeping proof-theoretic claims about constructing functions distinct from definitional claims about types where that distinction matters. Do not smuggle in the type system's assumptions and present them as philosophical conclusions. Then provide the residue report: which parts of the distinction map cleanly onto types and functions; which parts translate only with distortion; and which parts have no counterpart in a type system at all. I am especially curious about apparent residues such as someone changing their moral conclusions without acquiring any new descriptive belief, the difference between an argument's logical validity and its motivational force, and the possibility that the gap concerns our vocabulary rather than the underlying logic. Conclude by telling me whether my intuition that the whole thing is just type safety is roughly right, illuminating but reductive, or misleading, and say what the success or failure of the translation would and would not establish about the underlying philosophy.
