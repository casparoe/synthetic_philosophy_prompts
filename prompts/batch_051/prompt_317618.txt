Ok, so I recently got out of a six-year relationship and in the aftermath I've been reading a weird mix of Stoic stuff, grief books, and contemporary therapy-adjacent ideas, and I'm trying to figure out whether what I've landed on actually hangs together before I build a whole worldview on it. Here's roughly what I believe right now:

1. The big disruptive emotions are basically judgments: grief is mostly the thought that something bad has happened, plus some kind of assent or endorsement of that thought.
2. Negative emotions like grief, jealousy, and anxiety are bad for me, and the mature move is to stop assenting to them, working toward something like the Stoic idea of being unshaken (I know apatheia isn't supposed to be numbness, but you get it).
3. At the same time, the grief I've been feeling seems to carry information about how much the relationship mattered that I couldn't of gotten any other way; the intensity is the information, not a side effect of it.
4. So I also think I shouldn't treat those emotions as noise: I should mostly trust what they say about what I value, more than I trust the calm stories I tell myself afterwards about what I value.
5. I think a good life has to stay open to loss and disappointment; a life that protected itself from ever feeling that kind of pain would be worse as a life, not just less informed.
6. And I think at least some painful emotions aren't just useful signals of what matters; they're part of what it is to value something in the first place, so a good life includes some of them rather than just tolerating them.

I can't tell which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are. Can you go through the pairs, flag the real conflicts, rank them by severity, and tell me what the cheapest revision is and what each fix would cost? Be specific about which belief has to give.
