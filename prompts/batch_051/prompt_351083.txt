I'm part of a long-running animal cognition reading group, and two of our members have basically become the Canon People. One is a hardline Morganian: he treats the canon from C. Lloyd Morgan's An Introduction to Comparative Psychology (1894) as a standing veto on appeals to higher faculties like insight, mind-reading, or planning whenever a lower faculty—trial-and-error learning, associative conditioning, fixed action patterns—would do the job. The other is a cognitive ethologist who thinks the canon is a nineteenth-century bias: it smuggles in a linear scale of faculties, makes the lower explanation the default regardless of evidence, and systematically under-credits animals. They have actually agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the document.

A couple of things matter to them. They want the agreed claims to be substantive, not just parsimony-is-good platitudes. They want the disagreements phrased so each person would sign their own side's wording after reading it. And they want the cruxes to be testable, not just philosophical standoffs. I suspect one crux is whether lower can be made precise independently of the verdict; another is whether the canon gives a positive reason to prefer the lower explanation or just blocks a thin inference to the higher one; a third might be whether analogical inference from human first-person psychology is ever a legitimate source of evidence in animal cognition. The passage they keep circling is where Morgan lays down the rule—I'll paraphrase to avoid misquoting—that an animal action should not be chalked up to a higher mental capacity when a capacity lower on the psychological scale would account for it.

Please write the joint statement they should have produced. Format it as exactly five numbered agreed claims, each a sentence or two, no introduction before the first claim and no conclusion after the table. Then include a disagreements table with exactly three rows. Columns: Claim at issue, Morganian wording, Cognitive ethologist wording, and Test both accept as bearing on it (with each side's prediction in one clause).
