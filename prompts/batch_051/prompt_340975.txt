I've been following the Anthropocene fight for years and it feels like it's stalled in an instructive way. Geologists keep proposing a formal start date tied to a single physical boundary — the mid-20th century acceleration, the industrial revolution, early farming — while historians and social scientists keep saying a boundary can't mark a process, and that picking one is political even when it's dressed up as technical. Everyone's working from the same ice cores and sediment records, so its not really a disagreement about the data. My working theory is that the two sides are answering different questions under one name: when does the physical record first cross some stratigraphic threshold, versus which human change matters for the period. And underneath that maybe a quieter fight over who owns the word 'epoch'. Is that right, or is there a real empirical or conceptual disagreement in disguise? And if I'm right, what would count as progress — a better marker, a new distinction, or leaving the term informal?
