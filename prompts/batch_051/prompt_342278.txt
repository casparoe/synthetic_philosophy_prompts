In the Antinomy of Pure Reason of the Critique of Pure Reason, Kant says in effect that pure reason gets drawn into questions it cannot decline, because they arise from its own nature, but cannot answer, because they outrun every faculty of the mind. That bit is doing alot of work for me. I want a document from an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the First Antinomy: the thesis side (the world has a beginning in time and is finite in space) and the antithesis side (the world has no beginning in time and no limits in space). Here's the constraint: both sides agree to treat the dispute as a first-order cosmological dispute, not to dissolve it by appealing to transcendental idealism, and both accept Kant's own paraphrase of the conflict as a fair statement of what they're stuck on. Write the joint statement they could both sign after a year of good-faith back-and-forth. It should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept, including about what a "world-whole" would have to be, about infinite series, and about what the standard arguments for each side actually establish; (2) a disagreements table with the exact points where they part ways, each side's view worded so that side would sign it; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—and for each crux a concrete case or line of argument both agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about it. If some of their disagreement turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.
