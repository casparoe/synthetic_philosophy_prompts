I've been poking at Euclid's Elements, specifically Book VII, where he defines a unit as the thing that makes each existing thing be called one. Compare that with the way a physicist or engineer uses "unit" today, where a unit is mostly a conventional standard—a meter, a second, a kilogram—that we all agree on so measurements can be compared. Are these just two different senses of the same word, or is there a real conceptual break between Euclid's idea of a unit as something in things and the modern idea of a unit as a public yardstick? And if they are seperate, does Euclid's definition actually explain anything about counting, or does it just push the problem back one step?
