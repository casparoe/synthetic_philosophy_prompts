In a seminar on the continuum last week someone trotted out the line "God made the integers, all the rest is the work of man" to shut down a debate about whether the real numbers are discovered or defined, and I've been turning it over ever since because I can't tell what it's actually claiming. Sometimes it sounds like a boring point about notation, and sometimes it sounds like the wild claim that the continuum is a fiction we built. Lay out the distinct precise theses that line could be expressing, from the trivial to the substantive to the outright false, and for each say whether it's actually defensible. Then tell me wich of those readings would actually license the conclusion my classmate used it to support — that the reals are defined rather than discovered. The thing nagging me is that we do build the reals, via Dedekind cuts or Cauchy-sequence equivalence classes and so on, but the construction isn't unique, while any two complete ordered fields are isomorphic in a unique way, which sounds more like something you discover than something you decide.
