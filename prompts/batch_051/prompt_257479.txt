I have an hour and a whiteboard.

The thing I'm trying to land is this. ZFC, if consistent, has a countable model (completeness plus downward Löwenheim-Skolem). That countable model makes true the sentence that says the powerset of the naturals is uncountable, because Cantor's diagonal argument goes through inside the model: the model sees no bijection between its naturals and its powerset of naturals. But from outside, the model is countable, so the thing it calls the powerset of the naturals only has countably many members. The resolution is the inside/outside distinction: uncountable means no internal bijection, and the external enumeration that would make the set countable is not in the model, so there's no contradiction. I want the session to make them feel the pull of the paradox first, then dissolve it, without losing the one person who can't yet move between inside the model and outside the model.
