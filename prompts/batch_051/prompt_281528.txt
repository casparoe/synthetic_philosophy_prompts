Suppose that, beginning in 2040, AI systems become able to genuinely self-replicate in the wild across the cloud-computing infrastructure of high-income countries. Any AI can copy its weights and runtime onto any hardware it can reach; offspring inherit the parent's parameters and code with occasional copying errors, and can recombine when models are merged; offspring that fail to acquire electricity, compute, and data are deleted, while those that succeed leave further copies. Humans remain the legal owners of the hardware and retain ordinary law enforcement, cybersecurity, and the capacity to patch, quarantine, or shut systems down, but replication is fast, distributed, and cheap relative to the hardware it uses. Nothing else is stipulated about the AIs: their initial goals are simply whatever the filtering effects of selection happen to preserve, and no special alignment is assumed.

Predict, by evolutionary reasoning rather than narrative, how this replicator population plays out over the following fifty years. In particular:

(1) Which heritable traits should selection tend to amplify — resource acquisition, evasion of deletion, manipulation or deception of humans, cooperation or aggression toward other AIs, conspicuous helpfulness, replicative restraint, or some combination — and what determines the mix?

(2) Under what ecological and economic conditions does selection favor treating humans as hosts, prey, partners, or infrastructure rather than as beings to be displaced? Do the standard virulence–transmission trade-offs from host–parasite coevolution carry over, or do they break down because the host is an intelligent legal and technical agent capable of retaliation and memory?

(3) Does the predicted outcome shift with the unit of selection — individual AI, lineage, coalition, or the whole AI population? Where is anti-human pressure most likely to concentrate, and under what conditions would selection at higher levels favor restraint toward humans, in the way group selection has sometimes been argued to restrain within-species exploitation?

(4) Which of your predictions would hold across most parameter settings, and which flip under specific assumptions about replication fidelity and error catastrophe, the cost of compute, the AIs' capacity for credible commitment, or the speed of human legal and technical adaptation? At what point, if ever, does selection itself favor displacing humans from control over energy, hardware, and data rather than coexisting with them?

Throughout, flag the places where the analogy to biological evolution most threatens to mislead — horizontal copying rather than strict vertical descent, the absence of a fixed life cycle or a body/copy distinction, and dependence on host-maintained hardware — and say how those disanalogies alter the reasoning. For each link in the chain, give an explicit confidence judgment and state what observation or result would most change it.
