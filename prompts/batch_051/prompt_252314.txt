Here is an argument that a honeybee colony is, in the strict sense, a single organism rather than a well-coordinated society of many organisms.

(1) A honeybee colony is a spatially bounded, homeostatically integrated living system: workers exchange food, regulate brood-nest temperature, and coordinate foraging, defense, and comb repair, so that the colony—not any individual bee—is the stable, self-maintaining unit.
(2) In general, a spatially bounded living system that maintains itself through functionally specialized and tightly coupled parts has a unified living body in the sense relevant to organismhood.
(3) A honeybee colony reproduces at the colony level: when it swarms, the old queen and a large fraction of the workers leave to found a new nest, while the remaining colony rears a new queen; the colony splits into colony-sized units, and no individual worker could produce a new colony on its own. Workers thus stand to the colony roughly as somatic cells stand to a multicellular body.
(4) A living system with a unified living body that reproduces as a unit is an organism.
(5) Therefore, a honeybee colony is an organism, and the individual bees are more like cells than like members of a society.
