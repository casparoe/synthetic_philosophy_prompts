I spent most of my working life as an experimental physicist, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of time with the enthusiasm of a late convert. I have come, tentatively, to accept presentism: the thesis that only present objects and events exist, that the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist, and that what exists is confined to an advancing now. My reasons are partly experiential — time, as I live it, passes — and partly conceptual: I cannot yet see how a tenseless four-dimensional block can account for the difference between what is settled and what is still open, or for why deliberation feels the way it does.

I am not asking for a survey of the eternalism debate. I want something more diagnostic. Treat presentism as a hypothesis I have adopted, and ask what would count against it. For each serious challenge — physical, metaphysical, semantic, or phenomenological — I want to know:

(a) exactly what would have to be true for the challenge to land, stated as a premise a presentist must accept or reject;
(b) whether a careful presentist would in fact concede that premise, and in what form;
(c) how severe the challenge is if the premise is granted — fatal, seriously damaging, merely awkward, or dismissible;
(d) the strongest presentist reply available, and whether it defuses the challenge, merely relocates it, or leaves it standing.

I know enough physics to expect that the relativity of simultaneity, and the argument associated with Rietdijk and Putnam, is where presentism is said to be in most trouble; give that its due, but do not let it crowd out other considerations, and rank everything by how much it should actually move me. I would also like to know which alleged problems a committed presentist should not lose sleep over, and why.

One thing a scientific career teaches is that a hypothesis is only as good as the conditions under which it would be abandoned. So please be blunt: if the request as I have framed it rests on a confusion — for example, if presentism is too much of a family of views for the question "what would count against it?" to have a single answer, or if some of the standard objections trade on conflating what exists with what can be said truly — then say so and reformulate the task rather than complying politely. I would rather have a self-critical assessment that flags its own uncertainties than a confident taxonomy that hides guesswork. And if the honest conclusion is that presentism has been insulated by qualifications until nothing could refute it, tell me that too, and say whether that insulation is itself a problem for a metaphysical view.

After the prose discussion, close with two structured summaries. First, a table ranking the defeaters from most to least serious, with these columns: Defeater (a one-line description); Load-bearing premise (what must be true for it to bite); Would a presentist concede it?; Severity if granted; Best presentist reply; and Net assessment (defused, relocated, or left standing). Second, a compact JSON object with these fields: overall_exposure (a short phrase), strongest_defeater, most_overrated_defeater, qualified_into_unfalsifiability (true or false), and assessment_of_insulation (one or two sentences).
