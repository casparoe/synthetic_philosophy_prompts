Slightly weird one, but I want a proper case analysis, not the standard take. You're an expert on the political theory and law of customs unions, pooled sovereignty, and what membership actually consists in. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming the clean categories before you map the case.

Take Brexit and the United Kingdom's post-2021 trade arrangements as a case study for what joining a customs union consists in and what leaving one restores. Treat these as given: before the end of the transition period the whole UK was inside the EU customs union, with a common external tariff, no internal customs formalities for goods in free circulation, and trade policy negotiated centrally as an exclusive EU competence; after the Trade and Cooperation Agreement the UK has its own tariff schedule and its own trade-negotiating power, but goods moving between the UK and the EU face customs declarations and rules-of-origin checks; and Northern Ireland, under the Windsor Framework, stays aligned with EU goods rules and applies the EU customs code while remaining in the UK's customs territory—which is why the friction is between Great Britain and Northern Ireland rather than on the island of Ireland.

I already know the rough political stories: the taking-back-control story versus the you-cant-leave-a-customs-union-without-border-checks story. What I want is the conceptual anatomy underneath them. Spell out what a common external tariff, a customs territory, rules of origin, and delegated trade policy each do to a country's decision rights. Then run the episode through that machinery: what did the UK actually regain, what did it not regain, and which features of the deal are explained by the standard theory of customs unions. Then say plainly where the theory cannot see the phenomenon—dont smooth over the mismatch. In particular, Northern Ireland looks like a third thing, neither simple membership nor simple exit, and I want you to say whether it is a stable hybrid category the standard theory should add, or a kludge whose instability is itself evidence that customs-union membership is more binary than the sovereignty talk admits.
