I teach a seminar on the ethics of influence, and before I spend another term adjudicating among the usual theories, I want the specification written out properly. Give me the desiderata for an adequate account of manipulation—specifically, one that can distinguish manipulation from legitimate persuasion. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. It must, for example, count as manipulative the non-deceptive emotional pressure of guilt-tripping or targeted flattery; it must not over-generate so that every non-rational influence comes out manipulative, such as humor, aesthetic framing, a hard deadline, motivational interviewing, or a well-designed default; it must distinguish manipulation from coercion, from exploitation of a known vulnerability, and from ordinary appeals to self-interest; it must say whether manipulation requires an intention, or whether it can be built into the design of a choice architecture or a recommendation system and reach someone without any individual manipulator; and it must take a position on whether manipulation is wrong in itself or only contingently, and whether it is necessarily autonomy-undermining.

Then flag which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the commitments of particular camps—autonomy-first, reasons-responsiveness-first, deception-first, vulnerability-first. Finally, tell me whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and I am suspicious of pairing the requirement that manipulation always bypasses or degrades rational agency with the requirement that fully competent adults can be manipulated through their own ordinary reasoning; and say which single requirement, if any, eliminates the largest number of the standard views. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief scorecard for the main contenders at the end is fine, but the criteria are the deliverable.
