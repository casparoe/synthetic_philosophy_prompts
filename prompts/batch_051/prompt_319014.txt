I'm in a seminar on biological function, and I've gone back and forth with a classmate about whether the two main accounts of function are really rivals or just two descriptions of the same thing. On the etiological account, roughly: the function of a trait is whatever effect explains why the trait was selected for and maintained in the lineage. On the causal-role account, roughly: the function of a trait is whatever it does that contributes to a capacity of some larger system containing it, and which of its effects counts as its function depends on which capacity of the containing system we are trying to explain. I want to defend the claim that once the causal-role account is given its most natural biological reading, it collapses into the etiological account, and the following is my summary of that argument.

(1) For a trait in an organism, the relevant containing system is ultimately the organism, and the capacity we are ultimately trying to explain is survival and reproduction. (2) So, on the causal-role account, a trait's biological function is its contribution to the organism's surviving or reproducing. (3) An effect of a trait contributes to the organism's survival or reproduction only if that effect tends to be favored by natural selection in the environments the organism faces. (4) But the etiological account already says that the function of a trait is the effect for which it was selected. (5) Therefore the causal-role account and the etiological account pick out the very same effects, and the rivalry between them is only apparent.

I have a lingering sense that step 2 quietly promotes one among many legitimate causal-role analyses into 'the' function, and that step 3 overreaches in cases of novel environments or fortuitous benefits, but I cannot make the diagnosis precise. Is the argument valid? If it is not, say exactly which step or steps fail; for each failing step, give the strongest corrected version you can and then check whether the argument still goes through on that correction. If the argument is valid as written, say whether every premise is true. Please test whatever diagnosis you give against a concrete example, such as the noise a heartbeat makes, a case where the two accounts seem to come apart.
