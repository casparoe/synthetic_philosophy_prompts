I'm presenting a paper at a doctoral seminar in biblical hermeneutics next month. My thesis is that the conspicuous numbers in biblical texts—seven, twelve, forty, 666, the 153 fish, the patriarchal ages—should be read as conventional markers of completeness, covenant, testing, judgment, and so on, that is, as compositional devices rooted in ancient Near Eastern number symbolism rather than as a cryptographic code planted in the text. I also claim that the line between legitimate symbolic interpretation and numerology or pattern-hunting is not whether patterns exist but a difference in inferential discipline: a pattern counts as intended meaning only if it was available to and plausibly intended by the ancient author, and only within the limits of what that author could have controlled.

The audience is mixed. About half are historical-critical scholars who hold that meaning is constrained by human authorial intention and ancient genre conventions. Two are literary critics who treat the final form of the text as the locus of meaning, independent of authorial intent. The rest are first-year M.Div. students and a couple of devout lay auditors who have seen viral videos about hidden numerical codes in the Bible and are drawn to the idea that the text carries encrypted messages. The same thesis will therefore draw very different objections from different corners of the room.

My argument, in brief: (1) the numbers I discuss have well-attested symbolic values in the wider ancient Near Eastern world; (2) pattern is cheap—any sufficiently long text yields striking numerical patterns given enough ways of combining, summing, and rearranging digits, so the bare existence of a pattern is weak evidence of design; (3) the real dispute is therefore about evidence of intent, and I argue that cross-book totals and digit manipulations fail because no single ancient author controlled the shape of the canon; (4) I concede that a final-form text can carry symbolic weight beyond the author's conscious intention, while denying that this licenses the decoding of hidden messages.
