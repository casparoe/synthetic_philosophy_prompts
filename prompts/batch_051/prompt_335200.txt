After a long argument with a friend about the Meno problem, I wrote out the commitments I found myself defending. I should be grateful for a consistency audit of the set, rather than a general discussion of the issue. My present position is as follows.

(1) Knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief, and the Meno problem is a genuine problem: it cannot be dissolved by saying that talk of the value of knowledge is loose or merely honorific.

(2) In the familiar Larissa case, the person with the right true opinion about the road will guide the traveller just as well as the person who knows the way. For practical purposes, true belief is no worse than knowledge.

(3) What turns a true belief into knowledge, in the central cases, is that the belief was produced by a reliable belief-forming process. I am, to that extent, a process reliabilist about justification.

(4) The value of a reliable belief-forming process is purely instrumental: such processes matter only because they make true belief more likely.

(5) Whatever extra value knowledge has over true belief must be contributed by whatever condition turns true belief into knowledge; there is nowhere else for the surplus value to come from.

(6) I find the virtue-theoretic answer to the question of why knowledge is more valuable than true belief unpersuasive. The suggestion that the knower deserves credit for arriving at the truth, and that this credit is what supplies the missing value, seems to me to overstate the role of agency; in an ordinary case of perceptual knowledge I cannot identify any distinct value of that sort.

I can see that some of these may pull against one another, and I suspect at least one apparent clash would dissolve once properly diagnosed. Could you go through the set and tell me which commitments are jointly tenable and which are not; which pairs are merely apparently in tension and why the appearance is misleading; and, for the genuine tensions, how severe each is. Please order the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and say what the least costly revision would be and exactly what it would require me to abandon.
