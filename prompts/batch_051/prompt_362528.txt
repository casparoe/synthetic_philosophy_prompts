I am drafting the statement of my view on promissory obligation for a manuscript and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. I am inclined toward the following eight claims, but I have been told they pull in different directions, and I cannot see clearly where the fault lines are.

1. Promising is practice-dependent at the level of uptake: an utterance or gesture counts as a promise, and as the kind of thing that can generate promissory obligation, only against the background of a going social practice of promising with widely understood rules.

2. The wrong of breaking a promise is directed at the promisee, not primarily at the practice or at society at large; it is the promisee whom I wrong.

3. This wrong is not reducible to the wrong of having lied or of having caused harmful reliance. A promise can bind even if the promisee never believed it and never built anything on it.

4. The promisee therefore has special standing over the obligation: only the promisee can release me, and a serious promise to someone now dead can still bind me.

5. Yet a local practice can count an utterance as a valid promise while the utterance still fails to bind — for example, a promise extorted from me. Practice-validity is therefore not sufficient for promissory obligation.

6. Breaking a promise can be the right thing to do — if keeping it has become gravely harmful to a third party, say — and yet something remains: I still owe the promisee an explanation and a reparative acknowledgment.

7. A promise to do something seriously wrong at the time I make it — for example, a promise to help conceal a serious crime — generates no reason at all to do it, not even a pro tanto reason that ends up outweighed. That is different from a promise that becomes wrong to keep only later.

8. Promissory obligation is not a consequentialist calculation about the general benefits of trust or stable expectations; it is a distinct type of requirement, and its violation is a wrong to a person.

I recognize that some of these may only appear to conflict and others may not be jointly tenable. Please sort them out: identify the pairs that are genuinely in tension, distinguish the merely apparent tensions and say why they dissolve, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Finally, tell me whether one revision would do the most work, and what it would cost.
