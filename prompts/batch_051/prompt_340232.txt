I am drafting a position statement on divine foreknowledge and free will, and I want a consistency audit before I defend it in a seminar. I hold the following:

(1) God has infallible foreknowledge of every future free choice.

(2) A choice is free only if, with the entire past and the laws of nature held fixed, the agent could have done otherwise.

(3) For every possible free creature and every possible complete set of circumstances, there is a definite truth about what that creature would freely choose in those circumstances; God knows all these truths before deciding which world to create. I take this to be middle knowledge.

(4) Every true proposition has a ground: something in reality that makes it true.

(5) The ground of a counterfactual of creaturely freedom cannot be God's decree, because a divine decree that fixed what a creature would choose would remove the could-have-done-otherwise required by (2).

(6) The ground of such a counterfactual also cannot be the creature's actual or possible choice, because a possible creature in merely possible circumstances has no actual act to make a truth true.

(7) God's foreknowledge does not cause the choice it is knowledge of.

(8) I also think a coherent account of providence needs something like (3), because without advance knowledge of what free creatures would do, God could not select a world whose free choices are known in advance.

I want an audit rather than a lecture on which position I should hold. Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which pairs only appear to conflict on a naive reading? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. For each tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. Identify the single revision that would do the most to stabilize the set. Finally, say where your reasoning is least secure and which argument or consideration would most likely change your assessment of where the real problem lies.
