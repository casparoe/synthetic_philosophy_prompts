I work in the policy unit of a national government and we keep getting demands for official apologies for historical wrongs—colonial massacres, forced adoptions, medical experiments on prisoners, that sort of thing. Every one of these gets handled ad hoc: ministers improvise something warm, lawyers veto anything that sounds like admitting liability, and the statement that comes out reads as regret rather than apology. I want a reusable decision procedure my team can actually run on a proposed apology: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands off to a person because its a values call rather than a factual question. Ground it in whatever actually separates a real apology from regret or damage control—standing of the current government, continuity between the state that did the wrong and the state that apologizes, performative versus descriptive language, whether reparations are part of the speech act or a separate decision. It needs to be runnable by a generalist policy officer in an afternoon and end with something a minister can sign off on. Then run it on two cases: a forcible child-removal policy that ended in the 1970s where many victims are still alive, and a nineteenth-century colonial famine where no victims or perpetrators remain alive and the current government sees itself as discontinuous with the colonial regime. The second case is where I expect the procedure to say dont apologize and the politics won't like it; tell me whether that's the procedure working or a bug in the criteria.
