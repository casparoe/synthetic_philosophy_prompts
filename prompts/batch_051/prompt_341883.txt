I have no formal philosophy background; I build stream-processing systems, and the philosophical debates about the color phi phenomenon look to me like an engineering problem about out-of-order events and retroactive correction. Before I read further, I want the requirements specification written down, not a literature survey.

The phenomenon is this: two spots are flashed in quick succession at different locations, the first red and the second green. Observers reliably report seeing a single spot move from the first location to the second and change from red to green partway along the path — at a time before the green spot is actually flashed. The standard ways of modeling this are the Orwellian reading, on which the record of a completed experience is rewritten after the fact, and the Stalinesque reading, on which awareness is delayed so that the edit happens before anything reaches experience.

What must a theory of the timing of conscious experience get right to count as adequate? Write the desiderata, with the phenomenon or argument behind each. Cover at least: the color phi data; backward masking, where a later stimulus alters the perception of an earlier one; the distinction between the time an experience represents (event time) and the time at which the experience itself is produced (processing time); the question of whether there must be a determinate moment at which a content becomes the final, experienceable content or whether the idea of a final content is itself an artifact of the model; and the fact that all the evidence for these retroactive effects is a report the subject gives after the relevant window has closed. For each requirement, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. For example, the insistence that there is a fact of the matter, knowable in principle from the inside, about whether any given revision was Orwellian or Stalinesque looks to me like a camp preference rather than a genuine requirement.

Finally, flag any subset of these requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, and say why. My own suspicion is that a theory cannot both be answerable only to post-hoc reports and guarantee that it can settle, for every retroactive effect, whether the edit was Orwellian or Stalinesque. I want to know whether that trade-off should be built into the spec as a constraint rather than treated as a failure to meet an achievable requirement.
