I rewatched Batman Begins last week with my girlfriend and I keep comming back to the ending, which has been bothering me more than I expected. When Batman leaves Ra's al Ghul in the crashing monorail, he won't kill him, but he also says he doesnt have to save him. I'm trying to work out whether that's a real moral distinction or just a bit of Hollywood cheating. On the one hand the film clearly wants us to feel he kept his no-kill rule: he didn't pull a trigger or push him. On the other hand, Batman is the one who arranged the whole crash in the first place, and he knows exactly what will happen if he leaves. So I'd like a reading of that scene through the doing/allowing distinction and the killing/letting-die debate. Does it hold up as a case of letting die rather than killing, or does the fact that he causally set everything in motion collapse the distinction? And is the movie actually taking a stance on whether the distinction carries moral weight, or is it just using the vibes of it to keep Batman feeling clean? I'd rather have an honest reading that says where the philosophy fits the scene and where the scene is just moonlighting as philosophy.
