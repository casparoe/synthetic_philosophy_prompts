ok so I'm writing a paper and I've ended up with a set of views about love that I can't tell whether they actually fit together, and I'd rather find out now than in front of my professor. Here they are:

(1) Love is a response to the beloved's valuable qualities. If you ask me why I love my partner, I'll point to their wit, warmth, whatever it is about their mind that keeps surprising me. Those qualities are the reasons for the love.

(2) Love is non-substitutable. If I met someone with those same qualities but more of them, I wouldn't thereby love that person, and I don't think I should. Trading up just isn't what love is.

(3) There's also something conferral-ish going on. My loving someone makes them matter to me in a way they didn't before; it's not just me detecting value that was already sitting there waiting to be noticed. But I also can't decide to love someone — it's not voluntary in that way.

(4) Nonetheless, I think we're answerable for our loves. Loving someone who treats you with contempt doesn't just happen to you; it reflects badly on you, like an attitude you can be critisized for.

(5) Finally, the history does real justificatory work. I don't love a list of traits; I love this person, with our shared past and in-jokes and ways we've shaped each other.

My instinct is that (2) refutes a naive reading of (1), and (5) is supposed to fix that by making the qualities inseperable from the history. But I can't shake the feeling that (5) doesn't save (1) so much as quietly replaces it, and that (3) is doing something similar to (4). And I haven't even worked out whether (3) sits with (1) at all. Can you audit this package? I want the tensions ranked by how serious they actually are, with an honest take on which are only apparent, which force a real choice, and what the cheapest consistent version costs.
