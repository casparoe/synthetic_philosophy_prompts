In my philosophy of mind class we've been reading about teleosemantics, and my professor keeps reaching for the same analogy to make it feel intuitive. She'll say something like: think about the heart. Its job isn't to make a thumping sound, even though a healthy heart does that reliably; its job is to pump blood, because pumping blood is what hearts were selected for, and that's what explains why we have them. Then she says mental content is like that. A representation doesn't get its content from what it happens to be caused by, or from what some interpreter decides it means; it gets its content from what the mechanism was selected to do. The token state means P because the mechanism that produces it is present in the relevant population because tokens of that type correlated with P and that correlation did useful work. She uses this to push back on the idea that you need a homunculus reading of the representations, or an intentional stance from the outside, to fix meaning — the biological function does the fixing for free.

Then say whether the anti-homunculus conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing more of the argument than it should.
