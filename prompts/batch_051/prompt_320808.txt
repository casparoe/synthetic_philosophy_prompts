A friend keeps telling me the whole Great Divergence debate rests on a lottery fallacy. His line: asking why industrialization happened in Britain rather than the Yangzi delta or Gujarat is like asking why the one guy who won the lottery won. Somebody had to win, and once you know the winner you can always point to his numbers and make them look fated, but it's all retrospective storytelling. So he concludes that the usual deep explanations—coal, empire, institutions, wage rates, culture—are just-so stories, and the why-Britain-rather-than-China question isn't really well posed. I want to know how far this analogy actually carries before I conceed. Give me the correspondence table: what's the ticket, the draw, the odds, the winner, the winner's post-hoc story? Then show me where the mapping breaks down, and which breaks matter for his deflationary conclusion. Does the conclusion survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy doing all the work?
