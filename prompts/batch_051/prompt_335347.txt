I'm a software engineer and I keep getting dragged into this running argument on my team, and I honestly don't have the philosophy vocabulary for it. Half the team says there's no such thing as bad grammar: grammar is just a description of what speakers actually do, so saying that using literally to mean figuratively is "wrong" is like yelling at a thunderstorm for disobeying the weather forecast. The other half says that's absurd: languages have real rules, and if you break them you've made an error, the way a program that doesn't match the language spec doesn't compile.

Could you write a dialogue between two people who hold these views — a prescriptivist and a descriptivist — where they actually try to sort this out? Make both of them smart and make sure they respond to thier actual points instead of talking past each other. I'd like them to work through concrete cases like split infinitives, singular they, ending a sentence with a preposition, and literally as an intensifier. It'd be great if they eventually notice the argument might be partly about something else — social status, or whose dialect gets counted as "standard," or the difference between formal writing and speech.

Hard limit: 600 words, and I'd like just the dialogue — speaker names and lines, no narration.
