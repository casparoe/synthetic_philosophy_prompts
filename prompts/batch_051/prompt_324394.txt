I've got a mental rule I've been following for a while, and I honestly can't tell if it's a sensable response to the problem of other minds or just main-character syndrome with a philosophy coat on.

The rule is this: when someone tells me how they feel, I treat that as one piece of evidence, but I treat my own sense of what I would feel in their situation as stronger. The way I put it to myself is that I'm the only person whose inner life I can directly check, so my own reactions are the only first-hand data I have. Everyone else's self-report is, like, testimony about something I can never verify. I don't think other people are literally NPCs, but in practice I act like their inner lives are something I have to reconstruct from my own.

Concrete example: last week my sister said she wasn't angry about how my brother handled our mom's funeral arrangements, just tired. In her position I would have been furious, so I treated her as secretly angry, read between the lines with our brother, and later told him she was clearly upset and just covering it. She found out and got angry for real, saying I wrote her inner life for her without asking. That's what made me question the rule.

Is this rule defensible as an epistemic policy, or is it a bias dressed up as a rule? I care about the policy-level question, not just the abstract one: when does it track actual feelings, when does it fail, can it be restated so it keeps whatever is reasonable about the fact that I only have direct access to my own mind without turning into treating other people as NPCs, and am I entitled to it given that I can't actually read anyone's mind however much I'd like to? I'm especially curious whether my own case is even relyable as evidence about someone else at all.

- "verdict": one of "defensible as stated", "indefensible as stated", or "defensible only in modified form"
- "epistemic_core": the part of the rule, if any, that survives scrutiny, in a sentence or two
- "bias_component": the part of the rule that is doing the illegitimate work, in a sentence or two
- "restated_rule": a defensible version of the rule in one or two sentences, or null if you don't think there is one
- "conditions": an object with two keys, "works_when" and "fails_when", each a list of two or three specific circumstances
