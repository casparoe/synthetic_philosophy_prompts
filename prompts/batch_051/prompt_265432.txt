Right, here's one. I want you to build me a spectrum argument for the intransitivity of all-things-considered betterness — and I don't want the usual torture-versus-duration thing, and no population ethics either. Here's the spec. Construct a chain of, say, 6 to 10 options where each adjacent option is better than the previous one when the two are parked side by side, yet the final option is clearly worse than the first when those two are compared directly. The comparisions have to be all-things-considered, not just better in some one salient respect. Make it ordinary: a school governing board, a local council, a small firm deciding between actual options — no sci-fi, no money pumps, no examples that lean on food or drink. And the end reversal mustn't come from redescription, framing, or a threshold you quietly built into the setup; each pairwise judgement should be one a thoughtful person could honestly defend on the merits, even if it's contestable.

Then do the check. Lay out the chain, give each adjacent comparison and say why it holds, explain why the first beats the last head-to-head, and spell out the cycle that gives us intransitivity. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the heavy lifting, and say what happens if you remove it — the intransitivity should collapse, so don't pick a feature that's optional decoration. Finish by saying how a defender of transitivity would most likely attack the construction, and which objection would actually make you give up on the case rather than just patch it. Oh, and write the whole thing in British English. Don't worry if one of the pairwise judgements feels contestable; I'd rather see the weakest one flagged than hidden.
