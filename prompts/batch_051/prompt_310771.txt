Here's the criteria I've been using for something to count as solvable in principle: the solution can be specified in finite terms, each step is mechanically executable, the procedure is sound (it never gives the wrong answer), and it settles every input with a yes or a no. I've never been fully sure that last one belongs there. Now consider this: someone proposes to solve alignment by exhaustive sandboxing. For any AI design, enumerate every possible life history of the AI once it's deployed, simulate each one, reject the design if any history does serious harm, and certify it safe only after all histories check out. For an unsafe design it eventually hits a harmful history and rejects. For a safe design, though, the enumeration over every possible history never finishes, so the procedure never actually reaches the step where it prints safe. Is this a solution to alignment in principle under my criteria? Go criterion by criterion; where the answer is no, say whether that's because of the procedure or because of my criteria, and say what I'd have to give up to count this as a solution.
