Okay so I've been in an interpretability reading group for months and we keep circling the same question, which sounds dumb-simple: do the internal activations of a neural network actually represent anything? What's maddening is that everyone agrees on the empirical stuff. We've all seen the linear-probe results where a direction in activation space tracks something like sentiment or board state or whether a sentence is in French. We've all seen activation patching, where you edit a direction or swap in a feature and the model's output changes the way you'd expect if it were actually using that feature. Someone even trained a sparse autoencoder and found a feature that lights up on Golden Gate Bridge stuff, and the other side just shrugged. No one denies the vectors are there, or that they covary with inputs and outputs in reliable ways. And yet the two camps have not moved an inch in months.

One side talks as if the network literally has the concept in there: there's a truthfulness direction, a representation of the current board state, a feature for French text. The other side says we're projecting all of that. The activations are just points in a high-dimensional space, and calling one of them a representation of French is something we do with the vector, not something the vector is. Their line is that a thermometer doesn't represent temperature, it just covaries with it, and a transformer is a very fancy thermometer. The first side's line is that at some point a very fancy thermometer just is a representation, and that the second side is moving the goalposts.

My working theory is that the whole thing is stuck because they mean different things by representation, or more specifically because they disagree about whether representing requires a consumer inside the system that reads the representation, versus just being usable by a downstream readout. But I honestly don't know.

So my question isn't who's right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal? A dispute about standards of evidence? A hidden empirical question about whether the model causally uses the feature, or uses it in the way that representation-talk requires? Or is it bedrock intuitions about representation that no experiment will touch? And on your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a particular kind of experiment, a formal criterion, a distinction, a change of question — or should we just expect this one to stay stuck?
