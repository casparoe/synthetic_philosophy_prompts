I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy working on the aggregation of harms, and I'd like a consistency audit of a position I keep assembling before I build a paper around it. My working commitments are as follows. (1) In the dust-specks-vs-torture case — one person tortured continuously for fifty years, or an enormously large finite number of people each receiving a single dust speck in the eye, with no other differences — the torture is the worse outcome, and no finite number of such specks distributed one per person outweighs it. (2) Still, I don't think a speck is morally nothing: a world with one additional speck is, all else equal, slightly worse. (3) Within a single life, I accept ordinary aggregation: enough mild annoyances over a lifetime can be worse for that same person than one short, intense pain. (4) I accept that all-things-considered betterness is transitive. (5) I find it implausible that there is a sharp threshold on the spectrum of felt harms, such that harms just below it never count and harms just above it do. Do these commitments hang together, or am I quietly helping myself to something I haven't stated? Please rank any tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and for each say which commitment or unstated assumption would have to give. As you do, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on aggregation and the formal structure of betterness, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
