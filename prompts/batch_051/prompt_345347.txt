I have no academic training in philosophy; I worked for years as a paramedic and have been reading and thinking about these questions on my own. There is a pattern I keep noticing, and I do not have a word for it, nor any clear sense of whether it is one phenomenon or several.

Consider two situations. A person owns a potted plant, leaves town for three weeks during a heat wave, and does not arrange for it to be watered; the plant dies. We have no difficulty saying that his not watering it caused the plant's death. But a stranger living across town also did not water that plant, and nobody would say that the stranger's not watering it caused the death, even though the two non-actions are physically alike in the sense that neither person supplied water to the plant. In the second situation, a lifeguard watches a swimmer drown and does not move; we say the lifeguard's failure to act caused the drowning. Yet if another capable swimmer happened to be nearby and also did nothing, we would not say that swimmer's doing nothing caused the drowning.

It is not merely that we blame the owner and the lifeguard afterwards. The causal wording seems to be part of how we describe what happened in the first place. And the pattern appears outside dramatic cases: a nurse who skips a dose causes a patient's relapse; the patient's visiting cousin, who also did not give the medicine, does not.

I would like to know whether there is an established name for this way of picking out certain failures to act as causes while leaving countless others in the background. More important than the name, I want to understand whether this is one thing or a tangle of several—a fact about the world, a moral or legal judgment, a convention about what people are expected to do—and where the boundary lies between a non-action that counts as a cause and one that is mere background. If no exact term exists, what would you propose to call it?
