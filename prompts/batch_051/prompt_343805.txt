I keep noticing a parallel between Bongard problems and Goodman's new riddle of induction, and i can't tell whether it's deep or just the broad word underdetermination doing all the work. In a Bongard problem you get a handful of left boxes and a handful of right boxes and have to infer the rule the puzzle-maker had in mind, but the same displays are equally compatible with lots of rules: natural ones like big on the left, small on the right, and gerrymandered ones like contains a shape whose outline is closer in color to the background than to its own fill. Goodman's point is that a finite sample of emeralds supports both all-emeralds-are-green and all-emeralds-are-grue, and the tie only gets broken if you treat one predicate as more projectible than the other. Is this the same problem in two costumes, or are the two cases only superficially alike? The thing I can't decide is whether Bongard's puzzle-setter matters: there the correct rule is defined by what a person actually had in mind, which gives a fact of the matter the grue case lacks. Is that a real disanalogy?
