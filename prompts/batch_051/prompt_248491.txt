By cluelessness I mean the problem that for many ordinary actions the long-run consequences are so sensitive to unknowable downstream interactions that a consequentialist can't form a justified expected-value verdict. Make sure you cover the usual distinction between simple cluelessness (known branching outcomes, unknown probabilities) and complex cluelessness (we can't even enumerate the outcome space). I'm a philosophy professor, so I'll handle the philosophy; you be the translator. Restate the objection claim by claim in terms an ML engineer actually thinks in: policies, reward models, learned world models, environment distributions, OOD generalization, long-horizon rollout error, epistemic versus aleatoric uncertainty, robust MDPs, all of that. Don't give me a loose analogy; give a systematic translation, and flag every place where you have to stretch a term.

Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which only go across by distortion, and which have no counterpart at all. For each untranslatable or distorted piece, tell me whether that's because the ML vocabulary is missing something (say, real radical uncertainty) or because the philosophical claim was muddier than it looked.

Format the translation as a table with exactly these columns:
- source_claim: the philosophical claim in your own words
- ml_translation: the closest faithful rendering in RL/ML terms
- fidelity: one of clean, distorted, or missing
- residue: what failed to carry over, and why it failed

Then finish with a JSON object summarizing the table. Use these fields:
- clean_translations: list of source_claim strings that translated cleanly
- distorted_translations: list of objects, each with the fields claim and distortion
- untranslatable_claims: list of objects, each with the fields claim and why_it_has_no_counterpart
- verdict: a short string saying whether the leftover is a gap in the ML framework, a sign the cluelessness objection was less precise than it looked, or some mix of the two

You're an expert in both academic consequentialism and modern reinforcement learning. Start by brainstorming candidate mappings, and if a term resists translation, don't hide that.
