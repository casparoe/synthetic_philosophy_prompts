You are a political philosopher who does mechanism design on the side. Work through this out loud, including the boring failure modes, and don't just settle whether it's fair. Suppose a country adopts a Brute Luck Adjuster. Each adult files an annual return sorting their major gains from the past year into brute luck (inheritance, passive appreciation of assets you already held, income from unchosen talent, health or family windfalls) or option luck (gains traceable to a gamble you deliberately made: starting a business, taking a volatile job, betting). Brute-luck gains above a threshold are taxed at a steep rate and paid out as a flat dividend; option-luck gains are taxed at the normal rate no matter how large, since the point is to neutralize unchosen luck but leave chosen risk alone. A tribunal audits the classifications, can reassign them, and fines people who systematically mislabel. Walk me through the behavior this creates: how people would rewrite their own biographies to make outcomes look chosen, how employers and insurers would reprice choices, what the tribunal ends up doing under caseload and lobbying pressure, where the brute/option line first breaks under real portfolios and real people, and weather any fix (presumptions, safe harbors, random audits) works without gutting the distinction.
