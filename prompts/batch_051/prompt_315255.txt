Ever since I first ran into the careenium — the frictionless, two-dimensional world full of little spinning simmballs that occasionaly stick together, so that a big clump eventually appears to steer itself toward a light source — I've had the question the setup is built to pose stuck in my head: do the symbols push the substrate around? On one side, my reductionism feels basically airtight. Every simmball moves because of local forces, spins, and collisions; there is no point in the account where the careenium as a whole reaches down and gives a ball an extra nudge. The macro-level story — the clump wants the light — looks like a compressed description riding on top of the microphysics, not an additional cause on the same ledger. And I think the same is true of me: the spike trains and ion channels are the full causal story, and the decision is the pattern-summary of that story.

But I also have a firm, not-going-away intuition that when I decide to raise my hand, the decision itself — the contentful, symbol-level event — is what moves the arm. Not as an epiphenomenal byproduct, but in the same way the local forces move the balls. If someone told me the content-level causes are inert and the neural billiards did all the actual work, I'd have to conclude that I'm not steering my life any more than the careenium is steering toward the light, which is to say, not at all. And that conclusion feels unacceptable in a way I don't want to just bracket. I don't want the pre-packaged compatibilist line or the hard eliminativist line. I want the negotiation worked out: what exactly would it take for the causal-closure picture to be the thing that gives way, what exactly would it cost to drop the intuition instead, and whether there's a reading of cause — not a pun, a real disambiguation — on which the symbol-level pushes and the ball-level pushes are both true and both doing different work.
