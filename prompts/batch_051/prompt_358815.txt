Reading Mill's The Subjection of Women for the first time and this part stuck out:

“Again, in practical matters, the burthen of proof is supposed to be with those who are against liberty; who contend for any restriction or prohibition; either any limitation of the general freedom of human action, or any disqualification or disparity of privilege affecting one person or kind of persons, as compared with others.”

As a software engineer, this reminded me of the never-ending debate about whether the gender gap in tech is caused by natural differences instead of bias or pipeline stuff. If Mill were around today, would he say that people who claim women are naturally less suited to coding have the burden of proof? And would he think modern evo-psych or neuroscience studies about sex differences actually satisfy that burden, or would he see them as just another version of the old "it's natural" argument? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
