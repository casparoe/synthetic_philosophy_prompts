I want a piece of flash fiction for a philosophy-of-time zine. Write a short story in exactly five numbered scenes, each scene one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. The concept is presentism vs eternalism, but don't name the views in the story. Premise: the narrator has a small device—call it a tense switch—that doesn't move them through time; it changes wich theory of time is true in the world around them. In the presentist setting the past is gone and the future is open. In the eternalist setting the whole block is already there, so the past is still real but the future is fixed. They use it after their partner dies, partly to visit the partner, partly to figure out which world they can live in. Make the metaphysics show up in what the narrator can and can't do—like whether a voice from the past is really the partner or just a trace, or whether they can change a future the device shows them—rather than in dialogue about existence. End by forcing a choice between the two settings, not with "both are just perspectives." Also make eternalism genuinely tempting, even if it has a cost.
