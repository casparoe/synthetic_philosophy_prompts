I'm in a metaethics class this term and my reading group has been stuck for weeks on the same argument. Two of us think the is-ought gap is basically decisive: you can't derive a normative conclusion from purely descriptive premises unless something evaluative is already hiding in the setup, and a lot of so-called naturalistic ethics is just that hiding done more carefully. The other two are neo-Aristotelian naturalists of the Foot and Anscombe sort. They dont deny that the textbook is-ought inference is formally invalid, but they think that's a boring point because nobody actually reasons that way; they think there are natural facts about what a kind of organism is and does that are already normatively loaded — like what it is for a wolf to hunt in a pack, or what a good human life amounts to just in virtue of being the kind of animal we are. We've realized we've been talking past each other, and we thought it would help to write down the document we could all actually sign: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration.

Could you draft it for us? We'd like it to have (1) a numbered list of claims all four of us can accept, phrased so they're not vacuous but also not rigged toward one side; (2) a table of the disagreements that survive, where each side would accept the wording in its own column; and (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that answering them would make at least one side move. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and say in advance what each side predicts it would show. We've covered Hume's is-ought passage and Moore's open-question argument in class, so it's fine to use that machinery, but please don't let the document turn into a fight about whether the naturalists are simply committing the naturalistic fallacy — I want the formulations sharper than that. I suspect one crux is whether species-characteristic life and health already contain norms in a sense a biologist would have to countenance, and another is whether the open-question argument keeps its force once you fix that it's a human life we're talking about; you don't have to use those exactly, but they're the sort of thing we've been circling.
