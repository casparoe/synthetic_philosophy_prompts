The other says that's tidy, but a real faucet is coupled to thermal noise and tiny vibrations and water-pressure fluctuations, so a good chunk of the unpredictability is genuine, ontic randomness rather than just sensitive dependence. Their both too stubborn to let it stay a late-night argument, so they start sketching an experiment — and that's where I need help, because I don't want the scene to hand-wave.

How would one actually test the claim that an apparently irregular time series like a dripping faucet is deterministic chaos rather than predominantly genuine noise? I care about the logic more than the equations, but I want the precise thing the test leaves open, because that's what the characters will end up arguing about. End with a single sentence beginning 'Verdict:' that says in one sentence what the best test can and cannot show.
