After I retired from a career in experimental physics, I began reading epistemology, and I keep returning to a distinction that strikes me as both illuminating and, from an experimentalist's point of view, frustratingly underspecified. As I understand it, an epistemic bubble is a condition of informational absence: the person inside it never encounters the contrary evidence because her media diet, social graph, and recommendation systems simply do not deliver it. An echo chamber is different; it is a condition of pre-emptive discrediting, in which contrary voices do appear but always arrive framed as unreliable, compromised, or hostile. The suggestive claim associated with this distinction is that a bubble can be popped by supplying the missing information, whereas an echo chamber survives exactly that intervention, because the operative mechanism is not ignorance but a learned distrust of outsiders. I find that claim intuitively plausible, but as stated it seems to me closer to a promissory note than to a hypothesis.

Treat the distinction as a testable hypothesis rather than a mere taxonomy. To make the exercise concrete, imagine you can study a large online community organized around a contested empirical question, such as the safety of a widely used food additive, and that you can observe, and to some degree randomize, what members are shown, what they share, and how they respond when corrective information arrives. Design a study that would disentangle the two mechanisms: non-exposure to contrary information versus distrust-based pre-emption of it. Specify what you would manipulate or measure, what pattern of results would count in favor of the distrust mechanism rather than the information-gap mechanism, and how you would handle the obvious confound that real communities usually differ in both exposure and distrust at once. Then say what a positive result would and would not settle: whether it would license the strong claim that echo chambers are maintained by distrust, or only the weaker claim that distrust reliably accompanies them, and identify the residue that no empirical design can reach.
