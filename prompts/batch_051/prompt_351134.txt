I'm a back-end engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I want a sanity check on an analogy I can't get out of my head. Most of my work involves a split between two ways of describing a piece of software. One way is intentional: a module has a purpose — what it was built to do, what its interface promises, why the original commit exists, what the design doc says it is for. The other way is behavioral: a module has an actual role — what it genuinely does in the running system now, including the side effects nobody planned, the accidental behavior other services have come to depend on, and the documented purpose it no longer serves. These two descriptions drift apart constantly.

I've recently been reading about two accounts of biological function, and the split sounds to me like the same split wearing different clothes. On the first account, the etiological or selected-effect account, the function of a trait is the effect for which it was selected in the past: the job it was kept around for by its history of selection, whatever it happens to be doing today. On the second, the causal-role or systemic account, the function of a trait is the contribution it actually makes now to the operation of the containing system, with its history irrelevant. A clean instance of the gap would be a biological effect that was never selected for but has become load-bearing in the current system — which, as I understand it, is roughly what people mean by an exaptation.

The parallel I keep drawing is: selected-effect function maps to intended, documented purpose (the "why" carried by history), and causal-role function maps to actual behavior in production (the "what" carried by the live system). I need to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion caused by the fact that both fields use the word "function."

Please evaluate that. I'd like four things. First, state the mapping precisely, including which features on each side are doing the real work. Second, say what would have to be true of both domains for this to count as a substantive structural parallel rather than a shared metaphor of purpose-like talk. Third, identify where the structures come apart: the features of the biological distinction that have no real software counterpart, and the features of the software distinction that have no real biological counterpart. Fourth, give me the strongest false prediction the parallel would license if taken too literally — a place where the analogy looks compelling but says something false, and why exactly it goes wrong there. If the right answer is that the whole thing is only a metaphor, I'd rather hear that clearly than have the analogy flattered.
