Write a villanelle about māyā and the identity of self and Brahman in Advaita Vedānta. The two refrains should carry the two sides of the doctrine: one for the superimposed world of names and forms — the rope's snake, the desert water — and one for the single changeless substratum, Brahman, which Advaita says is not other than the self. Let the refrains alternate the way the apparent and the real alternate for someone still in the grip of māyā, and in the final quatrain let them come together so that the poem enacts the sublation, in which the snake is recognized as nothing but the rope and the world as nothing but Brahman, with no second thing. The poem should not merely state the doctrine; the alternation and final convergence of the refrains should do the philosophical work. A reader who knows Advaita should recognize superimposition and nonduality, and a reader who does not should still sense that the two refrains were never finally opposed.
