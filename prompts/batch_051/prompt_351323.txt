You are a professional philosopher specializing in American pragmatism and democratic theory. I am drafting a statement of my views on Dewey's experimentalism for a workshop, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Please think carefully before answering: begin by working through, in your own reasoning, exactly what each commitment commits me to, and what sort of evidence or argument would have to be given before you count two of them as genuinely in tension rather than merely different in emphasis. Then give me your final assessment.

Here is my position set as it currently stands:

1. Democracy is best understood as a method of collective experimental inquiry, not fundamentally as a device for aggregating pre-existing preferences.
2. Inquiry aims at warranted assertibility — a belief is good when it is justified enough by inquiry to act on — rather than at mirroring an antecedently ready-made world.
3. Expert scientific inquiry is fallible and provisional, but its conclusions are often warranted enough that, for purposes of policy, a democratic public should treat them as settled and reopen them only when new evidence or a new problem calls for it.
4. Everyone who would be substantially affected by a policy ought to be able to participate in the inquiry that shapes that policy, not merely receive its effects.
5. Democratic participation has intrinsic educative value: it is by taking part in collective inquiry that citizens form the habits of judgment that make better collective inquiry possible.
6. The standards and methods of inquiry are themselves revisable through inquiry; there is no fixed, final canon of method.

I want to hold all six, and I can tell that the difficulty, if there is one, is somewhere in the interplay among them, but I do not know where and I do not want you to assume in advance which one I would surrender. Audit the set: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, rank those tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me. Also identify at least one pair that only appears to be in tension and explain why it dissolves. Do not lecture me on Dewey; the object is my position set, and the work is tracing what these six commitments commit me to.
