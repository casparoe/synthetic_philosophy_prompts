I'm in a seminar on the ethics of autonomous weapons and there's this phrase the prof keeps using, "meaningful human control," and I think I've found a problem with it. Here's the argument I keep coming back to:

(1) For a human to control an action in a meaningful way, they have to be able to determine the concrete features of the action: which target gets engaged, when, with what degree of force, and whether to abort if the situation shifts.
(2) But a weapon is autonomous precisely insofar as it selects and engages targets using its own sensors and processing after being activated, without a human specifying those features at the time.
(3) So any genuine increase in autonomy just is a decrease in human control over those concrete features. I grant that the human still picks the mission, sets parameters, and maybe keeps a veto.
(4) But picking a mission and setting parameters is controlling the circumstances of the action, not the action itself. And a veto is only the power to stop the thing, not to direct it.
(5) So the demand that autonomous weapons stay under meaningful human control looks self-undermining: either you strip out the autonomy, so a human specifies the engagement in real time, or you let the weapon be autonomous and the human only controls the framing—whcih isn't meaningful control of the attack.

I half suspect this is a trick argument im playing on myself. Is the argument valid? If not, tell me exactly which step fails and what the correct version of that step would be. My worry is that I've defined "control" way too narrowly in step 1, but I can't tell if the actual slide happens between 3 and 4.
