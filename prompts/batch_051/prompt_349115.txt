Here's an asymmetry I keep tripping over, and I want to know whether it's justified or just a failure of moral attention. A chef who drops a live lobster into boiling water in an open kitchen gets protests, one-star reviews and, in some places, its now outright banned. But a lot of the people doing the protesting will buy frozen farmed prawns later that week from a system where breeding females routinely have one eyestalk cut or crushed off without anaesthetic to make them spawn, and where the prawns are killed by chilling or boiling. And the sea bass on the same menu may well have spent its last minutes gasping in air or in an ice slurry in a processing shed, and nobody treats that as a scandal. So visible killing at the table counts as cruelty, while invisible killing in the supply chain counts as ordinary agriculture. What principle, if any, could justify treating those two cases differently? The obvious candidates are visibility, distance, whether you do the killing yourself or merely allow it by buying, species, and whether the animal is a 'food animal'. The strongest candidate I can think of is that doing a thing yourself is worse than paying for it to be done. But that seems to fall over, because most of us would say personally gutting a fresh-caught cod is less awful, not more, than buying intensively farmed veal. So give me the strongest justification for the asymmetry you can construct, then tell me honestly whether it's a real principle or just a story about disgust and distance. If it fails, say what consistency would require, and in which direction we should move: treating the lobster case like the prawn case, or the prawn case like the lobster case.
