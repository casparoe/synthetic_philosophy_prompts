I run a small AI-alignment research group and am pitching a private funder on a four-month, one-investigator, entirely conceptual project. I want the one-page proposal to be built around the following question: is coherent extrapolated volition (CEV) a genuinely distinct target from an idealized-preference or ideal-observer target, or is it one of those views restated in alternative vocabulary?

Assume the usual alignment sense of CEV: the target is specified as what a coherent, extrapolated version of humanity's volition would want, where extrapolation includes knowing more, reasoning better, and becoming more the kind of agent one reflectively wants to be, and where coherence means the extrapolated volitions converge rather than merely aggregating existing, conflicting preferences. By idealized-preference accounts I mean views that idealize each person's actual preferences—full information, means–end rationality, perhaps imaginative acquaintance—and then face a separate aggregation problem across persons.

Within the group there is a live disagreement, and the proposal should be aimed at making progress on it. One side holds that CEV is an idealized-observer or idealized-preference account with the observer replaced by the species and the hard problems hidden inside 'coherent.' The other side holds that CEV changes the unit of analysis from the individual's preferences to the species' extrapolated volition, so that convergence is a substantive conceptual claim rather than a disguised aggregation rule. I do not need the proposal to settle this now; I need it to frame the question so that work done over four months could.

The proposal must do the following:

1. State the question so that it has a determinate answer: specify what would show that CEV reduces to an idealize-then-aggregate account, and what would show that the two are irreducibly different.

2. Explain why the obvious existing positions do not already settle the question. Reference positions by their content and argumentative structure; do not include a bibliography, literature review, or citations, as I will fill those in.

3. Defend a strictly conceptual or formal method, and say why that method rather than surveys, experiments, or textual exegesis. Be specific about the tools: formal models of preference revision and coherence, constructed cases of value change or conversion, analysis of what convergence could mean across persons, or whatever else you propose.

4. Lay out the possible conclusions and the considerations that would select among them.

5. Give an honest scope assessment for one page, four months, and one investigator: what the deliverable would be, what would count as success, and what would count as failure rather than mere inconclusiveness.

Write this for a technically literate funder. Do not smooth over the risk that the question is partly verbal; the proposal should say how it would diagnose and avoid that.
