I run a film discussion group, and next month we're watching Christopher Nolan's Inception. After a long argument with a friend about the ending, I want to try out a reading we didn't settle: my friend reads the spinning top as a test the film refuses to complete, leaving us with Cartesian doubt about whether Cobb is awake; I want to read the film through the Zhuangzi butterfly dream instead, on which waking doesn't settle anything because the dreamer cannot tell whether he is Zhuangzi who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is Zhuangzi, and the search for a criterion that would separate the two—and the assumption of a single self that either state belongs to—may be the mistake. Read Inception through that lens as a committed critic, not a surveyor of options: which scenes genuinely carry the Zhuangzian reading, where the film resists it and I have to concede ground to the Cartesian reading, and what the film adds to the debate that neither the butterfly dream nor standard dream skepticism already says. End with a definite verdict on whether Cobb's final choice is best understood as a Zhuangzian or a Cartesian move.
