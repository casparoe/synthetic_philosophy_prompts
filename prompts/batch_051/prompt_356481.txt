Hey, I'm on the board of a 40-unit housing co-op, and we're about to send a proposed bylaw change to the whole membership for a vote. I'd like you to draft the one-page memo the board will circulate before that vote. Here's the situation.

We have a reserve fund for big-ticket things like the roof and boiler, and right now the board can spend from it on "capital purposes" by a simple majority. A few years ago we spent a big chunk of it on a lobby remodel that we all later agreed was a mistake, and last year the board almost tapped it to cover an operating shortfall. So some of us proposed a new bylaw: reserve money can only be used for repairs and replacements, and any other use would need approval from 80% of all owners instead of just the board. The point is to make it really hard for us, or a future board, to raid the fund in a moment of enthusiasm or panic.

Now the argument. Half the board thinks of it as common sense—my neighbor calls it a Ulysses contract: tie yourself to the mast before the sirens start singing. The other half says its not a person tying their own hands, it's us tying the hands of owners who don't even live here yet, and maybe even us five years from now if we have better information. Someone pointed out the difference between Odysseus making his crew ignore him, which he asked for, and a clause that outlives the people who voted for it. There's also the question of whether requiring 80% even counts as binding, since it can still be undone, just with more effort. And one board member suggested a compromise where the rule lapses after ten years unless the owners reapprove it; some say that keeps us from governing from the grave, others say it guts the whole point because a future board can just wait out the clock.

Could you write the memo recommending a specific course of action—adopt the bylaw as written, adopt it with the sunset clause, reject it, or something else—with reasoning I can defend. Please engage the strongest version of the losing position, not a straw man. And begin with the memo itself, no preface or commentary.
