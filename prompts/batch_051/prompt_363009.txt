I chair the ethics advisory board of a fertility clinic that is revising its policy on preimplantation genetic testing. The medical staff has proposed offering patients polygenic risk scores for a set of non-disease traits—predicted educational attainment among them—so that, when choosing among embryos, patients can weigh those estimates alongside screening for serious single-gene disorders. One faction on the board argues that, once we permit selection against disease risk, refusing to permit selection on other valued traits draws a line without a principle; the other argues that selecting for non-medical traits shifts the clinic from preventing disease to facilitating the design of children, and that this changes the parent–child relationship in ways the first faction ignores. Draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with a clear recommendation, reasons the clinic's leadership can act on, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
