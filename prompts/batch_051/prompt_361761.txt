For a paper in my comparative government class, I am trying to settle what makes a state secular rather than established. We have studied four countries: France, with its policy of laïcité; the United States, which forbids the establishment of religion yet gives churches tax exemptions; England, where the Church of England is the established church; and Germany, whose system my teacher describes as separated but cooperative. To make the question manageable, I have set out five criteria for a genuinely secular state: (1) there is no officially established church and no single constitutionally privileged denomination; (2) the head of state has no role in any church's governance; (3) no clergy hold seats in the national legislature by virtue of their office; (4) the state does not act as a taxing or collection agent for any church; and (5) state officials play no role in appointing clergy. England fails this list clearly, while France and the United States, as far as we have studied them, pass it.

Germany is the case that troubles me. It seems to satisfy four of the five criteria: the German constitution states that there is no state church, the federal president has no ecclesiastical role, no clergy sit in the Bundestag by virtue of their office, and the state does not appoint Catholic bishops or Protestant clergy. But it violates criterion (4). The state collects the Kirchensteuer, or church tax: a religious community recognized as a public-law corporation may levy a surcharge equal to eight or nine percent, depending on the state, of a member's income-tax liability; the state tax office then collects the money along with the person's other taxes and passes it on to the religious community; and a member can stop the charge only by formally declaring that he or she has left the church.

Does Germany count as a secular state under these criteria, or does the church tax make it non-secular? Please work through the criteria one by one, and say whether the German case shows that criterion (4) — or the entire list — needs revision. I keep circling between two thoughts: that Germany is genuinely secular in the sense of having no established church, and that a government which becomes a church's billing department has already crossed the line my criteria are meant to draw. Which criterion is doing the real work, and which should be amended?
