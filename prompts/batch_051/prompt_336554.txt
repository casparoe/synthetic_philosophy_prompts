I am not trained in philosophy, but I have been turning over a puzzle for a long time: how can a thing remain the very same thing while it changes? Two pictures of this pull me in opposite directions. On one, a thing is wholly present at each moment it exists, so that the entire thing — not a slice of it — is there right now. On the other, a thing is a whole made of temporal parts, and what is present at any moment is just one part of the whole, much as a limb is only a spatial part of a body. Draft the joint statement that these two camps — the endurantists and the perdurantists — could both sign.

Please structure it as a short preamble stating each position in wording its advocate would accept; a numbered list of agreed claims about change, identity, and existence at a time, each non-vacuous; and a disagreements table in which each row gives the sub-question, the endurantist wording, and the perdurantist wording, each acceptable to its own camp. Include rows on whether temporal parts are genuinely analogous to spatial parts, on whether "wholly present" can be made precise, and on how one thing can be cool at dawn and hot at noon without contradiction. Then name the cruxes: the sub-questions whose settlement would make at least one side revise its view, and for each crux record the test or thought experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction stated in advance.

Anchor everything in two cases both camps agree to use: a lump of clay pressed into a statue and later squashed back into a ball, and a worm cut in two that regenerates into two complete worms. In each agreed claim and each table row, say how it bears on these cases. If, as you draft, you find that part of the disagreement is only verbal, identify that part and rewrite the formulations so it drops out of the remaining dispute.
