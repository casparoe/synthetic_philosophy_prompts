I co-mod a decent-sized Discord server for a competitive game, and our sanctioning clause (from the server's written code of conduct) says: "A member may be banned only for serious or repeated violations of this Code committed on this server or at server-organized events." That line was put in a few years ago after moderators started banning people for things they'd done in other communities, and the people who wrote it were definitely not picturing coordinated cross-platform pile-ons. Here's the case: a three-year member with a spotless record—he's helped a ton in the new-player channels, worst thing he's done is argue too much about balance patches—got accused of harassment in a completely different community, old screenshots started circulating on Twitter, and now we've had waves of brand-new accounts join the server demanding he be banned and disrupting every event until he's gone. The member says he didn't send anyone and wants to stay. Some mods read the clause as flatly forbidding the ban: whatever happened elsewhere, it wasn't "on this server." Others say that's a wooden reading, because the whole point of the rule was to protect the server, and right now the server is being wrecked. I want to know what the rule actually requires or permits here, and specifically how the plain words, the purpose of the rule, what the drafters would say if we could ask them about this exact case, and what a reasonable member reading it would take it to mean each come out—and wether they diverge. And a second-order thing: if we ban him to end the raids, are we punishing him for the mob's behavior, and does it matter that a deplatforming done for the sake of peace might not be the kind of "banning for violations" the rule talks about? Does the rule have anything to say about that, or is it just a gap? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning—no preamble and don't restate the question.
