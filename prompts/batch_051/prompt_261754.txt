I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have been trying to work out what I think about a family of questions concerning presence, detection, and location. The spur is a sentence in Thomas Aquinas's Summa Theologiae, Third Part, Question 75, Article 1:

The presence of Christ's true body and blood in this sacrament cannot be detected by sense, nor understanding, but by faith alone, which rests upon Divine authority.

I want a precise account of what it takes for something to be really present somewhere, or in something, that can handle the following cases. Here are my verdicts:

A person locked in a windowless room is really present in the room, even though I cannot see them from outside. I would say an infrared camera can detect them, but the camera does not make them present.

A sample of uranium sealed in a lead container is present in the container, even though none of my unaided senses can detect it. But if I say the radiation is in the container, I am not sure whether I mean that radiation is a thing present there or only that the uranium is present and is acting on the container's interior.

A drone is present in the field above which it hovers. If the drone streams live video to my phone, I would say the pilot is present to me, but I would not say the pilot is bodily present in my office.

When a bottle of ammonia is opened, I think the ammonia molecules are present throughout the room. But if someone says the smell is present in the room, I hesitate: is the smell itself present, or is it only the way the molecules affect my nose?

If someone says a government is present in a remote village because its statutes apply there, I do not think that is real presence. That is a way of talking about jurisdiction or authority, not about anything being located in the village.

Now consider the case that actually interests me because of Aquinas. Suppose someone says that after the consecration of the Eucharist, the substance of the bread no longer remains, and the substance of Christ's body is really present under the appearances of bread, even though no sensory or instrumental test could detect it, and even though it is not present with the local dimensions of a body. I find myself torn. My first four verdicts suggest that detection is not necessary for presence, and my fifth verdict suggests that I already distinguish real presence from merely metaphorical presence. But I also have a stubborn intuition that a physical body cannot be present somewhere without any spatial relation whatsoever, and that the same body cannot be wholly present in many places at once.

Is there one principle about presence and location that delivers my first five verdicts and gives a clear verdict on the sixth? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn? And what would an account of presence have to be like if the sixth case is to count as real presence rather than a metaphorical or merely juridical one?
