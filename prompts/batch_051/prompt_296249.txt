Take a slightly tweaked version of Mary's room. Mary grows up in a black-and-white room and learns every physical fact about human color vision. Here's the tweak: her reading materials also include a complete and correct solution to the meta-problem of consciousness. By the meta-problem I dont mean the hard problem itself; I mean the problem of explaining why creatures with our kind of brain are so strongly disposed to judge that there is a hard problem at all — why the explanatory gap feels so deep, why zombie scenarios strike people as intelligible, why it seems obvious that seeing red involves something over and above the physical facts. Crucially, suppose this solution is stated in purely physical and functional terms, and it is neutral about whether those judgments are true.

So Mary knows, in detail, exactly which neural and cognitive processes produce the intuition that first-time color experience has a leftover, and exactly which processes will produce her own reaction when she walks out and sees a red tomato. She walks out, sees red, and has the reaction precisely as predicted: it still feels like something new and left out of her previous knowledge.

Does the presence of a solution to the meta-problem change what the knowledge argument establishes?
