I've been trying to reverse-engineer the theory of value implicit in how health systems allocate resources, and I keep running into the same puzzle. The institutions I'm looking at normally compare everything through a single measure of health gain: quality-adjusted life years, which compress survival time and symptom relief into one score, with interventions then ranked by cost per QALY. Yet the same institutions repeatedly act as though certain comparisons cannot be made. They approve drugs for rare or terminal conditions at a far higher cost per QALY than they would accept for common chronic conditions. They give special weight to end-of-life treatment. They fund acute care, preventive care, and mental health care out of separate budgets, as if the outcomes belonged to different currencies. And in public, the people running these systems deny that the rankings amount to a price on a year of life, even as they keep using those rankings as a decision rule elsewhere. The carve-outs cluster in the hardest cases.

I want to know what this whole practice presupposes about the nature of value, and whether it presupposes anything coherent at all. Does the everyday use of QALYs presuppose that different health benefits sit on a single scale of value, while the carve-outs presuppose that at least some benefits do not? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can. In particular: (1) separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it is merely compatible with; (2) say whether the carve-outs genuinely require the view that some health outcomes cannot be placed on a common scale, or whether they can instead be explained as rough proxies for fairness and distributional judgments inside a fully single-scale picture; and (3) tell me what the system would have to change if we pushed it toward uniform cost-effectiveness on the one hand, or toward the thoroughgoing view that some health outcomes genuinely stand on separate scales on the other. Where those two rival replacements would license the same behavior, the practice is philosophically underdetermined; where they diverge, that is where its real implicit commitments show up. Please answer in American English.
