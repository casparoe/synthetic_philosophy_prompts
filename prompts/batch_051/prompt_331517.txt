I am a high school student preparing an essay for a philosophy competition, and I have been working out a position on free will and moral responsibility. I have become convinced that the important question is not whether we have free will in some abstract sense, but which variety of free will is actually worth wanting and whether that variety is compatible with determinism. Before I defend my view, I want to check whether the set of commitments I have arrived at hangs together, because I cannot tell where the strain is.

Here is what I find myself accepting:

1. Causal determinism, applied to human choice, is very likely true: every decision is the product of earlier causes and natural laws.

2. If determinism is true, then no one is ever able to do otherwise than they actually do, where being able to do otherwise means being able to act differently while holding fixed the actual past and the actual laws of nature.

3. The variety of free will worth wanting does not require the ability to do otherwise in that sense. It requires being capable of reflecting on one's motives, deliberating about reasons, and acting on the motive one reflectively endorses, in the absence of coercion, duress, and manipulation.

4. Praise, blame, guilt, resentment, and gratitude are appropriate when—and because—an action expresses the agent's own evaluative stance, what the agent cares about or stands for. Their appropriateness does not depend on whether the agent possessed alternative possibilities.

5. An evaluative stance produced in a person by genes, upbringing, and luck can still be the person's own, in the sense relevant to responsibility, provided the person does not feel alienated from it and would endorse it upon reflection.

6. If a team of neuroscientists secretly implanted a desire in someone in a way that bypassed the normal process of forming desires, then the person is not responsible for the resulting action—even if, at the moment of acting, the person reflects on the implanted desire, does not feel alienated from it, and endorses it.

I am not sure which parts of this set are in tension. Please audit it. For each apparent tension, say which commitments are involved and whether it is real or whether it dissolves once key terms are made precise. For any real tensions, rank them by severity and say what would have to be given up or revised, and what that revision would cost me. I want the specific pairs of commitments and the specific fixes, not a general claim that the set is complicated.
