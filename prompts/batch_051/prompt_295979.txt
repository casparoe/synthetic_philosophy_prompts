In my Christology seminar we have been reading Athanasius, and I keep getting stuck on a sentence near the opening of On the Incarnation of the Word. He writes: "being incorporeal by nature, and Word from the beginning, He has yet of the loving-kindness and goodness of His own Father been manifested to us in a human body for our salvation." I find myself reading this as saying that what is really present in Christ is the incorporeal Word, and that the body is only the medium in which he appears to us. But if that is what Athanasius means, then it seems he is denying that Christ had a complete human nature, and the later Chalcedonian two-natures doctrine would be a correction of him rather than a development. I am not sure where my reading goes wrong, because my seminar leader insists Athanasius is fully orthodox here. Is the move from "body" to "human nature" the weak step, or is it something in how I am taking "incorporeal by nature"? What would be the right way to read this sentence so that it does not collapse into one nature?
