As a journalist who covers law and ideas, I keep seeing the slogan "a government of laws, not of men" used to close an argument rather than make one. Most recently, a legal scholar quoted it in an op-ed to reject the idea that administrative agencies should have broad discretion to fill in vague statutes. What are the distinct claims that slogan could be making, from the nearly trivial to the strongly substantive? For each reading, indicate whether it is defensible, and then say which reading or readings would genuinely do the work the op-ed needed it to do.
