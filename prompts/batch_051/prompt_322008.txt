I've been arguing with a friend about why everyone hits the same wall with mutable state. Not recursion, not higher-order functions — those eventually click. But the moment you have two names pointing at the same list and one function appends to it, people's mental model just detonates, even people who've programed for years. My friend says it's pure cognitive load: you have to track aliases, stack frames, and time all at once, and working memory gives out. I don't buy it. I think there's a more conceptual issue hiding there. The way we ordinarily decide that something is the same thing over time runs through what kind of thing it is, what it's for, or its history — not through "it's in the same slot." A ship stays the same ship because it keeps the same form and role even as its planks get replaced; a person stays the same person because of continuity, not because they never move. Mutation forces a different notion on us: this object is the same thing even though everything about its contents changed, purely because it sits in the same box. That's closer to how a legal system or a computer tracks identity than to how people naturally do.

So here's the claim I want a probability on: of the total difficulty that otherwise-competent people still have with mutable state, the largest single source is conceptual discord between the slot-based identity mutation requires and the kind/history/function-based identity we use for ordinary objects — not cognitive load from tracking aliases and updates. I don't want "it depends"; I want your actual credence and the decomposition. How much rides on whether ordinary identity judgments really work the way I just described, as opposed to being a mess with no single default? How much rides on whether slot-identity is genuinely unnatural rather than just unfamiliar? How much rides on whether load effects remain even when identity is made explicit with boxes-and-arrows diagrams? Then tell me which sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and what one piece of evidence — a language-design experiment, a cross-cultural study, a teaching intervention, anything — would move your credence the most, and in which direction.
