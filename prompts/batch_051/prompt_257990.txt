At work I watched a KPI get gamed into uselessness within a year, so I'm curious what happens when a whole country is run that way. Suppose a wealthy country of about five million people, currently around fourth in the world happiness rankings, writes into its constitution that starting in 2030 the government must resign if the country drops out of the top five for two consecutive years, and every proposed law has to include an official estimate of its effect on the national average of a 0-to-10 ladder question about overall life satisfaction. The survey is run by an independent outside pollster; the government can't suppress bad results or change the wording, but it can change essentially any policy—taxes, healthcare, education, immigration, urban planning—so long as it argues the change helps the score. Predict what the country looks like in 2050. I want the chain of reasoning: which parts of the score get optimized first, which get gamed, how survey answers drift from actual well-being, and whether the country ends up better off by a non-gamed standard. Tell me which predictions are robust and which are only guesses.
