I'm on the committee that runs a community makerspace, and we're in a fight about our woodshop policy. For years the policy has been that women can only use the big machines if a male member supervises. Some people want to keep it and keep saying it's just natural—men are naturally more comfortable with the saws and women are naturally more careful, so the policy just matches nature. I started reading Mill's The Subjection of Women, and he says:

"Again, in practical matters, the burthen of proof is supposed to be with those who are against liberty; who contend for any restriction or prohibition; either any limitation of the general freedom of human action, or any disqualification or disparity of privilege affecting one person or kind of persons, as compared with others."

I know Mill didn't have a makerspace in mind, but I can't stop thinking about this. Apply what he's saying to our policy debate. Who has the burden of proof here, and what would that mean for how we decide if we don't have solid evidence about natural differences? Also, is an informal club policy like this even a "restriction or prohibition," or does Mill mainly mean laws?
