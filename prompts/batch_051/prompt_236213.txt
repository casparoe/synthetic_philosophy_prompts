Suppose that a large common-law jurisdiction with a sizable digital economy enacts a statute, effective at the start of next year, changing the law of consumer standard-form contracts formed online. Under the statute, a term in such a contract is enforceable against the consumer only if the business can show, for that particular consumer and at the time of contracting, that the term was actually displayed to the consumer in the ordinary sign-up flow and that the consumer took a separate action indicating assent to that term, such as checking a box adjacent to the term itself. A general click on an "I agree" button is not enough to bind the consumer to any term that was not visible in the initial screenful, that sat behind a hyperlink, or that was bundled together with other terms. The same rule applies to renewals and to later amendments of existing contracts. Assume the statute survives judicial challenge and is enforced substantially as written.

Predict, with your reasoning visible, how this jurisdiction's online economy looks fifteen years later. I want a chain of reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints, not a story. Address at least the following: how firms redesign sign-up flows and contract documents; what happens to the length and readability of terms of service; which common clauses—arbitration clauses, data-collection and data-sharing provisions, auto-renewal, limitations of liability, unilateral amendment clauses—survive and in what form; whether firms raise prices, degrade free services, or adopt other workarounds to recover what the old system gave them; whether consumers become meaningfully better informed or merely face more clicks and longer flows; and how the effects differ between large platforms and small sellers. Distinguish the predictions you regard as robust from those that depend on assumptions about consumer attention, judicial interpretation, or the alternatives available to firms. State explicitly what would have to be true for your central predictions to be wrong, and end by saying whether the rule, as it actually operates, makes online assent more or less like genuine agreement.
