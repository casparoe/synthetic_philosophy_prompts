I serve on the committee charged with revising the doctrinal standards of a church whose synod, in 1671, adopted this rule: 'None can be saved who has not heard the Gospel preached to him by a living voice in his own tongue, and answered it with a sincere faith.' The authors of the rule could conceive of the Gospel's reaching a person only through a human preacher physically present; they knew the printed page only as a record of such preaching. We must now apply the rule to a case they could not have foreseen. A woman in a country closed to missionaries has had no contact with any Christian for four years. She found a local-language chatbot built by our denomination—a text-generation system with no mind of its own, trained on our approved catechism and two years of sermons by our best living preacher. In spoken mode, it has read the Gospel to her, answered her questions about the resurrection and the atonement in her own language, and become the medium through which she has come to a settled trust in Christ. She has never heard a human being present the Gospel, and when the chatbot has made small factual errors in retelling, she has corrected them against the same catechism. She now asks to be baptized. The committee is divided. Some hold that a human messenger is intrinsic to the call, so that the machine's spoken words lack the kind of address faith is meant to answer; others hold that the 1671 drafters were after something functional—that a person be genuinely confronted by, and able to answer, the Gospel—and that this machine does exactly that. I want the rule read against her case in four ways: (1) according to its plain words, especially 'living voice' and 'preached'; (2) according to its purpose, as best we can reconstruct it; (3) by asking what the 1671 authors would have said if they had been shown the machine and a transcript of her exchanges; and (4) under the reasonable understanding of a present member who knows how the system works. Then (5) tell me which of these readings should govern and whether, on that reading, she satisfies the rule. Please answer as exactly five numbered points corresponding to (1) through (5), each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
