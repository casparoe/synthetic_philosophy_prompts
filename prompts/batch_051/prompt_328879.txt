I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. For the past several months I have been reading Kierkegaard in translation with a few colleagues, concentrating on his social criticism of the public, the daily press, and what he calls leveling, with occasional detours into the pseudonymous works. I have tried to write down, as plainly as I can, the position I have ended up in. What I want is not an introduction to Kierkegaard but an audit of whether my commitments hang together.

Here is the set of views I currently hold.

1. The public in Kierkegaard's sense is not a set of concrete persons but an abstraction: a third thing that emerges when many individuals relate to a view through a medium without any determinate person owning the view.

2. The daily press is the chief engine of this abstraction. Its daily, rapid, relatively anonymous circulation lets opinions circulate while no one in particular stands behind them.

3. Anonymity is central to the diagnosis. When no situated, answerable person can be identified as the speaker, talk detaches from responsibility, and that detachment is what makes the public possible.

4. Leveling is the logic by which the public operates: it flattens qualitative distinctions—between what is important and trivial, better and worse, committed and merely considered—so that commentary about a matter replaces decisive relation to it.

5. The only real counterweight is the individual's inward, passionate appropriation of what is said—becoming a single individual who exists in the thought—not better information, transparency, or institutional reform.

6. I believe this diagnosis maps onto present platform-driven discourse in an intensified form. Algorithmic recommendation separates speech from a situated and answerable author even when accounts bear real names, because the incentive structure rewards the detached, commentary-like stance toward everything that leveling describes.

7. Nevertheless, I also believe online anonymity should be protected in many cases—whistleblowers, victims of abuse, people speaking under repressive conditions—and I doubt that real-name policies or content moderation can fix the underlying problem.

Please go through these commitments and identify which ones pull against which. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, which commitment or distinction has to give for the cheapest repair, and what that repair costs. For any apparent tension that dissolves once terms are disambiguated, say so explicitly, and state the distinction that dissolves it. If the set is inconsistent, I would rather learn which specific commitment should be revised than be told everything is fine. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who have studied Kierkegaard from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
