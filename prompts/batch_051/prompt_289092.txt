I'm putting together the final session of a small philosophy-of-physics reading group, and I'd like to close by reading something aloud before we open the floor. The semester was mostly on the philosophy of time, and the last two meetings were on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. The rough shape of it: special relativity says there's no frame-independent fact about which spatially separated events are simultaneous — observers moving relative to each other cut spacetime up into now-slices at different angles. So an event that, by my slicing, is still in the future can be, by another observer's equally good slicing, already present (or even past), and vice versa. If every inertial frame is an equally legitimate perspective on what's real, and something counts as real when it's simultaneous with some current observer, then the claim that the future isn't real yet starts to look like an artifact of one particular slice. The intended conclusion is that past, present, and future are all equally real — a block — and the flowing now is more like a perspective effect.

Could you write a Petrarchan sonnet — 14 lines, octave then sestet, I'm not precious about the rhyme scheme — that does this argument structurally, rather than just name-dropping it? The octave should give the pre-theoretical picture: a felt, privileged present, the past gone, the future not yet fixed. The volta at line 9 should enact the turn into relativity: someone else's now, tilted differently through spacetime, catches a sliver of my future as already there. The sestet should then push the consequence in plain language — if their now is as real as mine, there's no objective flow, just perspective, and time is already there, all at once. The poem shouldn't mention Rietdijk, Putnam, special relativity, or the block universe by name; I want someone who knows the argument to recognize it in the reasoning, and someone who doesn't to still get a good poem out of it. And don't resolve the tension to neatly at the end — let the felt flow of time and the block picture both keep their pull, since that's honestly where the reading group keeps landing.
