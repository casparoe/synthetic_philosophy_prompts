I recently read a popular science article that summarizes the debate over the reality of the wavefunction something like this:

"Most physicists now accept that the wavefunction is not a physical object but a summary of our knowledge about a quantum system. The view that the wavefunction is only a state of information is called 'psi-epistemic'; the rival 'psi-ontic' view holds that the wavefunction is a real feature of the world. QBism is the most prominent psi-epistemic program: it says quantum states are subjective degrees of belief. For a long time it seemed impossible to distinguish the two positions experimentally, since they were thought to make exactly the same predictions. But the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem showed mathematically that the wavefunction must be real: no theory that treats quantum states as states of information can reproduce the predictions of quantum mechanics, even in principle. The psi-epistemic view is therefore ruled out, and the debate is settled."

I'd like an accuracy audit of this passage. Please go through it and say which parts are accurate, which are common simplifications, and which are wrong or overstated. In particular, I'd like to know whether 'psi-epistemic' is being used in the technical sense introduced by Harrigan and Spekkens, whether QBism is fairly classified, and whether the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem actually supports the conclusion the article draws from it.
