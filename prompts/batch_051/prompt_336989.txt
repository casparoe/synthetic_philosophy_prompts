I am working through the regress problem in epistemology, and I have an argument that seems to eliminate foundationalism in a few quick moves. Here is the background I am assuming. Foundationalism, for my purposes, is the view that justified belief has a stopping point: there are beliefs that are justified but not justified by any further belief; they are justified by something that is not itself a belief, such as a perceptual experience or a rational intuition. I am not asking you to adjudicate among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism wholesale; I only want to know whether the argument below successfully closes off the foundationalist option.

The argument:

(1) If foundationalism is true, then there is at least one belief that is justified without being justified by any further belief.

(2) But any belief must be justified in one of only two ways: either it is justified by other beliefs, or it is justified by nothing at all.

(3) A belief that is justified by nothing at all is not justified.

(4) So a belief justified without being justified by any further belief would be both justified and not justified, which is impossible.

(5) Therefore foundationalism is false.

If no step fails, say plainly that the argument is valid and that the conclusion should be accepted. Assess the argument on its own terms; I do not need a survey of the literature.
