For some time I have been puzzled by the claim that prosperity or abundance might be valuable beyond the consumption it makes possible — that there is something good about slack, fat, and unused capacity as such, even if no one ever draws on it. Suppose a polity were convinced enough of this to build it into institutions rather than merely assert it. A proposal I have been turning over, and would like you to analyze as a mechanism rather than defend or attack as an ideal, runs as follows.

The state creates an Office of Abundance. Each year the Office publishes an Abundance Index, which attempts to measure economy-wide unused capacity and optionality: reserve stocks of grain and fuel, spare generating capacity, vacant housing units held ready, duplicate supply chains, trained but unneeded workers, and the like. A parallel system issues abundance credits to enterprises whose activities measurably expand this index, including the deliberate construction of redundant capacity that is not expected ever to be used. These credits can be bought and sold, and they are accepted in settlement of certain tax obligations. In addition, the public budget must include a standing redundancy reserve of 15 percent that is, by statute, barred from producing anything that would show up as consumed output; it must be spent on options, buffers, and unused alternatives, or not spent at all.

I want to know how this institution would actually behave once people could predict it and game it. What would it incentivize among firms, among households, among the civil servants running the Office, and among the politicians who would control the index's definitions? How would firms manufacture slack that is counted as abundance without being abundance in the relevant sense? Would putting a price on slack simply turn abundance into one more positional good or a device for rent extraction? And what would the gap between the index and the underlying value, if one emerges, tell us about whether abundance really has the intrinsic worth the polity set out to register?
