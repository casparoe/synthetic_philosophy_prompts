A friend and I spent a long evening arguing about Hume on causation, and I left holding the position that unsettles me most, so I want help working out what I should let go of.

I am convinced by the regularity view. There is a strong case that it is the only account that asks for nothing occult: to call one event a cause of another is just to place the pair in a pattern of spatiotemporal contiguity, temporal priority, and constant conjunction. What we call necessary connection is never observed; when I look hard at the striking of a match and the appearance of the flame, I find the one event, then the other, and a felt expectation in me, and the reductionist in me says that is all I am entitled to. The view has system and economy, and it gives one crisp criterion for what makes a sequence causal.

And yet my intuition will not fall in line. There seems to me to be a real difference between the striking's making the flame appear and, say, a grandfather clock that happens to chime a second before the match catches every evening. In the first case the striking does something — it produces the flame — while in the second the regularity is merely accidental or, if deliberate, a contrived piece of staging. The regularity view can try to recover the difference by saying that in the first case the pattern is stable, or supports intervention and manipulation, or belongs to a wider lawlike web. But those moves feel as if they either strip the word production of its meaning or implicitly borrow the very modality they were meant to replace. I also keep stumbling on Hume's counterfactual characterization of a cause, the thought that the dependence of the effect consists in this: if the first event had not occurred, the second would not have occurred. That seems to quantify over unactualized possibilities, which is already more than any observed run of constant conjunction can license.

So I have a theory I admire and an intuition I cannot evict. I do not want a referee's decision; I want a negotiated verdict. What exactly would have to be true for my production intuition to be debunked as a projection of habit, and what exactly would have to be added to or given up in the regularity view for it to earn the right to speak of real necessity? Is there a stable reading — the one scholars call the New Hume — on which Hume never denies that there are necessary connections in nature, but denies only that we can know or form an idea of them, so that my intuition and the regularity view come out compatible, though at the price of the view's empiricist economy? Please answer in American English.
