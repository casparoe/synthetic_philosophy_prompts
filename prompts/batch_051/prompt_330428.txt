I'm a software engineer and I've been trying to get Al-Ghazali's occasionalism straight, specifically the part in The Incoherence of the Philosophers where he goes on about the cotton and fire. As I understand it, he denies that fire has any causal power of its own: burning isn't something fire's nature demands, it's just God's habit, so the only real cause is God. I keep wanting to translate this into terms I'm used to, like: imagine the world as a simulation with a runtime scheduler that actually advances every event, and the regularities we call "laws of nature" are just preferences the scheduler is hardcoded with. Now here's the variation I want to think through. Suppose the scheduler isn't a single agent with a single habit. Instead there are two maintainers, and they have genuinely seperate habits: one always runs the cotton-touches-fire-then-burns sequence, the other sometimes skips the burning and keeps the cotton intact. Al-Ghazali's opponents, the philosophers, would presumably say the fire still has a disposition to burn, and the second maintainer is just interfering with it. The occasionalist says there are no dispositions, only the agent's will. Does the simulation framing change anything? More specifically: with one scheduler, I can at least say "God's habit" is the standing rule. But with two maintainers and conflicting habits, do I lose the notion of a true regularity, and does that force me back to positing some causal power in the fire to explain why one sequence is the correct one and the other is a deviation? Or does the occasionalist have a move here that a distributed-systems person would actually respect?
