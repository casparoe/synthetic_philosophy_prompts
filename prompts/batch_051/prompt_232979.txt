Define "standing to blame" in a way that correctly classifies the following cases. Throughout, assume that the criticism in question is accurate, proportionate to the wrong, and sincerely delivered; the only possible defect is that this particular blamer is not in a position to make it. I regard Case 1 as a paradigm of standing and Case 2 as a paradigm of lost standing. The intermediate cases are ones on which I have no settled view, and I do not expect the definition to return easy answers for all of them.

Case 1. A woman who was pickpocketed on a bus blames the pickpocket for stealing from her.

Case 2. A legislator who accepted undeclared campaign contributions condemns an opponent for accepting undeclared campaign contributions, while actively hiding his own identical conduct.

Case 3. A man who committed insurance fraud in his youth, later confessed, repaid the money, and has since spent years speaking publicly against fraud, now criticizes a stranger who has just committed insurance fraud.

Case 4. A manager who suspected that her subordinates were paying bribes to win contracts, said nothing, and accepted bonuses tied to those contracts, now blames one of those subordinates for bribery.

Case 5. A stranger on a train tells a man who is verbally abusing his partner that the abuse is wrong.

Case 6. A close friend of a rape victim tells the perpetrator, with the victim's consent, that what he did was contemptible.

Case 7. A man who was once mugged while traveling denounces a pickpocket who preys on tourists in a different city.

Case 8. A doctor who was convicted of perjury in a matter unrelated to her practice criticizes another doctor for fraudulent insurance billing.

Case 9. A member of a minority group who has never engaged in a certain form of conduct criticizes a fellow member of that group for engaging in it, on the ground that it reinforces a derogatory stereotype about them both.

Case 10. The adult child of a woman who was defrauded by a telemarketer blames the telemarketer, though he himself was not defrauded.

Propose the definition, run it through all ten cases, and say explicitly where it returns a verdict you find least secure or where it surprises you. Then either revise the definition to bring it into line with your considered judgment, or defend the surprising verdict.
