Okay, quick brief: I'm a science-and-ideas journalist, and I want to write a parody piece for the magazine in the deadpan register of the Onion, about the endless "is philosophy useful to science" slapfight. You know the one — Weinberg, Hawking, Feynman, and basically every physicist with a book to sell has taken a swing at the philosophers, and then used a bunch of unmistakably philosophical moves while swinging. I want the piece to nail that without ever explaining the joke.

Write an Onion-style news article, headline plus around 500 words, reporting that a prominent theoretical physicist has published what he calls a definitive experimental refutation of philosophy. The target is the practice itself — scientists dismissing philosophy while doing philosophy in the act of dismissing it — not any named real person. The humor has to come from getting the actual positions right, not from strawmen. So: his "methodology" should quietly assume the verification principle, that only empirically testable statements are meaningful, and then try to test the verification principle empirically; he should treat falsifiability as if it were a law of nature discovered in the lab rather than a methodological proposal he happens to like; and when a colleague — maybe a philosopher, maybe just a grad student whos read a little Popper — points out that his criterion for meaningfulness is not itself empirically testable, he should declare the objection too confused to even be wrong, and then cite this as further evidence that philosophy makes no progress. The reader shouldn't need to know Carnap, Ayer, or Popper to follow it, but someone who does should feel the piece is fair to them and has actually understood what it's satirizing. Keep the physicist a warm, self-satisfied blowhard, not a villain, and keep the whole thing deadpan — no narrator winking. don't include any bibliography or citations.
