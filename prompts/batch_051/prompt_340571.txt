I work as a backend engineer, and over the past year my team has moved nearly all of its specification work into behaviour-driven development. A ticket is no longer a prose paragraph plus a few bullet points; it is a set of given/when/then scenarios, and we have adopted the rule that a feature is not done until its observable behaviour matches every scenario, while anything not exercised by a scenario is deliberately left unspecified. Around the same time, I read a short introduction to Peirce's pragmatism, and I have been unable to shake the feeling that our workflow quietly instantiates the pragmatic maxim: roughly, the idea that the entire content of a conception consists in the conceivable practical consequences we take its object to have, and that any supposed difference that makes no difference to those consequences is empty. The structural resemblance seems strong to me. Scenarios stand to BDD as practical consequences stand to the pragmatic maxim; settling what a feature means by its behaviour under test looks like settling what a concept means by the effects of its object; and my team's standing rule that untested differences are not part of the spec echoes the maxim's refusal to count consequence-free distinctions as meaningful.

But I can also see reasons the two might only be superficially alike. Peirce's maxim belongs to a larger picture of inquiry as a public, self-correcting, fallible process that holds every belief open to revision while converging, in the long run, toward truth. A BDD suite belongs to a delivery process that aims at passing a finite set of tests to satisfy a product owner; it says nothing about truth, long-run convergence, or the community of inquirers. I want a committed verdict, not a menu of possibilities. Whether you judge the parallel deep or superficial, state the exact mapping it rests on; say what would have to be true of both sides for any insight to transfer from Peircean pragmatism to specification-driven development, or vice versa; and name at least one specific claim the parallel would lead a person to predict that turns out false.
