I am a philosophy PhD student working on identity and persistence over time, but before returning to academia I worked for years as a database engineer, so I still tend to think in terms of primary keys, mutable rows, history tables, transactions, and append-only logs. I cannot shake the suspicion that the endurantism/perdurantism debate is, at root, a dispute between two persistence strategies for the same underlying entities—closer to a choice of schema than to a substantive metaphysical difference—and I want to find out whether that suspicion survives a careful translation.

Please restate the debate in that engineering vocabulary. Render endurantism as something like update-in-place: a single entity identified by a primary key persists through time, and its changing properties are new values for the same row. Render perdurantism as something like an append-only log: the entity is identified with the whole sequence of immutable, time-stamped records, and what we ordinarily call the object at a time is one record, or temporal part, within that sequence. Pay particular attention to the problem of temporary intrinsics—how one and the same entity can be straight at one time and bent at another without contradiction. In the engineering idiom this becomes a question about consistency constraints on time-varying attributes, and I want to know how the two designs treat it differently: whether time must enter the key itself, or whether it belongs only among the values.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims of each view translate cleanly, which translate only because a controversial assumption has been smuggled in, and which cannot be expressed in the target vocabulary at all? I am especially interested in endurantism's claim that an object is wholly present at each moment it exists; in what, if anything, unifies the sequence of records into a single entity on the perdurantist picture; and in whether the distinction between valid time and transaction time (when a fact held in the world versus when it was written to the system) does any philosophical work. Finally, I want a verdict on my underlying suspicion: is "it is just update-in-place versus append-only" a neutral translation, or does the analogy load the dice towards perdurantism, since encoding time as a column already invites one to treat temporal location as spatial position? If the translation cannot be made without begging the question, or if the two positions are not answering the same question to begin with, tell me that plainly instead of forcing the analogy.
