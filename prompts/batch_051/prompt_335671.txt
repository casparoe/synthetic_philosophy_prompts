i've been tinkering with a view i call "concentration desert." It's karma read as a theory of moral desert, and it started from this passage in the Lonaphala Sutta (Anguttara Nikaya 3.99, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu):

"Suppose that a man were to drop a salt crystal into a small amount of water in a cup. What do you think? Would the water in the cup become salty because of the salt crystal, and unfit to drink?"
"Yes, lord. Why is that? There being only a small amount of water in the cup, it would become salty because of the salt crystal, and unfit to drink."
"Now suppose that a man were to drop a salt crystal into the River Ganges. What do you think? Would the water in the River Ganges become salty because of the salt crystal, and unfit to drink?"
"No, lord. Why is that? There being a great mass of water in the River Ganges, it would not become salty because of the salt crystal or unfit to drink."

My reading: the same trifling evil deed produces different amounts of desert depending on the moral "mass" of the person doing it—same salt, different water. So desert isn't a fact about the act alone, D(act); it's more like D = act / moral_mass. A petty theft by someone with almost no moral discipline is more deserving of punishment than the very same theft by a highly developed person.

That sounds right to me, but it has a weird consequence: the better you are, the less punishment you deserve for the same wrong, so moral achievement systematically discounts guilt. Thats either a deep insight or a bug. Give me your honest assessment: is concentration desert a real theory of moral desert, or just a metaphor for harm being absorbed differently?
