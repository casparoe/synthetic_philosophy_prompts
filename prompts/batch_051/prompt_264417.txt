I'm putting together a handout for an undergraduate epistemology class on the norms of assertion — the question of what standard you have to meet before you're entitled to flat-out assert something. I'd like a 'which norm of assertion do you actually hold?' quiz, eight to ten questions, covering the knowledge norm, the belief norm, the truth norm, and a reasonable-belief norm. Each question should present a concrete everyday case with answer options that pull these four norms apart; include at least one lottery case, one stopped-clock Gettier-ish case, and one case where someone says what their role requires even though they privately doubt it. The point is to sort students by their verdicts on cases, not by which label they already like, so avoid asking directly whether assertion requires knowledge, belief, or truth. Keep the cases non-technical since the students are new to the debate. Finally, be upfront about any positions the quiz can't cleanly seperate — I suspect the truth norm and the knowledge norm only come apart in cases where someone is right by accident, and I'd rather be honest about that than pretend otherwise.
