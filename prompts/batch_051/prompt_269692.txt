Last year I was selected by lot to serve on a citizens' assembly in my city, charged with recommending changes to the local housing plan. The organizers kept telling us that this was precisely what made the assembly legitimate: it could not be captured by campaign money or career politicians, and it looked like the city in miniature. Afterward, a statistically minded friend said the thing I could not answer: an assembly is not a sample, because a sample is for estimating some quantity, whereas this body was created to make a binding decision. I half-suspect she is right, but I lack the vocabulary to say exactly where the analogy fails.

The reason I cannot let it go is that I keep noticing a structural parallel. In a randomized controlled trial, random assignment does a very specific job: it breaks any dependence between treatment and the unmeasured factors that influence the outcome, so the treatment and control groups are alike in expectation, and an observed difference can be attributed to the treatment rather than to the way people were selected. In sortition, random selection seems to be asked to do the same job: break the dependence between political power and the unmeasured factors -- wealth, ambition, incumbency, the temperament that makes a person run for office -- so that the chamber is like the population it decides for. The shared principle appears to be: when you cannot measure and control the selection pressures, hand control to chance.

What I want to know is whether this is a real identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Please treat me as someone who knows the shape of both areas but lacks the technical apparatus to settle it myself. You are a professional philosopher with expertise in both democratic theory and the logic of causal inference. Think carefully, and begin by constructing the strongest version of the analogy you can. Then test it point by point: state the intended mapping between the two cases, say what would have to be true of a randomly selected assembly for the logic of randomized experiments to transfer to it, and identify exactly where the structures diverge. Give me a committed verdict rather than a menu of options, and name at least one substantive claim the analogy makes about sortition that you take to be false or overstated. If you think it is partly deep and partly superficial, draw the line precisely rather than hedging.
