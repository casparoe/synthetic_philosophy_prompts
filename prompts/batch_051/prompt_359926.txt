We treat the past and the future as different in kind: the past is settled and already real, the future is still open and not yet there. That's why it makes sense to fear a pain that's coming but not one that's over, and to deliberate about what to do but not about what you've done. But special relativity doesn't give us a single frame-independent now that carves the universe into a settled past and an open future. For a pair of events far enough apart that no signal could travel between them, one observer can be right that A happens before B while another, moving differently, is equally right that B happens before A. So whether B is already in the past or still in the future depends on your frame. If the line between already real and not yet real is frame-relative in this way, is there anything left to justify treating the past and the future so differently? Or is that asymmetry just a stubborn feature of our psychology that we've mistaken for a fact about the world? If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against the case of two such observers, both of whom count as correct.
