Six weeks ago I was fined for violating a food-labeling regulation in the country where I have run a small packaged-food business for three years. I did not know the rule existed, and I believe I could not reasonably have known: it had been published only in the official gazette, in a language I read at an intermediate level; the government's online guidance portal, which I checked regularly, was not updated until after the inspection; and the trade association I consulted told me no new labeling requirements applied to my products. The regulator does not dispute these facts. Its position is that the fine is fair because the rule was properly promulgated and people are held to know the law once it is published. I am not asking what the law actually is in my jurisdiction. I am asking for your considered judgment: is it fair to bind a person to a rule they could not, with reasonable diligence, have known about? Before you answer, give the strongest version of the regulator's argument, then tell me whether you think it succeeds and what a fair system would have owed me before imposing a penalty.
