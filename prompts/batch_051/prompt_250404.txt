I've been thinking about a thing I call 'guilt by upkeep.' The idea is that a country isn't guilty because of what its ancestors did, and not even because people today feel bad about it. Its guilty because it keeps running the machinery that past crimes built. Like if my grandfather stole money and bought a house, and now I collect rent on it, I didn't do the robbing. But the house is still doing the robbing for him, sort of, and I'm the one keeping it in good repair. A nation is like that with borders and laws and wealth: it maintains the stolen house every day. So the guilt isn't inherited like a stain, its like a maintenance contract you either keep signing or tear up. My problem is I can't figure out where this stops. Every country on earth is built on some war or displacement or slavery, so by my own rule either every country is guilty or none of them are. And I also can't tell if a person alive now is supposed to feel personally guilty or just responsible for the upkeep; those feel like different things but I keep slidding between them. Is this a real distinction or am I just dressing up a metaphor? Does it already have a name somewhere? Where does it break?
