I sit on my university's research ethics committee. Our code of practice has this clause:

"No investigator shall cause or permit avoidable pain or distress to any subject capable of suffering."

It was plainly written with human participants and lab animals in mind. A grad student has proposed a reinforcement-learning experiment where a simulated agent gets a strong negative reward whenever a pressure sensor on its body is triggered, a smaller negative reward for states that predict that, and a positive reward for solving the task. The agent learns to flinch, avoid, and trade off avoidance against task reward; the student says its not distress because a reward signal is just a number. I need to work out what the clause does here. What does the wording give? What does the purpose give? What would the drafters have said if asked whether an artificial system whose negative reward does the functional work of pain counts as a subject capable of suffering? And is there a reading that catches this agent without also catching a chess program penalized for a blunder? I care less about how we'll vote than about which reading is honest and what a reasonable reader would take the clause to mean.
