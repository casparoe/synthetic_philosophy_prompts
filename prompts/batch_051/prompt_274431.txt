The ψ-ontic/ψ-epistemic debate has no shortage of positions, but I rarely see a clean statement of the bar every position has to clear before it has earned a hearing. Write that specification: what must any adequate account of the quantum state's status deliver, reproduce, or explain away — for the purpose of determining whether the wavefunction represents reality or is merely a predictive instrument? For each requirement, give the phenomenon, theorem, or inferential practice that motivates it; say whether it is a fixed constraint or a negotiable demand that a theory may reject only at an explicit price; flag any requirement that is really a camp-specific prejudice rather than a genuine desideratum; and note which distinctions a view must respect or may argue away (for example, ontic versus epistemic, and collapse versus information update). Cover at least the following: the Born rule's empirical success; single-system interference and the stability of superpositions; entanglement and Bell-inequality violation; the no-signaling constraint; the measurement problem and state update; the fact that an entangled subsystem has no pure state of its own though the joint state is pure; and the no-go theorems showing that, once preparation independence is granted, two distinct quantum states cannot be understood as overlapping states of knowledge about one and the same underlying ontic state. Identify the pairs of requirements that look jointly unsatisfiable — separability with Bell violation, unitary evolution with definite outcomes, epistemic modesty with preparation-independence-respecting no-go results — and state which one or two requirements do the most to exclude otherwise live positions. Then, concisely, score pilot-wave mechanics, spontaneous collapse, Everettianism, and a ψ-epistemic account (or QBism, if you regard it as a distinct contender) against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scoring is a coda, not the center.
