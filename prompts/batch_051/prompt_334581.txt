Two friends of mine got into a long argument the other night about a text message one of them got from her advisor. She is finishing a thesis chapter and had emailed him saying she wasn't sure she could finish the last section this month because of some family stuff. He wrote back: You've got a lot going on right now. That was the whole message. She was convinced he was telling her, gently, that she probably won't finish and should slow down. Her boyfriend said she was reading into it; all he did was acknowledge the obvious. They went back and forth and I ended up playing referee, and I honestly couldn't decide.

(1) The boyfriend said the advisor didn't say she wouldn't finish. The sentence just describes her situation; any negative prediction comes from her own anxiety, not from him. (2) She replied that if she had asked about a deadline and he responded only by commenting on her situation, he must have meant it as a reason: either he thinks the commitments undermine the plan, or he thinks the plan is unrealistic. Otherwise why mention it? (3) The boyfriend said she can't infer that because the advisor could sincerely add, 'I didn't mean that as a comment on your timeline, just that I know you're busy,' and that addition wouldn't sound contradictory. (4) She shot back that maybe it wouldn't sound contradictory, but that's only because people can retreat from what they communicate by re-describing it as an observation. The communication already happened. (5) He said if that were true, there'd be no difference between what someone says and what someone hears, and we'd be responsible for every possible inference. (6) Then she brought up a parallel: if you ask me whether I'm coming to your party and I answer 'I'm not feeling well tonight,' everyone knows I'm declining, even though I didn't say no. The boyfriend said that's different because illness is a standard reason for not attending, whereas having a lot going on is a standard reason to adjust a deadline. He said 'standard reason' is doing the work, and in the advisor's message it's too weak. She said that's circular: how does a reason become standard except through contexts like this?

They asked me to adjudicate move by move, especially whether the boyfriend's point (3) about the advisor denying an implication actually settles anything, and whether the girlfriend's move from 'relevant response' to 'he communicated it' is sound. Please rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief reasons, say what, if anything, both parties are missing, and say which of the two is more right overall.
