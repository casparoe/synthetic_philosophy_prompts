At a barbecue a couple weeks ago I got sucked into an argument about meat and animal suffering that I still can't shake. One friend said she'd switched from beef to chicken because a single cow produces a huge amount of meat, so eating chicken means way more individual animals killed per meal. Someone else said that's exactly backwards: chicken lives are so short, cramped, and awful that the relevant unit is suffering per meal, not deaths per meal, and by that math beef might actually be better—and anyway beef is worse for the climate, which causes its own huge amount of animal suffering further down the line. An econ-major friend then said we were all doing average comparisons when we should be thinking at the margin: whether my buying one more pack of chicken actually leads to one more chicken being raised and killed depends on how responsive producers are, and none of us actually knew. Then a fourth person said the whole debate was myopic, because even a vegan diet kills mice, insects, and other animals during crop cultivation, and wild animals suffer horrible deaths all the time without any human involvement—so maybe the ethical gap between beef and lentils is mostly noise.

I wasnt sure who was right, but the conversation keeps coming back to me. Could you walk me through the most important implications of this mess? In particular: which of these factors should actually dominate when comparing beef and chicken, and how should the crop-death and wild-animal-suffering points change how I think about going vegetarian or vegan? I'm not looking for a definitive diet plan, more for a sense of which arguments deserve real weight and which are red herrings.
