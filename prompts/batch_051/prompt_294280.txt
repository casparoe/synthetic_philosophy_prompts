In set theory, the axiom of choice states that for any family of nonempty sets there exists a function selecting one element from each set, even when no rule, property, or explicit description singles out the selected elements. This strikes me as structurally similar to the puzzle of Buridan's ass: an agent facing two options that are perfectly symmetric in every respect is supposed to be able to settle on one of them, even though no reason or criterion favors it over the other. Standard mathematics routinely treats the first kind of selection without a differentiating criterion as unproblematic, while the theory of action treats the second as a deep problem about freedom and rationality. I want to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure—one problem about choice under the absence of a selecting criterion, arising in two different domains—or only a loose analogy. State the intended mapping precisely, say what would have to be true on each side for results to transfer, and identify at least one consequence the parallel predicts that turns out false.
