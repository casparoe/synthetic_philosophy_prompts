Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a proponent of the argument from divine hiddenness — roughly, J. L. Schellenberg's argument that a perfectly loving God would not permit nonresistant nonbelief, but that such nonbelief occurs, so no such God exists — and a critic who grants that nonresistant nonbelief occurs but denies that a perfectly loving God would be compelled to prevent it, maintaining instead that God could have good reasons to permit it (for example, to preserve a space for genuine moral or spiritual development, or because a skeptical-theist stance should make us doubt our ability to rule out such reasons).

The statement should be constructive rather than adversarial, and should include:

1. A numbered list of claims both parties can sign, covering: a precise definition of nonresistant nonbelief; a reconstruction of the argument's logical form; and the concessions each side makes (for instance, that only nonresistant nonbelief counts against the target theism, that resistant nonbelief is irrelevant to the argument, and that the dispute turns on what a perfectly loving God would do rather than on any empirical disagreement about how many people disbelieve).

2. A disagreements table stating the residual disagreements, each in wording the relevant party would accept. At least one disagreement should concern whether human beings can reliably specify the content of perfect love well enough to support the argument's central premise; another should concern how far the apparent absence of good reasons for hiddenness licenses an inference against a perfectly loving God's existence.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one party. For each crux, give a concrete case, analogy, or consideration both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that case will show once fully described.

End the statement by identifying which parts of their dispute are substantive and which, if any, are merely verbal.
