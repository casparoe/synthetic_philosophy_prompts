I'm chair of the bylaws committee for a small academic society, and I need to circulate a written opinoin before Thursday's meeting. Our current constitution says the bylaws can be amended by a two-thirds vote of the full membership, and there's no higher body that can overrule us—we're the last word on our own rules. A few members have moved to amend the amendment clause itself so future changes need only a simple majority. Half the committee says this is obviously in order: the amendment clause governs all amendments, including itself, so if it gets two-thirds, it's valid, end of story. The other half says that's circular, because the new majority rule would then govern the very act that adopted it, and a one-time two-thirds majority could permanently lower the bar for every future change. Please draft the opinion: recommend whether we should rule the amendment in order, out of order, or something in between, give reasons I can defend, honestly engage the strongest argument against your recommendation, and address whether it matters that this amendment lowers rather than raises the future threshold.
