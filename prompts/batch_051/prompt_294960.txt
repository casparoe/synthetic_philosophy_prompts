I'm writing a paper on the political side of logical empiricism, and I keep getting stuck on a tension in Carnap's thought. As I understand him, Carnap held that value judgments are not truth-apt: they do not describe states of affairs, but express attitudes, proposals, or commitments. Yet he is also presented as a member of the left wing of the Vienna Circle, someone for whom the scientific outlook was bound up with social reform and rational economic planning. If the ultimate choice between a planned economy and a market economy is not a factual question, then a sentence like 'society ought to adopt rational planning' cannot be established or refuted by evidence; it can only be greeted with approval or disapproval. On that reading, Carnap's political advocacy looks like a non-rational expression of temperament, no better supported than its opposite. But that seems like a crude distortion of what he and his allies thought they were doing.

So I am trying to figure out what has gone wrong in my reconstruction. Did Carnap in fact treat some value claims as truth-apt at the end of the day, or is there room within a non-cognitivist account of values for genuine rational argument about planning? If the latter, what exactly is left to be rational once the fundamental value framework has been chosen — can the choice of framework itself be argued about, or is it merely a practical commitment? And how would a Carnapian distinguish a well-grounded choice of framework from an arbitrary one without quietly smuggling normative facts back in?
