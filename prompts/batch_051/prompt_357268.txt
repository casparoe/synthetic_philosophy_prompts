Here's a thing I keep coming back to. The standard defense of sweatshops — the one that says the job is voluntarily chosen and better than the worker's next-best alternative, so the transaction is mutually beneficial and not something outsiders should ban or boycott — treats that next-best alternative as a fixed fact about the world. But suppose the same development that creates the sweatshop jobs also degrades the alternatives: an export-processing zone gets built, smallholder plots are converted or cut off from water, informal trade gets squeezed by the new logistics. Now the job and the baseline aren't independent; the thing its compared against is partly an effect of the thing being defended. Does the argument survive that? Is the judgment that the exchange is mutually beneficial even well-defined once the baseline is endogenous like that? Or does the argument need a different counterfactual — and if so, which one, and does the repaired version still do the work the original was supposed to do? I'm interested in what the argument needs to be true, not in what the policy should be.
