ok so i was reading bradley's *appearance and reality* last night, the chapter called "relation and quality," and he has this passage where he says, roughly, that a relation can't be a property of the terms because then it isn't a relation, and it can't be a third thing standing *alongside* the terms either, because then the original two terms aren't actually related to anything—they're related to the relation, and you need *further* relations to tie those together, and so on. he concludes the whole notion of a relation linking distinct terms is "unintelligible," or maybe that it "leads to an endless process"—i don't have the page in front of me, i'm paraphrasing.

here's my problem. i take bradley's infinite regress seriously—i can't see a principled place to stop it, because wherever you stop you've just posited another unexplained linkage. but i also can't get myself to deny that when i put one pencil *next to* another pencil, there is a relation of being-next-to holding between them. i don't just mean the sentence is true or useful; i mean the two pencils, the actual things, really are related in a way that isn't just me projecting a form of description onto them. this intuition is about as firm as any i have, maybe firmer than the intuition that there are composite objects at all.

so which should yield? should i debunk the intuition by saying something about how we operate with relational concepts in practice and that's why it feels unshakeable? or should i say bradley's argument, or at least his standard formulations today, has a hidden assumption about what it means for a relation to "connect" things? or is there a reading of the passage on which he's only attacking a certain model of relations—like relations as independently real entities added to the terms—and leaving alone the view that the relation just *is* the terms being in a certain state, with no need for a third item?

what i'd like from you is the strongest version of each move and then a negotiated verdict: what exactly would justify keeping the intuition, and what would justify giving it up? and if the right move is to say bradley is attacking something weaker than i'm defending, i'd want to know how a bradleyan could honestly respond, not just have the regress waved away.
