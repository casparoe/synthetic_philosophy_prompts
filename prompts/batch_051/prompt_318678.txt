In Chapter V of Leviathan, Hobbes offers an explanation of why geometry has achieved the status of a science while moral and political philosophy have not: geometers begin by settling the significations of their words, whereas in other inquiries men begin from words whose meanings are uncertain and disputed. I want to understand the conceptual structure of this diagnosis. Please explain what Hobbes means when he treats definitions as the source of geometry’s certainty, and then address the following difficulty: if all the force of geometry comes from definitions, why is geometry not merely a verbal exercise? In other words, on Hobbes’s account, what distinguishes the necessity of geometry from the contingency of empirical claims, and can that distinction be maintained without covertly appealing to something non-linguistic?
