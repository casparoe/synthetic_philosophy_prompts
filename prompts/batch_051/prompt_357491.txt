Suppose that in 2032, a research consortium announces the discovery of a measurable computational signature that is necessary and sufficient for phenomenal consciousness. The signature can be assessed cheaply and non-invasively in any information-processing system. The protocol is standardized, and after two years of adversarial replication it becomes the broad scientific consensus. Before 2032, there was no accepted way to settle whether a system was conscious; after 2034, researchers treat the question as empirically decidable in nearly all practical cases. Nothing else about the world changes at the moment of discovery.

Trace the consequences over the following two decades. I want a mechanistic prediction, not a story. Be explicit about the incentives, enforcement mechanisms, economic pressures, status effects, and lines of legal doctrine through which change would actually travel.

In particular:

(1) Which sectors change first, and by what route? Consider at least technology companies running large models, intensive animal agriculture, medicine for disorders of consciousness, and criminal and family law insofar as they classify entities whose legal standing depends on whether they are conscious.

(2) Where does the test produce awkward verdicts — systems that score conscious but that most people had assumed were not, or systems that score non-conscious but that people are attached to treating as minded — and how do institutions and ordinary moral attitudes absorb, resist, or rationalize those verdicts?

(3) What new conflicts, industries, or abuses become predictable once conscious status can be certified at scale, for example around marketing claims, welfare labeling, litigation over standing, moral offsetting, or capture of the measurement protocol itself?

(4) For each major claim, indicate whether it is robust under reasonable variation in the details, a plausible guess, or dependent on a specific assumption; state which assumption would have to fail for the prediction to be wrong, and identify the single unknown you would most want resolved.

The underlying question is how the boundaries of moral patienthood get redrawn when sentience attribution shifts from open philosophical argument to a cheap, decisive measurement.
