I keep going back and forth on whether clicking "I agree" is agreement in any sense that should make someone feel bound beyond "a court will enforce it." My working critera for genuine agreement are these: (1) the person had a real chance to learn that terms existed and to inspect the important ones; (2) accepting was a distinct, deliberate act, not something bundled into the very action they were trying to take; (3) they had a walk-away option that didn't cost them something they were already entitled to or leave them much worse off; (4) the terms didn't include anything so one-sided or surprising that a reasonable person would refuse if they knew; (5) they weren't under time pressure or in a bind the other side knew about and leaned on.

The case: a startup runs a free web app for managing a chronic illness—med schedules, symptom tracking, an emergency card. After three years they introduce a paid tier and new terms. Users get an email and an in-app banner a month ahead: the terms will now include mandatory arbitration, a no-class-action clause, and permission to share de-identified symptom data with pharma partners; the changes are summarized in plain English, with a bold note that the arbitration clause limits the user's right to sue. On the day the new terms take effect, a long-time user logs in. The button says "I agree to the new Terms" and sits below the summary, separate from the "Open my dashboard" button. She opens the summary, skims it, doesn't think the data sharing matters much because it's de-identified, and clicks "I agree." She isn't in an emergency, though she is in a mild flare and wants her med schedule. She later learns the only way to export her three years of data would have been to log in—which required clicking "I agree" first—so she couldn't really take her data and leave without agreeing once. She now wants to sue over an unrelated billing overcharge and is told she agreed to arbitration.

Is she genuinely in agreement on my five criteria? Go criterion by criterion, say which of them are doing real work and which are just restating the conclusion. Then tell me whether this case shows the criteria need changing, or whether "agreement" is just carrying too much moral weight in adhesion situations like this.
