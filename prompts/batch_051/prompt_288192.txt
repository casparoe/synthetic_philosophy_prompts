I've been trying to work out what the laws of nature really are, and I keep ending up at an argument I cannot get rid of, even though I do not much like where it lands. My argument runs like this. (1) Suppose the laws of nature are just compact records of what happens in the world — an unsupported stone falls, a stretched spring recoils — written up as general statements. Then every future event is already covered by the record, because the record's truth depends on the future cases too, not only the past and present ones. (2) But if a future event is already covered by the record, the record cannot be what brings that event about or steers it; a record is made true by the events, not the other way around. (3) So if the laws are records, they do not govern anything; they only describe. (4) The only alternative, as far as I can see, is that the laws are something over and above the events — extra rules that force events to obey. But an abstract rule cannot reach down and push a stone. (5) Therefore either the laws do not really govern, or we are stuck with abstract rules somehow pushing matter around; and since the second is unacceptable, I should conclude that the laws do not govern. I keep wanting to escape the conclusion, but I cannot locate the step that would let me out. Is the argument sound? If not, which step exactly is the weak one, and what is the strongest reading of that step — does the argument fail on that reading, or does it actually go through? I am not asking for a survey of possible positions; I want your own committed verdict, especially on whether step (4) really is the only alternative.

"The argument, restated" — put my steps in your own words and flag any wording you will need to pin down (one short paragraph). "Checking the steps" — go through (1) to (5) one by one and say whether each step is true as written or only on a certain reading (about two paragraphs). "Where it breaks" — name the exact step or the shift in meaning that carries the conclusion, and say whether it is the place I suspected (the main section, about three paragraphs). "My verdict" — commit to whether the argument is sound, and say what I should conclude about the laws of nature (one short paragraph).
