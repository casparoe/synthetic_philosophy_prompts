I keep noticing an asymmetry in how we talk about self-chosen, rule-bound, pointless-in-the-good-sense activities, and I can't tell wether it tracks anything real. Take two people. One is a middle-aged guy who spends three hours a night studying chess openings and playing long games online. Everyone calls him a serious amateur, maybe a little obsessed, but basically admirable. The other spends the same three hours grinding ranked matches in a competitive video game, reviewing replays, working on mechanics. People start using words like addiction and wasted time. Both activities are voluntary, rule-governed, competitive, skill-based, absorbing, and produce nothing beyond themselves. Both can wreck your sleep and your relationships if you let them. So what is the asymmetry supposed to be tracking? Age of the tradition? Physicality? Spectator value? The fact that chess got classified as a game of intellect a few centuries ago? And where's the line: is speedrunning a retro platformer on the chess side or the video-game side, is dressage on the respected side because it's old and physical, and what about building ships in bottles, which is neither competitive nor useful? I want to know whether the distinction can be defended on principle, or whether it's just status and familiarity doing the work.
