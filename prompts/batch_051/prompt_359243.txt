I keep comming back to a lexical threshold version of negative utilitarianism and I can't tell whether the thing that bothers me about it is a real problem or just me wanting the view to be tidier than it's allowed to be. The view, as I understand it: below some threshold of pain intensity, pleasure and pain trade off in the ordinary way, but at or above that threshold the pain gets lexical priority — no finite amount of pleasure, anywhere, for anyone, can justify producing one instance of it. I like this because it gives me a clean decision rule (eliminate the worst suffering first, full stop) and because it dodges the conclusion that a big enough pile of mild enjoyments could justify torturing someone.

But here's the rub. I have a very firm sense that the pain continuum has no natural joint between severe and extreme. It shades continuously from a stubbed toe to bone cancer to whatever is worse. So any cutoff I pick is going to be stipulative. But then two pains that are almost indistinguishable — one a hair below the line, one a hair above — end up licensing totally different trade-offs: the first can be outweighed by a sufficiently large pile of pleasures, the second can never be outweighed by anything. That discontinuity feels like exactly the kind of arbitrariness I adopted the view to escape.

Is the arbitrariness intuition tracking something the view has to answer, or is it just demanding a sharpness the world doesn't owe me? Should I give up the lexical threshold, amend it (maybe the threshold is really a very steep non-linear weighting instead of a lexical cutoff), or keep it and debunk the intuition? Give me the strongest version of each move and say what would justify taking it.
