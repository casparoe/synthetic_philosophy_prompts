I am a novelist working on a book set in late colonial India, built around two characters: a British district officer who keeps a literal ledger of grain, revenue, and public works, and an Indian clerk who falsifies it. In my background reading, I keep encountering the framing that organizes much of the debate over the Raj: the balance sheet. Writers on what I would call the imperial side, with Niall Ferguson as the most fluent, open the books and enter credits—railways, the telegraph, a unified legal code, an administrative class, the suppression of practices they regard as backward—against debits—extractive taxation, deindustrialization, famine, racial subordination—and report a net positive, or at least a defensible, entry. Writers on the other side, with Shashi Tharoor as the most prominent, object that the whole exercise is a category mistake, because a life destroyed and a mile of track laid cannot be entered in the same currency; yet even they tend to reply with their own counter-ledger of plunder and suffering. So the balance sheet survives on both sides, and I suspect it is doing more argumentative work than either side admits.

I would like you to audit that metaphor as a piece of machinery. Give me the full correspondence table: in empire-as-enterprise, what corresponds to the shareholders, the capital raised, the revenue, the operating costs, the assets and liabilities, depreciation, goodwill, externalities, and the choice of baseline against which profit is measured? Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusions being drawn from the ones that are merely ornamental. In particular, say what the business metaphor quietly imports into an empire—a single unit of account for unlike goods, a determinate counterfactual, a standpoint from which the books are kept, a set of property rights, and the idea that one party's losses can be offset by another's gains—and test whether the Ferguson-style net-positive verdict and the Tharoor-style counter-ledger survive once those imports are made explicit. Finally, tell me whether some other image would carry the truth better, or whether my novel should leave the ledger open.
