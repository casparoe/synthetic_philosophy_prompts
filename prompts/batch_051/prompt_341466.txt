Compare the continuum hypothesis with the parallel postulate. Say that a statement is independent of an axiom system when neither the statement nor its negation can be proved from that system. The continuum hypothesis is independent of Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with choice; the parallel postulate is independent of Euclid's other postulates. Yet the two cases are treated quite differently: the parallel postulate is now widely regarded as an optional axiom that selects one geometry from a family of possible geometries, whereas the continuum hypothesis is widely treated as an unresolved question about a single set-theoretic universe, and some even doubt that it has a determinate truth value.

Is this contrast in treatment epistemically justified, or is it largely a product of historical accident? Give two respects in which the two cases are analogous and two respects in which they diverge. Then identify which of those respects carries the most weight in deciding whether the continuum hypothesis should be regarded as a genuine open question rather than as a convention for selecting a set-theoretic universe.
