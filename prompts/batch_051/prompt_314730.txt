Last week someone in my reading group was defending what he called 'free-floating rationales': the idea that reasons can exist without any reasoner — that a state of affairs can have a rationale even if there's no God, no person, no mind at all whose rationale it is, just a consideration counting in favor of how things went. I was still chewing on that when my aunt told me, after her fourth failed IVF cycle, that 'everything happens for a reason.' And I realised I have no idea what that sentence is actually claiming, or whether it's secretly the folk version of the reading-group view. So seperate out for me the distinct precise claims 'everything happens for a reason' could be making — brute causal determinism, narrative or divine teleology, cosmic ordering, the claim that a justification exists even if no one holds it, the claim that believing it is good for you regardless of truth, and any other readings you can find — and sort them from the boringly true to the wierdly metaphysically loaded. For each one, tell me: is it a platitude, is it actually defensible, is it flatly false? And which of those readings would my aunt actually need for her sentence to do the consoling work she was using it for — and is that reading among the true ones?
