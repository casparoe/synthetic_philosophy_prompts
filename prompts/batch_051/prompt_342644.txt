I've been trying to sort out the Korean Four–Seven debate, and the compressed version I keep coming back to makes me suspicious of the whole distinction, but I can't find the load-bearing step. Here's the setup. Mencius famously says all humans have four "beginnings": commiseration (like the alarm and compassion at seeing a child about to fall into a well), shame and dislike, deference and yielding, and a sense of right and wrong. Confucian texts also have a canonical list of seven emotions: joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hatred, and desire. Toegye (Yi Hwang) held that the Four Beginnings are the issuance of principle (li), while the Seven Emotions are the issuance of material force (ki). Gobong (Ki Dae-seung) and later Yulgok pushed back: the Four Beginnings look like just a subset of the Seven, namely the subset that happens to be in line with principle.

I'd reconstruct the pushback as:

(1) If the Four Beginnings are real episodes in a human mind, they are felt occurrent emotions.
(2) The Seven Emotions are meant to be a complete inventory of felt emotions.
(3) So each of the Four Beginnings falls under one of the Seven; compassion looks like a kind of love or grief, shame like anger with oneself as the object, and so on.
(4) If the Four Beginnings are a subset of the Seven, and all the Seven issue from ki, then the Four Beginnings cannot issue from a different source; "issuance from principle" can at most mark off the morally good subset of what is, underneath, entirely ki.
(5) Therefore Toegye's principle/ki contrast between the two lists is either false or just shorthand for "the in-accord subset of the Seven."

I half-belive this, but step (4) in particular feels like its smuggling something. Is the argument valid? If not, exactly where does it fail, and on what reading? If it is valid, does the two-source talk really reduce to shorthand?
