Resource egalitarians hold that the metric of distributive justice is the bundle of impersonal and personal resources people command, together with a requirement that they bear responsibility for their own preferences and choices. Capability theorists hold instead that the metric is what people can actually be and do. Explore the resulting stalemate over two phenomena, treated asymmetrically: resourcists argue that the capability approach cannot distinguish a deprivation that grounds a claim of justice from an expensive taste, since both show up as constraints on what a person can be and do; capability theorists argue that resourcism cannot handle differences in people's ability to convert equal resources into functionings, as with disability, without admitting a capability metric through the back door. Are these really the same kind of objection turned in opposite directions, or is one side's version more cogent? Assess whether either view can absorb the other's favored phenomenon through a principled auxiliary commitment rather than an ad hoc amendment, and then give your verdict as to which theory is more damaged by the exchange.
