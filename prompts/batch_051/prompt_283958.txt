so i'm on this little committee at my church that decides who gets one-time emergency help — rent, utilities, that kinda thing — and there's four of us who read the apps and vote. we keep a shared doc of past decisions because we're trying not to just go off vibes, but honestly the precedents kind of pull in different directions. here's the record:

- approved a month of back rent for Ms. Alvarez, a single mom with two kids whose hours got cut at the warehouse; she'd already picked up weekend shifts at a laundromat before she even applied.
- denied a man who wanted help with a car payment after he'd blown his savings on sports betting; we just didn't trust that the money would go where he said.
- approved a utility bill for Ms. Cuomo, who has a chronic illness and was about to get her power shut off while her disability appeal was pending.
- denied a woman who asked for grocery money, because we found out she had an off-the-books cleaning job she hadn't listed on the form.
- approved a security deposit for a kid aging out of foster care so he could take a warehouse job, even though he had no rental history and some of us thought he was risky.
- denied a single guy with no kids who was fired for being late all the time and asked for a security deposit; he had a construction offer but turned it down because it paid less than his old job.

now we've got Mr. Reyes. two kids. fired three months ago for pretty much the same chronic lateness. since then he's been working part-time at a gas station and applying for better jobs, but he's behind on rent and owes alot in late fees, and the landlord has filed for eviction. he's asking for a one-time payment so his kids don't have to move. he did turn down a full-time retail job last month because the shifts didn't work with childcare and it paid less than his old job, but he says he's not refusing to work since he took the gas station thing.

given our own track record, what's the more consistent call — approve or deny? which of our old cases is the closest match, and what principle would we be committing ourselves to either way? and if the precedents genuinely don't settle it, what extra fact about Mr. Reyes would settle it?
