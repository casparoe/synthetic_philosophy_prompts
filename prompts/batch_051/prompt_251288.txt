I'm making a "which theory of intelligence are you actually operating with?" quiz for a meetup where people keep talking past each other about whether LLMs are intelligent. I want eight or nine questions, each a short concrete case — a thermostat, an octopus escaping a tank, a spreadsheet, an LLM that fails arithmetic half the time, a dog that can't count but tracks a missing member of its pack, a person in a coma, a company that consistently outpredicts its competitors, maybe a slime mold — with two or three answer options. The scoring key should sort people by their pattern of answers across the cases, not by which label they pick for themselves. I want it built from the space of answer combinations first and the labels placed afterwards, so the leaves come out as something like functionalist, psychometric-g, multiple-intelligences, embodied/enactive, and a deflationary no-shared-essence view. Also note any combination of answers nobody seems to occupy and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag which positions the instrument can't really separate. Phrase the case questions so someone can answer without already knowing the labels, and make sure the quiz exposes people who say "it's intelligent if it behaves intelligently" but then refuse to call a spreadsheet smart.
