I got into a long, circular argument last night with a friend about the two-envelope thing, and now I've got to teach it in two weeks, and I'm worried I still couldn't make the actual flaw land. The setup: two identical envelopes, one has twice as much money in it as the other, you pick one, and before you look your offered the swap. His line was that if his envelope contains X, the other one is equally likely to be 2X or X/2, so swapping has expected value 1.25X, so he would always swap; but by symmetry that reasoning is available to both sides, which is mad. When I pointed that out he just said "the calculation's right" and carried on. Then we tried it with the tenner and the twenty from his wallet, where obviously swapping is just a coin flip, and he said that was a different game. I've got a hunch the real issue is what probability you're allowed to put over the possible amounts, but I don't know how to make that land for people who've never seen a prior in their lives, and anyway I might be wrong.

I'm leading a two-hour Saturday session on this for an adult-education philosophy circle. There'll be a retired actuary and a maths teacher who both think they already know the answer, plus a vicar and a couple of teenagers. Don't explain the paradox to me—plan how I should teach it. I need the order in which the pieces have to go so the resolution feels discovered rather than announced, and what depends on what. Give me the one concrete running example that should carry the whole session and say what it has to have in it and what it should avoid (I suspect using actual cash gets people hung up on utility and 'free money'). Then map the misconceptions I should expect—'the other envelope is 50/50 double or half no matter what', 'opening your envelope breaks the symmetry', 'its just a lesson about greed', maybe 'the infinities make it unsolvable'—and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off rather than cleaning it up after. Give me a couple of questions to ask at the end that would show whether people have actually got it rather than just nodding. And tell me what I should openly refuse to get into at this level.
