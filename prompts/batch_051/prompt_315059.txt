I am a retired natural scientist, and I have recently begun reading philosophy of religion. I have noticed that I bring to that reading a standing policy for evaluating reports of present-day miracles and spiritual gifts—healings, words of knowledge, predictive prophecy, glossolalia, exorcism. The policy is this: I assign such reports a low prior probability, and I require the kind of evidence I would have demanded before publishing a surprising result—pre-registration, controls, an independently confirmed diagnosis and outcome, an established baseline rate, and preferably replication. A few months ago a friend described a medically documented healing she regarded as miraculous; I asked whether the diagnosis and the recovery had been independently confirmed, what the spontaneous-remission rate was for her condition, and whether any natural causal explanation had been excluded. When she could not supply these things, I quietly set the report aside. In practice no contemporary miracle claim I have met clears this bar, so I accept none of them.

Lately I have begun to suspect that my rule is not the neutral scientific caution I took it to be. In the debate between cessationists and continuationists, my procedure seems to settle the question in advance: it assumes that if God were acting in such gifts, the events would have to behave like the natural regularities a controlled study is designed to detect, so that laboratory-grade evidence is the right kind of evidence. A continuationist could reply that a singular act of divine agency need not be repeatable or controllable, and that demanding a base rate for a miracle is close to assuming it is not a miracle. Is my policy rationally defensible, or is it a bias wearing methodological clothing? And what would a defensible version of the policy look like—one that keeps my protection against credulity without simply presupposing the cessationist conclusion, perhaps by distinguishing kinds of reported events, kinds of testimony, or between detecting a standing natural capacity and adjudicating a one-off historical claim?
