Okay so for my philosophy elective we get to pick any question and write a final essay, no outside sources, just argue it through. I'm doing seperating the art from the artist, because my friends and I basically have this exact fight every time some musician gets exposed for something awful and we have to decide if we can still play their songs at the function. Every take I see is either that the work is the work and I should stop making it about the creator, or that streaming them makes me complicit and that settles it. Neither handles the cases I actually care about, and I don't want to just add another hot take.

So before I read any more defenses of either side, write me the desiderata: what does any adequate theory of how an artist's moral failings should affect our judgment of their art actually have to get right? I want the full spec. Which cases it has to handle — an artist who is horrible in private but the work doesn't go near it; a work that is itself morally nasty but maybe that's exactly why it's gripping, like a novel written from inside a monster's head or a joke that punches down but is perfectly constructed; a dead artist versus a living one who directly profits from my streams; a work that explicitly preaches the very virtue the artist violated; and so on. Which distinctions it can't collapse: artist vs. work, moral value vs. aesthetic value vs. all-things-considered value, and judging the work vs. deciding what to consume. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and say which ones are non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of one camp. Then flag any pair of requirements that probably cant be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want to see, because I think that's where the actual disagreement is hiding. You can skip scoring the standard positions; I'd rather have a sharp list than a scorecard.
