I've been trying to pin down what the golden rule actually is, and I keep realizing I can't. Everyone knows the rough wording — treat other people the way you'd want to be treated — but that wiggles as soon as you test it. I don't need the history or what famous people said about it; I want a working definition of what counts as following the golden rule, not just the slogan. A definition that can actually decide cases.

Here are the cases I've been stuck on:

1. You find a dropped wallet and return it because you'd want someone to return yours.
2. You don't throw a surprise party for a friend who hates surprises, even though you'd love one yourself.
3. You tell a friend a hard truth about his relationship because you'd want to know, even though you're pretty sure he'd rather not hear it.
4. A nurse gets you to take medicine you're refusing by saying, look, I'd want someone to make me take it.
5. A guy who genuinely likes being hit figures the rule gives him a green light to hit other people.
6. You operate on a rule of never doing to anyone what you wouldn't want done to you, but you also never go out of your way to help anyone.

Part of me thinks there are two rules hiding inside the one slogan: a fairness test where I swap places with the other person, and a separate rule about respecting what the other person actually wants. These cases seem to pull those two readings apart, and that might be why I'm stuck.

My own rough views: the wallet case is definitely the golden rule. The nurse case, I think, is the rule being used as an excuse rather than the real thing. The masochist case feels like a cheap objection that depends on a dumb reading of the rule, but I can't say why without a real definition. And I keep flip-flopping on wether do-what-the-other-person-wants is a fixed version of the golden rule or just a seperate rule, and on whether the never-hurt-anyone rule is a stripped-down golden rule or something weaker.

So: propose a definition, run it through all six cases, tell me where it gives a verdict you didn't expect or where it refuses to give one, and either revise the definition or defend the verdicts. Then tell me what would change your mind about the definition — which case or argument would most likely break it, or where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
