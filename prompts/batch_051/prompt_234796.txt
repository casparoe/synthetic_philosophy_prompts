I'm not a philosophy person or anything, but I've got this friend who is weirdly opinionated about when its okay to break a rule. Not dramatic laws, just everyday stuff—like she treats every "no parking" sign as a question about how strict people should really be with each other. I've started keeping a list of her verdicts because I honestly can't always predict her. Here's her track record so far:

- Crossing the street against a "don't walk" signal at 3am, when you can see no cars for blocks: she says go, obviously.
- Parking in a fire lane in front of a store "just for two minutes" with your hazard lights on: she says absolutely not.
- Bringing your own candy into a movie theater because the concession stand is a ripoff: she says that's fine.
- Skipping the line at a packed airport security checkpoint because your flight is already boarding: she says no, wait like everyone else.
- Drinking a beer out of a paper bag on a park bench at night, in a park where alcohol is banned, when the place is empty and you carry the bottle home: she says she doesn't see the problem.
- Setting off fireworks in a dry canyon during a fire warning: she says that's insane.

The case we're arguing about now is this: a guy hops the turnstile at a subway station at 5am. The station is basically empty, no employee in sight, the train will run whether or not he pays, and he can afford the fare—he just doesn't see the point of paying when there's no one around to care. He's not slowing anyone down or taking a seat from anyone.

So what would she say about him, and what's the rule she's actually using? I feel like her record fits more than one rule, and I want to catch her on it. If these cases don't pin it down, what's the next case I should put to her to settle wether it's one rule or the other?
