Something's been bugging me about ranked-choice voting. The pitch when a city adopts it is always that you can finally vote sincerely: if your favorite loses, your ballot just transfers to your second choice, so there's no wasted vote and no reason to pick the lesser evil. But the same reformers, the next election, are distributing voter guides telling people to rank the compromise candidate first "just to be safe" or to bullet-vote (rank only one candidate) so their ballot won't transfer to somebody they dislike. If the method still rewards misrepresenting your ranking, what exactly was the sincere-vote pitch presupposing about voters' preferences and about what a fair voting rule can even do? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind the pitch, say which parts it strictly needs and which parts are just loose talk, and work out what a voting method that genuinely made honest ranking always optimal would have to look like — or whether the pitch itself is the inconsistency.
