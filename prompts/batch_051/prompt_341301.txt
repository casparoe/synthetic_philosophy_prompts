I want one essay question on indeterminacy of translation, but it has to be the kind students cant answer by retelling the rabbit story for two pages.

Could you write the question, a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme? It's a 90-minute final and I'm giving the essay 20 marks total.

The thing I most want the question to test is whether students can seperate indeterminacy of translation from plain underdetermination of theory by evidence. Most of them leave the course thinking that evidence can't force a unique translation and that that's the whole thesis. I'd also like it to force them to say whether Quine's indeterminacy claim depends on his behaviorism: would someone who thinks meanings are real mental items still have reason to accept the stronger claim that there's no fact of the matter about which translation manual is right? If you can, make them use the field linguist and the native informant to spell out what exactly is supposed to be indeterminate, reference rather than just word choice, and why gathering more observations would never settle it. One mean move I'd love them to grapple with is why translating gavagai as rabbit and translating it as undetached rabbit part are not just two readings of an ambiguous word.

For the marking scheme, I want it to name the specific moves that separate an A from a B. An A answer should clearly distinguish the two theses, say which one Quine actually holds, and say something precise about stimulus meaning or analytical hypotheses. A B answer will probably summarize gavagai accurately but treat it as a problem about translation errors rather than about there being no fact of the matter. Allocate the 20 marks across the moves you want, and note which specific confusions should cost marks.
