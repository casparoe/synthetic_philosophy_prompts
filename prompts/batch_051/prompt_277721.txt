Had a whole argument last night with a friend about the absent-minded driver puzzle. The way I understand it, the driver is heading home and there are two identical exits: the first one drops you in a rough part of town, the second one is his actual exit, and if he misses both he ends up sleeping at a motel. He's absent-minded, so whenever he reaches an exit he can't tell whether it's the first or the second one. My friend kept insisting the puzzle shows something deep about choosing a plan and then not being able to stick to it, even for a perfectly rational agent. But I dont see what's deep about it. It seems like the whole thing is just a memory problem. If the driver could remember which exit he was at, or just wrote it down, or used a GPS, the problem would dissapear. So isn't the absent-minded driver really just a puzzle about forgetting? Why does decision theory treat it as a real challenge to rationality instead of just setting it aside as defective information?
