I spent four decades in physical chemistry, and in retirement I have been sitting in on a philosophy reading group at the university. We have spent several sessions on forgiveness, and I keep noticing that the discussion runs one favorite case at a time — the unrepentant wrongdoer, the spouse who forgives an affair, the adult who claims to forgive a dead parent, the victim who insists that forgiving would be servile — without anyone first writing down what an adequate account of forgiveness would have to get right all at once. That specification is what I want before I read further. Please write the desiderata for a theory of forgiveness: the full list of requirements such a theory must meet to count as adequate, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. By a theory of forgiveness I mean an account of what forgiveness itself is, what it does to the emotional and normative situation of the victim and the wrongdoer, and when, if ever, it is appropriate, required, or impossible. Sort the demands people make into those that are non-negotiable for any adequate theory and those that are really preferences of one camp — for example, the demand that forgiveness must be earned by repentance, or the opposing demand that it must be grantable unconditionally. I am most interested in the question whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: does the requirement that forgiveness not collapse into excusing or condoning conflict with the possibility of unconditional forgiveness? Does the view that forgiveness is elective — a kind of gift one is always free to withhold — square with the claim that in some cases forgiveness is morally required? And which single requirement, if accepted, does the most to exclude currently popular theories?

Give me the result as a single JSON object with the following fields, and stay within the role specified for each field. "requirements": an array of objects, each having "label" (a short name for the requirement), "statement" (one or two sentences stating what an adequate theory must do), "status" (exactly one of "non-negotiable", "negotiable", or "camp-specific"), "settledness" (exactly one of "settled", "genuinely contested", or "camp-specific"), "confidence" (exactly one of "high", "medium", or "low"), "motivating_case" (the case or consideration that makes this a requirement), and "what_it_pressures" (which candidate views fail or are strained if the requirement is accepted). "joint_satisfiability": an array of objects, each having "requirement_a" and "requirement_b" (labels drawn from the requirements array), "verdict" (one of "jointly satisfiable", "jointly unsatisfiable", or "unresolved"), and "reasoning" (a brief statement of the tension or its absence). "hardest_requirement": an object with "label" and "why" (why this requirement excludes or pressures the most currently popular theories). "contestability_map": an object with three arrays — "settled", "genuinely_contested", and "camp_specific" — giving short phrases for the claims that fall under each heading; these should line up with the requirements list. Throughout, separate what is settled among people who work on forgiveness from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; where your own confidence is low, say so rather than sharpening the claim.
