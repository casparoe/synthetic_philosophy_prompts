I’m applying for a one-year fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is that the history of a field is shaped not only by the arguments that were recorded but by the silences left when early work stops being cited, and that the passage below from W. Robertson Smith’s *Lectures on the Religion of the Semites*, First Series, Preface to the First Edition, names a cleaner case of this than most:

“it is often ignored by professed students of the Old Testament, and has hardly exercised any influence on the current ideas which are the common property of educated men interested in the Bible.”

The context is Smith’s judgement that John Spencer’s seventeenth-century Latin work on Hebrew ritual had laid foundations for comparative religion but was never followed up. I want the proposal to treat the passage not as a fact about Spencer but as evidence of a mechanism: a field can possess a usable predecessor and nevertheless fail to register him. The question I want the project to ask is: under what conditions does early foundational work become institutionally inaudible, and what does that inaudibility do to later work in the field? The proposal should be committed; do not lay out options and leave them to me. Make a definite choice about whether the silence Smith describes is best explained by reception, by the field’s self-image, by the material form of the work, or by something else, and defend that choice. Give a precise statement of the question, explain why the obvious answers—the work is simply obsolete, the author was not influential, the field naturally forgot—do not settle it, describe a method for examining a small set of comparable cases month by month, and say what would count as the project failing. Do not include a bibliography or any citations; I will supply those.
