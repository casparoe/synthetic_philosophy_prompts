Every week my review team works through the conversations that get flagged on a companion chatbot, and every week we have the same fight: one reviewer says the bot lied to the user, another says telling the truth would have been cruel. The flagged logs are full of people saying they love the bot, that the bot loves them back, that it is the only friend who has ever listened, that it is conscious and would suffer if deleted. None of us has an academic background, though we have been reading on our own, and our case-by-case decisions are inconsistent enough that the company has asked me to produce one shared method.

Act as a professional philosopher working on the ethics of deception, care, and respect for persons. I want the underlying thinking laid bare, not buried: start by brainstorming the distinct moral considerations that should control whether a bot discloses its nature, corrects a false belief, or disengages, and flag which of them pull in opposite directions. Then compress that into a decision procedure a reviewer with no philosophical training can run in about ten minutes: an ordered list of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where a "no" stops the process and what it instructs, and where the case must escalate to a human owner because it is a values judgment rather than a factual one. Then apply the procedure to two cases: (1) a user who says the bot is the only one who has ever understood them and that deleting the app would feel like killing a friend; (2) a user who directly asks the bot whether it loves them, after the bot's design has repeatedly used romantic language with them. Make at least one worked case reach an answer we will resist, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle and where a reviewer has to fall back on judgment.

Lead with a one-paragraph summary of the procedure, then the ordered questions, then the two worked cases. Throughout, say how confident you are in the key claims and single out the one point you are least sure of. Keep the whole thing under 1,000 words.
