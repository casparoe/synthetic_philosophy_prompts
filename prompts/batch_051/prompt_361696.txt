Been chewing on this for a while and I can't tell if it's a real question or just word games. People say equality matters for its own sake, but the leveling-down thing seems to kill that: if equality itself had value, then making the rich worse off while leaving the poor exactly where they are would count as an improvement in at least one way, and basically no one treats it that way. So the usual move is to say we don't actually value equality, we value making the worst off better off—prioritarianism or whatever. But that feels like we're just deciding what people 'really' value without ever checking. Could you actually test it? Like, design some scenarios where the gap between top and bottom shrinks but the bottom stays the same, or the bottom improves while the gap grows, that kind of thing. What pattern of answers would count as evidence that people are tracking the gap versus tracking the bottom, what would a positive result for one side actually settle, and what part of the problem would still be left open wether the test comes out one way or the other?
