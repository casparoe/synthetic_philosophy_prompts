I'm drafting a position statement for an epistemology workshop on Sellars's critique of the given, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Individually, each of the following seven commitments seems defensible to me; what I can't tell is whether the set hangs together.

1. I accept Sellars's core negative claim: a bare sensation can cause a belief, but it cannot rationally support one. Only states with propositional content can stand in justificatory relations; nothing earns an epistemic role merely by being a nonpropositional item present to consciousness.

2. I am nevertheless a foundationalist. Empirical justification must terminate in non-inferentially justified foundations, because a pure coherence theory cannot explain how empirical thought is anchored in the world.

3. The empirical foundations are perceptual seemings. A perceptual seeming is a propositionally contentful state, such as its looking to me as if there is a red cube before me; it is not a belief, since I can have the seeming while suspending the corresponding belief, and its justification is not by inference from further beliefs.

4. Perceptual experience also has nonconceptual content. It presents specificity that outstrips my conceptual repertoire—an exact shade I could not re-identify, a shape I cannot classify—and this fine-grainedness is part of what makes experience a direct openness to the world.

5. I am a direct realist about perception. Perception fundamentally relates me to mind-independent objects, and I reject sense-data and any view on which what I am immediately aware of is something standing between the mind and the world.

6. I hold an internalist constraint on justification: whether I am justified supervenes on factors within my first-person perspective, and facts wholly outside that perspective make no difference to my justification.

7. I hold a reliabilist condition on knowledge: a true belief does not amount to knowledge if it is produced by an unreliable process. I understand this as a condition on the step from true justified belief to knowledge, not as a theory of justification.

Go through these pairwise and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against one another and which only appear to. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity, say which commitment would have to give, and describe the cheapest revision that restores consistency. Also tell me whether any single revision would ease more than one tension at once, and flag anything I seem to be implicitly committed to beyond the seven items listed. What I need is discrimination: don't tell me everything is in tension, and don't tell me everything is fine.
