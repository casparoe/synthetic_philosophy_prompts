im a software engineer and ive never taken a philosophy class, but choosing priors for bayesian models keeps making me wonder whether "total ignorance" picks out a unique rational prior or not. my gut calls are all over the place and i want someone to tell me if theres a principle behind them, and if not, which verdicts i should drop.

Here are the cases, more or less in order of how confident i am:
1. A coin comes from a mint i know nothing about; it may be biased, with probability p of heads anywhere in [0,1]. I'm convinced the only defensible prior over p is uniform on [0,1].
2. Same coin, but now i describe the bias with the log-odds q = ln(p/(1-p)). I know just as little about q as about p, so uniform over q ought to be just as rational — but it gives a different distribution. I think one of these has to be wrong, and i suspect it's the q one, but my only reason is that p feels more "real," which sounds circular.
3. A six-sided die, nothing known. I want to put 1/6 on each face for the next throw, but my prior over possible weightings of the die should still be spread out over the space of possible dies, not have all its mass on the perfectly fair one. This doesn't feel like a contradiction to me but i can't say why.
4. Take the old puzzle about a random chord of a circle: the probability it's longer than the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle comes out 1/2, 1/3, or 1/4 depending on how the chord is chosen. Here my gut call is the opposite of case 1: total ignorance just doesn't determine a unique distribution.
5. A die is weighted so the expected value of a roll is 3.9, with nothing else known. I find myself reaching for the maximum entropy distribution over the six faces, but i can't justify why high entropy is the right tiebreaker.
6. A process outputs a positive integer, nothing else known. I don't think there's a uniform distribution over the integers, so total ignorance gives me nothing there — yet in the coin case it felt like it gave me something solid.

Is there one principle (indifference? maximum entropy? something else?) that captures these, or am i just rationalizing gut feelings? Specifically i cant tell if cases 1 and 4 can both be right. Whatever account you settle on, say which of my verdicts it overturns and what it predicts for a case i deliberately avoided: all i know is that a scale parameter, like a standard deviation, is some positive real number. Should my prior be uniform over it, uniform over its log, or something else, and why?
