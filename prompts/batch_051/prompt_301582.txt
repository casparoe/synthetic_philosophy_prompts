Bit of a niche one, but I'm trying to sort out what actually does the work in the idea of a "Ulysses contract", because it keeps getting invoked in a debate I'm having about psychiatric advance directives, and I'd rather go back to the source than argue from slogans. The passage everyone leans on is from Homer's Odyssey, Book 12, where Odysseus reports Circe's instructions and then gives his crew their orders (Samuel Butler's translation):

"Therefore, take me and bind me to the cross-piece half way up the mast; bind me as I stand upright, with a bond so fast that I cannot possibly break away, and lash the rope's ends to the mast itself. If I beg and pray you to set me free, then bind me more tightly still."

Homer, Odyssey, Book 12, trans. Samuel Butler.

Now here's the case I'm stuck on, which Homer plainly never had in mind. A woman with bipolar disorder, when fully stable, draws up an advance directive saying that if she later refuses her mood-stabilising medication during a manic episode, her psychiatrist may treat her anyway; the document also says she waives her right to revoke it while she is unwell. Ten months later she is completely well, no hint of mania, and she wants to revoke the whole thing. The clinic says the waiver language survives until the review date she herself set. I want to know exactly how the Odyssey passage bears on this. What is doing the work in Odysseus's arrangement — the fact that he gives the orders, the crew's obligation to obey their captain, Circe's warning, the wax in the men's ears, something else — and does any of it transfer to a doctor following a directive the patient now wants to cancel? Or is the passage just a vivid story with no rule in it at all?

So, treat the passage as a sort of "rule" for self-binding, and ask how it applies to a competent, stable revocation its authors never anticipated. Go through the different ways of reading it — the plain words, the purpose of the arrangement, what Odysseus and his crew would have said if they'd been asked about a signed and witnessed document instead of a spoken command at sea, and what a reasonable person would take it to mean — and say which reading should govern here. And please seperate what is settled about Ulysses contracts from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this; don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
