I have been reading Mencius and Xunzi side by side for a course on classical Chinese political thought, and I kept finding myself thinking about a very different text: the Federalist defense of the U.S. Constitution. The framers famously designed the American system around the assumption that no person or faction can safely be trusted with unchecked power; Madison's argument in Federalist No. 51, roughly, is that a constitution must both enable the government to control the governed and oblige the government to control itself, hence the separation of powers, bicameralism, and the system of checks and balances. The standard contrast, as I understand it, is that Mencius holds that human nature contains incipient sprouts of the moral virtues that need the right environment to mature, while Xunzi holds that human nature is bad and that goodness must be built through deliberate effort, ritual, and institutional artifice. I want you to analyze the design of the U.S. Constitution — primarily the separation of powers and checks and balances — through the lens of that debate. Give me a careful mapping: which features of the constitutional design look like a Xunzi-style answer, in which human beings must be corrected by external structure and discipline, and which features could be defended from a Mencian position, in which the sprouts principally need the right material and educational conditions to flourish. Be precise about what each mapping commits the thinker to and where it strains.

Then run the analysis in the other direction. Mencius and Xunzi were primarily arguing about moral psychology, self-cultivation, and the possibility of becoming good, not about the design of a federal republic or the mechanics of ambition and faction; say plainly what the constitutional case misses or misdescribes about each position. Finally, use the episode to test the theories: does the American founding confirm or challenge the two accounts of human nature, and does it expose any blind spots in both — for example, about whether institutional arrangements can substitute for virtue, or whether the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi is partly a disagreement about what political institutions are for? I would like the analysis to be disciplined rather than forced, and to mark the points at which each theory falls silent.
