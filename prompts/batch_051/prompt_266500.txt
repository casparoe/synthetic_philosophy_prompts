A friend and I have just finished a long argument about the competitiveness constraint in AI development: whether a lab that genuinely prioritised safety would inevitably be outcompeted by labs that treated safety as an afterthought. The argument ended with him saying, almost as if it settled the matter, that safety is just an alignment tax. I have heard that phrase often enough that its compactness no longer registers, and I want to slow it down. What precise claims could that single sentence be expressing? For instance: that safety measures which do not improve measured capability impose a cost on the lab that adopts them; that safety and capability trade off against one another, so the cost cannot be engineered away; that in a competitive market, actors who bear avoidable costs are selected against; and that the phrase is really a claim about how models are benchmarked and compared, not about some intrinsic property of safety work. I want a proper menu rather than a single interpretation: for each reading, say whether it is a near-tautology, a substantive and defensible claim, or false; say which reading is probably doing the work when the phrase is used in these discussions; and say which reading, if any, would actually support the conclusion my friend used it to draw.
