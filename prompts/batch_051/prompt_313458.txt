I work in formal epistemology and decision theory, and I think fluently in the vocabulary of imprecise probability: a body of evidence is represented by a set of probability functions rather than a single one, with lower and upper probabilities; precision is how tight that set is; and decisions under ambiguity are evaluated by something like maximin expected utility over the set. I also know Keynes's theory of probability in broad outline: probability as a logical relation between propositions rather than a state of an agent's mind, the denial that all probabilities are numerically measurable, the claim that some probabilities stand only in relations of greater or lesser with no number attached, and the weight of argument, the amount of relevant evidence behind a probability judgment, as something distinct from the probability itself.

I want you to restate the second vocabulary systematically in the first. Go component by component: (1) the objective logical relation reading of probability; (2) non-numerical probabilities, including the difference between one proposition being more probable than another and one being twice as probable as another; (3) the partial ordering of probabilities when numbers are unavailable; (4) the weight of argument. For each, say what translates cleanly into sets of priors, lower and upper probabilities, and precision; what translates only if you distort it; and what has no expression in the imprecise-probability framework at all.

Then give me the residue report I actually care about. Is the part that fails to translate a defect of the imprecise-probability framework — a notion it ought to be able to express but presently cannot — or is it evidence that the Keynesian claim was doing several jobs at once and was less clear than it looked? Be explicit about where the two frameworks part company over whether probabilities are objective relations or subjective or decision-theoretic states, and over whether the weight of evidence is the same thing as the precision of an imprecise probability. My suspicion is that the weight of argument is where the translation breaks, and that ambiguity models quietly collapse it into precision; tell me whether that suspicion survives the exercise, and if it does, what a framework that kept weight and precision distinct would have to add.
