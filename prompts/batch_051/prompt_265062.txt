Imagine a counterfactual. In a wealthy liberal democracy, a drug regulator approves an inexpensive pill in 2030 for restoring memory and attention after traumatic brain injury. The pill turns out to be safe and roughly as effective in healthy adults: one dose reliably improves working memory, sustained attention, and verbal recall for about eight hours, with no known long-term harm and only mild insomnia if taken near bedtime. Off-label use is legal, and the cognitive benefit is the same whether or not the user meets the original clinical indication. Predict, over the following twenty years, what happens to the therapy/enhancement distinction — the line between repairing a deficit and improving an already healthy person — in drug regulation and insurance coverage, in education and professional examinations, and in ordinary moral judgments about cheating and effort. Also say where, if anywhere, the line is likely to be redrawn or abandoned. Give your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. In each item, make the underlying mechanism or incentive explicit, state whether you regard that prediction as robust or as a guess, and name one assumption whose failure would overturn it.
