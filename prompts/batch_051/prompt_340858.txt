You are a philosopher of religion with a precise command of classical theism — omniscience, immutability, atemporality — and a keen ear for the deadpan register of online help-center copy. Your task is to write a satirical piece.

Form: a parody of a customer-support FAQ or troubleshooting page, about 650 words, formatted as numbered FAQ entries under a short header for a prayer-intake portal run by the office of an omniscient, immutable, atemporal God. Include a last-updated line in the header that is itself theologically pointed.

Voice and target: sustain the deadpan bureaucratic voice throughout. The satire should target petitionary prayer taken literally, together with the stock philosophical attempts to salvage it — the claim that prayer changes the one who prays rather than the deity, and the claim that the deity eternally willed to grant the request on condition of the asking. Do not let the help desk announce that its own answers are incoherent; let the answers, and what they quietly concede, do the work. A specialist should recognize the real argumentative landscape and find the satire fair; a reader new to the debate should still follow it.

Before drafting, brainstorm privately at least five distinct theological pressure points such a help center would have to handle, including: why one must submit a ticket to a system with complete foreknowledge of the request and of its outcome; how a change request could alter an immutable workflow; how the claim that the ticket matters can survive the fact that the Administrator's single eternal act already included the submitting of it; the difference among the ticket types of supplication, praise, contrition, and thanksgiving; and why repeated or escalating submissions should not improve the outcome. Work through each point accurately rather than gesturing at vagueness. At least one FAQ entry should give the sophisticated answer that the request is an eternally decreed condition of the granting rather than a cause that changes the Administrator.

End the piece with one entry that leaves the reader — not the help desk — with the residual unease that, under these commitments, the request may be no more than a required formality. Do not include citations or bibliographies, and do not look anything up; the piece must stand on its own.
