Draft the joint statement that a grounding realist and a grounding deflationist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality. Define the realist as holding that some facts non-causally explain other facts through a distinctive objective relation of grounding: a hyperintensional relation, one that can distinguish between necessarily equivalent claims, which yields a fundamental/derivative ordering and is not reducible to logical consequence, supervenience, essence, identity, or causation. Define the deflationist as holding that everything grounding is invoked to explain is already explained by those other notions together with our explanatory practices, so the relation is either no distinctive thing at all or a dispensable shorthand, and the fundamentality hierarchy is at best a projection of which explanations we happen to find illuminating.

The deliverable has three parts.

First, a numbered agreed-claims section. The claims should be substantive rather than vacuous: each should be one that at least one side was initially inclined to attribute to the other and then conceded. Cover at least the explanatory phenomena both sides accept as data — apparent asymmetries such as between the truth of a conjunction and the truth of its conjuncts, between an object and its singleton set, and between fundamental physical facts and the facts of the higher-level sciences, such as chemistry or economics, on the assumption that the latter are not identical to the former — and cover the formal constraints a candidate relation would have to satisfy or explicitly reject: irreflexivity, asymmetry, transitivity, and hyperintensionality, including why logical entailment and supervenience alone seem too coarse to fix explanatory direction.

Second, a disagreements table. For each contested claim give three columns: the realist's preferred formulation, the deflationist's preferred formulation, and the neutral question the two formulations are answering. The formulations must be worded so that each side would accept its own column as a fair statement of its position. Explicitly flag any row where the disagreement is merely terminological.

Third, the cruxes. For each crux, describe a concrete case or kind of case that both sides would accept as evidence bearing on the question, and record each side's prediction about it in advance. At least one crux should concern whether one relation can unify cases as different as conceptual-logical truths, mathematical foundations, part–whole dependence, and the dependence of the higher-level sciences on fundamental physics. At least one should concern what exactly a deflationist must reproduce to count as having explained ground away.

End in a few sentences with the question the two parties would regard as the one most worth settling next, and why.
