I'm putting together a workshop on whether a company can literally be morally blamed, and I want the document the two camps should of written after a week of talking past each other. The camps are the corporate moral realists (a corporation itself can be a proper target of moral blame, not just fines or bad PR) and the individualists (moral blame attaches only to natural persons, and "blaming the company" is shorthand, a fiction, or a tool). Draft their adversarial-collaboration joint statement. Start with a numbered list of agreed claims that are substantive but neither slanted nor weaselly—cover what both grant about how corporate decisions actually get made, what both grant about individual responsibility inside a firm, and what both grant about the law's treatment of companies. Then do a disagreements table where each side's cell is phrased in wording that side would actually accept. After that, give me the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction. Make one crux the question whether a company can have the sort of mental state blame seems to require—and tease apart distributed knowledge (the group "knows" what no member knows) from whether anything in the group can feel guilt or remorse. Make another crux the question whether the legal/moral blame distinction is doing real work or just marking a vibe. Also invent one recurring example—maybe a manufacturer where the design, safety, and legal teams each hold only a fragment of the relevant facts and the committee turns over quarterly—and show how each side would describe what happened there. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.
