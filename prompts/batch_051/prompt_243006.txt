For a pragmatism reading group, I have been asked to write down my complete position on truth so the others can stress-test it before I present. Here is the set of claims I want to hold onto:

1. A belief is true just in case it would be unshakable under continued experience and argument — the view that inquiry would converge on in the long run, roughly Peirce's account.
2. A belief is true just in case it is expedient for us to hold it — what it is useful or satisfying for us to believe, roughly James's account.
3. Truth is not relative: when two people hold contradictory beliefs, at most one of them has a true belief, regardless of what either finds useful or satisfying.
4. Some claims about the past are determinately true or false even though no possible inquiry could settle them — for example, the exact number of coins that lay on a table at a particular moment, once every trace of the event has been destroyed.
5. The fact that a belief works in practice is a fallible but genuine reason to think that it is true.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say for each which claim would have to give and what the least costly revision would be, and then identify the single change that would do the most to make the whole set consistent.
