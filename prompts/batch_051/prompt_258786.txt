Suppose a country abolishes its estate tax and replaces it with a levy on the receipt of gifts and inheritances. Each citizen has a single lifetime tax-free allowance, set at the median inheritance of the previous generation among those who received any; all receipts above the allowance are taxed at progressive income-tax rates in the year they are received. No exemption is given for farms, family businesses, or the family home, but the levy on illiquid assets may be paid in instalments over fifteen years. The revenue funds a flat capital grant paid to every citizen at age twenty-five. Walk through how this scheme would actually behave: what it does to the incentive to split, time, or reroute transfers among relatives; how trusts, gifts in kind, and emigration would be used; how it bears on the position of those who start with nothing; and where it is likely to be gamed first and whether the failure modes can be fixed without abandoning the recipient-side principle.
