I'm putting together a little zine about bad decisions and I want one satirical piece for it. You know the dollar auction? It's the game where someone sells a pound coin to the highest bidder, but the second-highest bidder also has to pay their bid and gets nothing. The nasty thing about it is that once two people are bidding, carrying on seems sensible from each side: if you're in at 90p and the other person has bid £1, it looks better to bid £1.10 and loose only 10p than to drop out and be down 90p. But then the other side faces the same logic, and the whole thing escalates until both players are in for far more than the coin is worth. It's a neat little trap about escalation of commitment, sunk costs, throwing good money after bad, whatever you want to call it.

Could you write me a mock consultancy engagement letter, about 600 words, from a firm that openly sells escalation of commitment as a service? I'm imagining the deadpan voice of a big management consultancy, the sort of document that opens by saying how pleased the firm is to be appointed, with numbered scope-of-services clauses and a fee schedule. But the product is help with continuing projects that probably should be killed: frameworks for turning bad results into "early setbacks," a premium service for re-baselining budgets so overspends become the new baseline, maybe an actuarial table for how much further spend is now "required" to honour what's already been spent. The humour should come from pushing the logic of sunk costs to its actual conclusion, so a reader who has never heard of the dollar auction still recognises the everyday habit, while a reader who knows the literature finds it fair rather than just a caricature. Keep it deadpan throughout, no winking, but it should still be legible as a reductio. And please don't name any real firm or person.
