I've been keeping notes for a piece on population ethics, and I keep getting stuck on the procreation asymmetry: the claim that there is a strong, perhaps decisive, reason not to bring into existence a person whose life would on balance be miserable, but no corresponding duty—and on many views not even much independent reason—to bring into existence a person whose life would on balance be happy. I don't need another statement of the asymmetry; I need an explanation of why it holds, one that would satisfy a reader who suspects the whole thing is just status-quo bias dressed up as principle.

So far my candidate list has four entries. (1) The Benatar-style axiological asymmetry: the absence of pain is good even when there is no one whose pain is absent, while the absence of pleasure is not bad when there is no one deprived of it, with the four-cell pattern treated as basic. (2) The person-affecting explanation: in the miserable-child case there is a subject who is badly off, so a harm with a victim occurs, whereas in the happy-child non-creation case no one is made better off because there was no one to be benefited; wrongs need victims, while benefits need beneficiaries, and in the comparison that matters only the victim is available. (3) The negative-duty explanation: the asymmetry is a special case of the priority of not harming over benefiting—creating a miserable person harms, while failing to create a happy person merely fails to benefit. (4) A consent-based explanation: procreation always imposes an unchosen life, and it is justified only when the imposed life is one the person could reasonably endorse; the asymmetry tracks the difference between an unendorsable imposition and a merely unchosen one.

I want a fifth candidate that is not on this list and not a rephrasing, special case, or combination of its entries—in particular, not one that simply re-runs the negative/positive-duty distinction under another name or dresses the person-affecting view up as something new. The fifth account must explain the asymmetry rather than redescribe it. Then give me your committed assessment: of these five, which is doing the real explanatory work, and is the fifth a genuine contender or an artifact of the constraint? I would rather have a definite ranking with reasons than a survey of options.
