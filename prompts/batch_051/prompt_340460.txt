Had a long argument with a mate last night about whether individuals making small choices inside huge collective harms are doing anything wrong, and its left me wondering whether the positions I've been holding actually hang together. I'd like an audit of them rather than a lecture, because I genuinely can't tell where the problem is.

Here's roughly where I've ended up:

1. I accept the inefficacy objection for eating meat. One chicken or one burger almost certainly doesn't change how many animals are killed, so I don't think the wrongness of buying it can be explained by the difference my individual purchase makes.
2. But I do think a person can be morally required to cut their own carbon footprint before any collective action is in place — e.g. not taking a long-haul flight for a weekend away, or eating less beef even if the market for beef doesn't notice.
3. I think it's still worth voting even though one vote almost never decides an election.
4. I'm not a rule-consequentialist. "What if everyone did that?" on its own isn't doing the work for me here.
5. I suspect there's a real structural difference between a discrete production process (the meat market) and an aggregate accumulation process (the atmosphere), and that this difference, rather than expected value, is what makes individual climate choices different from individual meat choices.

Go through these and tell me: which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this stuff, and don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.

Please write in British English. Start with a one-sentence verdict on whether the package hangs together, then the ranked tensions. Keep it under 900 words.
