In a jurisdiction that has adopted the Nordic model, the operative criminal provision reads: "A person who obtains sexual services in exchange for payment commits an offence." The provision was enacted when the legislature had in mind street prostitution, brothels, and trafficking, and its stated purpose was to reduce demand for the purchase of another person's body.

A company now offers a subscription service, Eido, that generates real-time erotic interaction on demand: an AI improvises a bespoke scenario in the subscriber's preferred voice and style and drives a networked haptic device worn by the subscriber. No human being performs any sexual act, is paid for sex, or is recorded for the service; the entire encounter is synthesized at the moment of use.

Does the clause reach a subscription to Eido? Work through the main approaches to statutory interpretation—plain meaning, legislative purpose, what the drafters would have said had the technology been proposed to them, and what a reasonable reader of the provision would take it to ban—and say which reading is strongest. In answering, isolate what the Nordic model's criminalization of purchase is ultimately aimed at: exploitation, the commodification of sexual activity, demand that drives trafficking, public order, or something else—and explain how the classification of Eido depends on which aim is treated as basic.
