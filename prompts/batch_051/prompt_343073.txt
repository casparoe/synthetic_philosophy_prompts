I've landed fairly firmly on the view that the simple conditional analysis of dispositions is dead: that "x is disposed to M when S" is not equivalent to "if x were S, x would M," because the fink, mask, and mimic cases show the counterfactual comes apart from the disposition in both directions. But I want to know how much of that is a real result and how much is a stance I've picked up by osmosis. So don't just re-argue the cases for me. Tell me what would count against the anti-simple-analysis position. What would the world, or the best semantics of counterfactuals, or a sharper metaphysics of stimulus and manifestation conditions, have to look like for some non-gerrymandered version of the counterfactual analysis to be salvageable -- a version that doesn't just dismiss what the fink, mask, and mimic cases were getting at? Which defenses of the counterfactual analysis are live options and which are merely logically possible, and how would you rank them from decisive to merely awkward? And be honest about whether my view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- whether "no simple counterfactual analysis can work" risks nothing at all -- and whether that should bother me.
