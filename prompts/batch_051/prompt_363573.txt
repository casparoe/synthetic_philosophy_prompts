Please argue for the following position. In the account of sin in Abelard's Ethics, sin is located in consent rather than in the outward deed: someone who consents to what they believe they ought not to do has sinned fully even if they are prevented from acting, and someone who is forced into the outward act without consent has not sinned. Defend the corollary that the familiar legal asymmetry between completed and attempted wrongdoing cannot be explained by saying that carrying out the act makes the agent more guilty. It should instead be explained entirely by two considerations: first, the epistemic constraints on human judges, who cannot observe consent directly and must treat the deed as external evidence of it; second, the forward-looking purposes of punishment, such as deterrence and the maintenance of public order—rather than by any retrospective difference in the sin itself. In making the case, distinguish Abelard's claim that a deed without consent is no sin from his stronger claim that the deed adds nothing to guilt once consent is present, and answer at least one objection, for example the intuition that a person who was stopped before acting is somehow less culpable than one who succeeded.

Structure your answer into four sections: "The position stated" (one or two sentences); "The core argument" (two paragraphs); "Reply to an objection" (one paragraph); "Conclusion and qualification" (two or three sentences).
