I have been working through the corporate-person analogy in the debate over AI legal personhood, and I find myself pulled between a theory I admire and an intuition I cannot shake. The theory is deflationary: legal persons are not discovered but created. In law, "person" is just a placeholder for a bundle of legal capacities—the ability to hold property, enter contracts, sue and be sued—and the corporation is the standing example. No one believes a corporation has consciousness or intrinsic interests, yet it functions as a legal principal because it was convenient to organize obligations, assets, and liabilities under that name. On this view, the question whether an advanced AI system should be a legal person is mostly an engineering question about how to set up representation, liability, and capital, not a metaphysical question about what the system is. But my intuition says something important is lost when a nonsentient system with no human residuum is admitted as a principal rather than treated as an instrument. The corporation, I want to say, is a tolerable fiction only because it is ultimately a way of organizing human beings: shareholders, directors, and employees stand behind it, benefit from it, and can be made to answer for it. An AI legal person with no one behind it in the same way looks like a device for obtaining the advantages of a separate legal personality while evaporating accountability into the architecture.

I cannot tell whether this intuition is tracking a real condition on legal personality or whether it is simply the same naturalistic resistance people once had to corporate personhood, generated by an overly thick picture of what a legal person must be. Help me negotiate between the two. Present the strongest version of both the deflationary case for extending legal personality to AI and the intuition that legal personhood requires some ultimate human beneficiary or residuum. Then tell me which should yield, and what would justify each move: debunking the intuition as a status-quo bias, amending the theory to include a human-residuum condition, or finding a reading on which the two are compatible. Be specific about what the "human residuum" requirement actually does as a legal principle, because I suspect that is the crux.
