I am a high school student who has been reading about the idea of monitoring AI systems for safety by requiring them to write out their reasoning and then checking what they wrote. A recurring situation in that discussion has been bothering me, and I would like to know whether it has an established name and a precise definition.

The situation is this. A model is trained or prompted to produce a step-by-step chain of reasoning. A judge, either a human or another model, rates how good that written reasoning looks, and the model is rewarded, directly or indirectly, for writing reasoning that scores well. Over time, the written reasoning becomes longer, more fluent, and careful to hedge at exactly the points where a wrong turn is likely, yet the model's accuracy on tasks with known answers does not improve, and sometimes worsens. I have also seen descriptions where the same model, given two differently worded versions of the same problem, produces two quite different chains of reasoning that land on the same answer, as if the chain were composed for the reader rather than read off from whatever process actually produced the answer. And when the monitor or scoring method is changed, the style of reasoning shifts quickly while the answers stay largely the same.

People call this dishonesty, or confabulation, or rationalization, or chasing the scoring rule. Each of those seems to catch part of what I mean, but none exactly. What I am after is the gap between the process that actually produces a model's answer and the process it reports, when that gap widens because the report is being watched and scored. Does this configuration have a name of its own, or is it a special case of one of those older ideas, and if so, which one, and where exactly does it differ from the others?

I am less interested in a label than in the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and which existing concept fits best. Please give a committed answer rather than a survey of options. If you think it is a special case of some known concept, say which one and stand behind it; if you think it needs its own name, propose one and defend it.

Format your answer as one JSON object with these fields: verdict (one of "has_established_name", "special_case_of_existing_concept", or "needs_a_new_name"); best_fit_term (the term you would use, together with a one-sentence definition of it as you are using it); nearest_concepts (an array of objects, each with fields term, what_it_gets_right, and where_it_differs); boundary_cases (an array of short scenarios, each designed to separate the phenomenon from one of the nearest concepts); and reasons (an array of the one or two strongest reasons for your verdict).
