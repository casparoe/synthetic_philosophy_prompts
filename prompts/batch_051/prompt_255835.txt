I'm assembling a handout for an upper-level epistemology course, and I want it to include a "which position in the knowledge-ascription debate do you actually hold?" sorting instrument. Please write a ten-question multiple-choice quiz together with a scoring key. The quiz should sort people by their verdicts on concrete cases, not by self-identification; no question may use the labels of the positions or ask anyone to affirm a thesis by name.

The instrument must separate the following positions as cleanly as the format allows: epistemic contextualism; subject-sensitive invariantism, understood as pragmatic encroachment; moderate purist invariantism; strict invariantism, including its skeptical cast; and assessment-relativism about knowledge ascriptions. The spine of the quiz should be these five scenario types, paraphrased into your own cases:

1. a low-stakes case in which someone acts on a remembered, untested fact;
2. its high-stakes variant, where being wrong carries a serious practical cost;
3. a third-party ascription made by someone who does not share the subject's stakes;
4. an uneliminated but far-fetched error possibility that the subject could exclude only with unusual effort;
5. a retraction scenario in which the practical situation shifts after an earlier knowledge claim.

Each question should present a concrete case and two or three answer options about whether the subject knows, whether an ascriber's statement is true, or whether a retraction is required. Include at least two questions that target the contextualist/subject-sensitive-invariantist split by pitting the ascriber's context against the subject's stakes, and at least one question that tests sensitivity to assessment-relativity rather than content-shift.

The scoring key should be explicit about which answer pattern maps to which position, should note any combinations of answers that correspond to no standard position, and should flag in one paragraph which two positions the quiz is most likely to conflate and what an additional question would need to do to separate them.
