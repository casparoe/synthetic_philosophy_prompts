For a business ethics club handout, write a 'which position on corporate blame do you actually hold?' quiz. It should sort people into five views: (1) only individual persons can be genuinely blamed, so talk of blaming a company is just shorthand for blaming the people inside it; (2) corporate blame is real but purely as a legal or social practice, the way a chess piece is captured without being alive; (3) a company can be a group agent with beliefs and intentions of its own, blameworthy over and above its members; (4) corporate blame attaches to structures, routines, and incentive systems rather than to anyone's mental states; (5) companies can be blamed only in a forward-looking 'who should fix this and pay for it' sense, never as deserving guilt. Write eight questions, each a concrete case: the employees who made the decision have all left; a harm no individual intended but the bonus system predictably caused; a firm with an excellent compliance manual and a rotten tone at the top; a company that buys a smaller firm and inherits its wrongdoing. Options should never name the positions or use words like 'group agent' or 'mental states'; respondents should be sorted by what they say about cases, not by which label sounds good. Provide the scoring key, and flag any pair of views the questions can't cleanly seperate.
