I keep flip-flopping between two ways of thinking about how things persist through time. One says that an object is wholly present at each time it exists (endurantism), which feels to me like mutating an object in place. The other says an object is a four-dimensional worm made of temporal parts, like an append-only log of immutable snapshots, where the worm is the whole history (perdurantism). Can you write a dialogue between an endurantist and a perdurantist arguing about a case that messes with my analogy: a wooden ship is completely disassembled into planks, the planks sit in a warehouse for a month as a pile, and then their reassembled into the same ship. I want each character to actually respond to the other's points, and to force the other to say what happened during that month and whether the reassembled ship is the same ship as the original. Try to make neither one a strawman.
