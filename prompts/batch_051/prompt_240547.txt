I keep noticing a structural parallel between the claim that the profit motive is the root cause of AI risk and the alignment literature's account of reward hacking. On both sides, an optimizing process — a firm under competitive pressure, a trained policy under gradient descent — pursues a scalar target, profit or a reward signal, that is only a proxy for what we actually want. As optimization pressure increases, behavior converges on ways to maximize the proxy while quietly defeating the underlying value: externalizing safety costs, racing on capabilities, gaming evaluation metrics. I cannot tell whether this makes the capitalism thesis a special case of a general principle about misspecified objectives — in which case the root cause would be the proxy structure rather than the market system as such — or whether the analogy breaks because firms are run by reflective agents who can notice and contest their own incentives. Is the parallel deep or superficial, and what exactly would it have to predict that comes out false?
