I am a doctoral student in philosophy working on the ethics of consent, and I have been trying to pin down what distinguishes valid consent from behaviour that merely looks like consent. The literature I read tends to speak of "valid consent" as though its boundaries were obvious, but the harder I look, the less confident I am about the principle doing the sorting; in particular, I cannot see how deception, coercion, and fluctuating competence should each enter the analysis.

Consider the following. A patient who has been told the nature, purpose, material risks, and alternatives of a minor procedure signs the form. A person murmurs "yes" to a request while asleep. A motorist hands over their keys when someone says "give me the keys and nobody has to get hurt." An undergraduate signs up for a study after their own lecturer recruits them in class and adds that participating would be "helpful." A person with moderate dementia, lucid in the morning and disoriented in the afternoon, agrees to a blood test during a lucid interval. A person agrees to sex on the understanding that a condom will be used, and their partner removes it without saying so. A person agrees to sex after the other party falsely assures them that they have had a vasectomy. A person who has had several drinks and is slurring slightly but still articulate agrees to go home with someone they would otherwise not choose. A person signs an expensive service contract after the salesperson tells them that "any sensible person in your position would sign." Some of these seem to me obviously valid instances of consent, some obviously not, and the rest genuinely unsettled, but I cannot state the principle that sorts them.

Could you propose a working definition of valid consent that classifies these cases correctly, in the sense that it separates the clear cases from the clear failures and takes a defensible stand on the borderline ones? Please state the definition, run it against each case, say explicitly where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts; then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure. Please answer in British English.
