I just got back from a long argument with a friend about interpersonal utility comparisons, and it became clear that neither of us could state what an adequate account of them would even have to do. She held that utilities are merely ordinal and that any claim to the effect that A's gain is greater than B's loss is just a policy convenience with no truth value behind it; I kept insisting that some comparisons look unavoidable and obviously correct on reflection — a brief inconvenience to one person cannot sensibly be weighed against another person's unrelenting pain. We talked past each other because we had no shared specification of the task. Before I go back to her, I want the full set of desiderata. What must a theory of interpersonal utility comparisons get right? Give me a requirement list: the cases it has to handle (tradeoffs where a small benefit to very many people competes with a large benefit to one, comparisons across different kinds of welfare such as relief from chronic pain versus the satisfaction of achievement, comparisons between people with very different capacities for enjoyment, and so on); the distinctions it cannot collapse (comparing levels versus comparing units of scale, a common zero point versus a common unit, intrapersonal versus interpersonal comparability, measurability in principle versus elicitation in practice); the formal constraints it must respect (which transformations of any one person's utility function it may treat as irrelevant and which it may not); and the psychological or empirical findings it cannot ignore unless it gives a principled reason for overriding them, such as hedonic adaptation and the constructed character of reported preferences. For each requirement, say why it is a requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp. Then tell me which requirements are negotiable and which are not, whether any subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most to exclude the standard positions people actually take. End with a last line of the form \answer{...}, containing a short phrase naming that single most discriminating requirement.
