so i have this mental rule and i honestly don't know if it's rational or just me being dismissive. whenever someone tells me a "what are the odds" story — they were just thinking about an old friend and that exact person texted, they ran into their cousin in an airport in another country, the two strangers on their flight had the same birthday and weirdly similar jobs — i basically tune out and say "with enough people and enough moments, that kind of match has to happen to someone, and it happened to be you. it feels specific but it isn't." and then i move on. my whole justification is the birthday paradox, you know: in a room of 23 people it's already about 50/50 that two share a birthday, which sounds ridiculous if you ask people one at a time. so i treat almost every personal coincidence as a selection effect: the boring non-coincidences don't become stories, the one wild thing does, and then the person telling me acts like the wild thing was aimed at them.

last week it actually caused a small row. my coworker said that a few hours after her grandmother died, the clock in her kitchen stopped at the exact time on the death certificate. she's not even religious, she just said it felt like the universe gave her a little sign. i said it was a stopped clock and grief makes you notice. she was hurt and told me i use "statistics" as a wall so i never have to take anything as meaningful. it stung a bit because she's not fully wrong, i do use it as a wall.

so my question: is this rule defensible as an actual epistemic policy, or have i just dressed up a bias in birthday-paradox clothes? i'm aware i never actually do the counting — i never estimate how many event-pairs were in play or what the real base rate is, i just gesture at "big numbers" and walk away. is there a version of the rule that keeps the useful skepticism without making me unable to notice when a coincidence would genuinely be evidence of something? and how would i tell, from the inside, the difference between a coincidence that's only surprising because of the story-filter and one that's actually surprising?

keep it under 800 words. if you think the rule is only partly defensable, give me the repaired version i should actually use going forward.
