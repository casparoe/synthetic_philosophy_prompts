An athlete who moves to high altitude to raise his red-cell count is admired for his dedication; an athlete who takes a drug to produce the same change is banned and called a cheat. A child with a growth-hormone deficiency receives injections, and this is called medicine; a shorter-than-average but otherwise healthy child who receives the same injections is regarded by many people as questionable. A student with a diagnosed attention deficit is given stimulants, and this is treated as routine; a student without the deficit who uses the same stimulants to concentrate through an exam is judged to be gaining an unfair edge. What, if anything, justifies these differences? Is there a single line between treating a deficit and improving a normal person, or do the training/drug contrast and the deficit/health contrast rest on different principles, such as the value of effort versus the value of need? Does whatever line you draw survive hard cases such as preventing a disease before it appears, or slowing ordinary age-related decline, where no one is ill but no one is exceeding a normal baseline?
