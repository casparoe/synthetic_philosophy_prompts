An elected government, worried that its welfare state quietly tolerates social parasitism — people who take the benefits of generalized cooperation while declining to contribute — enacts the following scheme. Every adult citizen receives a monthly cash dividend, equal for all and paid unconditionally. Separately, each citizen must file an annual contribution record showing at least 400 hours of recognized contributory activity, where the recognized categories are paid work, unpaid care or registered volunteering, accredited education or training, documented job search, or a certified exemption for illness or disability. The penalty for falling short is not loss of the dividend. Instead, any citizen who falls short of the record and whose income lies above a set threshold pays a non-contribution surcharge of 15 percent of income above that threshold, capped at the value of the annual dividend; those below the threshold or holding an exemption simply keep the dividend and pay no surcharge. Surcharge receipts are recycled into the dividend fund.

Trace how this arrangement would actually behave. What does the design presuppose about what makes taking-without-contributing wrong, and does the surcharge operate as a deterrent, a public stigma, a fee for the privilege of free riding, or something that shifts with the taxpayer's income? How would people near the 400-hour margin or the income threshold alter their work, care, study, and self-reporting, and what does the scheme do to the informal economy and forms of contribution its categories leave out? Where would the mechanism be gamed first and most invisibly — padded records, purchasable certifications, strategic reclassification as a student or carer, high earners for whom the surcharge is a modest subscription — and which games are hard to distinguish from genuine changes in behavior? Does exempting the poor and the incapacitated merely relocate the boundary of the parasitic class rather than eliminate it, and would a defender be right to say that the scheme does not eliminate parasitism but makes it honest and compensates the fund? Finally, which failure modes, if any, could be fixed by adjusting the hour threshold, the surcharge rate, the cap, or the audit regime without collapsing the mechanism into punitive workfare on one side or an unconditional basic income that leaves free riding untouched on the other?
