I'm TAing an undergrad epistemology class and I've got a bunch of these to grade. The exam question was:

"Process reliabilism says a belief is justified if and only if it is produced by a reliable belief-forming process. Explain the bootstrapping objection to process reliabilism with a concrete example. Then explain the problem of easy knowledge, and say wheter it is the same problem as bootstrapping or a different one. Finally, discuss what you take to be the best reliabilist response to the bootstrapping objection."

Here's one student's answer, lightly edited for length:

"The bootstrapping objection is aimed at reliabilism about justification. Suppose Roxanne has a gas gauge that is in fact extremely reliable, and she has no independent evidence about it either way. She looks at the gauge, which reads FULL, and forms the belief that her tank is full. Because the gauge is reliable, reliabilism counts this belief as justified. She then reasons: the gauge read FULL, and the tank was full, so on this occasion the gauge read correctly. By repeating this move on many occasions, she inductively concludes that the gauge is generally reliable. But the crucial premise that the tank was full was itself obtained by trusting the gauge, so she has used the gauge to establish its own reliability. That is the bootstrapping objection: reliabilism seems to allow justified belief in the reliability of a source without any independent evidence, in a circular way. The easy knowledge problem is essentially the same problem, just run through knowledge rather than justification: a perceiver who knows by looking that there is a red table in front of her, and knows that the table looks red, can deduce that her color perception was accurate on this occasion, and over many such occasions that her color vision is generally reliable. The deduction is trivial, so closure of knowledge under known entailment gives her 'easy' knowledge of the reliability of her own perception. The main reliabilist fix is to add a condition requiring that the subject have some independent reason to believe the process is reliable, apart from the process's own outputs. This blocks bootstrapping because Roxanne cannot use the gauge's readings to support the gauge's reliability, and it blocks easy knowledge because a subject would not have perceptual knowledge in the first place unless she already had independent evidence that perception is reliable. Critics will say this leads to a skeptical regress, but the reliabilist can answer that we do have independent evidence about perception's reliability, from the coherence of our senses and the success of our actions."

Grade this out of 10 and give me the point-by-point comments you'd actually write on it. I don't want a general impression; I want the specific errors flagged, including any conflation between distinct problems or any response attributed to reliabilists that they would not actually accept. Also flag places that are correct but under-explained, since the rubric cares about that too.
