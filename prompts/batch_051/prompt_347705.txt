I've been going down a rabbit hole on Kelly betting and I keep tripping over the same thing. The usual story, as I understand it: when you have a positive edge on a repeated bet, Kelly tells you the single correct fraction of your bankroll to stake, and the derivation just says to maximize expected log wealth. But maximizing expected log wealth is a utility function, and a pretty particular-looking one at that—it encodes a very specific kind of risk aversion (constant relative risk aversion of one, right?). So saying Kelly is the optimal bet seems to smuggle in a particular taste for risk and then present it as a mathematical theorem. If I actually have a different utility function—say I'd rather maximize expected dollars and can ride out enormous swings, or I'm saving for a near-term goal—shouldn't I just maximize my expected utility, which might mean betting much more than Kelly, or much less? Kelly as objectively correct feels like sleight of hand: pick the one utility function that happens to give elegant formulas and then announce that everyone should use it.

Is there actually anything more to Kelly than that? Like, is maximizing the geometric growth rate doing some work that isn't just log utility in a trenchcoat, or is it literally just log utility with better marketing? Because right now I can't see why Kelly should be normative for anyone who doesn't already happen to have logarithmic utility, and I half-suspect the whole thing is a rhetorical trick.
