Ok, I've been reading Schopenhauer on music for a seminar and I built this argument that now worries me because the conclusion seems to undercut the whole section. In Book 3 of The World as Will and Representation he argues that music, unlike the other arts, does not copy the Platonic Ideas but is a direct copy of the will itself, and that it would still express the essence of the world even if there were no world of particular things. Here's what I did with that:

(1) If music is a direct copy of the will, not of the Ideas, then every piece of music stands in exactly the same metaphysical relation to the will.
(2) If every piece stands in the same relation, no piece can be metaphysically deeper or more truthful than another.
(3) So on Schopenhauer's view, any judgement that Beethoven is better than a mindless jingle is merely a matter of subjective taste or social habit.
(4) But Schopenhauer himself clearly treats some music as superior to others, and that evaluative stance is one of the payoffs of his account.
(5) Therefore his account of music is self-undermining.

I can't tell whether the slide is in (1) or (2), or whether the whole argument is fine and I just have to accept that Schopenhauer's evaluative talk is inconsistent with his metaphysics. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
