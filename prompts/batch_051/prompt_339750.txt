I read a long explainer in a technology magazine about proposals for keeping a superintelligent AI aligned with human interests, and one section summarized the idea of coherent extrapolated volition roughly as follows. The proposal is that instead of doing what people presently say they want, we should have the AI work out what each person would prefer if they knew all the relevant facts, thought faster and with less bias, and had more self-control, and then aggregate those idealized preferences — in effect, act on a kind of vote among everyone's better selves. Since idealization is meant to strip away the confusion behind ordinary disagreement, the hope is that these individual answers converge; if they do, CEV supplies a single well-defined target for the AI, and if they do not, the proposal offers no principled tiebreaker and falls apart.
