Consider the standard grandfather paradox: a time traveler journeys to a moment before his grandparents met and attempts to kill his grandfather. Now vary the case as follows. Each attempt is governed by a quantum trigger with an objective chance of 1/2 of succeeding and 1/2 of failing, with the outcomes independent across attempts; if an attempt fails, its failure is brute, not a jam, a slip, a misfire, or a change of heart. The traveler can arrange as many attempts as he wishes during the relevant week. Work through what the single self-consistent history view, the branching many-worlds view, and the changing-the-past hypertime view must each say about this case. In particular: what sequence of outcomes does the self-consistency view require, what probability does it assign to the grandfather's surviving twelve attempts, and is the stipulated objective chance even admissible on that view? What do the other two views predict instead? Say whether the probabilistic case still separates the three views or whether some of them collapse together, and at what number of attempts, if any, the self-consistency view incurs a distinctive explanatory cost. Be precise about the probability reasoning, state how confident you are in each part of your answer, and single out the point you are least sure of.
