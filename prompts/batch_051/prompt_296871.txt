I have been working on a view I call 'enforced persistence realism' about virtual objects and virtual harms, and I would like a frank assessment of it. The central claim is that a virtual thing is real in the relevant sense not because it resembles a physical thing or has physical effects, but because its existence and alteration are governed by processes independent of any one person's will. A screenshot does not count: nothing prevents any viewer from ignoring, editing, or deleting it. A scarce item in a server-side multiplayer game does count: no single user can alter it, and it persists across players, sessions, and the inattention of any particular observer. The corresponding thesis about harm is that a virtual harm is real precisely when it consists in the enforced loss of possibilities that the victim cannot restore by reinterpretation. On this criterion the permanent deletion of a rare item or the banning of a character is a real harm, while an insult in a dream or a defeat in a game one can restart at will is not, because nothing has been taken that cannot be restored by changing one's stance or beginning again. I suspect this captures why some virtual losses feel like genuine injuries without forcing me to treat every virtual event as equally real. Is this a coherent distinction, or am I smuggling in an unexamined notion of 'enforcement'? Does the view already exist under a nearby name? Where does it break, and what would it wrongly count as real or unreal?
