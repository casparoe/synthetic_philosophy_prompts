I keep wondering what would happen if an institution actually tried to take the thesis seriously, so here's the thought experiment I've been kicking around: imagine a university decides to make its exams open-external-storage. You can bring in any notes you want — notebooks, your phone, a laptop with all your files open — but you can't have another person or an AI answering questions for you in real time. The official justification is that if Otto's notebook is literally part of his memory, then taking it away during an exam is less like stopping a cheater and more like confiscating part of his mind. Walk me through how this would actually play out. What would students do differently in how they take, organize, and index their notes during the term? What would the exam end up actually testing, and does that drift away from what the professor thinks it's testing? Where does the policy get gamed first? Do the gaming strategies reveal anything about where the extended mind thesis needs to draw sharper lines between genuine coupled systems and mere tools?
