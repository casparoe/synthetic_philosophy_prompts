I'm chairing our local economic development committee, and next week we have to decide whether to endorse a neighborhood time-bank that issues paper "hours" for services like childcare, home repairs, and tutoring. One board member argues it's basically expanded barter—natural, need-based, exactly the sort of exchange Aristotle thought was fine. Another thinks the paper hours will inevitably be bought and sold for dollars at a discount and turn into a speculative instrument. I want a memo that uses the passage below as its philosophical test. Here's the passage from Aristotle's Politics, I.9 (1257a):

> with every article of property there is a double way of using it; both uses are related to the article itself, but not related to it in the same manner—one is peculiar to the thing and the other is not peculiar to it. Take for example a shoe—there is its wear as a shoe and there is its use as an article of exchange; for both are ways of using a shoe, inasmuch as even he that barters a shoe for money or food with the customer that wants a shoe uses it as a shoe, though not for the use peculiar to a shoe, since shoes have not come into existence for the purpose of barter.

Give the memo in these sections, with rough word counts: Summary (150), How Aristotle's distinction works here (300), The case for endorsing the time-bank (250), The case against (250), My recommendation (200). Dont include citations beyond the passage.
