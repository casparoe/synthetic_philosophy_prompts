I'm a philosophy PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on mathematical explanation in science, and I need a constructed case rather than an example drawn from the published literature. I want it built to a tight set of specifications so that it can serve as a diagnostic test for a line of argument I'm developing: the claim that one can grant the explanatory indispensability of mathematics without thereby settling the question of whether mathematical objects exist. For this to work as a test, the case has to generate real tension between two positions; I'd rather you tell me if my specifications conflict than hand me a case that fails them quietly.

Let me fix the terms I'm using. By the enhanced indispensability argument, I mean the position that runs roughly as follows: mathematics is not merely useful for formulating scientific theories; certain mathematical facts are indispensable to some of our best scientific explanations of empirical phenomena — the standard illustrations being the prime-numbered life cycles of periodical cicadas and the hexagonal structure of honeycomb — and because the mathematics is doing genuine explanatory work rather than serving as mere shorthand or bookkeeping, our best scientific practice commits us to the existence of the relevant mathematical objects. This is meant to be a stronger, explanation-based successor to the older Quinean indispensability argument, which turned on indispensable quantification within empirically successful theories rather than on explanatory role.

Please construct a one-paragraph case about a single empirical regularity in biology, ecology, geology, or the quantitative social sciences, realistic in scientific texture and suited to a non-specialist reader. It must satisfy all of the following:

(i) It is not the cicada case, not the honeycomb case, and not a trivial variant of either.

(ii) The best available explanation of the regularity cites one elementary mathematical fact — something in number theory, group theory, or geometry that can be stated in a paragraph and followed by an undergraduate. That fact must pass a strong test of explanatory indispensability: if you delete it and retain only a qualitative physical description, the explanation visibly loses content, so the mathematics is not there merely for computational convenience.

(iii) The same case must support a principled structuralist nominalist response — the response of a philosopher who holds that mathematical discourse tracks the structure of physically realized systems rather than a realm of independently existing abstract objects. This reconstruction must preserve the explanation's ontic content without committing to abstract objects, and it must not work by denying that the explanation is a good one, by vacuous rephrasing, or by quietly discarding the explanatory content required in (ii).

(iv) The case should isolate the contested link between explanatory indispensability and ontological commitment: the very feature that makes the mathematics explanatory should also be the feature the nominalist must claim to account for, so neither side can dismiss the other's verdict as off-target.

What counts as cheating: a case that stipulates the verdicts rather than generating them from the two positions, a nominalist reconstruction that amounts to changing the subject, or a mathematical fact that is really just a dressed-up cicada or honeycomb variant.

After presenting the case, walk me through the design. First, state precisely why an enhanced indispensability theorist would count it as a genuine mathematical explanation and what she would conclude about the existence of the relevant objects. Second, give the structuralist nominalist's reconstruction and why it is supposed to preserve the explanatory content while blocking that conclusion. Third, do the design checks: identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work of keeping both verdicts live; say what would break for each side if that feature were weakened or removed; and give the strongest reply an enhanced indispensability theorist could make against the nominalist reconstruction. Finally, separate what is settled in this area from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of mathematics, and please do not present a contested reading of the enhanced indispensability argument or of the structuralist nominalist response as if it were the consensus view.
