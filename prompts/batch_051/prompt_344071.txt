I'm a political science PhD student (I work on conceptual debates about political violence) and I have a side job coding conflict events for a dataset covering a big chunk of South and Southeast Asia; between us we code a few hundred reports a week. My PI just noticed how badly the 'terrorism' tag diverges across coders and told me to write the protocol for it. The codebook currently defines terrorism roughly as political violence by a non-state actor against noncombatants, meant to terrorize a wider audience. In practice the same incident gets tagged differently depending on who reads the report and how they feel about the group, and the data feeds papers making claims about civilian targeting, so this isn't just internal bookkeeping.

Please write me a decision procedure I can actually hand to our coders — mostly masters students with an hour of training and about five mintues per incident, working from one short news report that often only gives one side. It should be a checklist or flowchart in words: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the step where the procedure says "not terrorism" and what to tag instead, and the step where it stops and pushes the call to a human because it's a judgment rather than a fact. No citations needed and my PI will add those; they have to sign off on the protocol and then defend it to the funders, so it has to be justifiable fast.

I have a hunch about what's going wrong, but I don't want you to just adopt it if it's wrong. My sense is that coders are quietly running three different tests and mashing them together: who did it (is the actor non-state?), who it hit (are the targets noncombatants?), and what it was for (is the aim to coerce or intimidate an audience beyond the immediate victims?). When an incident passes two of three, they improvise. If you split the tests, tell me what to do with attacks that pass two out of three, and if you think the three tests are the wrong hinges, give me the right ones.

Then run the procedure on these four, saying exactly which step you reach and what the final tag is:

1. A separatist group detonates a car bomb at a midday produce market, killing three civilians. A statement afterward says the attack is what the occupation brings to its own people.
2. The same group ambushes a military supply convoy on a rural road, killing four soldiers, with no civilians present and no statement issued.
3. A government air force bombs a residential neighborhood at night, killing five family members; the government says insurgents had used the area and calls the strike counterterrorism.
4. Someone sets fire to an empty agricultural biotechnology lab at night, causing major damage but no injuries. A note left at the scene says this is only the beginning; abandon the work or else.

Two more requests. One: I've watched coders drift toward tagging based on whether they think the cause is just, and I want the procedure to state explicitly where the coder's view of the cause must be set aside, in a way that can actually be enforced by a second coder reviewing the file. Two: tell me honestly which part of applying the word terrorism no protocol can settle, no matter how well written — and whether that leftover is definitional, evidential, or moral.
