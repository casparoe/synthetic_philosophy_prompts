I've gotten a bit obsessed with reputation systems that work like staked collateral rather than like leaving stars on someone's profile, and I want to use you as a sounding board for a counterfactual. Suppose that from next January, a country of about ten million adults scraps credit checks, landlord references and criminal-record checks for three things only: renting a home, working in care or finance, and taking out a small-business loan. In their place the government runs a public vouch ledger. Every adult is seeded with 25 vouches. To rent you need 30 vouches lodged against the lease; to get a care-sector job you need 60 lodged with the employer; to borrow for a small business you need 150 lodged with the lender. A lodged vouch is held until the contract finishes, then returns to the person who gave it. If you default, seriously breach the lease, or get dismissed for gross misconduct within three years, the lodged vouches are wiped out and the people who vouched for you can't lodge any more for a year. Vouches are fully transferable before they're lodged, every adult's balance and history of who-vouched-for-whom is public, and the state doesn't mint new vouches except a 25-vouch grant on each person's eighteenth birthday.

Predict, with reasoning, how this economy looks twenty years on. I don't want a story; I want the mechanics. Specifically: where do prices for vouches come from and what do they settle at; who ends up vouch-rich and vouch-poor; what new professions or scams crystalise around the ledger (brokers, vouch farms, insurance, laundering through shell people?); whether defaults and sackings actually fall or just get better hidden; and what it does to people with weak or bad networks — migrants, ex-prisoners, someone trying to escape a slum landlord or a horrible job. Please distinguish the predictions you're genuinely confident in from the ones that hinge on assumptions like how well the state can police fake lodges or how thick the resale market gets, and finish by saying what single change would be most likely to make the whole system collapse.

Oh, and British English please.
