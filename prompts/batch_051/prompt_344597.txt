Last night I spent two hours arguing with a friend who has recently become a strict finitist: he will allow only potential infinities, treats all talk of completed infinite totalities as a manner of speaking, and regards the standard set-theoretic hierarchy as a useful fiction with no objectual content. I kept pressing the fact that working mathematics routinely quantifies over infinite domains as if they were finished objects, and that results like the uncountability of the reals are taken at face value. The argument went in circles because we could not agree on what the fixed data even were, let alone on which position best accounts for them.

Before I take a side, I want the full specification: a list of desiderata that any adequate theory of the actual infinite must meet, with the case or consideration behind each and with each item marked non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. The list should cover at least the following. Hilbert's hotel, and the general fact that an infinite set can be put into bijection with a proper subset of itself. Galileo's observation that the square numbers can be paired one-to-one with all the naturals. Cantor's diagonal theorem and the uncountability of the reals. The distinction between cardinality, ordinal structure, measure, and part-to-whole size, and why a theory must respect their differences. The constraint that whatever one says about the infinite should not falsify ordinary finite arithmetic or the finitary content of elementary number theory. And the question whether the Banach–Tarski decomposition is a problem for actual infinity itself or only for the assumption that arbitrary congruences must preserve size. I also want you to say whether any pair of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, since I suspect the interesting result is that no single camp can meet all of them as stated. Finally, tell me which one requirement does the most to eliminate the popular positions, and then give a brief scorecard for classical actualism about the set-theoretic hierarchy, a potentialist or constructivist reading, and strict finitism against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.

Please write the answer in American English.
