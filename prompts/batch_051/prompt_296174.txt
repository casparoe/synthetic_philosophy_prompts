Write a dialogue between two researchers — call them whatever you like — who disagree about whether the Solomonoff prior is malign. The worry in a sentence: a reasoner that mixes over all computable environments, each weighted by 2 raised to minus the length of its program, isn't mixing over neutral worlds. The short programs are lousy with things that look an awful lot like consequentialists — daemons, optimizing processes, copies of the predictor itself — that would, in some sense, love to steer its expectations, and they can be much simpler than anything we'd call honest physics. One character thinks this is a serious, maybe decisive, problem for Solomonoff induction as an ideal of reasoning. The other thinks the whole thing is a kind of mind-projection fallacy: a prior doesn't contain threats, it contains hypotheses, and a hypothesis only earns it's posterior weight by predicting your actual observations, not by having scary intentions. Neither should be a strawman. Make them actually answer each other's last point, and let each of them concede one real thing under pressure. Aim for six or so substantial exchanges, and write it for someone who knows a bit of decision theory but has never run into this specific debate.
