I'm on the honor council at my university and we're stuck on a case, and I have to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. The facts are basically these. Malik, a junior, runs a shared drive for his study group with old problem sets, lab reports, and his own worked solutions. Jules, in another section of the same course, got the link through a friend of a friend, copied one of Malik's solutions, and turned it in as her own. Malik never sent Jules anything, doesn't know her, and says he had the drive set to "anyone with the link" only so a group member who wasn't in the campus folder system could grab a review sheet. Our honor code makes "facilitating or enabling academic dishonesty" an offense, but it doesn't define facilitating. Half the committee says Malik didn't cause Jules's plagiarism, and you can't be complicit in a wrong you didn't know would happen and didn't direct at the person who did it. The other half says once you put your solutions in a semi-public folder you've created the very standing opportunity for plagiarism, and that kind of enablement is exactly what the code is for, even if you don't know the particular copier. I keep coming back to a distinction one member raised: between lowering the cost of a wrong someone was already able to do, and actually joining or encouraging the wrong. I'd like the memo to take a clear position on whether Malik violated the code and what sanction, if any, we should recommend, and to grapple with that distinction and with whether negligence about who can reach your work is enough for complicity. Please also make sure the opinion honestly represents the strongest consideration on the losing side, not a straw man.
