I'm a novelist working on a near-future novel set in a state that's just legalized cannabis, and I want the legalization mechanism to feel plausible enough that a reader who thinks about policy for five minutes doesn't immediately call bullshit. The scheme I've drafted is a kind of cap-and-trade for personal consumption. Every adult gets a monthly allowance of, say, 600 units, where one unit is defined as 10 mg of THC-equivalent: a 10 mg edible costs one unit, a gram of 20% flower costs roughly twenty units, and so on. Purchases are debited at the register through a state app, but units are freely transferable between adults — you can sell them, give them away, whatever. Possession itself is never penalized; selling without a license is the only criminal offense left, and home cultivation up to four plants is legal and doesn't count against your allowance unless you sell or share it. Licensed shops can sell to any adult as long as the buyer has the units, with no per-shop or per-day cap beyond that.

I'm less interested in whether this is a good idea than in how it would actually run. Who ends up holding the units, and at what price? What secondary market forms, and does it just become a subsidy from heavy users to light users who sell their allowances? How do shops and buyers game the flower-to-edible conversion — mislabeled potency, products that carry units along with them, or something I haven't thought of? Where does legal supply leak to minors or across the state line, and can enforcement tell a gifted unit from a sold one once the allowance is transferable? And wether the scheme can be patched without just collapsing into plain legalization or a black market with extra steps — I want the boring mechanics, not the moral.
