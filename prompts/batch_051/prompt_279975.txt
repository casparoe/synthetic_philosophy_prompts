I'm a software engineer and there's this asymmetry I can't stop noticing. We write binding contracts to pin down behavior in futures that mostly won't happen — prenups, wills, escrow agreements, severance deals, noncompetes. But I work with people who are convinced AGI is plausibly ten or fifteen years away; they'll spend real money on that belief, refuse to work on certain projects because of it, and still none of them has ever tried to write any kind of enforceable agreement about the world after AGI actually exists. Nothing about who gets control of the big compute clusters if one of them ends up running one, what values they'd have to respect, what they'd owe the rest of us. If the odds of my marriage failing are lower than there stated odds on AGI, why does a prenup feel sensible and a pre-AGI contract feel like a joke? Is that difference actually justified, or is it just that the far future feels fake? And if you had to draft one pre-AGI bargain a sane person would actually sign, what would it look like?
