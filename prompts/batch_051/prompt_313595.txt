Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated political philosophers who disagree about whether the problem of dirty hands in politics involves a genuine moral remainder. The first defends the view that a leader can face a situation in which every available option violates a serious moral requirement, and that the right choice can therefore leave an unfulfilled obligation: a real wrong that is not erased by the justifying reasons and that calls for acknowledgment, regret, and perhaps atonement. The second rejects this. On the second view, an overridden requirement no longer makes the act wrong, and the sense of leftover wrongdoing is at most a psychological residue or a form of agent-regret about one's causal role in a bad outcome, not the trace of a genuine unfulfilled obligation. Have them respond to each other's actual arguments, test the hardest cases (for example, authorizing torture, deceiving the public, or sacrificing some to save others), and revise their positions as the other lands objections. Neither should be a strawman, and the dialogue should end by pinpointing the exact source of the disagreement rather than resolving it. Please write in American English.
