My mate and I stayed up far too late last night having a long arguement about whether Dawkins and Williams are actually right that the gene is the unit of selection, or whether that's just a tidy bookkeeping convention that breaks down once you look at things like meiotic drive, transposons, and group-level effects. I run a small philosophy of biology blog and I want to turn this into a "which unit of selection do you actually hold?" quiz for my readers. Could you write it for me?

I'd like eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete evolutionary case with three answer options. Cases I'd like included at least: a sterile worker honeybee; a mouse element that ends up in more than its fair share of sperm but makes heterozygous males less fertile; a bird population that stays below the local carrying capacity in a way that seems good for the group; a transposable element spreading through a genome; a gene variant that helps early in life and harms later; and an early cancer where one cell lineage outcompetes others inside the body. Add a couple more if you think they'd sharpen the sorting.

The scoring key should map answer patterns to at least four positions: the strict gene's-eye replicator view; multi-level selection where groups can be genuine units; a replicator–interactor pluralism where genes replicate but selection acts on organisms (and sometimes groups); and a more developmental/process view that says the search for "the" unit of selection is a mistake. If some combinations of answers correspond to a position nobody actually holds, flag that and say whether it's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And please don't include any question that just asks people whether they think group selection happens or whether they're a Dawkinsian - the point is to sort them by what they say about the cases, not by the label they walked in wearing.

Put the scoring key in a table, and could you write the whole thing in British English? My readers are mostly in the UK and I can't be bothered to change -ize to -ise afterwards.
