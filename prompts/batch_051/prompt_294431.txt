write me a Onion-style news article, headline plus about 500 words, about a new strip-mall storefront called Precommitment Warehouse. The store sells "commitment locks": for a small administrative fee, it will make some future choice of yours binding, so you can't backslide—the marketing says it's for people who want to stick to a gym or a phone plan. The joke that should never be stated directly is that the store is a money pump. The customers are people with cyclic prefernces over three bundles (a phone plan, a warehouse membership, and a lawn-care subscription, say), each pair of bundles ordered in a strict preference cycle, and they pay a fee every time they trade around the loop. The piece should stay deadpan, and the humor has to come from the actual mechanics: at each pairwise swap the customer genuinely prefers the new bundle, nobody's being lied to, the cycle keeps running because of a wedge between willingness-to-pay and willingness-to-accept and because the per-trade fee is small enough not to break any single trade but large enough to matter over a loop. Include a customer who is a sophisticated chooser and signs up for a precommitment device to stop the cycle, only to find that choosing between precommitment devices is itself a new cycle he can be pumped on. Include a quote from the founder, earnestly explaining that the store "just makes your own revealed preferences binding, so your later selves can't contradict the you you really are," and a quote from a bemused local economist who says the customers are leaving with exactly the same bundle and less money, but who never uses the phrase "money pump." Don't explain the concept anywhere; let the article act it out.
