I have been working through the presentism–eternalism dispute in the philosophy of time, and I am struck by how long it has persisted among philosophers who largely accept the same physics. Presentists maintain that only the present moment and its contents exist: the past is gone and the future is not yet real. Eternalists maintain that past, present and future are all equally real, and that the apparent flow of time arises from the situation of an observer within a four-dimensional block rather than from any objective passage. Both camps engage with special relativity at length. Eternalists usually say that the relativity of simultaneity fits a block universe naturally, while presentists either dispute the metaphysical significance of relativity or try to recover a privileged global present by other means. The two sides also trade familiar objections: presentists charge that eternalism renders change unreal and cannot make sense of the felt openness of the future, while eternalists charge that presentism cannot account for truths about the past or for relations such as causation that link what exists now to what no longer exists.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not empirical, and perhaps not even directly about what there is. It may be about what the word "exists" is supposed to do in a metaphysical theory — about whether existence is a univocal notion we all already grasp, or a theoretical posit whose criteria are themselves among the things in dispute. But I suspect that diagnosis is too glib, since both sides talk as if they assert contradictory claims about one and the same world rather than choosing different conceptual schemes.

I would like a diagnosis of the structure of this disagreement, not a survey of the positions. Specifically: is the dispute verbal, methodological, or substantive; do the two sides differ about what counts as data, about standards of theory choice, or about which starting intuitions may be used as evidence; and is there any development — a distinction, an argument, a result from physics — that could settle the debate or at least reframe it so the disagreement becomes tractable? If you think no such progress should be expected, please say why and what that implies about what the participants are actually doing. Please write your answer in British English.
