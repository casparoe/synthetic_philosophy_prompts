Not a linguist, just someone who's wound up in too many online arguments about words. People keep reaching for some version of "a word's original meaning is its real meaning." I've seen it used to insist that "decimate" can only mean punishing one soldier in ten, that you can't use "literally" for emphasis because it originally meant, well, literally, and that "sinister" is bad to use for evil because it originally just meant left-handed. These feel like different moves to me -- sometimes it's a claim about what the word still means, sometimes about what the word secretly implies, sometimes about what we're allowed to say. Could you pull the distinct precise claims out of that one slogan? Tell me which of them are trivially true, which are actually false, which are defensible only in a narrow way, and which reading is doing the work when someone says a current use of a word is wrong because of where the word came from?
