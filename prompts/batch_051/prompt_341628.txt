I’m a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I need a thought experiment built to order. I want a single concrete system whose behavior forces two rival research programs about intelligence apart in a specified direction. Let the first view define intelligence as the capacity to improve at a task over time by extracting predictive regularities from feedback; call this the improvement view. Let the second view define intelligence as the capacity to form and use a causal model of the environment that supports accurate predictions under interventions, not just under passively observed distributions; call this the causal-model view. Now construct a scenario in which a system scores high on the improvement view and low on the causal-model view. The system should be a forecasting or control system embedded in a specific real-world setting such as a hospital, a supply chain, a power grid, or something similarly concrete. Constraints: no appeal to consciousness, human-like understanding, or a helpful designer who secretly supplies the causal model. The system’s improvement must be explainable entirely by mechanisms that a defender of the causal-model view would regard as inferential shortcuts—statistical pattern completion, calibration against outcomes, associative memory, or something similar. After laying out the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification: spell out exactly which behavioral evidence satisfies the improvement criterion, exactly which intervention-based test exposes the absence of a causal model, and why the improvement criterion does not detect the failure. Then isolate the structural feature of the scenario that makes the two verdicts diverge, and say what would happen if that feature were removed, and how a defender of the causal-model view would attack the case. Finish by answering this: does the revised scenario show that the two views are tracking different phenomena, or can the disagreement be dissolved by tightening what counts as using a model?
