I’m teaching Locke’s Some Thoughts Concerning Education tomorrow and want a case I can use to push the students. Construct a thought experiment—realistic, set in an 18th-century gentry household, no more than a paragraph—where a Lockean tutor has to choose between two of Locke’s own prescriptions that pull in opposite directions: on one side, treating children as rational creatures and reasoning with them, and on the other, forming habits through repeated practice and esteem/disgrace rather than relying on rules or explanation. The scenario should make it so that stopping to reason with the child would actively undermine the habit the tutor is trying to produce, so the two methods genuinely conflict. Then walk through what a consistent Lockean should do, identify which feature of the case does the work, say what happens if you remove the habit-formation goal, and end with how a defender of the “reason with children” reading would attack the case.
