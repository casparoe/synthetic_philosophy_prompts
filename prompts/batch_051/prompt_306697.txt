I have become convinced that heterophenomenology is the only viable starting point for a science of consciousness, and I want to know how confident I should be in that conviction. The version I hold has three commitments: that first-person reports should be treated as the raw data, but only as reports — as representations to be interpreted with the intentional stance — rather than as transparent windows onto consciousness; that the method must remain neutral about the ultimate ontology of what the reports seem to say exists; and that this neutrality is what allows the resulting science to proceed without settling the hard problem in advance. My worry is that the position may have been so heavily qualified over the years that it no longer risks anything: it may be compatible with every conceivable pattern of verbal behavior, and therefore with no particular empirical result at all. I would like you to take the position at its strongest, play it straight, and tell me what would count against it.

I am not interested only in the familiar reply that the method ignores actual first-person authority or the felt character of experience. I want a map of the position's real exposure. Specifically: what possible observations, experimental results, or conceptual developments would force a rational heterophenomenologist to amend the method, shrink its scope, or abandon it? Which already documented phenomena — such as split-brain confabulation, blindsight commentaries, aphasic report, anosognosia, or cross-cultural variability in introspection — come closest to doing that, if any? And which putative defeaters would defenders classify as mere noise, because the method's neutrality was never supposed to settle them?

Please answer in five named sections: 1. The position as you understand it, in about three sentences. 2. Candidate defeaters, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, with roughly a paragraph on each, around 400 words total. 3. What a committed defender should concede, around 200 words. 4. What a defender should dismiss, around 150 words. 5. Insulation and whether it matters: your honest assessment of whether heterophenomenology has become unfalsifiable and whether that matters for a methodological position of this kind, around 200 words.
