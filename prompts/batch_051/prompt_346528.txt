I'm a novelist, currently working on a book about a translator who starts believing the words do the seeing for her, so I've been reading a lot of Rorty. In Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity there's a passage early in the chapter on the contingency of language where he argues, roughly, that the world can cause us to have beliefs once we have a vocabulary, but it cannot itself propose or prefer a vocabulary. The world doesn't come with its own labels; truth is a property of sentences, and sentences are human tools. I won't get the wording exact, but that's the move that stuck with me.

I ended up in a long on-and-off argument with a friend who works in philosophy of mind. He thinks Rorty is just confused about representation: some vocabularies cut nature at its joints, and saying science works because of something other than getting those joints right is an evasion. I keep noticing that both sides agree about the everyday facts—science makes predictions, some descriptions work better for some purposes, ordinary people call things true all the time. The disagreement seems to be about whether "works better" is all there is or whether it tracks something extra. When I push my friend to say what the extra thing is, he reaches for phrases like "mind-independent structure" and then gets annoyed. When he pushes me on whether I really think geology didn't have a preferred vocabulary before there were people, I say that's a bad question, but I cant fully say why without sounding like I'm dodging.

This debate has been stalled for a long time, and I don't think it's because either side is stupid. I want a diagnosis of the stall. Is it a verbal dispute about the word "true" or "representation"? Are the sides answering different questions—one about causal explanation, one about justification? Is there a hidden factual or formal question underneath, maybe about whether perception has nonconceptual content? Or is the impasse a sign that the question is ill-posed? If there is real progress to be had, what would count: a distinction, an experiment, a change of question? And if not, what's the honest description of what the two camps are doing?
