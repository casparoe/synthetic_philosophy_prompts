Say a pain clinic starts scoring every patient on two 0–10 scales: one for how intense the pain feels, and one for how much it bothers them, and it triages by the bother score, with intensity used only to break ties. The idea comes out of pain asymbolia, where people report the sensation as strong but insist it genuinely doesn't bother them. Walk through how this system would actually go: what patients would learn to say to be seen sooner, what nurses and doctors would do with the numbers, which kinds of patients it would systematically fast-track or strand, and whether the whole scheme would just collapse back into a single intensity scale because people can't keep the two questions apart. And say what the failure or survival of the system would tell us about whether the badness of pain is a separate component or just another measure of how much pain there is.
