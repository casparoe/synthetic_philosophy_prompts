I've been reading alignment debates for years, and I have a policy for my own takes, not just other people's. I dont let myself endorse any procedure for deciding whose values an aligned AI should serve unless I can name a concrete value I actually hold that the procedure would, in some realistic case, override — not something I only claim to care about, but something where I'd viscerally feel the loss and still have to say the procedure was right. If I can't produce one, I treat my support for the procedure as my own values wearing a neutral mask, and I stop arguing for it.

The test is starting to bite. Last week I was defending an aggregation scheme — roughly, take everyone's suitably-informed preferences and maximize their satisfaction — and when I ran my own test I couldn't confidently point to one of my own preferences that I'd be willing to see lose. So I went quiet mid-thread, and a couple of people said I was being performative, or that a neutral procedure doesn't need to hurt me personally to be neutral. I think that's probably true in principle, but I also don't see how else to catch myself when I've dressed up my own values as the obvious output of an impartial procedure.

Is the policy defensible, or is it a bias with a nice name? Is there a restated version that keeps the self-check without requiring me to have a personal sacrifice case ready every time? I'm honestly not after a confident verdict — more whether the habit tracks a real distinction or is just a guilt ritual. And if the whole question leans on a confused idea of what it is for a procedure to be neutral between values, I'd rather you say that plainly than help me patch the policy.
