I'm in a political science seminar on decision-making in foreign policy and we keep circling back to this slogan people repeat: that when policymakers reach for Munich, or warn about "another Vietnam," the analogy is doing the thinking and they're just along for the ride. I find that claim wierdly hard to pin down. It clearly can't mean analogies always mislead — sometimes matching a new situation to an old one is just decent pattern recognition. But the narrower claim, "the Munich analogy specifically misleads," feels close to unfalsifiable: every time it's invoked badly, somebody says see, bad analogy; every time it seems to work, somebody else says it wasn't really the analogy doing the work.

The Thucydides trap makes this sharper for me: it's presented as if it has predictive content, but it's also vague enough that people can retrofit it onto any conflict between a rising and a ruling power.
