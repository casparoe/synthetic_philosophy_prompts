I am giving a seminar paper defending the claim that the standard multiple realizability argument against psychophysical type-identity is overrated: actual cross-species variation in the physical basis of pain shows only that a mental kind need not be realized by a single uniform physical property, not that mental kinds fail to be physical kinds. My move is to grant all the familiar cases—octopus pain, silicon replacements, alien minds—and argue that the antireductionist conclusion rests on an unnoticed semantic premise, namely that a mental-kind term must answer to a single physical profile. The audience is a philosophy of mind reading group: mostly functionalists who regard multiple realizability as settled, one committed type physicalist who will think my disjunctive move is a retreat, and two cognitive neuroscientists who will judge everything against actual cross-species neural data. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections this room will actually raise, phrased the way those people would phrase them, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is weakest, and include the question I most hope nobody asks. Keep the full prep sheet under 1,000 words.
