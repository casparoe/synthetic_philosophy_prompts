I'm a junior helping to run my school's philosophy club, and I've been put in charge of designing a short sorting activity for our next meeting, which is on hedonism and the problem of what pleasures have in common. The meeting will be framed around the standard disagreement between Bentham and Mill about whether pleasures differ only in quantity or also in quality, but I want the activity to dig underneath that disagreement: if the pleasure of a hot shower, the satisfaction of finally understanding a proof, the relief that a feared outcome didn't happen, the enjoyment of a sad song, and the pleasure someone takes in an activity they also find painful all count as pleasures, it's not at all obvious that they share a single felt quality, or even a single psychological role. I'd like you to write a "which theory of pleasure do you actually hold?" quiz for the club members.

Please make it eight to ten questions, each built around a short concrete case like the ones I listed, with two or three answer options per question that ask what the respondent wants to say about the case rather than asking them to pick a label. Include a scoring key that sorts answer patterns into the main positions on the question: the distinct-felt-quality view, attitudinal views, functional or relief views, pluralism, and a skeptical or eliminativist view. Build the quiz from the space of possible answers rather than starting from the names of positions: identify the questions on which the positions genuinely come apart, and have the scoring key show which combinations of answers map to which position. Where a logically possible combination of answers has no named defender, note it and say briefly whether it seems incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any two positions the key cannot reliably separate and say what additional question would separate them. The audience is high school students with no formal philosophy background, so the case questions should avoid technical vocabulary; the scoring key itself can name the positions and explain them briefly for the club officers.
