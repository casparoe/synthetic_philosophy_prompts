I cover the foundations of physics for a general-interest science magazine and am preparing a long piece on whether the quantum wavefunction describes the world itself or merely summarizes what an observer knows. The two camps I want to represent fairly are the ψ-ontic view, which takes the wavefunction to be part of the physical furniture of reality—distinct quantum states corresponding to distinct underlying states of affairs—and the ψ-epistemic view, which takes the wavefunction to be a state of knowledge or information about a deeper ontic state, so that two different wavefunctions can in principle be consistent with one and the same underlying state of affairs.

Please draft the joint statement such an adversarial collaboration would produce, a document both camps could sign. Begin with a numbered section of claims both camps accept, worded precisely enough that neither side later objects to the wording. This section should include an agreed account of what the standard no-go results do and do not establish: Bell's theorem, which rules out local hidden-variable completions, and the Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph theorem, which purports to rule out ψ-epistemic models under the assumption of preparation independence, the assumption that independently prepared systems have uncorrelated ontic states. Both sides should be able to sign the statement of what each result shows given its assumptions, and of why the ψ-epistemic camp takes the preparation-independence assumption to be the disputed step.

Then include a disagreements table. Each row should isolate one residual disagreement, with the ψ-ontic formulation and the ψ-epistemic formulation placed side by side, each worded so its own side would accept it as fair. Where an apparent disagreement turns out to be verbal, say so rather than letting it occupy a row. After the table, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete argument, case, or in-principle experiment that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it would show. The standard for success is that a committed ψ-ontic Bohmian and a committed ψ-epistemic QBist could each sign the document without wincing; if either side would disown a sentence, rewrite it.
