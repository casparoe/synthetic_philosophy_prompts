Alright, here's the setup. I'm writing a funding proposal for a small private foundation — the person behind it made their money off a GPL-licensed project and is now convinced the biggest unresolved problem in software freedom isn't technical but conceptual: nobody can say with any precision where one program ends and another begins, so derivate-work obligations end up resting on vibes. They want to fund maybe six people for two years, philosophers and legal theorists only, no empirical work and no digging through caselaw, just getting the concepts straight. I want you to act as an expert on the philosophy of software and intellectual property, think carefully, and hand me a defended agenda: which open conceptual questions in this area should this program prioritize, and in what order?

Start by brainstorming a longer list of candidate questions — e.g., whether the boundary should track textual inclusion, compile-time linking, runtime communication, shared data structures, or something else entirely; whether a plugin or dynamically linked library is one program or two; whether a clean-room reimplementation is a derivative in any philosophically interesting sense; whether trained model weights that have absorbed GPL code thereby create a derivative. Then cut it down to the four or five questions that matter most and rank them. For each one, say why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it actually is for philosophers rather than programmers or lawyers, and what would count as progress or a reason to demote it over the two-year window. I'm less interested in the list than in the reasoning behind the ordering.
