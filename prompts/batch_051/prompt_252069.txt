I'm putting together a handout for an EA-adjacent reading group session on repugnant markets and commodification, and I want a sorting instrument that places people by what they actually say about cases rather than by which label they recognize. Please write a decision tree with six to eight diagnostic questions that sorts a respondent into one of the following five positions, none of which should be named anywhere in the questions themselves:

- Anti-commodification: some exchanges are wrong because pricing the good expresses and entrenches a corrupting valuation of it, even with fully informed consent and no material harm to third parties.
- Background-injustice: the main objection to these markets is that they exploit preexisting deprivation or inequality; once background conditions are just, most such exchanges become permissible.
- Market-permissiveness: consenting adults may buy and sell anything they may do for free, and repugnance is a taste or bias that should not constrain markets.
- Repugnance-as-constraint: repugnance is a real social fact that market designers must respect or route around, irrespective of whether it is morally justified.
- Social-meaning externalities: the transaction is wrong when it shifts the social meaning of a good for the worse in ways that affect outsiders, not when it only harms the direct participants.

Build the tree from the distinctions first—for example, whether the wrong is located in the terms of the exchange, in its material effects on outsiders, in its expressive effects on outsiders, or only in unjust background conditions—and then locate the five positions in the resulting logical space. Each node should be a concrete case or claim someone can answer without already knowing which leaf it points toward. You must include at least these four cases: selling a kidney to a stranger, a hardware store tripling the price of generators after a hurricane, selling one's vote, and paying to jump the queue in a public hospital emergency room. Flag any combinations of answers that no listed position occupies and briefly say whether each gap is there because the combination is incoherent or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of positions your questions cannot cleanly separate, and give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
