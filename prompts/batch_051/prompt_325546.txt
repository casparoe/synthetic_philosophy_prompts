I’m taking an intermediate game theory course and keep getting tripped up by iterated elimination of dominated strategies once mixed strategies are allowed. My instructor gave us a handout that says, more or less: in a finite game, iteratively deleting strictly dominated strategies yields a unique surviving set no matter what order you delete in, and this stays true when you let pure strategies be dominated by mixed strategies. The handout then claims that allowing mixed strategies as dominators doesn’t actually add any new deletions beyond pure-strategy dominance: if a pure strategy is strictly dominated by some mixed strategy, it must already be strictly dominated by one of the pure strategies in the mix’s support. So, for purposes of iterated strict dominance, checking pure dominators is enough, and the set you’re left with is independent of whether you allow mixed dominance. I’m espcially suspicious of the support claim, since I thought the point of mixed dominance was to catch things pure dominance misses, but I’m not sure. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what’s accurate, what’s a common simplifying fiction, and what’s just wrong, and say whether the final conclusion follows from the accurate parts.
