My brother and I inherited our father's machine shop; I keep the books and he runs the floor. For months he has pressed me to sell him my half at a price far below what our accountant says it is worth, and he has said plainly that if I refuse, he will do his best to make the business fail. I assumed he was bluffing, since he depends on the shop for his living and has put his name on its reputation. But he has now missed payments to our largest supplier, told two long-standing clients that orders may not be filled, and signed a personal guarantee that will push him into bankruptcy if the shop misses its next lease payment. When I confronted him, he said that was the point: he has made backing down more expensive than seeing the matter through, so I should stop waiting for him to come to his senses. He added that the missed payments have triggered penalty clauses that will escalate on their own, so the situation is no longer fully in either of our control. He has also done this once before — in a dispute with a former partner, he followed through on a costly threat and lost a year of income rather than back down. I can either wait him out, hoping the mounting costs eventually break his resolve, or sell at his price. A friend of mine argues that a threat which damages the person making it is not a credible negotiating tactic, and that I should therefore call his bluff; but my brother is not behaving like someone who is bluffing. How should I think about this, and what should I actually do?
