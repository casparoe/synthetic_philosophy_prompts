I am a novelist, and for years I have worked almost entirely within the vocabulary of narrative craft: protagonist and antagonist, agency and obstacle, stakes and turning points, want and need, arc and payoff, and the question of what makes an ending feel just or hollow. Lately I have noticed that public language about grit, fortitude, and resilience seems to be doing something I recognize from the inside. It names certain figures as protagonists, treats the forces arrayed against them as scenery rather than as antagonists, and asks the audience to be satisfied by a climax in which the protagonist has changed rather than by one in which the obstacle has been removed. The endurance itself becomes the virtue, and the story's justice consists in surviving what should not have to be survived. I can see a version of this that is admirable and a version that is ideological; I do not trust my own sense of which is which, because my craft may be predisposed to make endurance meaningful even when it is not.

What I would like is a systematic translation. Please restate the debate over whether grit, fortitude, and resilience are genuine virtues or an ideology — the view that they are character strengths we should admire and cultivate, and the view that praising them individualizes structural failure, depoliticizes suffering, and blames people for failing to endure their own exploitation — entirely in the vocabulary of narrative craft. Take each position one at a time, and also translate how each position diagnoses the other's error. Then give me a residue report: which claims map cleanly onto concepts like agency, obstacle, arc, narrative payoff, and narrative justice; which claims can be made to fit only if I quietly change the meaning of my own terms; and which claims have no narrative counterpart at all. Finally, tell me whether the residue points to something the philosophical debate is missing, or something the narrative framework is blind to — and whether my suspicion that my craft already contains this argument, in disguise, is sound.
