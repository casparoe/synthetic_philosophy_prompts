So I've been messing around with an idea for a "thinking company" and I want someone to tell me where it breaks. You submit a hard question—anything from "should our town repair the old bridge or build a new one" to "is my business idea actually good." The company doesn't have any geniuses on staff. The top worker's whole job is to split the question into smaller questions; each smaller question has to be genuinely smaller than the one it came from, not just a restatement. Each one goes to a new worker with the same rule: if it can be answered by one ordinary person in under ten minutes using only stuff they already know—no googling, no phone calls—they answer it; otherwise they split it further. Answers then get passed back up and combined into one final answer, written by the top worker. Nobody below the top ever sees the whole original question, just there piece and a one-line note about what its for. Pay is piece-rate: workers get a small flat fee per question they answer themselves, and splitters get a flat fee per subquestion they create. Walk me through how this would actually behave. What do people start doing to game it? Do the answers get better or worse than just hiring one smart freelancer? What does the "no research" rule do? And are the obvious fixes actual fixes or do they just move the failure somewhere else?
