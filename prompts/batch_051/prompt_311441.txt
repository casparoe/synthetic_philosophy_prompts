I am a machine learning research engineer with no formal philosophy background. I work daily with attention mechanisms, sequence models, information bottlenecks, gating and routing, modular architectures, credit assignment, and self-supervised objectives. I want you to translate a philosophical debate into that vocabulary — and then tell me what the translation drops.

The debate is which animals are conscious and what principle should draw the line among them. As I understand it, there are at least three styles of answer. Global workspace theories say a state becomes conscious when it is entered into a limited-capacity workspace and broadcast to many specialized consumer systems; an animal is conscious to the extent that its nervous system has something like that broadcast stage — an attention bottleneck through which selected representations are shared across otherwise independent modules. Integrated information theories say consciousness is not a wiring diagram but a quantity: the integrated information, usually written Φ, that a system generates, which is high when the whole has information that cannot be decomposed into its parts. On this view consciousness is graded and wide, because any system with nonzero Φ has at least a trace of experience, and line-drawing becomes threshold-setting rather than architecture-hunting. Higher-order theories say a state is conscious only when the organism represents itself as being in that state — a second-order monitoring relation or self-model, not merely first-order processing. If any of those thumbnails is off, correct it as you go; I care about the translation, not exegesis for its own sake.

Restate each of these three positions, and the disagreement among them, in the vocabulary I actually use. Translate into architecture and information-processing claims, not metaphors: attention bottlenecks, shared workspace buffers, gating or routing, self-models or meta-networks, modularity versus integration, mutual information or effective information, credit assignment, training objectives, or whatever else in the toolkit is apt. Be precise about which parts of each theory are functional claims and which are not.

Then apply the translated positions to the contested part of the animal kingdom — bony fish, cephalopods, and insects — and say where each translated view draws its line and why.

Finally, the part I most want: a residue report. For each position, say what translated cleanly, what had to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Pay particular attention to whatever seems to do the philosophical work: the difference between being functionally integrated in the way a workspace or a Φ calculation requires and having a point of view, and the difference between a quantity and the claim that an organism for which that quantity is high also has subjective experience. I want to know whether my standing suspicion — that the dispute is ultimately just about architectures and integration measures — is right, or whether the part my vocabulary cannot express is the part that matters.
