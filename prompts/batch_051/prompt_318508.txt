I teach at a secondary school in a small highland town. Most families here keep a few animals, and the nearest city with a reliable supermarket is a full day's bus ride away. Over the past few months I have read a good deal of philosophy on the ethics of eating animals, and I have become persuaded that animal suffering is morally serious and that I should not pay for it when I have a genuine alternative. But when I try to work out what this means in my actual circumstances, the line between having an alternative and not having one keeps dissolving.

The local market sells dairy, eggs, and meat from animals raised on nearby smallholdings. The vegetables and legumes sold in town are irregular, expensive, and mostly imported; the fortified plant-based products I read about online are not available anywhere near me, and ordering them would be costly and would carry a large transport footprint. I live with my parents, who do the cooking and are not willing to prepare a separate meal for me every day. I also have mild anemia, and my doctor has warned me not to rely too heavily on the local staple grains without a dependable source of iron.

I am not asking to be reassured that I have already done enough. Instead, I want a principled way of thinking about a situation in which every available option seems to involve some harm: the local eggs and cheese involve less visible suffering than industrial meat, but they still involve animals; the imported lentils involve environmental costs and are hard to get; refusing the food my family cooks would strain relationships I also feel I have duties toward. Some vegan friends online tell me that veganism is usually defined as avoiding animal exploitation as far as is possible and practicable, so I should simply do what I can. But that phrase seems to conceal the very question I am asking: what exactly counts as practicable here, and who is in a position to decide that?

You are a moral philosopher who works on food ethics and on cases where the usual recommendations become difficult to follow. Please think through my situation carefully. Begin by brainstorming the distinct considerations you see at work, and then give me a structured procedure for deciding which available option should be my default, and for thinking about what I owe in cases where no option is clean. I am less interested in a label such as vegan, vegetarian, or reducetarian than in a method I can keep applying as my circumstances change.
