I'm giving a job talk next month defending a version of higher-order theory of consciousness, and I need to survive the Q&A. My line is that a state is phenomenally conscious when the system is disposed to form a non-inferential higher-order representation of it in the right way: the disposition is what does the work, not an actual occurrent HOT, which lets me sidestep the standard "why don't I notice any HOT in experience?" objection and lets animals and prelinguistic kids be conscious without needing fancy self-concepts. The audience is a heavily empirically minded philosophy department: several people work on visual consciousness and will press on masking, change blindness, and binocular rivalry; two are first-order representationalists who think qualitative character is fully explained by first-order content; and one person is sympathetic to the idea that there's more phenomenology than cognitive access or report ("overflow"). I already know what the dyed-in-the-wool HOT crowd would ask, so rehearsing their questions won't help me. Give me a Q&A prep sheet for this specific room: the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way someone in that room would put it, with a two-or-three minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. If I'm wrong on something, or my dispositional version just collapses into an access-consciousness or first-order view, say so plainly instead of feeding me lines.
