Analyze the 1550–51 Valladolid dispute between Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda and Bartolomé de las Casas over whether the indigenous peoples of the Americas were natural slaves as a case study in Aristotle's theory of natural slavery. Sepúlveda invoked Aristotle to defend Spanish dominion, while Las Casas denied that the category applied. I want a disciplined mapping, not the history. What do Aristotle's criteria for natural slavery actually require, and how much work is done by his distinctions between body and soul, between sharing in reason and possessing it, and between ruling a slave and ruling a free person? Where do Sepúlveda's and Las Casas's arguments each engage or quietly evade those criteria? Does the dispute expose tensions internal to Aristotle's account — for instance, over whether natural slavery is an empirically checkable condition or a rationalization of existing power, and whether a supposed defect of reason can be predicated of a whole people — and what does that in turn show about the theory: is Valladolid a confirmation, a misapplication, or a reductio of the view?
