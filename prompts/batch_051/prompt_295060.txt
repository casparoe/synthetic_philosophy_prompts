I have been rereading Douglas Hofstadter's satirical Person Paper, in which an imagined world uses a racial lexicon the way our own uses masculine generics, and in doing so makes visible a default that speakers otherwise do not notice. The object of the satire is the habit of running together words for male persons with words for persons generally — man, mankind, he — so that the generic human being is implicitly male and women enter language as a marked or special case. A colleague with whom I discussed the piece insisted that this is unfair to competent speakers. On his view, masculine generics are merely inherited grammatical conventions: readers and listeners know that he in a textbook includes both men and women, the words merely reflect default assumptions already present in the culture, and they add no independent causal pressure to think of a generic person as male. My suspicion is that the forms are not inert — that they quietly help to install and maintain maleness as the default in person-level thought. I want to know how one could bring evidence to bear on this disagreement rather than trading intuitions.

Please design a concrete study, or a small set of studies, to test whether masculine generics exert an independent causal effect on default assumptions about a generic or unspecified human being, over and above whatever prior biases a person brings to the text. Be specific enough that someone could run the study: the linguistic manipulations, the control conditions, the dependent measure, and the predicted outcomes under each hypothesis. Then state precisely what each possible pattern of results would and would not establish. In particular, if a short exposure to one phrasing or another shifts defaults in the lab, what would that prove about ordinary discourse outside the lab, about speakers' intentions, and about the further normative claim that the language ought to be reformed? And if it finds no shift, would that vindicate the mere-convention view, or are there slow-acting effects of lifelong usage that brief experiments cannot detect? Finally, say whether the difficulty of cleanly separating the two positions is merely a practical limit of any experiment, or whether it exposes a defect in the very idea that reflecting and producing defaults are two fully separable processes.
