I'm a software engineer who designs APIs and data schemas, and I have no formal background in philosophy. Recently I've been reading, in an amateur way, about calendar reform and the debate over whether the familiar BC/AD (or BCE/CE) system should introduce a year zero, the way astronomical year numbering does when it gives the year before 1 AD a zero, and over what could justify choosing any particular moment as the epoch from which all years are counted. The arguments I keep meeting seem to run in two different registers. One is a concrete off-by-one problem: in the usual sequence ... 2 BC, 1 BC, 1 AD, 2 AD ..., there is no zero between 1 BC and 1 AD, so an interval crossing that boundary comes out one year shorter than naive subtraction predicts, and the first century and first millennium run from 1 to 100 and 1 to 1000 rather than from 0 to 99 and 0 to 999. The other is a more philosophical claim that any epoch is just a convention and that no year number is more "correct" than another, only more or less convenient. The immediate trigger was a work task: I had to join two tables of historical dates, one using astronomical years with a zero and one using the conventional scheme, and the join kept dropping or double-counting rows around the boundary, which made the philosophical question feel suddenly practical.

I would like you to restate this debate — both the year-zero question and the question of what justifies an epoch — in the vocabulary I actually use day to day, as faithfully as you can: zero-based versus one-based indexing, signed versus unsigned integers, epoch timestamps (Unix time, say) versus human-readable labels, schema migrations and backward compatibility, monotonic version numbers, and special-case corrections like leap seconds or time-zone offsets. I am fluent in all of that; it is my native idiom. Then give me a residue report: which parts of the calendar debate carry over cleanly into these engineering concepts, which parts carry over only with distortion, and which parts cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. For each piece of residue, tell me whether the failure is a limitation of my engineering framework or a sign that the underlying philosophical claim was looser than it first appeared.
