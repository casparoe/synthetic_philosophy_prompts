I've read enough uploads/identity threads on LessWrong over the years to have all the cached takes, but I realized I've never actually fixed the list of things a theory of whole-brain emulation identity is supposed to get right. So before I read one more paper or blog post, write me the desiderata. Specifically: what does an adequate theory of whether (and in what sense) a whole-brain emulation is the same mind as the person who was scanned have to deliver?

I want each desideratum paired with the case or consideration that forces it, and I want you to flag which ones are negotiable and which are load-bearing. Then tell me if any pair or larger subset of them can't be jointly satisfied — I half-suspect most people's list is inconsistent but nobody says so — and which single requirement does the most work excluding the existing candidates. Keep the actual scoring of the main views brief (pattern identity, functionalist continuity, whatever the "the emulation is a different person with the same memories" camp is called), because the list is what I'm after, not another taxonomy.

For pitch: the cases I keep getting stuck on are the destructive scan where the original is sliced and the emulation wakes up; gradual replacement where a neuron or synapse gets swapped out for a faithful emulated part one at a time while the patient stays conscious throughout; the copy-and-delete/transporter problem where the original is scanned non-destructively and then painlessly killed after the copy verifies; the branching case where two copies are run in parallel and both remember being the original; and the whole sleep-is-death worry that if an instantaneous gap in consciousness is enough, then I die every night. I want the theory to have something to say about ordinary biological continuity too — it shouldn't imply I died when I was five or that molecular turnover kills me — but I dont want a list that just assumes ordinary continuity is the gold standard and then rigs the upload cases to match it, because that strikes me as exactly the kind of loaded "same" I'd like you to call out when you see it. Also mostly ignore legal personhood and upload rights except where they force a first-person identity answer; I care about whether I wake up, not what the law should say.
