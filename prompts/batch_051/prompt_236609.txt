I'm on a working group our state children's services department set up to respond to a proposed parenting licence. The bill has two parts. Part one would require every prospective parent to complete a short child development course and pass a basic written test, covering safe sleep, feeding, car seats, and recognising signs of serious illness, before either parent's name goes on the birth certificate. People who fail can retake the test as often as they like, and no one is barred permanently. Part two gives the licensing board discretion to refuse a licence to applicants whose stated intentions for the child's upbringing would, in the board's view, leave the child with too few future options; the draft explicitly mentions parents who plan too keep the child out of school, steer the child into a single career or marriage, or raise the child entirely off-grid. The group is split. Some see part one as a modest welfare measure and part two as a grotesque infringement on the freedom to raise one's children; others see part one as the thin end of the wedge and part two as the natural expression of a child's right to an open future. I need a short written opinion for our next meeting that recommends an actual course of action: endorse the bill as drafted, reject it, or amend it, and if amend, which parts to keep, drop, or alter. The opinion should engage the child's right to an open future seriously, and it should put the strongest argument for the side you think we should not take fairly rather than dismissing it. In particular, I want it to address whether the open future idea is the right sort of test for who may parent in the first place, as opposed to a constraint on what parents may do once they are parents. Please write the opinion as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, like a letter or email, and write it in American English.
