I've spent enough years on rationalist-adjacent forums to have a standing reflex against conceptual distinctions that look clean until you try to actually cash them out, so when our reading group circled back to Ryle's knowledge-how versus knowledge-that divide I got a bit obsessed. Specifically, I want to make an episode about intellectualism about know-how: the view that knowing how to do something is really knowing that some proposition is true, except grasped under a distinctively practical way of thinking. We've got a philosopher coming on who defends exactly that view, and I really don't want the first twenty minutes to be him restating it in podcast-friendly terms. What I'm after is a set of eight to ten interview questions that make him work. Please include at least one question each on: (a) Ryle's regress worry, where intellectualism seems to require that each intelligent action be preceded by some piece of propositional content being entertained, and the entertainment itself then needs to be explained, threatening an infinite regress; (b) the two-way pull between know-how and ability, like someone who can still teach a skill after loosing the motor capacity to perform it, or a beginner who lands a trick once by luck; and (c) whether the view ends up ascribing honest-to-god knowledge-that to animals, preverbal kids, and unreflective experts, or whether it just quietly redefines knowledge-that until the word proposition has stopped doing its usual work.

For each of those areas, give me a follow-up keyed to the reply you'd expect from a competent intellectualist: for example, if he says the proposition needn't be consciously entertained, ask what causal or explanatory work the proposition is doing beyond redescribing the performance afterwards; if he says abilities are just modular add-ons to the knowing-that, ask him what the practical way of thinking contributes specifically, and whether it doesn't smuggle the ability back in under another name. And somewhere in the back half, include at least one question about what would actually change his mind, because I've sat through too many interviews where that only shows up in the last minute and then gets cut for time.
