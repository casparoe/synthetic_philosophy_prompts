I keep getting tangled up on the word "possible." Everyone agrees that a fair coin landing heads is possible, and that a square circle is not. But past those easy cases I don't know what the word tracks. Take picking a real number between 0 and 1 uniformly at random: the chance of picking exactly 0.5 is zero, but 0.5 is sitting right there among the possible outcomes, so is picking 0.5 possible? Or flip a fair coin forever: an infinite run of all heads has probability zero, while any finite run of heads is possible, so is the whole all-heads sequence possible? And a glass of water turning into wine in the next hour is physically impossible and absurdly improbable, but is it possible in some weaker sense? Then there's "I could have been a plumber," which sounds like a possibility claim but has nothing to do with probabilities. I want a working definition of "possible" that gets the two easy cases right and gives a definite yes/no on the four hard ones, even if the answer is "yes in sense X, no in sense Y." Please don't just define it as "has non-zero probability"; that would be picking a side before looking at the cases. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: (1) state your definition and why it counts a fair coin landing heads as possible and a square circle as not; (2) apply it to picking 0.5; (3) apply it to the all-heads sequence; (4) apply it to the water-into-wine case; (5) apply it to the plumber sentence, and say whether one definition is doing all the work or "possible" is really two or more words sharing a spelling, revising if needed.
