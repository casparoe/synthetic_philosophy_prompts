A guy I know is trying to build a platform for moral trade — the idea that two people who care about sharply different issues can each do something concrete for the other person's cause, so both end up better off by their own lights without either changing their mind. Here's the version of the mechanism he keeps pitching me. Each user lists one cause they care intensely about, plus a menu of real actions they'd be willing to perform for causes they're lukewarm or even hostile toward: donate fifty bucks, phonebank for an hour, show up to a rally in a neighboring town, post a statement, canvass for a ballot measure, vote a certain way in a low-turnout local election. The platform matches people in roughly opposite directions and they each commit to doing one thing for the other person's cause. There's a deposit held in escrow; you get it back when you upload proof you did your side, and if you flake the money goes to the other person's cause instead. He thinks it works like gains-from-trade: everyones moral influence gets more bang for their buck.

Don't give me a defense or a takedown — treat it as a mechanism and tell me what actually happens if this gets any traction. What does it incentivize in users and in the causes themselves; how do people game it, including by faking both ends of a trade; where does it break down hardest; and which failure modes can be patched without turning it into something else. Also say something about the second-order effects — on how people start thinking about their own commitments, and on causes that refuse to participate.
