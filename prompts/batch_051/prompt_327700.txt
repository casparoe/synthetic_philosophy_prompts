Last week I was arguing with a friend about this: she'd been thinking about a guy from high school she hasn't spoken to in like ten years, and two minutes later he texted her out of nowhere, and she's convinced something was going on. I brought up the birthday paradox — you only need 23 people in a room for a shared birthday to be more likely than not, so lots of "what are the odds" moments are really just a huge number of unnoticed opportunities adding up — and she told me I was being a killjoy.

So now I'm trying to figure out what would actually count as a coincidence worth taking seriously, rather than a story that only feels wild in hindsight. The criteria I've been toying with are: (a) the two events are genuinely causally independent, (b) the match is specified in advance instead of being noticed after the fact, (c) the number of opportunities for a match was small, not birthday-paradox-sized, and (d) the matched detail is specific enough to have a low base rate, not just "someone called when I was thinking of people who might call."

Here's the case I keep going back and forth on. A person spends an hour of a long flight, for no particular reason, remembering a childhood neighbor they haven't seen in twenty-five years. When the plane lands they get a call from their mother, who says that very neighbor had asked about them that same day. There's no causal link: the neighbor didn't know they were being remembered, and the mother didn't prompt the memory. Is that a real coincidence under my criteria? Go through the criteria one by one, point out where they underdetermine the answer, and then say whether you think the case shows the criteria themselves need changing — maybe what actually matters isn't the raw odds but whether the two events can be fitted into a story that makes them feel connected, or something else I'm missing.
