Here's the claim I keep running into, stated as strongly as I can: Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z and the rest carve nature at its joints — being born in a particular year-range actually gives people shared, robustly discoverable psychological or social traits, over and above their age and the historical events they happened to live through. I write about science and ideas for a living, so I can follow regressions and effect sizes, but I never actually learned demography. Restate that claim, in its main variants, in the vocabulary of cohort analysis — age-period-cohort decomposition, mixed effects, cohort effects versus period shocks — and tell me which parts survive the restatement, which parts get bent to fit, and which parts have no counterpart there at all. Then give me your honest read on the leftover: does it show the statistical toolkit is too crude for what the generational-kind people are gesturing at, or that the strong claim was mostly a loose way of talking that hardened into a taxonomy? Dont just split the difference; and say what would change your mind about that read, or where your analysis is most likely to fail.
