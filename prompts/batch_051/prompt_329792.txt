For a working group on the moral significance of risk, I have been asked to write down the commitments I actually hold about imposing risks on people without their consent, so that the group can push on whether the package is jointly tenable. I would like a careful external audit of the position before I present it.

The position I currently want to defend is as follows. (1) Permissibility is settled ex ante: whether a risky action is justified depends on how things looked at the time of decision, and if the risk was justified then, the fact that the harm later materializes does not give the victim an additional moral complaint; the decision is not re-litigated after the outcome. (2) I accept an individualist constraint on risk justification: a practice is wrong only if there is some particular person who, at the time of the decision, had a reasonable complaint against it. I distrust arguments that add tiny probabilities across many people into a number of expected victims and treat that number as if it were an actual harm to an actual person. (3) I regard ordinary automobile use as permissible. My reason is not that its aggregate toll is small, but that road use is a mutually beneficial and reciprocally risky practice: each participant both imposes and accepts the same class of small risks, and nearly everyone has a strong prospective interest in the continuance of the scheme. (4) Yet I also want to say the following is wrong. A company decides to release a known carcinogen in minute quantities over a city, such that each of eight hundred thousand exposed people faces an additional one-in-eight-hundred-thousand lifetime cancer risk. The expected toll is one death, but no particular victim can be identified in advance, and no exposed individual's incremental risk is large enough, taken alone, to constitute a serious personal grievance. The release is not deceptive and is not aimed at any singled-out person, but I believe it is impermissible all the same. (5) Finally, I hold that actual consent matters in a way that hypothetical consent cannot fully replace. When a specific beneficiary imposes a serious risk on a specific other person and could easily have asked for consent but did not, the failure to ask is itself a wrong, not merely a diagnostic sign that the risk was probably unjustified.

Please audit these commitments. I want to know which are genuinely in tension, how severe each tension is, and which apparent tensions dissolve once the claims are read with care. For each genuine tension, say precisely what has to give and what the cheapest repair would be, and then identify the single revision that would leave the entire package most defensible.
