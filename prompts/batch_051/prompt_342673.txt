I have a standing view and a standing unease, and they have begun to collide.

The view: the genetic code is not a loose analogy; it is a code in a substantive sense. The mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids is arbitrary with respect to the underlying chemistry — the standard code is not the only one, and the same triplet can be assigned to different amino acids in different taxa, which is the signature of a convention rather than a chemically forced reaction. The code is also redundant: sixty-one triplets map onto twenty amino acids, and three serve as stop signals. It has reading frames, and mutations are transmitted as inherited differences. A system that maps messages onto signals by an arbitrary rule, redundantly, and transmits them across generations is exactly what we mean by a code; transcription and translation are the decoding apparatus.

The unease: a code, in the only sense I am sure I understand, is something agents use. Morse code, ASCII, and the rules of the road are conventions sustained by beings who mean things by their use of them. Transcription and translation, by contrast, are chemistry — base pairing, stereochemistry, catalysis, binding affinities — with no one meaning anything and no one interpreting anything. If an evolved causal mapping counts as a code, then a thermostat encodes the temperature, tree rings encode the weather, and a planet's orbit encodes Kepler's laws; at that point the word "code" is drained of interest.

I know the usual escape: drop the intentionality requirement and say that a code is any arbitrary, functionally sustained mapping — natural selection gave the genetic machinery the function of producing specific proteins, and that function is enough to bestow content. But I cannot see that this avoids the unease: "function" here seems either to mean mere effect, in which case every causal regularity is a code, or to smuggle in the purposiveness that caused the trouble in the first place.

I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the semantic intuition to be the thing I give up, what it would take for the mechanistic theory to be the thing I give up, and whether there is a reading — perhaps separating information in the sense of reducing uncertainty from content in the sense of being about something, or separating arbitrary mapping from convention — on which both sides survive.
