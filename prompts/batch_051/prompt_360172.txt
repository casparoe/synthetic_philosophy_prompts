I am preparing interview questions for a podcast episode on Anselm's ontological argument and its most famous medieval objection. The guest is a philosopher who defends the claim that Gaunilo's "lost island" parody fails because the greatest conceivable being is not on the same logical footing as the greatest conceivable island, and that Anselm's proof survives the parallel. The interview will center on this passage from Gaunilo of Marmoutiers, In Behalf of the Fool:

This being is said to be in my understanding already, only because I understand what is said. Now could it not with equal justice be said that I have in my understanding all manner of unreal objects, having absolutely no existence in themselves, because I understand these things if one speaks of them, whatever they may be?

Draft eight to ten interview questions that use this passage as the main point of pressure, rather than asking the guest simply to restate Anselm's argument. At least one question should press on Gaunilo's charge that any spoken-of object would, on Anselm's reasoning, seem to be in the understanding; at least one should ask what the guest thinks must be different about the idea of a being than which nothing greater can be conceived; and at least one should require the guest to state the strongest possible version of the island parody and then explain why it still fails. For each main question, include a follow-up that anticipates the guest's most likely reply and forces them to make a further distinction or concession.
