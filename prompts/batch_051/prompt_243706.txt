I'm on my town's heritage tree board and we're stuck on a case I need to draft a memo for. Our ordinance protects any single tree with a trunk over 36 inches from being cut down, and a developer wants to clear part of an aspen stand. The wrinkle: the whole 80-acre stand is one clone. Every trunk is genetically identical and they're all connected by a single underground root system, so a decent case can be made that the whole grove is one individual organism — some people even call stands like this among the largest organisms on earth. If that's right, the whole grove counts as a single tree under the ordinance and the developer basically can't touch any of it. If each trunk is its own tree, then only the trunks over 36 inches are protected and the rest can go. And to make it worse, a county road put in during the 70s severed the root connection between the east and west halves, so now we're also arguing about whether it's still one individual if the parts can't exchange resources anymore. One botanist on the board keeps saying a trunk is a ramet and the whole clone is a genet, and that the ordinance cleary means one or clearly means the other, depending on who's listening. I need a short memo for the board with a clear recommendation, bottom line up front, the reasoning, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. We have to apply the ordinance as written — please don't just say we should rewrite it.
