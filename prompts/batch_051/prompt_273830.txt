I spent my career in physical chemistry and have come to philosophy only in retirement, mostly formal epistemology and decision theory, so I read with a habit of watching for structures I recognize from the sciences I used to teach. One structural echo will not leave me alone, and I cannot tell whether it is a genuine isomorphism or a loose metaphor. Aumann's agreement theorem says that two agents who share a prior cannot remain in disagreement about an event once their posterior probabilities for that event are common knowledge: the posteriors must already be equal. The zeroth law of thermodynamics says, in the terms I was trained in, that thermal equilibrium is transitive—if system A is in equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in equilibrium with C—so that a single scalar temperature is well defined across systems in mutual contact; and the dynamic story, of bodies in thermal contact settling to the same temperature regardless of their starting points, seems to me to resemble the familiar extension of Aumann's result in which agents with common priors converge toward a common posterior as they repeatedly announce and revise their estimates.

The mapping I keep reaching for runs: common prior as the shared underlying dynamics or shared thermostat, common knowledge of posteriors as thermal contact, and agreement as equality of temperature. I can already see two obvious strains. A posterior about a single event sits inside a whole probability distribution, and Aumann's theorem forces agreement only on that one marginal, whereas a real Bayesian agent has commitments elsewhere; conversely, physical systems require no beliefs, rationality, or common knowledge to equilibrate, they merely exchange heat. I do not know whether these are fatal or merely reminders that analogies are imperfect.

My question is whether there is anything beneath the surface here—some shared fixed-point or consistency-under-symmetric-coupling structure—or whether all I have noticed is the uninteresting fact that coupling mechanisms which continuously expose two quantities to each other tend to force those quantities to match. Please give me a verdict, not a literature summary. State the mapping precisely, test it point by point, and say specifically what the parallel would commit us to that turns out false. Keep the reply to at most 500 words.
