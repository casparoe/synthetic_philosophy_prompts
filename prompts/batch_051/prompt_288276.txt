The idea: in the standard story, people see a few early choices pile up and rationally start ignoring their own signal because the public history swamps it, and the herd locks onto something that might be wrong. But nowadays everyone knows that story. So suppose a bunch of sophisticated agents watch an early consensus form and think, this is probably just a normal cascade, the early movers were noisy, I shouldn't update much from the crowd. So they lean on their own signal. But often their private signals are weak, and the real thing doing the work is the shared suspicion that the crowd is garbage. They aren't choosing the thing opposite the crowd because their signal points there; they're choosing it because they've overcorrected against the possibility of being herded. And because they all share the same overcorrection, they pile onto the opposite option and manufacture a new consensus that is just as fragile and just as disconnected from the truth, but flipped.

The example I kept using: a strip of restaurants where the first five visitors all pick the same touristy place. Ten years ago the next twenty people would follow. Today the next twenty all assume the pile-up is a herding artifact and pick the place across the street, even though they don't actually have better evidence—they just don't want to be the sheep. Then you get a reverse rush built out of nobody wanting to be part of a cascade, and the place across the street now looks like the genuine consensus.
