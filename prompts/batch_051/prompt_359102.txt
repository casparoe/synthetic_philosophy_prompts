I am compiling a list of objections to the procreation asymmetry—the claim, associated with Benatar, that the absence of pain in a life that never begins is good even where no one exists to enjoy it, whereas the absence of pleasure in such a life is not bad. So far my list runs as follows:

(1) The asymmetry is asserted rather than argued for: no independent rationale is supplied for treating pain and pleasure differently across the existence/non-existence divide, and a premise doing this much work cannot simply be stipulated.

(2) The view equivocates between value simpliciter and value for a person. The absence of pain is said to be good without saying for whom it is good, while the absence of pleasure is said not to be bad only because there is no one for whom it would be bad; held to a single sense, the asymmetry looks considerably less stable.

(3) It treats non-existence as though it were a state that could be compared with existence, and that comparison is suspect. There is no subject to receive the supposed benefit of absent pain, and the idea of a person's nonexistent counterpart having a welfare standing cannot be made intelligible.

(4) It has consequences that many who accept its spirit still find hard to own: that every conception harms the child, that even a life of great happiness is, on balance, worse than never beginning, and that prospective parents have decisive reason not to reproduce.

(5) The parity objection: if the absence of pain is good even where no one exists, then by symmetry the absence of pleasure should be bad even where no one exists. To deny that is to build an unexplained exception into precisely the point at issue.

I am reasonably sure these five cover the familiar ground. I want an objection to the procreation asymmetry that is not on this list and not merely a rewording, special case, or combination of the above. It may target the asymmetry itself, or diagnose why someone might be tempted to accept it, or challenge the work it does in the wider anti-natalist argument. Having supplied the objection, say honestly whether it is any good, and say how it stands in relation to the five above—in particular, whether it really is independent of them.
