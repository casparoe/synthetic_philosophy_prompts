I've been trying to figure out where the line is between a criminal law that's void for vagueness and one that's just a bit open-textured, and I'd like a working definition of "unconstitutionally vague" that gets a list of hard cases right. Here are the cases I want it to handle:

- a vagrancy-style law making it a crime to loiter or wander around in public with no apparent purpose (I'm basically certain this should be void);
- a law setting a 30 mph speed limit on a particular street (obviously fine);
- a reckless-driving statute where the definition is something like driving with conscious disregard for the safety of people or property (I genuinely don't know whether this should count as vague);
- an ordinance making it a crime to annoy a passerby;
- a prohibition on breach of the peace;
- and the familiar rule banning vehicles in the park, imagined as a criminal law applied to someone riding a bicycle.

So: propose a definition, run it through each case, flag where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it or defend the surprising call. And please seperate what's actually settled about vagueness doctrine from what's still contested among people who know the area — I don't want one particular theory presented as if it were the consensus.
