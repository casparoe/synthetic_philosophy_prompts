A friend of mine keeps defending the idea that morality is basically hygiene. Wrongdoing leaves a stain; bad company is contaminating; purity is something you can lose by contact and restore through ritual. She thinks this explains why we often feel disgust before we can articulate what's wrong, and she takes it to show that disgust is carrying real moral information and that purity is a basic moral category rather than just a metaphor — which I dont fully buy. I want an audit of the analogy, not a list of pros and cons. Give me the correspondence table: what maps to the dirt, the stain, the washing, the contagion. Then tell me where the mapping holds, where it breaks, and which breaks are fatal for her conclusion verses which are just cosmetic. Then take a stand: does her conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the hygiene picture doing the work the whole time? If a better analogy would do less damage, say what it is.

Structure it as: The mapping (about a third of the answer), Where it holds (a bit shorter), Where it breaks (longest), What the analogy smuggles in (short), My verdict (one paragraph, committed).
