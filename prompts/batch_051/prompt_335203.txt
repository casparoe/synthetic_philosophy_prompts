Consider two philosophical views about what justifies loving a particular person. On the appraisal view, love answers to value that the beloved has independently: her humor, intelligence, kindness, or more abstractly her capacity for rational agency. On the bestowal view, love does not discover such value but creates it: the lover's caring concern confers on the beloved a worth she need not have antecedently, and the lover's reasons are generated from within the lover rather than read off the beloved's properties. A third view locates love's reasons in the relationship's history rather than in either the beloved's qualities or the lover's bestowal.

Please take the conceptual claim that love has the structure of bestowal rather than appraisal and design the strongest empirical test of it that you can. Crucially, tell me not only what the test would show if it came out one way, but what it would and would not settle. Structure your answer in these sections, with roughly these lengths:

1. The claim. One short paragraph restating the bestowal claim precisely and distinguishing its descriptive reading (how lovers actually think and act) from its normative reading (what actually justifies love).

2. Where the views come apart. One or two paragraphs identifying the kinds of observable behavior or judgment that would have to differ if bestowal rather than appraisal is true, and why. Be specific about the minimal predictions each view makes.

3. Proposed test. Two to four paragraphs describing a concrete study: participants, manipulations or measured variables, outcome measures, and controls. Make sure the design can separate bestowal from mere commitment, loss aversion, or socially desirable responding.

4. Predicted results. One paragraph, or a short table-form list, saying what pattern of results favors bestowal, what pattern favors appraisal, and what pattern would undercut both or leave the matter unresolved.

5. What a positive result would show. One or two paragraphs. If the bestowal pattern emerged, would we have learned that bestowal describes real normative reasons for love, or only that lovers' psychology works as the bestowal view predicts? Distinguish these carefully.

6. What no test can settle. One or two paragraphs on the residual philosophical question: whether creating a reason by caring is the same as having a reason to care. Say explicitly whether empirical evidence could ever close the gap.

7. Overall assessment. One paragraph: how much would such a test advance the philosophical debate, and would a philosophically decisive version of the dispute remain even if the behavioral results were highly robust?
