I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for a piece I'm writing I need to work out what a political theorist I follow would say about a new case. She writes a monthly column where she gives a straight verdict on whether some act of civil disobedience was justified. She assumes there's a standing obligation to obey the law, so the column is really about when that obligation is defeated. I've kept notes on her rulings over the last year or so:

- Parents whose children attend a special school occupy the council chamber to stop a cut they say discriminates against disabled children. They had exhausted the complaints process and there was no realistic legal challenge left. They sat in, did no damage, and when the police arrived they went quietly and accepted their fines. Her verdict: justified.
- A man refuses to pay his council tax because he thinks the council spends it badly and the bins aren't collected often enough. He says the policy is daft, not unjust. There's a formal complaints route and a local election he could vote in, but he didn't bother with either. Her verdict: not justified.
- Animal-rights activists attack a delivery van outside a lab to disrupt animal experiments they regard as gravely wrong. They say they had no other way, but a driver was injured and the attack was plainly violent. Her verdict: not justified.

Next month's case is this. A teacher refuses to hand over pupil data under a new government scheme, arguing that the law is a serious violation of the children's privacy and therefore unjust. She goes public, she's nonviolent, and she says she'll accept whatever penalty she gets. But a judicial review route is available and a campaign group already has a legal challenge underway, which she hasn't waited for.

What should I predict her verdict will be, if she's being consistent with her earlier rulings? Which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and if they genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would she be adopting with each choice? Is she running a 'last resort' rule, a 'justice of the law' rule, a 'publicity and accepting penalty' rule, or some mix — and what further case would settle wich one it is?
