Something's been nagging me about the whole project of idealizing preferences, and I think I've finally figured out how to say what it is. I want someone to check whether the distinction I'm about to draw is real or just a vibe. I call it corrective vs creative idealization. Corrective idealization fixes an actual error: you'd stop wanting the thing if you knew one more fact, so the idealized preference is still yours, just with the mistake subtracted. Creative idealization doesn't subtract anything; it asks what you'd want if you'd been raised differently, or had different options, or had never formed the attachments you have, and then reads an answer off that counterfactual you. Those feel like completely different operations to me, but I think informed-desire accounts and the fancier coherent-extrapolated-volition type views quietly run them together, present everything as corrective (we're just helping you want what you'd already want if you weren't confused) while actually doing creative work behind the scenes whenever the case is outside anything you've ever considered. And here's the part I'm suspicious of: I think the creative step is where the idealizers own values leak in, because there's no fact of the matter to keep the extrapolation honest, and that's why idealized preference theories have a way of outputting whatever the person doing the idealizing already believed. Concrete case: the meat eater who'd go vegetarian after seeing what factory farming actually is — that's correction. But ask someone what they'd want if they'd grown up vegan in a different world, and the answer isn't recovered from them, it's fabricated. Is the distinction itself sound? Does it already exist under some name I don't know? And am I being unfair to the informed-desire people when I claim most of what they call idealization is really the creative kind, or is that actually true about how those accounts work?
