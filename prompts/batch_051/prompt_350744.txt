In my philosophy of law seminar this term, we have spent several weeks on self-referential rule systems, and the instructor introduced a simplified version of Nomic in which the players can amend the game's rules by following the game's rules. Since that session I have been turning over the following argument, which I keep finding almost valid even though its conclusion seems like a cheat. Suppose a club constitution contains exactly one amendment rule, which reads: "Any rule of this constitution, including this very rule, may be amended by a majority vote of the membership." At a meeting of all one hundred members, fifty-one members vote to replace that amendment rule with a new one, which reads: "Any rule of this constitution may be amended only by a unanimous vote of the membership." My argument that this replacement is valid runs as follows. (1) The validity of an amendment is determined by the amendment rule in force at the time the amendment is adopted. (2) At the time of the vote, the old majority amendment rule was in force, and it governed amendments to itself. (3) The vote satisfied the old rule, since fifty-one of one hundred is a majority. (4) Therefore the new unanimity requirement was validly adopted. (5) Once the unanimity requirement is in force, no future amendment may be adopted by a majority, including a ninety-nine-to-one vote aimed at restoring majority rule. (6) Thus a bare majority can, in one lawful move, impose a rule that permanently disenfranchises every future non-unanimous majority from amending the constitution.

I cannot tell whether the argument actually goes through or whether one of the steps is smuggling something in. In particular, I suspect that step (1) may be the weak point, because it applies an old rule to the very act that displaces it, but I cannot state the objection rigorously. Could you assess the argument step by step, say exactly which step fails if any does, and distinguish the strongest reading of each step from the reading that would make the argument too easy? If the argument is valid, explain why the conclusion, though startling, is not a contradiction. Please write your answer in American English.
