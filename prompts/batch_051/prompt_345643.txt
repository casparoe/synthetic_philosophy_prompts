I'm a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and I've absorbed the standard line that Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity give Occam's razor an objective, language-independent footing. But I've noticed that a certain compact argument for that conclusion looks almost too strong, and I suspect one step is quietly doing more work than it is entitled to. I want you to locate the failure exactly, if there is one.

Here is the argument:

(1) Occam's razor says that, all else equal, we should prefer the simpler hypothesis.

(2) Kolmogorov complexity turns simplicity into a precise number: fix a universal prefix-free Turing machine U, and let K_U(x) be the length of the shortest program for U that outputs the string x. Then x is simpler than y just in case K_U(x) < K_U(y).

(3) The invariance theorem says that for any two universal prefix-free machines U and V, there exists a constant c, depending only on U and V, such that for every finite string x, |K_U(x) − K_V(x)| ≤ c.

(4) So, switching from U to V merely adds a single fixed overhead to every hypothesis's complexity score: the two measures differ by a uniform additive constant.

(5) Adding the same fixed constant to every score leaves all pairwise simplicity comparisons unchanged.

(6) Therefore, the simplicity ordering among hypotheses is exactly the same no matter which universal machine we use.

(7) Therefore, Kolmogorov complexity supplies an objective, language-independent ordering of hypotheses by simplicity, and the worry that Occam's razor is merely an artifact of one's chosen description language — the grue-style worry — is dissolved.

I half-suspect the problem is in the move from (3) to (4): a bound that holds for all strings is not the same thing as a constant that is added uniformly to every score. But I want to be sure, and I want to know exactly how much the argument still manages to establish.

Please act as an expert in algorithmic information theory and formal epistemology. Before giving your verdict, brainstorm two or three distinct readings of "up to an additive constant" and say which reading would make (4) true and which would not. Then evaluate each step under the correct reading. If the argument is invalid, identify the first step that fails and explain precisely what the invariance theorem does and does not license. If a step is salvageable, say what replacement would make it valid. Finally, tell me what remains of the claim that Kolmogorov complexity gives Occam's razor an objective basis: for instance, is the right residual claim one about bounded ratios of prior probabilities, about asymptotic convergence of predictions, or about something else? Be as precise as you can about the quantifier order in the invariance theorem, and do not elide the difference between "there exists a constant such that for all x" and "for all x, the same constant..." I want the exact step, not a general meditation on language dependence.
