Lay out the desiderata for an adequate theory of the duty of legibility: the claim that we owe it to others, at least sometimes, to be interpretable — to make our reasons, feelings, or conduct readable rather than opaque. Fix the purpose as follows: the theory should be good enough to govern judgment both in close personal relationships and in impersonal settings such as hiring, lending, platform moderation, and officials explaining decisions to the public. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it binding, and say whether it is negotiable. Handle explicitly the distinctions between sincerity, transparency, and legibility, and between what is owed to particular persons and what is owed impersonally; also say whether the requirements leave room for permissible or even mandatory opacity to others, and how power asymmetries affect that. Indicate whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, since that would make the list itself a result. Then identify the single requirement that does most to exclude the stock positions — a blanket duty of transparency, a bare honesty-only duty, and the view that there is no such duty beyond not lying. Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the contenders; a table is fine.
