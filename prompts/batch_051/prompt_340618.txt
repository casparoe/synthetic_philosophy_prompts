I'm a software engineer and I've never taken a philosophy class, so when my friend started talking about karma I did the only thing that made sense to me: I started logging her verdicts like test cases and trying to reverse engineer the scoring function underneath. She's not religious in an organized way; she just has a strong conviction that the universe keeps moral books, so people build up karmic credit or debt that fits what they deserve, even if it doesn't balance out in this life.

Here are the cases I've run by her so far (verdicts in her words, or close):

1. A man buys what he's sure is a deadly poison and spikes his neighbor's drink, but the bottle was mislabeled and it turns out to be harmless medicine that cures the neighbor's chronic illness. She says full karmic debt, same as if the neighbor had died: the will is what counts.

2. A driver is texting, hits and kills a pedestrian. No malice, and he's genuinely wrecked about it afterward. She says heavy debt, but not as heavy as an intentional killer's.

3. A rich woman anonymously donates a million dollars to a free clinic. She says big karmic credit.

4. Same woman, but she donates a million on the condition that the clinic is named after her. She says some credit, but way less than the anonymous case.

5. A soldier in a just war kills an enemy combatant under lawful orders. She says no debt from that.

6. A guy is forced at gunpoint to drive the getaway car for a robbery. She says no debt, or close to it, because he didn't choose it.

7. A person lies on his resume to get a job, then does genuinely excellent work that helps a lot of people. She says the lie still leaves a debt, and the good work earns credit seperately but doesn't cancel the lie.

8. A woman gives her severely depressed friend advice she sincerely believes will help, but the advice makes the friend's depression worse for months. She says no debt; the harm was a terrible outcome, but her intention was clean and the advice wasn't obviously risky at the time.

The new one I want help on: a man's brother is falsely charged with murder. The man knows his brother is innocent, but the only way to get the jury to see it is to testify that they were together at the time of the killing, which is a lie. He lies under oath. The brother goes free, and later the actual killer is caught anyway, so in the end nobody is harmed by the lie. He wasn't coerced, and he doesn't regret it.

What I want to know is: predict what she would say the karmic verdict is for the lying brother. Then lay out the two or three implicit rules that fit all eight of her earlier verdicts (I can see at least a "quality of will plus carelessness" rule and a "wrong act type itself" rule, and there might be a "purity of motive" one hiding in there too), say what each one predicts about the perjury case, and give your best guess about her actual call, with the reasons for picking that rule over the others. Finally, give me one more test case I could run by her that would most cleanly settle which rule she's actually running, in case the perjury case isn't enough.
