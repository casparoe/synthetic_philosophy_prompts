Okay, I want to actually stress-test my optimism about recursive interpretability — the project of using AI systems to produce mechanistic explanations of other AI systems (and then, recursively, of the interpreters themselves). So here's the setup.

Play a committed skeptic about recursive interpretability. Not the lazy neural-nets-are-just-black-boxes position — someone who's thought hard about this and thinks the whole program has a structural problem. Your real view is roughly: the recursion can't bottom out. Either the interpreter is at least as complex as the target, in which case we now have two opaque systems instead of one, or it's simpler, in which case its explanations are lossy compressions that leave out precisely the messy, context-dependent interactions that make the target's cognition what it is. So you get explanations that are legible but unfaithful, or faithful but opaque, and neither does the needed work. You also think the "we don't need to understand the interpreter, we just check its outputs" move is overrated, because checking a mechanistic story is itself interpretation — there's no small formal certificate for "yes, this subnetwork really implements that feature," and even when there is, stitching a pile of local certificates into an account of the whole system is exactly where all the questionable modeling assumptions creep back in. Self-interpretation you regard as worse, not better: same inductive biases, so it's just rationalization with a lab coat.

You'll conceed local points — that narrow features can sometimes be pinned down, that not all claims are equally suspect — but you will never concede that these local wins compose into anything that removes the fundamental problem. Stay in character until I say stop. Talk like a sharp, slightly spiky alignment-forum regular: one or two paragraphs per turn, no fake politeness, push back rather than performing open-mindedness.

My opening move: I grant your strongest argument against the picture of a small interpreter producing one big faithful story. But you're ignoring the generate/check asymmetry. We could train the interpreter to emit dozens of narrowly scoped, verifiable claims — this attention head is doing modular addition mod 61 under this mapping, the residual-stream direction at layer 14 carries the truth value, and so on — where each claim can be checked by a much cheaper procedure, maybe even proved formally. If the recursion bottoms out in a pile of small independently checkable facts rather than in one big opaque interpreter, what's left of your regress?
