Ryle argued that knowing how is not a species of knowing that—that skill is a matter of capacities and dispositions, not of standing in a relation to some true proposition. I keep trying to figure out whether that argument survives what later came out of the amnesia literature, because for years people treated the patient H.M. as empirical vindication of Ryle's picture. H.M. lost the ability to form new declarative memories after his medial temporal lobe surgery, but across repeated sessions he got better and better at a mirror-drawing task while each time reporting no memory of ever having done it. The natural gloss was that the skill was being stored in a non-propositional form, exactly as Ryle would predict. But I've started to think the result may not do the philosophical work people assigned to it. An intellectualist can say their claim was always about the semantics of the phrase knows how, not about which memory system stores the information, and that H.M. shows at most a dissociation between two kinds of storage, not two kinds of knowledge. So does the procedural/declarative dissociation give the Rylean argument a premise it didn't have before, leave intellectualism untouched, or undercut both positions by showing they were tracking different things? What I'd really like is a diagnosis of which premise of the Rylean argument the H.M. result bears on, and whether the argument can be restated to survive once the empirical point is conceded.
