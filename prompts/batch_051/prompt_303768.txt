Here's my problem. I keep making judgments about how close to the truth a false theory is, and I'd like to know whether there is one principle behind all of them, or whether some of my judgments just have to go.

To keep this self-contained, don't worry about actual history. The true story is: a left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m.

Consider these theories:

T1. A left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m. (the truth)
T2. A left-handed man took the letter from the desk.
T3. A left-handed man took a document from the desk after 9 p.m.
T4. A right-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m.
T5. A left-handed man took the letter from the desk after midnight.
T6. A left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m. and he was wearing a red coat.
T7. A left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m. and it was raining.
T8. Somebody took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m.
T9. Either a left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m. or a right-handed man did.
T10. A monkey took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m.

My verdicts, roughly: T4 and T5 are about equally close, and both are much closer than T10. T3 is closer than T5, because getting the object slightly wrong (a document instead of the letter) bothers me less than getting the time wrong by hours. T6 is closer than T2, even though T2 is true and T6 is false, because T6 has the whole truth plus one false extra and T2 is just a true fragment. Between T6 and T7 I keep wavering; if forced I'd put T6 a little ahead, maybe because a detail about the man feels more relevant than a detail about the weather. And here's the one that puzzles me most: T9 is guaranteed true, but I want to say T4 is closer to the truth than T9. T4 commits and gets one feature wrong; T9 doesn't commit either way and so tells me nothing about which hand, even though it can't be false.

I have a rough candidate principle: score a theory by how many relevant features it gets right, minus how many it gets wrong, with error size mattering and some bonus for detail. But that principle seems to put T9 above T4, and it doesn't explain why a true fragment (T2) loses to a false completion (T6). Is there a single truthlikeness principle that captures all of this, or do I have to give up a verdict? And what would that principle say about T11: either a left-handed man took the letter from the desk after 9 p.m., or a monkey took it from the vault at noon?
