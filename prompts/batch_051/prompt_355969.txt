I am a distributed-systems engineer who works on smart-contract platforms and on-chain dispute-resolution mechanisms. My working vocabulary is contracts-as-code, validators, consensus, escrow, slashing, exit rights, forks, liveness and safety, trust minimization, and a code-is-law outlook. I have no formal legal background.

Restate the debate over mandatory arbitration clauses and the privatization of justice in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. In your own words, present the strongest version of the pro-arbitration position (roughly: parties consent to a faster, cheaper, private forum, and enforcing that consent respects autonomy and freedom of contract) and the strongest version of the critical position (roughly: in many consumer and employment contracts there is no meaningful consent; arbitration lets repeat-player companies select sympathetic private adjudicators, suppress class-wide claims, and route disputes out of public courts whose open proceedings and precedents protect third parties as well as the litigants). Then translate each side into the concepts I use every day, for instance consent under duress versus an involuntary transaction, captured arbitrators versus compromised validators, class-action waivers versus restrictions on coordinated exit or fork, and the loss of public precedent versus the difference between a private settlement ledger and a public, shared one. Do not force the analogy where it does not fit.

Then give me a residue report. Which claims in the legal debate translate cleanly into my vocabulary, which claims did you have to distort to make them fit, and which claims have no counterpart at all? Finally, tell me what you think the residue means: is my vocabulary missing normative concepts the law supplies (and what would they look like if someone tried to build them as protocol constructs), or does the translation failure show that parts of the legal debate are less operationally crisp than they first appear, for example because words like consent and choice are carrying more weight than their actual mechanics can support?
