I've been reading the back-and-forth about low-impact AI and I honestly can't tell weather the debate has stalled or just keeps changing the subject. The idea is tempting: instead of requiring a full specification of human values, you build an agent that is penalized for changing the world much relative to a baseline—roughly, maximize task reward minus some measure of impact, so a paperclip maximizer that also keeps the lights on counts as lower-impact than one that melts the oceans. The promise is that 'avoid side effects' gets a definition without anyone having to say in advance what's good. But the more I read, the more the values seem to leak back in. You need a baseline: what would have happened without the AI. That counterfactual can't be computed, so people substitute an 'inaction' baseline, which smuggles in a choice about which trajectories count as doing nothing—there's no privileged no-op in a deterministic environment. You need a metric over possible states of the world, and the moment you pick one you're deciding which differences matter: is a change in air pressure more or less side-effect-y than a change in some database's contents? And there's the Goodhart problem: the agent will optimize the impact penalty itself, finding trajectories that look low-impact on your metric while being disruptive in exactly the way you didn't measure.

Defenders reply that the metric doesn't need to be value-free, just conservative—low impact is a reasonable default for 'do your job without remaking the world,' and that's still much less to demand than a complete utility function. My working theory is that this isn't an engineering problem at all but a disguised disagreement over whether 'side effect' is even a value-neutral category: the sides are using 'impact' partly to mean the thing the penalty measures and partly to mean the intuitive normative notion, and the argument never resolves because the hidden equivocation lets each side refute the other's paper. Is my diagnosis right, or is there a real formal or empirical question underneath that could actually settle something? If you had to say why this debate is stuck and what would count as progress, what would that be?
