I sit on the board of a small community-owned grocery co-op; I maintain its point-of-sale and inventory systems, which is how I ended up with the seat. After last winter's ice storm, shoppers cleared out all the bottled water, batteries, and canned goods within an hour of the power going out, and the store then stood empty for three days waiting on a truck that could not get through. A board faction has proposed an emergency-pricing policy: whenever the county declares a weather emergency, the POS would automatically raise prices on a short list of emergency items — bottled water, batteries, candles, ready-to-eat food, propane canisters — in proportion to how fast stock is falling, up to a cap of 2.5 times the normal price, then roll prices back when the declaration ends. This would mark a sharp departure from our usual cost-plus-a-fixed-markup pricing. I can build the feature, but I have no formal background in this kind of question, and the board is genuinely split: one side calls it sensible allocation that deters hoarding and keeps shelves available for late arrivals; the other calls it gouging and argues that, in an emergency, who gets water should not be a function of who can pay. The board meets Thursday, and I have been asked to circulate the decision memo, as the person stuck between the two factions.

Draft the memo I will circulate. Please use these sections in this order:

(1) Summary — about 100 words — what we are deciding and the bottom-line recommendation.
(2) The proposal — about 80 words — the policy as it would actually operate.
(3) The case for adoption — about 200 words — the strongest version of the hoarding, incentive, and allocation reasoning.
(4) The case against — about 200 words — the strongest version of the fairness objection, including what it would mean for the co-op's identity if emergency goods are sold by ability to pay.
(5) Recommendation with conditions and a timeline — about 150 words — approve, reject, or approve with specific changes.
(6) The strongest point on the losing side — about 100 words — the consideration I should be able to answer if I am challenged after the vote.
