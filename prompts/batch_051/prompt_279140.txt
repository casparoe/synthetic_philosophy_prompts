I recently stayed up late arguing with a friend about a coincidence that arrived while we were trying to make a decision that mattered to us—an unusual name and a date that kept surfacing in unrelated places, exactly when they bore on the choice. His line was the standard deflationary one: enlarge the reference class far enough, and some coincidence of that general kind becomes close to inevitable; the birthday paradox is the clean demonstration that our surprise tracks the wrong probability. I feel the force of that as a theory. It is simple, it unifies a huge range of anecdotes under one pattern, and it gives a mechanical test for deciding when a reported coincidence is worth taking seriously. Yet I cannot make myself believe the theory about our token case. That some hit was guaranteed to happen to someone does not by itself settle whether this hit, in this shape, after this history, was evidence of anything. I am not interested in a bare verdict. Tell me what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that yields, what it would take for the deflationary view to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of what it is to take a coincidence seriously on which the calculation and the conviction both survive.
