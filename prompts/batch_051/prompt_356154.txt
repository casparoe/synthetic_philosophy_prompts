ok so a friend doing history of science sent me this little argument and i cant shake it. it starts from the newton–leibniz and darwin–wallace cases and ends somewhere that feels obviously too strong, but each step on its own looks fine. i keep feeling the slip is around steps (3) to (5), but i can't name it properly.

(1) When a discovery is "in the air" — the background ideas, methods, instruments and social pressures are all in place — it tends to get made near-simultaneously by people working independently. calculus (newton, leibniz) and natural selection (darwin, wallace) are the famous cases, but the history of science is riddled with lesser-known ones.
(2) In those cases it's reasonable to say the discovery would have been made around then by someone or other anyway. not inevitable in any spooky deterministic sense, just robust: it didn't hinge on the peculiar genius of one particular person.
(3) If the discovery would have been made anyway without a given person, then the discovery can't have happened because of that person. they were not a difference-maker.
(4) Credit tracks difference-making: you deserve credit for a discovery only if it happened because of you — that is, had you never existed, it wouldn't have happened (or at least not then).
(5) So newton, leibniz, darwin and wallace don't strictly deserve credit for calculus or natural selection, in the sense of "the discovery happened because of them."
(6) But the same reasoning generalises. pick nearly any celebrated discovery and you'll find the field was ready, with near-misses or actual near-simultaneous work. so almost nobody deserves credit for the discoveries they're famous for.
(7) And if that's right, the whole practice of scientific prizes, eponymous laws and "great discoverer" histories rests on a mistake.

i want to keep (1) and (2) — they seem like a basically right picture of how science moves — and i also want to keep the ordinary conviction that darwin and wallace each did something creditable even though they were breathing the same air. so which step should i reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? i'd also be interested in whether 'credit' can be saved without collapsing back into the difference-making account, or whether the whole notion is just confused.
