A college near me recently rescinded a student's admission after a screenshot surfaced of a racist joke she had posted on social media at fourteen. The college said the post was incompatible with its values; elsewhere, the same college says young people should not be permanently judged by mistakes made before adulthood, and that its role is to educate rather than to condemn. What theory of moral character and sanction does a practice like rescinding an acceptance presuppose? In particular, does it presuppose that a fourteen-year-old's post is strong evidence of the person she now is, that the university is not punishing her but protecting a community, or that the purpose of the sanction is to express the institution's values rather than to deter or rehabilitate? I would like the implicit theory reconstructed, with the parts the practice strictly needs separated from the parts it merely fits, and an explanation of what the university would have to change if it replaced that theory with a forward-looking one.
