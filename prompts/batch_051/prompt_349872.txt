I've spent the past few weeks trying to write a paper on rational choice under computational limits, and my advisor and I have gotten stuck on an argument that seems to show the position I wanted to defend is empty. We cannot tell whether it has a subtle flaw or simply forces us to pay a price we had been ignoring. The argument runs as follows.

(1) Satisficing is a stopping rule: one sets an aspiration level for how good an option must be and accepts the first option that meets or exceeds it. (2) The aspiration level is either fixed by considerations that track the choice situation — the likely quality of the options available, the cost of further search, what is at stake — or it is not. (3) If it is not fixed by any such considerations, the threshold is arbitrary, and a choice produced by an arbitrary threshold cannot be credited to a rational procedure in any substantial sense. (4) If it is fixed by such considerations, then fixing it requires forming expectations about the environment and weighing the expected gains from more search against its expected costs. (5) But that is precisely the optimization over search that satisficing was introduced to avoid; the agent is now doing the same expected-utility work as an unbounded optimizer, only under a different description. (6) Therefore satisficing either collapses into optimizing or rests on an arbitrary and unjustified threshold. (7) Since satisficing is the canonical account of what a bounded agent does instead of optimizing, it follows that bounded rationality has no independent content: either full optimization is smuggled back in, or the supposedly rational procedure is no more answerable to reasons than flipping a coin.

I find (7) very hard to accept, but I am not sure which premise I dislike least. I would most like to keep (3), because abandoning it seems to concede that a decision procedure can count as rational without being answerable to the choice situation at all. Which premise or step should one reject, and what does rejecting it force one to give up about the point of satisficing? In your answer, please separate what is settled from what remains genuinely contested among people working on this, and do not present a contested diagnosis as the consensus.
