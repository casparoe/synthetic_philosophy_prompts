Behavioral ecology person here, running into a wall. Whenever someone claims a display is a handicap in the handicap-principle sense, I can usually retell the same data as an index: the signal is reliable because it's tied to quality, and the cost is just a side effect that doesn't do honest-making work. A postdoc says the way to separate them is to lower the cost of producing the signal while holding the quality-to-signal link fixed, and see whether low-quality individuals start faking. That sounds right in theory, but I cant see how to run it without breaking the link you are trying to hold fixed. Design the best practical test you can of whether cost is doing the honesty work rather than just coming along for the ride. Say what result would point to each account, and then be honest about what even a clean result would leave open. Is the because-of-the-cost part the kind of thing an experiment can settle at all?
