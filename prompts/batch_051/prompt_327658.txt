ok so in my phil of mind class we're on the Malcolm vs Dennett debate about whether dreams are experiences, and our essay prompt asks whether their positions actually contradict each other or can be reconciled. I keep going back and forth and would love to hear how you'd lay it out.

The way I read it, in Dreaming (1959) Malcolm argues that a person sound asleep isn't conscious, judging, or aware of anything, so it's a confusion to think of a dream as a mental event that occurs during sleep; the waking report isn't evidence for some earlier inner process, it's more like the story is all there is to the dream, and there's no further fact about what was going on in your mind during the night. But then Dennett in Are Dreams Experiences? (1976) wants to say dreams are still experiences; he just rejects the assumption that the experience has to unfold in real time while you're unconscious. He floats a cassette idea where the whole dream narrative could get composed and loaded into memory at or around waking, so there's something it's like to dream, but the experience isn't located in the sleep period the way we normally assume.

I definately can't tell whether these are genuinely in conflict or whether Dennett is basically granting Malcolm's point that there's no ongoing conscious stream during sleep and then quietly buying back the word experience by relocating it to wake-up time. The TA wants candidate reconciliations—different senses of experience, different targets, one side changing the subject—and then a verdict on which reconciliations are honest and which are just special pleading. Sometimes it feels like Dennett's whole move is a verbal trick: if the dream experience is just a memory inserted when you wake, hasn't he conceded everything Malcolm actually cared about? But maybe Malcolm is just assuming an experience has to be contemporaneous with the period the dream is about, and that's the thing Dennett is entitled to reject. If they can't be reconciled, who has the better argument? And if they can, is the reconciliation just a way of making Malcolm sound right by changing what Dennett means by experience? Sorry if this is long, I just don't want to miss a distinction neither author drew explicitly that would make both of them come out clean.
