I read some forum stuff about how a sad melody basically imitates a sad persons body—droopy pitch, slow tempo, falling phrases like sighs—and that makes me want to do a joke piece instead of an argument.

Write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 400–500 words, reporting that a startup has finally settled the debate by scanning songs and giving each one a "measured sadness score" based only on the music itself: how much the melody droops, how slow it drags, how many downward intervals it has, etc. Keep it deadpan, like a tech product launch story.

The humor should come from getting the philosophy-of-music stuff right, not from lazy jokes about people crying in the car. Try to work these in naturally: the difference between feeling sad and hearing sadness in a song; the idea that music is sad because its shape resembles sad human behavior (slumping, sighing, dragging); the puzzle that people go out of their way to listen to sad music if it genuinely makes them sad; the fact that which chords or scales count as sad differs between cultures, so it cant be read straight off the acoustics; and the weirdness that the music isnt sad about anything in particular, while real sadness is usually about some actual thing.

Include at least one quote from a "concerned" music philosopher who says the sadness is supplied by the listener and that the startup is measuring the wrong thing. Dont wink or explain the joke as you go, but a reader should still be able to tell what view is being made fun of.

At the very end, after the article, add a last line beginning "Summary:" that says in one sentence what the piece is satirizing.
