Kierkegaard's pseudonymous work Fear and Trembling presents Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac as involving a teleological suspension of the ethical: a higher duty to God is taken to suspend the universal demands of ethics, and Abraham is held up as a knight of faith rather than as a murderer. If the book is right about Abraham, doesn't it follow that any believer who reports a private divine call may set aside ordinary moral duties whenever the two conflict? And if so, doesn't that reduce ethics to a divine-command view in which no shared moral order can stand? Please give a committed answer: take and defend a definite position on how the book should be read on this point and on what actually follows from it, rather than only surveying the interpretive options.
