I have been trying to work out how a rational agent should revise their beliefs when new evidence arrives in a form that does not make any single proposition certain. Suppose I catch a brief glimpse of a piece of cloth in poor lighting. Before the glimpse, my probabilities over its color are spread over blue, green, red, and a few other possibilities. After the glimpse, I have become more confident that it is blue and less confident that it is green, but I would not say I am certain of any particular color proposition. Similarly, I might receive a lab result that looks abnormal but could easily be a false positive; my probabilities over the patient having disease A, disease B, or no disease all shift, but none goes to 0 or 1. Ordinary Bayesian conditionalization seems to require that I conditionalize on some proposition that has become certain, such as the proposition that I had such-and-such a visual experience or that the lab report says such-and-such. But I want to remain modest even about introspection and about the reliability of the report, and the real change seems to be directly in the probabilities over the relevant partition of possibilities, not in any evidence proposition I can accept with certainty.

My question is whether there is a standard name for this kind of update, in which the probabilities of a partition of possibilities shift without any cell going to 1, and in which the probabilities of other propositions conditional on each cell of the partition stay fixed. I am also interested in whether this kind of update is a genuine alternative to conditionalization, or whether it can always be represented as conditionalization on some more complicated proposition after all. Is the requirement that the conditional probabilities stay fixed best understood as a definition, a norm, or merely a convenient assumption? I am not looking for just a label; I want the concept carved at its joints. What is essential to the update rule, what is optional, and what distinguishes it from nearby ideas like simply changing one's probabilities without any rule, or conditionalizing on a carefully chosen perception proposition?
