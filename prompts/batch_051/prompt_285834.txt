A housing cooperative founded in 1974 has in its bylaws the following clause: "Each member shall attend the annual general meeting in person, and no member may be represented at that meeting by proxy." In 2023 a member who has been hospitalized all week attends the annual meeting by real-time two-way video, with his camera off. He answers the roll call, speaks to one motion, and votes on each item as it arises; the others hear him at every relevant moment, and no one has cast a vote for him. The board must now decide whether he attended "in person" within the meaning of the clause. The community is self-consciously a tight culture: it prides itself on explicit rules, strict enforcement, and low tolerance for deviation. Work through four readings — the plain words, the clause's underlying purpose, what the 1974 drafters would most plausibly have said had they been asked about real-time video attendance, and what a reasonable member reading the bylaw today would understand. Then say which reading the board should adopt. In doing so, address whether the community's tight-culture self-understanding pushes toward literal physical presence, or toward treating the clause as demanding full, personal, real-time participation however delivered. What does your answer imply about how much rule-following a society should demand when an old rule meets a new technology it never anticipated?
