I'm working through dominance arguments in a game theory course, and a classmate has been pressing the following argument, which they take to show that allowing mixed strategies adds nothing to iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies. The conclusion strikes me as too strong, but I haven't been able to locate the place where the reasoning goes wrong — if it does. I'd appreciate a careful step-by-step evaluation.

The argument runs as follows. (1) Consider a finite two-player game, and let s be a pure strategy of player i. Suppose some mixed strategy σ of player i strictly dominates s, in the sense that for every pure strategy t of the other player, u_i(σ, t) > u_i(s, t); by linearity, this is equivalent to demanding the inequality for every mixed strategy of the other player. (2) For any fixed t, u_i(σ, t) is a weighted average of the payoffs u_i(s_j, t) of the pure strategies s_j in the support of σ. (3) If every pure strategy in the support of σ yielded a payoff no greater than u_i(s, t) against t, the weighted average could not be greater than u_i(s, t); since the average is greater, at least one pure strategy s_j in the support must itself yield a payoff strictly greater than u_i(s, t) against t. (4) Thus, for every opponent strategy t, there is some pure strategy s_j in the support of σ that beats s against t. (5) Therefore, some single pure strategy s_j in the support of σ beats s against every opponent strategy t — that is, s is strictly dominated by a pure strategy. (6) Hence any strategy that would be eliminated by mixed-strategy dominance would already be eliminated by pure-strategy dominance, and iterated elimination with mixed strategies is redundant.

Please tell me whether the argument is valid. If it is not, identify the exact step at which it fails, state the correct quantified version of the claim at that point, and give a small concrete game — payoffs as small integers — in which a pure strategy survives iterated elimination by pure-strategy dominance but is eliminated once mixed-strategy dominance is allowed. In closing, separate what is mathematically settled about this issue from what remains genuinely contested among people who work on game-theoretic solution concepts, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
