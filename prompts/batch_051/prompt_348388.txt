I've been trying to get my view of LLM "hallucination" into some kind of order, and I'd like an audit of whether my current position hang together. Here are the commitments I'm working with. (1) Frankfurt's core contrast is right: the liar deliberately tries to get someone to accept what he believes false, whereas the bullshitter is defined by indifference to whether what he says is true — the falsity of any particular utterance is incidental. (2) An LLM has no beliefs, aims, or genuine speech acts, so an individual hallucinated output is not literally a lie and not literally bullshit in that sense; the model isn't indifferent to truth, it simply isn't in the truth business. (3) But it also seems wrong to call hallucinations "errors", since error is a truth-tracking mechanism misfiring, and next-token prediction is not a truth-tracking mechanism. (4) So I'm inclined to treat a hallucinated utterance as a distinct category — call it unasserted plausible text — neither lie, bullshit, nor error, at the level of the utterance itself. (5) And yet I also hold that the broader practice of presenting these systems as answerers and search-replacements is bullshit in something close to Frankfurt's sense: the whole arrangement appears set up to generate convincing text while remaining indifferent to truth. (6) Finally, I think Frankfurt's notion was always meant to apply as much to a kind of public discourse — hot air, spin, the speech of the bull session — as to an individual speaker's motives. Go through this set and tell me which commitments pull against each other, which pairs are only apparently in tension, and how severe each real tension is. For each real tension, give me the cheapest revision and what it costs. Dont hedge — I want your definite verdict on whether this set, as stated, can be kept whole, and which single change would do the most work.
