Write the joint statement that a defender of a "DNA literally codes for proteins" view and a defender of a "code talk is just a productive metaphor" view could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Assume both sides are scientifically literate and agree on the molecular facts: nucleotide sequences are transcribed and translated, triplets map to amino acids, the code is redundant, a few triplets are stops, and the mapping is arbitrary in the sense that other assignments would have been chemically possible. They disagree about what those facts license. I want a numbered "agreed claims" section first, with claims that actually cost each side something rather than pleasant truisms, then a disagreements table. Phrase the residual disagreement so each side would accept the wording of its own position. Then lay out the cruxes, and make sure you cover at least these: (i) whether a codon can genuinely misrepresent—be "wrong"—in the absence of an external interpreter, (ii) whether the translation machinery counts as an interpreter or is just a further bit of causal chain, and (iii) whether the fact that the current code is a product of selection settles anything about its semantic status. For each crux, give the case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; the case of a synthetic biologist reassigning a codon in an engineered organism is fair game, but you should spell out why each side thinks it favors them. Don't resolve the debate—just write the document both camps could sign.
