I'm running a small philosophy meetup for undergrads and I want a "which account of the value of truth do you actually hold?" sorting quiz. The hook: nearly everyone says they value truth, but people split fast once you seperate (a) the value of having true beliefs from (b) the value of telling the truth, and once you give them cases where truth and welfare pull apart.

Please write 9 questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key. Audience is bright undergrads with no epistemology background, so define any jargon in the answer explanations, not in the questions.

The questions should sort people by how they answer cases, not by which label they prefer, so no question like "do you value truth for its own sake?" and no answer options that name positions.

At least these cases have to appear:
- a placebo that only works if the patient believes a falsehood about their treatment;
- a dying person asking a question where the truthful answer will make their last days miserable;
- a researcher who has a true result that they know is likely to be misused;
- the bullshit-versus-lying distinction (someone who makes sure they've got the facts right before they speak, versus someone who genuinely doesn't care either way);
- a community whose false founding story increases cooperation and stability;
- a self-deception case (someone believes a comfortable falsehood while sort of knowing better);
- a child asking whether Santa exists.

You can add one or two of your own cases, ideally ones where having-true-beliefs and telling-the-truth visibly come apart.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these leaves:
- strong veritism: getting and giving the truth is worth it even when it makes everyone worse off;
- eudaimonist instrumentalism: truth matters only as a means to well-being;
- a constitutive-norm view: belief and assertion are governed by truth-aiming norms that bind independently of welfare, but this isn't a claim that truth is a cosmic intrinsic value;
- a pragmatist-collapse view: "truth" just means what it's useful to believe, so the quiz's central question dissolves;
- a life-serving fictionalism: some untruths are conditions of flourishing, and the unconditional demand for truth is itself a preference to be evaluated by what it does for us.

Then flag two things. First, any leaf-pairs your questions can't separate and why — I suspect strong veritism and the constitutive-norm view will agree on most answers but for different reasons, so if that's right, say how the scoring key should handle it so the quiz doesn't just merge them. Second, unoccupied cells in the underlying space, e.g. "truth is intrinsically valuable but truth-telling is only instrumentally valuable" — is that cell empty, incoherent, or just unpopular? Give a one-line diagnosis of each empty cell you find.

Keep it usable as a handout: one page for the questions, one for the scoring.
