Something about the way constitutions handle their own amendment rules has been bothering me, and I want to understand what picture of law a practice like this commits a country to. Imagine a written constitution that does two things at once. It sets out a single amendment procedure — say, a two-thirds vote of the legislature, ratified by a majority of the regions — and it declares that this procedure may be used to change any part of the constitution except one: a particular clause, perhaps about the country's form of government or its basic rights, is declared unamendable, often along with the clause that declares it unamendable. Later, a two-thirds majority forms, completes the ratification procedure exactly as written, and amends the constitution by deleting the supposedly untouchable clause. When the amendment is challenged, the courts strike it down, not because the procedure was misapplied, but because the content of the amendment touched something the procedure itself may not reach.

What view of what gives a rule its binding force does this practice presuppose? It seems to rest on the idea that some rules are not products of the ordinary amending power and stand above it, so that the amending power can be used only within limits it cannot revise. But that is puzzling. If the amendment procedure is what makes new constitutional rules valid, how can there be a rule that the procedure is powerless to amend? What makes that rule valid, if the procedure is not its source? Does the practice really require a two-level picture — ordinary rules changed through the procedure, and a deeper layer that constitutes the procedure in the first place? If it does, why should the content of that deeper layer be whatever the original drafters happened to write down, rather than whatever the current political community actually accepts? I'd like someone to reconstruct the implicit theory here, say where the practice genuinely forces it and where the practice is compatible with several different theories, and work through what would have to change if we instead held that any amendment passed under the current procedure is valid no matter what part of the constitution it amends.
