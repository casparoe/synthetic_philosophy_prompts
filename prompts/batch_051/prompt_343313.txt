Longtime reader here. I keep noticing a structural bind in official apologies for historical wrongs, and I don't have a name for it—I'm honestly not sure whether its a real pattern or me overgeneralizing, so push back if I've got this wrong.

Take a state that apologizes for something generations ago, like a residential-school system or a colonial massacre. The current government stands up and says, in effect, 'we apologize for what we did.' For that to make sense as an apology at all—rather than some unrelated present-day people saying sorry for strangers—the speaker has to lean on continuity: the apologizing state is, in the relevant sense, the same institution that did the harm. But for the apology to mean anything, the same speaker also has to show the state has changed: new people, different norms, repudiated policies. And the more effectively it shows that, the less it sounds like the same agent that did the wrong. Each condition seems to pull against the other; the statement has to say 'we are the ones who did this' and 'we are not those people' at the same time, with the same 'we'. You can see the same bind in apologies for slavery or wartime forced labor, and I assume in a lot of institutional apologies generally.

What I see people doing in these debates is bouncing between two senses of 'we'—the persisting institution versus the present generation—to escape the bind, but that looks like equivocation rather than a resolution. Is there an established concept for this exact bind in work on collective agency, corporate responsibility, or official apologies? What are the live options: deny that one of the two conditions is really required, accept that official apologies can only ever be partial, or something else? And if the nearest named things are 'standing to apologize' or 'the non-identity problem applied to institutions,' I'd want to know precisely how this differs. I'm not really after the label—I want the carving, including the boundary cases. If the setup is confused, or the obvious answer is that individual apologies have the same problem because individuals change too, just tell me that plainly instead of handing me a nicer name for the confusion.
