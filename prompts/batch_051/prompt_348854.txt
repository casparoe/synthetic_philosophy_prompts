Construct a thought experiment where total and average utilitarianism come apart in the opposite of the usual direction: the total view prefers the smaller population (dont add the extra people), and the average view prefers the larger one. Start with one line saying what the case is supposed to show about how population size matters. Constraints: keep it small and concrete enough to enumerate (a household, a hospital ward, a housing block), no universes, no infinite populations, and the only change between the two options is whether some new people exist at specified welfare levels — nobody is being moved around. Negative welfare levels are allowed, but be explicit about what zero means. After the scenario, run the arithmetic for both views, identify the feature that makes the reversal possible and why the standard positive-welfare cases can't produce it, then say what happens if you delete that feature, and finish with the strongest reply an average utilitarian could give to keep the case from counting against them.
