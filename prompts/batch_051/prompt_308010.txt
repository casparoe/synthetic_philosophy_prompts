I've got this rule I follow and I'm not sure if it's actually smart or just a bias I dressed up as a rule. Whenever someone tells me the world is becoming less violent — in a news article, a YouTube essay, whatever — I immediatly check whether they mean the total number of victims or the rate per 100,000 people. If its per capita, I basically stop taking the claim seriously. My reasoning: per capita numbers can fall just because population grows, even if more actual human beings are getting hurt. Like, double the population and the same number of murders looks like half the murder rate, but the same number of people are still dead. That feels like a trick, not progress. So my policy is that raw absolute numbers are the honest statistic and per capita numbers are mostly for making things look nicer than they are.

Last week my history teacher pushed back.
