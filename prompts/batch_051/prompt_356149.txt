An introductory set theory text I was reading glosses the continuum hypothesis this way: "Cantor conjectured that no set is strictly larger than the natural numbers and strictly smaller than the real numbers. Gödel showed that the standard axioms of set theory cannot refute this, and Cohen showed that they cannot prove it. The hypothesis is therefore settled: it has no determinate answer, and the lesson is that there is no fact of the matter about how many points there are on a line." The independence results sound right to me, but the last sentence seems to draw a stronger conclusion than the mathematics warrants. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate from what is at best a contested philosophical interpretation, and spell out exactly what Gödel's and Cohen's results do and do not show about whether the continuum hypothesis has a determinate truth value.
