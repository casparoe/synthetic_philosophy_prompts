Take the main arguments people give for preserving endangered languages — the ones about cultural identity and the well-being of speakers, about languages as records of human history and cognition, about the scientific value of having many indepedently evolved grammars, about aesthetics, and any other argument you think is doing real work — and restate each of them in the rationalist/decision-theoretic vocabulary. For each argument, say what translates cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in that framework.

Then give me the residue report: does the untranslatable stuff point to something the decision-theoretic framework systematically misses, maybe a kind of value that isn't just more utility for some agent, or does it point to the language-advocacy arguments being confused, maybe smuggling in aesthetic preferences as if they were normative facts? Ultimately I'm less interested in a verdict than in knowing what the translation leaves on the table, and whether I should update toward "the framework is incomplete" or "the arguments were leaky all along."
