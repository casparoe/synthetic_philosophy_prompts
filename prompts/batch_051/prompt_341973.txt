I teach an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar and I'm setting the final exam. I want one closed-book essay question on the meta-problem of consciousness, 45 minutes, worth 25 marks, target answer around 600 words. The trouble with most questions on this topic is that a student can get by just reciting the whole hard-problem/easy-problem distinction and name-dropping illusionism, so I want one that can't be answered by that routine. I'd like it to force students to actually work through the deflationary thought: if a theory could explain, in purely functional or cognitive terms, why we find consciousness so puzzling and why it feels like there's an explanatory gap, does that dissolve the hard problem, leave it completely untouched, or something in between? And I want them to say wether a solution to the meta-problem can be genuinely neutral between realism and deflationism about phenomenal consciousness.

Please write me the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that says which specific moves separate an A from a B answer and which specific misunderstandings should cost marks. I'd rather the marking scheme be a list of named moves than vague descriptions.
