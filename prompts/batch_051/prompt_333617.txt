You are a professional philosopher specializing in theories of well-being and prudential value. Below are the settled verdicts of a colleague, Maya, about a series of well-being cases. I want you to work out what principle she is implicitly applying and predict her verdicts on some new cases. Take your time, and do not commit to a single principle too quickly: begin by brainstorming several candidate principles that could fit her track record, noting where they agree and where they come apart.

Maya's recorded cases:

1. Amara has lived for twenty-five years connected to an experience machine. Inside the simulation, she seems to write celebrated novels and seems to be surrounded by loving, interesting friends; none of this is real. Her actual desires are for real achievement and real relationships, and she would be horrified to learn the truth. Maya's verdict: Amara's well-being is low.

2. Ben is a virologist who spends a decade doing grueling, lonely, miserable fieldwork and succeeds in developing a cheap treatment for a neglected disease. He is left with little day-to-day pleasure, but he achieved the goal that mattered most to him. Maya's verdict: Ben's well-being is high.

3. Celia believes her partner is faithful, her colleagues respect her, and her friends enjoy her company; in fact each of these beliefs is false, though she will never discover this, and she feels deeply happy and secure. Maya's verdict: Celia's well-being is low.

4. Dev's only desire is to watch television and eat food he enjoys; he has no interest in more demanding pursuits, finds his life satisfying, and succeeds in living exactly as he wishes. Maya's verdict: Dev's well-being is fairly high, though not as high as Ben's.

Now here are the new cases. For each one, predict whether Maya would judge the person's well-being as high, low, or in between, and explain which candidate principle that prediction would line up with.

A. Nadia's sole, fully informed, stable desire is to count the blades of grass in her small yard with great precision, using a notation she invented. She succeeds at this, and the activity leaves her calm and mildly pleased. She has no desire for friendship, achievement, knowledge, or status beyond the count.

B. Omar has a stable, fully informed, autonomously formed desire to plug into an experience machine for the rest of his life. He knows the life inside will be entirely simulated, and that is fine with him: what he wants is the felt experience of writing novels and being loved. Once plugged in, he will never learn the truth.

C. Paul spends his career seeking an award from an academy he believes is the most prestigious in his field. He wins, and he feels deeply fulfilled. In fact the academy is a fraudulent front, the award is worthless, and he would not have wanted it had he known. He never learns.

D. Elena is a celebrated architect whose public buildings are widely used and admired, and she has a devoted partner and friends who genuinely care for her. But none of this is the life she wants: her fully informed and stable desire is to live quietly as a gardener, and she experiences her current life as mildly alienated, though she is not miserable.

Having made your predictions, please also do the following rather than only giving guesses. Identify at least three candidate principles about well-being that could be fitted to Maya's recorded verdicts, and say which of them her recorded verdicts already rule out. For each candidate principle that survives, state its prediction for each new case. Identify which new case or cases best discriminate among the surviving candidate principles, and explain why. Finally, state which principle you think Maya is implicitly running, what residual uncertainty remains, and what further case would settle it.
