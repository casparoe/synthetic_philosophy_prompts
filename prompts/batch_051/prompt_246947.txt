I've been trying to get clear on the Williams/McDowell debate about internal and external reasons, and specifically the objections to the internal reasons thesis. Ive got a list of the ones I keep seeing, and I dont need those repeated:

- the content objection: internalism can't make sense of categorical moral reasons, which seem to apply to people regardless of what they happen to want;
- the amoralist objection: a psychopath who lacks any moral motivation still seems to have reasons not to harm others, so reasons can't require a motivational foothold;
- the conversion objection: someone can acquire reasons by coming to see the world accurately, even if the motivation wasn't there before, so external reason statements aren't just false or incoherent;
- the fetishism objection: making reasons depend on pre-existing desires treats practical reasoning as a kind of fetishistic attachment to your current motivational set.

I know these aren't all equally strong, but thats fine. What I want is an objection to Williams's internal reasons thesis that is not on this list and not just a variant of any of them — not a rewording, not a special case, not a combination. Ideally something that attacks the picture from a different angle, not from moral categoricalness or from the possibility of conversion. Then tell me honestly whether the objection you come up with is any good.
