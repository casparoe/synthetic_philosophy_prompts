I'm a software engineer and don't have any formal philosophy background, so apologies if this is a standard question. There's an asymmetry in how people blame companies that I can't get my head round, and I can't tell wether anything justifies it.

Take two cases. In the first, a car manufacturer builds software that detects emissions tests and derates the engine during them; when it comes out, everyone says "the company lied" and the company gets punished. In the second, a group of traders at a bank collude to rig an interest-rate benchmark over several years; when that comes out, the bank calls them "a few bad apples" and the blame mostly sticks to the individual traders, not the bank as an entity. In both cases you have employees acting inside a corporate structure, using company tools and time, with harm that lands at company level. The second seems *more* coordinated, if anything, since it required a whole group acting together. The obvious differences are that the first involved a piece of software built into the product line and maybe senior sign-off, while the second was a process a group kept inside a desk and the bank denies knowing about it. But that just relocates the question: why should "senior management didn't formally endorse it" let the company itself off the hook, when the company is the thing that hired, paid, and promoted those people?

What is doing the work in our judgements here? Is it official policy versus unofficial practice, whether the firm made money from the action, whether there's a written product versus a process, whether the action fits some idea of corporate intent, or just who has better PR? Test whatever distinction you come up with against a few nastier cases: an unofficial quota system that makes fraud the only way to hit targets, a decentralised company with no board or single decision-maker, and a firm that quietly stops maintaining a safety feature and then lies about it. Does the asymmetry survive, or should we stop talking about companies as liars at all? Please answer in British English.
