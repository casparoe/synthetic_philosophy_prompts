I've been trying to wrap my head around Aumann's agreement theorem in epistemic game theory. As I understand it, two rational Bayesians with the same prior can't agree to disagree: if their posterior beliefs for some event are common knowledge, they have to be equal. The usual takeaway seems to be that persistent disagreement must come from private information, or from someone failing to condition on what the other person's opinion reveals about their information. But suppose we now get most of our information through personalized feeds, group chats, and ranking algorithms that decide which headlines each of us sees. Nobody has a clear picture of what the other person was shown, and arguably you don't even have a clear picture of you're own feed. Does Aumann's argument still have force in that environment? If so, what exactly would have to be common knowledge for two people to be forced into agreement? If not, is the theorem just assuming a world of shared public information, and what should replace it as the diagnosis of why we disagree so hard?
