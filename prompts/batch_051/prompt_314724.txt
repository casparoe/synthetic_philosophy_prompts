I keep running into "guns don't kill people, people kill people" used to end arguments about anything with a mechanism in it. Someone said it to me about a new intersection near my apartment that basically forces pedestrians to dash across four lanes, and told me the intersection isn't the problem, the drivers are. But I cant stop thinking about speed bumps: nobody has to signal me to slow down, the bump itself does it. Isnt that the object doing something rather than just sitting there? I want to understand what that slogan could even be claiming. It could mean a gun without a user is inert metal, so the person is the cause. It could mean laws should target behavior and not ban objects. It could mean technology itself has no values. It could mean something about where responsibility lands. Some of those sound obviously true, some sound false, and some maybe depend on what you mean by "cause" or "politics." Can you pull apart the distinct precise claims hiding in that one sentence, say which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false, and then tell me which reading is doing the work when someone uses it to argue that a designed object can't itself be political?
