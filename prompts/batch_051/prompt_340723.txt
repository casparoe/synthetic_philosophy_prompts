I'm trying to reverse-engineer an intermediate appellate judge who keeps drawing defamation cases about ratings, rankings, and forecasts, because I need to predict her call on something new before we file. Her track record, as best I can summarize it: she dismissed as opinion a restaurant critic's one-star pan that called a bistro 'a culinary trainwreck'; she dismissed a city magazine's 'five worst landlords' ranking that named names but gave no specifics; she protected a financial analyst who wrote 'we project ACME will breach its debt covenants by Q3' on the ground that a forward-looking projection with disclosed assumptions isn't a false statement of fact; she protected a credit-rating agency's downgrade of a municipality to B- even after the city showed the rating had been run on stale budget figures through a model the agency keeps confidential, saying a rating is an assessment of creditworthiness, not a factual report; and yet she let a defamation claim survive where a trade journal gave a contractor an 'F' and added 'consistently fails to pay subcontractors within 90 days,' reasoning that the nonpayment claim is a verifiable fact and the grade doesn't turn it into opinion.

The new case: a rental platform shows, next to each listing, a single 0–100 'landlord score' produced by an algorithm that blends tenant reviews with municipal code-violation records. The platform won't disclose how it weights the components, and directly under the number it says 'objective measure of building quality.' A landlord who received a 41/100 is suing, pointing out that his building has no open enforcement violations and arguing that the score falsely implies he runs a slum property. Her court has never dealt with a pure algorithmic score marketed as objective before.

How should I expect her to rule if she's trying to stay consistent with her own history? Which of the earlier cases is the closest analog, and what rule best explains the whole pattern? And if the precedents genuinely underdetermine this, I want to know what principle she'd be adopting either way and what future case would force her to choose between them. I care less about the outcome then about the exact line her reasoning draws.
