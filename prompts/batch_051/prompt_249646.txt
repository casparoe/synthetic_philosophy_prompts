I've come to accept a view about infinite regress arguments that I want you to stress-test. The view is that no regress is vicious or benign simply in virtue of its structure. Viciousness, on my view, is a verdict about a regress taken together with a standing explanatory or justificatory demand, not an intrinsic property of the sequence considered on its own. More precisely, I think a regress is vicious exactly when two conditions hold together: first, the task that generates the regress requires a terminus, something that discharges the original demand rather than passing it along to the next item; and second, each step merely defers that same demand to its successor. Where no completion-demand is legitimately in force, the regress is benign even if it never terminates. I take the truth regress (a proposition's being true yields a further truth about that proposition's being true, and so on) and causal regresses in cosmologies that posit an infinite past to be benign in just this way. One consequence I endorse is that the familiar move of dismissing a view because it leads to an infinite regress is always enthymematic: the critic must separately show that a completion-demand is genuinely in force and that each step merely defers it.

Tell me what would count against this, not just the stock objections. I want the cases, arguments, formal results, and developments in adjacent debates that would have to be true for something like the structural view of regresses, on which viciousness is a feature of the regress itself, to be right and for my demand-relative view to be false. Where are the live risks? In particular: which alleged counterexamples, a regress that is vicious without any completion-demand, or a regress that meets both of my conditions yet is still benign, actually bite, and which are merely benign regresses being misdescribed? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed defender of my view would themselves concede as counting against it and which they would dismiss as re-describing the demand or changing the subject. Finally, be honest about whether the demand-relative element insulates the view too much, whether the view can always be saved by denying that a completion-demand is legitimate, and whether that insulation, if real, is a defect in a view of this kind or a feature.
