I'm taking an upper-level seminar this term on the ethics of human enhancement, and we have spent the last two weeks on Persson and Savulescu's argument that cognitive enhancement without a matching moral enhancement is a disaster waiting to happen, because it gives people greater powers to do harm while leaving their motives roughly as they are. Our instructor keeps pushing us to stop treating this as a yes-or-no debate about whether moral bioenhancement is permissible, and to ask instead how any real policy built on the Persson–Savulescu worry would behave once institutions and self-interested people got hold of it. I have been trying to do that with a concrete mechanism, and I would like help thinking through how it would actually play out.

The proposal is this. A government maintains a public list of catastrophe-competent offices: positions with authority over nuclear weapons, pathogen research with pandemic potential, and large-scale training runs of frontier AI models. No one may take up one of these offices without first completing a six-month moral bioenhancement protocol, combining a clinically appropriate pharmaceutical component with intensive role-playing moral training written by an adversarial red team. Thereafter, each incumbent faces an annual moral fitness audit. Some audit scenarios are announced in advance, but others are unannounced temptation tests: a planted chance to leak confidential information, accept a bribe, forge a safety certification, or cover up a near miss. Scoring is done by a panel of officials plus fellow incumbents, with the writers of the temptation tests kept anonymous even from the panel. One failed audit triggers mandatory remediation; a second failure, or a refusal to undergo the initial enhancement, makes a person permanently ineligible for the listed offices, though they remain free to hold any other job.

I want you to treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position, and especially not as a question of whether moral bioenhancement works. What would this filter do to the pool of people who seek these offices, and who would self-select out of them? How would candidates, their institutional employers, and the audit agency itself game the scheme? Where does it break down, and which of those failures are patchable without gutting the original purpose?

Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
