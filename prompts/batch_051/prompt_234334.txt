A cohousing group near me has adopted a literal version of tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters — as a decision rule: every collective choice (meal plans, thermostat settings, conflict procedures, whether to get a dog) has to be justified in writing by its expected effect on craving and suffering, and any appeal to pleasure, fun, beauty, achievement, or meaning is officially disregarded, though these can be mentioned as side effects. I'm not asking whether the view is right; I want to know how the rule plays out. Over a year, what do the written justifications start to look like, how do people relabel things so they fit the ledger, how is the rule gamed, who leaves and who digs in, and does the community end up selecting for actual tranquility or for the best rhetoriticians of suffering? Also, can the rule even be applied to itself without a regress — if someone proposes keeping or scrapping the rule, does the justification have to appeal only to craving and suffering too — and are the failure modes fixable without abandoning the rule entirely?
