Please explain the following tension in Lewis's scorekeeping model of conversation. A presupposition is normally understood as a proposition that must already belong to the conversational score before an utterance is felicitous, yet the rule of accommodation appears to allow an utterance to introduce its own presuppositions into the score after the fact if no participant objects. Explain how these two commitments can be reconciled, and address whether the rule of accommodation is best construed as a descriptive generalisation about how interpreters actually update the score or as a normative rule governing what speakers are entitled to presuppose. Use an example in which a speaker presupposes something clearly informative, and discuss whether the model thereby becomes too permissive about what can be added to the common ground without any explicit assertion.
