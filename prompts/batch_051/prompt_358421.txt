ok so for a reading group on whether personal data should be treated as property, I said I'd write down my actual position so the others can poke at it, and before I send it around I want a consistency check from someone who won't just be nice to me. Heres the list of things I think I believe:

1. People have a genuine property right in data about them. Companies should generally need consent to collect or use it, and individuals should be able to sell it or license it.
2. The best justification is Lockean-ish: the data is generated by your own activity, so you've mixed your labor with something and thereby acquired a claim to it.
3. But facts themselves can't be owned. If a friend remembers that I have a dog, or a journalist reports true things about a public figure, that's not a violation of anyone's data rights, and I don't want a regime where you own your facts.
4. Data is non-rivalrous: if I use data about you, I don't use it up or leave less for you, the way I would if I took your car or your kidney.
5. The wrong in unauthorized data collection is the collection itself, not just the downstream consequences. Even if a company vacuums up my location history and never does anything with it, something has already gone wrong.
6. Once I freely sell or license my data, the right transfers for real. The buyer can use it in ways I dislike, and I cant later revoke consent to that particular use. I think of it like selling a chair.
7. Truly anonymous aggregate data isn't about anyone in the relevant sense, so using it for studies or models needs no one's consent.
8. Helping to produce a public good doesn't give you an ownership share: if my shopping history helps train a recommender that benefits strangers, I don't own a slice of the recommender.

So: does this set hang together? I can feel that some of these are pulling against each other but I cant tell which conflicts are real and which just look that way from a bad angle. Go through it and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, ordered by how severe the tension is, what each tension would force me to give up or revise, and which apparent conflicts dissolve once you look closer. If there is one single revision that would clean up the most, say what it is and what it would cost.
