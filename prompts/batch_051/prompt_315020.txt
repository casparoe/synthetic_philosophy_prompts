"Epicurus' reasoning is simple. Nothing can be bad for you unless there is a you there to feel it. Death, however, is the end of feeling: once you are dead, no person is left to suffer the loss. So death cannot be bad for the person who dies. What people actually fear when they say they fear death is the process of dying, and that process is over the moment death itself arrives. Death itself feels like nothing, and a state that feels like nothing cannot be a bad state to be in. Epicurus also noticed a symmetry between the time before we were born and the time after we die. We are not distressed by the eternity of non-existence that preceded us, so we should not be distressed by the eternity that follows. The conclusion is plain: death can be bad only for the people left behind, and for the person who dies it is an evil that never arrives.
