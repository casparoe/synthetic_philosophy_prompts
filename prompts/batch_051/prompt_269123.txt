I spent forty-one years as a sedimentary geologist, and I came away from that career with a habit of distrusting words like "catastrophe": in the rock record a sudden-looking break is often just a gap in deposition, or a slow change that happened to leave no trace. Since retirement I've drifted into the philosophy of history and the arguments about whether societies like the Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Maya lowlands, and Easter Island "collapsed" or just reorganized under stress. It feels like the same problem with the strata: the same evidence can be read as disaster or as continuity, depending on what you've already decided counts.

I'm putting together one public lecture for our local science forum—call it Did they fail, or did they move on?—and I want to spent my prep time on the questions that actually do the conceptual work. I can dig into two or three of the knots underneath the collapse debate; the rest I'll mention and wave at. Please rank these (or swap in ones I've missed) and defend the ordering:

– What is the "thing" that supposedly collapses: a political system, a population, an elite culture, a material style, a region?
– Is collapse fundamentally about rate—a year versus a generation versus three centuries—and if so, where should the conceptual line go?
– Does "collapse" smuggle in a value judgment, where the stuff we find impressive (monuments, writing, long-distance trade) stands in for the society while ordinary life gets ignored?
– Is "collapse" just a description of what needs explaining rather than an explanation, in which case does the concept do any real work?
– What would the archaeological record have to look like for "transformation" or "reorganization" to be the better frame—what evidence would actually settle it?
– Does a pandemic-driven depopulation count as the same phenomenon as a political unraveling, or have we lumped different things together?

Rank them by how much they determine the rest of the talk: which questions are upstream of the others, which would most change what I end up saying, and which are mostly verbal. Then tell me which two or three I should actually dig into, and what evidence or argument would make you reshuffle the top of the list. I'm not after a reading list or a literature survey; I want to know where to think hardest.
