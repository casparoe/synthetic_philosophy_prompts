My epistemologist friend has spent years sorting communities into echo chambers, epistemic bubbles, and a third pile for things that are neither, and I've written down a bunch of her past calls because I can't quite reverse-engineer the rule. Her track record looks like this:

- A woman who only watches one partisan cable network, never sees the other side's actual reporting, and couldn't reconstruct an opposing argument to save her life: 'epistemic bubble, not an echo chamber.'
- A flat-earth YouTube rabbit hole where every video says NASA, the universities, and the mainstream media are lying, and that the only people who can tell what's real are 'true researchers' who've done their own measurements—everyone else is a parrot: 'echo chamber through and through.'
- A socialist reading circle that reads the business press faithfully but only 'to watch capital think,' teaches that objective journalism is a liberal myth, and treats lived experience of exploitation as the only standpoint from which the news can be properly interpreted: 'echo chamber.'
- A wellness forum that says the FDA and mainstream medicine are pharma-captured, celebrates members who 'woke up,' and treats other members' recovery stories as the only evidence that counts: 'echo chamber.'
- A woman whose TikTok algorithm now shows her only home-renovation content, so she's come to think everyone in the country is renovating a kitchen: 'epistemic bubble, no question.'
- A retired engineer who reads a small hand-picked list of climate blogs, knows exactly what he's excluding, and says life's too short for junk: 'bubble-ish, maybe, but not a chamber—he built the filter himself and can see it.'

The new case I actually care about: a forum for people who've left a high-control religious movement. Members read the movement's old publications and mainstream reporting on the movement obsessively—more than anything else. They definitly don't say journalists or academics are lying or corrupt. What they say is that outsiders can't know what it was like from the inside. So whenever a reporter or researcher says the movement's abuse stories are exaggerated, or that it wasn't all bad, the standard reply is 'you weren't there.' At the same time, the same members trust ordinary news about everything else, and nobody in the forum shuns or demonizes someone who quits. I'm less interested in whether the final label is echo chamber, bubble, or neither than in (a) the rule she seems to be running, (b) the other rules that fit her past verdicts just as well, (c) your best guess about how she'd call this new case and why, and (d) the cleanest further case for telling the candidate rules apart.
