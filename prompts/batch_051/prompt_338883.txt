I am drafting a short statement of my position on free will and moral responsibility for a workshop, and I would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My current commitments are these:

1. I am a compatibilist: the truth of determinism would not by itself undermine free will or moral responsibility.
2. I take Frankfurt-style cases to show that moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise. What matters is how the action was actually produced, not whether the agent could have acted differently.
3. I think the right account of that production is a real-self or mesh view: an agent is responsible when the action issues from motives or values that are genuinely the agent's own — motives the agent identifies with, or that are appropriately responsive to reasons.
4. But I also hold that covert manipulation can undermine responsibility. A person who has been surreptitiously brainwashed or engineered to acquire certain motives may, at the time of action, wholeheartedly endorse those motives and act from them, and yet not be responsible for what they do.
5. My overall framework is Strawsonian: I take being responsible to be a matter of the aptness of our reactive attitudes, such as resentment, indignation, and gratitude, rather than a matter of some further metaphysical fact about the will.
6. Finally, I believe retributive punishment is defensible in principle: it can be appropriate to punish wrongdoers because they deserve it, and not merely to deter or rehabilitate.

Each of these seems to me individually defensible, but I am not confident they form a stable package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, how severe is it, and what would have to be revised to resolve it? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and then say which single revision would do the most to restore consistency and what it would cost.
