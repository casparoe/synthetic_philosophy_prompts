Give me a working definition of a 'statement of fact,' as opposed to an opinion, in the way defamation law needs it. I'll give you the cases; run the definition through all of them, tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either patch it or defend the surprising call: a review saying the clams were undercooked; a review saying it's the worst restaurant in town; a columnist saying the mayor is corrupt; the same columnist writing 'in my opinion, the mayor is corrupt'; a credit-rating agency downgrading a city's bonds to junk; a stock analyst's twelve-month price target of $80 with a sell rating; an election forecast giving a candidate an 85% chance; and a 'worst companies to work for' list. I suspect the words 'in my opinion' do less work then people think, and that ratings and forecasts are the hard middle of the line.
