Write a deadpan product-safety recall notice, roughly 700 words, issued by a fictional regulatory body (call it the Division of Moral Appliances) for the Trolley Problem, treated throughout as a manufactured intuition pump: a device marketed for producing reliable moral intuitions. Use the genre conventions of a government recall, including a notice number, an "affected product" section covering configurations such as the switch and the footbridge, a "hazard description," a section on "populations at special risk," a "corrective action" section, and a short FAQ with one evasive answer.

The satire should target the practice of using vivid, rigorously contrived thought experiments as probes of moral judgment while ignoring the concrete features that actually do the persuasive work: salience, proximity, wording, presentation order, and the contrast between killing as a means and allowing death as a side-effect. The humor must come from getting that material right; a moral psychologist or philosopher working on moral cognition should find every detail fair, not sloppy.

Work these ideas in accurately but lightly. The switch configuration and the footbridge configuration produce opposite verdicts even though the numbers are the same, which suggests the "intuition" is tracking the mechanism rather than the outcome. The footbridge configuration builds the single victim into the causal mechanism while the switch treats the death as a side-effect, and much of the device's deontological output is just sensitivity to that contrast. Verdicts drift or reverse when the same scenarios are presented in a different order. The apparatus depends on stipulations users often cannot imaginatively accept (the heavy bystander who will stop the trolley if pushed but cannot be saved). And users routinely mistake the resulting affect — a flash of up-close horror — for their considered moral judgment, even though the device has never been validated against actual moral behavior. Add one or two further jokes of the same kind, but do not resolve the critique into a lesson; let the deadpan register carry it and keep the whole legible to someone who has never heard of the doctrine of double effect. Invent the agency and its director; do not name any real living person.
