I keep noticing a way of arguing that I don't have a name for, and I'd like to know whether there is one and, if not, how it should be carved off from the things it resembles. It comes up mostly with small acts that are individually almost pointless. Someone is deciding whether to report a bug in some open-source program, make a ten-dollar donation, vote, or eat the factory-farmed chicken. The objection is always the same: your one action won't change anything, so the ordinary expected-value calculation says don't bother. Then the person replies something like: but if I do it, that tells me I am in the world where lots of people who reason like me also do it, so my choice is evidence about a whole correlated bunch of choices, not just my own. Some people push it further and say the correlation doesn't have to be causal — the other people never see my choice, they aren't copying me, and there might be no common cause anyone could point to; the correllation is just in how our values and reasoning are shaped. It seems most tempting in domains where the possible consequences are too many to enumerate, like open source, global poverty, or climate.

I know Newcomb's problem, Kant's universalizability test, and the superrationality idea from game theory, and this seems to sit between them without coinciding with any. Kant says act as if everyone did it; this move says the fact that I in particular do it is evidence about what similar people in fact do. Newcomb has a predictor and boxes; here there is no such predictor. Superrationality usually assumes all the players reason identically in a fixed game; here the setting is open-ended and the other players are more like a reference class I get to choose. Is there an established name for this specific move, and what would a careful version of it have to add to be sound? I'm not just after a label — I want the edges: where it falls apart, how it differs from those neighbors, and whether the non-causal version is coherent or just a way to multiply imagined efficacy whenever I feel guilty.
