i've been reading about what numbers actually are and i've got myself stuck between a theory that feels right on paper and an intuition that feels right in my gut. on the theory side, structuralism: numbers aren't little objects with hidden insides, they're just places in a structure, and everything about 2 that matters is how it sits relative to 1, 3, 4 and the rest. that seems tidy, and it explains why loads of different ways of building the naturals all do the same mathematical work.

but here's the rub. if you build the naturals the von neumann way, 4 = {0, 1, 2, 3}, so 2 is an element of 4. if you build them the zermelo way, 4 = {3}, so 2 is not an element of 4. neither construction breaks arithmetic, so arithmetic doesn't settle which one is "the real" 4. structuralists seem to think this shows the question "is 2 an element of 4?" just doesn't have a determinate answer until you choose a way of modelling numbers in sets, and maybe not even then. and i can't shake the feeling that that's a cop-out: 2 and 4 are numbers, membership is a real relation, so shouldn't there be a fact of the matter about whether 2 ∈ 4, even if we can't tell which answer is right? maths definately feels like the one subject where questions have right answers.

so what i want to know is which should give way, the theory or the intuition. give me the strongest version of both, then walk me through what each option costs: (a) debunk the intuition as a confusion about how we picture numbers and drop it, (b) amend structuralism so "2 ∈ 4" can be determinately true or false, or (c) find a reading where both survive because i'm conflating "the number 2" with "a particular set that models 2". don't just tell me what to think; tell me what would justify each move. and i don't need names or citations. finally, say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of.
