I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science who convenes a standing undergraduate tutorial on confirmation theory: about fourteen students, most with some science background but no prior philosophy of induction, and an 80-minute session. I want a structured game that makes them feel the force of Goodman's new riddle of induction before I ever introduce the terms 'grue' or 'projectible.' The game should split the class into two teams that observe the same sequence of card draws, form competing projection rules, and place bets with points (no real money) on the next card. One team should be led to project in ordinary color terms; the other should be led to project using a gruesome predicate defined relative to a visible cutoff in the deck — for example, a predicate that applies to green cards drawn before the cutoff and to blue cards drawn after it, with 'bleen' as its complement. The payoff structure and the instructions should be arranged so that each team can rationally stick with its own rule, and so that both rules fit everything observed so far equally well while diverging on the next draw; neither the evidence nor the score should decide between them. Then a debrief should map what happened to the underdetermination of projection by past regularities, the gerrymandering of predicates, the thought that induction is only justified relative to an entrenched or projectible vocabulary, and the reason appeals to simplicity or 'projecting the same pattern' cannot by themselves solve the riddle. Please give me concrete rules, materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, a payoff scheme, the exact scripts for each team, and a debrief outline. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong when I first run it — for instance, that the ordinary-vocabulary team will dismiss the gruesome rule as obviously rigged, or that students will treat the ordinary color categories as obviously correct rather than as exactly what is at stake — and how to handle those responses without lecturing up front. Finally, be explicit about which feature of the real scientific role of entrenchment this one-shot game cannot reproduce and that I must supply in the closing discussion.
