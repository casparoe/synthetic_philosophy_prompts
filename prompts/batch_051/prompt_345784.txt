I have been running a philosophy of language reading group, and for the past several months we have been stuck on the debate between semantic externalists and their internalist critics. What puzzles me is that the group has converged on nearly all the standard moves and still nobody has budged. Everyone accepts Putnam's Twin Earth setup: two speakers can be physically identical, molecule for molecule, while the stuff they call water is H2O in one environment and XYZ in the other, so their words pick out different substances and their utterances have different truth conditions. The externalists take this to show that meaning is constitutively world-involving, and that the lesson generalizes across natural-kind terms and beyond. The internalists grant the referential facts but reply that this just shows reference and truth conditions are the wrong object for a theory of what a speaker grasps and of what explains their behavior, since the twin speakers are behaviorally indistinguishable. Each side treats its own conclusion as forced, and neither has made a new argument in weeks — the debate feels less like an unresolved question than a clash of starting points.

My working hypothesis is that this is not really a factual disagreement about how language works, but a disagreement about what a theory of meaning is for: whether it answers to competent speakers' judgments about reference and truth conditions, or to the explanatory needs of a science of the mind. If that is right, the two camps are quarreling over the term meaning while pursuing different theoretical projects. But I can see rival diagnoses: it might be a substantive disagreement about whether wide, world-involving content can play a causal-explanatory role; or a bedrock difference about which cases count as semantic data. I want a diagnosis of the structure of this dispute, with reasons, not a re-litigation of Twin Earth. What kind of disagreement is this, and given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an empirical result, a formal framework, a revised question — or should we expect the impasse to be permanent?
