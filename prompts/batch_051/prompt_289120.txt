I am a doctoral student in epistemology, currently working on the relation between first-order and higher-order knowledge. Two visiting speakers in our seminar series—one who defends a substantive KK principle and holds that our present mental states are luminous, the other an anti-luminosity theorist who denies that any non-trivial condition is luminous—have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document rather than yet another exchange of papers. I have been asked to prepare a draft they can both sign, and I would be grateful if you could write it. Please structure the document as a numbered section of agreed claims, followed by a disagreements table, and then a cruxes section. The agreed-claims section should include statements both sides can accept without wincing at the wording; the disagreements table should record each side’s preferred formulation of the residual disagreement, with the proponent’s and the opponent’s own wordings set out side by side; and the cruxes section should identify the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case, argument, or experimental paradigm that both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side’s prediction in advance. Please explicitly disentangle at least these strands: the formal KK axiom in epistemic logic; the claim that whenever one knows one is always in a position to know that one knows; the stronger claim that knowing actually brings second-order knowledge with it; and the claim that certain phenomenal or self-presenting conditions are luminous, in the sense that their obtaining puts one in a position to know that they obtain. I suspect part of their dispute is verbal, especially around whether a failure to form the relevant second-order belief should be chalked up to an absence of knowledge or to mere inattention; if you judge that any part of the disagreement is merely verbal, please say which part and why. Among the likely cruxes I would like examined are whether any non-trivial condition is luminous; whether a sorites-style continuum of gradually changing felt states undermines luminosity through something like a margin-for-error argument; and whether our access to our own present experience differs in kind from our access to the external world. Please write the document itself in British English.
