Ive been going in circles about this and want someone to check the logic, because I think intro micro pulls a fast one on us. We learn revealed preference: if a consumer chooses x when y was available, then x is revealed preferred to y, and utility is just a number that ranks options by revealed preference. But doesn't that make "consumers maximize utility" true no matter what anyone does? Even someone who, in separate pairwise choices, picks A over B, then B over C, then C over A would seem to be maximizing utility at every step, since whatever they pick is revealed as best.

Here's the argument laid out:

(1) Choosing x when y is on the table just is revealing that x is preferred to y.
(2) Utility is nothing over and above a numerical ranking of options according to revealed preference.
(3) So for any choice from any set, the chosen option is by definition utility-maximizing, because it gets ranked top.
(4) Therefore "consumers maximize utility" can't be falsified; it holds even for someone with the cycle A over B, B over C, C over A.
(5) But revealed preference was suposed to make consumer theory predictive and testable, so something has gone wrong.

Which step fails? If the answer is that (3) only goes through when choices satisfy consistency conditions like transitivity, then isn't that the real theory — and "maximizes utility" is just decoration on top of it?
