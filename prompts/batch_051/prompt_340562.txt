In our reading group last week the conversation turned to whether it would actually matter if AI eventually displaced humanity, and one man kept saying 'humans are just a stepping stone' as though that settled the question. I spent forty years as a working biologist, and the phrase sets my teeth on edge because it seems to smuggle in exactly the sort of ladder-of-progress thinking I was trained to distrust — but since I retired and started reading philosophy I've realised I can't say crisply just what the slogan is asserting. Could you lay out the different precise claims 'humans are just a stepping stone' might be making (about evolution having a direction, about the particular historical moment, about values, and anything else), and for each say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false? And then tell me which reading, if any, would actually license dismissing worries about being replaced by AI, and which reading the man most likely had in mind. I'm especailly curious whether the slogan can be made true at all without assuming evolution somehow aims at intelligence.
