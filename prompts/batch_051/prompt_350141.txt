I co-run a reading group that's half ML people and half evolutionary biologists, and for the past few months two members have been going at each other about the slogan that evolution is a stupid optimizer: useful way to think, or a metaphor that does more harm than good? The optimizer camp points out that under standard single-locus, constant-fitness assumptions, the change in allele frequencies is literally proportional to the derivative of mean fitness with respect to the frequencies, so selection really is following a local gradient. They concede the objective is noisy, non-stationary, and partly constructed by the population itself, but they say that just makes it a harder optimization problem, not a different kind of process. The skeptic camp says that's exactly where the analogy breaks: once fitness is frequency-dependent or epistatic, there's no fixed surface to climb — its rebuilt by the very frequencies that are changing, like a committee that rewrites the rubric while the grades are still coming in — and there are simple models where mean fitness reliably goes down, so talk of landscapes and gradients invites people to infer a conserved quantity that isn't there.

They've agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration document instead of continuing to talk past each other, and I got roped into drafting it. Write the document they should sign. Begin with a numbered list of claims both camps accept: what the selection equations do and do not imply, what "optimizer" would have to mean for their disagreement to be substantive rather than verbal, and what the easy counterexamples to naive hill-climbing do and do not establish. Then present the remaining disagreements as a table, with each side's position stated in wording that side would endorse. Then list the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move at least one of them — and for each crux, give a concrete model, case, or measurement both agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts. If you conclude that part of the dispute is only about words, say which part and why.
