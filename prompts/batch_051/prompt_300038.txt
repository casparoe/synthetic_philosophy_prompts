Please give me your credence that the doctrine of divine simplicity is metaphysically coherent, in the following precise sense: there could be a being that is (i) identical to its essence and to its existence, (ii) such that all its attributes — omniscience, omnipotence, perfect goodness, and the rest — are really identical with one another and with the being itself, and (iii) wholly without parts, with no composition of actuality and potency, no composition of substance and accident, and no real distinction between the being and its acts of knowing and willing. Treat the question not as whether God exists or whether classical theism is true overall, but as whether this description picks out a possible being.

Decompose your answer into at least these components, with a rough probability and one sentence of justification for each: (a) that a simple intellect can hold one undivided act of thought that nonetheless encompasses the full plurality of created truths; (b) that a simple will can will the existence of contingent creatures without God being really changed by, or really related to, the created world; (c) that the identity of essence and existence in a being is coherent rather than a conflation of a thing with one of its properties; and (d) that the apparent multiplicity of divine attributes can be merely conceptual while each predication remains literally true. State a single overall credence, show how you combine the components (do not assume independence — tell me which correlations you are relying on), and then identify which component is doing the most work in your number and what argument or evidence would shift it most.
