For my philosophy of probability course, I've been trying to write down exactly what I believe about how objective chance should constrain rational credence, and I'd like an honest consistency audit before I have to defend the package in class. Here is the position I'm currently inclined to endorse.

(1) Humeanism about chance. The objective chance facts are not an extra layer of reality over and above the local, categorical facts about what happens where and when. Instead, the complete chance theory for a world is whatever set of history-to-chance conditionals, together with the laws, best systematizes the total mosaic of local categorical facts, including future facts. There is no further primitive "chanciness."

(2) The Principal Principle. For any proposition X and time t, my rational credence in X, conditional on the complete history of the world up to t and on the true complete chance theory T for this world, should equal Ch_t(X), the chance of X at t that T assigns.

(3) Undermining futures. A complete chance theory need not assign probability 1 to itself. Given (1), there seem to be worlds whose chance function at t assigns positive probability to possible future courses of events such that, if one of those courses had been the actual future, the total mosaic would have been best systematized by a different chance function, so that the chance function at t would not have been the one specified by T. I take such undermining futures to be possible, and indeed to be what a Humean should expect in any world with nontrivial chances.

(4) Conditional certainty. For any proposition P, P has probability 1 conditional on itself; this is just the standard behavior of conditional probability. So in particular, conditional on the proposition that T is the complete chance theory of this world, I am certain that T is that theory.

(5) Deference to expected chance. When I do not yet know which complete chance theory is correct, my rational credence in X, conditional on the complete history of the world up to t, should be a weighted average of the candidate theories' chances: C(X | H_t) equals the sum over mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive candidate theories T of C(T | H_t) multiplied by Ch_t(X), the chance that T assigns to X at t.

Does this package hang together? Please audit it as you would a colleague's view rather than giving me a lecture on the concepts. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which are only apparently so, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and then say what the least costly revision, or minimal coordinated set of revisions, would be, and what that revision forces me to give up.
