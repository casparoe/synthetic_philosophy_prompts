hey, I'm putting together the final for my upper-level undergrad seminar on intergenerational justice, and I want it to be on pure time preference and discounting the future. The class is a mix of philosophy and climate-econ kids. They've all got the basics down cold: they can tell you Ramsey thought discounting future utility was ethically indefensible and just a failure of imagination, and that Sidgwick thought the mere difference in time isn't a morally relevant ground. So a question that just asks them to define pure time preference won't seperate anyone. I want an in-class essay question (90 minutes, closed book, roughly 1200 words, 100 marks) that forces them to actually adjudicate something. The crux I'd like is this analogy students keep falling for in discussion: people clearly discount their own future within a single life (a headache tomorrow matters less to me right now than a headache today), and from that they conclude society may discount the well-being of different people who simply live later. I want the question to make them evaluate whether that intrapersonal-to-interpersonal move works — where it lines up and where it breaks. Then give me a model answer and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should name specific mistakes that lose marks: conflating the market discount rate with pure time preference, thinking zero pure time preference means you can't discount for uncertainty or growth, treating descriptive discounting as automatically normative, stuff like that. The hard part should be the analogy, not remembering labels.
