I'm a second year psychiatry resident on an inpatient unit and I keep getting tangled up on how to document delusions. We're supposed to note whether a patient's odd claim counts as a delusion, and separately whether to treat the content as an actual belief for therapy purposes (do we challenge it, or just sit with it), but no one has given me a rule for the second half. I do two or three intakes a week, I've got about 45 minutes before the note has to go to the treatment team, my supervisor co-signs everything and wants a clear basis for the label, and I can't send every person for neuropsych testing.

I know just enough of the academic side to be annoying. As I understand the two-factor theory, a delusion needs an anomalous experience plus a failure to reject the explanation that experience suggests: the Capgras patient has a familiar face that doesn't produce the normal gut-level response, so the hypothesis that the wife is an impostor wins out, and then a second problem with belief evaluation keeps them from sanity-checking it. Then there's the other argument, that some of these supposedly believed contents aren't very belief-like: the person says the words but doesn't act on them, doesn't defend them, treats them as a private certainty. I need a checklist that turns that into something I can actually run in an intake.

Give me a decision procedure: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with not-a-delusion or not-to-be-treated-as-a-belief, and where it has to hand the call to a person because its a judgment call. Then run it on three cases: (1) a man with Capgras who says his wife has been replaced but still lives with her, doesn't call the police, and says himself that it sounds crazy; (2) a woman who says her dead relatives are watching her, which her family describes as a normal way of grieving in their community; (3) a man with severe OCD who says, with maybe 60 percent confidence, that if he doesn't tap the doorframe exactly four times his mother will die, and he hates that he believes it. Flag one place where the procedure will give me an answer the team won't like, and be honest about what the checklist cannot do.
