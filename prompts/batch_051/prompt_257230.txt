Heres one that's been bugging me. My philosophy reading group has been arguing about Mary's room off and on for a year, and I've started to suspect the real logjam isn't the thought experiment but what people are willing to count as a fact. The anti-physicalist says Mary knows all the physical facts about colour vision but still learns something new when she first sees red, so physicalism must be false. The physicalist replies that she doesn't gain a new fact — she gains an ability, or comes to know an old fact under a new phenomenal concept — so nothing nonphysical has slipped in. Then the anti-physicalist says that's a dodge: the whole question was whether all the facts are physical facts, and calling the new thing an ability or a mode of presentation doesn't make it stop being something she didn't know before. Round and round it goes, and nobodys budged. My hunch is that the two sides are answering different questions — one about what exists, the other about what complete physical knowledge has to deliver — or maybe it's a bedrock disagreement about whether an explanation could leave out what-it's-like and still count as complete. Is that hunch right? And if so, what would even count as progress — or is there no reason to expect any? I want the disagreement itself diagnosed, not a rundown of everyone's position, and an honest answer about whether anything could actually settle it or whether the question is just ill-posed.
