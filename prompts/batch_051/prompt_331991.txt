I'm a PhD student working on predictability and model error in dynamical systems. In our group, "the butterfly effect" gets invoked constantly, but I've started to notice it is being made to do very different work: one colleague uses it to claim that deterministic systems are, for any finite predictor, indistinguishable from indeterministic ones; another uses it to claim that predictions beyond some horizon are unwarranted, not merely difficult; a third uses it to claim that a tiny perturbation should be assigned causal responsibility for a large downstream event. I want to pin down what the slogan could actually be asserting. Please lay out the distinct precise claims it might express — sensitive dependence on initial conditions, finite-precision limits on measurement or computation, the existence of absolute barriers to prediction, the difference between chaos and indeterminism, and causal attributions to small perturbations — and assess each one: which are trivial reminders about instability, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false or overstated. Then say which of the three uses above, if any, each reading actually licenses.
