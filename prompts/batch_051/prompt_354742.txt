I've been noticing a pattern in my own head for years and I don't have a word for it, so I'm hoping philosophy or psychology has one. I do not believe I'm the only real person. Ask me point-blank and I'll say everyone has an inner life as full as mine. But my automatic mental life doesn't seem to have got the memo. A few instances:

- I run into an old coworker at the pharmacy and I'm genuinely thrown that she was in the middle of a day that had been running for hours before I showed up. It takes me a second to mentally render the fact that her afternoon wasn't waiting for me to walk in. It feels like discovering the set continues behind the camera.
- When I imagine a bad thing happening to me next month, the scene is hot and detailed, almost pre-lived. When I imagine the same bad thing happening to a stranger, or even a close friend, the image is flatter, more like a weather report than a simulation. I'd never defend this as a claim about whose pain matters more; it's just how the pictures arrive.
- In memories of arguments, I cast myself as the reasonable one more or less automatically. It's not exactly lying to myself: my own reasons and pressures are fully lit from the inside, while thier reasons are blurry, so my side always looks better-reasoned.

The mismatch is what has me puzzled. I can say, sincerely, that I'm one person among billions and that my perspective has no metaphysical specialness. And yet my day-to-day experience seems built as if I'm the protagonist and other people are supporting characters whose lives only run while they're on stage. I know this is somewhere in the neighborhood of solipsism, but a real solipsist is claiming something much stronger, right? And it isn't ordinary selfishess either, because it's not about wanting more for myself — it's about how real things feel. Is there an established term for this exact configuration, and if it's a loose cluster rather than one concept, what are the nearest ones and where exactly do they part ways with what I'm describing? A bare label won't satisfy me; I want to know what the thing is and where its edges are — when does this normal background condition tip over into something genuinely philosophical, or genuinely delusional?
