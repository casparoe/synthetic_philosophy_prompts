As a retired chemist now working through philosophy, I have been struck by a structural resemblance between Frege's puzzle about informative identity statements and a situation that is almost routine in the natural sciences, and I would like help deciding whether the resemblance is deep or superficial.

Frege's puzzle, as I understand it, is this. If identity relates an object only to itself, then the claim that Hesperus is Hesperus and the claim that Hesperus is Phosphorus attribute the very same relation to the very same object. Yet the first is a trivial matter of logic while the second was a genuine astronomical discovery: someone could fully understand both names without knowing that they name one planet. Frege's response was to distinguish the reference of a name, the object, from its sense, the way the object is presented; the identity statement is informative because the two names present the same object under different senses.

The scientific situation I have in mind is the identification of a single entity through two independent investigative routes. A chemist may identify a metal as the source of a distinctive spectral line and, separately, identify the element occupying a particular position in the periodic table; the statement that these two are one and the same element is informative and was historically a discovery, not a piece of a priori bookkeeping. Likewise in theory change, two research programs sometimes converge on the same object from different theoretical directions—the particle introduced to explain one anomaly turns out to be the particle introduced to explain another—and the identity statement carries genuine news. In each case, what is asserted looks like the same relation, a thing's being itself, but the cognitive value of the statement exceeds that of a tautology because the thing is reached by two different routes.

I am uncertain whether Frege's apparatus of sense and reference is genuinely doing the same work here as the scientist's distinction between a description or detection procedure and the entity it singles out. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.

In particular: (1) state the exact proposed mapping between Frege's case and the scientific cases—what corresponds to the name, the reference, and the sense; whether a detection procedure corresponds to a sense or to something else; and whether the a posteriori character of the identity is preserved. (2) Indicate what would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer: if Frege's account of cognitive value carries over, what must be true of scientific descriptions, and if it does not, where the structure breaks. (3) Identify something the parallel predicts that appears false: a consequence of treating scientific identifications as Fregean identity statements that scientific practice would not accept, or a feature of Frege's puzzle that has no scientific analogue. (4) End with a verdict on whether I have found a deep identity of structure, a family resemblance, or a mixture.
