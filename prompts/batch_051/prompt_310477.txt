I've been chewing on this one for a while. In Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter draws a line from Gödel's self-referential formulas to self-aware minds—the strange loop idea. I spend a lot of my time listening to two people argue past each other about whether that analogy does any real work. One of them treats it as load-bearing: self-awareness just is a strange loop, and the incompleteness phenomena tell us something substantive about how an 'I' can think about itself. The other treats it as a seductive metaphor that collapses when you press it: a formal system that can encode a Gödel sentence isn't thereby aware of anything, and the self of experience isn't a sentence about its own unprovability.

They've agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and I want the joint statement they'd both sign. Please give me:

- a numbered list of claims both can accept about Gödel's theorem, about what the analogy would need to establish to count as explanatory, and about self-reference in formal systems;
- then a disagreements table, with each residual dispute worded so each side would accept the description of its own view;
- then two or three cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about it.

I especially want the crux about a formal system that contains its own Gödel sentence but has no consciousness at all, and the crux about whether the introspective thought 'I can't prove I'm consistent' is genuinely analogous to a system meeting its Gödel sentence. If some of the fight turns out to be verbal, keep that seperate from the substantive disagreements and say exactly which part is verbal and why. And write the whole thing in British English.
