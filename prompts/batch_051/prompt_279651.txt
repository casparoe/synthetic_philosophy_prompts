I have been trying to build a music database whose entries track something real, and I keep failing at the boundary between works, performances, and recordings. I need a working definition of a musical work that keeps the three distinct rather than collapsing them into a single category of listenable objects. Here are the cases it must classify: (1) a Beethoven symphony performed live from a score; (2) a studio recording of that symphony assembled from many takes and extensively edited; (3) a free-jazz improvisation with no pre-existing score, played once and never repeated; (4) a fully synthetic electronic track composed in a DAW, with no performance event and no acoustic source behind it; (5) a computer's mechanical MIDI realization of a Chopin étude, produced without human interpretive choices beyond the original encoding; (6) a DJ set built entirely from other musicians' recordings; (7) a generative music system that produces a continuous, non-repeating stream from a fixed ruleset; (8) an album of lightly edited thunderstorm field recordings.

I am confident that (1) and (2) are the same work, and that (8) is not a musical work in the operative sense, however much it rewards close listening. The cases that make me lose the boundary are (5), (6), and (7). I suspect the concept shifts depending on whether you anchor it in a score, a performance event, or an agent's act of fixing sounds, and those three anchors come apart exactly in these cases. Give me the definition, apply it to all eight, say where it yields an unintuitive verdict or no verdict at all, and then either revise it or defend the surprising classifications. I would also like to know which of the three anchors your final definition ends up closest to, and whether you regard that as a feature or a cost.
