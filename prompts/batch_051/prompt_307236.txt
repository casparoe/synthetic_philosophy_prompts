The paradox of blackmail is usually stated as the fact that two legal acts—asking for money and disclosing true information one is entitled to disclose—become illegal when combined in the form of a demand for payment in exchange for staying silent. Yet if the disclosure would itself be legal, then the threat to disclose is only a threat to do something one is permitted to do, and a threat to do what one is permitted to do should presumably not count as coercion, let alone extortion. Why should the law not treat blackmail as an ordinary sale of silence, and what exactly is left unexplained if we do?
