I have a standing rule about which mathematical results I treat as proved: I grant assent only when a proof is in principle surveyable by one competent human, and I withhold it when an essential inferential step is an unsurveyable machine search, as in the computer-aided proofs of the four-colour theorem and the Kepler conjecture. My difficulty is not with computers doing arithmetic I could in principle redo; it is with steps no single mind can check. But the rule strains under my own use: I still rely on the four-colour theorem in my work, and I do not find proof-assistant certification any more satisfactory, since the verifier's kernel is itself a program I trust rather than check. Is this policy a defensible epistemic principle, or is it a bias dressed up as rigor? Draw the line as sharply as you can, and say whether the rule can be restated to keep whatever is right in it without ruling out machine reasoning wholesale.
