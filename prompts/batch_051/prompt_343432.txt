I teach an upper-level seminar on collective responsibility, and two of my colleagues have been arguing for over a year about whether a corporation can itself be morally blamed, as opposed to having its members blamed or its conduct merely regulated. One defends the view that a corporation can be a genuine object of moral blame, over and above the blameworthiness of any natural person in it. The other holds an individualist reductionist view: so-called corporate blame is at best a metaphorical shorthand for, or a legal-liability and reputational response to, what natural persons have done. Both agree that firms cause serious harms, that they can be fined, censured, regulated, and dissolved, and that individual employees and officers are sometimes blameworthy. Their dispute is narrower and harder than those points.

Please write the document they should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed-claims section should include claims about how firms make decisions, how blame differs from liability and reputational sanction, and what the two sides jointly accept about the harms firms cause. The disagreements table should then make the residual dispute exact: for each row, state a crux in neutral wording that both sides accept as the disputed sub-question, give the defender's formulation of her own view, give the reductionist's formulation of his own view, and specify at least one concrete test, case, or argument both sides agree bears on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

The cruxes should be chosen so that settling them would move at least one side. Possible cruxes include whether blame requires a unified subject of experience or the capacity to understand and answer moral reasons; whether a corporate intention can come apart from and fail to reduce to the intentions of any individual member; whether burdens that fall on shareholders, employees, or future members make corporate blame unfair even if the firm is a genuine agent; and whether a firm's "character" can persist through complete replacement of its personnel. Use concrete scenarios throughout, such as a harmful policy produced by information distributed across employees so that no single person held the whole plan, a firm whose board and staff are entirely replaced, or an owner-operated business contrasted with a large multinational. Flag any disagreements that turn out to be merely verbal, and say what the genuinely non-verbal residue is. Separate what is settled among people working on this topic from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
