I'm stuck on an argument a colleague gave me, which he says is just Arendt's view about lying in politics pushed one step further. It goes:

(1) Factual truths only become politically real through testimony: someone has to witness, record, and get others to take up the report. Without that, a fact never enters the public world at all.
(2) But testimony can be fabricated, documents can be doctored, and in a mass society almost everyone accepts the record on trust; people judge competing accounts mostly by how coherent, repeated, and confidently vouched for they are, not by checking for themselves.
(3) There is no neutral apolitical referee that can just settle the facts; any would-be referee is itself inside the public contest.
(4) So a sufficiently organized liar who can keep the story consistent, repeat it, and control the institutions that vouch for it can make any particular fact stop functioning politically: people will orient themselves by the replacement story, not the original fact.
(5) What a fact is, politically, is exhausted by its being accepted and thereby steering action.
(6) So a fact displaced by a successful lie is no longer a political fact at all, and in politics truth just is whatever account survives the contest.
(7) But that cant be right. It erases any difference in kind between facts and opinions, and it leaves no room for Arendt's own worry that modern political lying attacks the shared factual world rather than merely changing what people opine.

I want to keep the idea that facts are fragile and depend on testimony (so (1) stays, maybe with qualifications), and I also want to say that a winning lie is still a lie.
