i've been thinking about how chat models "explain" their answers and i can't tell if i've reinvented something obvious or if i'm just confused. i started with what felt like a clean distinction. there's the reasoning the model does in the open before it answers, when it talks through steps and then lands on the answer — call that the working-out. and then there's the explanation it gives after the fact, when you ask "why did you say that" and it hands you a neat little paragraph — call that the write-up.

my original theory was that the working-out is the real reasoning and the write-up is basically made up after the fact, a story told after the answer to make it look considered. but then i noticed two things. first, the working-out can contain a wrong step and the final answer can still be right, which is strange if the answer is being produced by the working-out. second, the same model will give me a different working-out for the same answer if i change the wording of the question slightly, so the working-out seems like it's also being generated to fit the situation, not read off from some fixed chain of thought.

so now i think the real distinction isn't "real reasoning vs fake explanation." i think it's between two kinds of stories: an upstream story, told before the answer, which can steer what gets said next, and a downstream story, told after the answer, which can't. and my bigger claim is that a language model never reports its reasoning; it only produces text that sounds like reasoning, and whether that text matches whatever actually produced the answer is a seperate question that the model itself can't answer.

i want an honest assessment of that, not a reading list. is my upstream/downstream thing already a real distinction under some name? is my bigger claim right, too strong, or just impossible to test? and under what conditions would a model's verbal report of its own reasoning deserve to be called a report rather than a story?
