I've been going back and forth on forgiveness for weeks, and at this point I think the problem is that nobody lays out what a theory of forgiveness is even supposed to do before they start defending one. So before I read another account, I want the desiderata — the full requirements list.

It has to cover the cases: garden-variety forgiveness after an apology, forgiving someone who isnt sorry, forgiving the dead, forgiving yourself, a bystander or loved one "forgiving" on someone else's behalf when the actual victim refuses, forgiving the same person for the same thing when they keep doing it, and the limit case of wrongs people call unforgivable. For each case, say what decision it forces a theory into rather than just gesturing at it.

Then the distinctions a theory is not allowed to collapse: forgiving vs. excusing, condoning, forgetting, legal pardon, and reconciliation. And the two axes that have to be kept separate: who has standing to forgive vs. whether forgiving here is wise or warranted, and whether forgiveness is permissible vs. obligatory vs. supererogatory. The whole reason I'm doing this is that last bit — I want the requirements list to force an answer to whether forgiveness can be unconditional (no apology, no repentance, no change in the offender) and whether it can ever be a duty, including a duty to forgive the unrepentant. If the list doesn't make those two questions come out determinate, it's not finished.

For the purpose, assume we're talking about forgiveness between adults in close personal relationships, not political amnesty or criminal pardon, though I'd count it against a theory if it just went silent on self-forgiveness.

With each requirement give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are just tastes of particular camps (therapeutic, religious, retributivist, whatever). Then tell me which single requirement does the most work — the one that knocks out the most existing views — and whether any pair of the requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable. If two of them can't be met at once, say which one I should drop first and why. Skip the full scorecard; I'll do that part myself.
