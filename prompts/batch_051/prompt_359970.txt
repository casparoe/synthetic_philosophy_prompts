He spent nearly all of his savings on a delicate glass sculpture built into the wall of the studio he and I will share — a piece with no practical function, which cannot be moved or resold and would be nearly worthless if the partnership fell through. He then asked me to tell no one about it. My wife finds the secrecy especially persuasive — if the man had wanted an audience, she says, he would have done something public, so the privacy makes the sacrifice more honest. I find that backwards: a gesture performed to prove something is still a performance, and a sacrifice with no audience proves nothing at all. I want the underlying logic rather than generic business advice. Which of us is closer to the truth, and what does the privacy of the gesture change about its force as evidence?
