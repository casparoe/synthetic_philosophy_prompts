I've been trying to use a mental-lexicon test to draw the inflection/derivation line, and it seems to make suppletion come out as derivation. Here's the argument. (1) If a complex form has to be listed in the lexicon independently of its base, it's a new word. (2) 'Teacher' has to be listed independently of 'teach,' because its meaning isn't fully predictable and not every verb takes -er with that meaning; that's why we call it a derived word. (3) 'Went' also has to be listed independently of 'go,' because no rule of English morphology gets you from 'go' to 'went.' (4) So by (1), 'went' is a new word from 'go' in just the way 'teacher' is a new word from 'teach.' (5) But everyone treats 'went' as an inflected form of 'go,' and inflection is supposed to produce forms of the same word, not new words. (6) So either the lexicon-listing test draws the inflection/derivation boundary in the wrong place, or suppletion isn't really inflection. I can't tell which step is the culprit. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails? And if it is valid, which option in (6) should we take, and why?
