I've been trying to pin down what makes a statement racist, and for now I'm working with three criteria: (1) its literal or presupposed content ranks or essentializes racialized groups; (2) the speaker intended to express or stir up racial animus; (3) it predictably causes harm to a racialized group in a way the speaker could reasonably have foreseen.

The case I can't settle is this. A first-time state senate candidate runs a streaming ad. The copy is generated and A/B tested by a machine-learning system the campaign leases; the system only optimizes for watch time and donations. Out of thousands of variants, it keeps favoring the line 'let's bring order back to our neighborhoods' over B-roll of storefronts after a protest. The candidate approves the winning variants without thinking about race at all, and nobody on staff picked the line because of it's dog-whistle quality. After the election, outside researchers find the ad nudged up white viewers' scores on a standard racial resentment scale, while Black and Latino viewers' scores barely moved. And polls before the campaign had shown similar 'law and order' language moving the same needle in similar districts, in a pattern the campaign's pollster flagged years earlier in a memo the candidate never read.

So: is that line, as run in that context, racist on my criteria? Go criterion by criterion and say honestly where it's satisfied, where it fails, and where the definition just doesn't decide the case. Also say whether the case shows one of my criteria needs changing, or whether it shows something about who the 'speaker' even is when an ad is written by an optimization system.
