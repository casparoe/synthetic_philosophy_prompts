ok so here's roughly where I've landed on the impassibility question and I'd honestly like a consistency check before I have to defend this in a seminar. I hold all of the following:

(1) Perfect love has to include being affected by the beloved. If God loves us, then our suffering can reach God in some way; otherwise his love is just beneficence plus a nice disposition, not really love (or at least not perfect love).

(2) God is strictly impassible: creatures cannot act on God so as to change or determine his inner life. God is never passive, and his inner life doesn't gain or lose anything on account of what we do.

(3) The two-natures formula: the Son is one person with a divine and a human nature. In the incarnation the Son genuinely suffers and dies in the human nature, while the divine nature remains impassible, so it is true of the person that God suffers even though the divine nature does not.

(4) God has perfect first-personal knowledge of what human suffering is like. He doesn't merely know that creatures suffer; he is acquainted with the felt character of suffering. I don't see how perfect compassion works without that.

(5) Outside and before the incarnation, the divine life contains no suffering: no pain, no grief, no loss. The Son's suffering is real and not theater, but it is the human nature that supplies the suffering.

I think this set hangs together, but every time I explain it someone tells me I'm either smuggling passibility back in, making God ignorant of something, or making the cross a put-on. I want an audit, not a lecture. Which of these are actually in tension with each other, which only look like they are, and how severe is each tension? Then give me a committed verdict: if this were your position, which one commitment would you modify or drop first, what exactly would you change it to, and what does that cost the rest of the set? Don't just lay out options and leave me to choose.
