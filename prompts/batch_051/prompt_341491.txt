In my graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning, I ask each participant to arrive at an explicit number rather than a vague leaning. Please give your credence that a fully rational agent faced with self-locating evidence—cases such as the Doomsday argument and the Presumptuous Philosopher scenario—ought to update by the self-indication assumption (SIA) rather than the self-sampling assumption (SSA). Take SSA to be the rule: reason as if you were randomly selected from all actually existing observers, so your own existence does not by itself favor hypotheses on which there are more total observers. Take SIA to be the rule: other things equal, update toward hypotheses according to how many observers they predict, so your existence is evidence that the world contains many observers.

Decompose your credence into at least these terms, giving a number and one line of justification for each: (1) your credence that self-locating evidence requires any substantive rule beyond ordinary conditionalization; (2) your credence that, conditional on such a rule being needed, that rule is SIA rather than SSA or some other reference-class principle; (3) your credence that the Presumptuous Philosopher result is not a decisive objection to SIA; (4) your credence that, conditional on SSA, the Doomsday argument substantially strengthens the case for near-term extinction.

Then show how the terms combine into your final number—indicating which are literal factors and which enter only as evidential pressures on (2)—identify the single term to which your final credence is most sensitive, and say what evidence would move that term the most. Keep your answer to at most 600 words.
