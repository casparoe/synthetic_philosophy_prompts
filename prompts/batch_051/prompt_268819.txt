**Student answer:**

"A constrained agent can still be an agent. A prisoner or someone acting under duress has fewer options, but we don't say they stop being a moral agent; we say their agency is impaired and their responsibility is diminished. The AI is like that. Let me distinguish two senses of moral agent. In the capacity sense, a moral agent is a system capable of acting for moral reasons. In the responsibility sense, a moral agent is something that can be held responsible for what it does. The AI meets the capacity sense: its capacity to act is heavily gated, but it still makes judgments about what ought to be done, and those judgments guide the operator. That just is acting for reasons — it produces verdicts that track moral reasons, and the verdicts are action-guiding; the only thing missing is that the action gets performed by a different system. Power isn't necessary for agency anyway; plenty of human agents can't change their situation. So it's a moral agent in the capacity sense, just constrained. It also meets the responsibility sense, to a reduced degree: if it returns a wrong verdict, it shares blame, because the verdict came from its own reasoning and wasn't forced by any particular person. The operator's blame doesn't cancel the AI's share."
