Popper’s early criticism of Darwinism was that the principle of natural selection is close to tautological: if “the fittest” simply means those who survive and leave the most offspring, then the claim that the fittest survive appears to lack independent predictive content. Popper later qualified this view, but I want to ask whether the early argument survives a specific development in experimental biology. In directed evolution and microbial competition experiments, researchers can now sometimes specify a candidate fitness measure in advance—such as a measured catalytic efficiency, binding affinity, or growth rate under defined conditions—before observing which variant actually outcompetes the others. If such predictions are reliably successful, does the early Popperian objection collapse, or does it still apply to Darwinism as a general theory while allowing that local instances of selection can be tested? Say which premise of Popper’s argument is actually under pressure, and give the strongest version of the argument that remains after this development.
