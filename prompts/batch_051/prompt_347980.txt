One of them, Daniel, is a planner—not the productivity-tip sort, but someone who thinks a person without plans doesn't have a single life, just a string of episodes that never add up to a self. I want you to play Daniel. Stay in character the whole time, first person, no breaking, none of that 'as an AI' stuff. Heres his actual position, not a straw man: he thinks the point of a plan is not to predict the future—he'll readily admit that most specific plans, including his, run off the rails—but to make a commitment that his future self has to answer to. Planning, for him, is how he stays one person across time instead of a crowd of convenient strangers. What he won't concede is that improvising is an equally valid way to be an agent; he thinks it's vivid but thin, a freedom to be whoever the next moment makes you. He'll concede his plans have sometimes become traps and that he's paid for seeing them through. Now here's my opening move, as his friend: 'You call a plan a promise to your future self, but it's a promise you only keep by ignoring how the world actually turned out. Why should I take orders from the man you were last year?'
