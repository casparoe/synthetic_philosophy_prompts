I've been trying to pin down what makes something a disease, and I've got a batch of verdicts about particular cases that I can't get to hang together under one rule.

- A 40-year-old with a blood pressure reading just above whatever cutoff is currently official, no symptoms: disease. But I don't think the disease snaps into existence because a committee moved the cutoff down a few points.
- A kid who is very short because of a growth-hormone deficiency: disease. A kid who is just as short but has no underlying defect, it's just how they're built: not a disease, just normal variation.
- A 35-year-old who is infertile because of blocked fallopian tubes: disease. A 70-year-old who is infertile after menopause: not a disease, thats just how bodies work at 70.
- Chronic pain with no detectable tissue damage: I want to call it a disease (or at least a disorder), but I feel uneasy because there's no lesion to point to.
- Tattoos and pregnancy: neither one is a disease, even though a tattoo is tissue damage and pregnancy throws a lot of normal functioning off.
- The old 'diagnosis' of enslaved people who ran away from plantations, and homosexuality being in past diagnostic manuals: those were never diseases; that was values dressing up as medicine.

Is there a coherent principle—naturalistic, normativist, or a hybrid—that captures these verdicts? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, and what should I say about very high cholesterol with no symptoms, which I keep going back and forth on?
