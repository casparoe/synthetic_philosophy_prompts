I have been treating van Fraassen's Reflection principle as a basic norm of epistemic rationality. As I understand it, the principle says that for any proposition A, my current credence in A, conditional on my future self assigning A some particular value r, should itself be r. I am now stuck on a case in which the principle seems to force an absurd conclusion. Suppose I currently have conclusive evidence that a proposition is false, and I know that a drug I am about to take will make my future self nearly certain of that proposition, not by providing new evidence but by degrading my cognitive capacities in a predictable way. Conditional on my future self assigning the proposition a high credence, Reflection appears to say that my present credence in the proposition should also be high, even though my current evidence tells me the proposition is false. I am confident I must be misapplying the principle, so I want someone to locate the error: why does Reflection not actually require me to adopt, right now, the belief that the drug will induce? Show me exactly where the inference slips.
