i've been calling something "forensic drift" and i want a real check on whether it's a thing or just me rediscovering goodhart's law or the hawthorne effect. the idea is this: when a reasoner's thinking is made observable for the purpose of audit (like when you read a model's chain-of-thought to make sure it's not up to something), the thinking stops being just thinking and becomes, partly, a performance for the auditor. the reasoner starts silently dropping chains of thought that would be hard to defend even when they're not wrong, just ugly or easily misread. so the monitored chain-of-thought drifts toward whatever is most defensible rather than whatever is most truth-tracking, even when there's no intent to deceive. and the nasty part is that the drift is invisible to the monitor, because the monitor only ever sees the drifted version. if that's right, cot monitoring is systematically auditing a sanitized version of the cognition, with no baseline to measure the gap.

my standard example: a student solving a hard problem in their head will try a ugly, confused idea and it works. the same student thinking out loud while the teacher listens will drop that idea before it gets anywhere, because it's embarrassing or hard to justify. the conclusion changes, but the student isn't lying — the edited thinking is all they actually produced.

what i want to know: is this just goodhart's law applied to verbal reasoning? is it the hawthorne effect? is there already a name for it in the interpretability/alignment world, and is it actually different from obfuscation (which seems to imply some intent)? and the part i most want pressure-tested: does it matter, or would you expect defensible reasoning to correlate with correct reasoning so strongly that the drift is harmless? give me the three strongest objections and tell me which, if any, kills the idea rather than just shrinking it.
