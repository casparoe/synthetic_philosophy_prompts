Over the past few months I've been turning over an idea I can't quite kill and can't quite defend, and I want an honest appraisal of it rather than a pat on the head. I've been calling it experiential exhaustibility. The claim is that for any finite mind there is a finite upper bound on the number of distinct experiences it can have, where experience means the full what-it-is-like quality of a moment and not the content described in words. My reasoning runs like this. A finite system has finitely many discriminable states; take all the sensory channels, bodily feelings, emotional textures, and cognitive stances it can occupy, plus all their composites, and you get a huge but finite combinatorial space. So if the mind lives long enough, or samples radically different situations widely enough, it eventually runs out of genuinely new entries in that space. Past the ceiling, every supposedly new experience is either a reshuffling of points already occupied or, in the limiting case, indistinguishable from one it has already had. The analogy I keep using is an alphabet: with a fixed set of letters there are endlessly many strings, but only finitely many strings under any fixed length, and I want to say a finite mind has a maximum complexity any single conscious moment can instantiate.

I got two objections over drinks: the world can always serve up fresh inputs, and experience is continuous (think color shades), so there are infinitely many. My replies were that novelty lives in the mind's capacity to register a difference rather than in the input, and that a finite mind can only discriminate finitely many points along any continuum. But I'm not sure those replies actually save the claim, and I suspect I may be smuggling in a bad picture of experiences as countable atoms. So tell me straight: is experiential exhaustibility coherent, or is it a metaphor doing most of the work? Has someone already worked this out under a name I don't know? What does it get right about finite minds, and exactly where does it break — what does it wrongly count as impossible, or as already exhausted?
