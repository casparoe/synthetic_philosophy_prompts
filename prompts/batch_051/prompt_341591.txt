The sea-battle argument, in the form in which it comes down from Aristotle's On Interpretation, asks us to consider whether the assertion that a naval battle will take place tomorrow is already true or already false today. If it is already true, the argument runs, then the battle cannot fail to occur; if it is already false, then the battle cannot occur; and since the assertion must be one or the other, the future seems settled in advance. One influential reply, typically read back into the passage, is to reject bivalence for just these cases: until the matter is settled, the assertion is neither true nor false.

Now vary the case in two ways. In the first variant, tomorrow's battle is not left to the admirals' still-open deliberations but to a genuinely chancy mechanism. At first light a device will inspect a sealed chamber containing a single atom of an isotope with a twelve-hour half-life; if the atom has decayed during the night, the fleet attacks, and if it has not, the fleets withdraw. The mechanism is public knowledge, and everyone is resolved to obey its verdict without further discretion. In the second variant, the chamber contains a wholly deterministic mechanism whose initial state already determines whether the attack will occur, but no observer can inspect that state before dawn; there is a present fact of the matter, and only human ignorance remains.

Work through both variants under the two competing treatments. What does a committed bivalent semantics say about each — can it point to the fact that now makes the assertion true or false, or must it allow that future facts suffice, and does either move still face a fatalism objection? What does the truth-value-gap or three-valued treatment say: does its original rationale transfer to the chancy case, or does a determinate objective probability behave differently from a genuinely open future? Say which difference between the two variants is doing the real work, which standard position about bivalence survives the variation, and which position is most strained by it.
