Last week at a meetup someone argued that a Kardashev type II civilization would have a stronger claim on our moral attention than a pre-industrial one, because it could do more. I stopped listening internally the moment he said 'Kardashev.' I have this rule: any time a civilization scale shows up in a normative argument—Kardashev's energy ladder, Barrow's inward scale about manipulating smaller and smaller structures, anything of that sort—I treat the argument as suspect, usually suspect enough to stop engaging. My justification is that those scales measure control, not advancement: energy throughput and manipulation fineness say nothing about whether a civilization is wise, coordinated, robust, or even sane once it has that power. But after the meetup I realized I couldnt have reconstructed his actual argument if you'd paid me, and that's exactly the move I'd criticize in someone else if they'd pattern-matched to a word and checked out.

So the question is about the rule, not him. Is it defensible to discount normative arguments that use civilization scales as a proxy for importance? Is there a restated version that keeps the real worry about one-axis theories of progress without giving me a license to never engage? And from the inside, how do I tell the difference between applying that rule as a tool and using it to protect a prior?
