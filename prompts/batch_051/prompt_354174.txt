In a course on the philosophy of biology, the instructor repeatedly met questions about what genes do with the claim that DNA is a code and that genes carry information, as though the phrasing settled the issue. A textbook goes further, arguing that since the genome is a store of information, it can literally be read, copied, and edited like a text, with mutations counting as errors in that text. I want to know what these expressions actually commit a person to. Lay out the distinct precise theses that DNA is a code and that genes carry information could express, from the weakest reading — nothing more than the fact that nucleotide triplets map to amino acids — to the strongest — that the genome is genuinely semantic, with content independent of any interpreter. For each reading, say whether it is plausible and whether it supports the textbook's conclusion that the genome is text-like; then say which reading, if any, the conclusion actually requires and whether that reading can be defended.
