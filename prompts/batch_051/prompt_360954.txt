I chair the Interim Approvals Committee of the Vanaia Marine and Terrestrial Reserve Authority, which licenses any deliberate release of a self-propagating genetic construct anywhere in the reserve. An applicant consortium has applied for a permit to release a gene drive on a small, ecologically closed atoll in the reserve — call it Site K — where ship-borne rats introduced in the colonial period have extirpated two endemic ground birds and now threaten the last population of a third, the K-banded crake. The drive in question biases inheritance of a female-sterility allele and is expected to collapse the rat population within roughly a dozen generations; the applicants describe it as self-limiting because its separate elements are inherited apart and one of them will be diluted over time. They have also notified us, for information rather than for current approval, of a second phase in which they would introduce a genome-edited proxy of an extinct Site K ground rail to restore the island's lost seed-dispersal and soil-turnover functions.

Our enabling legislation requires a precautionary default for interventions whose consequences may be irreversible, but it also instructs the Committee to give real weight to the restoration of ecological integrity. The applicants' central argument is that a decision to withhold approval is no less irreversible than a decision to grant it: on current projections the crake will disappear within fifteen years, and no rat-free habitat remains to receive a translocated population. The opponents' central argument is that what is technically self-limiting is not ecologically reversible — if the rat collapse releases an invasive shrub, triggers a mouse eruption, or otherwise shifts the island into a new state, no one can recover the prior state by recalling the construct, and the second-phase rail would be a novel organism rather than a return of the species.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before our vote. It should recommend either approval, rejection, or approval with conditions; if it recommends any form of approval, it should specify the conditions, monitoring requirements, time frames, and the off-ramps under which the Committee would halt the release or the second phase. It should also state explicitly whether and how the de-extinction phase should bear on the current decision, and it should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side without caricature.
