Here's a thing that's been bugging me. Most countries with conscription have a "conscientious objector" exemption, but it's almost always limited to people who object to war in general. If you're a *selective* objector — someone who says "I'm not a pacifist, I'd fight in a just war, but I think this particular war is unjust" — the state generally says too bad, you're going anyway. That seems backwards. The selective objector is using the state's own just-war standards and judging the current war against them; the absolute pacifist rejects the whole framework. Yet the pacifist gets the exemption and the selective objector gets punished. So what theory of violence, or of the state's authority over conscripts, does that practice presuppose?
