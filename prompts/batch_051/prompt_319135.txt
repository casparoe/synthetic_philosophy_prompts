I work in safety assurance for autonomous mobile robots — warehouse AMRs, mostly. We spend our days writing safety cases for a bounded ODD, defining hazard severity classes, setting residual-risk thresholds, deciding between certification and runtime monitoring, and figuring out when a machine has to drop into a minimal risk condition or hand control to a certified human. The running joke on my team is that as soon as a robot gets wheeled somewhere outside the ODD it was approved for, we've basically got an unlicensed teenager driving a forklift.

Someone recently sent me down a philosophy rabbit hole about licensing parents, and it hit close to home. The argument, as I understand it: we license driving and performing surgery because (1) they can seriously harm people and (2) doing them competently takes genuine skill. Parenting has both features — a bad parent can wreck a child's life, and parenting is hard to do well — so by the same logic, parenting ought to be licensed too. There's also a second piece, the child's right to an open future: the idea that children hold their future autonomy in trust, so parents and the state shouldn't foreclose major life options before the child is old enough to choose. I gather the licensing argument is associated with someone named LaFollette and the open-future bit with Feinberg, but I haven't read either.

I want you to restate this whole position — the licensing argument plus the open-future constraint — in the vocabulary I actually think in: safety cases, ODDs, hazard classes, residual risk, certification, minimal risk conditions, capability thresholds, maybe objective functions and reward specification. Be as faithful as you can, and where an engineering concept only sorta fits, flag the fudge rather than silently bending the philosophy to the vocabulary. Then give me the residue report: what in the philosophers' position did not survive the translation, and is that residue evidence that my vocabulary is too poor for the subject, or that their claims are less sharp than they look?
