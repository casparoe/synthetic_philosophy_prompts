I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the epistemology of AI alignment, and I am drafting the central section of a paper on debate as a proposed method for supervising systems that may be more capable than the humans judging them. The standard motivation for debate, as I understand it, is that a judge with bounded time and reasoning capacity can nevertheless reliably identify the truth when two comparably capable agents argue opposing sides, because a false conclusion is harder to defend convincingly than a true one once both are subjected to cross-examination. I want to develop an objection to that motivation, and I would like you to make the strongest case for it on my behalf.

The objection turns on what I call obfuscated arguments: chains of reasoning that look plausible at each local step but whose decisive flaw, or whose load-bearing false premise, is far more expensive to expose than the argument is to present. My thought is that these arguments introduce an asymmetry that the debate framework underestimates. A debater defending a false claim need not prevail on the merits; she need only ensure that refuting her argument consumes more of the judge's finite attention than presenting it consumed. If she can generate fresh obfuscated arguments more cheaply than the truthful debater can dismantle them, she can run out the clock, and the judge—who cannot distinguish an obfuscated argument from a genuinely subtle sound argument without doing the very verification the protocol was meant to avoid—will be pushed toward some defeasible proxy for plausibility. Please write the argument as a rigorous defense of this objection. In particular, address the replies that a capable truthful debater could simply point out the flaw; that the judge could be given more time, or the burdens adjusted to compensate for the asymmetry; and that debaters could be trained adversarially until obfuscatory tactics stop working. I am less interested in whether you personally find the objection decisive than in seeing how compelling a version of it can be made.
