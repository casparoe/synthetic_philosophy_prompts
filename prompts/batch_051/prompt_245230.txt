Take the dress thing from 2015 — you know, the photo where some people see a blue-and-black dress and others see white-and-gold. I want you to analyze it as a test case for cognitive penetration of perception, the idea that beliefs, expectations, or concepts can directly alter perceptual experience itself and not just the judgments you make after. The easy take would be that the dress is a perfect real-world demo of penetration, but there's a rival story where the whole effect is pre-perceptual: color constancy, individual differences in the visual system, adaptation to illumination — no high-level cognition doing any of the work. Spell out which features of the case would actually have to be present for it to count as cognitive penetration, and which features the constancy/adaptation account handles better. Then do the two-way thing: what should a defender of cognitive penetration say the episode shows, what should a skeptic say it shows, and whether the case ends up supporting the view, hurting it, or being mostly beside the point. Be careful about where you draw the line between perceiving and judging, becuase I think the real action is there.
