So I write fiction, which means I spend an embarrassing amount of time deciding what gets rendered as a scene and what gets thrown into summary. Scene: a woman blinking away a speck of dust, the sting, the watering eye, the three seconds of being annoyed. Summary: a million people get dust in their eyes. Heres my theory, which I've been calling focal ethics. I think the dust specks versus torture puzzle isnt really about how to aggregate harm at all. It's about the fact that every aggregation is already shot from a particular focal length, and there isn't a neutral one.

The basic claim: any judgment about how harms stack up against each other has to decide, usually without noticing, how many individual experiences get rendered close-up (as scenes) and how many get collapsed into a count (as summary). There's no focal length zero, no view from nowhere; even doing the arithmetic in the abstract is just a very long lens. So when someone says the torture is obviously worse, or obviously not worse, they aren't reporting a magnitude. They're reporting the focal length they imported without checking it. Torture comes pre-filmed as a scene, because it's one person and prolonged. A trillion dust specks only enter the frame as summary, because nobody can hold a trillion close-ups at once. If you force both into the same register, the comparison dissolves: both as summary, you get two ledger entries and the felt difference collapses; both as scene, you get a loop of tiny stingings that each feel like nothing much, and the sum still won't come into focus. So the stronger claim I want to defend is that beyond some number of subjects, an aggregate of pain isn't a quantity at all — it's a change of narrative register being mistaken for a quantity.

I know this sounds adjacent to scope insensitivity and the identifiable victim effect, but I don't think it's the same. Those say we're bad at big numbers. I'm saying there's no big number to be bad at until you've picked a focal length, and the dilemma hides this by giving each option a different default lens.

So: is this an actual distinction or just a craft metaphor with delusions of rigor? What's the nearest existing idea, under a name I should have known, and where exactly does mine break? Give me the strongest objection from the torture side, the one I'd have to answer to keep the whole thing from falling apart.
