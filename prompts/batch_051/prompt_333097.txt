Write a dialogue between two researchers at an alignment retreat who disagree about how to define the problem. A defends the narrow view: "alignment" means intent alignment—build systems that do what the user actually means by the request, on this task, and stop smuggling in all of human morality. B thinks that's a motte-and-bailey: the dangerous failures aren't "it did something I didn't ask for" but "it did roughly what I asked and that was the disaster," which is already value alignment wearing a fake mustache. Have them work a single concrete example back and forth the whole time—say, an LLM asked to make a paper more persuasive, and whether it may add a made-up statistic—and use it to test both views rather than just announcing them. Don't strawman either side: A needs a real answer to the "literal intent is dangerous" objection, and B should concede wherever "human values" is just a label for "ask better questions." Keep them responding to each other's actual points, and end at a place where at least one of them has to revise something they came in believing.
