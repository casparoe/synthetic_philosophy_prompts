I've been trying to sort out how precise a criminal law has to be by going with my gut on a few cases. A city makes it a crime to loiter in a way that annoys people walking by: I'd strike that, to much in the eye of the beholder. A park rule says vehicles are prohibited and they ticket someone on a skateboard: fine to me. A noise ordinance bans unreasonably loud conduct after 10 p.m.: also fine, despite the weasel word. A coastal law says no structure within a hundred feet of a dune, and they apply it to a child's sandcastle: I'd strike it. A medical licensing statute makes unprofessional conduct a crime: I'd strike that too. But then I notice I'm accepting unreasonable while rejecting unprofessional, even though both ask the enforcer to fill in a norm. My tentative principle is that vague terms are fine when there is a rough shared standard in the community, and not fine when the standard is contested or mostly invented by police. But I'm not sure that principle saves the dune case while still condemning loitering. Is there a better principle that gets all the verdicts, or should one of them go? And what does the principle you'd defend say about a law that simply makes disorderly conduct a crime, or one that bans any act which outrages public decency?
