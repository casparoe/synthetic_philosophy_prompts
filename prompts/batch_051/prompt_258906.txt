I'm a teaching assistant for a philosophy of religion course, and this week we covered William James's Gifford Lectures, published as The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902). On the midterm I gave the following question:

"James identifies four marks of mystical states: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, and passivity. Explain what each mark amounts to, and then evaluate whether, taken together, they imply that if the noetic mark is genuine, mystical 'knowledge' is something the person cannot communicate, even in principle. In answering, distinguish noetic quality from ordinary propositional knowledge, and ineffability from mere difficulty of description."

One student submitted this answer:

"James's four marks are ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, and passivity. Ineffability means the experience cannot be described adequately to someone who has not had it; noetic quality means it carries a sense of having grasped a truth; transiency means it cannot be sustained for long; passivity means the subject feels taken over rather than acting. According to James, these four marks are jointly necessary and sufficient for a state to count as mystical. Since ineffability means the content cannot be put into words, it follows that mystical states do not even have propositional content for the mystic — if they did, the mystic could say it. So although the noetic mark gives the mystic a feeling of knowing, what is known cannot be 'knowledge that'; it is at most a kind of wordless acquaintance. James's position is therefore that mystical knowledge, if it exists, is incommunicable in the strong sense that the mystic cannot formulate what was learned, and cannot know that they know it, because knowing that would require being able to articulate the proposition."

I would rather have a slower, criterion-by-criterion assessment than a general impression. Also, tell me how confident you are in your assessment and identify the single point you are least sure of.
