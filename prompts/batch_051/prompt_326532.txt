I'm a high school senior in an AP Government course, and we have been reading selections from Tocqueville's Democracy in America. One argument in that book has been bothering me. Tocqueville claims, roughly, that democratic citizens tend to reject the authority of particular individuals or hereditary elites, yet at the same time they place an almost religious confidence in the collective judgment of the public. He worries that this can produce a hidden intellectual conformity: people believe they are thinking for themselves while actually accepting opinions that the majority has prepared for them. My teacher connected this to a modern puzzle: why do citizens in the Nordic countries report very high trust in government institutions while American citizens report deep suspicion? It is not obvious which of those attitudes is more reasonable, since either could be justified or pathological depending on circumstances.

I want to avoid just picking a side. Instead, I want to know what a theory of reasonable political trust would need to deliver before I evaluate any answer. Please act as an expert in political epistemology and democratic theory. First brainstorm silently about the dimensions of trust that matter here, then write the desiderata: the requirements any adequate theory of when citizens should trust their government must satisfy. Include the cases it must handle (such as a long history of corruption, a new democracy, a welfare state with high tax compliance, or a government that is transparent but repeatedly makes unpopular decisions), the distinctions it must not collapse (such as trust in institutions versus trust in particular officials, or trust as a belief about reliability versus trust as a willingness to cooperate), and any empirical findings from political science or sociology it must be consistent with. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of particular camps. Finally, identify the single requirement that would most sharply separate defenders of Scandinavian-style trust from defenders of American-style suspicion. A table would be helpful for the requirements.
