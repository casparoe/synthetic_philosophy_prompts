I'm on the committee that reviews the common first-year humanities course at my university, and we've hit a standstill over something that was supposed to be trivial: which dating convention the entire course should use. The historians want standard BCE/CE with no year zero, where 1 BC is followed straight by 1 AD, because that's how the scholarship and the sources are written. The physicists and astronomers want astronomical year numbering, which inserts a year zero so intervals across the BCE/CE line subtract cleanly. A third member wants us to teach everything in the Human Era or Holocene calendar, which adds ten thousand years to CE so 2025 becomes 12025, on the grounds that all of recorded history uses positive numbers and the epoch isn't tied to one religious tradition. I have to send the dean a written decision by Friday. Please draft that memo: pick one convention for all first-year materials, give a rollout timeline, and seriously engage the strongest argument for each losing option rather than dismissing them. Continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and write in American English.
