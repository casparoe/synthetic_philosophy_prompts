I hold the view that no particular finite sequence is random or non-random: randomness is at most a property of a process, an ensemble, or an infinite sequence, and saying that one finite string is more random than another is a category mistake. What would count against this view? I want the considerations that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be, including formal results where they exist. In particular, I want to know whether the compressibility of a finite string — for example, the length of the shortest program that prints it and halts — shows that some finite strings are objectively more random than others, and whether the various attempts in algorithmic information theory to define randomness for finite strings (such as finite analogues of Martin-Löf tests) put pressure on my position. Which of these considerations would a defender of my view plausibly concede as counting against it, and which would they dismiss as merely stipulative, machine-relative, or question-begging? Finally, assess whether the position has been defined so as to be unfalsifiable, and say whether, for a view that concerns the boundaries of a concept, that kind of insulation is a defect or a feature.
