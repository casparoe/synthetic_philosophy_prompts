I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page proposal, roughly 1,200 words. My half-formed idea concerns the relation between free riding and social parasitism. The usual debate runs the two together as variants of the same wrong—taking the benefits of a cooperative scheme without doing one's share—and then asks whether the wrong is fundamentally about unfairness, harm, or a failure of reciprocity. I suspect that conflation is doing a lot of unearned work. A free rider in the standard image would not be missed: if she disappeared, the scheme would produce the same benefit at the same cost, and what she does is take a benefit while avoiding a fair share of the burden. A social parasite, by contrast, lives off the efforts of others in a way that looks closer to exploitation: her taking is not just an unearned surplus but a load on others, often identifiable others, and it may persist through something like manipulation of their goodwill rather than through a merely passive omission. These feel like different wrongs, or at least wrongs with different objects, but the fairness-centered accounts do not mark the difference.

Turn this into a proposal. State the question precisely so that it has a determinate answer—something like: is the wrong of social parasitism reducible to the wrong of free riding as usually understood, or does it include a distinct element of exploiting persons or their goodwill, and what practical or theoretical difference does that make? Explain why existing fairness-based, harm-based, and reciprocity-based accounts leave this open. Give the approach: I imagine starting with a conceptual mapping that separates the benefit received, the burden avoided, and the causal position of the shirker, then testing candidate accounts against a set of constructed cases, including cases of benefiting without contributing where no resentment seems fitting, and cases of parasitic dependence where there is no cooperative scheme at all—an imposition on a household, a dependant who lives off affection. I would also like a route through the reactive attitudes: a conjecture worth examining is that free riding primarily licenses a demand for fairness, while parasitism licenses something closer to contempt or a moralized judgment of dependency, and that the two can come apart. Say why this method is better than a straightforward literature survey or a defense of one existing position. Then say what the possible conclusions are, which considerations would decide between them, what a successful deliverable would show, and what would count as the project failing.
