The standard view says honoring sunk costs is irrational, since a cost once incurred cannot alter future incremental costs or benefits and therefore should be irrelevant to what one does next. A colleague who works on reputation in repeated games disagrees: when one's decisions are observed, continuing an unprofitable project can signal that one follows through on commitments, and that signal can change how others respond in ways that make the original investment pay off. Design the strongest test you can of whether this is genuinely rational commitment or merely escalation-of-commitment bias. Specify the conditions, state which result would support each reading, and say explicitly what a positive result could not settle—in particular, whether the line between rational and irrational honoring of sunk costs is partly normative and therefore not entirely experimental. Write the answer in American English.
