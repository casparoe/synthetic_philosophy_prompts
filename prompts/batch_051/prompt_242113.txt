My friend laid out the following argument, and I can't decide whether it is a genuine discovery or a trick of wording. Here is the argument as he gave it:

(1) For any proposition asserting the existence of some entity, the rational default is not to believe that proposition unless one's total evidence favors it over its negation.
(2) The proposition that God exists asserts the existence of an entity.
(3) Therefore, the rational default is not to believe that God exists.
(4) Atheism, properly understood, is simply not believing that God exists.
(5) Therefore, atheism is the rational default, and anyone who asserts that God exists bears the burden of proof.
(6) But agnosticism is the view that one's total evidence favors neither the proposition that God exists nor its negation.
(7) If one's total evidence favors neither side, then by (1) the rational stance is not to believe that God exists, which by (4) is atheism.
(8) Therefore, the consistent agnostic is already an atheist, and the supposed third position differs from atheism only rhetorically.

I resist (8), because agnosticism feels like a genuinely distinct stance, but I cannot point to the exact step that goes wrong. Is the argument valid?
