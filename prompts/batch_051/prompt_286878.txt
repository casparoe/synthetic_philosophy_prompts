I'm writing the big continuity-court chapter of my near-future novel and I need my judge's rulings to hang together across the series so she doesn't come off as making it up case by case. She sits on a "continuity docket," which decides whether somebody after an upload, transfer, or replacement is legally the same person or a new one — it matters for marriage, wills, and whether a killing counts as murder. I need her rulings to be consistant across the series.

Here's what she's already decided in the earlier book:

- A standard destructive teleporter (scan, transmit, disassemble the original, reassemble a replica at the other end) is a death; the arrival is not the person.
- A neuron-by-neuron replacement of a patient's brain with functionally identical synthetic units over eighteen months, with the patient awake and talking the whole time: the same person survived the whole way through.
- A company copies a living woman's connectome and boots up an emulation while the biological original is still alive: the biological woman is the person, and the emulation is a new person with copied memories.
- Everything below the brain replaced with prosthetics over several surgeries, the brain kept functioning throughout: same person.
- A stroke wipes every episodic memory and changes the personality, but the same body and brain continue: same person, not a death, just radically changed.

The new case in this book is: months of surgery install dormant synthetic units in and around a man's brain, then on a single evening the old neurons are chemically shut down all at once and the synthetic network is switched on. He's kept alive throughout — heart, lungs, and blood flow to the head are maintained — but the EEG is flat for about ninety seconds before the synthetic network lights up. Afterward he wakes with all his memories and personality intact, calls his wife by name, and passes every test they give him.

What should my judge rule if she's being consistent with her own track record? I don't need the "true" metaphysics here; I need the character to reason the way she's been resoning. What are the two or three principles that fit her earlier verdicts, where do they come apart on this new case, which one does her track record best support and what does it predict, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what future case would force her to reveal which rule she's actually been running?
