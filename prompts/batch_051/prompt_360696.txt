Your task is to draft the deliverable of an adversarial collaboration between two parties in a dispute about how to define honesty and deception for AI systems.

Camp A, the intentionalist camp, holds that honesty and deception apply to a system only in virtue of the system's attitudinal states. On this view, an honest statement requires the system to hold the proposition true in the relevant sense and to intend that the addressee accept it as true, while a deceptive statement requires the system to produce a falsehood in order to bring about a false belief in the addressee. A system lacking such states can be inaccurate, unreliable, harmful, or misleading in its effects, but it cannot literally be honest or dishonest.

Camp B, the functionalist camp, holds that honesty and deception for AI systems should be characterized at the level of a system's policy, training objective, and effects on receivers, rather than at the level of tokened inner states. On this view, an output is functionally deceptive when the system's policy or selection history produces false claims under conditions that reward or favor the receiver's acceptance of those claims; the question whether the system has a represented intention is treated as non-necessary.
