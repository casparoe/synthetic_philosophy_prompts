I've been turning this over for weeks and I can't tell if it's deep or just me pattern-matching. The parallel is between performance legitimacy in comparative politics — the claim that a regime can earn genuinely accepted authority, maybe even something like a right to be obeyed, by delivering growth, public goods, stability, and order rather than through the procedures that selected it, with Singapore and post-reform China as the standard cases — and the way production machine-learning systems actually get trusted in industry. In the ML case, a model is deployed and defended so long as it keeps hitting the metrics its operators care about: accuracy, ROI, false-positive rate, uptime, churn. The opacity of the training process, the missing interpretability or audit, sketchy data provenance: those get treated as nice-to-haves, and usually surface only after the system fails in public. Both cases look like the same underlying pattern — authority or trust grounded in measured outcomes rather than in the quality of the process that produced the thing — and in both, the outcome metric is fixed by the very party whose legitimacy is being tested, which smells like the same bootstrapping problem.

Here's why I hesitate. A government claims authority over persons who can talk back, coordinate, and revise their standards; a model gets delegated authority over decisions and has no leverage of its own. And the bare fact that it works might mean different things in the two settings: a citizen who accepts Singapore's system is not just a stakeholder in a prediction pipeline. So: is there a genuine structural identity here — one abstract account of consequentialist legitimation under an outcome metric — or does the analogy break exactly where it matters? If it is real, what would have to be true of both sides for lessons to transfer (for instance, could the known fragility of performance legitimacy when the economy stalls tell us something about model governance under metric drift)? And what is one concrete prediction the parallel would make that comes out false?
