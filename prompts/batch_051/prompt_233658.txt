I have been reading Dōgen on being-time, or uji, in translation, and I keep getting stuck on the same point. He seems to reject the ordinary picture in which time is a kind of river or container through which things pass, and to claim instead that a thing's being just is its time: each moment, each so-called dharma-position, is time itself and is complete on its own. But if each moment is self-contained like that, how is there any continuity? The person who sat on the cushion yesterday is, it seems, the same one who sits today, and Dōgen clearly does not want to give up the idea that practice unfolds over time. Please give me a careful, intuitive explanation of being-time and, in particular, of how Dōgen can allow that things change and persist through change without quietly bringing back the river picture he was trying to leave behind. I would also like to know what the word time is actually doing in the view: if everything is simply impermanent, what does saying that things are time add beyond that?
