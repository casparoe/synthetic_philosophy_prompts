I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep getting pulled back into a question I first met when I read Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot — the one where he translates the same tiny French poem dozens of times and spends the whole book worrying whether you can keep a poem's shape without turning its sense into something else. From the book I walked away with a working list of criteria for when something counts as a translation of a poem rather than an imitation or a brand-new poem:

1. A reader could reconstruct roughly what the original says from the translation alone.
2. The formal features are carried over — meter, rhyme scheme, stanza shape, sound patterning — rather than swapped out for new ones.
3. The translation produces an effect in the target language that is recognizably the same kind of effect as the original.
4. It works as poetry in it's own right in the target language.

Now the borderline case. There's a small tradition of homophonic translation, where the translator matches the sound of each syllable of the original with a target-language syllable and ignores what the words mean, so the original's sonic contour is preserved while the content comes out as something else — often nonsense, or a strange accidental poem. Take a homophonic translation from French into English of a short, tightly rhymed love poem. The English follows the sound of the French closely, so criterion 2 passes if sound patterning counts as form, and maybe criterion 4 passes if the accidental English poem is striking. But criterion 1 plainly fails: you could not reconstruct the French poem's claims or images from it, except maybe a faint mood. Criterion 3 is murky: the original is a tender lyric and the translation is funny and disjointed.

Go through my criteria one by one, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me whether the case is a genuine borderline translation or a clear non-translation, and whether the criteria themselves need changing — for example, whether criteria 1 and 2 are really tracking two different projects that got bundled together. And because I know translation theory has real fights about exactly this stuff, please separate what is settled (for instance, some formal features of a source language cannot be carried over at all) from what is genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
