so here's what i want: write me a sestina about reflective equilibrium as a method. i run a small philosophy reading group and the last session is on method — not just Rawls in ethics but the broader thought that justification is a matter of going back and forth between considered judgments and general principles, where neither side is foundational and both get revised until the whole set stops fighting. i'd like to close the session with a poem, and i want the form to earn its keep.

a sestina seems basically made for this: the same six end-words keep coming back in a different order each stanza, so the poem has to re-read its own material in a new light over and over, the way a principle looks different once the judgments have moved, and the judgments look different once the principle has moved. i want the envoi to read as a provisional stopping point, not a foundation — a place where, for now, the pieces have settled.

for the six end-words, they dont have to be jargon, but something like judge, rule, case, wide, narrow, fit would be good. if it helps, think narrow vs wide reflective equilibrium as part of the pressure. the poem should still work for someone who has never heard of the idea.

accuracy matters: a Rawls person, or a Goodman person, or someone familiar with the live worry that the method is just dressed-up rationalization, should recognize what's going on and not wince.

after the poem, give me a short note on where the poem takes liberties with the idea or leans on a contested reading of it — for instance whether it treats wide reflective equilibrium as the same kind of thing as narrow, or whether it suggests the method justifies rather than merely describes. and say which objection to reflective equilibrium you'd have to be genuinely moved by for the sestina conceit to stop pulling its weight — the point where the form-content match would feel fake instead of earned.
