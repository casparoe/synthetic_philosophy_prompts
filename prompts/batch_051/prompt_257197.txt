I'm taking a philosophy of mind course and got into a long argument with a friend about the color phi phenomenon, the one Dennett makes so much of. I keep going back and forth on how to classify it, so I wrote out my criteria. For me, a revision counts as Orwellian if: there was a determinate content in consciousness at the earlier time; that content gets replaced by a different content before the person can report or act on it; the replacement happens in memory rather than during the construction of the experience itself; and the original content is afterwards inaccessible even in principle. A revision counts as Stalinesque if the editing happens before the content ever becomes conscious, so there is no earlier conscious content to overwrite, and the only thing the person ever experiences is the already-revised version.

Now the case. In the standard setup, a red dot flashes at one location and then a green dot flashes a short distance away very soon after. Subjects dont report two separate flashes; they report a single dot moving continuously from the first spot to the second and changing from red to green roughly halfway along the path. The puzzle is how the brain can put the color change in the middle of the trajectory before the green dot has even appeared. The Orwellian reading says the subject first consciously sees something like a red dot, maybe already in motion, and then once the green dot arrives the memory gets rewritten into the moving-and-changing dot story, so the report reflects the revised memory. The Stalinesque reading says consciousness was delayed long enough for the whole moving-and-changing dot to be assembled before the subject was ever aware of it, so there was never a conscious red-only phase.

So, on the criteria I gave, is the color phi report an Orwellian revision or a Stalinesque construction? Go through criterion by criterion and say wether they underdetermine the verdict. And does the case show that the criteria themselves are the problem, maybe that they presuppose a single moment when a content was really conscious, when the case suggests there might be no fact of the matter? I'd also like to know whether one of my criteria is doing no work and should be dropped. Please write it as continuous prose, like an email, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
