i've been trying to get a distinction straight about jootsing, using the MU-puzzle as the testbed (MI, rule 1: append U to anything ending in I, rule 2: double the part after the M, rule 3: replace III with U, rule 4: delete UU). The standard solution jumps out of the rules and notices that the number of I's is never divisible by 3, so MU can't be reached. I want to pull that apart into two moves. 'frame-jumping' is when you jump out of the rules but keep the goal fixed—you still want to know whether MU is derivable, you just find an invariant outside the system that settles it. 'goal-jumping' is when you jump out of the goal as well: you stop asking whether MU is derivable and ask why you wanted MU rather than some reachable string, or whether the puzzle was even the right puzzle. my claim is that most of what gets praised as jootsing is only frame-jumping, and that goal-jumping is the move that actually matters when the 'system' is your own preferences or a trained policy. is this distinction already in the literature under some other name? can it be made sharp, or does goal-jumping just collapse into frame-jumping once you redescribe the system as including the goal? and is my central claim true, or unfair to the usual examples?
