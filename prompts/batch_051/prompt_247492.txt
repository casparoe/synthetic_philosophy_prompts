I'm a novelist — meaning I'm exactly the wrong person to be writing this memo myself — and I sit on the board of a small writers' residency upstate. Nine years ago we bought a derelict barn on the property and, after a very successful capital campaign, started turning it into a third writing cabin. It was supposed to cost 180k. We're at 430 now, the shell is maybe two-thirds done, and the contractor has just told us that finishing it properly will take another 190, give or take. The board is badly split. Half of us feel we're past the point of no return: the half-finished shell can't be used as-is and might fetch 60k from a local buyer if we're lucky, we'd still owe the contractor for work already done, and walking away would mean the last six years of fundraisers, site visits, and long board meetings were for nothing. The other half says all of that is backwards-looking; they say the only real question is weather an extra 190 is the best use of money we don't have, and that finishing would mean cancelling two residency fellowships and draining our maintenance reserve. I've been asked to circulate a short written opinion before next week's vote. Write the memo: it should recommend one concrete course of action (finish, sell, mothball, whatever), stand behind it with reasoning a skeptical board will accept, and honestly steelman the strongest argument on the losing side rather than dismissing it. And no 'it depends' hedge — pick.
