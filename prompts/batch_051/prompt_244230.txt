I recently read a short essay in a popular magazine about the ethics of consumption that attacked a distinction I had not thought much about. The author summarized the distinction like this:

"Some writers now insist on separating being frugal from promoting frugality, as if these were two different projects. But this is a distinction without a difference. Frugality is not an abstract good hovering above people's choices; it is simply the pattern formed by many people choosing to consume less, repair rather than replace, and decline upgrades. Promoting frugality, then, can only mean being frugal in ways others can observe. Every visible act of thrift is a small public argument for thrift, and every visible act of waste is a small public argument for waste. The idea that one could promote frugality without being frugal—say, by spending money on a campaign to persuade others to consume less—is self-undermining, because the spending is itself unfrugal, and the example of the spending speaks louder than the slogan. Conversely, even a quiet frugal life promotes frugality, since it lowers one's demand on shared resources and shows that a satisfying life can be had with less. The two therefore come apart only if you treat frugality as a good to be maximized rather than a way of living. There is no real choice between living the value and spreading it; they are two descriptions of one commitment."

Something about this strikes me as too neat, but I cannot locate exactly where it goes wrong. I can see that example matters and that an extravagant campaign for thrift is in tension with itself. But the author seems to slide from "being frugal can promote frugality" to "being frugal is the only way to promote frugality," and I am not sure that follows. Could you go through the passage and tell me which parts a philosopher working on this distinction would accept, which parts they would wince at, and how the distinction between being frugal and promoting frugality should actually be drawn? Does the author's conclusion survive once the distinction is stated carefully?
