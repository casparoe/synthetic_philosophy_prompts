Jeremy Bentham's hedonic calculus has no special 'sexual' category: a sex act is good or bad entirely through the pleasures and pains it produces, just like eating, dancing, or a warm bath, and he thought punishing private consensual sex almost always produces more pain than it prevents. The special horror people attach to sex is, on his view, mostly leftover taboo dressed up as principle. Yet nearly everyone—including plenty of self-described utilitarians—still treats sex as a moral domain all its own. A consensual one-night stand can be called wrong, degrading, or exploitative in ways a consensual rich meal or competitive game usually can't, and societies keep whole separate legal codes for sexual offenses. What is the strongest justification for treating sex as morally special in a way other pleasures aren't? State the principle, test it on at least a couple of hard cases (consensual sadomasochism, adult incest, paid sex, casual hookups), and say whether the asymmetry survives or whether Bentham was closer to right.
