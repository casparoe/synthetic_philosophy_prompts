The Halden Foundation's 1978 trust instrument contains the following clause, Article IV, Section 2: "The annual income of the Trust shall be applied to the support of working authors and the craft of serious prose. No part of the Trust shall be used to support any mechanical or automated system for the composition of literary prose, such support being contrary to the Founder's conviction that literature is the work of the human person alone." In 2024, the Board must decide whether to fund Loom, a nonprofit that provides emerging novelists with AI-assisted drafting and revision software under the supervision of editor-mentors; the software proposes passages and revisions, but each funded writer must substantially rewrite the final manuscript by hand, and the program markets itself as an aid to time-poor writers rather than a publisher of machine-written work. The drafters of Article IV could not have foreseen generative AI. Does Article IV permit or bar the grant? Address the question through four lenses, in this order: the letter of the clause; the purpose of the Trust as a whole; what Margaret Halden would have directed if asked today, where "what she would have directed" is taken in the idealized sense of coherent extrapolated volition—the preferences she would have after full information and coherent reflection, not merely her recorded beliefs; and what a reasonable trustee reading the instrument today would take it to mean. Answer in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Items 1–4 should give one lens each. Item 5 should state which lens should win in this case and why, and then name the single consideration or piece of evidence that would most likely reverse that verdict.
