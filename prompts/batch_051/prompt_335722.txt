My co-founder and I are in the middle of a row about a clause in our founders' agreement, and I genuinely can't tell whether I'm being sensible or just squeamish. We're both convinced AGI could arrive within our lifetimes and that whoever ends up controlling it will be, for all practical purposes, beyond any outside checks. His idea is that we write a short set of principles now — what we'd do with that kind of system, who we'd consult, what we would refuse to build — and agree that neither of us may amend them later, no matter what we've come to belive by then. His argument is that right now we're both frightened enough of what the other might do to bargain fairly; once one of us actually has the relevant power, there'll be no reason to keep any promise and every reason to renegotiate from strength. I can see the logic, but I also can't shake the second thought: a promise made by a version of me that can't imagine what he's promising about has no obvious right to bind the later version — and maybe no practical power to do it either, since whatever enforcement existed won't survive the power shift. So: should I sign something like this? If I do, what should it actually contain so it isn't either toothless or a kind of contract with a person who won't exist? And which of my two instincts — 'now is the only fair moment' versus 'that later me owes me nothing' — is doing the real work here?
