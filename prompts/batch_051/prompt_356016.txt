So I've been trying to nail down legal personhood, and the criteria I keep coming back to are: the entity can hold rights, it can bear legal duties, it can sue and be sued (even if only through a representative), and it has interests of its own that the law could in principle protect. Now take this borderline case: an AI system appointed as trustee of a small fund. It enters contracts through a human rep, buys and sells assets, can be sued in its own capacity if it mismanages things, and a court can order money moved out of accounts held in its name. But it can't feel harm, can't be punished in any way that matters to it, and its "preferences" are just whatever its objective function currently says. Does it qualfiy under my four criteria? And here's the kicker: a corporation is clearly a legal person and also can't feel anything, so does the corporation show my fourth criterion is doing no work, or is there a real difference between corporate interests and the AI's "interests" that should make me keep it?
