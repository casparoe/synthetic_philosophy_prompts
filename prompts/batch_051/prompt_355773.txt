So I just had a kid, and like a normal person I have spent the last two months at 3am reading population ethics. The problem is my gut verdicts on cases dont line up into any principle I can state, and I want you to tell me what principle, if any, captures what I actually think, and which of these verdicts I have to give up.

Here are the cases.

1. The extra friend. World A: a million people, all at 10/10 wellbeing their whole lives. World B: the same million at 10/10, plus one extra person at 9/10. I think B is not worse, and probably better. Adding a very happy life cannot make the world worse. This one feels non-negotiable.

2. The big happy expansion. World C: ten billion people at 10/10. World D: twenty billion people at 8/10, no one else affected. I think D is not worse. I might even pick D, because 8/10 is still an excellent life and there are twice as many people living it. If someone said D is worse because the average dropped from 10 to 8, I would think they were confused.

3. The barely-worth-living horde. World E: ten billion people at 10/10. World F: a hundred trillion people whose lives are barely worth living: enough food and shelter, no pain, mild passing pleasures, nothing more. I gather this is the repugnant conclusion, the claim that F is better. I reject it. I cannot get myself to believe F is better; it seems worse, or at least not the kind of world I would ever choose to bring about if E were available.

4. The single barely-positive addition. Same as case 1, except the added person is a 1/10. The original million stay at 10/10 and lose nothing. I still think adding this person is not bad. It may not be a big improvement, but bringing into existence a life that is more good than bad cannot by itself make the world worse when nobody existing is harmed. I also cant see how it could be worse just because it lowers the average from 10 to 9.999.

5. The everyone-takes-a-cut expansion. World G: ten million people at 10/10. World H: those same ten million people at 9/10, plus ten million new people at 9/10. I think H is not worse, probably fine, maybe better. But this is where I start sweating, because if I accept H, I feel like I've signed up for a road that ends at case 3, and I reject case 3.

6. The below-average addition. Same pattern as case 1, but smaller: ten people at 10/10, one added person at 8/10. If someone told me the world gets worse because the average fell from 10 to about 9.8, I would think they had lost the plot. Yet I also keep reaching for average wellbeing to do some work, because it is the only thing I have found that blocks case 3.

My only candidate so far is something like: total wellbeing matters, but each additional life counts for less the more people there already are. That sounds like an ad hoc patch, I can't state it precisely, and I have no idea whether it handles case 4 and case 3 together.

So: is there a real principle behind these verdicts, or do I have to give some up? If I have to give some up, which ones does the least damage? State the principle precisely if you can, say which of my cases force each part of it, apply it to a case I keep going back and forth on, and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. The extra case: a world of ten people at 10/10 versus a world of nine people at 10/10 and one person at minus 2, a life not worth living on balance. Don't just say the theory broke. Tell me the best repair.
