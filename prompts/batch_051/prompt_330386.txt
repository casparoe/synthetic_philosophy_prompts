I want a working definition of "a boring life" that gets the following cases right. For each case, tell me whether your definition makes the life boring, not boring, or genuinely unsettled, and flag any verdict that feels forced.

The cases:
1. A monk who performs the same liturgy, same meals, same chores, same schedule for fifty years and reports deep absorbtion and zero restlessness.
2. A backpacker who changes country every two or three weeks but complains of crushing, existential boredom the whole time.
3. A prisoner in solitary confinement in an empty cell with nothing to do.
4. A concert pianist playing the same piece for the thousandth time, still finding new details in it.
5. A farmer whose life follows the yearly seasons with little change from decade to decade, and who says she is content.
6. A restless, novelty-hungry person who cycles through new partners, jobs, and cities, finds an ordinary Tuesday unbearable, and says her life is varied but not happy.
7. A person in a permanent coma.
8. A security guard whose eight-hour shifts watching an empty parking garage are deadening, but whose evenings and weekends are rich and varied.

My pre-theoretic verdicts: 3 is clearly boring, 7 is clearly not boring, 1 and 4 i could argue either way, and in the 8-style case I want the definition to allow that the day is boring even if the life as a whole isn't — so make sure your definition can handle temporal grain.

Then use the definition to answer one substantive question: does it make novelty a basic constituent of well-being in its own right, or only something instrumentally good because it prevents boringness? If your definition gives the wrong answer on any case, revise it instead of hiding the misfire, and if it commits you to a surprising verdict, defend it.
