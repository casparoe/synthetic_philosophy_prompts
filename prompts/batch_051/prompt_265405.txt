Please write a 500-word ironic op-ed in the voice of a Kantian animal-welfare advocate, for the back page of a philosophy magazine, defending the claim that all our duties regarding animals are indirect duties to ourselves. The piece should remain deadpan and internally consistent: every argument the author gives must be one a strict Kantian can make from the Doctrine of Virtue without quietly granting that animals are themselves morally considerable. Center the piece on the passage in Kant’s Metaphysics of Morals, Doctrine of Virtue, section 17, where Kant says that a man who has his dog shot because it can no longer serve him has not violated a duty to the dog, since dogs cannot judge, but has damaged the humane disposition in himself that he is obliged to cultivate toward other human beings; and that treating animals humanely is a duty of man to himself, because it cultivates the sympathetic feelings that make him more morally serviceable to other humans. Use the passage to generate the humor: the advocate should sincerely present the position that when we refrain from beating a horse, the beneficiary is our own moral character, and that the horse is at most a useful occasion for self-improvement. At least one joke should turn on why even gratitude toward a loyal old horse is, on this view, owed to oneself and not to the horse. Do not let the author slip into saying that animal pain is morally relevant in itself or that cruelty wrongs the animal; if the author flirts with that thought, it must be immediately reinterpreted as a defect in the agent’s humanity. Write continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—the way one would write an email or a letter.
