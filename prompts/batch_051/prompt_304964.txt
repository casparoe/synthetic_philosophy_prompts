An ML team trains a model, holds out a slice of the data, and then announces that the model "generalizes" — that it learned the underlying rule, not just a lookup table — because its held-out accuracy is high. But the held-out slice is drawn from the same distribution as the training data, so the model is mostly being asked to interpolate among nearby examples. What theory of generalization or novelty is that practice presupposing? Does it actually require the strong claim that the model could make an intuitive leap to cases unlike anything it trained on, or is it compatible with a much more deflationary story about memorization? And if you built an evaluation practice around that stronger notion of novelty, what would it have to do differently, and what would even a pass there leave unsettled?
