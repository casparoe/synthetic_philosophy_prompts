For a paper on artificial intelligence and trust, I need a working definition of 'deception' that applies to AI systems, which generally lack beliefs and intentions in the human sense. Please propose a definition and test it against these cases: a person who knowingly tells a falsehood; a poker-playing AI that makes large bets with weak hands; a language model that confidently outputs a false answer because it is statistically likely; a chess engine that makes a move whose main effect is to tempt the opponent into a losing capture; a recommender system that labels sponsored results as 'recommended for you'; a model whose operators have instructed it to give false answers to certain questions; and a radar decoy that produces the signature of a full-sized aircraft. My leanings: the poker bot and the instructed model are deceptive, the hallucinating language model is not, and I cannot decide about the recommender system, the chess engine, or the decoy. Run each case through your definition, flag any verdict that surprises you, and say where the definition is least stable.
