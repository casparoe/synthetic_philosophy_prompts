For the past year I have found myself returning to a curious kind of report about my own life: the moments I would honestly defend as best are not the ones in which I acquired or accomplished something, but the ones in which I wanted nothing in particular and was not uncomfortable—sitting outside with no impulse to check my phone, the hour after a hard run, the days before a new project had announced itself. This has pushed me toward the view my partner dismissively calls tranquilism: that the only things of intrinsic value are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering, that craving and suffering are the only things intrinsically bad, and that everything else—pleasure, achievement, love, absorbing work—is at most instrumentally good insofar as it prevents or dissolves craving or suffering. My partner says this conclusion overreads the experience, because it is hard to tell whether what made those moments good was the absence of craving or the presence of something else I was simply not attending to.

I would like your credence that tranquilism, so defined, is true. By true I mean specifically: a state of affairs is intrinsically better to the extent that it contains less craving or less suffering, and intrinsically worse to the extent that it contains more of either; no other feature makes it better except as a means to those two absences. My own credence is roughly 0.6, and my partner's is closer to 0.2. Please give your number, but more importantly decompose it. Assign rough probabilities to the sub-claims that seem to you to matter—for instance, whether the absence of craving is a positive condition rather than merely a neutral baseline; whether craving itself is intrinsically bad even when it is not presently felt as suffering; whether ordinary goods like love and achievement have merely instrumental value; whether the view is self-undermining because it rests on a craving for non-craving; and whether it can avoid the conclusion that a life of dreamless sleep or early death would be best.

Then show how the sub-claims combine into your overall credence, say which of them your final number is most sensitive to, and tell me what evidence, argument, or experience would most move the number—and by roughly how much. If you would rather not put a single number on it, say what would count as resolution instead, and, if you can, indicate where the disagreement between my 0.6 and my partner's 0.2 most plausibly lives.
