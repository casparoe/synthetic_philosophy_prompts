I've been reading Feyerabend alongside a pretty orthodox 'there is a scientific method' textbook for a philosophy of science course, and it's striking how much the two sides talk passed one another. The monist says good science follows such-and-such rules; the anarchist points at a historical counterexample to the rule; the monist replies that the counterexample is a caricature or bad history; and round it goes. I'd love a document that forces both sides to actually put down what they agree on, and then pin down exactly where the disagreement survives.

So: write the joint statement that a committed defender of a unified scientific method (roughly: there is a stable set of norms or rules — something like testing ideas severely and letting observation arbitrate — that seperates science from non-science and explains its success) and a committed Feyerabend-style epistemological anarchist (roughly: every substantive methodological rule that has been proposed has, at some point, been broken by good science, and methodological pluralism is a virtue rather than a concession) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please don't flatten either side: the anarchist is not someone who thinks evidence doesn't matter — their target is monism about method, not observation itself — and don't just reduce them to the slogan that anything goes. The monist should likewise not come across as someone who has never noticed that working scientists break the rules on paper.

Give me, first, a numbered 'agreed claims' section that is substantive rather than vacuous. Then a disagreements table. Columns should be: (1) the residual disagreement, formulated so each side would accept the wording of its own position; (2) the crux behind it — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; (3) the argument, case or historical episode both sides agree would bear on the crux; and (4) each side's prediction recorded in advance. Three or four disagreements is about right. If a crux is historical (for example, whether a particular episode is best described as method-following or rule-breaking), use an episode a well-read non-specialist would actually know; if a crux is conceptual, say what would count as settling it. The test of the whole thing is that both parties could sign it without embarrassment.
