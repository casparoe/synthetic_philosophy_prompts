I keep running into "meaningful human control" in autonomous weapons debates, and I'm starting to think the phrase is doing different jobs for different people. Last week a campaigner used it to shut down a proposal for autonomous point-defense interceptors — fixed systems that would engage incoming rockets inside a small perimeter, no human in the loop for each individual shot, though a human could switch the whole thing off. He said that still violates meaningful human control, full stop. I've seen the same phrase in my own org's doctrine docs, but nobody there can agree on what the bar actually is.

So treat "meaningful human control" as a slogan with no fixed meaning. Give me exactly five distinct precise claims it could be expressing. For each one, spell out what "control" amounts to (who controls what, and when), say whether the reading is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false, and say whether that reading would actually support the campaigner's rejection of the point-defense system. Ideally the five should differ in what "meaningful" adds over just having a human somewhere in the loop, and they should run from weak readings pretty much everyone accepts to strong readings almost nobody applies. Number them 1 through 5, one or two sentences each, no introduction or conclusion. American English.
