A friend of mine has gotten really hung up on the frame/outer/inner message distinction from GEB — that is, the frame message being the "this is a message, decode me" signal, the outer message being the meta-instruction about wich code or convention to apply, and the inner message being whatever you get after decoding. His claim is that the meaning of a communication is located in the outer message, and that the inner message is just inert marks that do very little on their own. I want to know whether that claim is testable, rather than just a façon de parler. Design the best test you can of the thesis that the meaning-carrying work is done by the outer message (plus frame), not by the inner message. Be concrete: what you vary, what you hold fixed, what outcome counts for his view and what counts for mine, and what sort of receiver or system you'd run it on. Then say what a clean result would and wouldn't settle. Where does the question stop being testable, and is that untestable residue the part that's actually doing the philosophical work?
