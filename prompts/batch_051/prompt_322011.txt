Since I became executor of my uncle's estate, my family has been deadlocked over what may properly be done with what he left: whether to scatter his ashes, keep them, or divide them among relatives, and whether to sell, donate, or quietly destroy his heavily annotated prayer book. Trying to arbitrate this has shown me how little I actually understand the concept I keep reaching for. I want a working definition of desecration, one that classifies actions without simply tracking my own disgust or the claims of the religion in question.

Define "desecration" in a way that handles all of the following:

(a) a protester publicly tears pages from a holy book and sets them alight while denouncing the religion;
(b) a teenager, alone in his room, throws his own copy of the family's sacred text into the bin because he no longer believes;
(c) a daughter has her late mother's rosary made into a necklace that she wears every day;
(d) a city exhumes a nineteenth-century cemetery to build a road and reburies the remains in a new plot, with a recorded ceremony;
(e) a guest accidentally knocks over an urn at a funeral and then sweeps the ashes back into it;
(f) a soldier urinates on the body of an enemy combatant;
(g) a museum displays a mummy without consulting any descendant or religious community;
(h) a family sells a deceased aunt's annotated prayer book at an estate sale, where a stranger buys it to read.

I am confident that (a) and (f) are desecrations and that (e) is not; the others I cannot firmly classify. Propose a definition, run it through all eight cases, and say where it misclassifies, where it is silent, and where the pressure runs the other way—where the definition seems sound and one of my verdicts should instead be revised.
