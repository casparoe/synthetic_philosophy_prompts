I been trying to work out something about Aristotle that came up in a podcast, and I want to test it against a case close to home. The podcast described phantasia (usually translated as imagination) as the thing in us that produces phantasmata — sort of retained sense-images, like a faint after-echo of things we have seen or heard that stays after the thing itself is gone. These aren't the same as believing something, and they aren't the same as seeing something, but Aristotle apparently thought the mind never thinks without them: whenever we reason, compare, or decide, we are doing it with the images phantasia stores and shuffles together. A friend of mine boiled this down to a checklist for wether something counts as thinking, which we argued about: (1) the person is combining, comparing, or drawing something out of stored sense-images; (2) those images ultimately come from the senses; (3) the whole thing is aimed at truth or at deciding what to do, not just a reflex; and (4) the person can do it on purpose instead of its just happening to them.

Here is the case I keep picking at: my sister has aphantasia — she says when she tries to picture anything there is simply nothing, no mental pictures, no inner voice, no hearing remembered sounds. She is a civil engineer, and she thinks through load calculations and design constraints in a step-by-step way, but she insists she does it entirely without imagery, more like a felt sense of pressure or number relationships than anything like pictures or words. So, does she count as thinking? Go through the criteria one by one and say where she passes and where she fails. And if the criteria end up excluding her, is that a problem for Aristotle or just for my friend's checklist — for instance, could phantasia be nonvisual or not really picture-like, or would Aristotle just say she is using images without realizing it?

Also, if you think her case shows the criteria are too narrow, tell me which criterion is doing the work and how you would amend it. And can you write the whole answer in American English?
