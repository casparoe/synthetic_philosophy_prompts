For a piece I'm working on, I keep coming back to two camps in environmental history that talk past each other. One treats pathogens, volcanic winters, and climate shifts as historical actors in their own right — not just backdrop but causes with a kind of agency. The other says that's a category mistake: only human institutions, choices, and coercive structures act in history, and volcano-talk is either a metaphor or the first step into the environmental-determinism trap. I want to run a thought experiment: an adversarial collaboration between a committed member of each camp, with the deliverable being the document they'd both sign. Don't give me a debate summary or pick a winner — give me the constructive statement they could jointly put their names to. Format it as (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, substantive enough that each side recognizes itself but not hedged into mush; then (2) a disagreements table. For each row: the precise point where they still differ, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own view; the crux — the subquestion that, if settled, would actually move at least one of them; the case or test both agree bears on that crux (use things like the 1815 Tambora eruption and the year without a summer, smallpox in the conquest of Mexico, and the wet summers behind the 1315-17 Great Famine); and each side's prediction about what that case will show, writen down in advance.
