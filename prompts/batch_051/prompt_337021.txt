I'm trying to get clear on what my gut says love is responsive to. I love my partner for her patience, her dry humour, the way she listens, but if a stranger had exactly those qualities I'd admire them without loving them—so the qualities alone don't seem to do it. Yet when I ask myself why I love her, I point to her qualities. If she changed permanently after an accident—became impatient, cruel, humourless—I don't think I'd stop loving her, and leaving would feel like a betrayal, though maybe I'd be loving the person she was. I can't imagine loving someone I found completely worthless, and I think loving a genuinely cruel person is a mistake, not just an odd choice. Then there's a case that won't fit either way: I care about an ugly amateur painting because my late mother made it, and that feels like a kind of love, but its value seems to come from the relation rather than from anything the painting has. I'm torn between "love responds to value" and "love creates value." Is there a principle that gets all of this, or close? Which of my verdicts would it force me to drop, and what would it say about the case I keep going back and forth on—a partner with dementia who no longer recognizes me?
