I'm working on a piece about Simone Weil's late essay on getting rid of political parties, and I've boiled down the arguments she gives against parties, as I understand them, to these five: (1) a party is totalitarian in embryo, because it demands total loyalty and treats its own growth as an end in itself; (2) it turns politics into a clash of collective wills, where the question is who wins rather than what is true or just; (3) once you join a party your public positions are decided for you, so individual judgment gets suppressed and you end up defending views you haven't genuinely thought through; (4) elections become a choice among ready-made labels, so voters are not really deliberating about the concrete problems in front of them; (5) party spirit is a kind of collective pressure that makes disinterested thought nearly impossible, even for smart people. I suspect this is basically the complete list. Give me another objection to political parties in that same spirit — not a rewording, special case, or combination of the ones above — and then say honestly whether it is any good, meaning whether it adds something those five don't already capture.
