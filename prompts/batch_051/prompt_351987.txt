I'm a retired soil chemist, and lately I've been reading philosophy of law for fun, which my wife finds baffling. Something keeps bugging me, and I want it worked through with categories, not just named. Suppose a city has an ordinance from 1921 making it a misdemeanor to keep a shop open on a Sunday. It has never been repealed. But nobody has been cited under it since the mid-1960s; the police don't write tickets for it; the city attourney's office has an informal, unwritten policy against charging it; and when a repeal comes before the council every decade or so, it gets voted down as a matter of heritage, even though most of the people voting it down shop on Sundays themselves. Then a new mayor, sore at one big-box store for backing his opponent, quietly tells the police to start enforcing the ordinance again — but, in practice, only against that one store.

My working list of criteria for whether a legal system is governed by the rule of law, at least with respect to some rule, is this: (1) the rule was enacted by a valid authority and is still formally in force; (2) people can know in advance what will get them punished; (3) officials apply the rule, or stand ready to apply it, rather than treating it as decorative; (4) enforcement is even-handed, not aimed at whoever the current officeholder dislikes; and (5) people can rely on the settled official practice — including long non-enforcement, when that is the settled practice — without the ground shifting under them as a suprise.

So the question: before the new mayor, was this ordinance a law in the way rule-of-law systems have laws, or just dead words in the code? Is the city in better or worse shape, as far as the rule of law goes, after the mayor revives it? Please go through my five criteria one at a time for both moments — before and after — and say honestly where the criteria themselves are the problem (maybe I'm missing a sixth criterion about desuetude, or conflating two different failures). I'm not after a treatise; I want to know whether the case shows my criteria need amending.

Format: answer in American English, with sections in this order: "Set-up" (a few sentences restating the case as you understand it), "Criterion by criterion" (roughly 400–500 words, the core), "Where the criteria break" (a paragraph on where the list underdetermines or misclassifies the case), and "Bottom line" (two or three sentences on before vs. after, and which criterion, if any, I should change).
