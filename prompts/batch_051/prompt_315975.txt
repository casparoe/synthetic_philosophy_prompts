I have been working through the Great Filter, and I want your help reasoning through a specific hypothetical. Suppose that in 2050 an uncrewed submersible in Europa's ocean collects cells from a hydrothermal plume. Over the next decade, scientists become convinced this is a true second origin of life, not contamination from Earth: the cells use a chemistry distinct enough from ours (say, different nucleotide bases, or molecules with the opposite handedness from the ones Earth life uses) that shared ancestry is effectively ruled out. All the life found is unicellular; nothing with a nucleus, nothing multicellular, no evident predation. Accept these findings as given for the question.

What I want is a reasoned prediction of how this one discovery should change where we locate the bottleneck between nonliving chemistry and a galaxy-colonizing civilization. In particular: (1) Which transition should we now think is most likely to be the Great Filter, and how much should our confidence shift? (2) Does the discovery mainly support a picture in which the origin of life is relatively easy but something after simple cells is very hard — and if so, which later transition (complex cells, multicellularity, intelligence, technological survival) should collect most of the pessimism? (3) What would the discovery still leave open, given that we have exactly one new data point and our own existence is part of the sample we are reasoning from? Please make your chain of reasoning visible: mark which steps are robust and which are guesses, and say what future observation or argument would most change your conclusion.
