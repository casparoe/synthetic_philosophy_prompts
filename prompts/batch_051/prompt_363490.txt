In debates about AI policy I keep encountering the slogan "tool AI, not agent AI." It is used to dismiss quite different systems in the same breath: an autonomous coding agent that takes a GitHub issue and opens a pull request, an assistant that books flights on a user's behalf, and sometimes even a chatbot that asks follow-up questions rather than answering immediately. The people who invoke the slogan seem to think it settles the matter, but I doubt they all mean the same thing by it.

What are the distinct, precise claims that slogan could be making? For each candidate reading, spell out what it would take for a system to count as a "tool" or an "agent" on that reading; whether the reading is nearly trivial, moderate, or very strong; whether it is defensible; and whether it actually supports the conclusion that systems like a coding agent or a booking assistant should not be built or deployed. Present the readings as a menu rather than arguing for just one, and say which readings the slogan's users most likely need for their dismissals to go through. Please separate what is fairly settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and be careful not to present a contested reading as the consensus.
