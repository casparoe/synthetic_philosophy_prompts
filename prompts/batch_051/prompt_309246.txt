I'm moderating a panel with a classicist who defends the view that Plato's discussion of drunkenness in the Laws is a serious educational proposal: properly run drinking parties let citizens rehearse resisting pleasure, the way facing danger lets them rehearse courage. Her line is that wine deliberately loosens self-control so the party can serve as a controlled test of character, and she has a stock reply to the worry that Plato also calls wine dangerous and restricts who can drink. Give me a sequence of questions that hits the weaker parts of her reading, not generic "what drew you to this" stuff. Press the courage analogy once you notice wine impairs the very judgment that's supposed to learn restraint, press whether the real training is being done by the sober supervisors running the party rather than the drinkers, and ask whether the Symposium undercuts her, since it opens with everyone agreeing to take it easy after the night before. For each question, include the follow-up you'd ask after her likely move, and end with one question about what would actually count as evidence against her reading.
