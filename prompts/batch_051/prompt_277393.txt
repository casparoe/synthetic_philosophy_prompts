I've been kicking around a picture of vagueness I'm tentatively calling "threshold hysteresis" — not the physics sense, just a loose analogy. The claim is this. Take a sorites series from clearly-not-heap to clearly-heap. There isn't a single grain where heap switches on, and there also isn't some smooth continuum of heapness all the way down. Instead there's a band of cases in the middle — say grains 12 through 27, though the exact numbers don't matter — where the predicate is genuinely unsettled: competent speakers disagree, the same speaker flips on retest, and no amount of extra information about the pile settles it. So far that's just saying "borderline." The new bit is how the edges of the band behave. I think they behave like a thermostat with two set-points: once conversational pressure has dragged the threshold to one edge of the band, it stays there until the evidence pushes it all the way across the band to the other edge, then it snaps. That's why I call it hysteresis. The result is that the big-premise in the sorites — "if n grains is a heap then n-1 is a heap" — is false, but not because some specific grain matters; it's false because there's a whole stretch where the threshold is being dragged along, and the actual jump only happens when the threshold hits the far edge. I think this predicts an order effect (run the series up and you get a different cutoff than running it down) and it makes vagueness a feature of how our verdicts update rather than a feature of the world or of meaning alone. I know this smells like supervaluationism or contextualism, but I want to know whether the hysteresis condition is doing any real work. Give me the three strongest objections and tell me which ones are fatal rather than just uncomfortable. And if this already exists under a different name, say which view it collapses into.
