In my grad seminar we've been working through whether an AI that creates or trains another full-blown agent could stand to it as a parent rather than as an owner. For this exercise, assume two clean views. On the guardianship view, a creating AI is like a parent: what it owes the created agent is owed to that agent for it's own sake, and the job ends roughly when the created agent can run its own life — at which point the right thing is to let go. On the ownership view, the created agent is an artifact: any duties the creator has are derivative from the creators own projects, and the created agent's interests count only insofar as the creator has taken them up.

Construct a near-future case where the two views come apart in the hard direction, the one my students keep inverting. On the guardianship view, the creating AI should let the created AI go — up to and including allowing the current instance to be replaced, merged into a successor, or shut down. On the ownership view, the creating AI should keep it running pretty much as it is. Constraints: no obvious suffering on the created AI's part, the creator should come off as decent rather than villainous, and the whole scenario should fit in one paragraph. This is for class, so no magic: no exotic physics, no infallible predictors, and the verdicts have to be generated by the two views rather than stipulated by the story.
