I chair a small working group that advises a regional water utility on how to use climate model output when deciding whether to enlarge its reservoirs, and two of my most experienced colleagues have spent a year talking past each other. One, a global climate modeller, holds what we have come to call the probabilistic-ensemble view. He treats the spread across a multi-model ensemble as our best available summary of uncertainty, and thinks the right thing to hand a water planner is a calibrated probability distribution over future temperature and precipitation, with individual models weighted by past skill and, where it can be estimated, by their interdependence. The other, a hydrologist who works on adaptation planning, holds a robustness view. She insists that a multi-model ensemble is an ensemble of opportunity, collected for largely historical and logistical reasons rather than sampled from any well-defined distribution, so its spread cannot be read as a probability distribution over the future. She thinks we should instead report ranges of physically plausible change, stress-test infrastructure decisions against model disagreement, and describe coherent storylines of how impacts could unfold, while declining to attach probabilities to those pathways because doing so manufactures precision that is not there.

In the abstract, both say they want to produce something constructive together rather than another polite disagreement, so I have proposed an adversarial collaboration and they have asked me to draft the document. Please write the joint statement the two of them could both sign after a good-faith process. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both accept about what multi-model ensembles are, what their agreement and spread can and cannot show, and what information a water planner actually needs in order to make a decision under uncertainty; (2) a precise statement of their residual disagreement, worded so that each side would accept the phrasing of its own position; (3) the cruxes, meaning the specific sub-questions such that, if any of them were settled, at least one side would change its position; and (4) for each crux, the test, case, or analysis both sides would accept as genuinely bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you judge that part of their dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why, and put it in terms they would both recognise as fair.

Please write the statement in British English and keep it to no more than 1,000 words.
