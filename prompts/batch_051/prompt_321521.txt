I'm trying to get a taxonomy of possible Great Filter stages on paper, and I want an extra one I haven't already got. The candidates I've collected so far are:

1. Abiogenesis: getting from prebiotic chemistry to the first self-replicating cells.
2. The prokaryote-to-eukaryote transition: life stayed as simple cells for a couple billion years.
3. The evolution of complex multicellularity, with sexual reproduction maybe folded into that bottleneck.
4. The evolution of an intelligent, language-using, tool-using species.
5. The shift to a technological civilization capable of interstellar signals or spaceflight.
6. Civilizational self-destruction after powerful technologies arrive: nuclear war, engineered pandemics, artificial intelligence, resource exhaustion.
7. Planetary habitability windows: life arises, but the planet becomes uninhabitable before complex creatures can develop.

Give me one more candidate stage that is not on this list and is not just a relabeling of one of them. In particular, don't give me something that's already covered by items 2 or 3 under a different name. It can sit anywhere between chemistry and galactic civilization, but ideally it should be one people don't talk about. Then tell me honestly if it's actually a good Great Filter candidate. Also, say what evidence or argument would make you change your mind about it—what would convince you it's overrated, or that it basically reduces to one of the items I already listed after all.
