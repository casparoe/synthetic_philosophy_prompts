I cover science and ideas, and after a few years reporting on citizens assemblies, sortition, and democratic reform I've landed on a set of views I want to put in one long piece — but I want to know if they actually hang together before I commit. Here's where I am: (1) a standing legislative chamber chosen by lot should take over a big slice of what elected parliaments now do — setting the main tradeoffs and priorities — because elections mostly select for people who are good at campaigning and fundraising, not governing. (2) For the offices we still keep elected, I'm for strict term limits; career politicians are the problem, they get captured by donors and by the reelection clock. (3) But I also think governing is a real skill, and in a lot of policy areas we should defer to accumulated experience, including people who've done the job a long time. (4) And I think what makes a democratic decision legitimate is that voters can retrospectively punish the people who made it, by throwing them out at the next election. Are these four compatible? I cant tell which are genuinely pulling against each other, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix would be. Rank the real tensions by severity, and flag any that dissolve.
