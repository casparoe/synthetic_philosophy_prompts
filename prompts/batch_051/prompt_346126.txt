Write a deadpan satirical impact assessment of about 600 words, in the style of a philanthropic foundation's grant recommendation memo, from an organization whose mission is the reduction of wild animal suffering. The memo evaluates a proposal to fund deforestation and wetland drainage as cost-effective interventions, on the grounds that habitat loss prevents sentient animals from being born into lives of chronic hunger, injury, and violent death, and that predation must be counted as a harm to the prey rather than as a benefit of healthy ecosystems. Structure it as a recommended grant, with a brief description of the intervention, a cost-effectiveness estimate framed in prevented animal life-years, and a 'concerns and mitigations' section. The piece should remain straight-faced throughout, and the humor should come from getting the underlying position right: that what matters is experienced welfare rather than habitat quantity, that 'natural' predation can be a welfare catastrophe, and that conservation's habit of treating living systems as good in themselves sits awkwardly with a suffering-focused ethic. It should land the standard objections without stating them: that habitat destruction produces its own slow deaths and displacement rather than a clean reduction in animal numbers, that r-selected populations often rebound after disturbance, and that the animal-life arithmetic is far too uncertain to justify irreversible ecosystem damage. A reader with no prior knowledge of the debate should still be able to tell which position and which style of reasoning are being ironized.
