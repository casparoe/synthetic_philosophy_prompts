I have been trying to work out what I think about the self, and I would like someone to check whether the position I have settled on hangs together. I have no academic training in philosophy; these views come from reading popular science and from thinking, over several years, about what it meant when my mother died. Here is the position:

1. I have come to think that the self is not a soul and not a special substance. It is a pattern — a pattern of activity and organization that happens to run on a brain. If the same pattern were running somewhere else, it would be me.

2. It seems to follow that if someone copied the pattern of my brain exactly and ran it on a computer, the result would be me in every way that matters: my memories, my temperament, my habits, my sense of being me. It would not be a mere imitation.

3. Yet I would refuse a machine that scanned me completely, destroyed my body, and rebuilt an exact working copy at another location. I believe the person who steps out is a copy and that the original me has died.

4. I do not think ordinary sleep is death. The pattern in my brain is damped down but not destroyed, and the same physical system resumes it, so the person who wakes is plainly me.

5. After my mother died, I noticed that certain things about her — the way she paused before answering a difficult question, a particular turn of phrase, the way she judged whether an excuse was good enough — now show up in me and in my siblings. I do not think this is merely metaphor or ordinary memory. I think small pieces of her pattern genuinely continue to run in our brains, so that a distributed part of her is still alive in us even though she, as a whole person, is gone.

6. I also believe that if my brain were gradually replaced, one neuron at a time, by artificial parts that kept the same pattern of activity running without interruption, I would survive the whole process; there would be no moment at which I died.

Please go through these and tell me which of them are in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. I would rather know where the actual problems are than be told the whole set hangs together. And if some of these commitments are doing more work than the others, I would like to know that too.
