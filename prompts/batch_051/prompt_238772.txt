I'm a novelist, and I've been rereading Borges's The Library of Babel for a story I'm drafting and I cant stop thinking about it next to the actual-infinity stuff—Cantor, Hilbert's hotel, the finitist refusal to treat a never-ending series as a completed whole. The library is built from a fixed alphabet and fixed book length, so the number of books is finite, just stupidly large; but the story talks about it as if it's total, endless, and somehow already there all at once. I want a reading of the story through that lens. Does Borges actually depict a completed infinity in Cantor's sense, or just a very large finite totality, and does the difference matter for what the story is trying to show? Where does the story genuinely capture something about actual infinities, and where does it trade on a confusion a strong finitist would pounce on? I dont so much care whether Borges made a math error as whether the experience of living inside a completed totality—finite or infinite—adds something to the debate that Hilbert's hotel walks past.
