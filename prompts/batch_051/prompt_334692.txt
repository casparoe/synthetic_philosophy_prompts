I put my credence in that claim at roughly 0.2; my colleague puts hers at roughly 0.75. I do not want a neutral survey of the debate. I want your own number and the derivation underneath it.

Please assign rough probabilities to the sub-questions that carry the weight, at least these: whether human mathematical cognition is algorithmic at all; whether, granting it is algorithmic, it can be identified with a single fixed, consistent, recursively axiomatized system rather than a shifting, self-modifying, or inconsistent one; whether the step from Gödel's first incompleteness theorem to the principle that a human who accepts the soundness of such a system can recognize a truth it cannot prove is valid, or instead presupposes an idealized picture of what humans can actually grasp; whether an algorithmic system could match human mathematical output through probabilistic, approximate, or otherwise non-deductive means without ever proving the relevant Gödel sentence; and whether a demonstrated gap between humans and any fixed formal system would mark a difference in kind or merely show that human mathematical competence is an unstable, idealized description. Attach a number and a one-line justification to each, show how they combine into your overall credence, then identify the single sub-question that most plausibly explains the gap between 0.2 and 0.75, and state one concrete mathematical or empirical result that would move your own estimate most.
