ive been trying to work out what makes something one living thing instead of a bunch of living things, and I cant tell whether I have a real rule or just a feeling. Here are my gut calls on some cases that came up.

A single E. coli bacterium: one living thing, obviously.

A Portuguese man o war. Everyone thinks its a jellyfish, but its actually a colony of specialized little pieces (the float, the stinging tentacles, the feeding parts, the reproductive parts) all budded from one egg, so theyre clones. I still want to call it one animal. It looks like one, acts like one, and the pieces cant make it on their own. The word "colony" just feels like a technicality to me.

An ant colony: not one living thing. Even though it has specialized castes, keeps its nest warm, forages, fights, and reproduces as a whole. I just cant do it. I know that makes my man o war call look shaky, because an ant colony also acts like one thing. The only difference I can point to is that the man o wars pieces are physically stuck together and the ants can walk away.

A lichen: I know its two different species, a fungus and an alga or cyanobacterium, but the alga lives inside the fungus, and the lichen grows and reproduces as one package. I'd call it one living thing.

A cow with its rumen bacteria: no. The cow is one living thing; the bacteria are separate, even though without them the cow would starve on its normal grass diet. This bothers me because it sounds a lot like the lichen, two species that need each other, but I give it the opposite answer.

A human with all the microbes on their skin and in their gut: no. Those microbes are tenants, not part of me, even if they affect my digestion, immunity, maybe even mood. I just dont want to count my gut bacteria as me.

A big aspen grove where every trunk above ground is a clone and theyre all connected by one underground root system: yes, the whole grove is one living thing, even though above ground it looks like a forest of seperate trees.

Some slime molds: normally theyre lone amoebas crawling around eating bacteria, but when food runs out they stream together into a slug that crawls as one blob, then builds a stalk and makes spores. The slug can include cells from different parents. While its crawling as a slug I'd call it one living thing, even though an hour ago it was thousands of separate living things, and after it makes spores each spore is its own thing again.

So: is there one rule or test that gives me all of these answers? If there is, state it precisely and show how it handles each case. If there isnt, tell me which of my calls I should change and what the cleanest rule is that keeps the rest. Also tell me what your rule predicts for a case I didnt list. And if my real rule is just "looks like one thing from here," say that too.
