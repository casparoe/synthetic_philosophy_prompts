A nine-member standing panel at a national medicines regulator decides by simple majority vote whether new vaccines may be approved; each decision is binary, approve or refuse. As a baseline, assume that every panel member votes correctly on any given application with probability 0.60, and that their votes are statistically independent of one another.

Rank the following five proposed reforms from the one that would do the most to improve the panel's expected probability of reaching the correct collective decision, to the one that would do the least to improve it (or would reduce it). Justify the ranking using the Condorcet jury theorem, and for each proposal state which of the theorem's assumptions it preserves, strengthens, or undermines. You need not compute exact probabilities, but do indicate where the supplied numbers matter and where a qualitative argument suffices.

The proposals are:

(a) Raise every member's individual accuracy from 0.60 to 0.65 through a training program, leaving the panel's size, voting rule, and independence unchanged.

(b) Enlarge the panel from nine to fifteen members by adding six new members drawn from the same recruitment pool, each with individual accuracy 0.60 and with votes independent of one another and of the original members.

(c) Enlarge the panel from nine to fifteen members by adding six new members who each have individual accuracy 0.60 but whose judgments are heavily correlated with one another, because they were trained in a single methodological school and rely on the same analytical pipeline.

(d) Introduce structured deliberation before each vote, expected to raise each member's individual accuracy to 0.65 while making their errors moderately positively correlated.

(e) Replace the simple majority rule with a two-thirds supermajority requirement, on the ground that a false approval is substantially more costly than a false refusal.

Where the classical theorem cannot settle a proposal, say what further information would be needed and under what conditions the proposal would improve collective accuracy.
