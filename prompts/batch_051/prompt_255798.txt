I'm an undergrad in a course on bounded rationality and I've gotten wierdly into satisficing vs. maximizing. For a term project I'm trying to collect every genuinely distinct argument that satisficing is rational — not just psychologically realistic, but actually rationally defensible. So far I've got these, in roughly the words I understand them:

(1) Search costs: looking until you find the optimal option costs time and effort, so once that cost is counted, the satisficing stopping rule is really the maximizing strategy for the larger problem of deciding how long to decide.
(2) Portfolio: effort spent optimizing one decision is effort you can't spend on the rest of your life, so satisficing the small stuff leaves more for the big stuff and wins on the whole portfolio even if each individual choice comes out worse.
(3) Robustness: optimal options are often brittle because they're fitted to one model of the world, one set of probabilities, one set of weights; a good-enough option can be less sensitive to model error, so under uncertainty about your own model satisficing beats maximizing across the nearby scenarios.
(4) Incommensurability: if the options are only partially ordered or there's no single best because values are plural, "maximize" has no defined target, and the sensible requirement is to pick something above a threshold.
(5) Regress: deciding how long to deliberate would itself require deciding how long to deliberate about that, and so on, so the maximizing demand can't even get started and bottoms out in a satisficing pick.
(6) Ecological/adaptive: in realistic environments, simple satisficing heuristics often outperform exhaustive optimization, because optimization overfits or costs too much; evolution seems to have built satisficers rather than maximizers.

I keep feeling like there's a seventh argument I can't get into words — one that's less pragmatic, less "thinking is expensive and the world is noisy," and more about what rationality itself consists in. Am I missing anything, or is that just a vibe?
