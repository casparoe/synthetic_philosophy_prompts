I keep running into a move in arguments about whether god exists and I cant shake it. The move goes like this: in a criminal trial you dont start from "guilty," you start from "not guilty," and the prosecution has to prove its case. By the same logic, the default position on god is atheism — just not believing — and the religious person has the burden of proof. My friend, who's an atheist, uses this to say that when a theist can't produce evidence the discussion is basically over, and he's not being unfair or dogmatic by leaving the conversation there; he's just applying normal standards of evidence.

I started trying to actually line the two situations up and I can already see it isn't a clean fit. In a trial the jury has to reach some verdict either way. Saying "not guilty" doesn't mean the jury thinks the person is innocent, it might just mean "not proven." The rule about the prosecution having the burden exists partly because the state has all the resources and power and we'd rather let guilty people go than lock up innocent ones. And in a trial you're deciding what to do to a person, not just trying to be correct. But I can't tell how much of that actually damages the argument versus just being interesting differences that don't matter. An agnostic, for instance, seems like they could use the exact same courtroom line to say the default is just "not proven yet," not "there's no god."

Walk me through how far this courtroom analogy actually holds. I want: what maps onto what (a table is fine), the places it breaks, which breaks actually matter for the conclusion versus which are cosmetic, and whether the conclusion — that the rational default is atheism and the burden is always on the theist — survives once the disanalogies are out on the table. Also tell me whether there's a better analogy for this debate, or whether the whole burden-of-proof framing is itself doing more work than it should be.

Also, please seperate what's settled among people who actually work on this stuff from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as if it's the obvious consensus.
