A parallel has been nagging me and I would like a philosophical verdict on it. In ecosystem forecasting we learned to distinguish parameter uncertainty from model inadequacy: a model can be extremely well-calibrated on short-horizon data and still go badly wrong further out, not because the fitted coefficients are noisy, but because unmodeled feedbacks, thresholds, and rare transitions branch the possible trajectories faster than any dataset can discipline them. Reading discussions of consequentialist cluelessness, I thought I recognized exactly this structure. An expected-value treatment of consequences does serviceable work for nearby, separable effects, just as a forecast model does for the short-term state variables it actually represents, and it comes apart precisely where long-horizon branching and neglected interactions dominate; the further future is not merely harder to estimate but unavailable to estimate in any principled way. I would like to know whether this is a genuine identity of epistemic structure or only an attractive metaphor.
