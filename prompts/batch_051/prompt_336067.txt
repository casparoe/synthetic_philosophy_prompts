I'm putting together a parody piece for the joke issue of a science-ed journal, and I want it to be funny to learning-sciences people but also to anyone who remembers ninth-grade physics. Write me a deadpan, roughly 600-word reviewer report on a manuscript submitted to a made-up journal, Cognitive Physics Review. The reviewer should evaluate it using the exact criteria that make misconceptions theoretically interesting in the learning sciences: coherence across contexts, systematicity, generative predictions, resistance to instruction. The twist should be that, judged by those criteria, the manuscript is excellent—maybe even stronger then Newtonian mechanics in classroom studies—and the reviewer slowly realizes this makes the manuscript a better example of a beautiful theory than of a correct one. The humor has to come from the actual features of misconception research (kids don't make random errors; their systematic errors are theory-like and survive instruction), not from the physics being wrong. Stay in reviewer voice the whole time, and don't break character to explain the joke.
