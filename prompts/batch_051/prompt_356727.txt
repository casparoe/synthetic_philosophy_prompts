I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about when inequality matters, by checking my gut reactions to concrete cases.

- A $500 monthly supplement to a single parent struggling to make rent, versus the same to a lawyer who is already comfortable: if I can fund only one, the supplement to the single parent clearly does more good.
- A small tax increase on high earners to fund free school meals: I call that an improvement overall.
- A pay award that makes everyone in a firm better off but gives the top earners five times the gain of the lowest earners: I still call it an improvement, but the widening gap genuinely bothers me.
- A policy that makes the best-off worse off while leaving everyone else exactly as they were, so the gap narrows but no one gains: I do not think that is an improvement in any respect, and I don't think it's good just because there is less inequality.
- A world in which everyone is at a modest but equal level, versus a world with the same modest level for most people and a much higher level for a fortunate few: I cannot settle this one.

My tentative principle is that equal gains to the worse off count for more than equal gains to the better off, while inequality has no value in itself — only effects on people do. State the sharpest version of this principle, run it against all five cases, and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. Then tell me what would change your mind: which of my verdicts are you most inclined to reject, or what consideration would push you to think equality matters for its own sake? Lead with the principle, and keep the response under 450 words.
