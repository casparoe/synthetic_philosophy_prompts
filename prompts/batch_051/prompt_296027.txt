I am a PhD candidate in philosophical theology, writing a paper on the conceptual and moral viability of penal substitutionary accounts of the atonement. For the critical section I have assembled what I take to be the standard objections. They are: (1) the trinitarian objection, that penal substitution sets the Father against the Son and makes the Father's demand for the death of an innocent into the governing image of God; (2) the forgiveness-extinction objection, that if a punishment-debt is paid in full by a substitute, nothing remains for God to forgive, so the doctrine cannot make sense of forgiveness as gratuitous remission; (3) the non-transferability objection, that guilt and desert attach to agents and cannot be discharged by punishing an innocent third party, so vicarious penal substitution rests on a legal fiction; (4) the proportionality objection, that if sin against an infinite God merits an infinite penalty, no finite death can pay it, while if the Father accepts a finite substitution, the alleged requirement was never genuinely proportional; (5) the exegetical objection, that the doctrine reads early modern penal-juridical categories back into texts whose working idiom is cultic, covenantal, and participatory; and (6) the moral-influence objection, that a doctrine which requires comprehending a retributive transaction before it can move the hearer is self-defeating as a means of changing the hearer's affections. I want objections that go beyond this list. Please give exactly five further objections to penal substitution, none of them a rewording, special case, or combination of the six above; in particular, do not give me another way of saying that God has been made cruel, that forgiveness is extinguished, that guilt is nontransferable, that the arithmetic of infinite desert is incoherent, that the exegesis is anachronistic, or that a penal transaction cannot be communicated to the unpersuaded. For each of the five, state the objection in one or two sentences and include a clause indicating whether you think it succeeds on its own terms or merely relocates the difficulty. Present the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English.
