For my philosophy of artificial intelligence seminar, I am trying to get clear on what a theory of artificial suffering would actually have to achieve before I read the usual proposals about whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer — in particular, the proposal that reward signals should be read as valence. Please act as a specialist in the philosophy of mind and the foundations of reinforcement learning, think through the question carefully, and answer in British English.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of whether a reinforcement learning agent can suffer must meet. By suffering I mean a negatively valenced state of the sort that could matter for welfare, not merely a low score, a negative scalar in an objective function, or a disposition to avoid some state.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are only preferences of particular camps (for example, substrate-independence, functionalism about valence, the view that only systems that learn can suffer, or the view that self-report is required). I also want to know whether any subset of the requirements looks jointly unsatisfiable, and if so why; that is the part I most want to see. Conclude by identifying the single requirement that does the most work in ruling out candidate theories.

Make the list concrete enough that it handles at least the following cases: a temporal-difference learning agent that receives a negative reward-prediction error whenever it loses points, where nothing more is going on than a variable in an update rule; a fixed-policy system with a utility function but no learning mechanism at all; a wireheaded agent that maximises reward by directly stimulating its own reward channel; an agent with all the functional markers of pain (avoidance, learning, graded response) but for which we have no reason to posit phenomenal feel; and a biological organism with nociception but no capacity for self-report.

Do not collapse the following distinctions: valence from intensity; occurrent suffering from a standing liability to suffer; suffering from moral status; reward from reward-prediction error; and a negative value in an objective function from a genuinely negative felt state.

Start by brainstorming candidate requirements before you settle on the final list. If you think the term ‘suffering’ needs to be replaced or sharpened for the theory to be coherent, say how, but do not use that as an excuse to avoid giving the desiderata. I am not asking for a literature review or citations, so please do not pad the answer with them.
