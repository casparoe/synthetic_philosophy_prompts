"The Condorcet jury theorem says that if a group of voters chooses between two options, and each voter independently has probability p > 0.5 of choosing the correct option, then the probability that a majority chooses the correct option is greater than p, and this probability tends to 1 as the number of voters increases. First, the theorem can fail through informational interdependence. If 1,000 voters all follow the same news source, and that source is correct 60% of the time, then each voter has a 60% chance of being right, but the majority's chance of being right is still only 60%, because the voters are effectively one person repeated 1,000 times rather than 1,000 independent signals. Second, strategic voting can also defeat the theorem. If voters care about being on the winning side or about party discipline, they may vote against their private judgment. A committee majority can then adopt the option that fewer members privately believe is correct, so the votes no longer aggregate the members' information. Thus the theorem shows that deliberation among voters with independent private information will lead the group to the correct answer."
