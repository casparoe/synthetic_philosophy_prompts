I am a casualty actuary, and the mental instrument I reach for first is the one I was trained on: a hazard becomes a probability distribution over losses, and a policy question becomes expected losses, tail risk, and cost per statistical life saved. Lately I have been trying to follow the less technical discussion of how much risk a society should tolerate — where to draw the line in environmental and workplace safety, how to think about rare but catastrophic failures, how far governments should go to reduce the risk of terrorism, and whether some risks should be refused even when the expected numbers come out in their favor. The positions in that discussion seem to run from thoroughgoing cost-benefit balancing, through precautionary stances that single out uncertain, irreversible, or catastrophic harms for special treatment, to views that treat the involuntary imposition of a serious risk as a wrong in itself even when the aggregate expected harm is small.

I would like you to translate that whole debate into the vocabulary I actually use: expected loss and expected fatalities, the value of a statistical life, tail risk and risk measures, risk pooling and risk appetite, portfolio-level diversification, and the distinction between well-characterized risk and deep uncertainty. I am fluent in all of that, so do not explain the tools to me. Go through the positions one at a time and restate each as faithfully as the vocabulary allows, without quietly importing the assumptions of my framework in place of theirs.

Then give me the residue report: which of their claims survive translation intact, which can be expressed only by stretching my concepts, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For each piece of residue, say whether it points to something a risk-based or actuarial perspective is structurally unable to see — a genuinely moral or political dimension it was not built to represent — or whether it marks a place where the public debate is doing less work than it appears and is leaning on sentiment rather than on a principle. I want the translation to be honest enough that I can tell the difference between the two.
