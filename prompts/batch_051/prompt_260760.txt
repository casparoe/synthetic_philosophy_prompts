My opening challenge: In chapter XIV of his Second Treatise of Government, John Locke describes prerogative as the power to act according to discretion for the public good, without the prescription of the law and sometimes even against it. He also says that when a controversy arises about whether prerogative was used rightly, there is no judge on earth between the executive and the people, so the people must ultimately judge for themselves. Here is my objection: this seems to give no answer at all to the question of who governs during a genuine interregnum or constitutional gap. If positive law is silent and only the people’s retrospective judgment can settle whether a prerogative act was legitimate, then at the decisive moment the executive is acting without present warrant, and the gap is simply filled by de facto power until, perhaps, a later popular judgment retroactively blesses or condemns it. That looks less like a constitutional doctrine of gaps and more like licensed illegality with a delayed referendum by force. How do you answer that from within your Lockean position?
