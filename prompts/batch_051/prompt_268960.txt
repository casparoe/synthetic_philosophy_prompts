I keep seeing 'strategy stealing' waved around but I've never seen the proof written out carefully, so here's my attempt for Hex (players take turns claiming cells, each trying to connect their two opposite sides), and I don't trust it.

(1) Suppose the second player has a winning strategy.
(2) The first player steals it: make some arbitrary first move, then on every later turn pretend to be the second player and follow that strategy, and whenever the strategy tells her to claim a cell she already occupies, she claims any other free cell instead of skipping.
(3) Since in Hex having an extra cell already claimed can never make your position worse, this stolen strategy still guarantees the first player a win.
(4) So if the second player has a winning strategy, the first player has one too.
(5) But two players cannot both have winning strategies in the same two-player no-draw game.
(6) Therefore the second player has no winning strategy, and since the game is finite and has no draws, the first player has a winning strategy.

I keep going back and forth between two spots. Step (3) looks like it's smuggling in a monotonicity assumption that's false in some games (zugzwang, where being forced to move is bad). But a friend says (3) is fine as a stipulation about Hex, and the real quiet step is (6). Which step fails, for which classes of games, and is the argument actually valid as a proof for Hex?
