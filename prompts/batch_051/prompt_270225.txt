In my work in formal epistemology, I keep running into a particular claim about rational disagreement. The starting point is a convergence result: when agents with different priors but shared likelihoods conditionalize on a growing stream of shared evidence, their posterior opinions eventually come arbitrarily close, so the priors are said to "wash out." Many colleagues then infer that persistent, sincere disagreement among well-informed people is best explained by non-Bayesian updating, unshared evidence, or motivated reasoning—not by different starting assumptions. That inference is usually stated as if it were already established. I want to know how one could actually test it. Design the most probative empirical or experimental test you can of the claim, state explicitly what results would count for it and what would count against, and then say exactly what the test could never settle even in principle.
