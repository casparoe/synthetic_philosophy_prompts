I recently started volunteering with a group that has moved nearly all of its direct action online, and the questions this raises are no longer academic for me. The standard liberal account of civil disobedience — the Rawlsian one — treats it as a public, nonviolent, conscientious breach of law whose point is to appeal to the community's sense of justice; what makes it compatible with a broadly democratic order is precisely its publicity, its communicative intent, its nonviolence, its sincerity, and the willingness of those who engage in it to accept the legal consequences. But the actions we now debate often look quite different from that picture: a distributed denial-of-service action that temporarily takes down an agency website; a coordinated flood of automated comments on a regulatory docket; a swarm of accounts that occupies an online forum. These actions are planned through encrypted channels, carried out by software rather than by physically present bodies, and designed so that no one has to step forward to be arrested — and sometimes no one publicly claims them at all.

My question is whether the standard justification survives this development, needs repair, or is effectively dead for these cases. I see the possibilities as follows. The argument might carry over intact if these are just the same public, communicative, nonviolent practice in a new medium. It might survive only if its requirements are reinterpreted — perhaps an action can count as communicative because a statement is issued on its behalf, nonviolence can be understood as the absence of physical harm, and accepting arrest is not essential. Or these actions might fail exactly the conditions that did the normative work, in which case they are not civil disobedience in the relevant sense and would need a different kind of justification altogether. I want you to take a definite position rather than surveying these options: identify the premise of the standard account that this development tests most directly, and tell me whether the argument can be restored in another form, and if so, which form is least costly and why.

Please commit to one answer and argue for it. Write in American English.
