I chair my university's Academic Policy Committee. The Philosophy Department has proposed that students in its compulsory second-year course, Practical Ethics, be allowed unrestricted use of general-purpose large language models while writing the major take-home essay, on two conditions: each student submits a one-page statement of how they used the tool, and each then sits a fifteen-minute oral examination in which they must explain and defend the essay's argument under questioning. The department argues that this mirrors the conditions under which graduates will actually work, and that a prohibition would be unenforceable because detection is unreliable and adversarial. A committee bloc opposes the proposal on the ground that it institutionalizes epistemic dependence: the course exists to certify that students can form, articulate, and revise normative judgments of their own, and the proposal would let someone be certified on the strength of an argument they never constructed and may not understand, with the oral examination testing only whether they can absorb and repeat a generated line of reasoning. The underlying disagreement is about what the credential certifies: a capacity to produce and defend a position, or a relation of understanding between the student and the material. I need to circulate a short written opinion before the Faculty Senate votes next month. Please write the committee's memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with amended conditions. The memo should be something the Senate could act on, with a concrete bottom line and a time frame, and it should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly rather than caricaturing it. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary; the memo's first sentence should state the recommendation, and the rest should give the reasoning.
