Last night me and my friend got into a three hour argument about whether someone born in Germany in 1995 inherits any guilt for the Holocaust, and he kept pulling out a Jaspers-style four way distinction between kinds of guilt. Criminal guilt: individuals committing crimes, something for courts. Political guilt: every citizen of a state bears some of it for what the state does, just by being a member, because they benefit from it and can vote. Moral guilt: what I personally did or failed to do. Metaphysical guilt: he described it as a kind of bottomless co-responsibility you have just by being human — if other people are doing wrong and you don't put yourself on the line to stop it, you're implicated even when you couldn't have stopped it.

I work in insurance — reinsurance and liability — so my brain kept trying to hammer his four kinds into the vocabulary I actually trust: fault-based liability, strict liability, the assigned risk pool, subrogation, statutes of limitations, whether a liability is transferable or inheritable, tail risk, moral hazard, and whether you can put a number on a loss at all. It wouldn't go cleanly, and thats what started bugging me.

So here's the task. Restate the four kinds of guilt in that liability-and-insurance vocabulary as faithfully as you can: which are strict, which are fault-based, which expire, which can be subrogated or transferred, which have a counterparty, which would be uninsurable, and so on. Then give me the residue report — which parts map cleanly, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts don't map at all. In particular: does metaphysical guilt fail to map because my vocabulary is too thin to say something real, or because it isnt a liability-like thing in the first place? And for the actual argument: is inherited collective guilt more like an assigned risk pool — you're in it because you're a member of the pool and you pay whether or not you caused the loss — or more like a debt an estate inherits and can eventually discharge? Or is neither, and my friend's distinction is doing work my vocabulary cant see?
