For more than a decade, a debate has been running through computational linguistics, natural language processing, and the philosophy of language about what vector-space word embeddings actually show us about meaning. On one side are people who read the geometry of embedding spaces as semantic structure in a fairly literal sense: similar words cluster, distances correspond to degrees of similarity, directions encode relations, and the familiar observation that one can add and subtract vectors to complete analogies is taken to show that the vector space has recovered something like a coordinate system for concepts. On the other side are people who think these patterns are mostly artifacts of how the models are built and evaluated. They point out that antonyms and other non-synonymous associates regularly turn up as nearest neighbors, that analogy performance rises or falls depending on corpus, preprocessing, and choice of test items, and that whatever embeddings encode is a graded record of co-occurrence rather than something capable of composing into sentence meanings, distinguishing necessary from accidental features, or being true or false. The two sides have been trading the same demonstrations and counterexamples for years, and nobody seems to move.

I am not asking you to decide which side is right. I am asking for a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. Is this a verbal dispute about the word 'meaning'? Is it a substantive dispute about facts that evidence could in principle settle? Is it a difference about what should count as semantic evidence in the first place, or about whether strong performance on a benchmark amounts to a theory of meaning? Are the two sides answering different questions under one heading, say, the engineering question of what representation is useful for prediction versus the philosophical question of what linguistic meaning is? Give me the diagnosis that best fits the actual structure of the impasse, and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a distinction that dissolves the disagreement, an experimental protocol, a formal criterion, a change of question, or a reason to think no progress should be expected.
