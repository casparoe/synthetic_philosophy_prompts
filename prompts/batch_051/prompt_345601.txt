I've come to accept the central bet of iterated amplification and distillation: alignment and competence survive decomposition. A task too hard for a human overseer to evaluate as a whole can be broken into subtasks each of which is easier to evaluate than the whole; if each subtask is checked by a competent overseer who can lean on the currently distilled model, the resulting tree of queries can stay faithful to the original task even though no single human ever held the whole thing in view. Distillation then just amortizes that tree into a fast policy; on my view it adds no new goals, and it preserves alignment by construction rather than by luck. The alignment lives in the decomposition, competence comes from the recursion, distillation preserves both.

Give me the map of what would count against this. I'm not asking for the standard list of objections; I want defeaters ranked by how decisive they'd be, from fatal to merely awkward. Which possible observations, experiments, or formal results would most move a committed IDA proponent? Which assumptions does the view actually risk—answer-invariance under decomposition, overseer honesty and competence at every node, the absence of correlated blind spots across queries, the assumption that distillation can recover everything relevant from behavior—and which of those failure modes are live rather than merely logically possible? Say what this view predicts that its rivals don't, and what would distinguish it empirically from a skeptic who thinks any decomposition is a reinterpretation. What should a defender concede counts against them, and what may they dismiss as an engineering problem rather than a counterexample? End with an honest verdict on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is a problem for this kind of view. The evidence I'd personally find most persuasive would be a clean case in which a seemingly faithful decomposition demonstrably produces a policy that optimizes something else—not because a single subtask was misspecified, but through some recomposition failure—so weigh that especially carefully.
