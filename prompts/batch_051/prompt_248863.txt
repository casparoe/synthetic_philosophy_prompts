The word "unpredictable" seems to run together several distinct things: determinism, sensitivity to imperfect measurement, absence of a usable model, and plain ignorance. I would like a definition that separates them. Test it against these cases: a solar eclipse visible from a named city on a given date fifty years from now; the exact configuration of a double pendulum ten minutes after release, when its equations of motion are deterministic and chaotic but its initial conditions were measured only to finite precision; the next value of a pseudorandom number generator whose algorithm and seed you possess; the face a fair coin shows just before it lands, given the most complete measurements physically attainable; the time at which a particular radioactive atom decays; whether a river with a century of gauge records floods this spring; the closing price of a stock one year out; and a weather forecast from the same atmosphere model at two days versus two weeks. I take the eclipse to be predictable and the two-week forecast to be unpredictable; the rest I find genuinely unsettled. Offer a definition of "unpredictable" or of degrees of predictability, apply it to each case, and then say which verdicts you would defend and which the definition forced on you.
