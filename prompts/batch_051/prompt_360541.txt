I keep returning to a structural parallel and I can't decide whether it is deep or merely formal. The scale argument in discussions of insect sentience runs roughly as follows: we are uncertain whether insects have conscious experience, but even a modest credence in their sentience, multiplied by the trillions of insects affected by agriculture, pesticides, and land use, yields an enormous expected quantity of suffering or foregone welfare — enough to swamp many moral priorities we currently treat as serious. This looks, shape for shape, like Pascal's Wager: even a small positive probability that God exists, multiplied by an infinite reward and punishment, yields an infinite expected value, so the expected payoff of a life organized around that possibility swamps every finite consideration. In both cases a low probability is kept from being negligible by an enormous magnitude, and the conclusion is that our ordinary priorities are irrational.

But the two arguments have very different reputations. Pascal's Wager is widely discussed as a pathology of expected-value reasoning — the infinite utilities and the many-gods objection are standard criticisms — while the scale argument is treated in much recent work as a serious moral consideration. I want to know whether the difference in treatment tracks a real structural difference or just a difference in prestige.

Where exactly does the analogy hold, and where does it snap? Is the finite-but-astronomical magnitude of insect suffering enough to avoid the problems that infinite utility creates, such as non-Archimedean preferences or the impossibility of saying how low the probability can go before the expected value stops dominating? Does the scale argument have its own version of the many-gods problem: if consistency forces me to assign some small credence to sentience in bacteria, plants, or even microphysical panpsychism, do the vastly larger numbers at those levels reactivate the same hijacking? What would have to be true of the insect case for the Pascalian objection to do more than caution against point-estimate arithmetic? And name at least one thing the parallel would lead me to expect about the insect case that turns out false or misleading.
