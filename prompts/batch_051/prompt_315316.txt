Build me a thought experiment where a regularity in a group counts as a social norm on a Bicchieri-style account but is not a convention on Lewis's account. That's the awkward direction — most stock examples go the other way, with a convention nobody feels any oughtness about (like which side of the trail people pass on). Constraints: keep it ordinary (an office or an apartment building, nothing exotic), one paragraph for the scenario, and the norm can't be an obviously stupid rule; the divergence should come from the structure of the situation, not from the agents being irrational. For the norm side, use a Bicchieri-style test: people follow it because they have (i) empirical expectations that enough others follow it, and (ii) normative expectations — they think enough others think they ought to follow it and may sanction them if they don't. For the convention side, use Lewis's rough conditions: everyone conforms, everyone expects everyone else to conform, everyone prefers to conform on condition that the others conform because it's a coordination equilibrium, and there's an alternative regularity that would have worked just as well. After the scenario, check each condition one by one and show why the norm account says yes and Lewis says no. Then say which structural feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you remove it, and how a committed Lewisian would push back and try to make the case come out as a convention anyway. Also tell me, in a sentence, whether this regularity plausibly evolved out of an earlier convention or started out as something else.
