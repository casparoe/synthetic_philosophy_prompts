I am a materials scientist, not a philosopher. My working vocabulary is drawn from continuum mechanics and the solid-state side of physics and chemistry: stress and strain tensors, contact forces and traction, elastic versus plastic response, interatomic potentials and force fields, electron density, Coulomb and exchange repulsion, the Pauli exclusion principle, binding energy, vacancies and dislocations, microstructure versus bulk constitutive behavior, and structure-property relations. Those terms are native to me, and I will not need them explained. I know the ancient atomists only through the crude one-liner that Democritus and Epicurus thought the world is tiny hard billiard balls moving in empty space. I want a translation that is more honest than that.

A colleague has given me the following one-paragraph summary of the atomist account of matter, and I ask you to treat it as the target of the translation rather than correct it as history. Bodies are compounds of atoms and void. Atoms are indivisible, partless corporeal units. Atoms are impenetrable and perfectly solid, containing no internal void, so they can push one another or rebound but cannot be broken, squeezed, or merged. All interaction among atoms is by contact. The void must be real because motion requires somewhere for a body to move into. Atoms differ only in shape, size, position, arrangement, and motion. The perceptible qualities of a compound, such as color, taste, temperature, and macroscopic hardness, are not properties of its atoms but arise from their arrangement and motions.

Please restate this package in my vocabulary, element by element. For each element, say what the atomists would be asserting if they were forced to speak in my terms, and say where the mapping is forced rather than faithful. Do not tell me that the atomists anticipated modern band theory or density functional theory, and do not smuggle my assumptions into their position and then call the result a translation.

Then give me the residue report. Separate the package into three classes: what translates cleanly; what translates only under distortion, with the distortion named; and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For the second and third classes, render a verdict in each case: is the residue there because the materials-science vocabulary is missing a real conceptual option, or because the atomist claim was less clear than it looked once someone pressed on it? In particular, say what becomes of impenetrability and solidity once they are no longer primitive, and what becomes of the void once I bring in my usual picture of empty space. I want to know whether my vocabulary dissolves the atomist distinctions or genuinely explains them.

Write the answer in American English. No citations or reading list are needed; I want the conceptual mapping only.
