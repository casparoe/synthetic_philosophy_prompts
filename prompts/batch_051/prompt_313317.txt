This is going to sound like rules-lawyering, and maybe it is, but Ive got a genuine question about terminal vs. instrumental value and I'd like a second opinion before I bring it to the board. I run a small thinking-society (founded 2012) whose charter says, in Article 3: "The Society exists solely to improve the judgment of its members. Every tool, metric, document, and procedure of the Society is a means to that end. A member who treats any such means as an end in itself—by granting it authority independent of its usefulness for improving judgment—has broken faith with the Society, whatever their formal standing."

That clause was written against things like one founder's blog becoming an unchallenged canon and the yearly member ranking turning into a status contest. Nothing like the current problem: two years ago we set up an internal LLM we call the Digest, which reads every thread and outputs a short summary plus an "epistemic status" rating for each post. One member—call him D—has gotten extremely into it. He will not make a decision about grad school, moving, or even a breakup without running it through the Digest. He settles arguments by citing its ratings ("the Digest gives your objection a 2.7, so I've updated maybe five percent") and defends its summaries as more reliable than the members themselves. A few people have started calling him a Digest-worshipper, and someone taped a sticky note to his monitor that just says "IDOLATRY?" with an arrow pointing at the screen.

I raised Article 3 with him. His reply: he is not treating the Digest as an end; its just extremely useful for improving his judgment, so by the plain words of the clause he is fine. And now I'm stuck, because I can see at least four readings that pull in different directions. The words, read literal, arguably let him say "it's useful, hence allowed." The purpose of the clause seems aimed straight at this pattern. The 2012 founders, if asked, would never have imagined an internal LLM but would probably recognize the pattern and say it counts. A reasonable new member reading the charter today might think the Digest is exactly the sort of tool the clause licenses, or exactly the sort of thing it forbids, depending on how much of D's behavior they have seen. Does Article 3 apply to D, and which reading should govern—the text, the purpose, what the founders would say if asked, or what a reasonable member takes it to mean? And if they don't converge, is there a deeper problem with trying to enforce that kind of clause as a written rule at all?
