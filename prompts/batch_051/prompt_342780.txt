Consider Curry's paradox. Take any sentence P you like — say, "0=1" — and define C as the sentence "If C is true, then P." With the Tarskian biconditional (a sentence is true just in case what it says is the case) and classical logic reading the conditional materially, one appears able to derive P with no further premises about the world.

Now consider a variation intended to reproduce Curry's paradox in the style of Yablo's liar — that is, without any sentence referring to itself. Let S_1, S_2, S_3, … be an infinite sequence of sentences, where for each n, S_n is the sentence:

"If every later sentence in the sequence is true, then P."

So S_n says: if S_{n+1}, S_{n+2}, S_{n+3}, … are all true, then P. No sentence mentions itself, and because each sentence refers only to strictly later sentences, there are no finite cycles of reference either.

Work through how much of Curry's paradox survives this transformation. Assume classical two-valued semantics, apply the Tarskian biconditional to each S_n, and read the conditional materially.

(1) Show carefully whether the infinite sequence, together with the T-scheme, forces P in the way ordinary Curry does. Give the full truth-value assignment argument.

(2) Identify the exact place where the argument uses the fact that the sequence is infinite. What happens if the sequence is cut off at some finite N, with S_N replaced by an ordinary ground sentence (say, "1=1", or P itself)? Show whether the finite chain still forces P.

(3) Compare this variant with both standard Curry and Yablo's liar. Which standard responses to Curry — for example, abandoning the material conditional, rejecting contraction or absorption in a substructural setting, going paracomplete or paraconsistent, or restricting the T-scheme — transfer more or less naturally to this version, and which structural feature of the modified case does the work?

(4) Finally, assess whether the variant genuinely avoids circularity, or whether the self-reference has merely been displaced into the global definition of the infinite sequence itself. Does your answer affect what the paradox should be taken to show?
