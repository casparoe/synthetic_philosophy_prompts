Me and my brother keep going around on taste, basically whether saying "this movie is just better than that one" means anything beyond "I happened to like it more." His line is that once you account for genre and what a person usually enjoys, it's all preference, so calling someone's taste worse is just snobbery. My line is that there's something real there, at least in the clear cases: if someone has seen a ton of horror movies and still thinks a mess with a nonsensical plot and flat characters is as good as a tightly made one, they aren't just having a different preference, they're kind of wrong, the way you can be wrong about whether one argument is stronger than another.

So here's what I want your number on. The claim is: for many pairs of works in the same medium and broad genre, one really is better than the other, in the sense that people who've spent serious time with that kind of thing, pay close attention, and aren't trying to be contrarian will tend to converge in their verdict, and we're justified in treating that convergence as tracking something in the works rather than just a clique of taste. Give me your credence in that claim, between 0 and 1, and decompose it. Specifically I want to know how much rides on: (a) whether works have aesthetic properties like being clumsy or well-paced independent of any particular viewer, (b) whether more exposure actually makes judgments converge rather than just making people more confident in their own preferences, (c) whether the disagreement that remains among experienced viewers is mostly bias and noise or genuine differences in what they're after, and (d) whether convergence could come from shared human wiring and shared training instead of from anything in the works themselves, and whether that would even matter for the claim. Give each a rough probability and say how you combined them, and tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to. For reference, mine is around 0.65 and my brother's is around 0.2, and I'd like to know which of those sub-questions we're probably really disagreeing about even if neither of us has put it in those words.
