I am a third-year undergraduate currently taking a historiography course, and we have been arguing over whether the standard Western divisions — the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, the Scientific Revolution — track real seams in the historical record or are retrospective labels imposed on a continuous past. The concept I keep reaching for, and cannot state cleanly, is that of a genuine historical period.

I would like a working definition of a historical period: something that lets one say of a stretch of time whether it is a real unit or break, as opposed to a conventional box. Propose that definition and run it through each of the following cases. I am fairly confident the Middle Ages is not a genuine period: it looks to me like a gap invented by people who needed something for a rebirth to emerge from. I am fairly confident the Industrial Revolution is a genuine period, even though I cannot say exactly where it begins or ends. The Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution strike me as largely later constructions, though I suspect a defensible core of the second survives. I have no settled view about the Cold War, the Anthropocene (treated as a historical unit rather than a geological one), the Axial Age (the proposed roughly simultaneous turn toward universalizing thought in the middle of the first millennium BCE), or Late Antiquity (the proposed long transition between classical antiquity and the Middle Ages).

Tell me where the definition gives a verdict you would want to resist, whether you would revise the definition or defend that verdict, and whether the cases end up requiring different senses of period — in which case say whether those senses can be ordered, or whether the term is doing several jobs at once and should be replaced by finer vocabulary.
