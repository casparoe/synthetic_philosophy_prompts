I'm on my hospital's clinical ethics committee and this case has split us right down the middle, mostly over whether we're allowed to expect any consistency from a patient across time. Ive been asked to send round a short written opinion before we meet on Thursday, and I want you to draft it.

The patient, Ms G, is 54, with a slow-growing cancer that doesn't need emergency treatment but does need a decision in the next few weeks. Three options are all medically reasonable: a long-term daily drug, a course of radiotherapy, or surgery. She's been assessed for capacity four times by two different clinicians, and each time she's been found able to understand, retain and weigh the information and communicate a choice.

The problem is that her choices over the last nine weeks form a loop. Offered the drug or the radiotherapy, she chose the drug, firmly, saying she couldn't face the radiotherapy suite. Offered radiotherapy or surgery, she chose radiotherapy, saying surgery terrified her. Offered surgery or the drug, she chose surgery, saying she was sick of the drug's side effects. Each choice was made with apparent understanding, but when the team pointed out she'd chosen differently a fortnight earlier she either denied saying it or said she'd simply changed her mind.

She's now consented to surgery and is booked in, but the team can see the pattern: whichever pair she's offered, she picks whichever one lets her avoid the treatment that looms largest at that moment. Acting on each pairwise choice as it comes has already cost her repeated pre-op assessments, cancelled slots and a lot of anxiety. Half the committee says the rule is simple: she has capacity, so the choice she makes at the time is the one we respect, full stop. The other half says a person whose choices go round in a circle like this isn't having her autonomy respected when we keep acting on each fresh choice; we're just running a money pump, and each turn of it costs her something real without getting closer to a treatment that reflects any values she actually holds.

Please draft the opinion. It should come down on one side and say exactly what we should do and by when, and it should give the strongest version of the other side a proper run rather than setting up a strawman. The options on the table are roughly: go ahead with the surgery as she has now consented; pause everything and do another capacity assessment with the cycling itself front and centre; or hold off treatment until she can state a stable ranking and then follow that.
