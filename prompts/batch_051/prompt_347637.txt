Act as a philosopher of psychiatry who works on diagnostic classification and has read Hacking on looping effects. I sit on a clinical standards panel, and I'm trying to work out what principle our own track record actually commits us to before we vote on a new diagnosis. I'd rather not just follow my gut, so think carefully: start by brainstorming the candidate principles, then narrow down.

Our past decisions:

- We struck homosexuality from the list, and later deleted a residual diagnosis for distress about one's sexuality, on the ground that the category itself seemed to be doing the harm.
- We classified ADHD as a disorder even though its boundaries are fuzzy and diagnosis rates vary hugely by country, because the associated impairment was substantial.
- We refused to classify burnout as a disorder. We accepted that it can be as impairing as mild depression, but said the causal problem is the working environment, not a failure inside the person.
- We added prolonged grief disorder, despite objections that it pathologises bereavement, reasoning that the grief had become self-sustaining and no longer tracked the original loss.
- We put internet gaming disorder only in a research appendix rather than the main list, saying the evidence for a distinct internal dysfunction wasn't there yet.

The new proposal is "climate anxiety disorder," for cases where dread and preoccupation with ecological breakdown is severe, has lasted over a year, and seriously impairs work and relationships, even though the person's read on the climate situation is broadly accurate and proportionate.

If the aim is consistency with our own precedents, what should I vote? Say which of our earlier decisions is the closest analog, what principle we'd be adopting with each possible vote, and what single new case would best settle which principle we actually hold.
