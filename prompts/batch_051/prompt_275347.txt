I am preparing a graduate seminar on the history of adaptationism, and I want to build one session around a controlled counterfactual encounter. In 1979, Stephen Jay Gould and Richard Lewontin published a broad critique of what they took to be the ruling style of evolutionary explanation: the habit of treating every conspicuous trait as if natural selection had shaped it for its present function, and of resting content once a plausible adaptive story has been told. They argued that this habit screens out alternative explanations—developmental constraint, architectural necessity, correlated responses to selection acting elsewhere, drift, and historical inertia—and they pressed the architectural analogy of the spandrels of San Marco, structural by-products that were later decorated but never selected for the use they now display.

Now imagine Darwin could read that critique. The question I want the seminar to pursue is not whether he would accept modern evolutionary theory, but something finer: what, specifically, would he endorse in the spandrels critique, and where would he resist it? On one side, Darwin often sounds like exactly the pluralist the paper says evolutionary biology needs: he disclaims the idea that every difference between species is useful, he invokes the correlation of growth, use and disuse, and other non-selective causes, and he treats imperfections and rudimentary organs as positive evidence rather than embarrassment. On the other side, he founded the very explanatory practice under attack, and his books are saturated with adaptive conjectures; he also insisted that natural selection is the main, though not the exclusive, means of modification. He might further object that the spandrels analogy runs origin and current utility together, since a by-product can later be co-opted for a new use.

Reconstruct, as realistically as the evidence about Darwin's actual commitments allows, the reply he would most likely have made. Where would he agree with Gould and Lewontin, where would he accuse them of overstatement or of attacking a caricature, and how would he restate the proper division of explanatory labor in his own terms? Is the historical Darwin best classified as an ally of their pluralism, as a target who happened to carry pluralist caveats, or as someone their dichotomy fails to capture? Argue for a definite placement, and indicate which parts of your reconstruction are well grounded in his views and which are speculative projection.
