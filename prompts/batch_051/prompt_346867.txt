I'm currently taking a seminar called Sovereignty and Political Integration. Our professor has kept a caseboard all term: a numbered list of hypothetical arrangements and her one-line ruling on each. She has never stated a general criterion, and for the final exercise we are meant to predict her ruling on a new case using only the caseboard. I'd like to work through the reasoning in advance.

The recorded rulings are these:

1. A free-trade area: the states abolish tariffs between themselves but keep their own external tariffs, and create only a secretariat to monitor compliance. Her ruling: no transfer of sovereignty; the states remain fully sovereign.

2. A customs union: a common external tariff (a single tariff applied to outside goods at every member's borders), set by a ministerial council that must decide unanimously; each state may withdraw unilaterally on one year's notice; there is no court. Her ruling: no transfer of sovereignty; the states remain sovereign.

3. A customs union: the common external tariff is set by a ministerial council acting by qualified majority, so a member can be outvoted; there is no court; but the treaty has no unilateral withdrawal clause, so a state can leave only with the unanimous consent of the other members. Her ruling: transfer of sovereignty over trade policy.

4. A common market: a legislative council acting by qualified majority, free movement, and a court whose rulings take direct effect inside the member states; withdrawal is unilateral. Her ruling: transfer of sovereignty.

5. A confederation: a joint defense council that can act only by unanimity, no court, each state retaining its full international personality and a unilateral right to withdraw. Her ruling: no transfer; the states remain separate sovereigns, and no new sovereign state has been created.

The new case is this. The Vordane customs union has a common external tariff set by a ministerial council acting by qualified majority. There is no court, no legislature, and no direct effect; the tariff is implemented by each member's own customs administration. Any member may withdraw unilaterally after giving twelve months' notice. The union's only binding majority decisions are its periodic votes fixing the external tariff.

Please work through: (1) what principle or principles make all five recorded rulings consistent; (2) what each such principle predicts about whether a Vordane member has transferred sovereignty over trade policy; (3) which earlier ruling is the closest analogue under each principle; (4) your best-guess prediction of her ruling, with your reasons for preferring the principle behind it; and (5) if the caseboard genuinely underdetermines her verdict here, specify one further hypothetical that would force a clean choice between the rival principles.
