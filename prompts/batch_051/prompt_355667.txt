I am a retired natural scientist, recently arrived in philosophy, and I would like an honest assessment of a concept I have been calling the deadline quotient.

In the Great Filter discussion as I have encountered it, the road from abiotic chemistry to an observable technological civilization is treated as a series of independent gates—abiogenesis, complex cells, multicellularity, intelligence, technology, and so on—and the question is which gate is nearly closed, that is, which transition has the lowest probability. I suspect this is the wrong quantity. A transition can have an extremely low probability per year per lineage and still occur early if it has an astronomical number of parallel trials; a modest transition can do the real filtering if it typically arrives only after the window for everything downstream has closed. So I propose ranking transitions by the ratio of the expected waiting time for the transition, given that all earlier stages have occurred, to the length of the window during which the transition can still do its work before later stages become impossible. I call this ratio the transition's deadline pressure, or DQ. The Great Filter would then be located at the transition with the highest DQ, and if every DQ sits comfortably below one, there is no single filter at all: the galaxy's apparent emptiness would be the accumulated product of many individually unsurprising delays.

The reason this seems to me more than a notational change is that it shifts which transitions are the live candidates. Chemical difficulty and waiting time can diverge. On Earth, the appearance of life from non-life looks from the record to have been geologically fast, while some later biological transitions consumed a very large share of the remaining clock even though they were less chemically astonishing. I suspect the effective filter may sit in that long biological middle—eukaryogenesis, oxygenic photosynthesis, or the acquisition of cumulative culture—rather than in abiogenesis or in some future catastrophe.

I would be grateful for a candid appraisal. Is this a genuine distinction, or does it collapse into an ordinary probability model once rates and population sizes are specified? Is the DQ well-defined, or does it smuggle in a clock that selection has no access to? Has this already been worked out under another name? And am I right that it scatters the filter across many steps rather than concentrating it in one?
