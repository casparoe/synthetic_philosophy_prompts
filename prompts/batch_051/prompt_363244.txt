I've been going in circles on this. Say a man sees two people drowning, his wife and a stranger. He saves his wife. Now imagine the same man, before jumping in, stops and thinks, okay, I'm allowed to save her because she's my wife. Most people I've talked to feel the second man is somehow worse — that the extra thought is a flaw, one thought too many. But I can't tell whether that feeling tracks something real and testable, like the pause showing he loves his wife less or turns even his closest relationships into a calculation, or whether it's just a moral judgment about the act itself that no experiment could get at. Is there any way to test it? Like, give people the two versions of the story and see if they systematically judge the pauser as colder or less admirable, or change the wording of the justification and see if the reaction shifts. Set out the strongest test you can, say what result would count for each reading, and be honest about what the test would leave unsettled even if the result came out clean.
