I'm a retired biochemist — forty years in the lab — and this fall I got roped into teaching a continuing-ed seminar on weakness of will and temporal discounting. Its my first time grading philosophy rather than lab reports, and I'd like a second pair of eyes. Here's the midterm question I set:

"Behavioral economists sometimes model impulsiveness with hyperbolic discounting, where a delayed reward loses value by a factor like 1/(1 + kD) instead of by a constant exponential factor. Hyperbolic discounting allows a smaller sooner reward to outrank a larger later one as it comes near, even when the ranking was the other way around earlier. Does this model explain weakness of will (akrasia), or does it just re-describe the cases? If it does explain them, what does that explanation show about whether the weak-willed choice is rational at the moment it is made?"

And here is one student's answer, typed out as submitted:

"Hyperbolic discounting does genuinely explain weakness of will, and it improves on the old philosophical description. Akrasia is standardly defined as acting against your own judgment about what is best. The hyperbolic model gives the mechanism. Under exponential discounting, a person's ranking of a smaller sooner reward and a larger later reward is stable: if the bigger later reward is preferred now, it is preferred all along. Under hyperbolic discounting the curve is steeper for short delays, so the two curves can cross. In January I can prefer $50 on April 1 to $40 on March 1, but on February 28 I prefer the $40 due tomorrow. That reversal is the engine behind akrasia. Once we see the crossing, though, the supposed irrationality disappears. At the moment she chooses the $40, it is genuinely worth more to her by her own discount function, so she is doing what she most values, and hence what she judges best, at the time of action. The only conflict is between her earlier and later self, not within the choice itself. And since reversals of this kind are stable across the population, they are not a glitch but the normal way humans discount the future; calling such a stable pattern irrational as a matter of principle would be a stretch."
