You are a professional philosopher who works in metaphysics and mereology, and who also has real experience designing active-learning classroom simulations. Think carefully and from first principles. Before committing to a design, brainstorm at least two candidate mechanics for the activity described below, then briefly say why you chose the one you develop in full.

I teach an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar: 16 students, one 75-minute session, mostly philosophy majors but not yet familiar with the technical literature on material composition. Design a runnable in-class activity that makes them feel the force of the special composition question — the question of when several things compose, or make up, a further single thing — before they ever hear that label. They should not receive the standard menu of answers (nihilism, universalism, restrictive views such as contact or fastening, organicism) until the debrief. The activity should be arranged so that, through their own rulings and the pressure to rule consistently, participants construct a general principle and then find the next case breaking it; the frustration of revising the principle, or the strangeness of biting the bullet, should be where the philosophical force shows up. The facilitator must not be the source of the tension.

Please supply: (a) the rules, roles, and materials, with any stakes not requiring real money; (b) a structured sequence of cases that moves from easy to genuinely uncomfortable rulings, including at least one case involving a living thing; (c) a timeline in minutes for the full session; (d) a debrief script that maps moments in the activity onto the standard positions on composition and shows what each position costs its advocate; (e) the predictable ways this goes wrong the first time it is run (for example, groups treating it as purely a matter of word choice, or refusing to commit to any rule) and how to handle each one in the moment; and (f) an honest note about which feature of the philosophical terrain the activity cannot reproduce and what the facilitator must therefore supply afterward.
