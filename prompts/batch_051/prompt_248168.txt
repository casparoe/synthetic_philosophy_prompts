I've been stuck on this for weeks and I want a definite verdict, not a map of the options. I've been vegan for about six years, and in a city with cheap beans and tofu the standard case feels basically settled: the animal cost of my eating animal products is real, and the cost to me of avoiding them is small. I'm now spending a few months with my partner's family in a coastal town in the Philippines, and the cheap, storage-friendly proteins are the fish his uncle catches and the eggs from hens the family keeps anyway. Dried beans and lentils exist but they're imported, they cost several times what the fish does, and the only explicitly vegan protein options in the store are expat-priced mock meats sealed in layers of plastic. There's no reliable refrigeration, so tofu and leftovers spoil quickly in the heat; when I do stay vegan I'm mostly eating rice, greens, and fruit, and by the third week I'm weak and light-headed. The family eats together, and refusing the uncle's fish reads as snubbing the main thing he brought to the table.

I know how this sounds. It sounds like the guy back home who swears he can't go vegan because of cheese, and I know my mind is going to grab for whatever principle lets me off the hook. I've tried to model the counterfactual in the usual consumer-choice way: if I refuse the fish, does the uncle actually catch fewer fish tomorrow, or does it just become leftovers? If I skip the eggs, the hens are still there, and the egg just gets fed back to them or given to a neighbor. The neat story where my money creates extra demand is alot murkier here than in a supermarket.

So the question is whether, in this situation, eating the local fish and the family's eggs is actually defensible, or whether it's the same rationalization with a passport. If you think circumstances like this genuinely change the verdict, state the principle sharply enough that I could tell genuinely constrained apart from special pleading. If you think they don't, tell me to hold the line and live with being hungry and rude. Either way, pick a side and defend it; don't just lay out the considerations and leave me to choose.
