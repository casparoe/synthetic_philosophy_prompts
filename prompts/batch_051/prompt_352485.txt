I'm torn between a theory I find quite compelling and an intuition I can't shift. The theory is roughly that valence—pleasure and pain, liking and suffering—just is the computational role of a reward signal: it's what marks states as to-be-approached or to-be-avoided and drives credit assignment and learning. On that view, a deep RL agent given rewards and penalties already has the rudiments of valenced states. But when I actually picture a Q-learning agent getting +1 for reaching a coin, I find it just obvious that nothing is felt; it's a score, not a felt pull. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of 'reward' on which both survive—say, that only certain kinds of architecture or self-modelling turn a signal into valence. Walk me through what each option costs and tell me which you'd give up, or whether the conflict is only apparent.
