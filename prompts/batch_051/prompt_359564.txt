Ive been going back and forth on whether deplatforming is best understood as punishment, and I want to work through a single case properly rather than just repeat talking points. Take Alex Jones's deplatforming in August 2018. By that point he'd spent years claiming the Sandy Hook shooting was fake and the families were crisis actors, on top of a lot of other stuff; within a few days Apple, Facebook, YouTube and Spotify all pulled his content or accounts, and he was left mostly with his own website and app. I know the facts, so don't waste space summarizing. What I want is a disciplined analysis of the episode through the philosophy of punishment. Treat the platforms as at least functionally punishing agents: what exactly were they doing — retribution, deterrence, incapacitation, expressivist condemnation, some mix? What would each theory say they should have done, and which one best rationalizes or criticizes what actually happened? Then say where the analogy to legal punishment strains. The platforms aren't the state, there was no trial or consistent published standard, Jones had no real appeal, and the harm fell partly on employees and maybe on other deplatformed people who never did anything remotely comparable. After that, run the argument the other way: does the Jones case suggest deplatforming is better modeled as a business refusing service, or does it show that punishment theory needs to be stretched to cover private, centralized platforms in ways Bentham or whoever never imagined? A friend of mine keeps telling me it's just a bar kicking out a belligerent drunk, and im not sure whether he's missing the point or puncturing the whole analogy. I want the mapping to be tight — who is the punisher, what is the offense, what counts as the sanction — and I want you to say where the theory is silent instead of forcing it to talk.
