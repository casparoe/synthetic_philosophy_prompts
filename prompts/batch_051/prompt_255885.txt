Okay so my friend Kai has this game where I give him a scenario and he tells me whether the person in it did something fine or not fine, specifically about doing something risky to other people without asking them first. He's weirdly consistant about it, so I started keeping a list of his calls. Here are the ones I have so far:

1. A guy sets off fireworks in a cul-de-sac a couple houses down from him on the 4th of July. The chance of starting a roof fire at any one house is small, but if one started it could be really bad, and he easily could've knocked on a few doors first. Kai says not fine.

2. A school nurse gives the flu spray to a whole kindergarten class at the start of flu season. For two kids, the parents never signed the permission form and she can't get ahold of them that morning, so she goes ahead anyway. Side effects are rare and mild, and all the kids in the class benefit because fewer of them get the flu. Kai says fine.

3. A food truck parks outside an office park and sells pastries with nuts in them without putting up any allergen sign. For the small share of customers with a serious nut allergy, the risk is really bad if something goes wrong, and the truck could easily post a sign or ask at the window. Kai says not fine.

4. A delivery driver speeds through a residential street late at night with urgently needed medicine for the neighborhood clinic. The added risk to anyone walking around at that hour is tiny, and there's no way to ask everyone along the route seperately in time. The people on the street are, in a loose way, the same people the clinic serves. Kai says fine.

So now the new one. A startup cuts a deal with the town council to test delivery robots on the sidewalks in my neighborhood for six months. Residents were never asked directly. The chance that any individual person gets bonked by one over the whole test is something like 1 in 400,000, though if an old person or little kid took a fall it could still be serious. The robots would only carry packages for a few local businesses, so most residents get no real benefit from them.

Walk me through it like this: what are the candidate rules that fit his old calls, what does each one say about the robot case, and which of my four cases is the closest analog on each reading. Then give me your best guess about what Kai would actually say, with your reasons. If the old cases genuinely underdetermine it, just own that: say what rule each answer would commit him to, and give me one or two more scenarios I could run by him that would cleanly settle which rule he's actually running on. And if I'm setting this up wrong somehow — like if wanting someone's gut calls about risk to be consistent is a confused thing to want, or four cases is too thin to count as a track record — say that instead of giving me a confident-sounding answer.
