I am the lead systems designer for a persistent-world game with a player-run economy. Two months ago a duplication exploit minted roughly forty percent of the server's total gold before we patched it, and the cleanup argument forced me to read macroeconomics seriously for the first time in my life. I am fluent in the vocabulary of virtual economies — faucets, sinks, gold supply, velocity, posted prices and auction-house lag, crafted gear and raw commodities, bind-on-pickup goods as nontradable real wealth — and I have only a hobbyist's grasp of macro.

Please restate the debate about what "printing money" creates in my game-economy vocabulary, then tell me what did not survive the translation. Take the three positions I have been reading about, in the loose form I understand them. (1) The quantity-theoretic/monetarist line: money is largely a veil; issuing more of it creates inflation and transfers purchasing power, not new real wealth. (2) The Keynesian/liquidity line: when an economy has idle resources, newly created money can call those resources into use, so it can create real output for a while, at least until resources tighten. (3) The endogenous-money/chartalist family of views: most money enters existence as bank credit or government spending rather than as a fixed stock being printed, and the binding limit on creating it is real resources and inflation rather than the issuer's ability to find the gold.

Restate each of the three in my vocabulary, one by one. Use the incident as the running example where it helps: early exploiters bought crafted gear before prices moved, starter-zone players ended up with better equipment as the duped gold passed through low-level markets, and auction-house prices adjusted with a lag before settling at a new, higher nominal level. Then give the residue report: for each position, say what translates cleanly, what you had to distort to fit, and what has no counterpart in a virtual economy at all. End by answering the question I actually care about. My working hunch is that "money is a veil, printing creates inflation and transfers" maps cleanly onto my world, and that the other two positions are mostly about institutional details a game economy lacks; but I want you to stress-test that hunch rather than flatter it. Are the residues a sign that my vocabulary is missing something essential about money, or a sign that part of the macro dispute is less substantive than it looks? Keep the reply to no more than 900 words, with one subsection per position and a final subsection for the residue report.
