Suppose a person is destructively emulated: their brain is scanned and destroyed, while a simulation is constructed that reproduces the functional behavior of every neuron and synapse at a resolution sufficient to preserve all information-processing regularities. Please give me your credence that the resulting emulation is the same person — that the procedure counts as survival of the original mind rather than the creation of a duplicate. I don't want "it depends on your theory at each step"; I want a number and the derivation. Decompose at least into these sub-questions, with a rough probability and a one-line justification for each: (a) a system with the same functional or computational organization as a human brain would be phenomenally conscious at all; (b) conditional on (a), neuron-and-synapse-level emulation captures whichever organizational features are decisive rather than requiring molecular, glial, or field-level detail; (c) personal survival is a matter of psychological continuity rather than biological continuity; and (d) the preservation of an abstract causal pattern, rather than the continuous operation of the original physical process, is sufficient for personal survival. Show how the terms combine, including the main correlations, identify which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, and say what single empirical result or argument would move that sub-question most.
