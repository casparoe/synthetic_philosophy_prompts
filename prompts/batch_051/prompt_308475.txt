I sit on a regional board that reviews serious incidents involving automated vehicles, and I'm honestly stuck on the case we vote on next Tuesday. A Level 4 delivery van (no driver, and the remote monitor only sees fleet alerts, so nobody could take over in the moment) ran a red light and hit a cyclist who had the signal. The operator says its perception system misclassified the cyclist as a shopping cart in the glare; the model vendor says it was a rare lighting case their validation set never covered; the city says that intersection was flagged as risky, but the company choose the route anyway. Our rules force a choice: every incident gets attributed to a responsible party, or it gets declared a 'no-fault automation failure.' Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. Recommend which finding we should adopt, and if you attribute responsibility, name the party and say what corrective action we should require within 60 days; if you go no-fault, tell us what we should say to the public about blame when nobody in the chain looks like they fell below a reasonable standard. Either way, engage the strongest argument for the outcome you don't pick before you reject it.
