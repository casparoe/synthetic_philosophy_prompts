I'm in a historiography seminar and we just spent a week on whig history, and I've got a borderline case I can't settle. The criteria my professor gave for calling a piece of historical writing whiggish were roughly these: (1) it organizes itself around a present-day category or institution and selects its material by how relevant it is to that present thing; (2) it plots the past as a trajectory of development toward the present — a 'how we got here' story; (3) it praises past figures for anticipating present ideas and blames others for obstructing them, instead of taking them on their own terms; and (4) it has a congratulatory or triumphalist tone, so the present comes out as the rational endpoint and defeated positions look like mere errors caused by the backwardness of the time.

The case is a hypothetical book a collegue told me about, The Making of Prediabetes: A Prehistory of Insulin Resistance. The author reconstructs nineteenth-century medical arguments about early signs of diabetes. She is scrupulous about not treating old clinicians as if they were groping toward prediabetes: she does not praise anyone for anticipating the modern category or blame anyone for missing it, and she works hard to reconstruct the categories those doctors actually used. Her whole polemic, in fact, is that prediabetes is a messy and somewhat arbitrary construct built out of diagnostic thresholds, insurance risk, and pharmaceutical marketing — she is not congratulating the present at all. But she admits up front that she chose her materials by starting from the present category and working backwards, and the chapters are arranged as a continuous story of how the pieces that would later get assembled into prediabetes came together. So she has a 'how we got here' structure without a 'we were right to get here' verdict.

Is this still whig history? It seems to hit criterion (1) squarely and maybe half of (2), but it deliberately inverts (3) and (4). Is present-centered selection by itself enough to make history whiggish, even when the author's stated aim is to deflate the present category? Or does the directional structure itself smuggle in a whiggish telos no matter what the author intends? Run through the criteria one by one, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and tell me whether the case shows the criteria themselves need changing. End with a last line of the form Verdict: whig, Verdict: not whig, or Verdict: criteria need revising.
