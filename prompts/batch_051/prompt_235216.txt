I had this long argument with a friend about whether karma is basically just temporal credit assignment from reinforcement learning, and he eventually wore me down but I'm still not convinced he's right. Here's the parallel. In reinforcement learning, when a payoff arrives long after a bunch of decisions, the algorithm has to decide which earlier actions get the credit or blame, and it usually discounts the far-past ones so old actions count less. Karma looks like the same structure in moral language: your present circumstances are the delayed payoff, and the theory assigns that payoff to deeds from earlier in your life or previous lives, with old deeds somehow ripening or fading on a schedule of their own. So the mapping would be deeds = actions, circumstances = delayed reward, karmic accounting = credit assignment, and the decay of old karmic results = an eligibility trace. My friend says this is just pattern-matching, because credit assignment is a heuristic for improving a policy and has nothing to do with desert, and the discount rate is a free parameter rather than a rule about what anyone deserves. I cant tell whether that objection kills the analogy or is actually the most interesting part of it. Can you work out the mapping carefully and say where it holds and where it comes apart? And what's one thing the parallel would predict that turns out false once you look at how karma doctrines actually work.
