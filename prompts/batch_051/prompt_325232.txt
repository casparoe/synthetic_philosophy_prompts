In my graduate seminar on Locke's theory of property, I want to set aside the exegetical question of what Locke himself meant and examine an institution that attempts to operationalize his pairing of the labour theory with the sufficiency proviso. Suppose a community of a hundred or so adults holds its surrounding land as an unowned commons of uneven quality: rich bottomland, thin wooded slopes, and stony upland. On the first day of each month, any adult who currently holds no claim may file a claim to one parcel. To perfect the claim, the claimant must complete and register a visible improvement — clearing, fencing, planting, terracing, or cutting a drainage channel — within one week. Before the claim vests, the Recorder must certify that the taking leaves the remaining commons as ample and as good for every other member as the parcel was in its unimproved state. To make this certification, the Recorder holds a sealed-bid auction, using scrip issued equally to every adult each month, for one-year leases over all parcels that would remain in the commons; the claim is granted only if the Recorder's pre-improvement appraisal of the claimed parcel is no higher than the average winning lease bid for the parcels that would remain. Lease scrip is destroyed. If a claimant leaves an improvement visibly neglected for a full year, the claim lapses and the parcel returns to the commons. The Recorder cannot claim land while in office, is paid a fixed salary, and may not be overruled.

I would like you to treat this not as a policy proposal to be endorsed or dismissed, but as a mechanism whose behaviour needs to be worked out. Walk through what it incentivizes at each point: which parcels get claimed first, and by whom; how the auction and its scrip constraint would be gamed by claimants, prospective leaseholders, and speculators; how the lapse rule would be manipulated; and what the Recorder, despite the fixed salary and tenure protections, would be pressured or tempted to do. Then assess whether the scheme's likely failure modes can be patched without gutting its Lockean core, and whether the patches would introduce new failure modes of their own.
