You are advising a small foundation that can fund about six person-years of conceptual work, by philosophers and mathematically trained alignment researchers rather than ML engineers, on whether "avoid side effects" can be made precise without a full specification of human values. The live proposals pull in different directions: penalizing changes in the agent's ability to achieve a broad distribution of possible goals; measuring relative reachability in a state-transition graph; penalizing deviations from an inaction baseline; requiring impact to be reversible or spatially localized; and treating side effects as interventions on variables outside a task specification. A recurring worry is that any choice of goal distribution, inaction baseline, or task boundary already smuggles in a value judgment. Which two or three of these directions should the foundation prioritize over the next three years, and which should it defer? Defend the ordering rather than merely listing it: for each direction, say what it is upstream or downstream of, how tractable it is with philosophical and formal tools alone, whether a satisfactory answer would actually change how low-impact agents are specified or evaluated, and what a negative result would teach. Finally, state what formal or empirical development in the next two years would most change your ranking.
