i keep coming back to the fix-versus-analyse debate in conceptual engineering and i've got a little apparatus i want stress-tested. i call it load-bearing senses. the idea is that a concept isn't just a label, its doing several jobs at once—tracking something out in the world, letting people coordinate (contracts, laws, measurements), and often quietly making some social arrangement feel natural or inevitable. now, some of those jobs are load-bearing: people's plans or institutions depend on the concept staying roughly as it is, so if you yank it, the load has to go somewhere first. my rule is that you can only ameliorate a concept in the parts that aren't load-bearing; if the part you want to fix *is* load-bearing, you have to relocate the load first (change the law, the metric, the ceremony), and then fix the concept. that's why, on my view, demoting pluto went down easy—nothing downstream much cared whether we had nine planets or eight—but changing 'marriage' to include same-sex couples was a huge fight, because the load-bearing bit (who inherits, who gets the visa, who's next of kin) had to be re-done across thousands of places. i know this is adjacent to the descriptive-versus-ameliorative distinction, and maybe to the idea that some concepts just track a real kind while others are more like tools. but i genuinely want to know: is the load-bearing criterion doing any work, or is it just a dressed-up version of 'change the cheap stuff first'? and does it break when the load-bearing uses are exactly the harmful ones—say a slur, or a concept whose whole job is to make a hierarchy look natural, where waiting to relocate the load feels like leaving the harm in place? give me the strongest version of my view, the nearest existing idea, and the case where my triage gives the wrong advice.
