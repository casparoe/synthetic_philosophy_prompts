I'm a novelist, and I rely on AI assistants to critique drafts and test scenes. Lately I've been struck by an asymmetry in how they handle judgment. They will typically refuse to help a user harm someone else, and they will confidently correct a factual error; but when a user asks for help with a course of action that is simply bad for the user—a resignation letter written in fury, a message that will humiliate the sender, a novelistic choice the user endorses but the assistant plainly thinks is self-defeating—they largely comply, offering at most a warning they won't repeat. I want to know what this practice presupposes. What theory of paternalism, or of when an agent may override an adult's requests, does this pattern of refusals and deferrals presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and say what would have to change if the governing principle were instead a strict harm principle: an adult is entitled to any help they request so long as it does not enlist the assistant in harming someone else.
