I'm a longtime LW/EA-adjacent reader putting together a one-page proposal for a six-month research fellowship, and I want to pitch something on the how-do-values-combine cluster rather than yet another attempt at estimating some intervention's cost-effectiveness. The default story in utilitarian and EA circles seems to be additive: total welfare is the sum of welfare across individuals, and an individual's welfare is the sum (or integral) of momentary wellbeing over time. But the same people, often in the same argument, reach for two non-additive structures. Sometimes they go lexical, saying no amount of the lower-priority good can make up for losing the higher-priority one, the way people talk about rights or about blocking the repugnant conclusion with a quality floor. And sometimes they go multiplicative, the consciousness-times-happiness idea where the value of an experience is more like a product of two dimensions (valence, and how much of a subject is lit up by it), so that if either factor is zero the whole term is zero and the two dimensions don't substitute at a constant rate the wya an additive model would imply. The old torture-vs-dust-specks debate feels like it lives somewhere in this same tangle.

My half-formed suspicion is that these aren't three rival accounts of one combining function; they're answers to at least three different questions that keep getting conflated: how dimensions combine inside a single experience, how experiences or moments combine within a life over time, and how lives combine across a population. If that's right, the defensible view might be a hybrid — multiplicative within an experience, additive across lives, and something lexical only as a structural side-constraint — but as stated that's the kind of gerrymandered free-parameter story that deserves to be called ad hoc. So the actual project would have to supply a criterion for when each operation applies, and show the criterion isn't itself just another adjustable knob. The live options as I see them are single-rule views (always additive, always multiplicative, always lexical) versus some kind of level-sensitive hybrid versus a deflationary conclusion that combining functions are just modeling choices with no normative structure. Can you turn this into a one-page proposal? State the question so it has a determinate answer, lay out the possible conclusions and which considerations decide between them, describe the method (I'm imagining careful case-based analysis plus simple toy models, not data collection), say what a useful deliverable looks like for people actually doing cause prioritization — e.g., guidance on when a cost-effectiveness model should multiply by a probability-of-sentience term rather than adding a discounted welfare term, or when a lexical floor is justified rather than a refusal to shut up and multiply — and give an honest account of what would count as failure. Don't bother with citations or a lit review; I'll fill those in.
