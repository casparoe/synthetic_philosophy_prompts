I want a working definition of concept possession—what it is for a creature or system to have a concept such as DOG, SEVEN, or PLATYPUS—that gets the following cases right. Prototype, exemplar, and theory-theoretic accounts are competing attempts to supply exactly such a definition, but I want the definition itself, tested against the cases rather than a verdict as to which school is right.

(1) An adult who recognizes a dog of a breed she has never seen before, distinguishes dogs from wolves and cats, accepts that dogs are animals and cannot also be reptiles, and can say something coherent about what she is going on. [I take this to be clear possession.]

(2) A sorting machine that routes photographs labeled "dog" and "not dog" with near-perfect accuracy, but has no capacity to answer whether a dog is an animal, to expect a dog to bark, or to recognize a crude drawing or a dog in disguise. [I take this to be clearly not possession.]

(3) A two-year-old who uses "dog" for the family dog, the neighbor's cat, and a sheep, never for birds or trucks, and explains that dogs are "fuzzy." [No settled view.]

(4) A person with intact visual identification but degraded encyclopedic knowledge, who can still point to the dog, say "dog," and sort dog from cat photographs, but cannot say whether dogs are animals, whether a dog could be an insect, or what "dog" means. [No settled view.]

(5) A novice who has been told only that a platypus is an egg-laying Australian mammal and can repeat that, but cannot recognize a platypus in life or in a picture and cannot say whether a platypus has a beak. [No settled view.]

(6) A child who can count seven objects, says that seven comes after six and before eight, and uses "seven" correctly in simple arithmetic, but seems to have no image, exemplar, or prototype associated with "seven" and cannot say what seven "looks like." [I take this to be clear possession, and I would distrust a definition that excludes it.]

(7) A border collie who retrieves "the ball" across balls of many sizes and materials and ignores non-balls, generalizing to novel ones, but who cannot say what a ball is and shows no sign of using BALL in any reasoning beyond the retrieval routine. [No settled view.]

I am confident about (1), (2), and (6), and genuinely unsure about (3), (4), (5), and (7). Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. In doing so, say where the definition is most vulnerable and which of my starting verdicts, if any, it forces me to abandon.
