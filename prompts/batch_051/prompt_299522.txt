I've been trying to get clear in my own head about when I actually blame someone for what they did, and I keep making up little situtations to test my gut. My verdicts so far:

- A driver runs a red light on purpose because she's late. Secretly her brakes have been disabled, so even if she'd tried to stop, the car would have gone through anyway. I blame her fully.
- A cashier empties the register with a gun to his head. I don't blame him.
- A man steals from his sister because of an irresistable craving, hates himself for it, says it feels like a physical force. I blame him much less, probably not at all.
- A woman with severe OCD scrubs her hands bloody, knows she should stop, wants to stop, can't. I don't blame her.
- A hypnotized guy barks at a stranger because of a post-hypnotic suggestion, but it feels to him like he just decided to. I don't really blame him.
- A neuroscientist can predict exactly what a man will choose from his brain activity, but does nothing to interfere. I still blame the man.

The rule I thought I believed was that you can only be responsible if you could have done otherwise. But my first case seems to wreck that, and now I can't tell what I actually think. Is there one principle that gets all or most of these? What would it say about a guy being blackmailed into doing something he'd already fully decided to do anyway? And which of my verdicts, if any, do I have to drop to make the rest hang together?
