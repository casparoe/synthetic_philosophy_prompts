The hole argument is typically levelled against substantivalism about spacetime. Take any solution of the field equations, choose a matter-free open region—call it the hole—and apply a diffeomorphism that fixes everything outside and on the region's boundary while smoothly shifting the metric and the spacetime points inside. Because the field equations are diffeomorphism-covariant, the original and shifted models are both solutions and are observationally indistinguishable, yet they assign different metric fields to the same points. The substantivalist, who treats spacetime points as robustly real individuals, seems forced to regard them as distinct possible histories; but then the full state outside the hole does not determine the state inside it, and determinism collapses. The usual escapes are relationalism or 'sophisticated substantivalism', which identifies diffeomorphically related models as representations of a single physical possibility.

Suppose, however, that spacetime is not fundamentally a smooth manifold but a discrete structure of the kind defended in causal set theory: a locally finite partially ordered set whose elements are the fundamental events, with the manifold, its metric, and its smooth symmetries emerging only as large-scale approximations. In that setting the hole construction loses its obvious grip: a symmetry of a discrete causal set that fixes every element outside a chosen region while moving elements inside is no longer guaranteed, because any permutation must preserve the partial order with all the elements held fixed outside. The compactly supported diffeomorphism, though it may survive as an emergent symmetry of the approximate manifold, is not a symmetry of the underlying discrete structure.

Assess whether the hole argument survives this changed assumption. Identify which premise the discrete setting bears on, and say how far the damage extends. Does the substantivalist now escape the argument while remaining a substantivalist; does the pressure merely migrate to a global analogue, such as the distinction between a causal set and a relabelling of its elements; or can a discrete version of the hole argument recover the original's force? Say what, if anything, remains of the argument once the continuum is demoted from fundamental to emergent.
