Last night me and a friend got into a long argument about Laplace's demon, and he kept reaching for Wolfram's computational irreducibility to tell me the whole picture was dead from the start. His line was: even if an intellect knew all the forces and all the positions, for plenty of systems there is no shortcut, you have to run the system step by step, so the demon is stuck unfolding the universe at the same speed the universe unfolds. He said that means Laplace didn't just get quantum mechanics wrong, he got wrong something deeper about computation and explanation, because the only explanation of some systems is the running of them. I pushed back and said all that shows is the demon needs patience and unbounded speed, and lawlike step-by-step unfolding still hands you the next state whenever you need it, so the predictability point would stand and only the hope of a short explanation dies. Given that Laplace never heard of Wolfram or Kolmogorov complexity, what do you think a brought-up-to-speed Laplace would actually have conceded, and what would he have flatly rejected? And what does computational irreducibility actually kill in his picture, determinism, predictability, or just the hope of a compressed explanation of the future?
