So I just got home from a long argument with my friend about whether an actual infinite could exist. I was on the "it can't" side, and I kept reaching for the standard stuff: Hilbert's hotel, Galileo's thing about the squares and the naturals, Thomson's lamp, the Grim Reaper paradox, and the old kalam line about how you can't form an infinite by successive addition. My friend's response was that every single one of these is secretly either a bijection/part-whole mismatch (Hilbert's hotel, Galileo) or a supertask-style puzzle (Thomson's lamp, Grim Reaper), and that the successive addition move is just a mushier version of the supertask point. I need one more argument or puzzle against actual infinities that is not on that list and that has a different structure from those two buckets — not a rewording, special case, or combination of the ones I already listed. If there is one, give it and say what it adds over the hotel and supertask moves; if you think my friend is basically right that those two buckets cover everything worth saying, say so and explain why.
