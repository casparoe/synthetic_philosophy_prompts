Here are my gut verdicts about when one world is better than another. For these purposes a world is a countably infinite collection of people (the same infinity as the natural numbers), each with a lifetime welfare level; higher is better, 0 is a neutral life, and negative levels are lives not worth living. Some worlds contain the same individual people; others contain wholly new people.

Let W1 be a world whose people are all at welfare 1. Let W2 be the same people, but with their welfare levels alternating 1, 0, 1, 0, ... . Let W3 be the same people again, with the pattern shifted: 0, 1, 0, 1, ... . Let W4 be the same people, all at 0. Let W5 be W1's people, all still at 1, plus a wholly new countably infinite group of people all at 0. Let W6 be W1's people, all still at 1, plus a wholly new countably infinite group all at -1. Let W7 be W1's people, all still at 1, plus a wholly new countably infinite group all at 1.

My verdicts:

1. W1 is better than W2. W2 lowers infinitely many of W1's people and raises nobody.
2. W2 and W3 are equally good. The only difference is which particular people receive the 1s and which receive the 0s, and that seems immaterial.
3. W2 is better than W4. Infinitely many people are better off in W2 than in W4, and no one is worse off.
4. W5 is just as good as W1. Adding zero-welfare lives does not make a world better or worse.
5. W5 is just as good as W2. W5 has the same welfare distribution as W2—countably many 1s and countably many 0s—just differently arranged among people.
6. W6 is worse than W1. Even though W1 already has infinitely many happy people, adding infinitely many miserable people seems to make the world worse.

I notice the problem: by 4, W1 equals W5; by 5, W5 equals W2; so W1 should equal W2—but 1 says W1 is better than W2. So my verdicts cannot all be right. Is there a precise principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures as many of these verdicts as possible? State the principle, say exactly which of my verdicts—or which structural assumption about betterness, such as transitivity—it cannot keep, and tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: W1 versus W7. Is adding infinitely many more happy lives to a world already containing infinitely many happy lives better, equally good, or incomparable?
