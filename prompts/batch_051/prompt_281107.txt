Here's one from The Mind's I. There's an old thought experiment where Einstein's brain gets turned into a giant book of instructions, so that by mechanically following the rules a not-particularly-clever reader can hold a conversation with "Einstein." The usual intuition was that the book by itself obviously isn't conscious; if anything is, it's the book-plus-reader process, and the reader is doing the actual thinking. Now we have LLMs, which look like a real version of that machine: a frozen file of numbers (the book) plus a simple, fixed execution loop (the reader). But the division of labor seems inverted—the file does the interesting work and the loop is dumb as rocks—yet the whole thing converses and arguably reasons. Does the Einstein's-brain argument survive LLMs? Which premise is really under pressure: that a static text can't house cognition, or that the executing agent is the one doing the thinking? If the original intuition is dead, is there a repaired line to draw (weights vs. running computation, storage vs. process), or did the thought experiment's lesson just turn out to be wrong?
