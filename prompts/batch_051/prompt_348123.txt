I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics, and my working vocabulary for foundations is the standard contemporary package: constructive versus classical existence proofs, axioms as stipulative definitions, the analytic/synthetic distinction, and the dispute over whether mathematical objects are invented or discovered. I'd like you to restate Hobbes's view of geometry in that vocabulary — in particular, his claim that geometry yields fully demonstrable knowledge because we construct the figures ourselves and can therefore know their causes, and the further claim that a science of politics could in principle attain the same status because the commonwealth is also a human artifact. Please take each of these claims in turn and translate it honestly, without reading the commitments of my framework back into Hobbes. Then give me the residue report: which parts map cleanly onto constructivist proof or stipulative foundations, which parts have to be stretched or fudged, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all, including how the translation should absorb the fact that Hobbes's own mathematical practice was largely rejected by the mathematicians of his day. Finally, say whether the residue shows that Hobbes was gesturing at something my vocabulary captures better, or that his thesis that we know only what we make bundles together claims that modern foundations rightly keep separate. Throughout, separate what is settled about this material from what is genuinely contested among scholars, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
