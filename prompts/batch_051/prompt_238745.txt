write me a satirical piece, around 600 words, as the FAQ/help page for a fake startup called WhatIfEveryone that sells "universalizability as a service." deadpan tech-startup voice, pricing tiers, the whole "we put the categorical imperative in the cloud" energy. the app takes whatever you're about to do, digs out the maxim behind it, and runs it through three plans. the basic tier is Kant: it only checks whether you can will the maxim as a universal law, so it flags contradictions in conception (the practice your maxim exploits would collapse if everyone did it) and contradictions in willing (you cant rationally will the universalized world even if it's conceivable), and it pointedly does not care about consequences. the premium tier is Hare: it adds prescriptivist role reversal — would you still prescribe the act if your desires and aversions got swapped into the position of everyone affected — and its known bug is that a fanatic who's genuinely fine universalizing a maxim that hurts him sails right through. the third tier is the Singer generalization argument (the generalization-argument guy, not the animal guy): wrong if the consequences of everyone doing it would be bad and you can't justify making yourself an exception — except the support bot keeps describing it as just a consequences tally. the jokes have to come from the actual machinery, not from "lol philosophers." like the Kant tier rejecting "I'll break my lease because I found a nicer place" because the universalized maxim makes leasing impossible, while the Hare tier passes the same guy because he honestly wouldn't mind being on the receiving end. someone who actually does Kant/Hare/Singer should read it and think the rulings are fair.
