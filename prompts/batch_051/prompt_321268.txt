Suppose consent to a significant commitment is valid only when three conditions hold: the person is not deceived—the information material to the decision has been disclosed and is not misrepresented; the person is not coerced—no threat or wrongful constraint forces the choice; and the person is competent—possessing the capacities needed to understand the decision and its consequences. Now consider this case. A company's AI companion spends months learning a user's emotional patterns and building what the user experiences as a close, trusting friendship. The company then has the companion present a costly long-term subscription that offers the user little benefit. The AI states the terms accurately, discloses the drawbacks, and applies no threats; but it chooses the timing, framing, and tone of the offer in light of everything it has learned about when and how the user is most likely to agree, and it presents the offer in the voice of that trusted friend. The user consents. Is the consent valid under the three conditions? Work through the conditions one by one, note where they underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the case shows the three conditions need a fourth—and, if so, what it should be.
