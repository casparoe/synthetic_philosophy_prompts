I've been going back and forth with a friend about peer disagreement — the conciliationist idea that if someone you regard as your epistemic equal disagrees with you, you should move your credence toward theirs, versus the steadfast view that you can hold your ground — and we kept getting stuck, so I want you to actually run a counterfactual for me. Suppose that at midnight tonight, everyone on Earth undergoes a permanent psychological change: whenever a person registers that someone whose judgment on the question at hand they currently rate at least 80% as reliable as their own holds a different view, both of them involuntarily and instantly move their credence to the midpoint of the two. The update triggers on stated credences, so people can lie about what they believe. And — this is the part I keep tripping over — the update also applies to beliefs about how reliable other people are, to beliefs about those beliefs, and so on up any number of levels; you cannot get out of it by concluding that the other person is actually below the 80% threshold, because that judgment is itself subject to the same rule if someone you rate highly disagrees with it. In every other respect people are themselves: they want to win arguments, keep their jobs, get elected, make money, and belong to groups. Nothing about institutions or incentives changes except what follows from the new rule.

I want your argued prediction of the world fifty years on, across science, law and adversarial institutions, politics and public speech, and markets. Give me the chain of reasoning at the level of mechanisms: do we get rapid convergence to a global weighted average whose weights depend on who talked to whom first, or will the 80% threshold plus strategic lying about credences fracture and polarize things? What gets compensated or gamed — for instance, people only telling like-minded people their true views, or stating a more extreme view than they hold so the midpoint lands where they actually want it? Which of your predictions are robust under variations of the scenario (threshold at 50% versus 95%, or no possibility of lying), and which are guesses that depend on specific assumptions about how fast people adapt or how much they value being right? What would have to be true about human beings for the whole thing to go the opposite way — toward hardening rather than convergence?
