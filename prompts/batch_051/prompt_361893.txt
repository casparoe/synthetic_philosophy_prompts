Suppose a research group of three or four philosophers of physics has four years and can pursue only one of three strands of work on the dispute between Humean best-system theories and anti-Humean accounts of laws of nature. Frame the dispute as a disagreement over what job a law must do. On the anti-Humean side, a law must explain its instances, whether by governing them or by flowing from the dispositional nature of properties. On the Humean side, a law must first systematize the world's regularities by the best balance of simplicity and strength; explanation, to the extent it is legitimate, comes only after that job is done. The group's stated aim is to force a genuine concession from one side or to show that the debate rests on a false alternative. Rank the three strands below and defend the ordering according to importance for that aim, tractability within four years for philosophers working without empirical collaborators, neglectedness, and how many downstream commitments depend on the question. Also state what evidence would change the ranking.

Strand 1, the explanation question: Can the explanatory-circularity objection to Humean laws be sharpened into a criterion that does not presuppose governing laws? The group would specify when a generalization explains its instances, determine whether best-system laws meet that standard, and decide whether any failure is a defect of the laws themselves or only of a particular explanatory practice.

Strand 2, the bookkeeping question: Develop and evaluate the proposal that laws are nomic bookkeeping — devices for tracking which models, counterfactuals, and inductive projections transfer from one context to another — rather than summaries of regularities or second-order governors. The group would test whether this third role captures what each side wants, whether it collapses into one of the standard positions, or whether it makes lawhood dispensable.

Strand 3, the taxonomy question: Replace the two-way split with a three-way classification indexed by where nomic necessity, if any, enters the world: no primitive necessity at all; necessity grounded in the dispositional nature of properties but involving no relation of governance; or necessity as a primitive higher-order relation over properties. The group would determine which contrasts among these positions are empirically or formally detectable, which are merely verbal, and which existing arguments depend on the binary framing.

For each strand, specify (a) the finding that would count as decisive, (b) the commitments it would force at least one side to revise, (c) the principal risk that it ends in a verbal dispute or loses contact with scientific practice, and (d) the evidence within four years that would move it up or down. Finally, state the background assumption doing the most work in your ranking and say how the ordering changes if that assumption fails.
