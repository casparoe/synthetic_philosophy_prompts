Counterfactual: In 1785 Jeremy Bentham completes the manuscript on sexual morality that in our timeline he never published. Suppose that, instead of leaving it in his papers, he releases it anonymously in London as a short pamphlet. It contains, in recognizable form, his utilitarian case that consensual sexual acts between adults should not be criminalized: the acts themselves produce no direct harm to unconsenting third parties; mere popular antipathy or religious asceticism is an illegitimate ground for punishment; and the misery inflicted by the penal law outweighs whatever mischief the prohibited acts cause. Nothing else about Bentham’s other projects, his personality, or the political situation of the 1780s is changed.

Give an argued prediction of how this event plays out over the following fifty years in Britain. I am less interested in a story than in a causal analysis. Separate the prediction into at least three threads: (1) Bentham’s own career, reputation, and subsequent influence on law reform; (2) the fate of the pamphlet’s arguments in parliamentary, legal, and literary debate; (3) the effect on the early utilitarian movement and its public identity. For each, identify the mechanisms, incentives, and constraints at work, and assign a rough confidence to the predicted outcome. Then say which thread you are least confident about, and what assumption would have to fail for your main prediction to be wrong.
