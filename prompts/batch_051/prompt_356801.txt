My roommate keeps giving me the same line about ethics: morality is just like table manners. Different cultures set their tables differently, and no one thinks one culture's fork etiquette is more correct than another's in any mind-independent way — so, he says, no culture's moral code is more correct either; it's all just local convention dressed up with a feeling of wrongness. The comparison is doing the real argumentative work for him: he doesn't have an independent arguement that there are no moral facts, he just borrows the obviousness of table-manners relativity. Start by brainstorming the correspondences (custom, social pressure, the function of manners, the phenomenology of "that's wrong"), then work through the disanalogies, separating the ones that actually matter for his conclusion from the harmless ones. Take a definite position: does his relativist conclusion survive the audit, or was the analogy carrying the whole weight?
