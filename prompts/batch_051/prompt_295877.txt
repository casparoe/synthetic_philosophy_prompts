In De Rerum Natura, Lucretius argues that body is whatever can be touched, and that nothing can act or be acted upon except through body touching body; void is simply where body is not. Construct a thought experiment in which a strict Lucretian atomist must explain, from first principles, why a squeezed sponge re-expands when released. Constraints: the setup must remain an ordinary, macroscopic scenario; the atomist may posit only tiny, solid, impenetrable bodies and empty void; and she may not help herself to non-contact forces, air pressure as a primitive, or “pores” that merely rename the phenomenon. After the scenario, state exactly what the atomist must say happens at the atomic level, why the void is doing real explanatory work, and which feature of the case brings the contact-action principle closest to an unearned restatement. Finish with what a defender of Lucretius would say to stop the case from counting.
