Me and a friend got into a long argument about whether you can ever say one person's migraine is worse than another person's, which led us into the whole problem of comparing utility across people. I think I've found a workaround, which I'm calling the shared-benchmark method, and I want an honest read on it.

The setup is this. You don't compare Alice's and Bob's experiences directly. Instead, you give both of them a common physical benchmark, like holding a hand in 8°C water for 30 seconds. Then, for any experience you care about, you ask each person how many 30-second hand-dunks that experience is worth to them in an indifference sense. Alice might say her last migraine was worth 40 dunks; Bob might say his was worth 15. Now both migraines are expressed in units of standard dunks, and since a dunk is a dunk for everyone, you can finally compare: Alice's migraine is worse for Alice than Bob's is for Bob. I know people usually try to use money or self-rated 0-to-10 scales for this, but the physical anchor is supposed to be the novel part.

The obvious objection is that people differ in cold tolerance, so the same water isn't the same experience for everyone. But couldn't you calibrate for that too — measure skin temperature or stress hormones, or normalize each person's scale against their own range? I want to know whether the underlying idea is sound, whether someone already worked it out under a better name, and where exactly it breaks if it breaks. Is the comparison still circular somewhere?
