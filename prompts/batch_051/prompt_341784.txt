Okay, so I've basically ended up convinced by the standing view of hypocrisy: the idea that when someone blames another person for a wrong they themselves are committing (or have committed in the relevantly parallel way), the problem isn't only that they're being obnoxious or that the target will predictably tune them out. The blame itself is defective — the blamer lacks something like standing, the way a judge in the wrong jurisdiction lacks standing to rule. The criticism can be entirely true, the target can be fully at fault, and still there's supposed to be something wrong specifically with the act of blaming. My friend keeps needling me that I'm not allowed to critisize his driving because of a speeding ticket I got two years ago, which isn't the same thing at all, but its gotten me thinking about the more careful version of the view.

What I want is a map of what would count against it. Not just the obvious point that the content of the criticism can be true — that's compatible with the view, not a defeater. I mean the observations, cases, intuitions, and arguments that should actually move someone who holds the standing view: cases where hypocritical blame feels fine or even valuable, reasons to think standing isn't a genuinely moral or deontic notion but just a pragmatic or conversational one, cases involving reformed wrongdoers or third parties, asymmetries with other reactive attitudes (if a dishonest person can't blame someone for lying, can a coward still blame someone for cowardice? can a miser praise generosity?). Which of these are decisive and which are merely awkward? Where is the view most exposed, and has it maybe been insulated into unfalsifiability by being stated in a way where the defect is supposed to be obvious but never gets specified? I'd also like to know which of these a committed defender would actually concede counts against them, and which they'd dismiss as changing the subject. For me personally, cases where a third party has standing but a fellow wrongdoer doesn't are the most likely to make me reconsider, so spend some time there.
