I've spent the last year stuck between two friends in my EA-adjacent reading group, and I want them to produce an adversarial-collaboration document instead of arguing. One of them, M, is a committed marginalist: she takes the existing institutional landscape as mostly given at any decision point, and thinks the honest move is to evaluate concrete interventions—bednets, cash transfers, deworming, maybe targeted occupational training—and fund the best expected-value one, because those estimates are at least wrong in a legible way. The other one, S, is a systems-change person: he thinks the whole marginalist frame is in the weeds, and that what actually matters is shifting the rules, feedback loops, and incentive structures—policy, norms, institutional design, tech governance—even if you can only be roughly right about the direction. They've talked past each other for months and I think the real disagreements are finer then they admit.

Write the document they should have signed. It should be a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. I don't want mush like "both sides think doing good is hard"—the agreed claims should be substantive and stated accurately enough that each side would nod along, including claims about what marginal estimates do and don't capture and about what systems-level efforts need to prove. Then the table should have rows for each real disagreement, written so that M's own view is phrased in M-friendly wording and S's in S-friendly wording, not a strawman of either.

Then a cruxes section: the sub-questions such that if they were settled, at least one side would actually change its allocation. I suspect one crux is whether the historical record of policy-and-institutions changes gives enough signal to estimate the success probability of a given systems campaign ex ante, rather than just telling nice stories afterwards. Another is whether successful marginal interventions tend to sap or build appetite and capacity for structural change (the "patch that relieves pressure" worry vs. the "wins create coalitions" hope). For each crux you identify, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and write down the prediction each side would make about it in advance.

Feel free to add one more crux if it's genuine, but no filler. Format it as: (1) Agreed claims, (2) Disagreements table, (3) Cruxes with predictions.
