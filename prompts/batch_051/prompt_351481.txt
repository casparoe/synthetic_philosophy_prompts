I keep hearing people in AI safety say the intelligence explosion will be like a chain reaction, and I’m a retired physicist so I know actual chain reactions pretty well. I’ve also just read I. J. Good’s 1965 paper Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine, where he sketches the idea that the first ultraintelligent machine will be able to design an even better machine, and so on in a loop with no human needed in the design step after that. His picture seems to be doing a lot of work for the view that takeoff will be extremely fast — the hard takeoff folks sometimes talk as if fast just follows from self-improving loop.

Here’s the analogy I want you to audit: recursive self-improvement as a nuclear chain reaction, with criticality as the point where the system’s own intelligence is enough to sustain the next design generation, and supercriticality as the explosive takeoff. I’d like the following sections:

1. Correspondences (150-200 words): Make the table explicit — what plays the role of the fissile material, the neutron population, the capture/fission cross-sections, the moderator, the control rods, etc.?

2. Where the analogy holds (100-150 words): Which features of a chain reaction does recursive self-improvement actually share?

3. Where it breaks (200-250 words): Which features of the physical case have no analogue, or map to something that behaves differently? In particular, what in a design-and-build loop corresponds to the fixed per-interaction reproduction factor, and does the analogy quietly assume that smarter is scalar and transferable like a neutron count?

4. What the analogy smuggles in (100-150 words): What does it import about speed, inevitability, and the absence of external inputs — fuel, energy, institutions, chips, experimental data?

5. Verdict (100-150 words): Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion that AI takeoff will be explosive or fast survive? Or was the nuclear analogy carrying that conclusion the whole time? If it fails, is there a better analogy for Good’s loop?
