I'm drafting a short essay on AI welfare and I'd like comments on the argument before I develop it further. The essay targets a move I keep seeing: because a reinforcement learning (RL) agent's policy is shaped by a scalar reward signal, people speak as though that signal is the agent's valence — its pleasure/pain axis, or at least a primitive analogue of it. My working claim is that this step from reward to valence is too quick, because reward in RL is a training signal defined relative to the designer's objective and used by the update rule to assign credit, whereas valence, whatever else it is, is an evaluation made from the agent's own point of view. My sketch has three moves. First, a dissociation: the functional role of an RL reward signal is to supply the scalar that determines the sign and magnitude of policy or value updates — when, where, and how strongly to adjust behavior — while valenced affect plausibly involves the state being evaluated as good or bad for the agent, with downstream effects on attention, motivation, and the agent's own priorities. Second, a diagnosis of the slide: the word "reward" is inherited from animal learning, where reinforcers typically arrive alongside felt pleasure, pain, or aversion; when the term is imported into RL, the hedonic connotation quietly comes along even though only the mathematical role has been preserved. Third, an overgeneration check: a deflationary view should not imply that nonhuman animals with structurally similar learning signals cannot suffer. So I want the difference to be architectural rather than about the presence of a scalar signal itself — something like: reward becomes valenced only when the signal is integrated into a self-model, broadcast in a way that lets it modulate the agent's other goal-directed processes, and able to alter what the agent cares about rather than merely what it does.

I'd appreciate critical comments on the argumentative structure rather than the prose. In particular: (a) Does the first move do real work, or have I just relabeled "credit assignment" and "evaluation" without showing they can come apart? (b) Does the architectural fix in the third move secretly reintroduce valence under another name, and does it set the bar too high — would it deny felt pain to fish, rodents, or human infants? (c) The strongest objection I can anticipate is that valence just is a functional role in learning and choice, not an extra felt quality, in which case an RL reward signal does satisfy the role and I've changed the subject to phenomenology; how should I answer that without surrendering the essay's point? (d) Which single architectural feature — self-model, global broadcast, or endogenous goal revision — would most plausibly mark the transition from reward to valence, and is my distinction still doing any work once I concede that that feature is what matters?
