I keep getting stuck on something in Paul and I'd be curious how you'd sort it out. In 1 Corinthians 11 (ESV) he tells the Corinthians that "every wife who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head," and that if she won't cover her head "she should cut her hair short," and he asks, "Does not nature itself teach you that if a man wears long hair it is a disgrace for him, but if a woman has long hair, it is her glory?" (11:5-6, 14-15). Then he closes the whole discussion with "If anyone is inclined to be contentious, we have no such practice, nor do the churches of God" (11:16). But in Galatians he says that "as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ," and that "there is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free, there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (3:27-28).

These two seem to be pulling in opposed directions: one enforces a gender-differentiated practice in the assembly and grounds it in nature and the creation sequence (11:7-9), while the other says baptism dissolves those exact distinctions. Can the two be reconciled, and at what cost? I want the candidate reconciliations layed out—different senses of "in Christ," one text being about soteriology and the other about liturgy, the head-covering rule as a local custom or adiaphoron, a shift in Paul's own thinking—and then an honest verdict on which readings are real distinctions in the texts and which are special pleading. The hard part for me is this: Paul himself appeals to nature and the order of creation to justify the covering, so the usual "that was just his culture" move has to explain why his own stated basis isn't universal. And is there a principled criterion for sorting Paul's commands into cultural versus transcultural that doesn't get reverse-engineered from whatever we already want to keep or drop—one that also gives a stable answer for the dietary laws and the slavery texts?
