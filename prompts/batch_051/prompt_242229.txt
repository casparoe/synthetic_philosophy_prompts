Since I retired I've been reading philosophy, and I keep noticing what might be a real structural parallel or might just be me pattern-matching. In physics we spent whole careers using explanations that are frankly teleological: a particle "chooses" the path of stationary action, a ball rolling downhill "wants" to minimize potential energy, an isolated system "seeks" maximum entropy. Nobody thought the particle had a mind; it was just a compact way of packaging a variational principle that we could, with enough work, write out as differential equations. Now I'm reading about Dennett's intentional stance, and the way people predict a chess engine or a self-driving car by saying "it thinks I'm about to castle," "it wants to control the open file," or "it believes the road is clear" feels like exactly the same move. The as-if talk earns it's keep because it predicts behavior, not because anyone thinks there is something it is like to be Stockfish. So my question is whether the intentional stance is literally the same kind of explanatory device as these as-if goal attributions in physics, or whether that resemblance is superficial. If they are the same, I'd expect that anything we can do with belief talk about an AI we could, with enough work, replace with talk about its training objective and its weights with no loss. Is that actually true, or does the belief description do predictive work that the lower-level story can't? And if they are not the same, where exactly does the mapping break? I'd also like to know whether the parallel cuts the other way: if the intentional stance is just what physicists have always done, does that make physics more intentional than people admit, or make the intentional stance less interesting? I'm not after a reading list; I want to know whether the structure actually matches and where it comes apart.
