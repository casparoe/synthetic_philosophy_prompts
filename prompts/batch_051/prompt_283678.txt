By analogy with a meme, an antimeme is information that tends to suppress its own transmission: an idea, fact, or memory that is hard to notice, hard to retain, or hard to pass on. Some antimemes are inert (the information simply fails to stick), while others are active and self-concealing (the information, or the thing it describes, edits itself out of records and minds). I am preparing to teach George Orwell's Nineteen Eighty-Four, and I want to use this concept without reducing the novel to an illustration of it.
