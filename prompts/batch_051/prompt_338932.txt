Spent another evening arguing in my philosophy-of-music group and hit the same wall as always. The question was what a musical work actually is: an abstract type that performances token, just the score, a sound-structure, or something constituted by the performance tradition itself? Everyone in the room is smart, everyone knows the standard moves, and nobody has shifted in a year. My own hunch is that we aren't really having one debate — the word 'work' is doing three seperate jobs: (a) what makes two performances count as performances of the same piece, (b) what did the composer actually create, and (c) what is a recording of it, especially when the thing was never performed but assembled in a studio. Diagnose this properly. Is it substantive, verbal, or several debates welded together under one label? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move it — or is the honest answer that the question just isn't well-posed? I don't want a survey of the positions; I want the structure of the disagreement itself.
