I'm taking an upper-level epistemology course this term, and the section on accuracy-first approaches to epistemic rationality has pulled me in several directions at once. I need a consistency audit before I turn in a paper defending a package of these views. I'm genuinely unsure where the instability is — some of the following may conflict only apparently, and some may conflict more deeply than they look.

The package I find myself endorsing is this:

1. Veritism: the only fundamental epistemic value is accuracy, where the accuracy of a credence is a matter of how close it is to the truth-value of the proposition in question. Any other epistemic norm, if legitimate, must be shown to serve accuracy.

2. Probabilism via accuracy dominance: rational credences obey the probability axioms. The argument is that any non-probabilistic credence function is accuracy-dominated: there exists some probabilistic credence function that is more accurate in every epistemically possible world, so adopting non-probabilistic credences involves a sure loss of accuracy.

3. Strict propriety: a legitimate measure of accuracy must be strictly proper. That is, any probabilistic credence function should assign itself higher expected accuracy than any distinct alternative credence function. This underwrites the thought that a rational agent may regard her own credences as optimal by her own lights.

4. Rule-pluralism: there is nonetheless no uniquely correct scoring rule. The Brier score and the log score are both legitimate strictly proper scoring rules, and there is no further accuracy-relevant fact that privileges one over the other.

5. Imprecise credences: some bodies of evidence are too sparse, mixed, or equivocal to warrant a single precise credence. In such cases, the rational doxastic state is not a single probability function but a set of probability functions — a credal set — representing the agent's unresolved uncertainty.

6. The Principal Principle: when one knows that the objective chance of an outcome is 0.7, one's credence in that outcome should be 0.7, and more generally one should defer to known chances in one's conditional credences.

Please audit this package. I want to know which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with which others, and which apparent tensions dissolve once the ideas are stated carefully. For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, and for each, specify what the least costly revision is and exactly what it would force me to give up. Don't just tell me that each of the six could be challenged from somewhere or other; I want the strongest version of the charge that the package as stated is jointly unstable, followed by a recommendation about which single revision would do the most to restore coherence.
