One of my colleagues keeps insisting that loyalty to your own group is just bias with better branding. His evidence: in minimal group experiments you can get people to favor their own arbitrarily assigned group over the other within minutes, before there's any shared history or mutual obligation, so the "virtue" story looks like a post-hoc rationalization. Another colleague says that's way to fast. A genuine virtue, she says, is a disposition that's responsive to the right reasons, and real loyalty is supposed to track relationship facts — shared projects, reciprocity, vulnerability — not merely a label. On her view the lab result only shows we have a fast and dirty in-group heuristic, not that loyalty is nothing more than that.
