I keep running into people who talk as though Solomonoff induction just is the proof that Occam's razor works, and whenever I try to pin down how much it actually buys you, the same loose ends come up. The prior is defined over programs on some universal Turing machine, and a different reference machine gives a different prior even though the differences are supposedly just a constant; in the limit that washes out, but all actual data we ever see is finite, and constants can be arbitrarily huge. The whole thing is also uncomputable, so people fall back on MDL-ish or compression-based approximations, and its not obvious those inherit the nice convergence guarantees. And at the risk of sounding dumb: isn't there something circular about using a simplicity-biased prior to justify a simplicity bias, since the coding scheme you pick already encodes your notion of what counts as simple?
