I want a thought experiment built to order, not a general essay: construct a scenario where someone prays to a God known to be both omniscient and immutable, and the prayer is genuinely rational as a request — asking for healing or rain or a job, that sort of thing — not as meditation or self-improvement. Constraints: no having the person doubt God's omniscience or immutability, no making God secretly changeable after all, no vague appeal to mystery at the crucial step, and no special revelation the scenario just posits. After the setup, take the two standard objections one at a time. First: an omniscient God already knows what you need, so the prayer can't inform him. Second: an immutable God can't be moved to act by your prayer. Show how your scenario blocks each one — how a prayer can fail to inform and fail to change God and still not be pointless. Then be honest about which structural feature of your scenario is doing the real work. If you deleted that feature, would it collapse back into the standard 'petitionary prayer is pointless' conclusion, or would something else still hold it up? Finally, write the strongest attack a smart defender of the pointless-prayer view could make against your scenario.
