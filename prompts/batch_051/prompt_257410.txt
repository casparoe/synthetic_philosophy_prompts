I am a novelist, currently outlining a novel whose protagonist is woken most nights by dreams that present themselves to her, with great vividness, as conversations she has just been having. The book's interpretation of her—whether she is living through something or confabulating something—turns on a question I cannot answer cleanly: does she undergo these dreams, or does she merely find them in memory when she wakes? I would like an explicit, decomposed probability rather than a loose intuition.

Please give me your credence that an ordinary vivid dream is an experience: a conscious mental episode with qualitative character and felt duration that occurs during sleep, such that the later report is normally a recollection of an episode that was actually taking place. I do not mean the trivial claim that people produce dream reports. Decompose the probability into the sub-questions that do the most work, and make sure the decomposition includes at least these three.

First, the probability that the detail and felt duration of dream reports are best explained by an antecedent sleep-time mental process, rather than by the operations of waking memory and reporting alone—the skeptical, deflationary line associated with Norman Malcolm.

Second, the conditional probability that, if some sleep-time process does produce the material, that process is conscious during sleep—something it is like for the dreamer then and there—rather than a nonconscious process whose contents are made conscious only at or after waking, as in the cassette-style alternative associated with Daniel Dennett.

Third, the probability that the correct standard for what counts as an experience is permissive enough to include sleep-time subjective character even when the waking capacities that usually accompany experience—judgment, reflection, online report—are absent.

For each of the three, give a number and one sentence of justification, and then show how you combine them into your overall credence. Finally, tell me which of the terms you are least confident in, and what one piece of evidence or argument would shift that term most.

For orientation: my own rough credence is about 0.6, while a friend who works in a sleep laboratory puts himself at 0.3. Once you have decomposed the question, say which sub-question most plausibly houses that difference.

Please keep the entire reply to no more than 450 words.
