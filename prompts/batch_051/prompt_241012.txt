for a reading group I'm in, I'm writing a short 'where I stand' note on whether it makes sense to assign probabilities to your own choices, and I'd like someone to go through it before I circulate it. here's roughly what I think, in no particular order:

(1) I'm convinced that deliberation crowds out prediction. while I'm actually trying to decide whether to do A, I can't coherently feed a prior probability about whether I'll do A into the decision; my choice is the thing being settled, not another piece of evidence about itself.
(2) There's nothing special, in principle, about predicting my own choices as against predicting a friend's. same evidence, same base rates, same determinism. any asymmetry is just the fact that I happen to be inside one of the two heads.
(3) I'm perfectly happy assigning probabilities to my own future choices when I'm not currently deciding them: right now I'd give about 4-to-1 that I'll skip the gym tomorrow.
(4) I'm a causal decision theorist. choosing an act is about making the best causal difference, not about being in good news.
(5) My past base rates are powerful evidence about my own future choices. if I've ordered the salmon ninety times out of the last hundred dinners, I should be roughly 0.9 on ordering it tonight.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look as if they are, and how severe each real tension is. for any real tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, and if the whole lot can't be made consistent, tell me which single claim I should revise first.
