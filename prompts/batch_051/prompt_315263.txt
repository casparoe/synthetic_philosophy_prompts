Solomonoff induction is usually presented as a Bayesian vindication of Occam's razor: it assigns higher prior probability to hypotheses with shorter programs. But "shorter" is defined only relative to a chosen universal Turing machine, and the choice of machine looks arbitrary. For any two competing hypotheses, it seems one can design a universal machine on which the favored hypothesis is simpler, and another on which the opposite is true. Since the simplicity ranking can therefore be reversed at will, why should Solomonoff induction be regarded as a justification of Occam's razor rather than a restatement of whichever simplicity metric we happened to encode?
