I did sensory neuroscience for thirty years, mostly in awake behaving animals, and since retiring Ive been reading philosophy of mind for the first time. The Malcolm–Dennett dispute over whether dreams are experiences has stalled in a way I recognize from lab arguments but cant quite name. As I understand it, Malcolm holds that dreaming is not something that occurs during sleep at all; the waking report is the criterion for having dreamt, so there is no hidden occurrence for a neuroscientist to find. Dennett answers that dreams are experiences, but with no fixed content or timing, and he has that thought experiment where the whole dream narrative is loaded into memory in the instant before waking with nothing in the brain to distinguish that from a dream that truly unfolded over minutes. Both positions seem compatible with all the same data, so nobody moves. Is this a substantive disagreement or a verbal one about the word experience? Is it really a fight over what counts as data, with Malcolm treating first-person wakeful report as the only admissible evidence and Dennett allowing third-person theorizing about memory systems? And here is the part where I want your help: my old field can now reconstruct the gist of waking visual experience from brain activity. Suppose we could do that in real time during REM. Would that settle anything, or would each side simply interpret the result differently? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this is, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress.
