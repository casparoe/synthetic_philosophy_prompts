I've been going back and forth with my brother-in-law since we watched one of those Hitler documentaries, about whether particular individuals actually push history or whether the structural conditions would have thrown up someone similar anyway, and we keep landing on the same test case: without Hitler, would there have been a Nazi Germany, or at least something like the Holocaust? Could you write a dialogue between two historians, one who thinks Nazi Germany and the extermination of the Jews required Hitler's specific obsessions and risk-taking, and one who thinks interwar Germany's structural conditions plus European antisemitism would have produced something recognizably similar, with each trying to talk the other out of their view and actally responding to the other's points? Make both sides genuinely good, no strawmen, and push them to get concrete about the causal mechanism — say, whether the regime's radicalization before and during the war is better explained by Hitler's personal obsessions or by rival agencies outdoing each other in the leader's name — rather than trading slogans.
