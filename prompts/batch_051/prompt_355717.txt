Article 7 of our federation's Basic Charter, adopted in 1848, provides: "Every public task shall be assigned to the smallest territorial unit capable of performing it. A larger unit may act only where the task, by its nature, cannot be adequately performed by the smaller unit, or where its performance by the smaller unit would impose substantial burdens on other units; and even then the larger unit shall act only so far as the smaller units cannot by agreement among themselves secure the same end."

A constituent state has now imposed a 1 percent levy on every sale of virtual land—parcels of "land" inside persistent online worlds—concluded through a platform accessible from that state's territory. The platform operator must withhold the levy at the point of sale and remit it to the state; the proceeds are earmarked for village schools. The platforms are incorporated in other states and in other countries. Buyers and sellers may be anywhere; a single parcel can be resold many times in an hour; the worlds' internal "land registers" are kept on servers whose physical location shifts with network load; and no step of the transaction occurs on the state's territory except, in most cases, the residence of one party.

The federation, invoking Article 7, proposes to prohibit the state's levy and replace it with a uniform federal levy. The state replies that financing village schools is a public task the state is fully capable of performing as the smallest territorial unit, and that nothing about virtual land requires uniform national action. The federation replies that the state's levy imposes substantial burdens on residents and businesses of other units, that no agreement among the smaller units can prevent this because the sales have no stable location, and that Article 7 therefore authorizes federal action.

Does Article 7 authorize the federation to prohibit the state levy and enact the uniform one? Work through the answer that the text, the purpose of subsidiarity as the 1848 drafters would have understood it, and the reasonable expectations of a platform operator reading the Charter today would each give. Then say which of these approaches should govern where they diverge, and identify the feature of the case—the earmarking, the withholding, the non-locatable asset, or the residence of one party—that does the most work in your answer.
