ok so I just spent an hour arguing with a friend about whether the interventionist/manipulationist account of causation is secretly circular, and I cant shake the feeling that one of his steps is sleight of hand but I cant say which one. We were talking about the view that X causes Y just in case there is some possible intervention on X that would change Y, with everything else held fixed. My friend laid out the circularity worry like this:

(1) X is a cause of Y iff there is a possible intervention on X that would change Y, when all the other variables that matter are held fixed.
(2) But not any old change to X counts as an intervention. For I to be an intervention on X, I has to (a) change X, (b) make X stop responding to its usual other causes, (c) affect Y only through X, and (d) be independent from any background factor that might affect Y along some other route.
(3) Heres why thats a problem. Say I am testing whether a drug causes recovery, and I randomly assign people to get the drug or not. To know that my random assignment is actually an intervention on the drug, I need to know that it changes who gets the drug, that it doesnt change recovery some other way (doctors treating the randomized groups differently, people who didnt get the drug seeking other treatments), and that it really breaks the usual routes that determine who takes the drug.
(4) But the things I need to certify there — "assignment changes who gets the drug," "assignment affects recovery only through the drug," "assignment breaks the usual causes of drug-taking" — are all causal claims. So I am using causal knowledge to certify that something is an intervention.
(5) Therefore, on this account, you cannot apply the definition of "X causes Y" without already knowing some causal facts, because you need a certified intervention to apply it.
(6) So the account defines causation in terms of interventions, and interventions partly in terms of causation. That is circular. And if it is circular, then it cannot be telling us what causation actually is, or underwrite causal inference.

So: is this argument valid? If it is not, which numbered step should I reject and why exactly? If it is valid, is the circularity actually a problem for the view, or is it the harmless kind? I can see each step sounding obvious, but I have a suspicion that step 5 or 6 is doing something too fast and I cannot articulate what.
