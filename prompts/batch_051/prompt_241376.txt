I got into a running argument at a party a few months ago with a friend who does philosophy for fun, and I can't stop poking at it. His claim was that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg: if your parents had conceived a month later, or a different sperm had won, the resulting person would be someone else, not you, even if their whole life went basically the same. He called it "origin essentialism" and insisted it's a conceptual truth about identity, not a scientific claim. I'm a software engineer, so my instinct was, fine, lets test it — but I honestly cannot figure out what a test would even look like. If you poll people, most probably agree with him, but people also agree with all kinds of false stuff. Is there any way to bring evidence to bear on a claim like that, or is it just unfalsifiable in the worst sense?

I've been half-designing a study in my head: you hold a person's life story fixed and vary the biological starting point across conditions — same parents but a different egg, same egg but a different sperm, same parents but a later conception, cloning with the same genome but no gametes at all — and ask people whether the resulting person "could have been" the original person. But I keep tripping over what the word "you" is doing in the vignette, and I don't know if a positive result would tell me anything about identity or just about how English speakers use "same person." So: set out the strongest test you can of origin essentialism, say what result would convince you either way, and be straight about what the test could never settle even in principle. Lead with the bottom line, and keep the whole response under 500 words.
