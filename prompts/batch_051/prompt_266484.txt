I work as a software engineer, mostly building systems on top of large language models, and I have no formal philosophy background. I keep running into a tension between a view I find theoretically compelling and an intuition I cannot shake, and I want something more than a summary of the two sides: I want to know what it would take for either side to give way, or whether there is a stable reading on which they are compatible.

The theory, as I hold it: everything a trained model outputs is a function of its training data together with the inductive biases of its architecture and training procedure. On this view, what we call creativity or an intuitive leap is really sampling from a learned conditional distribution. When an output looks like a leap, the usual explanation is that it occupies a region of representation space that feels far from any training example under our low-dimensional intuitions about distance, but is actually close to many examples once the model's own geometry is taken into account. A machine can interpolate, recombine, and perhaps extrapolate a short distance along directions already present in the data manifold; it cannot form a genuinely new abstraction. The appearance of novelty, when it occurs, is mostly a human artifact: we notice the one striking success and not the thousands of near-misses, we cannot inspect the entire corpus to confirm the ingredients were absent, and we narrate a leap after the fact because the output surprised us.

The competing intuition is firm for me because I have built and run systems whose outputs did things I would not have predicted from the training data. I do not mean a surprising continuation or a clever adaptation. I mean cases where the output seems to require abstracting a structure that no training example states or, to the best of my ability to tell, implies. For example: a model produces a dynamic-programming formulation for a problem class whose training solutions are all brute force; or it generates a refactoring built around an abstraction that neither the codebase nor the surrounding corpus ever names. My intuition says these are not just high-dimensional interpolation. There is a difference in kind between recombining existing forms and forming a new abstraction that makes the solution shorter, more general, or conceptually leaner than the inputs it was trained on. If that difference is real, then the theory is missing something, and a model is not merely a sampler over a fixed manifold but a system that can construct new compressions or new levels of representation under task pressure.

What I want worked through is which of these should yield, and why. Should I treat the intuition as a selection artifact and drop it? Should I amend the theory so that it can count some operations as genuine leaps without going mystical, for instance by distinguishing extrapolation from interpolation, or by defining novelty in terms of finding a representation that compresses the problem better than any recombination of training material? Or is the conflict dissolved once I stop equating being in the distribution with being already written down in the training set, so that a leap can be both real and fully mechanistic? I specifically want an account of what would justify each move: what kind of evidence or argument would debunk the intuition, what would force an amendment to the theory, and what a compatibility reading would have to claim about the phrase training distribution in order to be more than just a relabeling of the problem.
