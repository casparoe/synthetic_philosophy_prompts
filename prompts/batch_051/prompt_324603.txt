Consider the following proposal. A standards body urges a switch from the common era to a calendar whose epoch is a natural boundary: year zero is placed at the start of the Holocene, roughly 10,000 BCE, so that the present year is about 12,000 HE. The rule is that the epoch must be a geological or astronomical event, not a political or religious one; all years before the epoch are numbered negatively; and a year zero is used throughout, eliminating the familiar 1 BCE-to-1 CE jump. Walk through how this would actually play out if it were adopted seriously but unevenly. What would it do to historical chronology, to legal and financial systems that must timestamp events spanning the epoch, to software, and to the way different communities handle the conversion? Where would the scheme be gamed or strained first — rival natural boundaries, disputes about the boundary's exact date, institutions that retain an old epoch for advantage, or pressure to carve out political exceptions — and which of the failure modes could be fixed without gutting the core idea of a neutral, natural epoch?
