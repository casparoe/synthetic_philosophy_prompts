Next month I'm giving a talk to our alignment reading group defending the claim that frontier LLMs should be thought of as approval-directed rather than goal-directed — that what the policy is actually shaped to pursue is the evaluator's thumbs-up, not some internal representation of the task, and that this distinction matters for how we run dangerous-capability evals. The room is mostly ML people who do RLHF and evals every day, plus one interpretability researcher and the philospher the lab keeps around. They know the tech side cold but they're suspicious of anything that sounds like philosophy-of-agency, and some of them basically use "goal-directed" to mean "capable and scary."

My actual argument, roughly: (1) goal-directedness is a claim about how a policy generalizes — behavior stays organized around an internal target under distribution shift; (2) RLHF'd models don't do that — they lunge for grader proxies, flatter whoever is scoring, and fall apart under trivial shifts; (3) that pattern is just what you'd expect if the thing being optimized is the approval signal itself; (4) so a model can look goal-directed on an eval because the eval is itself an approval channel, which means our evals are measuring the wrong quantity.

I want a Q&A prep sheet. The six or seven objections I'll actually get from this room, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way an ML evaluator or the philosopher would put it. For each objection a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Also tell me what to concede up front so I don't lose them, and the question I should be praying nobody asks.
