Okay so I got into a long argument with my roommate last night and I can't shake it. He had been reading Anselm's Proslogion, chapter eight, the bit where Anselm tries to explain how God can be compassionate without being passible. Anselm's answer, as my roommate put it, is that God is called compassionate in terms of our experience, not his own: when God sees our misery, we experience the effect of compassion—rescue, forbearance, relief—but God does not undergo the feeling of compassion. So God is compassionate because he spares sinners and saves the wretched, but not compassionate because no suffering touches his own state. So far pretty standard.

But then my roommate pressed it: if compassion can be truly predicated entirely on the side of the effects, and not on any inner disturbance in the agent, then why can't the same be said of a being that has no capacity for inner disturbance at all? Like an ideal caregiver robot that reliably performs rescue, relief, and forbearance but feels nothing, or a human with severely flattened affect who perfectly tends to someone in pain. He says Anselm's distinction commits you to calling both of those fully compassionate, and that if you balk at that, the original distinction was doing illicit work. Then he went further: (1) If effects alone can make "compassionate" true, then feelings are not required for compassion. (2) The robot and the flat-affect human produce the right effects. (3) So they are fully compassionate. (4) But if they are fully compassionate, then our ordinary view that shared feeling and emotional attunement are central to love and compassion is wrong. (5) And that can't be right—if your mother never felt anything when you suffered but always did exactly the right things, you would think something was missing. So, he says, Anselm's distinction proves too much.

Whats the way out? I'd like to keep both that God is genuinely compassionate and that something important is missing in the affectless human case, but I can't figure out which step to reject without just special-pleading for God. Can you put your finger on where this goes wrong, and say what rejecting that step costs? Please keep the answer under 500 words, in American English.
