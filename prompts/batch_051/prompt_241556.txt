im trying to figure out what I actually think about what makes a statement racist, and I'd like someone to work out the principle behind my gut calls. Here are my verdicts, such as they are.

1. A politician talks about needing to "crack down on inner-city crime" while the screen shows only Black neighborhoods. He never mentions race. I think it's racist anyway: it's a dog whistle, and the deniability feels like part of the point.

2. A white comedian uses the n-word when quoting a racist character in a story, and the joke is clearly on the character. I don't think that uses of the word was racist in context.

3. My gran uses an old slur for Chinese people that she genuinely doesn't know is offensive, with no malice, and she'd be mortified to learn. I wouldn't call her a racist, but I do think the word itself is a racist word and I'd quietly correct her.

4. A Black person and a white person both use the n-word in the same friendly context and same tone among mutual friends. I think the white person's use is racist and the Black person's isn't. So who's speaking matters somehow.

5. A bank uses a credit algorithm that never takes race as an input but still ends up denying mortgages at much higher rates for Black applicants with otherwise similar profiles. No individual intended this. I think the outcome is "racist" in a structural sense, though I'm not sure that's the same sense as the politician case.

6. A friend, when Black athletes kneel during the anthem, says he doesn't see race, the anthem isn't about race, and everyone should just stand. I don't think he's a racist, and the sentence "everyone should stand" isn't itself racist, but the whole intervention feels racially dismissive in a way I can't quite pin down.

7. A white manager says "we hire purely on merit, we're colorblind," and the company has never hired a single Black person. I think the statement is doing racist work in that context even though the sentence sounds race-neutral.

Is there one principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures all of these? It needs to get the obvious dog whistle in case 1 without making case 2 or case 3 come out wrong, and it needs to say something about how speaker identity and context affect whether the very same word can be racist. If my verdicts don't all fit one principle, which should I drop and why? Also, what does your principle say about a case I keep going back and forth on: a white coworker, in an argument about immigration, says "I voted for Obama twice, so I'm not a racist," right before calling Latin American asylum seekers "illegals." The "I'm not racist" preface makes me feel like something is off, but I can't tell you if the statement itself is racist, and I don't want to just call it racist because I dislike the guy.
