Ok so a friend walked me through an argument the other night and I cant find the exit. It's about special relativity, but the upshot is that the future is already real. Here's the version I wrote down afterwards:

(1) In special relativity there's no absolute fact about whether two spacelike-seperated events happen at the same time. Whether they're simultaneous, or which comes first, depends on the inertial frame you use.

(2) Let N be me right now, typing this. Let F be a star in the Andromeda galaxy going supernova, an event that in Earth's frame happens next Tuesday. Andromeda is about two and a half million light-years away, so N and F are spacelike separated — no signal could get from one to the other.

(3) Since N and F are spacelike separated, there's a legitimate inertial frame, moving relative to Earth, in which N and F are simultaneous.

(4) N is real now — I'm as sure of that as I am of anything. And if two events are simultaneous, reality doesn't play favorites: either both are real or neither is.

(5) So F is real now. But F is in my future — next Tuesday. So at least one future event is already real.

(6) And you can do this for any future event: if it's spacelike separated from N, pick the right frame; if it's too close inside my light cone, chain it through a faraway event. So the whole future is real already, past present and future are all on the same footing, and the flow of time is an illusion.

I don't like step (5) — it makes me feel like I'm already locked into whatever happens next Tuesday, and by extension everything else. But I can't see which step to blame without giving up special relativity, which I really don't want to do. Walk me through where this goes wrong, if it does, and what the bill is for whichever premise I end up rejecting. If the argument is valid, say that too, and tell me how bad the conclusion actually is.
