I'm a systems programmer -- I live in processes, threads, signals, interrupts, preemption, exception handling, watchdogs, and race conditions. Philosophy people keep going on about a case where a guy, Jones, is about to shoot someone. A neuroscientist, Black, has implanted a device that will detect if Jones is about to chicken out and will make him shoot anyway; but Jones shoots on his own, and Black never has to lift a finger. Philosophers say Jones was morally responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise, so the principle of alternative possibilities (you're responsible only if you could've done otherwise) is false.

Restate the whole Frankfurt/PAP situation in systems-programmer vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. I want the agent as a process, courses of action as code paths, Black's device as a supervisor thread or interrupt handler with a watchdog that only fires on a certain signal, actual execution versus what could have executed, scheduler determinism versus nondeterminism -- whatever mapping is actually forced by the argument rather than decorative. Dont smuggle in philosophical assumptions under the hood and call it a translation; if there's no counterpart in my world, say that straight.

Then give me the residue report. What doesn't survive the translation? In particular: when a philosopher says Jones couldn't have done otherwise, am I supposed to hear (a) given the full machine state, only one code path was reachable, (b) the binary had other branches but they weren't taken on this run, or (c) in nearby counterfactual runs with slightly different state, another path would've been taken? And does the Frankfurt argument only go through on one of those readings? I also want to know whether the fight between sourcehood people and leeway people maps onto anything I can actually decide in code -- like who owns a side effect versus what branches were live -- or whether that fight is partly an artifact of an underspecified could. Then tell me which way the residue cuts: is it that the philosophers need better specs, or that my vocabulary is too thin for what they're pointing at?
