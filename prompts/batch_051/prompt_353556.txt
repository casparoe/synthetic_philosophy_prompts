I'm revising the section on cause prioritization for my grad seminar on the ethics of giving, and I decided to actually write down the decision procedure I've been teaching off and on for years and run it past someone before I put it on a handout. Here is the list of commitments as I currently hold them:

(1) The right way to choose between causes is to direct marginal resources wherever the expected good per dollar is highest, full stop. (2) I teach the importance–tractability–neglectedness framework as the practical way to estimate that, so in class I write the expected value of a marginal dollar as roughly importance times tractability times neglectedness. (3) But I also insist that neglectedness is not a fundamental or separate consideration; its just a proxy for the fact that as more funders pile into a cause, the best remaining projects get used up and marginal impact falls. If I had a direct estimate of marginal impact, learning a cause was crowded would add nothing beyond what's already in that estimate. (4) I think importance should be measured as total good at stake, not per person affected, so a problem that harms twice as many beings is twice as important, other things equal. (5) On that measure factory-farmed animal welfare should dominate global health in importance, and I think it would be dishonest to soften the numbers just to avoid that conclusion. (6) Still, I give nearly all my own donation money to global health, because I think our estimates of the tractability of animal welfare work are too uncertain to bear the weight of the importance numbers. (7) For the same reason I don't prioritize existential-risk reduction, even though on the total-at-stake measure it looks like it dwarfs everything else, because the tractability there is close to inscrutable. (8) And I think this is legitimate epistemic modesty rather than status-quo bias: when the numbers are that soft, your allowed to prefer the robust, lower-ceiling intervention over the higher-ceiling one that might be a mirage.

I'd like a coherence audit of this whole set before class. Could you go through and say which commitments actually pull against each other, which only seem to because I'm using one word in two senses, and how bad each tension is? For the real tensions, please rank them by severity and say what the least costly fix would be and what it would cost. Then tell me which single revision would make the package most defensible overall, even if that means revising one of the commitments substantially.
