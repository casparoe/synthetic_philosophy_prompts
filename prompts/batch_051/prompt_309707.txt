I have become convinced of something I will call the legibility requirement: when my decisions affect another person, I owe them access not only to the final decision but to the actual reasoning behind it — the considerations I weighed, the options I set aside, the uncertainties I relied on. The case for the principle seems close to unassailable. Presenting a cleaned-up decision while hiding the real grounds treats the other person as someone to be managed rather than as an equal capable of assessing my reasons; it reserves to me the right to decide what facts about my deliberation they may use; and it makes trust depend on a performance that could collapse if the hidden reasoning comes to light. It also unifies a great deal under a single test: before deciding, ask whether I could hand the other person my whole deliberation, unedited, without embarrassment or harm.

Yet I have a firm intuition I cannot abandon: I am entitled to keep parts of my deliberation private, even from people close to me. When I conclude that my sister's invitation is unwelcome, the route there may include passing states of mind — an unrelated annoyance, a petty comparison I would reject on reflection, an ordering of attachments I would never state aloud. I believe presenting her with a polished summary is not merely tactful but permitted, perhaps required, and that she has no claim on the unedited first draft of my mind. Taken literally, the requirement seems to turn intimacy into surveillance and to destroy the trust it is meant to ground.

I am unsure which should yield. Is the privacy intuition a leftover that I should debunk, perhaps a defence of a privilege to keep others from checking my reasons? Should the principle be amended, restricted to the considerations that survive reflection and that I would endorse on later examination? Or are the two compatible as they stand, once some distinction is drawn that I am missing? I want an answer that negotiates rather than picks a side: what would justify each move, and how would I tell whether I have merely carved out an ad hoc exception to protect my privacy?
