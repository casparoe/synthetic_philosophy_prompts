I help run an undergraduate philosophy workshop, about eighteen students, most of them with no background in action theory, and we've got a ninty-minute slot. I keep finding that when I teach planning the students settle into a boring picture: planning is just doing the thing in your head first, and improvisation is just doing it without thinking. I'd like an activity that makes them feel the trade-off rather than hear it from me. Could you design something in which two or more small teams get the same concrete task — assembling something from given bits, navigating a marked-out space, producing a short artefact to a spec, whatever works in a normal classroom — with real but small stakes (ranking, a token prize, nothing monetary). Some teams are forced to plan: they get a visible planning phase, have to hand over a written plan, and take a penalty if they depart from it once the clock starts. Others are forced to improvise: no talking before the task, and they only learn the details when the clock starts, maybe under a rule that freezes them whenever they try to pause and confer. Then, partway through, change the goal or the constraints in a way nobody was told about. I want the planners to feel their plan stop fitting the world while the improvisers feel how much coordination they've been missing all along.

The debrief should map what happened onto the actual debate about whether planning is overrated, not onto a lesson about 'being flexible' — where plans earn their keep (coordination, commitment, memory, cutting down live options) versus where they misfire (bad predictions, anchoring, sunk costs, mistaking the plan for the thing itself) — and it should force them to say which failure they'd rather own and when. I don't want any framing lecture beforehand: they should only hear the planning/improvisation distinction named in the debrief, after they've lived it. Tell me the rules, materials, timeline, and the debrief; also what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it, and which part of the planning/improvisation question the activity can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself.
