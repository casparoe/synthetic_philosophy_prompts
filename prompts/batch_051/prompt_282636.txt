Okay so I've gotten a little obsessed with this after watching my company handle a failed product launch. They fired exactly one person, a mid-level manager, and then the rest of the org talked about it as if the failure literally left the building with her. And I kept thinking: it didn't. The failure is still here. We just moved it onto her.

I've been calling this "guilt conservation." The claim is that in a group, guilt—or blame, or moral taint—behaves like a conserved quantity. It isn't destroyed when someone is punished or expelled; it's transferred. A scapegoat is essentially a storage device: the group pours its accumulated guilt into one person, and the punishment or exile is the ritual that lets everyone else feel clean. Because the guilt is conserved, though, it doesn't vanish; it just gets parked in the group's relationship to that person, in the story about them. That's why the "closure" after a scapegoating never quite lasts—the same dysfunction comes back, and soon you need another one.

I also have a corrolary I've been calling "scapegoat capacity": each scapegoat can absorb only so much. Once a group uses someone up, the excess leaks back out as anxiety, new rules, factional blaming, or the need for ever more elaborate rituals. The two goats in Leviticus make more sense to me under this than under ordinary ideas of punishment: one goat is killed and one is sent off carrying the sins, as if sin has to go somewhere rather than just stop existing. Even at work: after the firing, the department didn't get healthier. It got quieter, and then after a few months we found a new person to fail.

So here's what I want you to do. Act as a professional philosopher who works on ritual, moral psychology, and the anthropology of sacrifice. Before you settle on a verdict, brainstorm two or three distinct ways to make "guilt conservation" precise, because I'm sure it can be read more or less charitably. Then tell me: is it a real concept, or just a physics metaphor stretched over ordinary resentment? Has someone already worked this out under another name? What does it get right, and where does it actually break—what does it count as a wrong or a transfer that isn't one? Be candid; I'd rather find out it's stupid than keep using it wrong.
