I live with three other people. Our landlord wants to sell the house and has offered each of us a cash-for-keys payout if we're all out by the end of next month. It might be a good deal or it might be bad: we'd each get about four grand, and rents nearby have dropped enough that moving could leave us slightly ahead, or it could leave us worse off once moving costs and the fact that our current rent is way below market are counted. We all want the same thing—not to lose a good housing situation—and we've each looked into different parts of it (one housemate's been browsing listings, I've been checking what comparable places rent for, another talked to a real-estate friend about what the landlord likely stands to make). The actual issue is our decision rule. Since we moved in, we've operated on the principle that any one housemate can make a time-sensitive call and the rest of us will honor it, because we used to miss deadlines waiting for everyone to reply in the group chat. Right now, two of my housemates and I lean against taking the payout, but the fourth is pretty enthusiastic about it. Under our rule, we take it: he just has to say yes. A friend told me that's not obviously crazy—if someone has actually looked at the numbers and come away positive, that's real information, and requiring all four of us to agree would mean one obstinate person could block a good move forever. But I can't shake the feeling that the rule is rigged in favor of acting, not acting well: the more people you add, the higher the odds that somebody's optimism will steer the whole group, even if everyone is individually sincere and competent. I cant tell if that's a real structural problem or just me being annoyed at getting overruled. If it is real, what's a better rule for the four of us?
