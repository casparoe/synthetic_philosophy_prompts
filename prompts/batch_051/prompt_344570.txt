Ordinary life treats some disputes over the application of a word as unserious even when every underlying fact is settled. The question whether a hot dog is a sandwich is the standard joke example of a dispute that is just semantics. Yet when astronomers redefined planet so that Pluto no longer counted, the same logical structure — a classificatory term applied to an object whose measurable properties everyone accepted — became a public controversy treated as a substantive scientific question. Is there a principled difference between the two disputes, or does their seriousness just track how much people care about the label? Give the strongest criterion you can for separating verbal from substantive disputes, test it on nearby hard cases (for example, a legal dispute over whether an e-scooter is a vehicle, or a philosophical dispute over whether numbers exist), and say whether the asymmetry in how we treat these two cases is justified or should be abandoned.
