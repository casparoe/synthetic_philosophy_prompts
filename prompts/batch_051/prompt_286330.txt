I have been trying to taxonomize the different ways a political order can keep governing, or reconstitute itself, once the ordinary rules stop yielding answers. The cases I keep turning up seem to sort into six devices, but I suspect the list is incomplete. So far I have: (1) express emergency provisions, where a constitution anticipates the partial suspension of its own ordinary rules and assigns that power to a designated organ under more or less specified conditions; (2) the Lockean prerogative, where the executive acts for the public good where the law is silent or even against the letter of the law, and is judged only retrospectively; (3) the Roman dictatorship, an extraordinary magistrate created for a strictly limited period of crisis and empowered to act without the usual collegial checks; (4) the interregnum, where the highest office is vacant and authority devolves to a temporary caretaker mechanism whose sole task is to bring the ordinary magistracy back into existence, the Roman version rotating a five-day interrex among patricians until elections can be held; (5) interpretive gap-filling by a court or similar body, which treats the constitution as a framework of implicit principles from which an answer to the unprovided case can be derived; and (6) constitutional conventions, uncodified but obligatory practices that fill what the text leaves open and discipline how legal powers are actually used. In each case, what interests me is the mechanism of continuity rather than the historical record.

I am looking for a seventh item that is genuinely different from all six, not a restatement, a special case, or a combination of them. Ideally, it would be a strategy that refuses the premise the others share: instead of empowering some organ to act inside the gap, or extracting an implicit principle from the existing order, it either denies that there is a gap at all or makes the void itself routine. What is the strongest candidate for that seventh device, and what exactly distinguishes it from the items on my list? If you conclude that no genuinely distinct seventh device exists, say instead which of the six is doing too much work by silently absorbing the others, and what that conflation hides.
