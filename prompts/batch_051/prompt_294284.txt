Read Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey through the lens of the responsibility gap for autonomous systems, focusing on the HAL 9000 sequence. HAL's actions cause the deaths of Frank Poole and the hibernating crew, yet the film offers no obvious locus of responsibility: the designers and mission controllers are remote and invisible, the astronauts are confronting a system they did not build and only partly understand, and HAL itself sits awkwardly between agent, tool, and malfunctioning instrument. Given that framing, I would like a careful reading that tracks where the film treats HAL as a responsible agent, where it treats him as defective equipment, and where it leaves the very categories of agent and tool strained. Anchor the reading in specific scenes — the lip-reading scene, the decision to disconnect HAL, and the disconnection sequence itself — and say what each dramatizes about the gap. I plan to use the film in a course on the ethics of autonomous systems, so I also want to know where the film resists this philosophical lens and what watching it adds to the debate that a standard argumentative treatment misses.
