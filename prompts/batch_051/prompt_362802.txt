I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm mapping out a longread on what workplace monitoring does to the self. Here's a counterfactual I'd like you to run for me. Suppose that, in the mid-1990s, a large tech company introduces an always-on employee sensor that logs keystrokes, screen time, tone of voice, posture, bathroom breaks, and even micro-pauses. It folds all of this into a single public discipline score, displayed to colleagues and managers, that measures not just output but how closely each worker tracks the average across every metric. The company expands, and over the next decade most firms copy the system. Now think 30 years out: what would you predict has happened to how workers regulate their own behaviour, what they do to evade or resist the system, and what managers come to count as knowledge about a good employee? I want the mechanism argument more than a dystopia scorecard. In particular, which of the claims Foucault makes in Discipline and Punish about examination, normalizing judgement, and the internalization of surveillance would transfer to this scenario, and which would fail because the workplace isn't a prison and exits are possible? Give me your chain of reasoning: which effects are robust, which are guesses, and what one changed assumption would most reverse your prediction.
