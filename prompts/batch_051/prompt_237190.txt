A friend and I just spent a long evening arguing about Parfit's repugnant conclusion, and I left suspecting that the standard escape routes—average utilitarianism, critical-level views, and person-affecting restrictions—all lean on the assumption that the huge population is nearly uniform. Let me offer a variant to test this. World A contains 100 million people, each at welfare 100, so its total welfare is 10 billion units and its average is 100. World Z* contains 100 billion people, but they are sharply unequal: 10 billion live at welfare 2,000, while 90 billion live at welfare -100, lives that are not worth living. The total welfare of Z* is 11,000 billion units and its average welfare is 110. Work through what total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, critical-level utilitarianism, and a person-affecting view would each say about A versus Z*. Which of these verdicts flip relative to the classic repugnant conclusion, which stay the same, and does the variant make the usual escapes from the repugnant conclusion look more or less robust?
