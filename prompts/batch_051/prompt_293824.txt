I've been trying to pin down what "meaningful human control" over autonomous weapons should require. My working list has four conditions: (1) an identifiable human understands the target and the surrounding situation; (2) that human has enough time and information to actually deliberate, not just rubber-stamp; (3) they can intervene or abort up to the point of no return; and (4) there's a identifiable human who can be held accountable for what happened afterward. Now take this borderline case: an operator launches a loitering munition after designating only a general area, not a specific target. The system loiters for hours and, in the last few seconds, picks its own aim point using a classifier. The operator can abort by holding a button, but the console shows only a grainy video feed and a countdown, not the classifier's analysis, so in practice operators almost never override. Does this case qualify under my four conditions? Be candid: does the case show that one condition is doing all the work, or that two of them are really the same condition in different clothes?
