My friend Dana has a whole theory about helping people, and after years of arguing with her about it I still can't predict her. Some data points:

- When there was a ballot measure to tax everyone in the state to pay for free breakfasts for kids in districts she's never even driven through, Dana campaigned against it and called it legalized robbery: nobody has a claim on money you earned.

- Her sister once drained her savings to pay off a cousin's debts after the cousin sobbed and said the people he owed would break his legs. The sister later said she got nothing out of it and felt hollow. Dana said the whole thing was wrong, and that giving because someone pointed a guilt gun at you isn't generosity, it's letting them use you.

- But Dana is always holding up a different friend who quit her job to care for her dad for two years after his stroke. Dana says that wasn't self-sacrifice, because her dad's wellbeing is part of her own life; in Dana's phrase, she'd have been miserable doing anything else, which is basically the highest compliment Dana gives.

- A guy at her gym jumped into a river for a kid he'd never met, and afterwards said he didn't decide anything, he just couldn't have kept standing there. Dana said exactly: that's why it was right, because he couldn't live with himself otherwise.

At Thanksgiving a cousin of hers announced he's donating a kidney to a stranger on the national transplant list. He says he doesn't know who'll get it, doesn't expect to feel good about it, and is doing it purely because he thinks it's his duty, end of story, and he'd do it again even if it took years off his life. What would Dana say about the kidney donation, and which earlier verdict is the closest analog? Also, what rule is she actually running, what other rules fit those earlier calls just as well, and what further case would settle which one is really hers?
