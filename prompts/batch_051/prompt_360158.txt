my friend and i spent all last night going in circles about whether a perfect forgery of a painting is actually worse than the original. i kept saying if two canvases are molecule-for-molecule identical, there is no difference you can point to that makes one better, and preferring the original is just snobbery about provenance. he kept saying that i would still be upset if i learned mine was the fake, and that reaction tells you something real about what we value. can you write me a sonnet were the octave is my side of the argument, the volta is the moment the forgery is discovered even though nothing visible changes, and the sestet has to say what actually changed without falling back on the original having a special aura. make the structure do the work: the turn should be where my friends point overtakes mine. it should be accurate enough that someone who works on aesthetics would nod along, no citations needed, and a few imperfect rhymes are fine.
