I've been reading these forums long enough to have seen every version of this argument get rerun, and last week someone started it again: is the statue and the lump of clay that makes it up the same thing, or are there two things sitting in the same place? Everyone talked past each other so badly that I want to turn it into one of those forced-choice quizzes for the blog, where people answer concrete cases and only get told their position at the end, rather than picking a label they already like. The audience is mostly smart general readers with no academic metaphysics background, so the questions have to be jargon-free: never ask whether someone is a colocationist, just ask whether there are two things in the same space or one, and what actually survives when you squash the thing.

Can you write me an eight-question quiz with a scoring key and a short paragraph for each result? Each question should be a small case in the same family: the classic one where someone buys a lump on Monday, shapes it into a statue on Tuesday, and squashes it back into a ball on Wednesday; one where the statue is insured for much more than the clay is worth and then gets squashed, and the owner asks whether they lost one thing or two; one where a restorer replaces all the clay bit by bit while keeping the shape identical. The case judgments should sort people into at least these leaves: the identity view (statue and lump are literally one object), the constitution/colocation view (two coinciding objects with different persistence conditions), hylomorphism, four-dimensionalism (the statue as a temporal part of the clay's spacetime worm), mereological nihilism (no composite objects), and a deflationary no-deep-fact view. The fracture lines I actually care about are: can two material objects fully coincide, does the statue or the lump survive squashing, and do composite objects exist at all. Also flag which pairs of leaves your questions can't seperate, and note any answer combination nobody occupies and whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. If a question is really sorting people by which label they already like, cut it.
