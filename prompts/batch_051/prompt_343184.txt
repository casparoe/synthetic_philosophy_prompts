I got into an argument with a friend who works in robotics about the frame problem, and afterward he sent me his version of what it comes to: "The frame problem is the problem of saying what does and doesn't change when an action is performed. If a robot stacks one block on another, it needn't re-infer that the table is still brown or that all the other blocks are still where it left them; you add frame axioms that list the non-changes so the robot can ignore them. So the frame problem is really just a bookkeeping problem about efficiently representing the effects of actions, and it was essentially solved with the situation calculus and successor-state axioms." I've also seen people carefully distinguish the frame problem from the qualification problem and the ramification problem, and philosophers write about it as if its the deepest puzzle about intelligence. Is my friend's summary fair? Walk through it claim by claim and tell me what's accurate, where it's been oversimplified, and whether the "essentially solved" part is right once you include the more philosophical readings.
