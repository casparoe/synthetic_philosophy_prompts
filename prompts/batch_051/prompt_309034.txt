In English class we read Orwell's "Politics and the English Language," and the thing that stuck with me was the causal direction of the argument: vague words, stale phrases and euphemisms are politically dangerous because the writer uses them to avoid picturing what he's actually saying, and the reader learns to not picture it either—so political language and political thought rot together. But now a ton of exactly that kind of prose (corporate apologies, politician statements, bureaucratic letters) gets drafted by LLMs. In that case there is no individual writer with a guilty conscience doing the evading; the model isn't hiding anything because it isn't thinking anything at all. Does Orwell's argument survive that change? It seems like the step where bad language corrupts thought depends on there being a mind on the other end trying not to think, so maybe the argument now has to attach to the person prompting the model or the institution publishing the text—but is that still the same mechanism, or does the argument just not bite when the evasive sentence doesnt have any individual thinker behind it? I'm less interested in whether AI prose is "bad writing" and more in whether Orwell's causal story still goes through.
