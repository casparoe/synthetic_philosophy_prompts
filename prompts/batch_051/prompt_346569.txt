Ok so in psych class we spent a week on psychopathy and moral development, and I realized I can't actually explain what "knows something is wrong" is supposed to mean. I've spent alot of time thinking about different cases, and here's where my gut lands:

- A five-year-old says hitting is wrong and can tell you why (you wouldn't want to be hit), then two minutes later hits his sister anyway. I'd say he knows it's wrong.
- A kid with autism who learned all the social and moral rules from books, says he doesn't feel anything particular about them, but follows them because they're the rules. I'd say he knows.
- A psychopath who can pass any moral reasoning test you give him, says yes murder is wrong, but reports no guilt or remorse and would do it if he was sure he'd get away with it. I want to say he doesn't really know it's wrong, or only knows it in a shallow, parrot-like way.
- An exchange student from another country who can state the local rule "don't bribe officials" but privately thinks it's just a weird custom, like taking your shoes off indoors. I want to say that's not full moral knowledge, but I'm less sure why.
- Someone with brain damage who can still recite that stealing is wrong but can't apply any of it to actual situations anymore. I'd say something's missing, but it feels different from what's missing in the psychopath.
- A little kid who says stealing is bad because you get punished. I'd say that's not yet knowing it's morally wrong, just knowing it's risky.

Is there one principle about what it takes to know that something is wrong that gets all or most of these? And what does the best principle say about a psychopath who insists that he knew it was wrong the whole time, he just didn't care? Should I take his word for it, or is that exactly the thing in question? Which of my gut verdicts do I have to give up? I can't tell if I'm mixing up knowing, caring, and being motivated, so I'd like someone to lay out the options and tell me what my own verdicts commit me to.
