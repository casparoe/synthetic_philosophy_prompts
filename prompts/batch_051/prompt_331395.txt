Ok so I have this thing I keep noticing and I want to know if it has an actual name, because the names I've heard don't quite fit.

First one: my school is drowning in AP classes. Genuinely nobody wants to take four or five APs — the kids taking them complain nonstop and the kids taking two feel like slackers. What happened was a couple years ago a bunch of people got waitlisted at good colleges (or so the story goes), so everyone decided they needed one more AP to be safe. But then everyone had that AP, so it stopped being a signal, so the safe number went up again. Now people take five to look like the old three. The move makes total sense for each kid: if you drop the extra AP and nobody else does, your the one judged against the higher bar. But the bar only stays high because everyone keeps jumping over it.

Second one is smaller. Group chat for our robotics club. It started as people posting when they finished a part. Then one guy posted a screenshot at like midnight and got praised as the grinder, so now the norm is you have to post visible proof of progress every couple days even if your part hasn't changed. Everyone has agreed, out loud, that it's fake and wastes time, and then the meeting ends and we all go post screenshots anyway.

So the pattern: each person does the sensible thing given what everyone else is doing, the sensible thing changes because everyone did it, the result is worse for everyone than before, and even knowing it's happening doesn't give anyone a way out that doesn't cost them personally. It's not quite tragedy of the commons, because we're not using up some shared resource — if anything the thing being used up is just the old lower bar. And it's not exactly a prisoner's dilemma, because there isn't one big choice to defect; it's a bunch of small escalations against a moving target.

Does this have a name? I've heard arms race and race to the bottom, but arms race sounds too warlike, and race to the bottom sounds like standards dropping, which is the opposite of what my school thinks its doing. If there's an established concept, I want the carve too: what makes this different from those nearby ideas, and where the edges are. Like, when is a bad stable situation just a coordination problem that one person could break by stepping out, and when is it this thing where stepping out alone just punishes the one who steps out? And if there's no name that actually fits, what would you call it, with a definition that doesn't accidentally swallow up ordinary bad habits or plain old coordination problems?
