I serve on a state clemency board. Every month we review petitions from death-row prisoners asking the governor to commute their sentences to life without parole; our vote is a recommendation to the governor. Guilt has already been settled by the courts; our narrower question is whether something about this particular case makes carrying out the sentence the wrong thing to do. I usually have the file for a few hours before the board meets. I am not trained in law or philosophy, but I have been reading on my own about the three ideas that keep appearing — retribution, deterrence, and the risk of executing an innocent person — and I cannot turn them into an actual way to vote. Retribution seems to say some crimes are so bad that the sentence should stand no matter what else is true; deterrence seems to be a fact about whole populations and therefore silent about the one file in front of me; and innocence risk can never be reduced to zero, so I need a rule for how much unresolved doubt should halt everything.

Please write me a reusable decision procedure I can run on each petition before I vote. Make it a sequence of questions, in the order I should ask them. For each question, say what would count as a sufficient answer to move on, and say whether a "no" stops the process with a recommendation to deny or just changes the path. Mark clearly which steps are judgment calls rather than facts and which should be handed to the full board as a values decision. Then run the procedure on three petitions: one convicted solely on the testimony of a jailhouse informant who has since recanted, with no physical evidence tying him to the murder; one whose guilt is certain and whose crime was the brutal murder of a child, but who now has advanced dementia and no memory of the crime or trial; and one who shot a store clerk during a robbery twenty-three years ago, has been a model prisoner since, and shows no measurable future dangerousness. Tell me where the procedure gives an answer I will probably dislike, and finish by saying plainly what the procedure cannot decide for us.
