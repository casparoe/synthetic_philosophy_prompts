A friend of mine, after a philosophy of perception course, summarized Sellars's attack on the "Myth of the Given" like this:

"Sellars argued that the foundationalist picture of empirical knowledge is incoherent. There cannot be a level of experience that justifies our beliefs simply by being directly felt or by presenting itself to the mind. For an experience to justify a belief, it has to be able to stand in an evidential relation to that belief, and only something conceptually articulated can do that; bare sensations cause beliefs, but causes are not reasons. So nothing is given to the mind as evidence, and no non-conceptual awareness can play a justificatory role. All awareness is therefore conceptually mediated. And because inner sensory episodes were supposed to be just such a non-conceptual given, Sellars concludes that the very idea of an inner sensory episode is part of the myth and should be abandoned, not merely reinterpreted."

Pay particular attention to whether the moves from "no non-conceptual item can justify" to "all awareness is conceptual" to "inner sensory episodes should be abandoned" are moves Sellars actually makes.
