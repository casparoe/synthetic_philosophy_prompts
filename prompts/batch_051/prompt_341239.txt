During a training phase, a participant in a concept-induction study was shown six cards labeled dax and no labeled non-examples. I will write a card as a list of colored shapes, with B for black and W for white, so {B(circle), W(square)} means one black circle and one white square; spatial position on the card is irrelevant.

The six dax cards were:
1. {B(circle), W(square)}
2. {B(triangle), B(triangle), W(circle)}
3. {W(square), B(triangle)}
4. {B(square), W(circle)}
5. {W(triangle), B(circle)}
6. {B(square), B(circle), W(triangle)}

She then classified five further cards as follows:
- {B(square), W(triangle)} — dax
- {B(circle), B(circle), B(circle)} — not dax
- {W(square)} — not dax
- {W(triangle), W(triangle), B(circle)} — dax
- {B(triangle)} — not dax

The next card is {B(circle), W(circle)}. Predict how she will classify it. Then lay out the two or three simplest rules consistent with her entire record, state what each rule predicts for this next card, give your best guess as to the rule she is actually using and why you favor it over its rivals, and describe one further card that would settle the matter between your favored rule and its closest competitor.
