ive been in the weeds on Goodman’s new riddle for a while and I think I have a homemade fix that might just be a rehash, so I want it checked. I’m working through Fact, Fiction, and Forecast, and there’s that passage where Goodman answers the grue problem by saying the difference between green and grue isn’t a matter of what’s in the world but of what predicates have actually been projected: entrenchment is, on his telling, a record of past projections, not a reflection of some deeper fact about the predicates themselves. I keep feeling like he gave up too early, so I’ve got a principle I call temporal extensional constancy, TEC for short. A predicate has TEC just in case its extension at a later time doesn’t depend on when the predicate was tokened or applied. So green has TEC: a thing that’s green at one time stays green later, no matter how we timed our uses of green. Grue lacks TEC: whether the sentence that an emerald is grue is true depends on whether the emerald was first observed before the future time, so the same object can drift across the extension just because the clock moved. My theory is that projectible predicates are exactly the TEC predicates, and entrenchment is just the downstream effect of us having noticed, over time, that TEC predicates pay rent. What I want to know is whether this is a genuine alternative to Goodman’s entrenchment story, or whether I’ve just reinvented something already in the literature, maybe about natural predicates or spatiotemporally uniform classes. Give me the three strongest objections to TEC as a criterion, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than just uncomfortable.
