I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and I've been stuck on physicalism in a way that feels different from the usual "hard problem" back-and-forth. The impasse is Hempel's dilemma, and it seems like the field has learned to live with it rather than actually resolve it. The dilemma: if "physical" means whatever current physics picks out, physicalism is almost certainly false, since current physics is incomplete and doesn't treat phenomenal properties as such. If "physical" means whatever some future, completed physics picks out, physicalism becomes near-empty: for all we know, the final physics could include irreducible experiences in its primitives, and then physicalism would be compatible with a position everyone meant to rule out.

Honestly, my hunch is that the debate stalls because the two horns answer different questions about what a definition of "the physical" is supposed to do. One side wants the term to be quasi-ostensive, tied to actual physics as it currently stands; the other wants a contrast term that mostly excludes souls, entelechies, and meddling gods. If that's right, the disagreement is about the dialectical role of physicalism, not about what exists, and more metaphysics won't settle it. But i keep worrying I'm just papering over a substantive question — for instance, whether physics has a determinate subject matter across theory change, or whether the idea of "future completed physics" is coherent enough to do any work. I'd like a diagnosis of the disagreement itself rather than a survey of proposed definitions of "physical." Is this a verbal dispute, a dispute about standards, a dispute about a hidden empirical or formal question, or is the question just ill-posed? And concretely, what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a reformulation, a reason to drop the question?
