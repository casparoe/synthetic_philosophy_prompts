Write a villanelle about generics and their exceptions — the fact that "ducks lay eggs" and "mosquitoes carry malaria" remain true despite the drakes that never lay an egg and the overwhelming majority of mosquitoes that never carry malaria. Choose one specific generic statement as the first refrain and a specific exception to it as the second refrain. The form should do the conceptual work: the refrains alternate as the stanzas cycle through other members of the kind, and then meet in the final two lines in a way that leaves the generic standing rather than refuted or revised, so that the poem's structure itself shows why a generic is a claim about the kind rather than a universal claim over its members. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of language would recognize a specific account of generics, and good enough that a reader who knows nothing about the debate would still find it a well-made poem. After the poem, add a short note of about 150 words that commits to the specific account of generic truth the poem assumes, gives the reason for treating the exception as compatible with the generic, and identifies where the poem takes liberties with the account for the sake of the form.
