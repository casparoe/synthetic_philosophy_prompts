Someone in a recent discussion about whether habitat destruction reduces wild animal suffering kept falling back on "nature knows best." Their line was that clearing a wetland or scrubland can't be net-good for the animals, because a system that emerged on its own will, almost by definition, be better than whatever humans replace it with. That's got to be packing together a bunch of different ideas about what "best" means there: best for species persistence, best for population health, best for total welfare, most stable, most evolved, most adaptive, or something else. Could you unpack "nature knows best" into the distinct precise claims it might be making, from the banal observation that organisms are adapted to their current environment up to the strong normative claim that natural states are welfare-optimal? For each reading, say whether it's trivial, substantive but question-begging, or just false, and which ones would actually support the anti-intervention conclusion in that habitat destruction debate. Also, say how confident you are in the overall assessment and flag the point you're least sure of.
