I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am working on a piece about whether Occam's razor can be made precise. As part of my reporting, I have been sitting in on a reading group led by a researcher in algorithmic information theory, who each week rules on a small induction puzzle: a finite binary string is shown, and several candidate programs compete to explain it. Her record, as I have kept it, is this. Faced with a 200-bit string that simply alternates 010101..., she preferred a short program that prints 01 forever to a fair coin that happened to land that way and to a 200-bit lookup table. Faced with the first 500 bits of the binary expansion of the square root of two, which I ran through a standard battery and which came out statistically unremarkable, she preferred a short program that computes successive binary digits of the square root of two to a fair coin. Faced with 100 bits I had recorded from a physical fair coin, she declined to prefer a lookup table containing exactly those bits: the table, she said, fit the data trivially but paid its full 100 bits, whereas the fair coin is a short probabilistic program and was at least as good. Faced with a 280-bit string consisting of the 7-bit block 0110100 repeated forty times, she preferred the looping-block program to both a fair coin and a 280-bit table. Her repeated gloss is that a hypothesis wins only when the compression it achieves pays for the cost of stating it.

The next case I want to test against her record is much shorter: the 8-bit string 01010101. The competitors are a program that prints 01 in a loop forever, thereby predicting alternating bits without end, and an 8-bit lookup table that stores the string and says nothing further. On one reading of her method, a repeating pattern is a repeating pattern and she will take the looping program; on another reading, 8 bits is too little data for the bookkeeping of a loop to earn its keep, and she will refuse to extrapolate. I would like a prediction of her verdict, the candidate rule or rules consistent with all four of her earlier judgments, what each candidate rule says about this 8-bit case, your best guess and the reason for it, and the further case that would most cleanly separate the candidate rules.

Please end with a final line beginning exactly "Verdict:" followed by one sentence stating the predicted verdict and the rule.
