I'm on a university research seed-funding committee. We've got a proposal from a group in our education school asking for $90,000 to run a six-month randomized pilot comparing two after-school math programs, with the winner to be rolled out district-wide next year. (Right now the district can only afford the cheaper one anyway.) One committee member keeps citing Good's theorem, saying the expected value of information is never negative, so the only real question is whether we can spare the $90k. Another member says that's a misreading: if the pilot's results won't change what we actually choose, the value of the information is zero, and $90k is just waste. Draft the committee's decision memo — fund, reject, or fund with conditions — in under 450 words. It needs a clear bottom line and an honest treatment of the strongest objection to whatever you recommend.
