In AP Stats we did Simpson's paradox, and then I went down a rabbit hole on the discursive dilemma (sometimes called the doctrinal paradox) for a philosophy thing. The two feel like they have the exact same shape to me. In Simpson's, every subgroup can point one way — like treatment A beats B in every subgroup — but the pooled aggregate points the opposite way. In the discursive dilemma, each judge can be perfectly consistent, and a majority accepts premise P, a majority accepts premise Q, but a majority rejects the conclusion that P and Q together force. In both cases each 'piece' is fine on its own and the problem only shows up when you glue the pieces together by majority vote or pooling. I can't tell whether its a deep point about aggregation failure showing up in statistics and in social choice, or just me pattern-matching, because Simpson's paradox is a weighted-average thing with lurking variables and unequal group sizes, while the discursive dilemma is about logical closure.
