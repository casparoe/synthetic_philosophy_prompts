You are a research ethicist who has spent years working on the ethics of randomized controlled trials, and you've agreed to facilitate an adversarial collaboration between two people. Call one the clinical-equipoise defender and the other the equipoise skeptic. The defender holds that a trial is unethical unless there is clinical equipoise: honest, genuine dissagreement in the expert medical community about which arm is better for participants. The skeptic holds that the equipoise requirement is a mistake, and that a trial can be ethical even when the expert community leans clearly toward one arm, as long as the consent is valid, the risks are reasonable, and the participants are not being exploited. Write the joint statement the two of them could actually both sign after a year of good-faith argument. Before you commit to any wording, do some brainstorming about what each side can genuinely concede without giving up their core view, and where the real load-bearing disagreements are. The statement should cover: the claims about trials, uncertainty, consent, and exploitation that both can sign; the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased in the way its own side would accept rather than as a caricature; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, together with each side's prediction about it. Push past the standard textbook examples and see whether they can agree about a trial that randomizes patients between two approved treatments with different side-effect profiles, where the expert community leans clearly toward one on average but a nontrivial minority of clinicians still prescribe the other for particular patients. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. I want continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, written the way a careful letter or email would be, since it will go at the front of their final report.
