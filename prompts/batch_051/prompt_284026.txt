I keep noticing a pattern in explanatory writing that I don't have a label for, and I'd like to know whether one exists — or, if none fits well, whether a precise one can be carved out. In economic journalism, bond markets are described as punishing a central bank because they doubt its resolve; the explanation does not require that any particular trader ever framed that thought, yet the aggregate behavior is treated as answerable to that rationale. In a biology lecture I heard that the reason fever has been maintained across so many species is that it makes the body less hospitable to pathogens; the immune system does not know this, and natural selection has no mind, yet the explanation is offered as more than a metaphor. In machine learning, one might explain a trained network's hidden unit by saying it is trying to pick up the horizon, or that the model took a shortcut because the optimizer found it cheap; no designer intended it, and the system never represented the rationale. These explanations support counterfactuals and predictions — if the market had really believed the central bank was committed, it would have priced assets differently; if fever were an incidental side-effect, treating it would not carry the tradeoffs it does — so the reasons seem to be doing genuine explanatory work despite being disconnected from any mind that could have them.

I can see rough affinities with as-if explanations, with invisible-hand stories, and with functional explanation in biology, but none of these labels seems to capture the exact phenomenon: a reason that explains a system's form or behavior while no agent, past or present, actually held it as a reason. Is there an established name for this? If so, what are its boundaries — for example, must some process like selection or competition have underwritten the rationality of the arrangement, or can a one-off physical system have such reasons? Does it matter whether a designer once had the reason, or whether individual participants had reasons of their own from which the aggregate rationale emerges? And how should one distinguish the cases where this kind of talk is legitimate and explanatory from the cases where it is merely misleading anthropomorphism? I am not mainly after the label; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
