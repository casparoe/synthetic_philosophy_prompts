An adoption panel may spend six months deciding whether a couple is fit to raise a child; the same couple can leave the panel's last meeting and conceive a child that evening with no assessment whatever. We scarcely notice this, even though we would call it unthinkable to run a nursery, teach a class, dispense medicine, or drive a car without first satisfying the state that one is competent and not dangerous. Nor is the state reluctant to judge natural parents once the child exists: it will remove a child for abuse or neglect, compel schooling, and override refusals of necessary medical treatment, all in the name of the child's right to an open future, the idea that each child should reach adulthood with a reasonable range of meaningful choices left open. In other words, the state judges adopters' fitness before the fact, judges natural parents' fitness after the fact, and refuses even in principle to judge natural parents' fitness before the fact. Is there a defensible justification for this asymmetry, or is it an unprincipled tribute to biological parenthood, a role whose standards the state is willing to enforce only after a child is already harmed?

I want the strongest case for the asymmetry made and then stress-tested, not a list of slogans. Consider, at minimum, these candidate justifications: procreative liberty and bodily integrity; the idea that the state has no say over a child who does not yet exist, whereas an adoptable child is already a ward or responsibility of the state; the poor predictive power of ex ante screening; the historical danger of eugenic or discriminatory gatekeeping around reproduction; the difference between declining to confer a good the state controls (a child in care) and intruding on a relationship the state did not create; and the thought that a future person has no right to be born only to screened parents. Test each against the cases that strain it hardest, such as: a person with a known history of child abuse who announces an intention to conceive; a couple rejected twice for adoption who then conceive naturally; state-funded IVF, in which the state is causally and financially involved in creating the child it would decline to screen for; compulsory schooling and court-ordered medical treatment, which are already ex ante impositions on all parents; and removal for abuse, which concedes that the state can judge parenting competently after the fact. Then return a considered verdict: keep the asymmetry, narrow it, reverse it, or dissolve it some other way.
