Two members of my research group, Lena and Marcus, got into a long argument last week after Marcus admitted that he had been using a large language model to produce and interpret most of the statistical analyses for his thesis. He said he had checked the outputs against a few hand-computed cases and the results looked right, but he could only explain at a rough level why the model had done what it did. Lena said this was a clear case of eroding his own understanding: Marcus now possessed correct outputs without the ability that understanding is supposed to give him, namely the capacity to answer follow-up questions about why the result held and whether it would survive small changes to the data. Marcus replied that we all already depend on instruments and experts, and the boundary of the individual mind cannot be where epistemic agency stops; Lena uses a statistics package whose algorithms she has never read, and she trusts a radiologist to read a scan without being able to do it herself. Lena answered that a statistics package is different from a model that generates an interpretation along with the computation: because the model's explanation looks like reasoning, Marcus was at risk of acquiring the illusion of understanding, and the distinctive danger is not the tool but the false sense of comprehension it induces. Marcus shot back that this confuses the causal origins of an idea with its epistemic status; if he can use the model's explanation to answer follow-up questions and see how it connects to his other beliefs, then the understanding lies in the person-plus-tool system that he is directing, just as it would if he had borrowed the explanation from a colleague. Lena said a colleague can be interrogated indefinitely and held accountable, while the model cannot be asked what it really means; when the system is a black box that emits text, there is no point at which the explanation is genuinely integrated with what Marcus already believes. Marcus replied that genuine integration is the right standard but it cuts in his favor, because integration is shown by what he can do with the explanation, not by where the explanation was produced, and Lena had merely asserted that he had not integrated it. They asked me to referee, and I honestly could not settle it.
