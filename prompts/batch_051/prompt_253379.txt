I'm the faculty speaker at my university's commencement, and I've been asked to talk to the graduates about hope. Heres my problem: a good chunk of the class are econ and data-science majors who have spent four years learning that hope is mostly a cognitive bias—it makes people update badly and underestimate risk—while the parents in the audience are expecting something warm and uplifting, not a lecture. Write the five-minute speech. It should build on the idea that hope isn't the same as an optimistic prediction: you can hope for something you think is unlikely, and the hope does its real work by keeping you oriented toward the outcome (noticing relevant opportunities, refusing to give up early) rather than by estimating that it will probably happen. It has to land for both the skeptical students and the parents, and it can't pretend that sustained, honest hope never gets crushed. Make the very last line a one-sentence summary of the argument, starting with 'Summary:'.
