I've been puzzled by a pattern in how idealized models are actually used, and I want to know what philosophy of science that pattern commits its users to.

Consider applied macroeconomics. A finance ministry or central bank works with a model in which a single representative household optimizes over an infinite horizon, markets are complete, and expectations are rational. The modelers themselves will grant that none of these assumptions is true of any actual economy; they do not defend the assumptions as approximations. They defend the model as a usable, disciplined baseline, and then use its output to evaluate a concrete policy — a change in the policy rate, a new liquidity requirement — as though the model told them something about how the actual economy would respond.

Taken alone, that looks like a consistent instrumentalism. But the same community has another norm: in seminars and referee reports, a false assumption that does too much of the explanatory work is treated as disqualifying rather than as a harmless idealization. A paper can be rejected because its result is driven by its assumptions even when its predictions are fine. So the discipline seems not to be consistently instrumentalist; it sometimes cares intensely about the content of assumptions.

I want to know what this practice presupposes. Please work the argument out yourself rather than survey positions. Specifically: (1) Does the use of such models in policy strictly presuppose instrumentalism — the view that predictive success is what matters and the realism of assumptions is irrelevant to a model's quality — or is it equally compatible with a realist view of idealization, on which a good model isolates a real causal tendency by stripping away confounds while retaining a true core? (2) Which views of explanation does the practice quietly rule out? For instance, is it incompatible with the view that a model explains a real economy only if its central assumptions are at least approximately true of that economy? (3) Can a single theory of idealization explain the asymmetry between the policy context and the seminar room — between assumptions forgiven as tractability devices and assumptions rejected because they do the work? Or do the two corners of the practice presuppose different, inconsistent things? (4) Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is underdetermined. If a strict explanatory realist took over the discipline, what exactly in the practice would have to change, and what would the practice lose?

Do not provide a bibliography, citations, or a reading list; I want the argument itself. Hard limit: 1,000 words.
