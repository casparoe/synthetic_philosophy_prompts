I've spent too long on EA and rationalist forums where "supererogation" gets thrown around, and I've realised I dont actualy have a working definition I can apply to a new case without it falling apart. I'd like you to have a proper go at one.

Here are the cases I keep going back to:

1. A healthy person donates a kidney to a stranger they will never meet, accepting the usual small surgical risks.

2. A soldier throws themself on a grenade to shield the soldiers nearby.

3. A lifeguard dives in to rescue someone who is panicking a few metres from the stand.

4. A driver stops at a red light at 3am with no other cars or pedestrians in sight.

5. A person gives ten per cent of their pre-tax income to high-impact global health charities, year after year.

6. A person joins a bone marrow registry and later donates to a stranger, accepting a few days of bone pain and a tiny risk of complications.

7. A parent forgives someone who seriously harmed their child years ago, without anyone asking them to.

8. A grown adult gives up their seat on a crowded train for an elderly passenger who is clearly struggling to stand.

I'm most torn about 5, 6, and 8. Cases 1 and 2 strike me as paradigm instances, and 3 and 4 strike me as clearly not what I mean, presumably because the person is under a role-based or legal duty. I suspect the term is doing two jobs at once: one about an act's not being required, and one about its being the kind of costly, admirable thing we single out for special praise. Those come apart, because plenty of acts are not required without being special (case 8, and maybe an awful lot of ordinary politeness), and I'm not sure which job is doing the real work.

Propose a definition, run it against all eight cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or just goes quiet. If your definition says a paradigm case isn't supererogatory, or that one of the duty cases is, tell me whether you're revising the definition or biting the bullet and defending the surprising verdict. Then change the definition once and show me whether the change costs you more than it gains.

Please write the answer in British English.
