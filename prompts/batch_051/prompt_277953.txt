Me and my friends in the linguistics club got into a dumb argument about whether "dived" or "dove" is the regular past tense, and it turned out nobody could actually say what a regular past tense even is. The obvious answer is "you add -ed," but that seems to sweep a lot under the rug. think→thought and sleep→slept don't add -ed, but nobody wants to put them in the same bucket as go→went. Our teacher says went is suppletive—the past tense got borrowed from a whole other word—while thought is a different kind of thing, and she mentioned that some forms got messed up by sound changes over time so they use to be regular and now just look weird.

But then I started thinking about verbs like sneak: my parents say "sneaked" and I say "snuck," and that "snuck" form apparently spread by analogy fairly recently. So did it start out as a mistake and become regular once enough people used it? What I want is a working definition of regular past-tense formation (or "rule-governed" past tense) that nails these: helped, walked, and googled are regular; went and was are not; and it gives a definite verdict either way for think→thought, bring→brought, sleep→slept, dive→dove vs. dived, sneak→snuck vs. sneaked, a borrowed verb (say someone "friended" someone online), and the fake verb "to bling"—is its past tense "blinged," "blang," or "blung"? Then tell me which of those verdicts you're least confident in, and whether "regular" is really one category or whether it's doing two or three jobs at once.
