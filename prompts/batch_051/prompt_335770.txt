I'm in a decision theory course right now and we touched on the idea that you can't cleanly assign probabilities to your own choices while you're still deliberating about them — that deliberation crowds out prediction, or something like that. I've been turning this into a little theory of my own, and I want it stress-tested.

I call it probabilistic precommitment. The claim is that when I say "I'm 80% sure I'll skip the gym tomorrow," I'm not predicting myself from the outside; I'm making the decision in installments. The 0.8 isn't an estimate of what I'll do, it's the strength of a partial decision I've already started making. On this view, assigning a probability to one of my own future choices just is a way of advancing the choice while posing as its observer. That would explain why it feels a bit off to announce "I probably won't go" and then, right after, decide: the so-called prediction already collapsed some of the deliberation it claimed to be reporting on. It also predicts that probabilities near 0.5 should feel like real open uncertainty rather than like being half-decided, which, honestly, matchs my own experience but might just be me introspecting badly.
