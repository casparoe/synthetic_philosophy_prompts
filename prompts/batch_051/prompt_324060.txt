I want to stress-test my reading of Nietzsche’s eternal recurrence. In The Joyful Wisdom, aphorism 341, a demon tells you that this life as you now live it will have to be lived again and again, every pain and every joy, in the same order. Please play a sharp, committed Nietzschean who treats the doctrine as a practical test of affirmation, not a cosmological claim about time really circling. Stay in character until I say stop, keep replies to a paragraph, and don’t let it turn into bland life-advice. Here’s my opening move: if the thought is only a test, then someone who shrugs and says “sure, let it all recur, I don’t care either way” seems to pass just as well as a person who actively loves their fate. What in the image of endless recurrence excludes mere resignation? Or can a passive person pass the test, and if so what has the doctrine achieved?
