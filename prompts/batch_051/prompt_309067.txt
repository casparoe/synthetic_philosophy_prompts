A colleague of mine works at the boundary of the philosophy of interpretation and biblical studies. For years I have kept a running file of her verdicts about whether a given number in scripture carries symbolic theological weight or is only an incidental quantity; I have recorded only her verdicts, not her full reasons. A commentary assignment now requires her to address a case she has never explicitly ruled on, and I want to know what her own record commits her to.

Her recorded verdicts are as follows:
- The seven-day structure of Genesis 1 is symbolically deliberate: seven functions there as the number of completion.
- The twelve tribes and the twelve apostles are deliberate schematizations of the people of God, not mere coincidences of the historical record.
- The forty days of the flood and the forty years of the wilderness wandering are formulaic spans of testing or purification.
- The 666 of Revelation 13:18 is symbolic: the passage presents the number as a code to be calculated.
- The 144,000 of Revelation 7:4 is symbolic: it is visibly constructed as twelve times twelve thousand within an already visionary passage.
- In contrast, she has denied symbolic weight to the 318 trained men of Abraham in Genesis 14:14, to the thirty-eight years of the man’s illness in John 5:5, and to the 969 years of Methuselah in Genesis 5:27, treating each as a reported count or a chronological span.

The new case is the precise number of fish brought ashore in the post-resurrection catch of John 21:11: 153. For what it is worth, 153 is the seventeenth triangular number, the sum of the integers from 1 to 17, just as 666 is the thirty-sixth triangular number. I am less interested in whichever symbolic meaning one might attach to 153 than in the structure of the judgment her record requires.

If she is to remain consistent with her own recorded judgments, should she now treat 153 as carrying symbolic theological weight or as a concrete narrative detail? Lay out the distinct interpretive principles that fit her prior verdicts equally well; say what each principle predicts about 153; identify the closest precedent in her record; state which verdict about 153 is best supported and why; and name the further case, drawn from the same scriptural corpus, that would most decisively distinguish the principles that remain live after the 153 case is settled.
