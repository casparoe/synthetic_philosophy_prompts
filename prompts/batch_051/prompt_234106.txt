I am a novelist writing a book that follows one family over roughly fifty years, and I intend the novel's central institution to be a privately governed floating city. I want the city's political and institutional trajectory to be the product of defensible mechanisms rather than authorial convenience, so I am asking for a reasoned prediction, not a story.

Set the scenario concretely. In 2032 a consortium completes the first phase of a purpose-built floating platform on the high seas of the central Mediterranean, outside the territorial waters and claimed exclusive economic zones of the surrounding coastal states. The platform begins with four thousand residents and is governed by a corporation registered in a major commercial jurisdiction. Every adult resident and every firm signs a compact: a contract that prohibits violence, theft, and fraud; commits signatories to binding arbitration before a private tribunal for contractual disputes; requires payment of a single monthly governance fee; and otherwise leaves residents free to purchase protection, education, health care, and additional dispute-resolution services from competing providers. The compact explicitly guarantees a right of exit, though using the platform's transport infrastructure to leave carries a departure fee. Nearby states tolerate the settlement but do not recognize it as a state, and they continue treating its residents as their own nationals for purposes of passports and taxation. Over the next five decades the platform grows, through in-migration and births, to roughly ninety thousand people.

Predict, with reasoning, how this settlement's governance will actually have evolved by 2082. I need the mechanisms made explicit and kept separate from the conclusions. In particular: which people and firms select in, and which select out; how the corporation's incentives change once residents face high costs of leaving; which public goods the platform manages to supply and which it fails to supply, with special attention to defense, contagious disease, environmental damage, and the treatment of children and incapacitated residents; how the surrounding states' posture changes as the settlement becomes harder to ignore; and whether the formal right of exit remains an effective constraint on the corporation. For each link in the chain, tell me whether it is robust, merely plausible, or a guess, and state what would have to be different for that link to fail. Conclude by assessing the three stock endings — the libertarian utopia, the company town, the failed state — saying which of them the starting conditions make least likely, and what change to the initial conditions would be required to make each of the others genuinely plausible.
