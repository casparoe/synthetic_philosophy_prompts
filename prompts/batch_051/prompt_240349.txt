I'm taking a jurisprudence seminar and we're trying to reconstruct an appellate judge's vagueness doctrine from her published opinions; our professor insists there's a single rule under the surface, but my study group is split. The votes we've assembled so far: she struck down a city ordinance making it a crime to "loiter in a manner that annoys, alarms, or harasses passersby" (no definition, no examples); she struck down a state law making "conduct offensive to public decency" a felony (again no definition, no examples); she upheld a statute making it a crime to knowingly possess a "controlled substance" without authorization, because the statute cross-referenced a detailed schedule that listed exactly which substances counted; and she upheld an ordinance against riding a "motorized scooter" on the sidewalk, where the ordinance defined "motorized scooter" by weight, handlebars, floorboard, motor type, and top speed. Half the group thinks she only cares whether the key term is defined somewhere; the other half thinks examples or concrete standards would be enough, and its genuinely unclear whether the earlier cases settle it. The bigger question we're supposed to answer is how precise a criminal law actually has to be, and whether a list of examples can do the same work as a definition.

The new case on the syllabus: a state statute makes it a crime to operate a drone in a public park "in a manner that disturbs or is likely to disturb the peace, repose, or enjoyment of any person." The statute doesn't define that phrase, but it lists five examples (introduced with "including, but not limited to"): flying within ten feet of another person, hovering over a picnic blanket, following someone after they asked the operator to stop, amplifying audio from the drone, and flying in a marked nesting area during nesting season. How should she rule if she's trying to be consistent with her own precedents? Lay out the candidate rules that fit her past votes, say what each predicts for the drone statute, identify which earlier case is the closest analog, and give your best guess at her vote with the reason for preferring one rule over the other. And since we're stuck, tell me what further case would cleanly separate the two rules — the one I should hope our professor adds to the reading.
