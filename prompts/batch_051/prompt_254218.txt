I'm a novelist. I'm trying to write a dialogue between two people standing in front of a war memorial: one insists a community is only morally serious if it keeps its founding atrocity at the center of public life—curriculum, plaques, apologies, anniversaries, the lot—while the other thinks that whole apparatus has become a way for the descendants of the perpetrators to launder themselves: they perform the past so they don't have to touch the present. I keep making the second one a glib cynic, so write the dialogue for me with two equally sharp views, with real back-and-forth. Each should answer the other's strongest point, not a softened version of it. Push the disagreement to where it gets genuinely hard—say, whether remembrance is a duty regardless of its effects, or only as good as what it does to people now. Don't make it dramatic; let the philosophy do the work. Afterwards, add a short note saying how confident you are in the arguments you've given each side and which point your least sure about.
