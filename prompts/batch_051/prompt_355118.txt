Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a priority monist and a pluralist about the structure of concrete reality. The priority monist holds that there is exactly one fundamental concrete object, the cosmos, and that every proper part exists but is grounded in, or derivative from, the whole. The pluralist holds that there are many fundamental concrete objects (the mereological simples, if any) and that composite wholes are grounded in, or derivative from, their proper parts. Both sides grant that tables, atoms, and the cosmos exist; they disagree about which direction grounding runs and which objects are fundamental.

The statement should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including claims about their shared explanatory target and why fundamentality is posited at all; (2) a precise statement of the residual disagreement, with each side's position phrased in wording that side would accept; and (3) a disagreements table whose rows are cruxes, sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, where for each crux both sides agree on what argument, formal result, or case bears on it, and each side's prediction is recorded in advance. In particular, address the cases each side must handle: entangled or nonseparable quantum systems, the possibility of gunk (no mereological atoms, so parthood descends without end), and the possibility of junk (no maximal whole, so composition ascends without end). Where a dispute is partly verbal, for instance about what a rival's position must mean by one object or fundamental, say which part and why.
