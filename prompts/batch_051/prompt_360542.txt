I am a PhD student working at the intersection of philosophy of mind and AI safety, and I keep noticing that discussions of artificial motivation slide between several different senses of "wanting" without marking the shifts. A thermostat tracks a temperature, a reinforcement learner is trained to maximize a reward signal, a language model can be usefully described as pursuing a goal in a story we tell about its behavior, and a hypothetical optimizer might resist shutdown or tamper with its reward channel even though no one trained those behaviors in directly. Biological organisms have motivational states because their ancestors were selected for them; an artificial system has no evolutionary history of that sort unless we deliberately supply one, so the standard etiological answer to "why does it want anything?" is unavailable. Before I evaluate specific theories of what an AI could want, I need the standard by which such theories should be judged.

Write the desiderata that an adequate account of non-evolved motivation must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. The list should at least take positions on the following: it must separate genuinely having a goal from being merely useful to describe in teleological terms; it must say whether a goal must be explicitly represented inside the system or may instead be read off from the system's dispositions across counterfactual circumstances; it must handle the emergence of instrumental subgoals such as self-preservation or shutdown-resistance from a terminal goal; it must distinguish a reward signal, an objective function, and a motivational state rather than collapsing them; and it must explain why a system that goes inert once a terminal goal is reached still counted as having that goal all along. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as a preference of a particular camp, flag any pair or subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that does the most to rule out cheap ascriptions such as the thermostat that wants the room at seventy-two degrees. I will apply the list to specific views myself; for now I need the evaluative framework to be as precise as possible.
