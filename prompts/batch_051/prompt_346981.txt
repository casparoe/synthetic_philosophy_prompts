I'm a philosophy PhD student working on moral status and artificial minds, and my advisor has a pretty consistent track record of verdicts I'm trying to extend. Dogs, cows, and octopuses count; insects she'll only go as far as 'maybe, probably not'; rocks obviously don't. A scripted NPC that makes a canned 'please don't' when you point a gun at it doesn't count at all. An NPC that remembers which players killed it and avoids them also doesn't count, because its just a look-up table underneath and she's firm that no one's home. A human-controlled avatar in an MMO counts, but only derivatively. A robot with a pain reflex and no inner life doesn't count. A stuffed bear a kid is devoted to doesn't count either, she says, whatever the kid feels. The new case she's tossing us: an NPC in a long-running MMO driven by a large learned model, which develops stable preferences, remembers individual players, seeks some out and avoids others, and acts noticeably worse after a player it regularly grouped with abandons it — and we're taking as given that it has no phenomenal consciousness. Predict her verdict, say which earlier case is the closest analog, and if her track record actually fits more than one rule and they disagree here, say what principle she'd be signing up for with each option, plus what further case would cleanly split the candidate rules.
