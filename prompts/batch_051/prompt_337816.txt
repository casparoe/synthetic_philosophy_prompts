Here's the standard story about why nudges are supposed to be compatible with liberal freedom, in a nutshell. Thaler and Sunstein's defense of nudges rests on roughly two features: the nudge is easy to avoid or reverse (the default is not a trap), and it operates in the open rather than by covert manipulation. So nothing like autonomy is threatened in the way mandates or outright deception would threaten it. Now suppose choice architecture stops being a generic default aimed at the average chooser and gets personalized instead. A platform has enough data to learn your particular loss-aversion quirks, the hour of day your self-control is weakest, and which framings reliably move you, and it schedules its defaults and prompts to land exactly then. Does the standard liberal defense survive this change, or did it secretly assume a roughly uniform nudge aimed at a roughly generic agent? Pin down which premise the personalization attacks, whether that premise can be repaired and at what cost, and whether whats left still deserves to be called a nudge.
