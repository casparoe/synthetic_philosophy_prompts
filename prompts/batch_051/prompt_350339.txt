I write about science and ideas, and I am working on a piece about how philosophers compare rival ethical theories. Please explain a specific conceptual issue that arises in that comparison: the tension between parsimony and fit with considered moral intuitions. In particular, discuss why a theory that traces all moral requirements to a single basic principle—for example, a duty to avoid harm or to universalize one's maxims—can appear both more theoretically virtuous and less faithful to the texture of actual moral life than a pluralist view, and then explain what would have to be true for simplicity to count as evidence that an ethical theory is true rather than merely as a practical convenience in using or teaching it.
