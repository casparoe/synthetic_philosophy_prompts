Me and my friend spent basically a whole evening arguing about whether a company itself can be blamed, as opposed to just the people inside it. She has strong views and they're weirdly consistent. Her track record so far: she blames an oil company that ran a deliberate campaign to manufacture doubt about climate science, because top executives signed off on it; she also blames a carmaker whose engineers built a defeat device with management approval. She blames a rail company after a derailment caused by decades of deferred maintenance, even though no single person decided to skip maintenance—the cost-cutting culture was real and every plant manager cut corners a little. But she does not blame a restaurant chain when one franchise manager harassed staff, even though the chain's reporting line was clearly broken, because the manager was violating explicit policy and acting for himself. And she does not blame a software company when an engineer shipped a patch without the required code review, against policy, and it caused a data breach; nobody above him knew.

Now she's got a new one to rule on: a bank where low-level branch employees opened a ton of fake accounts to hit sales quotas. The written policies obviously forbid opening accounts without consent, and senior management says they didn't know about the specific fake accounts, but the quota system was brutal and branch managers were rewarded for hitting numbers, so the behavior was completely foreseeable and went on for years. I think she'll blame the bank; our mutual friend thinks she won't, because the employees where low-level and were violating policy. Which way should I predict she'll go? I'm less interested in the final yes or no than in working out which rule or rules could fit her track record so far, which of her earlier cases is the real closest analogy, and what additional case would cleanly settle which rule she's actually running.
