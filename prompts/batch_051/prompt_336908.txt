The semantic camp thinks a slur like the n-word has, as part of its literal truth-conditional content, a derogatory component on top of the neutral classification — say, a semantic element amounting to contemptible or inferior. The pragmatic camp thinks the at-issue content is identical to the neutral counterpart and all the nastiness is carried pragmatically, by conventional implicature, presupposition, or expressive content (they don't agree internally on which). Draft the joint statment they could both sign. Format it as: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about slurs — offensiveness, projection from embeddings, behavior under negation and conditionals, reclamation and in-group use, cross-linguistic variation, acquisition; (2) the precise residual disagreement in wording each camp accepts for its own side; (3) a table of cruxes, where each row gives a concrete test case or piece of data both camps agree bears on the question plus each side's prediction written down in advance. Make sure the cruxes include one on embedding under negation and conditionals, one on reclamation or in-group uses, and one on a slur that lacks an uncontroversial neutral counterpart. Flag anything that looks like a merely verbal dispute.
