The constitution of a republic contains the following provision, which I quote in full: "Should the office of President become vacant by death, resignation, or removal from office, the Vice-President shall succeed to the office and serve for the remainder of the unexpired term." Nothing else in the constitution addresses succession, and the President serves a fixed four-year term that ends at noon on 1 March following the election, when the elected President takes the oath.

I am working through a case the drafters plainly did not anticipate. In the most recent election, Arlen was elected President and Beza was elected Vice-President, and the result was certified in December. In mid-February, before the March inauguration, Arlen died. On 1 March, the outgoing President's term expired, and no one took the presidential oath.

On 2 March, who, if anyone, is lawfully President? The three live claims I can see are: Beza's, on the argument that the clause covers Arlen's death just as it would cover the death of a sitting President, because Arlen was the person lawfully entitled to the office; the outgoing President's, on an argument from necessity or from the principle that the office may not stand empty; and no one's, on the argument that the quoted clause is silent and the constitution therefore produces an interregnum. I want to know what the clause itself requires once it is tested against the standard approaches — the plain words, the evident purpose, what its drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the text would take it to mean — and whether those approaches converge on any of the three claims. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
