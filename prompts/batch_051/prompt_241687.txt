I have no formal training in history or law, only a long-standing amateur interest in how different states handle their churches, and I would like help thinking through a counterfactual that I have been turning over on my own.

In 1949, when the Federal Republic of Germany was founded, the Basic Law carried over the older German arrangement under which recognized religious bodies—above all the Protestant regional churches and the Roman Catholic Church—may have the state collect a church tax from their registered members as a surcharge on the income tax; a member who formally records an exit from the church no longer pays it. Suppose that the drafters of the Basic Law had instead rejected that model and adopted something like the American approach for West Germany: no established church, no state-collected church tax, and the churches, their schools, and their hospitals and social services would have to depend on voluntary donations, ordinary state subsidies, and income from property they already held. Everything else about occupied and divided postwar Germany—the rubble, the refugees, the Cold War, the beginning of reconstruction—is held fixed.

Give me your best argued prediction of how West German religious life and church–state relations would look by, say, 1985, and why. I am less interested in a yes-or-no verdict on whether this world would be better than in the visible chain of reasoning about mechanisms, incentives, and constraints: how much did the church tax actually shape membership, institutional capacity, and political influence? Which outcomes would probably change under the counterfactual—membership rates, church finances, church-run schools and welfare provision, the standing of a large Christian Democratic party, the demand for secular social programs—and which outcomes would likely persist anyway because they rest on deeper factors? Please mark clearly which links in the argument are robust and which are guesses, and state what would have to be false about churches, voters, or parties for the prediction to be wrong.
