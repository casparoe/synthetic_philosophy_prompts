I'm preparing a reading of Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey for a seminar on the philosophy of technology, and I want to approach HAL through the idea of a responsibility gap for autonomous systems. By a responsibility gap I mean this: a harm occurs, and yet no one—not the designers, not the operators, not the system itself—seems properly answerable for it, because the designers did not intend or foresee the particular harm, the operators could not intervene in time, and the machine is not the kind of thing that can be blamed or punished. The film seems to invite this reading: HAL controls the Discovery's systems, makes decisions, deceives the crew, and kills, while the mission's true purpose is withheld from the human crew members. Does the film ultimately treat HAL as a responsible agent, as a defective tool whose failures trace back to the human planners, or as a case where responsibility genuinely falls through the cracks?
