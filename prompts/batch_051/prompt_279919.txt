I retired from physical chemistry a few years back and started sitting in on a moral philosophy reading group, mostly for the pleasure of watching smart people argue. One thing keeps tripping me up, and I've never found the language for it. In my old field, if a model needed a new free parameter for every anomaly, that counted against it, and a model that had to be patched with special clauses was treated as failing rather than flexible. But in the reading group I watched a friend lay out a moral theory that started as three duties — keep promises, don't harm, help others. Within an hour it had grown to five duties plus four "except when" clauses, and he couldnt state it in one sentence anymore. When I said it was starting to look like epicycles, he said something like: morality might just be that complicated, and a simpler theory would get the cases wrong. I couldn't tell him at what point the complexity itself should count against the theory, rather than just being the price of getting the cases right. So my question is narrow. I am not asking wether simplicity is a virtue in ethics in general, and I don't want just a label. Is there an established name for this specific configuration — the point or trade-off at which an ethical theory's parsimony stops being a merely aesthetic nicety and becomes a real, counting defect, or for the pattern of theories surviving by accumulating individually reasonable provisos? If there is a name, I want to know exactly what it picks out, how it differs from "the theory is ad hoc," and how it differs from parsimony as scientists use the term. If there is no name, what would you call it, and how would you define it so it carves at the right joint?

I'd appreciate the answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "nearest_existing_terms": an array of objects, each with "term" and "how_the_described_thing_differs"; give me the two to four closest concepts you'd reach for first.
- "best_fit": the single existing label you would use if you had to, or null if none fits well.
- "coined_term": a proposed new label if best_fit is null, otherwise null.
- "definition": a one- or two-sentence definition of the phenomenon itself, not just the label.
- "defect_criterion": the rule for deciding when complexity starts counting against a moral theory rather than just being an acceptable cost.
- "boundary_cases": an array of three or four short test cases, each with "case", "in_or_out", and "why".
