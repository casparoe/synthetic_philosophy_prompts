For a small working group on the long-run future of artificial intelligence, I have been asked to facilitate an adversarial collaboration between two researchers who disagree about the character of an AI takeoff. The first researcher holds the fast-takeoff view: once a machine becomes capable of doing AI research at a roughly human level, a tight software feedback loop can form, and the transition from roughly human-level to radically superhuman capability could occur in weeks or months, too quickly for human institutions to react. The second holds the slow-takeoff view: capability growth will remain spread over years or decades because it depends on hardware production, energy, data, organizational integration, and lessons that only come from deployment at scale, so the process will be comparatively visible and incremental.

I would like you to draft the joint statement such a collaboration could actually produce. It should open with the substantive claims both sides can sign—about what an intelligence explosion is, about which feedback loops matter, and about the conditions under which either picture would be true—written so as to avoid empty formulations. It should then state their remaining disagreement precisely, with each side's view worded as that side would accept, and it should identify any portion of the dispute that is merely verbal or definitional. It should then set out the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. Plausible candidates include the shape of returns to cognitive improvement, the degree to which AI systems can substitute for human AI researchers across the full research pipeline, and how strongly hardware, energy, and deployment bottlenecks throttle a purely software feedback loop. For each crux, the statement should say what observation or argument both sides count as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

Please write the entire document as continuous prose, in the register of a jointly signed open statement or a long letter, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
