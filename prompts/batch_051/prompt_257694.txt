I've been trying to cash out Rawls's original position in decision-theoretic terms and something seems off. He says the parties behind the veil don't know their place in society, but they are supposed to be rational in a fairly strong sense. Then he has them pick the difference principle. But the difference principle is basically maximin applied to shares of primary goods—it only looks at the worst-off position. Doesn't that mean Rawls is silently assuming the parties have extreme, maybe infinite, risk aversion? A rational agent behind the veil who can assign probabilities would presumably choose something like average utilitarianism with some risk adjustment, not maximin. So what's the actual argument that the original position yields the difference principle, rather than just assuming the conclusion by making the parties pathologically pessimistic? Or am I importing a framing Rawls would reject?
