I'm trying to sort out whether the lying/misleading distinction tracks my moral intuitions, or whether I should drop it. Here are my gut verdicts. When my aunt asks if I like the scarf she knitted, I say "It's really something" while thinking it's hideous: fine. On an intake form at a new clinic, I write "No" next to "Do you have any chronic conditions?" even though I have an anxiety diagnosis: not fine. When a colleague asks if I'm annoyed about being passed over for promotion, I say "I'm focusing on other things" while I'm actually furious and resentful: not fine. When the same colleague asks if I'm annoyed, I say "A bit" when I'm furious: fine. When my child asks if Santa is real, I say "Many people believe he brings presents" while not saying I'm one of them: fine. When my child asks if Santa is real, I say "Yes" when I do not believe it: not fine.

I keep wanting to say my rule is something like "never assert the contrary of what you believe, but it's acceptable to imply a falsehood with a true statement." But that does not explain why "I'm focusing on other things" felt not fine while "A bit" felt fine, and it probably mishandles the clinic form. What principle, or small set of principles, would actually capture this pattern? Which verdicts, if any, should I revise? And what does the principle you propose say about the case I keep going back and forth on: telling my elderly neighbor that her dog is at the vet when the dog actually died, to spare her from distress she cannot do anything about?
