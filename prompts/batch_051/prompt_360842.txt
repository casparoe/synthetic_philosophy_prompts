My brother-in-law just got back from Hargeisa and spent the whole visit calling Somaliland a country, which is the kind of thing I'd normally wave off, except I realized I couldn't actually explain why it isn't one. So I went and read up on it. The facts as I understand them: it declared independence from Somalia in 1991 after the central state collapsed, and it's governed itself ever since — police, army, courts, its own currency and passports, reasonably competitive elections — so it ticks the usual Montivideo boxes (population, territory, government, capacity to enter into relations). But for more than thirty years not a single UN member state recognized it. The closest anyone came was the January 2024 memorandum with Ethiopia, where landlocked Ethiopia got Red Sea access through Berbera and in exchange gave what I can only describe as a vague promise to maybe move toward recognition, which never materialized. So you have this thing that looks like a state in every everyday sense, and the international system mostly treats it as a rebellious part of Somalia.

I want to use this to get a handle on the recognition debate in international relations. As I understand it, the declaratory theory says recognition just reports a fact that already exists — if the criteria are met, you're a state, and others acknowledging it is a formality — while the constititive theory says the acknowledgment is what actually makes you a state in international law. Run Somaliland through both views and tell me what each one gets right and wrong about this case. In particular: where do the two theories actually disagree here, as opposed to talking past each other about the word 'state'? Does the Ethiopia memorandum matter to either theory — and should the informal dealings Somaliland has (foreign liaison offices, trade, security cooperation) count as 'recognition' in whatever sense the theories care about? And what does the case show about the theories: does it confirm one of them, refute one, or reveal that the question 'does a state need others to acknowledge it?' is actually several different questions stacked on top of each other?
