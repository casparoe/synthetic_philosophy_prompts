I keep running into the claim that the mind is a Swiss Army knife: a kit of specialized, evolved tools rather than a general-purpose computer. The analogy is usually made to do real work — to license the stronger, massive-modularity conclusion that we should stop positing any domain-general central cognition at all, and that what looks like general reasoning is just a confederation of task-specific modules. Yet Fodor, who did most to give mental modules their current shape, held that genuine modules are confined to peripheral input systems such as vision and language; central systems, he thought, are the opposite of modular — slow, unencapsulated, and sensitive to considerations from any domain. I would like an audit of how far the Swiss Army knife analogy carries. What maps onto what, and which features of a knife are quietly imported that a mind built only from Fodorian modules would lack? Could a mind made only of such modules do the global, abductive, belief-revising work the analogy is meant to explain away? And does the massive-modularity conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is there a better analogy for the intended point?
