I'm a software engineer, never took a philosophy class, so I want the test-design version rather than the jargon. I keep going in circles with a friend about beef vs. chicken. His claim is that, per calorie, beef causes less animal suffering than chicken, because one steer feeds a lot of people while replacing those calories with chicken means killing far more animals. My objection is that his ledger only counts the animals we meant to kill: it leaves out mice, voles, and insects killed during feed and forage production, the wild animals displaced or affected by pasture and cropland, and the fact that wild animals suffer in ways our normal accounting ignores. I've tried Fermi-estimating this and it just breaks. So: design the strongest concrete test or measurment plan for the claim that beef causes less animal suffering than chicken, all things considered. What exactly would you measure, in what kind of system, over what timeframe, and what result would favor beef versus what result would favor chicken? Then be honest about the residue—what the best version of this test still fails to settle, like how many mice equal one steer, whether a chicken's misery is comparable to a cow's, and whether wild suffering belongs in the ledger at all.
