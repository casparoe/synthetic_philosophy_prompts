I sit on a university disciplinary board. A registered student organization hosted a campus event at which an attendee over 21 smuggled in a flask concealed in a coat pocket. The organization had posted a no-alcohol notice, checked IDs at the door, and required attendees to sign a form acknowledging the prohibition, but they could not reasonably have detected the flask without physically searching each person. The campus code states that any registered student organization at whose event prohibited alcohol is found is responsible for the violation "regardless of the precautions taken," and the sanctioning guidelines list suspension of recognition as the baseline penalty. Half the board reads this as a strict-liability provision: the harm was real, the text is clear, and the rule exists to give organizations strong incentives to minimize risk. The other half reads it as requiring at least negligence or recklessness: punishing without fault treats the organization as a scapegoat for a harm it took reasonable steps to prevent, and cannot deter what was not detectable at acceptable cost. I need to circulate a written opinion before next week's meeting. Draft the memo: it should recommend a concrete disposition, explain the reasoning, and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side. In particular, address whether a strict-liability rule's deterrence rationale can justify punishing an actor who could not, at reasonable cost, have prevented the violation.
