Dōgen's doctrine of uji — being-time — holds, roughly, that time is not an independent container in which beings exist and events occur; each being and each occurrence is itself time, so that the arising and perishing of a thing is not something that happens to it within a temporal medium but is the very being of that time. Suppose Dōgen had been given a competent briefing on analytic metaphysics and asked to place himself with respect to three positions familiar from contemporary debates: presentism (only the present exists), eternalism (past, present, and future all exist equally), and the growing block (the past and present exist, while the future does not). What is the most defensible prediction of where he would land — one of these three, or a rejection of a presupposition all three share, such as the assumption that reality is composed of times standing in earlier-than and later-than relations? Identify which elements of uji push toward each option, where each reconstruction would strain, and give your final prediction with a rough weighting.
