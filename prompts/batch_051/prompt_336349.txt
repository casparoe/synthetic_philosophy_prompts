I am helping our school's debate coach compile a record of the rulings she has given on our team's fictional political ethics cases, and I want to check a prediction before our next session. Her rulings so far are as follows.

She judged a mayor justified when, without city council approval, he moved money from the parks budget into emergency levee repairs days before a hurricane, hiding the transfer under a misleading line item; the levee held and a low-lying neighborhood was spared.

She judged a president justified, though not cleanly, when he ordered a drone strike on a terrorist cell that also killed a civilian passerby; the strike prevented an attack that was being planned on a shopping district.

She judged a state senator justified when she threatened to publish an embarrassing but true story about a colleague unless the colleague withdrew an amendment that would have gutted a rural hospital funding bill; the colleague withdrew the amendment, and the story was never published.

She judged a police chief justified when she sent an officer undercover as a health inspector to photograph records in a suspected arms dealer's warehouse; the photographs led to a conviction, and no one outside the investigation was harmed.

She judged a governor not justified when he spent public money on surveillance of the one journalist most critical of him, to dig up material to discredit her; nothing useful was found and no public benefit came of it.

The new case is this. A defense minister authorizes immigration officials to keep an innocent man in detention — the brother of a terrorist suspect who has gone into hiding — even though the brother has done nothing, because the suspect is expected to surrender to free his brother and would then likely be captured before he can carry out a bombing he has planned. The minister is warned that the detention is almost certainly unlawful and that the brother may spend months in custody.

Predict how she will rule on this new case, and say which of her earlier cases is the closest analogue. I want to know which principle she is implicitly applying. My candidates are: (a) a lesser-evil rule that permits harmful or deceptive means when they are necessary and proportionate but draws an absolute line at deliberately sacrificing or seriously harming an innocent person as a means to an end; (b) a fuller consequentialism that asks only whether the expected outcome is best; and (c) a dirty-hands view on which such means can be right but still wrong the official and leave a remainder that she must acknowledge. The phrase "justified, though not cleanly" may matter a great deal, or it may just be a loose description of a costly trade-off. Which candidate best fits her record, and what additional hypothetical case would most sharply test which candidate she actually holds?
