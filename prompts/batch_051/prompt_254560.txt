A university's Campus Expression Board has decided these cases over the past few years, and it now treats them as precedents:

(a) It allowed a student group to host an invited speaker with extreme political views; many students found the views offensive and protested outside, but the talk went ahead in a ticketed auditorium with advance warning.
(b) It sanctioned a student who distributed flyers falsely stating that a named professor had embezzled research funds, a falsehood that led to the professor being placed on leave pending an investigation.
(c) It allowed a theater group to stage a play that was advertised in advance as offensive, containing sexually explicit content and insults directed at religious figures; some audience members walked out.
(d) It sanctioned a student group that gathered outside a named student's dormitory on several consecutive nights, chanted that they were coming for the student, and banged on the windows until the student moved off campus.
(e) It allowed a political group to cover common-area bulletin boards with posters bearing a slogan many students called tasteless or insulting to their ethnic group, though no individual was named.

The next proposal is this: a campaign group wants to hang, over the main entrances of every dining hall and library for one week, large banners displaying sexually explicit images and insulting statements about a religious tradition, with a caption stating that the point is to offend as many people as possible. Students could avert their eyes, but they cannot use the dining halls or libraries without passing under the banners. The banners name no individual, threaten no one, and incite no action.

If the Board wants to be consistent with its own precedents, should it approve or reject the proposal? Work through the rule or rules that best fit the earlier decisions, such as a strict Millian rule on which mere offense is never enough to justify restriction, a captive-audience offense rule, and a targeted-harassment rule. Say what each rule predicts, state which precedent is the closest analog, and explain what principle the Board would be adopting either way.
