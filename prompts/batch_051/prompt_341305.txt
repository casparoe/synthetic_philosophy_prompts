ok so i've been reading about cultural evolution on the side and i want to know if my views hang together. here's where i'm at:

1. i think 'meme' is more than a metaphor: some cultural stuff (phrases, song hooks, coding practices, tool techniques) actually replicates — there's a thing that gets copied, and the copies cause more copies.
2. but i also think most real-world cultural transmission is reconstructive: people infer the point or function of what they see and rebuild their own version, so copying is noisy and often low-fidelity, not a byte-for-byte copy.
3. i think high-fidelity copying is a precondition for cumulative culture: if every copy is a blurry reconstruction, improvements can't pile up over generations.
4. but i obviously think cumulative culture is real (handaxes to microchips), so the fidelity has to be there somehow.
5. i also think selection doesn't need discrete gene-like units; it can act on continuous, blended, overlapping variants, like a quantitative trait.
6. but then i can't say what the "unit of selection" is in culture, and i worry that without units the meme idea collapses back into a metaphor.
7. my hunch is it doesn't collapse — a population-level process can be genuinely Darwinian even if the units are fuzzy or nested (a phrase inside a routine inside a whole practice).

wich of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do? rank the real tensions by severity, tell me the cheapest fix for each, and then tell me which single revision would buy me the most consistency overall.
