Things get weird because the days can be bought and sold internally at whatever price coworkers agree on, and unused days roll over year to year (or cash out at a low fixed rate). So instead of the unit deciding to walk out as a unit, you'd get something more like an open-source labor action: whoever wants to spend their days walks, and everyone else keeps working.

I keep flipping on wheter this guts collective action or just makes it more flexible, and my coworker thinks it's obviously a union-busting scheme disguised as flexibility. Walk me through how it actually plays out. What does it do to low-paid vs high-paid workers, to free riding (the people who sold their days keep working and still get any raise the strike wins), and to the employer's incentives — could management just buy up strike days, and should the rules let them? What happens to bargaining power when the total pool of available strike days, and their going price, becomes public? Where does the thing get gamed first, and can the obvious holes be patched without turning it back into a plain collective strike?
