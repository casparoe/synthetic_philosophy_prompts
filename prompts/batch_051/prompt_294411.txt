Ok so I've been going through the tool-analysis in Heidegger, the readiness-to-hand vs present-at-hand distinction, and I want to sanity-check whether a case from my own life actually fits or whether I'm just forcing it. Here's my working list of criteria for something being ready-to-hand equipment: (1) you encounter it by using it, absorbed in some task, rather than by inspecting it as a thing with properties; (2) what it is is defined by its in-order-to, what it's for, plus a whole web of references to other equipment, materials, tasks, and whoever the work is for; (3) when it works it withdraws, and when it breaks down it suddenly shows up as something present-at-hand, obtrusive, just-there.

Now the case. I've been pair-programming with an AI coding assistant for about half a year. On a good day it genuinely disappears: it autocompletes a function and I accept it without thinking, the way I don't think about my keyboard. It's caught up in the same referential whole as my editor, the tests, the codebase, the teammates I'm building for. But its failures are strange. A hammer fails by getting too heavy or breaking; this thing fails by answering. It emits confident, plausible code thats subtly wrong, or answers a question with a tone that's just slightly off, and then I stop and stare at it as an output-producing thing instead of coding with it. It also talks back when I ask it to explain itself, which a hammer never does, and sometimes I catch myself treating it like a junior colleague rather than a tool. So my question: does an AI pair-programmer count as ready-to-hand equipment on those criteria? Go criterion by criterion, be honest about where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and say whether you think this case stretches the readiness-to-hand/present-at-hand distinction to the point where it needs a third category, or whether the strain is just me misusing the concept.
