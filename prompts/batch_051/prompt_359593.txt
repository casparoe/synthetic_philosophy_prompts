I am putting together a prompt for a project on the political and philosophical dimensions of the attention economy. I would like an analysis of a particular regulatory mechanism, using William James's account of attention from The Principles of Psychology as a lens. In the chapter on attention, James argues something like the following: attention is the mind's selective possession of one object or train of thought, and it entails withdrawal from competing objects; voluntary attention is an effortful, repeated achievement rather than a steady state; and the capacity to bring back a wandering attention is closely tied to self-control and character. I am paraphrasing rather than quoting, but I want you to take that account seriously as a premise for the analysis. Now consider this proposed rule: a jurisdiction requires that any online service whose principal revenue comes from advertising must make its recommender systems, autoplay, and variable reward features off by default. Users may enable them only after a twenty-four-hour waiting period and only by writing a short statement of what they intend to get from the algorithmic feed; after sixty minutes of use, the feed locks and presents a plain chronological view for the rest of the day. Treat this as a mechanism to be analyzed, not as a position to defend or attack. What does it incentivize among users, among the companies, and among advertisers? How would it be gamed, and what second-order effects arise in the surrounding ecosystem? Are the most serious failure modes fixable without gutting the scheme, or do the fixes create their own problems? Please write the answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write an email or a letter.
