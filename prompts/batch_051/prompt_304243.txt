I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is this. Work on whether a metaphysical dispute is merely verbal usually asks whether, under a charitable reinterpretation of the disputed terms, the two sides would turn out to accept the same claims. But the hardest metaontological disputes — over composition, persistence, or fundamentality — look less like disagreements about what the disputed expressions refer to than disagreements about which concepts are worth using at all. I suspect our diagnostic tools are built for the first kind of disagreement and not the second, and that the distinction between reference-level and concept-level verbalness has not been drawn cleanly. Turn this into a fellowship proposal, with no citations (I will add them), structured as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the question stated precisely; (2) why existing ways of separating verbal from substantive disputes leave that question open; (3) the approach and its main phases, and why this approach rather than applying existing diagnostics to more cases; (4) what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure; (5) a realistic scope for one year.
