Here's a claim I keep circling around and I'd like to know how one could test it. Schrödinger was influenced by Advaita Vedanta and held, roughly, that consciousness only ever shows up as one, never as many: the plurality of separate minds is something we infer after the fact from seeing many bodies and interacting socially, not something we directly experience. Now imagine someone defends a more modest, partly empirical version of that: the sense of being a separate, bounded self is a construction or an inference laid on top of experience, rather than a direct readout, so there's no direct experiential evidence for consciousness being fundamentally split into distinct subjects. How would you test that? I want a concrete design (from cognitive science, split-brain or other neurology, meditation research, developmental or cross-cultural psychology, wherever it fits best), a precise statement of what result would favour the 'constructed self' reading and what result would favour the 'separate selves are directly given' reading, and an account of why those two readings are hard to seperate. Then say what part of Schrödinger's original claim — the properly Vedantic bit about there being only one consciousness in reality — no test of this sort could settle. Please write the answer in British English.
