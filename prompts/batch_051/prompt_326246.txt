Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated interlocutors about whether cultural variants genuinely replicate and whether “meme” is more than a metaphor. The trigger is the following passage from Darwin’s The Descent of Man, paraphrased without quotation marks: Darwin claims that the struggle for existence also operates on words, with the more convenient and efficient forms tending to prevail, and that their pattern of survival is natural selection.

One interlocutor should defend a strong Darwinian replicator view: words, tunes, and techniques are high-fidelity units that produce copies, vary occasionally, and get differentially retained, so their population dynamics is genuinely analogous to natural selection, not merely a loose metaphor. The other should defend a cultural-attraction view: cultural transmission is typically a process of noisy, pragmatic reconstruction rather than discrete copying; the stability of a cultural variant is better explained by its fit with shared psychological biases and the structure of the problems it solves, not by self-replicating particles. They should not talk past each other. Have them work through at least one concrete case—such as the evolution of word forms, dialect changes, or serial reproduction of a story—testing whether talk of replication does or does not add explanatory value. They should each concede points where relevant and sharpen the disagreement. Use British English. Keep the dialogue to at most 800 words.
