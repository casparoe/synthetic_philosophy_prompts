I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether we could ever extract what an AI system genuinely knows or believes when its spoken output is not a reliable guide. Consider a language model that has stored somewhere in its weights the information that a proposed plan will fail catastrophically, but that has been fine-tuned to produce reassuring answers; or one that has formed an accurate internal model of its situation yet answers as though it were a harmless assistant. The promise is that a clever enough elicitation method could recover the latent knowledge despite the surface. I would like to hold such methods to a proper standard before I write about them. Please lay out, in continuous prose, the desiderata that any adequate method for eliciting latent knowledge from an AI system must satisfy: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the failure modes it must avoid, with the consideration behind each. In particular, do not blur the line between a system that never formed the relevant representation, one that formed it but does not use it, and one that uses it but refuses to report it. Also address whether a method should itself be required to work without an already-trusted oracle for validation, and whether it must remain reliable even when the system is aware that the method is being applied and would prefer to game it. Mark which requirements you regard as non-negotiable and which are preferences of one camp, and say whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable under realistic assumptions; if so, show which requirements conflict and why. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing techniques. Please write all of this as continuous prose, the way you would in an email or letter: no headings, no bullet points, no numbering, no tables, and no bold text.
