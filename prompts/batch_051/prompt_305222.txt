I am an undergraduate serving on my university's academic integrity council and am currently taking a course on moral responsibility in which we have been reading about complicity: the conditions under which a person can be blameworthy for wrongdoing she did not cause or carry out. A case on this week's docket puts that question unusually sharply, and I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's hearing.

The case concerns a first-year physics student. She noticed that her lab partner had, without her involvement, edited several cells in their shared data spreadsheet so that the measurements matched the predicted curve. She did not alter or fabricate any data herself, did not draft the portion of the report that relied on those results, and did not discuss the edits with her partner afterwards. The falsification was discovered by the teaching assistant. Our honor code's complicity provision makes a student who knew of or reasonably suspected an integrity violation and failed to report it liable to the same penalty as the student who committed the violation, up to and including suspension. The student admitted that she suspected the edits were fraudulent, but she says she feared retaliation, believed the lab partnership and friendship would end if she reported, and insists that her silence was not itself an act of dishonesty.

The council is divided. Some members see her silence as a form of passive participation in the fraud: the project and the grade were shared, and by saying nothing she allowed the fabricated results to be submitted as though they were also hers. Others see the provision as imposing an unreasonable duty of surveillance over one's peers, punishing her for a wrong she neither caused nor intended, and doing so on a par with fabricating data.

Please draft the memo I will circulate. It should recommend a concrete finding—exoneration, a reduced sanction, or the full equal sanction—with explicit conditions and a time frame, and it should reason through the complicity issue rather than simply applying the rule. I especially need a serious, accurate treatment of the strongest consideration on the side the council does not adopt, since the losing members will have to be moved by it.
