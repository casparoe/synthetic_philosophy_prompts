Suppose that starting next spring, a cheap, side-effect-free pill comes on the market. For about twelve hours it removes the felt badness of sadness and grief while leaving everything else alone: someone who has just lost their mother still knows she died and still judges that this is a terrible loss, but the ache itself is gone. It doesn't make you happy, and it doesn't touch fear or anger. Within a few years, taking one after a death or a breakup is about as routine as taking ibuprofen after you tweak your back. I want a reasoned prediction of how this plays out over the next two or three decades, not a story. Look especially at funerals, close relationships, art, and moral learning, but follow whatever chains of mechanism seem load-bearing. For each main effect, say how confident you are, what produces it, and what would have to be true for you to be wrong. Also: where would you expect a norm of 'you should take it' or 'you shouldn't' to develop, and why? Answer in British English, please.
