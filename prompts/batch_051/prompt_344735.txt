I have been trying to settle a question about biological individuality, and I keep running into a tangle. A Portuguese man o’ war is usually described as a colony made of many specialized units that develop from one fertilized egg, with some units handling feeding, others stinging, and others reproduction. That sounds almost exactly like how a single animal body works: one fertilized egg produces many cells and organs that divide the labor of staying alive. So I do not see what makes the man o’ war a colony rather than one organism whose organs happen to be called zooids. Could you explain the difference and give me a committed answer about whether it should count as one organism or many? It seems like if it comes from one egg and behaves as one coordinated whole, it must be one individual, and calling it a colony is just a historical habit.
