I’m drafting an essay about safe Pareto improvements and would like comments on the argument before I tighten it up. The essay’s central claim is that the category is doing more normative work than people admit, because what counts as a Pareto improvement depends on a baseline and on whose preferences are taken at face value, and those are exactly the contested distributional questions the concept is supposed to bracket. My current line is that a policy can look like a safe Pareto improvement under one specification of the status quo and a clear harm under another, so the concept is less of a neutral constraint and more of a way of laundering a particular entitlement structure. I also have a section on uncertainty and ex ante versus ex post safety: if we don’t know ex post preferences, a change that is ex ante safe for everyone can still leave some people ex post worse off, and a change that is ex post safe may require transfers that are not themselves credible. I suspect the useful notion is something like safe Pareto improvement relative to a specified set of preferences and a specified decision-theoretic stance, but then it inherits the familiar problems from social choice and welfare aggregation.

My question is whether this is a fair reading of the concept, or whether I’m attacking a strawman that no one actually holds. I’d especially appreciate comments on whether my uncertainty section is just restating the difference between Pareto efficiency under risk and Pareto efficiency after the state is revealed, and whether that distinction is less damaging to the idea than I think. One request on format: please respond in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if you were writing me an email or a letter.
