I'm taking a course on the way historians have begun to treat non-human things — epidemics, climate, volcanic eruptions — as participants in history rather than mere background. We've read about the claim that the Black Death's bacterium reshaped European labor and property as much as any parliament did; about the 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora and the cold, failed harvests, and hunger of 1816; and about the Little Ice Age's effects on crop choices, revolts, and settlement. The phrase "non-human historical actors" keeps coming up, and I cannot tell whether it is a precise category or an evocative metaphor.

I'd like you to propose a working definition of "historical actor" (argue for "historical agent" instead if you think it is the better term) that sorts the following cases correctly. Some I am confident about: a foreign minister signing an alliance is a historical actor; a crowd storming an armory is; a pebble dislodged on a mountainside is not; the Earth's daily rotation is not. The cases where I need the definition to do real work are these:

– Yersinia pestis in the Black Death;
– the 1815 Tambora eruption, considered through the cold, failed harvests, hunger, and migration of 1816;
– the Little Ice Age as a whole — a slow multicentury cooling with no single onset and no single event;
– the annual Nile flood, insofar as it anchored the Egyptian calendar, irrigation, and perhaps the standing of the state;
– a price signal, such as a spike in grain prices that coordinates strangers' responses without anyone intending it;
– a high-frequency trading algorithm whose orders trigger a flash crash.

Give a definition you are willing to defend, run every case through it, and mark the places where it yields what feels like the wrong answer or no answer at all — then either revise the definition or defend that verdict. I want a committed position and your reasons, not a survey of options. I'm especially worried about this: if a volcano can count as an actor, does the definition smuggle in the environmental determinism historians warn against, or can it hold volcanoes and germs as causes and constraints while still reserving something for intention or choice? Finish by saying which of your verdicts is least secure, and why.
