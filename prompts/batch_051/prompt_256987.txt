Compare two positions in the Korean Four–Seven debate about how the emotions hook up to li (principle) and ki (the material or energetic stuff of actual occurrences). On Toegye's side, the Four Beginnings—compassion, shame and dislike, deference, and the sense of right and wrong—issue from li, with ki following along; the Seven Emotions—joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, and desire—issue from ki, with li riding on top. On Yulgok's side, the Four Beginnings are not a seperate kind of emotion at all: they're just the morally proper cases of the Seven, and in every case it's ki that issues while li is only the ground or norm. Compare the two. Is the real dispute about metaphysics, moral psychology, or exegesis of one line of Zhu Xi—and could the two positions come out as mostly verbal, or would something substantive be lost if you collapsed one into the other? Which side, if either, gets the better of it once you distinguish those layers?
