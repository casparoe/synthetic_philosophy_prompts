I am trying to work out when someone has the standing to blame another person for a wrong, and I find my own case-by-case reactions pulling in different directions. Here are my verdicts on seven cases.

- A friend who constantly lies to his partner, and who berates me for a small lie I told. I do not think he has standing to blame me for this; his hypocrisy robs the blame of its force.
- A former smoker who quit a decade ago, telling a current smoker that smoking is weak-willed. I think he does have standing. His past does not undermine his blame, because he now lives by the norm he asserts.
- A complete stranger, with no relationship to me, who reads that I broke a promise and blames me for it. I think she has standing; she correctly identified a wrong, and that alone seems to me enough for her to blame it.
- Someone who has never broken a promise, but honestly says he would have done the same in my position if he had been tempted, blaming me. I think he lacks standing, even though he is not a hypocrite in the ordinary sense.
- A colleague who encouraged me to take the loan I later defaulted on, now blaming me for the default. I think he lacks standing; he helped produce the very wrong he now condemns.
- A moral philosopher who publicly says my action was wrong, though he is arrogant and greedy in his own life. I still think he has standing to blame me for my specific wrong; his unrelated vices do not disqualify him.
- My partner, whom my broken promise directly wronged, blaming me for it. I think she plainly has standing, and her blame has an extra force the stranger's blame lacks, though it is the same standing rather than a different kind.

Is there a single principle about standing to blame that captures these verdicts, or at least most of them? State it precisely, explain which case motivates each of its clauses, and then apply it to a case I have not listed: a person who blames a friend for cheating, but who cheated years ago in a different relationship and now finds the idea of cheating genuinely unthinkable. Finally, tell me honestly which of my listed verdicts, if any, your principle forces me to overturn.
