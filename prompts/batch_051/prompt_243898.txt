I'm on the city council of a mid-sized city, and we have to sort out a weird one. On the books since 1911 there's a misdemeanor ordinance against fortune-telling, palmistry, and similar services for money. It hasn't been enforced in living memory — nobody on council can remember a single prosecution — and there are at least six or seven storefront tarot readers and astrologers openly advertising downtown, a couple of them in city-run markets. Now a downtown business association is petitioning us to do something about a new psychic shop that's drawing crowds and blocking the sidewalk; their petition asks us to "enforce the law equally" and it name-checks the other shops, so they're not just after the newcomer. At the same meeting we have a seperate petition from a civil liberties group asking us to repeal the ordinance as vague and effectively dead.

Council is split three ways. Some members say the obvious answer is repeal: a law that's gone unenforced this long is dead in every way that matters, and reviving it now just because a business group is annoyed would be arbitrary. Others say that's backwards — it's still the law, and a council that picks which laws to enforce is doing exactly the thing that makes people cynical about government. A third group wants what's basically been the status quo: leave it on the books and quietly not enforce it.

The city attorney has warned us that we can't just instruct the police not to enforce a valid ordinance; if we want it dead, we have to repeal it, and if we don't repeal it, nobody can promise it won't be enforced next month by some future administration. So the live options look like: (1) direct resumption of enforcement, with a public notice and warning period first; (2) repeal outright; or (3) leave it on the books and let the city attorney's office make the call case by case, which is just formalizing what we've been doing.

Draft the decision memo I can bring to council. Give a clear recommendation among those three (or a defended variant), deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side, and end with a bottom line we can vote on. Don't just wave at words like 'desuetude' or 'rule of law' — I want the memo to actually say why a law's non-enforcement over time should matter for what we do now, if it should matter at all.
