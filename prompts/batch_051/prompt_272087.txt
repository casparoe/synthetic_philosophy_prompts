Clinical practice guideline panels routinely convert descriptive findings into instructions. They review trials showing, for example, that a drug lowers HbA1c or reduces a composite event rate, and they conclude that clinicians should prescribe it or that screening should be offered. The panels carefully distinguish quality of evidence from strength of recommendation, and they concede that values enter somewhere in the transition from evidence to recommendation. Yet the transition itself is rarely articulated as a premise: the document moves from data about outcomes to an ought without stating the normative step that licenses it. That move is the is-ought gap in action; and when the outcome is described as a "clinically beneficial endpoint," it can look like the naturalistic fallacy too, as if "beneficial" were already doing normative work inside a descriptive sentence. With surrogate endpoints the gap widens: panels will recommend a drug on the strength of a change in a laboratory marker, defending this by saying the marker is validated because it tracks outcomes that matter. That defense is still descriptive, and it does not by itself yield a prescription.

I want the implicit metaethics of this practice reconstructed, not a defense or attack on it. Specifically: does the practice presuppose a naturalistic reduction (that certain health outcomes just are good, so the ought sits inside the description), a Humean view with a suppressed normative premise (that the listed outcomes are worth pursuing), a constructivist view (that the panel's standing generates the norm), or is the practice philosophically underdetermined because it never has to commit? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits well with. Then tell me what would have to change in guideline production if the practice were forced to comply with a strict ban on deriving an ought from an is: would value premises have to be made explicit, would the evidence-review and recommendation-writing functions have to be separated, and would any recommendations survive? Finally, say how confident you are in that reconstruction and identify the single point you are least sure of.
