I'm a novelist, not an academic philosopher, so bear with me. My current book follows a woman who works for a ministry that computes, for each citizen, the single action they have most reason to take. To write the last act, I needed to pin down the narrator's actual view of practical reason, and somewhere in the third draft I realized the notebook I'd kept for her was just a list of things I apprently believe myself. Now I'm not sure whether I've given her a coherent position or a heap of attractive ideas that don't quite fit, and I'd like an audit before she has to defend it on the page.

The position, as it stands in my notes:

a) The question what I should do, all things considered, has a single answer in any given situation; saying that a moral ought and a rational ought can come apart is a confusion, not a deep discovery.
b) My own life going well is a source of reasons for me that doesn't borrow its force from anyone else's good. If I were the last person alive, it would still be a mistake, for me, to waste my remaining years.
c) Other people's well-being is also a source of reasons for me, and not merely because it serves my own. From an impartial point of view, equal pains matter equally no matter whose they are.
d) Sometimes the impartial side simply wins: a clear-headed person can be required to give up a very large share of their own good to prevent vastly greater harm to others, without expecting anything back.
e) But sometimes the personal side wins: it can be fully rational to prefer a substantial gain for yourself or your family over a somewhat larger gain spread thinly across strangers, and I'd distrust someone who had no preference in such a case.
f) And yet I keep thinking there is normally a fact of the matter about which side carries more force. Reasonable people who deliberate well mostly converge, and when they don't, the culprit is partiality, missing information, or sloppiness, not a crack running through practical reason itself.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look as if they do, and what is the least costly revision that would make the whole set consistent, and what would that revision cost? If you can order the genuine tensions by how hard they are to fix, that would help. I can feel something is off, but I honestly can't tell which commitment is doing the damage.
