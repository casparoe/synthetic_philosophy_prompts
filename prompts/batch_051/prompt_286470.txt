Me and my roommate have been going back and forth about markets in things that feel like they shouldn't have a price, and I'm trying to figure out whether my gut tells me anything coherent. Here's where I land. Selling a kidney to a stranger for $40,000, both people informed, surgery goes fine: I still think it should be illegal, even though they're both better off. Getting paid $50 to donate plasma: totally fine, I've done it. Selling your vote in a national election for $100: not fine, and I'd punish the buyer more than the seller. A hardware store tripling the price of generators after a hurricane: not fine. A contractor charging double his usual rate to rebuild after the same hurricane: fine, even if his own costs haven't doubled. Paying healthy volunteers $6,000 to enter a Phase 1 drug trial that has real side effects: fine. Selling a spot in the DMV line: fine, I guess, though I'd feel like a sucker for not paying. A rich family buying a building for a college in exchange for their mediocre kid's admission: not fine.

My rough rule has been something like: a market is wrong when the money corrupts the thing being exchanged, or when poverty makes the choice basically coerced. But I can't get it to fit the patterns. The kidney/plasma pair is the worst for me — both are body products, both carry some medical risk, both can be described as helping someone, but one feels corrupting and the other totally ordinary. And if its about risk, then the Phase 1 trial should be worse than selling a kidney, but I don't feel that way. The generator/contractor pair bothers me too: both are just charging more because a disaster made something scarce, but one feels like extortion and the other like fair overtime.

So is there a principled account that captures most of these, and if so, which of my verdicts does it make me give up? I'd also want to know what it predicts for cases I keep dodging — paid surrogacy, selling your organs after you're dead, or a healthy person selling a kidney specifically to pay for their kid's surgery (would that be worse or less bad than selling it to spend the cash?). And please say where your own reasoning is shakiest and what argument would change your mind about the principle you end up with.
