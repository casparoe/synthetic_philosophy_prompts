I keep flip-flopping on whether there's any defensible sense in which a sentence can be bad grammar, as opposed to just grammar of a different variety wearing a social judgement. I want to approach this by first settling what a theory of grammatical correctness would have to deliver, where adequate means good enough to tell me when, if ever, it's fair to call a sentence a mistake. So: give me a numbered list of exactly eight items, each a sentance or two, no introduction and no conclusion, in British English. Items one to six should each give one desideratum plus the case or observation that forces it, and say whether you think it's non-negotiable or just a camp prejudice. Item seven should name the pair of desiderata you'd bet cannot be jointly satisfied, and why. Item eight should give a one-sentence verdict on how a strict prescriptivism and a strict descriptivism each fare against the list. Feel free to use examples like the double negative, between you and I, singular they, or literally as an intensifier, but each case has to earn its place — this shouldn't read as a list of pet peeves. And don't settle the debate; I want the measuring stick, not the measurement.
