I've somehow ended up on the committee for a small annual prize at my institute: the money goes to the research proposal the committee judges "most likely to get us closer to the truth" about a messy, contested empirical question in our field. Trouble is, when I asked the rest of the committee what "closer to the truth" is even sposed to mean when every serious contender will almost certainly be false in some respect, I got five incompatible answers and a lot of hand-waving. So before we open nominations, I want the criteria settled. Write me the desiderata for an adequate theory of verisimilitude (truthlikeness): the full list of jobs any such theory has to do, with the motivating case or problem behind each one. I know the rough history already — Popper wanted to make sense of false theories being better or worse approximations to the truth, and his own proposal had the embarassing result that no false theory comes out closer to the truth than any other — so skip the recap and give me the demands. It has to rank plainly false theories intelligibly (Newton vs. Aristotle, say), it has to not reward safe near-tautologies that are true but contentless, it must not flip its verdicts when the same claims are redescribed in different words, and it should make sense of how real science gets somewhere via idealizations and successive corrections even while each stage is, strictly, false. For each desideratum, say what case or puzzle forces it. Also tell me which of these demands are negotiable and which are not, and flag any pairs that look like they can't be jointly satisfied. Then give me a quick scoreboard of how content-based accounts and similarity-between-possible-worlds accounts do against your list — but keep it to the reasoning, no reading list or citations; I can track down the papers later.
