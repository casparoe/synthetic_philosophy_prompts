i'm writing a short position piece for a philosophy of biology reading group and wanted a consistency check before i circulate it. my current commitments are:

(1) I'm a physicalist about the whole show: fix all the microphysical facts and you've fixed every other fact, full stop.
(2) But I also think higher-level explanations can pick out real causal patterns — a mating display, a market panic — and aren't made epiphenomenal by the fact that it's all particles at the bottom.
(3) I find the crane/skyhook distinction compelling. skyhooks, mind-like designers smuggled in at the base, are no explanation; cranes are just existing mechanisms redeployed incrementally to build new complexity.
(4) I think culture and deliberate design are cranes, not skyhooks. once natural selection gives us language and planning, institutions and artefacts become real causes in their own right, with no miracles added.
(5) I think a good chunk of evolutionary psychology is greedy reductionism: it insists on a dedicated adaptation for behaviours more plausibly read as byproducts, learned routines or cultural inventions, and it ignores how multiply realisable many of those higher-level patterns are.
(6) But I also think greedy reductionism is only a methodological sin. given (1), the microphysical facts do in principle determine the cultural facts, so the greedy folks are wrong about what's worth explaining, not about what exists.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and which single revision leaves me with the most defensible package?
