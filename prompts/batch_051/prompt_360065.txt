I recently started using a decision-support system at work that gives me normative recommendations about how to handle difficult cases: it argues for what I ought to do, but I am free to ignore it, and the system cannot do anything in the world except produce text. I caught myself thinking of the system as having made a poor moral judgment, and then I realized the blame did not obviously make sense if a moral agent must be capable of acting on its judgments. I have since worked out the following argument, and I would like you to tell me whether it is valid and, if it is not, exactly which step fails and why.

1. A being is a moral agent just in case it is the kind of thing that can properly be held responsible for the judgments it offers to others.
2. A human consultant who can only offer recommendations, and who has no power to carry them out, can still be properly held responsible for a negligent recommendation; her inability to execute the advice does not remove her responsibility for the advice itself.
3. What grounds the human consultant's responsibility is a set of capacities she has: she forms judgments by weighing reasons, she can justify or revise those judgments when challenged, and she addresses them to others as reasons for action.
4. A text-only decision-support system has the same three capacities: it weighs considerations, it can justify or revise its recommendations under challenge, and it presents its recommendations to a user as reasons for action.
5. Therefore, by parity with the human consultant, such a system can be properly held responsible for its judgments even though it cannot act on them outside of language, and so it is a moral agent.

I keep feeling that step 4 moves too quickly, but I cannot say exactly what capacity the human consultant has that the system lacks without simply assuming my conclusion. Please tell me whether the argument goes through; if it does not, identify the precise step and the reading under which it fails, and if it does go through, say what the conclusion really commits me to.

Please write your answer entirely in continuous prose, the way one would write an email or a letter: no headings, bullet points, numbered lists, bold text, or tables. You may of course refer to the steps by their numbers.
