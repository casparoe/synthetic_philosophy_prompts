Suppose panpsychism is true in the form most often defended: every fundamental physical entity instantiates at least some minimal form of experience. It then looks as though a single human body contains on the order of 10^28 candidate microsubjects—electrons and other fundamental constituents—whose states are constantly being rearranged as the body moves, metabolizes, and dissipates heat. If each of those microexperiences carries even a tiny negative valence on average, ordinary human activity would seem to be causing an aggregate of suffering that dwarfs every human-scale harm put together, and the natural conclusion would be an ethics of stillness: minimize physical rearrangement at almost any cost. Do panpsychists have a principled, non-ad-hoc way of blocking this inference, or is panpsychism quietly committed to that conclusion? Spell out what would have to be true about the relationship between microexperience, valence, and moral patienthood for the catastrophic result not to follow.
