Please give me your credence in the following thesis, and show your work: not just the number, but the decomposition that produces it.

Thesis (P): The distinction between possibility and probability is one of degree, not kind. In any fixed context, an assertion of the form "It is possible that p" in natural language is best modeled as the claim that the probability of p exceeds a contextually determined threshold, which may lie anywhere at or above zero. The relevant probability function is supplied by the flavor of modality in play: credence for epistemic possibility, objective chance for physical possibility, and so on. Where no probability function is ordinarily recognized, as with many metaphysical possibility claims, the thesis holds that one is nonetheless implicit and recoverable from contextual standards. On this view, probability is the more fundamental notion: "possibly p" is a coarse report of much the same quantity that "probably p" reports finely, and probability cannot in turn be reduced to qualitative possibility without importing non-modal, measure-theoretic machinery.

Precisification: read "best modeled" as the account of the entailments, compatibility judgments, and embedded uses (under negation, quantifiers, and conditionals) of modal language that best trades off empirical adequacy against simplicity; take the thesis to be a claim about the semantics of natural language rather than directly about metaphysics, unless you flag how your number would change for the metaphysical reading. One thing that would count as settling the thesis in either direction is a standing demonstration that one direction of reduction handles the core data more cleanly than the other.
