Student answer: "DNA really is a code, not just a metaphor. Transcription and translation show this: each three-base codon maps to a specific amino acid or a stop signal, so a gene specifies the exact sequence of amino acids in a protein. The mapping is arbitrary, and that's what makes it a genuine code rather than a mere chemical reaction. Nothing in the chemistry of the codon CAG forces it to stand for glutamine, just as nothing about the sound 'dog' forces it to stand for dogs. The cell is therefore encoding a message. DNA also carries information in the Shannon sense: its sequence is highly nonrandom, and you can measure it in bits, since the genome is a low-probability arrangement with high information content. When a ribosome reads the mRNA transcript and adds the matching amino acid, it is behaving like someone who knows the code and is decoding the message. So information really does flow from DNA to RNA to protein, just as Crick's central dogma says, and DNA literally contains instructions for building an organism."
