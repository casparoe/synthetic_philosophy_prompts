I'm putting together a handout for a discussion group on whether stuff that happens inside virtual worlds can count as real harm, and I want to sort a pile of cases from 'definitely a real harm' down to 'not a harm at all, just a bad afternoon.' Could you rank the cases below and give each one a score out of 10 for how real the harm is (0 = no real harm, 10 = just as real as the offline equivalent). For each case, say in a sentence or two what's doing the work in your score: money, physical pain, attachment, permanence, consent, ownership, community, or whatever it is. Then tell me where you'd draw the line between a real virtual harm and one that's merely felt, and whether talk of 'real objects' helps or just confuses things.

The cases:

1. A player uses a rule exploit to take another player's collection of rare cosmetic items, built up over six years and worth roughly $3,000 on resale sites, and then sells the collection for cash.
2. In a VR combat game with haptic feedback suits, a player repeatedly kills a stranger's avatar specifically to trigger the suit's mildly painful electric shock, doing it purely to drive the stranger off the server.
3. The volunteers running a small text-based multiplayer world announce they're shutting the server in two days. A couple hundred regulars lose a world they'd logged into nearly every night for fifteen years; one of them had written descriptions for several hundred rooms, never backed up.
4. Someone gets into a private in-game memorial for a guild member who died and replaces the monument with cruel messages.
5. During a virtual concert, an attendee is flooded with explicit threats through the platform's chat until she logs off and afterwards won't attend similar events. The messages exist only on the platform and she was never in physical danger.
6. A player's account is banned by a false positive in the anti-cheat system. The character had 4,000 hours played. The company later admits the error but says accounts can't be restored, so the character is just gone.
7. In a virtual world for sharing art, someone copies another creator's sculpture block-for-block and sells the copies for in-world currency; no real money changes hands.
8. An older sibling deletes a kid's single-player world that the kid and their dad had been building together for two years. It's unrecoverable; the kid is phisically fine.
9. A streamer organizes raids of "griefing" on new players, destroying their starting bases in a competitive survival game until many of them uninstall.

A few things I'd like beyond the ranking. Which distinctions do the most work in your ordering—individual vs communal, exchangeable for cash vs not, escapable vs unavoidable, chosen vs imposed? Are there any two cases where one has more real-money loss but the other has more real harm? And which of your rankings would you hold onto hardest, versus which could a good argument talk you out of? I want the reasoning, not just the list.
