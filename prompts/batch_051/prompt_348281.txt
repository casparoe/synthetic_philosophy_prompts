Write me a satirical postmortem, roughly 600-700 words, on the blog of a fictional smart-home company (call it whatever you want) whose whole brand is frictionless living. Their explicit marketing line is that a well-designed device withdraws from attention: you never notice the thermostat, the lock, the doorbell — the house just works around you. Two days ago their cloud backend went down for six hours and every gadget in customers' homes suddenly started blinking, ignoring commands, or forgetting its schedule. The post should be written deadpan by the company's 'resident phenomenologist,' a consultant hired to spin the outage in terms of Heidegger's readiness-to-hand: the broken equipment stopped being ready-to-hand and became present-at-hand, and, the way Heidegger thinks breakdown does, it made the whole equipmental context light up. Customers, he argues, finally saw the referential whole of their smart home: how the doorbell depends on the router depends on the servers depends on the cloud, the 'towards-which' of the morning routine, the workshop structure of the house. His conclusion is that the outage was a triumph of disclosure and the company should be thanked for letting the world reveal itself. Use the actual corporate postmortem sections — Impact, Root Cause, Timeline, What We Learned — and make the last section pure startup-Heidegger, every lesson really a passage from a seminar. The satire should target, first, the habit of turning a service failure into cosmic insight, and second, the assumption underneath the marketing that making tools invisible is automatically the goal. The humor has to come from the actual content: somebody who knows the difference between ready-to-hand and present-at-hand should see that the consultant is half right about the phenomena and totally wrong about what follows. Stay deadpan, never wink, and dont let him cave at the end and admit the outage was bad — the entire joke is that he cant. Use the jargon the way a startup would misuse it, not the way a grad student would.
