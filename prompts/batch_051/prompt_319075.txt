Here's an asymmetry in how we run conversations that I can't quite get past. If I assert something — say, that the new postdoc is a disaster — everyone feels entitled to demand reasons. But if the same content is smuggled in as a presupposition — say, "it's surprising the lab hasn't had to carry him yet" — it mostly enters the record for free. People accommodate it: the conversational score gets updated as if the point were established, just because nobody objected fast enough, and objecting reads as a weird, agressive move. So asserted claims face an evidential bar and presupposed claims face almost none, even when the presupposition is doing the controversial work. Is this asymmetry justifiable? Give the strongest account you can — efficiency, charity, keeping every conversation from turning into a background audit, whatever carries weight — and then stress-test it against loaded cases like "have you stopped fudging the numbers?" or "even our weakest student could do this." If you'd keep the asymmetry, give the principle that separates harmless accommodation from cases where the scorekeeping is being exploited, and say whether the remedy is just that we should all object more.
