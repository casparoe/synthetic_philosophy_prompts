Descartes in the *Discourse on Method* (Veitch translation) opens with this: "Good sense is, of all things among men, the most equally distributed; for every one thinks himself so abundantly provided with it, that those even who are the most difficult to satisfy in everything else, do not usually desire a larger measure of this quality than they already possess."

I want to twist the standard brain-in-a-vat scenario around that line. Suppose the envatted brain isn't being fed a false world by an evil scientist or a supercomputer. Instead, the simulation is generated by the brain's own "good sense," in a closed loop: whatever degree of reasonableness or confidence the BIV expects itself to have, the simulation feeds back exactly that, so the BIV keeps getting evidence that its own judgment is well distributed. Skeptical hypotheses it entertains get returned to it as signs of its careful, proportioned thinking.

How does this change the usual Putnam-style anti-skeptical argument about brains in vats? Does the self-satisfaction mechanism give the BIV's terms like "brain" and "vat" a reference, or does it break the externalist premise entirely?
