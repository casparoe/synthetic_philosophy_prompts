I recently got pulled into an argument about consciousness that has stayed with me. A friend with whom I have been reading philosophy of mind keeps leaning on the familiar picture of the mind as a theater: sensory experience is the show on a stage, attention is a spotlight, and the self is an audience seated in the dark, watching the performance unfold. He uses this picture to support the conclusion that there must be some place—in the brain or at least in the mind—where everything is assembled into a single, finished conscious experience; otherwise, he says, there would be a show with no audience and no definite moment at which the show is on. Another friend objected that this is exactly the Cartesian theater that Dennett's multiple drafts model was meant to replace, and that the model says there is no such place and no such moment: there are only many parallel, continuously revised interpretations of incoming signals, none of them privileged as the final version. The discussion stalled there, and I still cannot tell whether Dennett's view denies something introspectively obvious or only denies a bad picture of it.

Please audit the theater analogy as an argument rather than simply dismissing it. I would like three things. First, a correspondence table with columns: Theater element; Mental counterpart the analogy suggests; How well it maps (strong, partial, or weak); What the mapping captures and what it quietly distorts. Second, a disanalogy table with columns: Feature imported from the theater; What the mind or brain does instead; Whether this matters for the conclusion that there must be a central place (yes or no, with a sentence on why). Third, after the tables, a JSON object with fields: conclusion_survives (boolean, whether the conclusion the analogy was used to support survives once the disanalogies are on the table), what_the_analogy_was_doing (string, the rhetorical or argumentative work the analogy was performing), best_replacement_analogy (string or null, a better analogy if there is one), and bottom_line (string, a two-sentence verdict). In the second table, separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the central-place conclusion from the ones that are only cosmetic.
