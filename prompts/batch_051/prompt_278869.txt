My workplace has a monthly “argument club,” and a few of us want to turn it into a competitive league: two people take sides on a question, three judges score them like a boxing match, and we track an Elo-style ranking. The proposed rules give points for the number of distinct objections raised, for making the other person retract something or fumble, and for flagging fallacies by name; they subtract points for concessions and for changing your mind. Some people say this will make everyone train argument skills the way athletes train. Others say it will just select for confident people who never admit they’re wrong and who pile up irrelevant objections to farm points. I’m on the committee that has to set the rules, and I honestly don’t know whether scoring an argument improves reasoning or just gamifies it. Is there a version of this that would actually make people reason better, or should we drop the league idea entirely?
