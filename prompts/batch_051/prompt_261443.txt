Two people in my metaphysics reading group have been at it for a semester now: one is a presentist — she holds that the only things there are, full stop, are the things that exist right now — and the other is an eternalist, who holds that past, present, and future things all exist and the universe is a four-dimensional block. They've decided to do an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to write up the document they should have produced.

Make it look like this. First, a numbered list of claims both can sign, worded so neither side flinches, and dont let them be vacuous — the interesting part is how much they can actually agree on. Then a disagreements table. For each genuine disagreement, the first column states it in neutral language that both accept as a fair description of what is at issue; the second column gives the presentist's way of putting the point; the third gives the eternalist's way. Each formulation should be one the holder would say yes, that is exactly what I mean. After that, a short section on the disagreements that turned out to be merely verbal — things they thought seperated them but didn't. Finally, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them. I suspect one crux is the triviality charge against presentism (whether the view collapses into the tautology that whatever exists now exists now), another is about truthmakers for past-tensed truths — what, right now, makes it true that Socrates was snub-nosed or that there were dinosaurs — and a third is whether special relativity really rules out an objective now. For each crux give the concrete case or argument both parties would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show. Don't resolve the cruxes; the deliverable is something both could put their names to, with whatever residual disagreement made exact.
