In a row about whether lab-grown meat would be a morally fine thing to normalise, a mate kept shutting me down with "the yuck factor isn't an argument." She used it on two moves I actually think are different: (1) my own hesitation about eating the stuff, and (2) a worry that losing a widely shared food-related disgust might quietly do something to our idea that eating is bound up with purity and sanctity, not just nutrition. I can't pin down what the slogan is even claiming. Could you seperate out the distinct precise claims "the yuck factor isn't an argument" could be making — from the uncontroversial to the radical — say which are defensible and which are false, and say which reading would actually support each of her two dismissals? A menu I could bring back would be ideal.
