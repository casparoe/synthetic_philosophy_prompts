I keep getting pulled toward a Lockean memory criterion of personal identity: past me is me just in case I stand in the right memory/consciousness relation to him, because it feels like the only view that explains why my own past is specially mine. But Reid's brave officer gets under my skin: the old general remembers the brave officer, the officer remembers the flogged boy, but the general doesn't remember the flogging. Under the memory criterion, then, the general is the officer, the officer is the boy, and the general isn't the boy — which violates transitivity of identity, and that one I can't talk myself out of. The standard move is to shift to psychological continuity, overlapping chains of memory, but I can't tell whether that saves the view or just quietly abandons the thing that made it attractive. I'm looking for a negotiated verdict: exactly what should give way — the criterion, the intuition about transitivity, or only a sloppy formulation of one of them — and what would justify each move instead of just papering over the clash.
