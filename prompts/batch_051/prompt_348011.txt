Ever since my wife and I bought a place near the river, I've had one rule about flood forecasts: anything more than about a week out, I treat as no better than the historical average. The reasoning I give myself is that the weather is a chaotic system — a tiny difference in today's conditions blows up and you get the butterfly effect, so a 10-day flood outlook can't be pointing at anything real. But my friend who did a semester of nonlinear dynamics says im misusing the word unpredictable. He says the atmosphere is stuck on a strange attractor: you can't say exactly where on it you'll be, but not every outcome is equally likely, and the spread of an ensemble forecast is real information about which parts of the attractor are in play. So the habit is: when a flood outlook gives probabilities two weeks out, I quietly replace them with baseline frequencies and feel like I'm being appropriately humble. Is that defensible? Or am I throwing away the one kind of information chaos theory actually leaves you with? Is there a better version of the rule — say, shrinking the probabilities toward baseline instead of flattening them to it — and how would I even tell where 'too far out' begins?
