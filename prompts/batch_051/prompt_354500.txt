Here's something I've been turning over and I'd like a careful verdict rather than a shrug. My rough criteria for when we're warranted in taking the intentional stance toward a system are these: (a) treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires lets you predict what it does — you ask what it ought to do given those states, and that predicts it well; (b) the intentional description actually earns its keep, meaning it buys you predictions you wouldn't get as cheaply from the design or the physics alone; (c) the beliefs and desires we attribute hang together as a roughly rational package, so we're not projecting attitudes onto the thing just to flatter ourselves. Now the case: a current large language model, not fine-tuned as a game player but deployed as a conversational assistant. It says it thinks, revises its answer when corrected, sometimes says it was trying to do something and just missed. Within a conversation, you can predict its next move fairly well by treating it as an eager, oddly inconsistent interlocutor with partial knowledge and a strong desire to be helpful. But across conversations there are no stable beliefs: rephrase the prompt and the apparent views flip; it has no standing goals that persist when no one is talking to it. Does this thing qualify? Go criterion by criterion. Where it fails, tell me whether the case is just outside the criteria or the criteria need redrawing — and whether the intentional stance is being earned here or just loaned to the system by our habits of reading text.
