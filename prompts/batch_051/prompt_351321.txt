I've come to think of rationality as basically a martial art: you drill katas — calibrated-forecast exercises, premortems, double-crux conversations, Fermi estimates — in artificial settings, and the claim is that the trained moves transfer to real, messy, high-stakes reasoning, the way a boxer's footwork shows up in a street fight. I want to know what would count against this picture. Give me the evidence and arguments that should weaken it, sorted from decisive to merely awkward, including at least: the psychological work on near versus far transfer; the fact that people can be superb at puzzle rationality while hopeless in real-world decisions; the selection worry that the drills merely flatter or filter for people who already reason well; and the suspicion that 'they didn't practise enough' or 'wrong kata' is always available when transfer fails. Then give me an honest verdict on whether the view has been insulated from refutation by those excuses, and whether its a problem for a view of this sort.
