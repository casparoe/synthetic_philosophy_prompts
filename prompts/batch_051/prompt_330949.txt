I've been circling a disagreement I keep meeting in debates over how words should be used. One side says we should stop merely describing how a concept such as addiction, intelligence, or consent is currently used, and instead reshape the concept so it does better work, singling out the cases that matter for law, medicine, or policy. The other side says that once you reshape it you are no longer talking about addiction, intelligence, or consent; you have changed the topic and cannot claim to have discovered anything about the original thing. Before I take a side, I want the full specification of what a good account of concept revision would have to get right. Give me the requirements list: the cases it must handle, such as a revision that tightens a scientific classification versus one that merely borrows a word's prestige; the distinctions it must not collapse, especially the difference between explaining current usage and recommending a better usage; and the constraints that follow from the fact that meanings are held by communities of speakers, not by individuals. With each requirement, say in a sentence or two why it is a requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of one camp. Then identify the single requirement that most clearly separates a genuine improvement from a relabeling.
