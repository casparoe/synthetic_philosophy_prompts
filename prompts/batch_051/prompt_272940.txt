I teach a seminar on the ethics of consent, and after class a student brought up a case that broke the checklist I've been using all term. The checklist has four conditions for valid consent: (1) competence—the person has the cognitive and emotional capacity to make the decision; (2) information—they understand what they are agreeing to, including its main foreseeable consequences; (3) freedom from coercion and deception—no threats, no lies, no manipulative pressure bearing on the decision; and (4) settledness—the consent reflects a stable, enduring aim of the person rather than a passing impulse or a decision they would disown a week later.

Now the case. Imagine a future in which a medically licensed "desire-architecture" procedure can rewrite a person's deep preferences in a targeted way. Amir, a 30-year-old who has spent years envying his older brother's life as a monk, voluntarily signs up for the procedure after a full consultation: he is competent, knows exactly what it will do, pays for it himself, and is counseled overnight before he finalizes the consent form. The procedure succeeds exactly as described. Amir emerges with a wholehearted, stable desire to join a monastic order; he understands the vows, faces no penalties if he walks away, and the new desire feels entirely his own—he describes it as finally seeing what mattered. The order admits him on the strength of what appears to be freely given consent. But a week later the order's ethicist learns about the procedure and raises the question wether the vow was ever the product of valid consent.

Go through the checklist condition by condition and say whether Amir's consent to the vows passes, fails, or is underdetermined on that condition. Then tell me whether you think the checklist itself is missing something—perhaps a condition about the history of a desire or about who authored it—rather than tweaking the case to get a clean answer. Give the response as a JSON object with these fields: "overall_verdict" (one of "valid", "invalid", or "underdetermined"; lead with your bottom line), "condition_assessments" (an array with one object per condition above, each containing "condition", "status" as one of "passes"/"fails"/"unclear", "reasoning", and "confidence" as a number from 0 to 1), "where_the_checklist_is_silent" (a string naming the issue the four conditions fail to settle), "proposed_new_condition" (an object with "name", "statement", and "would_it_change_the_verdict"), and "biggest_disagreement" (a string saying what a smart defender of the consent's validity and a smart critic of it would most plausibly part ways over).
