So I got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether all the cross-cultural moral psychology stuff means morality isnt objective. By the end I wrote down what I actually think and now I'm worried it doesnt hang together. I'd like a consistency check before I present this anywhere. Heres what I hold:

(a) I'm a moral realist: at least some moral claims are true or false independent of what anyone believes or feels about them.

(b) The WEIRD literature is basically right that a huge chunk of what we thought were universal moral psychological patterns are really artifacts of Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic samples.

(c) I dont think the fact that cultures disagree morally is by itself a good argument against moral objectivity. Disagreement can be explained by different non-moral beliefs, different circumstances, or by one side simply being wrong.

(d) Reflective equilibrium is still the right method for ethics: you go back and forth between considered judgments and general principles, revising until they fit.

(e) Some moral intuitions are near-universal and probably reflect an evolved core—harm and fairness mostly—while others, like purity or loyalty, vary a lot across cultures.

(f) When doing moral psychology, we should be suspicious of intuitions that are idiosyncratic to WEIRD people. But when doing normative ethics, the demographic origin of an intuition shouldn't by itself decide whether the intuition tracks the truth.

Which of these are actually in tension? Which only look like they are? And what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by how severe they are rather than treating them all as equal.
