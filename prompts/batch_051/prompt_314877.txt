Me and a colleague have been arguing for a while—I’m a PhD student in logic, he’s in philosophy of math—about whether 2 is an element of 4. We agree on all the formalism: in the von Neumann construction, 2∈4; in the Zermelo construction, 2∉4; both satisfy second-order Peano arithmetic; both are isomorphic; etc. But we can’t agree on whether the question itself has an answer. I tend to think it’s just a debate about what we mean by “number”—once you fix a reduction, it’s either true or false, but ordinary arithmetic doesn’t pick one reduction, so there’s no fact of the matter. He thinks there’s a real mathematical fact, and the fact that we can’t see it is just because our concept of number is underdetermined by our explicit theories.

It feels like one of those disputes that has persisted for decades without anyone changing sides, and I’m starting to suspect the debaters aren’t actually disagreeing about the same thing. My working diagnosis is that one side is asking a question about what our notation refers to, and the other is asking a question about what exists in the intended structure, and those are different questions with different success conditions. But maybe that’s too deflationary, and underneath it there’s still a genuine disagreement about what mathematical objects are. I’d like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is, and what would count as progress given that diagnosis. Could a new formal result settle it? A better theory of reference? A sharper distinction about what a reduction is supposed to do? Or is the problem just ill-posed once you give up the idea that numbers have an internal identity beyond their relations?
