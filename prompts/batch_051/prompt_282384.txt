A student brought me the following summary, from a popular philosophy magazine, of Frankfurt's challenge to the principle of alternative possibilities:

"Frankfurt showed that free will is not needed for moral responsibility. His strategy is to attack the principle that a person is morally responsible for what she did only if she could have done otherwise. In his thought experiment, Jones is deciding whether to shoot Smith. A neuroscientist, Black, has implanted a device that will force Jones to shoot if he is about to decide not to; in the event, Jones decides to shoot on his own and Black does nothing. Jones could not have done otherwise, because if he had been about not to shoot, the device would have made him shoot. Yet Jones is clearly responsible, since he did what he wanted of his own accord. Frankfurt concludes that because determinism similarly deprives us of the ability to do otherwise, determinism does not undermine moral responsibility."
