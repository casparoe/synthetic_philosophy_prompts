I have been reading about Gilbert Ryle's notion of a category mistake, and I keep noticing a structural parallel with something I work with every day as a software engineer: the distinction, in a strongly typed language, between a program that is syntactically valid and a program that typechecks. I would like help deciding whether the parallel is deep or just a loose analogy.

On the philosophical side, as I understand it, a category mistake is a sentence that is well formed by the surface grammar of the language but ill formed at the level of the hidden kind structure our words presuppose. The standard illustration is a visitor to a university who is shown the colleges, the library, the lecture halls, and the administrative offices, and then asks where the University itself is. The visitor treats the University as one more object on the list rather than as something of a different kind. On Ryle's account, the ghost in the machine is the same error writ large: dualism treats the mind as a special kind of substance lodged inside the body, which makes mental terms look like mechanical ones and produces the allegedly intractable problem of how the two substances interact.

On the software side, a type error has a very similar shape. In a language whose type checker distinguishes booleans from integers, the expression `not 5` is lexically and syntactically valid, but a type checker rejects it because the operation expects a boolean and the argument is an integer. The checker is not faulting the program for computing the wrong value; it is faulting it for being malformed in a respect the parser cannot see. So the mapping I have in mind is this: natural-language syntax plays the role of the parser's grammar; our implicit sense that certain concepts cannot be combined in certain ways plays the role of the type system; a category mistake is a type error in ordinary speech; and Ryle's claim about the ghost in the machine is the claim that the dualist's theory does not typecheck — the mind-body problem is an artifact of assigning mental terms to the wrong category.

I want three things from you. First, state this mapping precisely and say where the two structures genuinely match and where they come apart; I am especially unsure how important it is that a type system is stipulated by a language designer while conceptual categories are discovered from ordinary use, and that type errors can be made decidable while category mistakes cannot. Second, say what would have to be true of both domains for results to transfer from one to the other — for example, would anything like subtyping, parametric polymorphism, or the interface/implementation distinction carry over to Ryle's attack on dualism, or are those ideas false friends here? Third, name at least one thing the analogy predicts that seems false or strained on the philosophical side, or, if you think the analogy is deeper than I have given it credit for, say what it predicts that Ryle would be committed to and whether that commitment is defensible.
