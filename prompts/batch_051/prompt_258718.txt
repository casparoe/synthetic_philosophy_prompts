A book I am reading on money and banking ends a chapter with the following summary of what ‘printing money’ does:

"When a central bank prints money and spends it, it adds directly to the private sector's wealth: new purchasing power comes into existence, and households are richer by the amount issued. Since the new money is a government liability that need never be repaid, this lets a currency-issuing government obtain real resources—roads, wages, the interest on its debt—without imposing a comparable cost on anyone. The only genuine constraint is inflation: with idle factories and unemployed workers, printing is nearly costless, because the new spending calls into use resources that would otherwise have sat idle; at full employment it merely bids up prices. The revenue involved is seigniorage, best understood as an inflation tax on people who hold money, and it is why such a government need never be forced to default. There is also a liquidity gain: base money is the most liquid asset, so the public holds it at a zero yield, and the interest the government avoids paying on the corresponding debt is pure fiscal gain."
