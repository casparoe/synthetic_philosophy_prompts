In my experimental philosophy class we've been going through a load of Knobe-effect cases, and my friend has such a consistent pattern that I want to predict her before I show her next weeks case. So far she says: if a CEO approves a project just to make money, knowing it will pollute a river as a side effect, then he intentionally polluted the river; if the same CEO approves a project just to make money, knowing it will clean up a river as a side effect, then he did not intentionally clean the river; and if a doctor prescribes a drug that will cure a patient's illness, knowing it will also cause pretty bad nausea as a side effect, and the doctor genuinely regrets the nausea but thinks the cure is worth it, then he did not intentionally cause the nausea. In every case the agent definitely knows the side effect will happen and only acts for the main goal.

Next week's case: a developer buys a small apartment building purely to turn a profit, and he evicts its long-term tenants, fully within the law and in line with standard practice, knowing it will uproot them and cause them real distress. He doesn't care about the distress at all. Does my friend say the developer intentionally caused the tenants' distress?

What I want: predict her yes or no first. Then lay out the two or three rules that fit all her earlier verdicts equally well, and say what each one predicts for this new case. Then give your best guess about which rule she's actually running and why, and be honest about where that guess is shakiest. Then give me one further case that would finally tell the rules apart, and say what she'd have to say in it if each rule were the right one.

Also, could you write it all as plain paragraphs, like an email? No headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables.
