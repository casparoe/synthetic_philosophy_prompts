I'm a decision-theory person — I think in utility functions, indifference curves, lexicographic preferences, and the Archimedean axiom, and you can use all of those terms. I keep running into a view in moral philosophy that I want you to translate into that vocabulary, and then tell me what gets lost.

The view is lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. As I understand it: negative utilitarianism says suffering is the only thing with fundamental moral weight; pleasure matters only insofar as it relieves suffering. The lexical-threshold version adds that there's an intensity cutoff for suffering such that, past the cutoff, no amount of pleasure or happiness — for anyone — can compensate for even one instance of it. A moment of torture isn't outweighed by a million gourmet meals or a million blissful bystanders. The same people often say that above the cutoff, pleasure and pain just aren't commensurable.

My hunch, in my own language, is that this is just a lexicographic preference ordering with 'suffering above the cutoff' as the first criterion and everything else subordinate — which is exactly what you get when you drop the Archimedean property and let one good dominate others. But I cant tell whether 'incommensurable' is doing the same work as 'violates continuity,' or whether it's pointing at incomplete preferences, or at something about adding utility across people that my toolkit doesn't model.

So restate the view as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, and please dont smuggle in decision-theoretic assumptions and pretend they're just a translation. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly onto lexicographic preferences, what you had to fudge (the cutoff itself, the asymmetry between pain and pleasure, aggregating across people), what has no counterpart in my framework at all, and whether the residue is my toolkit missing something or the philosophy being fuzzier than it looks.

Respond as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro, no conclusion: (1) the clean translations, (2) the fudged translations, (3) what has no counterpart, (4) your diagnosis of whether the residue comes from my toolkit or the view, (5) whether my hunch that it's just lexicographic preferences is right or missing something.
