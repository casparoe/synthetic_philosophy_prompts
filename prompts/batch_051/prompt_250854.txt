As someone who builds software but has no formal background in philosophy, I keep running into the Church-Turing thesis and finding that people mean very different things by what kind of claim it is. The readings I already know and do not need repeated are: (1) the stipulative reading, on which it defines the informal notion of effective computability as Turing computability and so is not the sort of claim that could be false; (2) the empirical-hypothesis reading, on which it is a fallible claim about the limits of any finite procedure, so far unrefuted but in principle refutable; (3) the convergence argument, on which the fact that lambda calculus, general recursive functions, register machines, and Turing machines all pick out the same class of functions is evidence rather than coincidence; (4) the vagueness reading, on which effective procedure has no sharp extension, so the thesis has no determinate truth value and mainly functions as a heuristic; (5) the theorem-style move, on which adding explicit axioms about what discrete deterministic devices can do turns the thesis into a provable theorem; and (6) the physical Church-Turing reading, the distinct and stronger claim that every physical process is Turing-computable. I want a reading of the epistemic status of the Church-Turing thesis that is not on this list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of items already on it. Then tell me honestly whether that new reading is any good, and whether it captures something the listed readings miss. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble and no restating of the question.
