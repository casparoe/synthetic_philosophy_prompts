Write an essay on the following controversy concerning causal discovery from observational data. Assume the standard framework: variables are represented as vertices of a directed acyclic graph; a distribution satisfies the Causal Markov Condition relative to the graph when each variable is independent of its non-descendants conditional on its parents; the distribution satisfies Faithfulness when the only conditional independencies it exhibits are those entailed by the Markov Condition; and the graph satisfies Minimality when it meets the Markov Condition but no proper subgraph of it does. To make the idea concrete: Faithfulness fails, for example, when a variable is influenced through two distinct causal routes whose contributions exactly cancel, so that the distribution displays an independence the graph does not entail.

The controversy is this: is Faithfulness a justified default assumption when one infers causal structure from observed independencies, or should causal discovery proceed under the weaker Minimality assumption, or can even Minimality not be taken for granted? Defenders of Faithfulness commonly argue that violations require exact cancellations between distinct causal paths or deterministic relationships, and therefore occupy a measure-zero subset of the parameter space under standard parameterizations; that such fine-tuned violations are not the sort of thing a scientific method should anticipate; and that learning causal structure would be badly underdetermined without something like Faithfulness. Critics reply that the measure-zero argument depends on a choice of parameterization and a prior over that parameter space that are themselves in need of justification; that a set of measure zero is not thereby negligible for all legitimate purposes, and such states may even be selected by design or by regulatory mechanisms that enforce balance between opposing influences; and that the weaker Minimality condition retains what is defensible while avoiding the stronger claim that the world contains no canceling structure.

Weigh these and any further considerations you judge important, and defend a definite conclusion about what may legitimately be assumed in causal discovery and why.
