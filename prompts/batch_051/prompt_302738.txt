I recently read Karl Popper's essay Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind. In the section where he sets out what he takes Darwinism to assert, he insists that, in its standard formulation, natural selection cannot be tested by observation: organisms that are fit survive, but the only independent measure of fitness is survival itself, so the doctrine comes close to saying that survivors survive. He then, however, refuses to conclude that the theory should be discarded. Instead he says it is better treated as a metaphysical research programme, a broadly heuristic framework within which genuinely testable hypotheses can be proposed when particular selective pressures, environmental constraints, and mechanisms of heredity are specified. He even says he does not want to give the impression that he thinks the theory is without value.

My tutor, by contrast, summarised the whole position like this: 'Popper argued that natural selection is an unfalsifiable tautology, and therefore scientifically worthless; he later retracted the claim when the modern synthesis made the theory genuinely testable.' Reading the essay myself, I can see where the first clause comes from, but I cannot find the later retraction in this piece, and the phrase 'scientifically worthless' seems stronger than anything Popper thinks in the passage I described. I do not know whether the essay elsewhere contains a different statement that would vindicate the tutor's summary, or whether my tutor is conflating this piece with something he wrote earlier or later.

Take my tutor's summary clause by clause. Which parts are accurate, which are subtle distortions, and which are false as a reading of Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind? In particular, does the essay's distinction between Darwinism as an untestable framework and particular evolutionary models as testable hypotheses allow one to say both that the core theory is unfalsifiable and that it is scientifically fruitful, without contradiction? And does the essay's later discussion of an emerging mind depend on any claim that natural selection is empirically testable, or is it compatible with the metaphysical-research-programme reading?
