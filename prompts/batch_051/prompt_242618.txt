I'm in a philosophy of photography seminar and we've been going through the transparency debate — the view that photographs are different from paintings because they're produced mechanically by the scene itself, so we literally see the photographed object through the picture rather than seeing a depiction mediated by someone's beliefs. I keep getting stuck on the fact that my phone doesn't really take a photograph in that sense anymore: it stacks a burst of exposures, aligns them, does HDR merging, runs scene detection to pick a tone curve, and in night mode or AI-editing modes it fills in detail that was never recorded in any single frame. Does the transparency argument survive cameras that compute rather than record? I'm not asking whether an AI-edited image is a "real photo" — I want to know exactly which premise of the transparency view computational photography bears on, and whether the view can be repaired by saying the pipeline is still counterfactually dependent on the light from the scene, or whether at some point the causal chain gets too thin for us to still be seeing through the picture.
