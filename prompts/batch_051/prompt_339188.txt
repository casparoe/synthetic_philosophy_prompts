I am committed to a broadly computational realism about virtual objects: the sword in an online game is a real particular because it is a stable, structured state of a physical system, with real causal powers over players and machines, and not merely an image or an idea. From this I am tempted to conclude that harms carried out through such objects are real harms: erasing a world someone spent a year building, or taking goods a community relies on, does genuine damage. But I also have a firm intuition that harming a simulated character no one owns or cares about is not a wrong at all, and that deleting an abandoned private virtual world wrongs no one, however rich its contents. Present the strongest case for each side. Then tell me which should yield: the theory, which may need to be amended so that a virtual object's moral significance is tied to the interests of actual persons; the intuition, which may be overgeneralizing from ordinary physical objects; or the framing of the conflict, which may rest on an unstated premise that realism about an object carries moral conclusions about how it may be treated. Be specific about what each resolution commits me to, rather than just telling me which side wins.
