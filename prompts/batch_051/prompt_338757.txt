I have been thinking about whether major political upheavals are best explained by slow structural pressures rather than by the ideas and choices of the people involved. I would like you to analyze the French Revolution through the structural-demographic account of secular cycles associated with Peter Turchin and his collaborators: the claim that pre-industrial states tend to pass through recurring phases of expansion, stagflation, crisis, and depression, driven by population growth against a limited resource base, elite overproduction (more people trained and expecting elite positions than the existing institutions can absorb), popular immiseration, and a state whose revenues stagnate just as its commitments grow. Using those mechanisms as the lens, say what the theory would identify as the decisive pressures in France before 1789, which features of the Revolution it explains well, which it misdescribes or leaves out (for example, the novel ideological content of the Revolution or the role of contingent decisions), and what the episode in turn shows about the theory's limits — whether the Revolution confirms the model, is an anomaly for it, or is a case the model never anticipated. Keep to widely known facts, and be explicit about the points at which the theory is silent rather than stretching it.
