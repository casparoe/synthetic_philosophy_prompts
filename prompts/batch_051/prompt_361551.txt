Take the dark forest answer to the Fermi paradox, understood roughly as follows: in a universe where the intentions of other technological civilizations cannot be verified across interstellar distances, where the cost of a successful first strike is low relative to the cost of being struck first, and where suspicion tends to compound the longer two civilizations observe each other, the rational policy for any civilization is to keep silent and, upon detecting another civilization, to destroy it before it can do the same. Now change one assumption. Suppose detection can be one-way: there exists a class of civilizations—call them silent observers—that can watch other civilizations through means that are in principle undetectable, emitting no signal of their own. The rest of the cosmos is mutually visible, and everyone knows that silent observers may be present even though no one can tell which regions of the sky are being watched. Every visible civilization thus acts under the standing possibility that any civilization it can see is already being watched by a civilization it cannot see, and that the hidden watcher may choose to remain concealed or to reveal itself later, at a moment of its own choosing.

Run the dark forest argument in the original case, then run it again under this variation, focusing on the decision problem of a visible civilization that has just detected another visible civilization. Does the argument for a preemptive strike still go through, or does the risk of an unseen third party alter the calculation—for example, by making an attack more dangerous because it reveals the attacker's exact capabilities, location, and dispositions to a hidden observer, or by making the detected target less dangerous because it, too, must worry about hidden observers and should therefore be comparably inhibited? Say whether the silence-and-strike policy survives the variation, is weakened, or is reversed, and be explicit about any further parameters on which your verdict turns, such as the probability that a given visible civilization is itself a silent observer in disguise, the likely preferences of silent observers once they are safely hidden, or the discount rate over cosmic timescales. Finally, state which observation or consideration would most change your verdict, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
