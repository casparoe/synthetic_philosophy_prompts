Newtonian mechanics, as it stood around 1800: no, because it treated gravitational action at a distance as brute and had nothing to say about matter or light. Quantum electrodynamics: no, because it's really just an effective field theory — the divergences and renormalized parameters mean it's only valid below some energy scale. The Standard Model: no, because of all its free parameters and because it stays silent about gravity. General relativity: no, because it's classical and singularities signal where it breaks down. A grand unified model that reproduced all known particle data but still had three parameters that had to be measured: no, because the parameters weren't explained. Does she call this a final theory?
