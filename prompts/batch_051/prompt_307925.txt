ok so i keep seeing people talk past each other about what a "unit" is, especially when Euclid comes up. one camp treats a unit as just the number one and thinks the old talk is totally dispensable. another camp treats a unit as a chosen chunk or measure (a meter, an hour, an apple) and thinks one is just what you get after you've picked a unit. a third camp treats a unit as a kind of indivisible atomic bit of a multitude. i want the full spec before i take any side. give me the desiderata that a theory of the unit has to meet, where by "unit" i mean the thing Euclid is talking about in the Elements book VII when he defines a unit as that by which each thing that exists is called one, and a number as a multitude made up of units, and also the thing people mean when they say a measurement comes in units. i'm asking for a theory good enough to handle both Euclid-style arithmetic and actual measurement practice, not a theory of the One in some grand metaphysics.

for each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it. the cases it has to handle, off the top of my head: why the Greek treatment seems to resist calling one a number even though modern arithmetic starts from 1; how a unit differs from a point (a point has no part, and the old line was that a point is a unit with position while a unit has no position); how counting three apples and measuring three meters can share a kind of oneness without the unit being an apple or a meter; the fact that in modern set theory or peano arithmetic the unit mostly disappears and only 1 remains; and the fact that units seem to come in kinds (a unit of time can't measure a length) but any two numbers can be added.

then do the real work: say which of the demands people usually make on a theory of the unit are genuine requirements and which are just camp prejudices (number-theorists vs measurement-theorists vs, i don't know, neoplatonists about the one), whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement knocks out the most candidate accounts. end by telling me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would make you drop or rewrite one of the requirements, and where your list is most likely to fail.
