I have been reading interpretability and anomaly-detection writeups for years, and the question I keep hitting is the one the whole enterprise seems to presuppose: what makes two systems count as implementing the same mechanism, as opposed to merely matching input-output behavior? The stakes are direct in anomaly detection, where we want to know whether a new model's suspicious behavior is the same mechanism already catalogued or something genuinely new. I want a working definition of "same mechanism" that gives clean, defensible verdicts on these cases: (1) two mechanical clocks with the same arrangement of gears but different gear materials; (2) two circuits in different transformer models that produce identical next-token behavior but use disjoint sets of intermediate features; (3) a person adding two-digit numbers by carrying and a calculator doing the same task through a different sequence of steps, with the same outputs; (4) natural selection and a genetic algorithm that both sort a population through differential reproduction and mutation; (5) one network whose behavior is supported by a single modular circuit and another network that produces the same behavior through distributed components that each do several unrelated things at once; (6) the same algorithm run on two different hardware substrates, one serial and one parallel.
