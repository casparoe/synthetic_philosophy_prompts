I'm a novelist, not a psychologist, so I'll probably mangle some of the terminology. The book I'm drafting has a character, a cultural psychologist turned public intellectual, who keeps making a version of the WEIRD argument about morality. Her point is that WEIRD people (Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic) are outliers on all sorts of things: how they reason about fairness, how much they third-party punish, what they count as a moral violation in the first place. From this she concludes that our moral convictions are calibrated to our cultural niche, not to any independent moral facts. Her line is: a moral sense that adjusts itself to the local culture can't also be a moral fact-detector. So the realist, she thinks, has to explain why WEIRD morality is somehow more objective and not just, well, WEIRD.

I don't want her to be a sock puppet, and I don't want to strawman her either. So here's what I need worked through.

The claim I want tested is roughly: the kind of cross-cultural variation in folk morality that the WEIRD literature documents is the kind that undermines moral objectivity. It doesn't just show that people disagree; it shows that moral judgment is tracking cultural membership rather than moral reality. The alternative is that the variation is more like ordinary error or unequal information, and that under the right conditions people would converge.

How would one actually test that? I want a concrete study or natural observation, the pattern of results that would favor each reading, and a candid account of what the test cannot reach. I suspect there's a hidden jump from "moral views vary" to "therefore no moral view is more objective," and I want to know if any experiment can bear on that jump or if the jump is philosophical all the way down.

Write it up in exactly these sections, in this order, with rough word counts:

- Bottom line (120 words): your overall take before the detail.
- The claim, restated in its strongest form (250 words): reconstruct her position charitably.
- The best concrete test (350 words): design it. Say exactly what result would favor her reading and what result would favor the alternative.
- What the results would and wouldn't settle (300 words)
- The residue (200 words): what no experiment reaches even in principle.
- My honest verdict (150 words): is her argument mostly right, mostly wrong, or is it not that kind of argument?

Don't give me a reading list. I want the argument itself tested.
