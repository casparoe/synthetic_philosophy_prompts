I have been trying to define what it is for someone to have met a duty to be legible to others, and I have four criteria plus a case that fits them awkwardly. Work through whether the case meets the definition, and say whether the case shows the definition needs revision.

The four criteria:

1. Behavioral predictability: other people can form reliable expectations about what the person will do from their observable patterns, without needing privileged access to their inner states.

2. Access to reasons: the person makes available, in speech or in durable records, the actual considerations that move them to act, and those considerations connect intelligibly with what they do.

3. Shared interpretive burden: the effort of understanding the person is distributed fairly; they do not require others to shoulder an elaborate interpretive load while bearing none of it themselves.

4. Audience-appropriateness: the person is legible to the people with whom they actually stand in the relevant relationships, not merely to a private circle of specially trained readers.

The case: M is a reliable and trusted collaborator. Every evening M publishes a complete log of the day's decisions, recording with scrupulous truthfulness the reasons and intentions behind each significant act. There is no omission and no deception. But the log is written in a private shorthand of M's own devising: invented notation, compressed cross-references, and allusions to conversations that only a few people have had with M. Three long-time colleagues have learned to read it fluently. For everyone else, reading a single entry takes hours and often fails to connect the stated reasons to what M actually did. M refuses to publish simplified summaries, on the ground that any summary would distort the reasons and that anyone is free to learn the notation.

Does M meet my four criteria for being legible to others? Take them one by one, flagging where a criterion gives an unclear verdict and what additional facts would settle it. Then consider whether the case shows that the underlying duty is not one thing but a composite, with a truthfulness component and an understandability component that can come apart. If you think the definition needs changing, propose the smallest revision and reapply it to M.
