I need you to draft the joint statement they'd both actually be able to sign.

Cover at least this: the claims both accept, including the exact arithmatic of the even-money gamble that multiplies your wealth by 1.5 when you win and 0.6 when you lose — if you go all in each round — why its expected value rises 5% per round while its time-average growth rate is negative, and what Jensen's inequality has to do with the gap between them. Then the precise formulations of where they still differ, each phrased in wording its own camp would accept. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one of them. My own guesses about the cruxes, which you should fix or replace if they're wrong: wether the single-shot and repeated versions of a gamble are answerable by one rule or need different rules; whether an institution that pools thousands of parallel bets actually realizes the ensemble average in a way an individual never does; and whether the ergodicity camp can recover ordinary risk aversion, insurance, and time discounting without quietly re-importing the utility machinery they say they're replacing.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table (statement, and each side's accepted wording), then a cruxes table (crux, what would settle it, each side's prediction). Keep it tight enough that two vain, busy people would actually read it.
