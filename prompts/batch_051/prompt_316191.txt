Me and my friend went in circles for like two hours yesterday about whether we should all be having way more kids, because on his view a bigger population is just more good lives. Near the end he landed on a line that's still bugging me: if a couple decides not to have a third kid, the world ends up with one fewer person than it could of had, and that's the same in his eyes as someone quietly dying — except nobody mourns the one. I couldn't say anything back except that one person existed and the other never did, and he kept calling that magic. What's the strongest principle that actually justifies counting deaths but not lives never lived? Test it against the hard cases: a child who would've had a miserable life, a couple who could have had ten, the difference between preventing a life and ending one. And tell me whether that principle also settles the original fight, or whether 'is a bigger population better' ends up coming apart from all of this.
