A friend tried to use Popper against my view that social science can eventually find large-scale patterns in history. His argument went: (1) Popper says the future of human affairs cannot be predicted because future knowledge, including scientific knowledge, will change the course of events, and we cannot predict what we will know before we know it. (2) But the same is true of the information carried by genomes: future adaptations depend on mutations we cannot predict, and those adaptations change the future course of life. (3) So if Popper's argument rules out laws of history, it equally rules out laws of biological evolution. (4) But that conclusion is absurd: evolution has mechanisms and lawlike regularities, even if we cannot predict which species will appear. (5) Therefore Popper's argument must be bad. I find step (4) convincing, but I cannot tell whether the flaw is in step (1), the analogy in step (2), or the inference in step (3). Walk me through which step, if any, fails, and give a definite verdict on whether the argument succeeds or collapses.
