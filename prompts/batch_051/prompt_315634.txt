I just moved back to help run my uncle's trucking and import business, and I keep tripping over the line between a gift and a bribe, so I've started keeping a mental list of how he calls them. A few of his past calls: he called it corruption when a customs officer held our containers until we paid a cash facilitation fee, though he paid it and stayed angry about it for weeks. He saw nothing wrong with the competiting broker who leaves a big fruit basket at the port office every New Year for the whole staff, since no files are pending. He called it corruption when a rival funded the mayor's re-election rally and then got a city contract with no bidding. He also calls the ward politician corrupt for handing out cash envelopes to voters before elections, even though the politician insists its just helping his constituents. And he says the families who bring a goat to the village headman at the harvest festival aren't bribing anyone, because nobody was asking him for a favor that week.

The case I have to decide now: we're about to bid on a city logistics contract, and a cousin who knows city hall says the real rule is to donate to the mayor's community development fund before bids go in. It's a registered charity, the amount is never tied to any specific bid, and the mayor always says it plays no role, but the cousin says he's never seen a firm that skipped the fund win. I have to decide whether we write the check, and I don't just want his verdict, I want the rule behind it.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion: your best guess at what my uncle would say about the fund donation; the rule he's most likely running and what it predicts for our case; the strongest rival rule that also fits all his past calls and what it predicts for our case; which past case he'd treat as the closest analogy; and one further made-up case that would force him to show which of those two rules he really holds.
