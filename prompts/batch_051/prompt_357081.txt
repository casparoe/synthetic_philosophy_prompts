Counterfactual: Charles Darwin dies of illness in South America in 1834, midway through the Beagle voyage and before he has formed his transmutationist views. Hold everything else fixed. Predict, with reasoning, the development and reception of the idea of evolution by natural selection over the following fifty years. Does the idea emerge at all; and if it does, when and from whom—Alfred Russel Wallace, another naturalist, or no one within the window? In what form does it appear, with what proposed mechanism and body of evidence? Does it become established science, or does it remain a heterodox speculation for longer than it did in actual history? And how much of the actual outcome depended on Darwin in particular—his patience, his standing, his networks, his accumulated evidence—rather than on the idea having become inevitable independent of who articulated it? Lay out your answer as an explicit chain of inferences, mark which links are robust and which are guesses, and state which single premise of the counterfactual is doing the most work.
