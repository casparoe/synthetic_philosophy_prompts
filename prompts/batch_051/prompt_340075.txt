I am a novelist writing a novel set in a near-future city-state that treats semantic change as a problem of public order. In this city-state, a Ministry of Correct Usage publishes the only authorized dictionary, and each entry fixes a word's proper meaning by reference to its etymology: normally the earliest attested sense, or, for derivatives and borrowings, the sense of the earlier word from which it descends. Schools grade students against that standard and penalize current, drifted usage, and a minor tribunal can fine firms whose advertisements, contracts, or public signs use a word in a sense that appeared after its etymologically original sense. I want the institution to feel as if it was built by people with a real, though contestable, philosophy of language, not as a blunt totalitarian joke, and I want whatever tensions it has to be philosophical tensions rather than mere plot devices. What theory of meaning does this cluster of practices presuppose? Please separate what the practices strictly presuppose from what they merely fit well with; identify the points at which the institution's commitments are underdetermined by rival theories of meaning, and the places where its practices are in tension with one another; and then explain what would have to change in the Ministry's day-to-day work if its implicit theory were replaced by the view that a word's meaning is fixed by its use in a linguistic community. Write the answer in American English.
