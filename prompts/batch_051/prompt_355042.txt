I'm giving a talk at a mid-sized philosophy department next month, and I want to be ready for the kind of Q&A I'll actually get rather than the Q&A I'd get from people who already like the Boethian move. The talk defends a standard Boethian solution to the foreknowledge problem: God infallibly knows what we'll do, but that doesn't threaten free will because God is outside time, so he doesn't know our actions as future—he knows them in a timeless present, the way you might know a current fact. So his knowledge doesn't add any fixity, causal or logical, over and above what's already true. My loose argument is: (1) the "necessity of the past" worry doesn't get going, since God's act of knowing isn't temporally past relative to my act; (2) atemporal knowledge isn't prediction, so it doesn't make the known event necessary; and (3) what freedom requires is only the absence of causal forcing, and timeless knowledge can't be a force.

The audience is mostly analytic philosophers, but not philosophy-of-religion people: there are two B-theorists about time who will probably say I've just relabeled eternalism and bolted theism onto it, a presentist with strong open-future commitments who thinks "outside time" is as literal as "north of the equator" and twice as suspicious, an ethicist who cares a lot about whether blame requires alternate possibilities, and a medievalist who gets annoyed when Boethius is used as a slogan instead of an argument. I've spent too long sparring with libertarian and open-theist friends, and I suspect the objections I'll get from this room will target different joints.

Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I should actually expect from these people, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased roughly the way someone in that room would put it, with a reply I could give in about two minutes and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. The objections should be the ones that arise becuase of who's in the room, not the standard internet-atheism lines.
