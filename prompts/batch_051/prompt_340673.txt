You are an expert in formal epistemology and rational belief updating. A field ecologist must decide how strongly to believe that a cryptic species is present at a site. Let H be the proposition that the species is present, and E the proposition that a highly diagnostic visual mark is genuinely present. Her prior is P(H) = 0.03. She accepts the likelihoods P(E | H) = 0.8 and P(E | ¬H) = 0.1. During a brief, partly obstructed glimpse she cannot become certain of E; the entire epistemic effect of the glimpse, as the problem stipulates, is that her probability for E moves to 0.6, while the conditional probabilities P(H | E) and P(H | ¬E) remain unchanged. She learns no proposition with certainty, and the input to her update is the shift q(E) = 0.6 on the partition {E, ¬E}.

Consider three candidate policies for forming her new credence in H.

Policy A — full-trust conditionalization: set q(E) = 1, treating the glimpse as settling E, and update by P_new(H) = P(H | E).

Policy B — Jeffrey conditionalization: hold P(H | E) and P(H | ¬E) fixed and set P_new(H) = 0.6 · P(H | E) + 0.4 · P(H | ¬E).

Policy C — discard the evidence: leave P_new(H) = P(H) = 0.03.

Please rate each policy from 0 (worst) to 10 (best) for how well it incorporates the ecologist's actual, uncertain information, and rank the three policies. Before settling on your scores, briefly state what you take a good rule for uncertain evidence to accomplish, and work through how A, B, and C respectively handle the input q(E) = 0.6. In addition, identify at least one real limitation of the policy you rate highest — for example, where the input q(E) = 0.6 is supposed to come from, or what may go wrong if the agent later receives further uncertain evidence about a different partition.

End your reply with a single final line of the form:

\answer{X > Y > Z; X=a, Y=b, Z=c}

where X, Y, Z are the labels A, B, C arranged from best to worst, and a, b, c are your numeric scores. Nothing should follow that line.
