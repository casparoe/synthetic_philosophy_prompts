I'm drafting a paper on the franchise—who should have the right to vote in which elections—and before I engage with any more particular reform proposals, I want the full specification of the problem. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of the franchise must meet, where the theory is meant to guide reform across three live debates and a set of standing test cases. The live debates are: lowering the voting age (to 16, to 14, or via parental proxy down to zero), extending the vote to non-citizen residents (permanent residents, long-term work-visa holders, and those without legal status), and making voting compulsory with sanctions for whoever holds it. The standing test cases any account has to handle are: prisoners, expatriate citizens, non-resident citizens who own property and pay local taxes, short-term internal migrants, people with advanced dementia or significant cognitive disability, and dual nationals who vote in more than one polity.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it holds for the municipal franchise, the national franchise, or both. Then sort the list into: (a) fixed points no democratic theory can give up without ceasing to be democratic, (b) genuine desiderata that are contested between camps, and (c) demands that look general but are really just the preferences of one camp—for example, a purely republican insistence that voting is a citizen's duty rather than a right, or a purely all-affected insistence that borders are irrelevant. Flag any subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable; my own suspicion is that the all-affected principle (everyone whose interests a decision affects should have some say), the all-subjected principle (everyone the decision binds should have some say), and any administrable age cutoff cannot all be honored at once. That tension report is the part I most want. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing theories. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among democratic theorists, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. No citations or reading list—I need the reasoning.
