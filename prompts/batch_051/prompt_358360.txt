I work on systems that infer users' preferences from their observed behavior, and I keep running into a distinction I don't have a settled term for. In one case, a user repeatedly skips the gym at the last minute even though she has paid for a membership, arranged childcare, and maintains that exercising is the goal she most wants to keep. The system reads the skips as evidence that she prefers staying home; she describes the skips as lapses from a goal she endorses. In another case, a customer buys the cheapest version of a product every month but says he cares about labor standards, and the system infers that price is all he cares about. I need to separate the preference that best rationalizes observed choices from the preference the person would affirm on reflection. Does this distinction have an established name, and is it a distinction the field treats as real rather than as one side being confused? If there is no exact term, propose one, define it, and give its edge cases. A bare label won't help; I want the conceptual carving. Keep the response under 400 words.
