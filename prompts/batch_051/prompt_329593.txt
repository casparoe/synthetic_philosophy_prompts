I'm a new member of a city drug policy commission, and one of the senior members keeps saying we should decide the current proposal "strictly on our own precedents" instead of re-litigating the whole philosophy each time. Here's the record I've been handed.

In 2016 the commission recommended decriminalizing possession of all drugs for personal use, up to a ten-day supply. Cases get diverted to a civil panel with a health referral and maybe a fine. The rationale at the time was that criminal punishment does more harm than the use itself, and a lot of members cited Portugal. In 2019 it rejected a proposal to legalize recreational cannabis sales, 6–2, mainly because commercial legalization would likely normalize use and the public-health evidence was too mixed. In 2020 it approved safe consumption rooms for heroin users on harm-reduction grounds, saying keeping people alive and linking them to services trumped the objection that we'd be "facilitating" use. Last year it rejected a proposal to allow supervised therapeutic use of psilocybin, with the majority saying the trial data wasn't there yet and we shouldn't get ahead of the clinicians.

Now we're asked to legalize and regulate cannabis home cultivation for personal use only: a household could grow up to four plants, no sales, no advertising, with fines and possible seizure if you sell or give it away. I'm less interested in members' personal drug politics and more in whether our own record already answers this. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with those precedents? Which precedent is the closest analog, and if the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with a yes versus a no? And which old decision does each answer put the most strain on?
