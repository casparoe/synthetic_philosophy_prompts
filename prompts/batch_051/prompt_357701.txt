I'm a novelist working on a near-future novel about a lab deciding whether to deploy a general-purpose AI assistant, and I keep stalling on a scene where the two senior safety researchers—who've spent years arguing past each other—are told by the board to produce a joint statement before the launch vote. One of them, I'll call her Marta, is a worst-case person: she thinks you don't ship unless you can strictly bound the probability of catastrophe across every environment or input the system could plausibly encounter, and if you can't produce that bound, the honest answer is no. The other, Dev, thinks that's fantasy for something that talks to the whole internet; he wants the safety case to rest on excellent average-case performance across a huge, representative, adversarially red-teamed test distribution, plus continuous monitoring after release, because you can't enumerate every possible situation ahead of time. They're not caricatures—each concedes real points to the other, which is why the document is hard.

I'd like you to actually draft the document they'd sign. I'm imagining something like: first a numbered list of claims both accept, not hand-wavey ones but claims with actual content about what they each know regarding current systems, tail risk, distribution shift, red teaming, deployment monitoring, and what would count as evidence; then their residual disagreements set out in language each would accept as fair to their own view; then the cruxes, the narrower sub-questions where settling one would force at least one of them to change something, each paired with a concrete test or scenario both agree bears on it, plus what each predicts. In the novel the assistant can take actions in the real world—booking things, spending small amounts of money—so a failure mode isn't just an embarrassing answer. I don't need narrative gloss or character voice; I need the document itself to read like a genuine adversarial-collaboration paper that could circulate in the lab, with the two positions (worst-case guarentees vs. average-case empirical safety) kept recognizably distinct. Feel free to invent technical details as long as they stay internally consistent.
