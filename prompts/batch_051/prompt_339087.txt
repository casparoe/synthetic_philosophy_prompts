Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about whether guilt can be transferred in the way scapegoat rituals seem to assume. One of them defends the coherence of the ritual logic: a community's guilt is a real moral status, not just a feeling, and it can be loaded onto a goat, a sin-eater, or some other substitute and disposed of, roughly the way a debt can be assigned. The other says guilt is nontransferable: it supervenes on what a particular agent freely did, so a ritual can change the community's emotions or mark a fresh start, but it can't change who is actually guilty. Make both of them genuinly good at arguing, and make them respond to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other. They should press on the hard cases: whether debts are really analogous (transferring a debt normally needs the creditor's consent, so who is the 'creditor' for guilt?), whether collective guilt makes transfer easier or harder, whether forgiveness is itself a kind of transfer, and whether the defender is stuck saying you could pay someone to take the blame for a murder. At some point one of them should bring up the distinction between guilt as a stain that can be washed off and guilt as culpability that has to be owned, and they should disagree about which one scapegoat rituals are actually tracking. Have each of them concede something real under pressure, and end with the disagreement sharper and better posed than it began, not resolved.
