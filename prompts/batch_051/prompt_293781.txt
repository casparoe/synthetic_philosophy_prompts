I am a trust and safety policy lead at a large social media platform. My team has to decide, under intense time pressure and often with incomplete information, whether a coordinated wave of social sanctions—mass reporting, deplatforming calls, denunciation threads, pressure on employers or advertisers—should be treated as legitimate social enforcement or as coordinated harassment that our platform should suppress under its anti-abuse policies. The platform is large enough that even a few hours of a pile-on can end someone's employment and reputation. I want the older liberal principle, associated especially with Mill's On Liberty, that society may use its opinion to disapprove, warn, and avoid a person, but crosses into a different and objectionable form of coercion when it inflicts serious social penalties, translated into a practical decision procedure my team can actually use. The procedure should not require my reviewers to be philosophers or to have read Mill; it should be a sequence of yes/no questions or tests, with clear thresholds and stopping points, that a policy analyst can run in about fifteen minutes. It should also mark, at each step, where the question is one of fact versus one of values, and where the decision must be escalated to a human with authority rather than settled by a checklist.

Then run the procedure on two realistic cases and show the reasoning: (1) a coordinated campaign against a mid-level journalist who wrote a column denying a widely accepted historical fact, where the campaign includes contacting her employer and advertisers; and (2) a coordinated campaign against a small creator for tweets she posted as a teenager a decade ago, where the campaign is mostly ridicule and ratio-building but also includes repeated false reports to our platform. I want the procedure to expose where my team would be tempted to override it—either because it lets something through that we personally find repellent, or because it stops something we sympathize with—and to state plainly what the procedure cannot settle.
