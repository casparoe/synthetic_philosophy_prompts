I manage a small family-run landscaping business: the crew is my father, my older brother, my sister, and one employee who's been here twenty years and is basically family. I'm the manager on paper, but my father started the business and still expects final say, so our Monday meetings are where disputes actually get settled. We keep hitting the same wall: people argue from fair rules and job titles, but I've been thinking about this thing I read on Confucian role ethics, where a person isn't an independent self that then enters into relationships — you just are your roles (daughter, older brother, boss, teacher, father) and the question is always what a good version of that role would do in this situation. I want a decision procedure I can actually run in under 30 minutes at those meetings, starting from roles instead of neutral fairness rules. Tell me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer, where the procedure stops, and where it hands the decision back to a person because the roles conflict or it's a values call. Then run it on two cases: my older brother wants to fire the longtime employee for talking back to him, and my father insists we keep a money-losing contract because he shaked hands on it.
