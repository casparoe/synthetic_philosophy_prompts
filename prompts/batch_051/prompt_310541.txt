I've been covering the neurotech and "mind uploading" beat for nearly a decade now, mostly writing skeptical pieces about startups that promise immortality and deliver a fancy MRI and a terms-of-service. Lately I've been embedded with a company that does whole-brain emulation for terminal patients: they slice and scan the brain at high resolution and reconstruct a running simulation of it. The founder keeps insisting its not a copy but a continuation, since the original brain is destroyed by the scan and the emulation wakes up with the patient's memories up to the moment of the procedure. The families, for the most part, just want to believe the person is still in there somewhere.

Write a short story set in that world. I'd like it told from the point of view of a journalist on "upload day," following a patient and their adult daughter through the consent process, the final goodbyes, and the moment the emulation first comes online and responds. The story should dramatize the tension between the "same mind continuing" view and the "faithful replacement copy" view, and it should put real pressure on the fidelity issue — maybe the emulation is not quite identical in some small but unsettling way (a forgotten joke, a shifted preference, a slightly different laugh). Don't just have the characters state their positions; show how the divergence, or just the fear of it, tests each of them. And please don't end with a cheap reveal, like the emulation being secretly evil or the whole thing being faked; keep the ambiguity honest.
