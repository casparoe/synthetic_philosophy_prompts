I'm presenting a paper next term at a sociology department seminar and I need a prep sheet for the Q&A. The thesis is that generations — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z and so on — are not real kinds. They're retrospective labels stuck onto arbitrary 15-to-20-year blocks, and the habit of treating them as if they pick out shared psychologies, values, or political subjects is doing bad social science. My positive proposal is that we should mostly drop generation talk in favour of age-period-cohort analysis, talking about specific birth cohorts plus period effects instead. The audience is a mixed UK sociology department: two demographers, a cultural sociologist who works on memory and collective identity, a political sociologist who uses Inglehart-style generational replacement to explain value change, and some critical grad students who will be primed to say either that I'm reifying 'generation' or that I'm being naive about identity categories. I expect the demographers to push on the identification problem: age, period, and cohort are exactly collinear, so how can I claim cohorts are real and the other two are artefacts? The cultural sociologist will probably say I've forgotten Mannheim — that generations aren't about birthdays but about shared historical experience forming a social location. The political sociologist will ask, fine, but generational replacement actually predicts real value change, so what do I put in the regression instead? I can handle those to varying degrees. What worries me most is the objection that I've built a strawman: nobody in the room thinks generations are natural kinds, they think they're social kinds that structure identity and action, so the paper may be aimed at the wrong target. And then there's the positionality jab — this sounds like a Millennial who resents being stereotyped. Give me the objections ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way this audience would actually say them, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is weakest; flag which ones have real force and what I should concede up front rather than fight. Include the question I should be most afraid of and how to answer it without sinking the talk. Please write the replies in British English.
