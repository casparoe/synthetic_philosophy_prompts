In Quinn's self-torturer scenario, a person faces a long sequence of settings on a device that delivers an electric shock, running from no shock to excruciating pain. Each single increment is so small that the person can be offered a modest payment to advance one step and accepts, yet at the end of the sequence the person occupies a state he would have paid a large amount to avoid if offered the whole jump at once. One philosophical reading of this is that the choices reveal rationally permissible intransitive preferences: each adjacent state is strictly preferred to its predecessor given the payment, while the initial state is strictly preferred to the terminal state. A rival, deflationary reading says the apparent cycle is an artifact of something other than preference: adjacent states may be indistinguishable rather than strictly ranked, the per-step stakes may be too small to elicit the agent's actual preferences, or the agent's baseline and reference point may shift as he acclimates along the way.

Write a prompt asking how one could test the conceptual claim that the self-torturer's choices reveal genuinely cyclic strict preferences, rather than a stable transitive preference ordering combined with one of those deflationary mechanisms. The response should set out a concrete experimental design: how to elicit choices between adjacent and non-adjacent states, how to measure discriminability of adjacent states independently of preference, and how to control for stake size, acclimation, and reference-point drift. Then it should state which patterns of results would favor the intransitive-preference reading and which would favor each deflationary reading, and explain why the readings are hard to separate empirically. Finally, it should say what even a clean positive result would not settle; in particular, address whether the question of whether such preferences, if real, are rational is something any experiment could decide, and whether adjacent-step choice data can ever by itself distinguish a semiorder built on just-noticeable differences from a strict-preference cycle.
