so i'm taking a social and political philosophy course and we just did free riding and public goods. nothing earth-shattering, but it lined up weirdly with something that happened. for our tutorial presentation we got a group mark, but the department has this peer evaluation form at the end where you rate each member's contribution and the tutor can adjust someone's individual grade up or down depending on what the others say. one guy in my group ghosted every meeting, claimed he'd cover the intro, and then sent like two bullet points the night before. i filled in the form honestly and now i feel less like i assessed his work and more like i ratted him out.

so here's what i've been stuck on: the policy seems to presuppose that grades should track individual contribution, maybe even desert — that it's wrong for someone to recieve the same mark as everyone else just because the final product is good. but the same university is totally fine with other kinds of free riding. everyone benefits from the few students who actually talk in seminars, and nobody gets penalized for taking those benefits without contributing much back. the library, the reading lists, the lecturer's notes, the study group someone else organized — it's all one big public good and we're all free riding on whoever put in the work.

what theory of fairness (or desert, or reciprocity, or whatever) does the peer evaluation policy actually presuppose, and is it consistent with the rest of the university's practices? or is the policy really just an incentive scheme dressed up in moral language, and if so how would it look different if we admitted that? i have to write a short paper trying to reconstruct the implicit view, and i dont know whether the practice strictly presupposes one particular theory or is compatible with a few different stories.
