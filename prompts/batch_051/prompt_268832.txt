I have been trying to understand the sense in which Solomonoff induction is supposed to formalise Occam's razor. As I understand it, the idea is that simpler hypotheses deserve higher prior probability because they correspond to shorter programs in some fixed, general-purpose programming language. When I put this to a colleague, he dismissed the whole project as merely moving the arbitrariness from the hypothesis to the language, and his argument has been nagging at me since. I am convinced there must be a flaw in it, but I cannot say exactly where it is.

His argument runs as follows:

(1) If simplicity is measured by the length of the shortest program that outputs the data to be explained — or encodes the hypothesis that yields them — then that length is always relative to a programming language.

(2) For any finite data set, one can design a bespoke programming language in which those data are generated by an arbitrarily short program — for instance, a single character stipulated to output exactly that data set.

(3) Therefore, once the data are in hand, any data set can be made to have an arbitrarily short program by choosing the language accordingly.

(4) Therefore, comparisons of program length cannot distinguish genuinely simple hypotheses from contrived ones, since either side can be made to look simpler merely by switching languages.

(5) Hence Solomonoff induction does not genuinely justify Occam's razor; it merely relocates the arbitrariness from the choice of hypothesis to the choice of language.

I find each step individually plausible, yet the conclusion seems far too strong. Is the argument valid? If it is not, say exactly which step fails, why it fails, and what the fixed-language version of the simplicity claim actually commits one to. If you think the argument is sound, say so and explain what, if anything, remains of the idea that Solomonoff induction formalises Occam's razor.

Please also tell me how confident you are in your assessment, and single out the point on which you are least certain.
