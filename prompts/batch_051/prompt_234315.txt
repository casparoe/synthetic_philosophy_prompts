I keep going back to the standard argument for hedonism: if you ask why anything else is good, the chain of reasons is supposed to bottom out in pleasure, so pleasure must be the one thing good for its own sake. But the argument seems to need pleasure to be one kind of stuff, and my own list of pleasures doesnt look like that at all. A warm shower when you're cold, the last step of a hard job, scratching a bug bite, losing a fear you didn't know you had—I cant find a shared ingredient, even though each one is obviously pleasant.

Then I read that neuroscience has its own version of the problem. Wanting and liking look like different systems, and the old brain-stimulation result where an animal just keeps pressing a lever seems to be about a runaway wanting circuit, not a shared pleasure signal. If wanting, liking, and relief are different machinery, the one-common-currency assumption under the hedonist argument is false as a matter of wiring, not just a matter of how it feels.

Does the argument die there, or just need repair? The only fix I can think of is to say pleasure is like health: not one mechanism but a loose cluster of things that still have something in common at a higher level. But then I can't see the difference between saying all good things are pleasures and just saying all good things are good, which would make the view stop saying anything. So: dead, repairable, or does it split into separate hedonisms for each flavor of pleasure?
