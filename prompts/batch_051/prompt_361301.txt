I'm giving a seminar at an astrobiology institute next month defending the claim that the great filter is most likely the origin of eukaryotes, not abiogenesis and not the emergence of intelligence. The argument in a nutshell: life appeared on Earth almost as soon as it was habitable, but the prokaryote-to-eukaryote transition took on the order of two billion years and seems to have happened only once, whereas once eukaryotes existed, complex multicellular life and then tool-using intelligence followed pretty quickly on evolutionary timescales. The audience is mostly working astrobiologists who are comfortable with the Copernican principle but suspicious of philosophers doing statistics, plus a philosopher of science or two who hate uninformative priors, and a SETI person who thinks we've searched a negligable corner of parameter space. What I need is a prep sheet for the Q&A: the eight or so objections this particular room will actually raise, ordered by how hard they'd be to handle, phrased the way an astrobiologist (or that kind of philosopher) would put them, each with a roughly two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. And tell me the question I should be praying nobody asks.
