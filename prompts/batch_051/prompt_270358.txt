I am a novelist, not a logician, and I have been researching paraconsistent logic in order to write a character — a graduate student who has convinced herself that the Liar sentence (the sentence that says of itself that it is false) is both true and false, and that the correct response is not to conclude that everything follows, but to adopt a logic in which contradictions do not explode. I wanted her position to be genuinely defensible rather than a mere plot convenience, so I tried to construct the strongest version of her view I could. In doing so I built the following argument, and I now cannot tell whether it is a serious difficulty or an artifact of my own notation.

Let K be the sentence: “If K is true, then every sentence is true.” If sentences like the Liar are legitimate, K should be legitimate as well. Now reason as follows. (1) Suppose K is true. (2) Since K says exactly that if K is true then every sentence is true, modus ponens applied to (1) and K yields that every sentence is true. (3) So from the supposition that K is true we have derived that every sentence is true. By conditional proof, it follows that if K is true then every sentence is true; but that conditional just is K, so K is true. (4) Since K is true, and K says that if K is true then every sentence is true, modus ponens once more yields that every sentence is true. (5) Therefore, from the mere availability of a sentence structurally like the Liar, together with modus ponens, it follows that every sentence is true — including “0 = 1” and “paraconsistent logic ought to be abandoned.”

The dialetheist’s entire project was to permit true contradictions without collapsing into the view that absolutely everything is true. Yet this argument appears to force exactly that collapse, and it never once appeals to explosion. The only suspicious moment I can detect is that the truth of K seems to be used twice — once to establish itself and once to detach — but I cannot articulate why that should be illegitimate. Which step should my character reject, and what is the price of rejecting it? If she keeps every step, can she honestly maintain that the conclusion does not refute her?
