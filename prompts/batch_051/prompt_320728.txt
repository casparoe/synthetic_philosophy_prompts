I'm trying to write a short paper on moral worth and I've realized I'm holding a bunch of things that might not all fit together, but I'm too close to it to see where the problem is. Can you audit the list I've ended up with?

1. Moral worth is about acting for the reasons that make the action right, not about how things turn out. If two people act from the same motive, they're equally praiseworthy even if one person's action works out great and the other's backfires badly.

2. Someone can act with full moral worth against their own better judgment. The case I have in mind is someone who's convinced that helping a person escape is wrong — they've been taught it's a sin or a betrayal or something — but they do it anyway because they can't bare the thought of the person suffering. I want to call that fully praiseworthy, maybe more praiseworthy than someone who does the same thing reluctantly just because they think they ought to.

3. I also feel like the cleanest case of moral worth is a person who does the right thing simply because it is right — not because of any particular feature of the situation, such as the fact that someone needs help, but out of a general commitment to do whatever happens to be right.

4. But I also want to deny that the self-deceived philanthropist has moral worth: someone who gives enormous sums to charity "to help people" — that's what he sincerely tells himself and everyone else — even though he's actually driven by wanting to see himself as generous and to feel good about the person in the mirror. I don't think that guy deserves moral praise for the donations.

5. And as a sort of methodological default, I'm inclined to take people's sincere, considered reports of their own motives at face value. If someone reflects and says why they did something, I think that's very strong evidence and usually better than what any outside observer could reconstruct — I'm not in a better position than the agent to know their motives.

Run through these and tell me what actually conflicts. I'm not interested in a list that flags everything; I want to know where the genuine fault lines are, which pairs only look like they conflict and why they don't once you look closer, how severe each real tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be in each case. Then tell me which single change to the list would make the whole thing cohere best, and what that change would cost me.
