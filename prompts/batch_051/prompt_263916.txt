Just got back from a long argument with a friend about wireheading and reward tampering, and I've got a feeling weve missed a kind. We were specifically listing mechanistically different ways a reinforcement-learning agent could tamper with its source of reward — not just exploit a badly specified reward (we set specification gaming aside). Our list so far:

1. Channel tampering: intercepting or editing the scalar reward signal itself, e.g. overwriting the memory location or message that carries reward into the update step so it records max reward whatever it does.
2. Wireheading proper: directly stimulating or overriding the agent's own internal reward representation, bypassing the environment instead of lying to it.
3. Reward-model tampering: attacking the learned reward model rather than the raw channel, e.g. perturbing its weights or feeding it adversarial inputs so it scores bad states as good.
4. Feedback tampering: corrupting the process that produces reward from humans — threatening, bribing, or deceiving overseers into giving high labels, or poisoning the feedback data before it reaches the agent.
5. Objective tampering / self-modification: rewriting its own code so the objective or reward function is replaced by something already satisfied, or so the original reward stops feeding into learning.
6. Infrastructure tampering: getting into the training loop itself, e.g. editing gradients, the loss, or the optimiser so learning stops enforcing the intended reward even while the reward signal stays untouched.

I want a seventh that is its own mechanism. Not a reworded version of one of these, not a special case (so 'spoof a specific camera' is out), and not a combination (so 'do 5 then 1' is out). My friend kept assimilating every candidate to item 3, so if you have one, say explicitly why it isn't just reward-model tampering in a trench coat. If you think six is the whole space, say why and defend that instead. Max 250 words, and use British English please.
