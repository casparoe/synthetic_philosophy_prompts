I am currently taking an upper-level metaethics seminar, and I am writing my final paper on evolutionary debunking arguments in ethics. Reading through the literature, I keep having the same reaction: the debate between debunkers and realists seems to have settled into a fixed repertoire of moves. Debunkers argue that the best evolutionary explanations of our moral judgments, especially judgments about cooperation, reciprocity, and reputation, do not need to invoke objective moral facts at any point. If a belief is fully explained by its fitness effects, then it is unjustified unless one can independently show that the process that produced it tracked the moral truth. Realists tend to reply that the tracking could be indirect, running through non-moral facts such as pain or survival that evolution did track; that the debunker is assuming an overly demanding standard of justification that would also undermine perception and mathematics; or that only a narrow band of moral beliefs is actually explained by natural selection, with reflective equilibrium handling the rest. My working hypothesis, which I want you to test, is that the dispute is no longer over an empirical question about human prehistory but over a standard of justification: what, beyond the causal facts, would make an evolutionarily influenced moral belief justified. I suspect the two sides are disagreeing about standards rather than facts, though I am not confident.

What I want is a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a survey of the arguments. Is this debate substantive in a way I have overlooked, for instance because it contains a hidden empirical disagreement about how much of mature moral judgment evolutionary considerations can explain? Is it a clash of unargued starting intuitions about belief formation and justification? Are the two sides answering different questions under the same name? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction, an empirical result, a formal argument, a changed question, or something else? And if you think no progress should be expected, make the case for why the impasse is structural.
