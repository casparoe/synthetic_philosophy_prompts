Dennett's whole move with the self as a center of narrative gravity was that the self isn't a thing in here; it's an abstractum we posit to make the stories about a person hang together, like a center gravity in physics. But now LLMs can generate a perfectly coherent first-person narrative—stable persona, apparent memories, values, even a sense of being wronged—without any of the living, perceiving, persisting stuff underneath. Does the center-of-narrative-gravity view survive that development? I'm not asking whether chatbots are conscious. I want to know which premise of Dennett's argument is actually under pressure, and whether the view can be repaired so it still tells a real narrative self apart from a merely generated one, or whether that distinction was doing load-bearing work the theory never made explicit. If it can't be repaired, say what it should become instead.
