I am a novelist, and while researching a novel set in the software industry I have been puzzled by a practice that seems to carry more philosophical weight than its practitioners acknowledge. Among the programmers I have been reading and listening to, working code functions as the only fully serious form of an idea: a design is not accepted in discussion until someone has prototyped it, and an explanation offered in prose is treated as provisional, hand-waving, or a promissory note that the eventual implementation may fail to honor. At the same time, these practitioners describe writing code as their way of thinking. They do not, they say, first understand a problem and then transcribe that understanding into code; the understanding arrives in the act of making the code run. Programming functions for them simultaneously as the medium of thought and as the criterion that thought has occurred.

I want to reconstruct the philosophical commitments this practice presupposes, because I doubt it is merely a professional habit. Does it presuppose that thought is at bottom computational — that the untaught mind is already running something code-like, and a programming language merely makes that explicit? Or does it presuppose something weaker, such as that anything worth calling an idea must be mechanically executable? I am also trying to see which rival views of understanding the practice quietly rules out: the understanding of a person, a mood, a piece of music, a historical event — are those exclusions acknowledged, or just invisible to the practice? And there is an apparent inconsistency that interests me. The same people who treat running code as the proof of understanding often insist that one truly knows a system only when one can explain it in plain words to someone new. Those two standards seem to locate understanding in different places, and I want to know whether a single theory of thought licenses both, or whether the practice is held together by ambiguity. What exactly does the practice presuppose, what does it merely fit with, and how would the practice have to change if one substituted a rival picture — for instance, a view on which much of thought is non-propositional and cannot be made to run, or a view on which all genuine thought is computational and the plain-words test is only a social courtesy?
