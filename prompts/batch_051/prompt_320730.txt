One of my friends has become a strict internalist about practical reasons: you have a reason to do something only if there's a sound deliberative route from your current motivational set to your doing it, and any alleged reason that floats free of what you already care about is just an "external reason" and not really a reason at all. As a joke he hired a life coach, and now he's furious. Write the deadpan complaint letter he leaves for the coaching company, about 500 words. The humor has to come from getting the internalist position right: he can complain that the coach promised him reasons to excercise without first showing health is (or would be, after sound deliberation) in his motivational set, that "you'll thank yourself later" smuggles in a future motivational set he may never have, that talk about objective values is just external-reasons bluff dressed up in a branded workbook. Make sure he stays consistent—no accidentally reaching for an external reason. The letter should work for someone who's never heard of the internal/external reasons debate, but by the end it should leave the reader with the genuine question: if reasons can only hook onto what a person already cares about, what is advice even doing?
