I cover science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether LLM hallucination is better called lying, bullshit, or plain error. An AI person I interviewed gave me this argument and it seems to prove too much:

(1) Frankfurt's bullshitter isn't the liar: the liar knows the truth and hides it, but the bullshitter just doesn't care whether what he says is true.
(2) When an LLM invents a case citation or a fake anecdote, it isn't trying to deceive — it has no beliefs about the citation at all — and it isn't aiming at truth and missing, which is what error would be.
(3) So LLM fabrication fits the Frankfurt profile: text produced without regard for how things actually are.
(4) But by that standard, autocomplete, a Markov chain, or a parrot squawking memorized strings also count as bullshit whenever the output fails to match the world.
(5) Yet those systems don't have a stance toward truth to neglect — they're not indifferently bullshitting; they're just processes.
(6) So either Frankfurt's account overgenerates to mindless systems, or step (3) helped itself to a richer notion of bullshit than (1) actually gives.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd like to keep the idea that hallucination isn't ordinary lying or ordinary error, but I don't want to be forced to say my phone's autocomplete is a bullshitter. What am I paying?
