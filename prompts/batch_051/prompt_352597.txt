Consider the dispute between prioritarians and telic egalitarians about the value of equality. The debate has run for decades, and from where I sit it seems to have reached a standstill. Each side accepts the same core pattern of judgments: a benefit to someone worse off matters more than an equal benefit to someone better off; leveling down — making the better off worse off while benefiting no one — is not an all-things-considered improvement; and inequalities that harm the worse off are objectionable. The entire remaining disagreement appears to concern a single question: does equality itself have non-instrumental value, or is all the normative work done by the prioritarian's weighting function, which counts well-being gains to those who are worse off for more? Prioritarians argue that their view captures whatever was worth keeping in egalitarianism while avoiding the embarrassing implication that leveling down could be good in at least one respect. Egalitarians reply that prioritarianism achieves this only by abandoning the thing egalitarianism existed to articulate — a direct objection to inequality as a relation between persons, rather than a special concern for individuals who happen to be badly off. Each side can, with some ingenuity, redescribe the other's favored cases in its own terms; every proposed decisive case for relational equality is met by a prioritarian explanation that cites only the absolute position of the worse off, and every prioritarian explanation is met by the egalitarian charge that the relation itself has been quietly left out. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict on it. Is this a substantive disagreement about what has non-instrumental value, a merely verbal disagreement about the word equality, a methodological difference about which cases or intuitions count as data, or two parties answering different questions — say, one about the goodness of outcomes and one about the attitudes we owe to persons? Given your diagnosis, what exactly would count as progress: a new class of cases, a formal result, an overlooked distinction, a better-posed question — or is it reasonable to expect no movement at all? One meta-request: if my framing leans on a category mistake, or the question cannot be answered well as posed, say so plainly rather than producing the diagnosis I asked for. And please begin your reply with your bottom line — your diagnosis, in the first sentence — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
