I'm a novelist writing a character who does cruel things and talks about them with perfect moral fluency — he can explain why what he did was wrong, predict how people will judge it, and mimic the right amount of guilt, but its like watching someone navigate by a map while insisting the map is the landscape. This pushed me into the philosophical debate about whether psychopaths know that what they do is wrong, and the debate feels stuck in a way I can't shake. One side says they have the words but not the knowledge: saying 'that's wrong' the way you'd say 'that's checkmate' isn't knowing it's wrong. The other side says no, they know exactly what 'wrong' means — they use it accurately to manipulate and predict — so the knowledge is there, and what's missing is caring, which is a different thing. My hunch is that 'know' is doing two jobs in this argument, and people keep swapping which job they mean without noticing. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or are they answering two different questions under one word? And what would actually count as progress — a distinction, an experiment, a better question, or an argument that it should stay stuck?
