During my career in the physical sciences, I learned to distrust explanations that do not show a causal path from an action to an outcome. That habit is probably why I keep returning to the following idea from recent decision theory. Consider a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma between two agents who run the same decision procedure, in a world large enough that many similar agents exist or might exist. Causal decision theory says to defect, because the counterpart's move is fixed by the time one acts. But an evidential argument says cooperation can still be the maximizing choice: conditional on this agent cooperating, the probability that similar counterparts cooperate is higher, so the act is evidence about what one will receive. Call this evidential cooperation in large worlds. The striking feature is that the act need not change anything outside the agent's own decision procedure to be worth doing in this sense.

I would like a proposal for a term paper of roughly six thousand words, one semester, on the following question: does evidential cooperation in large worlds supply a reason of the right kind to ground a moral requirement to cooperate, or does it supply only an epistemic or prudential reason—a reason to take oneself to be the sort of agent whose counterparts will probably cooperate? My suspicion is that it yields the latter, and that this is why the argument can feel morally inert even when its arithmetic is sound; but I want the project designed so that suspicion could be refuted.

The proposal should state the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why the usual readings of the evidential result leave it open, and lay out an approach. One candidate approach is to construct stripped-down cases that vary the causal channel and the evidential channel independently: no causal connection but full evidential connection; full causal connection but an evidential link broken by private information; and a case in which an agent chooses in advance which decision procedure to adopt. The proposal should say why this kind of case-by-case isolation is better than trading intuitions about the standard examples. It should also identify the possible conclusions, the considerations that would decide between them, what would count as a failed version of the project, and what could make the project too large for one semester. Please do not provide a bibliography or trace citations; I will fill those in myself. The proposal should refer to positions and arguments by their content rather than their sources.
