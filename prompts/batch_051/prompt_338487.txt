After a scammer got my cousin and it turned out she couldn't do anything because the statute of limitations had run, I went down a rabbit hole on why we even have these deadlines. The defenses people give all start to sound the same: fading evidence, letting defendants get on with their lives, not rewarding people who sleep on their rights. I want to seperate which of these are doing real moral work, not just practical or administrative work. So here's the ask: rank the standard rationales for statutes of limitations (and feel free to add ones I'm missing) by how much moral weight they still carry if we imagine the epistemic problem of stale evidence is solved (perfect records, no memory decay). Give a tier list or an ordering with a sentence for each rationale. End with a final line in the format \answer{first > second > third} that just gives the bare ordering.
