I was elected to the membership committee of my housing cooperative only last month, and the committee is now deciding whether to suspend the membership of a resident who has spent the year using our open meetings to argue that certain neighbours should be deprived of voting rights on account of their religion and national origin. He has not threatened anyone and has not violated the letter of our current rules; his conduct has been relentless advocacy and procedural manoeuvring. Our chair keeps ending discussion by repeating the slogan "we can't tolerate intolerance," as though it were the practical lesson of the paradox of tolerance and as though it settled the matter. Before I vote, I want to know exactly what that slogan commits us to. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims it might be expressing, from the most trivial to the most radical, and say for each reading whether it is true, false, or merely misleading, and whether it would in fact justify suspending or expelling this member? I need a menu of readings to take to the next meeting rather than one settled interpretation; please also say which reading the chair most likely intends and which readings would license more than we intend. In particular, I want help separating readings about what tolerant members should feel from readings about what the cooperative as a body may or must do, and readings about the member's attitudes from readings about his conduct.
