I'm a backend engineer at a company that runs a small developer Q&A site, and I own the answer-ranking logic — basically the code that decides which answers people see first and how votes get surfaced. We show a vote count next to every answer, default-sort by score, and throw a "trending" badge on answers that got their first few votes quickly. I've noticed a pattern that bugs me: an answer that picks up two or three early upvotes often runs away with it, even when a later answer is clearly more accurate, and we've had a few cases where the community locked onto a wrong answer for days because it was first. I've read just enough about information cascades and rational herding to suspect this is one of those, but I don't have the background to know how much of that actually transfers to online voting, and I don't want to cargo-cult a theory I half-understand into our product.

So here's what I'd like: a reusable procedure — a checklist or decision tree, in plain language — for evaluating any proposed change to how we rank, sort, or display votes and badges. It should tell me how to distinguish the bad kind of cascade, where early votes drag everyone else along even though people would privately disagree, from cases where early votes are actually informative and we should just leave the feature alone. It has to be something I can run in a 30-minute design review, with no formal econ or philosophy background, and it should flag the places where I'm really just making a judgment call instead of pretending the theory resolves it. Test it on three concrete cases we're currently considering: (1) hiding vote counts for the first two hours after an answer is posted; (2) defaulting to an "oldest first" sort instead of "highest score"; and (3) adding a "controversial" sort that surfaces answers with close to a 50/50 vote split.

And please make at least one of the worked cases a place where your procedure tells me my own favorite idea is wrong or incomplete — I'd rather get that than a procedure that just flatters what I already want to ship. Also, if I'm leaning on a confused premise — say "information cascade" doesn't really apply to online answer ranking the way I think, or the line between cascades and people just reasonably trusting highly-upvoted answers is too murky to build a procedure on — say that outright rather than handing me a checklist anyway.
