In the novel I'm working on, a character spends an afternoon trying to free a seagull tangled in fishing line — nearly drowns himself doing it — and then an hour later watches a fox kill a rabbit and thinks, well, that's just the place working as it should. A reader told me the two reactions contradict each other, and now I cant stop noticing the same thing in real life. We'll crowdfund an operation for a stray cat that got hit by a car, but almost nobody thinks we should stop a lion from killing a zebra even when we could do so easily. A boy who tortures a mouse is chilling in a way a cat doing the exact same thing isn't. Is there any defensible reason for these asymmetries, or are they just sentimentality and squeamishness? If you defend them, defend them against the case where the suffering and the cost of preventing it are identical and the only difference is whether a human caused it or nature did.
