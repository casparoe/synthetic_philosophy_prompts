In the classic vignette behind the term "sphexish," a digger wasp stings a cricket, drags it to the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, then returns for the cricket; if an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away during the inspection, the wasp repeats the inspection-and-return sequence indefinitely rather than simply dragging the cricket in. I have been using three criteria for calling behavior genuinely flexible rather than sphexish: the agent varies its means when circumstances change, abandons a routine once the routine stops serving the goal, and generalizes competently to novel situations. Now apply these criteria to a model-based AI planning system that can replan around novel obstacles, abandons failed routes, and generalizes across unseen environments, but whose objective function is fixed externally—when the goal becomes unreachable, it does not revise or abandon the goal, only recomputes the best attainable approximation forever. Is that system flexible or sphexish? Work through the criteria, and where the verdict is unclear, say whether the criteria themselves confuse flexibility of means with flexibility of ends, and whether that distinction should matter for ascriptions of free will.
