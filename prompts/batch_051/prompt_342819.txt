One thing I keep coming back to from a seminar on early Chinese philosophy is the old standoff between Mozi's impartial care and the Confucian idea that care is naturally graded by closeness. Mozi, on my reading, thought partiality is the root of a lot of harm—wars, theft, feuds—and that the cure is to care about other families, clans, and states the way you care about your own, reinforced by the thought that impartial care actually pays off in peace and cooperation. The Confucian reply was that this runs against the grain: affection starts with parents and children and thins out as you move outward, and a decent society works with that grain instead of pretending we can or should love a stranger in another state exactly as much as our own mother.

I want to use the 2021 vaccine-nationalism episode as a test case for this debate, because it's one of the rare moments where governments had to do something like applied moral philosophy in public. Roughly: rich countries had secured far more doses than they needed for first shots and were moving to boosters, while COVAX was still trying to get first doses to health workers in low-income countries. What would Mozi's impartial care say a rich-country government owed here, and is it even fair to apply it to a state that's suppose to prioritize its own citizens? What does graded love say—does it end up licensing exactly the hoarding that happened, or can it condemn it from within its own resources? Then I want the view flipped: what does this episode do to each framework? Does it make impartial care look noble but politically impossible, and graded love look like a decent account of moral psychology but a convenient cover for national selfishness? Or does the episode mostly reveal that both were aimed at a different question—more about individual cultivation and social order than about distributive justice between states—and that neither should be forced to speak to it? I know the facts well enough; I want a disciplined mapping of theory onto case, both directions, with an honest account of where each framework goes quiet.
