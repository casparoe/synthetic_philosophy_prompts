Here’s a thing that’s been bugging me. Take congregations that hold the regulative principle of worship: the rule that corporate worship may include only what Scripture commands, so whatever is not commanded is forbidden. They’ll use the principle to ban a guitar or a choir anthem, since the New Testament nowhere commands instruments or anthems. But the same congregation meets in a heated, air-conditioned room, projects lyrics on a screen, prints a bulletin, and amplifies the preacher through a mic. None of those things are commanded either, and a couple of them shape the service as much as a guitar would. Is there a principled line here, or is it just selective traditionalism? I want the strongest version of the distinction these churches might be tracking (something like elements vs. circumstances, where the substance of worship is fixed but the practical scaffolding is left to prudence), and an honest verdict on whether it survives hard cases like the projector, the PA system, and a metrical psalm in English — a rhymed, metered translation made for singing. Don’t just defend the thing lazily; press on it until you can say whether the asymmetry should be kept or dropped.
