Every time my uncle and I argue about whether our country should get involved in some overseas conflict, he eventually pulls out "those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it." His actual argument seems to be that Munich proved appeasing an aggressor always makes things worse, and the slogan is doing the work of "we already learned this lesson." When I point out that Vietnam is the usual counterexample — treating every regional strongman as Hitler can get you a different disaster — he just repeats the line, slower. I want to take the slogan itself apart instead. What are the distinct claims that line could actually be making, from the basically trivial to the substantive or probably false? For each reading: is it the sort of thing that would actually support his Munich-therefore-act conclusion, and does it survive the Vietnam reply, or does it quietly assume the very thing we're fighting about (that this case is a Munich and not a Vietnam)? Lay them out as a menu I can send him, with verdicts.
