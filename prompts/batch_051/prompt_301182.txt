Right, here's the situation. I sit on the Global Speech Review Board at Loom, a social platform (fictional, but run with it). We're incorporated in the US, but only about a third of our users are there. Last Tuesday we got a formal order from a data protection authority in an EU member state to delist a six-year-old video posted by an account in Ohio. The video is a news-style explainer about a regional politician's old drunk-driving conviction. Under our own rules it stays up: it's accurate, non-harassing, and the conviction is on the public record. The authority says that under their right to erasure the video has to be delisted globally, not just geo-blocked, because a delisting that only hides it from EU IP addresses is trivially circumventable. Our US counsel says global delisting would be prior restraint on American speech and would turn Loom into the enforcement arm of every jurisdiction with a law we find distasteful.

I need a memo for the Board, say 900–1200 words, recommending what we should do: comply globally, delist only in the EU, or refuse and litigate. The memo needs a clear bottom line and a fair treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.

You're an expert on internet governance and political philosophy. Start by brainstorming the strongest arguments on all sides before you commit — don't just pattern-match to 'free speech good.' In particular, we've been going back and forth on three default rules for who's law should govern a piece of cross-border speech, and I'd like you to take a position on them somewhere in the memo: (a) the law of where the poster is, (b) the law of where the content is seen, or (c) the platform's own community standards as a kind of private constitution that no single state can override. Is there a defensible principle here, or is any choice just picking which state gets to be sovereign and dressing it up as philosophy?

One more thing — write it in British English (colour not color, 'the Board are' if that's your thing), and don't just tell me the legal doctrine; I want the conceptual position underneath it.
