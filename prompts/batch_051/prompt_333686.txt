Longtime rationalist-blog lurker here, so I care less whether a scheme is nice in theory and more about the actual incentive gradient it produces. A friend group of mine has gone hard on tranquilism — the view that the only thing that matters is the absence of craving/suffering, and that wanting nothing is the only real win. They live in a shared house and want to install this norm: every time someone voices a craving or future-directed want in common space or the group chat ("I want the dishes done", "I hope it doesn't rain Saturday", "can we talk about something else"), they put $2 in the jar. Each week the jar is split evenly among everyone who self-reported zero voiced cravings that week. Anyone can flag an unlogged voicing; if the others at the weekly check-in agree by majority that it was a craving, the speaker pays double and the flagger gets the $2. Past-tense reports and bare sensory descriptions are free.

I dont want to litigate whether tranquilism is true; I want to know how this actually runs. What does it do to what gets said, to who ends up with the money, and to whether anyone actually stops craving? Where does it get gamed first — do people launder wants as predictions, complaints about other peoples cravings, or "I notice the thought arising that..."? Does it select for cravinglessness or for performative equanimity, one-upsmanship, and strategic silence? And can the obvious failure modes be fixed without turning the house into a policing apparatus that generates its own cravings around status, fairness, and being seen as the least attached?
