Article IV of our confederation's 1871 compact reads: "The high contracting parties, being resolved that the Confederation shall present one commercial frontier to the world, agree that no member state shall conclude any treaty or arrangement of commerce with a foreign power, nor alter the common tariff, nor open any port to foreign goods save by common ordinance; any such act shall be null and void." A member state, Varenne, has just concluded a Digital Economy Accord with a non-member power. The accord provides for the cross-border flow of data, mutual recognition of electronic signatures and certifications, a shared framework for taxing digital services, and a standing joint committee to align future digital-trade measures. It says nothing about duties, quotas, or the physical entry of goods. The compact predates every technology at issue by more than a century. Three other members have petitioned the tribunal to declare the accord void under Article IV. Does Article IV reach it? Argue the question through the words as they would have been read in 1871, the purpose of a single commercial frontier, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable member state would now understand the clause to prohibit. Tell me which approach should prevail, and what your answer implies about what the member states actually surrendered when they joined.
