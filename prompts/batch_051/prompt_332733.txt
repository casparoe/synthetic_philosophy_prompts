I'm in a philosophy of physics class right now and I've developed a habit I want pressure-tested. My policy is: when I'm trying to understand why some physical thing happens the way it does, I only count a symmetry argument as a real explanation. Last week in section we were talking about why a free particle keeps moving at constant velocity in empty space. To me, 'because spacetime is homogeneous, so momentum is conserved, so constant velocity' felt like the only real answer, and my classmate's story about no forces acting just sounded like a restatement, not a why. I've been applying the same rule everywhere: if it doesnt route through a symmetry, its just description. My roommate says I'm overcorrecting and that plenty of good explanations are causal or mechanistic and never mention symmetry at all. Is my habit defensible? What is symmetry actually adding when it looks explanatory, and when does it overreach? And is there a version of the rule that keeps what I like about it without dismissing causal explanations wholesale?
