The Treaty of Versailles include a "war guilt" clause: Germany accepted responsibility for all the loss and damage caused by the war, and that clause was used to justify reparations. At the same time, basically no individual Germans were put on trial for starting the war; the blame was assigned to the nation itself. Then the Fischer controversy in the 1960s set off decades of fighting over whether the documents actually showed that Imperial Germany had deliberately pushed for a European war, yet the whole argument treated it as obvious that a nation -- not just its leaders -- could be the kind of thing that causes and is responsible for a war. What theory of collective responsibility does the war guilt clause actually presuppose?
