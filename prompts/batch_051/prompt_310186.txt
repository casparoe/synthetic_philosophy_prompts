I have been reading about how various European countries regulate commercial surrogacy and paid egg donation, and I keep noticing a configuration for which I have no name. A country makes it a criminal offence to pay a surrogate or an egg donor on its own territory, and it defends the prohibition by saying that payment turns women's bodies into commodities and exploits poorer women. Yet the same country will, when its citizens have obtained those services in a country where payment is lawful, recognise the intended parents as the child's legal parents and grant the child citizenship. The state is entirely open about this, so the arrangement is not hidden hypocrisy; nor is it simply private evasion of a law, since the state's own institutions validate the outcome of the foreign transaction. I am trying to work out whether this two-level structure—a domestic prohibition combined with official recognition of the legal results of the same practice when carried out abroad—has an established name. If it does, I would like to know how it differs from the nearest related concepts, such as fertility tourism and regulatory arbitrage; if it does not, I would like a proposed term, defined in a way that marks the boundaries of the phenomenon rather than merely labelling it. Please answer in British English.
