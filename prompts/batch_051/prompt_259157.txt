Me and my flatmates got into it at dinner last night and it's still bugging me. One of them (A) announced she's cutting fish out of her diet and switching to prawns and mussels, on the grounds that fish sentience is pretty well established whereas crustaceans and bivalves are much less likely to feel pain. Our other flatmate (B) said that was backwards. His first move was that farmed prawns live in crowded, mucky ponds and are usually killed by boiling or asphyxiation, whereas a wild-caught fish at least had something like a natural life before the trawl — and that if there's any real chance the prawns suffer, the sheer numbers involved in prawn farming make the switch a welfare disaster. She came back that you can't run ethics on 'any real chance' — we'd never mow grass or swallow a mouthful of bacteria if bare possibility were enough — and that what matters is the strength of the evidence, which is much weaker for crustaceans than for teleost fish. He replied that this is a double standard: the fish-pain literature is itself contested, with plenty of scientists reading fish responses as nociception without conscious suffering, so if 'contested' is enough to discount prawns it's enough to discount fish; her line, he said, is just being drawn where her dinner happens to be.

Who's right about what here? Please adjudicate move by move — rate each of the four moves seperately from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating, then say what, if anything, both of them are missing. And could you write your answer in British English?
