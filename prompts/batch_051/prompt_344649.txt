Question: "Explain the no-trade theorem and what it does and does not imply about speculative trade in financial markets."

Student answer: "The Milgrom–Stokey no-trade theorem says that when traders are rational Bayesians with a common prior, a trade based only on differences in information can't be common knowledge as mutually beneficial: if someone is willing to buy at a price, the other side infers the buyer's private information, revises his own beliefs, and refuses, and vice versa. So the sheer volume of speculative trade we actually observe looks like a serious problem for the rational-expectations view. This is basically the same point as Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result — with common priors and Bayesian rationality there's no room for disagreement, so once each side sees the other's willingness to trade they should converge on one valuation. Since they conspicuously don't, the no-trade theorem implies rational informed agents can't speculate at all, and the observed volume must be pretty direct evidence that traders are behaving irrationally."
