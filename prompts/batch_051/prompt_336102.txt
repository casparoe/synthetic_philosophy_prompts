I'm in a class on philosophy of language and AI, and there's a move my classmates keep making that I can't quite pin down. Whenever someone brings up ChatGPT confidently inventing references or giving a fake-but-polished answer, one of my classmates says the model is basically Frankfurt's bullshitter: it doesn't care whether what it says is true, it just cares about producing something that sounds right for the situation. He then uses that to argue that calling these outputs "hallucinations" does real work — a hallucination sounds like a glitch you patch, while bullshit sounds like something you'd hold a person responsible for. So the analogy is being used to license two claims: (1) the model's outputs are bullshit in the strict Frankfurt sense, and (2) "hallucination" talk is itself a bit of bullshit and lets the developers off the hook.

A different friend pushes back that an LLM can't bullshit or lie because it has no intentions at all, so the output is just error — and if anyone is lying, it's the company marketing the thing as reliable. I'm trying to work out whether the analogy is actually doing philosophical work or is just rhetorically handy.
