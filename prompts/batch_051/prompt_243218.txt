Dewey's whole pitch was that inquiry is experimental: you treat a policy as a hypothesis, act on it, and revise in light of the consequences. He also thought a public only forms when people affected by the indirect consequences of some activity notice those consequences and start deliberating about them. Now suppose a platform can run continuous private A/B tests on its users — feed rankings, prices, moderation rules — and watch the consequences in real time, while the affected users never form a public or even know an experiment is running. Does Dewey's experimentalism about democracy survive this development, or does cheap private experimentation split off the experimental half of his view from the democratic half? In exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, say which premise the development bears on, whether the argument still goes through, needs repair, or is dead, and whether it can be restored in another form.
