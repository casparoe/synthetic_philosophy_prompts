I'm a journalist who reads a lot of history in my spare time, and I keep tripping over a pattern I don't have a word for. Take dueling. For a couple hundred years, moralists and clergy condemned it, and in plenty of places it was illegal, but men kept doing it because walking away from a challenge meant losing face in front of their peers. Then, in the space of a generation or two in the nineteenth century, it pretty much collapsed in Britain and the United States. But if I look at what actually changed, it wasn't that somebody finally landed a new, devastating argument against it. The arguments were all old. What changed was more that the whole thing started to seem ridiculous—like theatrical aristocrats playing at being knights, something a serious middle-class man would be embarrassed to be mixed up in. And once that happened, the very same people who had shrugged off the moral case for years started repeating it as if it were the thing that had persuaded them.

I've seen the same shape in the end of footbinding in China, at least as popular histories tell it. It lasted for centuries and there was always critics, but it unraveled with surprising speed around the turn of the twentieth century—not because someone came up with a new moral consideration, but because it became a mark of backwardness, a humiliation on the national stage, so that respectable families lost face for doing it. Then afterward the old arguments got promoted into the official cause, and the fact that they had been hanging around impotently for centuries got quietly forgotten.

Is there a name for this exact two-stage thing? First, a longstanding practice is dislodged mainly by a shift in what counts as respectable or shameful, not by new arguments or new material incentives. Second, the arguments that were available and ineffective all along get written back into the story as the real cause. I'm not just after a label, though—I want to know what this is and isn't. Is it just Whig history, or something narrower? Were the old arguments necessary preconditions that had to be lying there waiting for the respectability flip, or is the story that the arguments finally won mostly a flattering after-the-fact tale people tell about their own side? If there's no established word for the whole pattern, how would you name and define it, and what would be a borderline case that looks like this but fails at the last moment?
