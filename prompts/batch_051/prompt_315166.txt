I'm putting together a handout for a philosophy reading group on the Trinity — mostly people who've done a little analytic philosophy but aren't specialists — and I want a "which account of the Trinity are you actually committed to?" decision tree. It should start from the bare doctrine phrased blandly: the Father is God, the Son is God, the Holy Spirit is God, the three are distinct persons, and yet there is only one God. The tree should walk someone through the logical forks until they land in a named view.

I'd like these leaves to show up: social trinitarianism, the Latin / classical one-substance view with subsistent relations, the relative-identity account, the constitution view (statue/lump style), mysterianism, and the two heretical destinations people tend to arrive at accidentally, modalism and tritheism.

The branch nodes should be about concrete commitments, not about which label people like. The forks I suspect do the actual work are: wether "God" is functioning as a proper name or as a nature-term; whether identity is absolute or relative to a sortal; whether there is one divine mind and will or three; and whether the distinction between persons is eternal or only something we see in how God relates to creation. Use whichever forks actually separate the leaves, and drop any that don't do work.

Phrase each node so someone can answer without knowing which view they're heading toward — so no "do you accept the relative identity move?" nodes. Also flag any combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy and say briefly why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?), and flag pairs of leaves the tree can't really seperate even though the labels differ. One sentence per leaf on its most awkward commitment would be great.
