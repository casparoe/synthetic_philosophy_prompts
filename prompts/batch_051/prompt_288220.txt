I’ve been trying to figure out how to update my beliefs when I learn a conditional probability rather than an outright fact. This came up because I’ve been applying to grad schools and a mentor told me my offhand statement — “if I get into a top program, there’s a 60% chance I’ll go” — should maybe be treated like the old Judy Benjamin problem, where a soldier is dropped into a war zone divided into four regions (Red HQ, Red Company, Blue HQ, Blue Company), starts with each equally likely, and then gets a radio message saying that if she’s in Red territory, the probability she’s at HQ is 3/4. My gut on that original case is: the message only tells me something about the conditional within Red, so I should keep my overall Red probability at 1/2, keep my Blue probabilities exactly as they were, and just shift Red HQ to 3/8 and Red Company to 1/8.

Here are a few more of my verdicts, and I’d like to know what principle, if any, captures them. If the prior is non-uniform — say Red HQ 0.1, Red Company 0.4, Blue HQ 0.2, Blue Company 0.3 — and I get the same message, I still want to leave Blue untouched and just adjust within Red to make Red HQ 0.375 and Red Company 0.125. If I already have P(HQ|Red)=0.75, hearing the message again changes nothing. If I know I’m in Red with probability 1, then learning that same conditional is just learning P(HQ)=0.75, so I update to 0.75 for HQ. But here’s the part that’s bugging me: if I learn two conditional probabilities, say P(HQ|Red)=0.75 and then P(Red|HQ)=0.75, I feel like the order shouldn’t matter. But when I apply my “just fix the relevant conditional and leave the rest alone” rule one message at a time, I get different final probabilities depending on which message I process first. So either my rule is wrong, my order-independence intuition is wrong, or I need a different way to handle simultaneous constraints.

As an expert in formal epistemology, please think carefully and start by brainstorming candidate principles — Adams conditioning, relative entropy minimization, something else — then test them against my verdicts. Which of my verdicts would each principle save, and which would it overturn? What do you think the best principle is, stated precisely, and what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: whether the original Judy Benjamin message should also shift my overall Blue probability upward, as I’ve seen claimed for the minimum relative entropy update? I want an honest assessment of whether my gut is coherent or whether I’m just pattern-matching to a rule that falls apart under more than one message.
