I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've caught myself outsourcing parts of my job to AI tools in ways I would've swore I'd never outsource. I have clear gut verdicts about some cases and no idea what to say about others. Can you help me find the principle behind them?

- I run an interview transcript through an AI to strip out the ums and false starts and give me a rough summary. I check any quote I actually use against the recording. Verdict: fine.
- I get an AI to translate the key paragraph of a paper in a language I only half-know. I back-translate as a sanity check, I can tell it's plausible, but I couldn't catch a subtle error. I flag the translation for my editor. Verdict: fine.
- I get an AI to summarize a dense 40-page paper I don't have time to read, and I write the news item mostly from the summary, never looking past the abstract. Verdict: not fine.
- After doing all the reporting and actually understanding the study, I let an AI draft the plain-English "what does this mean" section of my story and then edit it hard. Verdict: fine, if a bit embarrassing.
- I have an AI draft an explainer on a field I've never covered, and I edit it into shape while learning the field as I go. Verdict: not fine. It feels like laundering an understanding I don't have.
- I let a coding assistant write a script that runs a statistical test for me, even though I don't really understand the test. The numbers look fine. Verdict: not fine.

My rough theory is that the AI is fine when it does something mechanical that I could check and would otherwise do myself—transcribing, translating, wordsmithing—and not fine when it does the understanding itself, like synthesizing a paper or deciding what a statistical result means. But the translation case doesn't fit neatly: I can't fully check the translation either, and I'm basically taking it on trust like I would a human translator. And then there's the case I keep going back and forth on: I'm about to receive a 10,000-page document dump, and I want the AI to flag the newsworthy pages and then read only those. My half-baked theory says that's not fine, but the realistic alternative is not covering the story at all.

What principle, or small set of candidate principles, best captures my verdicts? State it precisely, say which of my verdicts it can't accommodate or has to fudge, and tell me what it says about the document-triage case and one or two other journalist-AI cases I haven't thought of. I want the reasoning, not the literature, so no citations. Keep the whole answer under 500 words.
