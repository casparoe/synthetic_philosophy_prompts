Define a "legitimate stakeholder" in a neighborhood's housing and zoning decisions. I want a definition that clearly includes both a fifth-generation owner-occupier on the block and a long-term renter in the building next door, and clearly excludes a tourist visiting for the weekend. Then classify each of these, with a definite answer either way: the adult child who grew up on the block but has been priced out to a nearby town; a commuter who works in the neighborhood five days a week but lives elsewhere; a landlord who owns twelve apartments there but lives abroad; a homeless person who sleeps in the neighborhood park; a family from another part of the country who would rent a unit in the proposed building; and the developer proposing it. I lean toward counting the priced-out child and the future tenants as stakeholders and the absentee landlord as not, but I do not yet have a principle that yields that. Propose the definition, run it through all nine cases, and tell me where it surprises you or where you have to revise it.
