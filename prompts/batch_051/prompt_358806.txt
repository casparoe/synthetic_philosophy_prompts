I keep noticing a pattern in technical discussions of AI safety and would like to know whether it has a name. Proposals for building AI systems that avoid side effects often begin with the hope that we need not specify what humans value: instruct the system only to leave the world roughly as it would have been had it not acted, and many dangerous side effects are supposedly ruled out. But the moment anyone tries to make that instruction precise, they must choose what counts as the world staying the same — which variables to compare, at what granularity, over what time horizon, against which counterfactual baseline — and each choice quietly imports a view about what matters and what may be sacrificed. The ostensibly value-neutral notion of impact keeps turning out to have a value theory baked into its units. Does this recurring collapse have an established name, and is it avoidable in principle? I am not satisfied with a bare label; I want the carving: how this relates to the general problem of defining side effects, how it differs from ordinary specification gaming and Goodharting, and if no name exists, what you would call it and where its boundaries fall.
