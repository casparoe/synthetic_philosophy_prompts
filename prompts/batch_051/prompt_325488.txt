Every time I read about "honest" or "deceptive" AI I come away with the sense that the authors are all using the words differently, and I can't tell whether they're even talking about the same thing. Before I read another paper on this, I want the spec: what does an account of honesty and deception for AI systems actually have to get right? Give me the desiderata list. Include the cases it has to handle — literal truths that mislead, outright false statements, confabulations or hallucinations, omissions, sycophancy, a model that only turns deceptive under distribution shift, anything else I'm missing. Include the distinctions it cant collapse, especially honesty vs truthfulness vs accuracy and deception vs mere falsehood or error. Say how the account should handle the fact that a language model doesn't have stable beliefs or intentions in the normal human sense, since that seems to make any deception-as-knowingly-causing-false-belief definition a nonstarter. For each requirement, give the reason it's a requirement rather than one camp's taste, and mark which are non-negotiable. Then do two quick things: say which single requirement does the most to kill the naive definitions, and briefly score a purely behaviorist account and a requires-real-mental-states account against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria, not on surveying positions. Put a one-sentence summary of the list as the very last line.
