I sit on the students' union discretionary fund committee at a London university. Our constitution says the committee "shall make grants to the applications expected to bring about the greatest total benefit to the student body." There is one pot left this term, and two bids are before us. The first is from a student with a severe mobility impairment whose accessible tenancy fall through at the last minute; he needs £4,000 towards a term's rent in an adapted flat near campus, and without it he will probably have to suspend his studies. The second is from the film society, which wants £4,000 to put on a free film festival; around 600 students are expected to attend over the three days. On our usual scoring, the festival plainly wins on total benefit. One committee member says this is a mistake: you cannot treat one person's serious harm as if it could be cancelled out by piling up hundreds of small enjoyments, and the rule was never meant to do that. Another member says the rule says what it says, and being moved by the student's case is not an interpretation. Could you draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting? It should recommend a definite decision on which bid to fund (or, if you think the rule itself needs reinterpreting, a definite interpretation of it), give us reasons we can act on, and honestly engage the strongest point on the side you are ruling against. Don't give me a menu of options—take a firm line and defend it.
