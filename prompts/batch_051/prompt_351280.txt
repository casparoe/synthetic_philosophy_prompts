A state film fund's conditions for historical drama contain the following two clauses:

1. "No project may knowingly attribute to an actual person, living or dead, any speech, action, or motive for which the documentary record gives no support."
2. "A drama must present a person, not an archive. Where the record is silent about the private scenes and inner life of such a person, the project is expected to supply invented but plausible scenes and motives, provided these are characteristic of the person's known commitments."

The clauses appear to point in opposite directions: the first seems to forbid the invented private scenes and inner motives the second seems to require. But perhaps the fund would not have published both if it thought them unreconcilable.

Work through whether they can be reconciled, and at what cost. In particular: (a) give the reading of "attribute," "no support," and "characteristic" on which both clauses can genuinely govern a single film, and the reading on which they cannot; (b) specify the exact conditions under which a wholly invented scene about a real dead person would be permissible on your reconciled reading, and say whether biopics that advertise themselves as faithful to the record could meet them; (c) identify which reading gives each clause independent work and which reading turns one clause into a dead letter; and (d) conclude with the interpretation the fund could honestly have intended, stating the principle about what we owe the real dead that this interpretation embodies.
