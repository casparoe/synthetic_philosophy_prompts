Question: Explain why petitionary prayer to an omniscient and immutable God might seem pointless or incoherent, and then evaluate one serious attempt to show that such prayer is nevertheless coherent or worthwhile.

Student's answer:

The obvious problem is that petitionary prayer seems to be an attempt to get God to do something, but an omniscient God already knows what we need before we ask, and an immutable God cannot change His mind or His plan. If God is immutable, then there is no point in asking Him to do something He was not already going to do, because that would require God to change. It follows that prayer cannot influence what God does. Prayer can still be worthwhile, however, because its real purpose is to change the person who prays: it fosters humility, gratitude, and trust, and it reorders the person's desires toward God's will. A critic might say this turns petition into mere self-improvement. In response, one can say that God, from eternity, could have willed to bring about certain goods only on the condition that they are requested, so the request is a genuine cause of those goods even though God never changes His mind. Since God is outside time, though, the prayer and the answer are simultaneous from God's point of view; so it is more accurate to say that prayer accompanies the blessing than that it causes it. In the end, petitionary prayer is coherent, but only if we give up the idea that it moves God and instead see it as a way of placing ourselves within a plan God has already fixed.
