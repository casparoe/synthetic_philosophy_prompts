I keep going in circles on the boundary problem in democratic theory: who should get a say in a decision before you even start counting votes. All the usual answers look broken in different ways. 'All affected interests' is so broad it drags in nearly everyone once you allow trade, climate, and globalised supply chains. 'All subjected to the decisions' seems circular, because somebody has to decide who counts as subjected before the demos exists. And 'social membership/residency' mostly just blesses the current border regime. I've signed up to write a long post defending one of these rather than doing a survey, so I want to spend the wordcount where it actually does work. Rank the main criteria (plus whatever fixes or hybrid views you think are pulling weight now) by how much they should matter in a serious defence, and say which ones are overrated. Please defend the ranking — what makes a criterion earn its keep is probably not just that it's clever — and tell me what would change your ordering. Answer in British English.
