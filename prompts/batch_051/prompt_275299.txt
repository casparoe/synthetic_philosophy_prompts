For an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar (16 students, 80 minutes), design a structured role-play that makes participants feel the force of the Ship of Theseus problem before it is named. No lecture up front, and the name should be withheld until the debrief. Split the group into two parties: one represents the maintained ship, whose planks are replaced one by one as they rot; the other represents the rebuilt vessel made from the removed original planks, and each claims to be the original ship. The rules, roles, and materials should force each side to commit early to a criterion of identity—spatiotemporal continuity of the maintained vessel, continuity of the original parts, or continuity of the design—and then discover the costs of that criterion as the replacements accumulate. Include a debrief that maps moments in the role-play onto spatiotemporal continuity, the distinction between constitution and identity, and the question whether identity is settled by fact or by decision. Finally, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and which feature of the philosophical problem the role-play cannot reproduce, so I know what the facilitator must supply.
