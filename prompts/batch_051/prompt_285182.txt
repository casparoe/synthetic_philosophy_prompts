A state or country with a working citizen-initiative process is considering the following adjustment. If a ballot measure passes with less than 55 percent of the vote, it takes effect only provisionally for four years. If no early vote is forced, the measure automatically returns to the ballot at the end of that period: a second win makes it permanent, a loss repeals it outright. But at any point during the four years, opponents of the measure may force an earlier re-vote by collecting signatures equal to half the number the initiative needed to qualify for the ballot; the re-vote is held within three months and carries the same consequences. Walk through how this rule would actually behave in practice. What does it incentivize for the measure's sponsors, for the organized opposition, for ordinary yes and no voters, and for the officials implementing the law during the provisional period? How would the two sides time their campaigning, implementation pressure, and signature drives — and who would end up controlling the trigger in practice? Where is the mechanism gamed first, and can its main failure modes be fixed without gutting the idea?
