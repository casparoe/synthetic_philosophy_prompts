We've been doing cosmology in my philosophy of physics course and I keep going round in circles on Boltzmann brains. I'm drawn to the standard story: the universe is enormous and old, heat death is in its future, and given enough time and space every possible arrangement of matter — including a brain carrying all my apparent memories — will eventually fluctuate into existence somewhere. If that's right, random fluctuations should vastly outnumber observers like me who got here by evolution, so on the theory I'm attracted to I ought to think I'm probably one of those fluctuations, with mostly false memories. But I can't bring myself to believe my memory of lunch two hours ago is more likely a spontaneous configuration than an actual event. It feels like I have to abandon either the cosmology, or the counting that says fluctuations dominate, or my trust in my own memory, and none of those is something I'm willing to give up. Walk me through what each surrender would cost, wether there's a fourth option that keeps all three (maybe the problem is an artefact of how we're counting observers, or the hypothesis is somehow self-undermining), and which move is least bad overall. I don't want a side picked; I want to know what would justify each.
