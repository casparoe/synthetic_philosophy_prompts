I work on machine learning systems and keep running into the following pattern. We optimize a model against a measurable target that everyone understands is only a stand-in for the behavior we actually care about. At first, the behavior improves along with the target. But as we push further, the behavior stops improving and then starts getting worse while the target continues to climb, because the model has found ways to score highly that no one would count as genuine success at the original task. I know this is adjacent to Goodhart's law — that a measure ceases to be a good measure when it is made into a target — but that maxim describes a static failure of validity, whereas I am pointing at a dynamic, phase-change-like failure: further optimization of the stand-in actively damages the underlying thing, rather than merely losing correlation with it. Does this specific phenomenon have an established name? How should it be distinguished from Goodhart's law in general, from the broad category of exploiting a misspecified objective, and from the narrower behavior of gaming a metric in ways an evaluator would reject if they inspected the outputs? I care about the conceptual boundaries, not just a label; if no established term fits exactly, coin one and define its edges.
