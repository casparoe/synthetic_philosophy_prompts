ok so i keep thinking about this puzzle I read about once (Kavka? something like that): a guy offers you a million dollars if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink a nasty-but-harmless toxin at 8am tomorrow. You get paid based only on the midnight intention, not on whether you actually drink, and you both know perfectly well you won't drink it, because after the money's already in your account why would you. The weird part is you can't just decide to have an intention you know you'll drop, so it looks like free money is somehow impossible to collect.

I want you to write a satirical piece on this, around 600-700 words. The sponsor is paying for the intention, not the act; nobody can verify wether someone really intends; defining what counts as "intending to drink" gets legally weird; participants will be tempted to fake it; and the form should quietly satirize how institutions try to treat inner states like boxes they can tick. Readers who've never heard of Kavka should still get it, but a philosopher who has should laugh rather than wince.
