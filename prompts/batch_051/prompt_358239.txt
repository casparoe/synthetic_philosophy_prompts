Me and my friend just spent three hours going in circles about the foundations debate—formalism, intuitionism, logicism. He keeps saying Hilbert basically won because math is just manipulation of finite symbols and a consistency proof is all the security you need. I kept saying that doesn't explain why math seems to be about something, and Brouwer's point still stings: a proof that some number exists by showing it can't not exist isn't the same as constructing one. My hypothesis is it's not one debate at all—it's three different questions sharing a name: logicism asks what mathematics reduces to, formalism asks what licenses a proof, intuitionism asks what a mathematician actually does in time. Is that right? If so, which question is the real foundational one, or is that itself the trap?
