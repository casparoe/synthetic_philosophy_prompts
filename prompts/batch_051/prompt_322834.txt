I'm part of an interdisciplinary reading group on scientific change, and I keep running into a pattern I don't have a term for. A research community treats a small set of foundational commitments as effectively non-negotiable, so that when an observation conflicts with what those commitments predict, the community almost never asks whether the commitments themselves are wrong. Instead, it adds a new auxiliary assumption, a new parameter, or a new boundary condition that explains away the anomaly. Often these additions make new testable predictions, and sometimes those predictions succeed. But in other cases I have watched the additions accumulate until the original core commitments do almost no work: any outcome, however surprising, can be handled by adding one more locally plausible patch, and the community no longer seems to treat failed novel predictions as especially damaging. I can think of one historical example, the proliferation of epicycles and equants in late Ptolemaic astronomy, and one contemporary example from a field I would rather not name, where a dominant model explains every failed intervention by invoking an additional unmeasured contextual factor. I have read Popper, and I know what an ad hoc hypothesis is, but this is not a one-off ad hoc move; it is a long-term pattern in which a tradition survives by changing everything around its protected center while the center itself becomes harder and harder to test.

Does this pattern have an established name? How is it different from ordinary scientific caution about auxiliary hypotheses, and what criterion separates a healthy version of the pattern from a pathological one? I am not looking for a bare label; I need the boundaries and essential features. Please act as a professional philosopher of science. Before giving your final answer, brainstorm how this pattern relates to nearby concepts and where it might fit only imperfectly. Then tell me the best name if one exists, and if none exists, propose a term with a clear definition and boundary cases.
