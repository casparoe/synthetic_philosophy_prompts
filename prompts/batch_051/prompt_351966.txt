In my formal epistemology course we keep building these idealized Bayesian reasoners, and there is a background assumption in the setup that is starting to bug me. The agent gets treated as already certain of every logical truth and as automatically respecting every entailment: if one claim follows from another, the agent cannot be more confident in the first than in the second, and anything that is a theorem gets probability one. But real reasoners have all these beliefs that look uncertain even though the facts are settled in advance by logic or mathematics alone.

A number theorist can think the twin prime conjecture is probably true and make career decisions partly on that, even though the conjecture is a determinate mathematical statement whose truth is fixed by the axioms. I can be eighty percent sure that a proof I just wrote is valid, even though the proof is sitting right in front of me and the only question is whether it goes through. A chess engine assigns a numerical evaluation to a position even though the position already has a game-theoretic value that is in principle determined. And two programmers can be fairly but not completely confident that two chunks of code compute the same function, even though whether they do is a mathematical fact. None of these are cases of missing empirical data, and theyre also not just cases of not having computed far enough yet, because the interesting question is what a reasonable degree of belief should look like before the computation finishes and how it should move as proofs roll in.

So my question is: does this specific kind of uncertainty — about entailments, theoremhood, computational equivalence, the values of finite games — have a name of its own? I know it is next to bounded rationality, but bounded rationality mostly says agents have limited time and compute; it does not tell you what to believe about the sentences you have not yet decided. I want the exact phenomenon carvd off from ordinary empirical uncertainty on one side and from mere computational limitation on the other. If the established label exists, give it and tell me how it relates to those neighbors; if not, coin one and define it with a couple of boundary cases that show where it stops applying.
