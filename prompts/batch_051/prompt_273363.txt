i'm teaching a short unit on philosophy of music and want to close the session on expression with a poem i can read aloud. Write a sonnet about the puzzle of sad music. The octave should really sit in the strangeness of it: its just air moving at certain frequencies, no actual person inside the music feeling anything, and yet "sad music" doesn't feel like a metaphor the way "sad willows" does. The volta should come when the speaker stops looking for the sadness as a property of the sound and starts noticing how the music moves — falling phrases, a dragging tempo, the way a melody droops or hesitates the way a person's voice and body do in grief — and entertains the thought that the sadness is something we hear in the shape and movement rather than something the air itself contains. The couplet shouldn't decide it; have it leave the speaker holding both thoughts at once and name exactly what's still unexplained (for instance: if the sound merely resembles grief, why does it feel like grief itself rather than like a picture of grief?). Keep it accurate enough that someone who knows the resemblance-vs-arousal debate would recognize what the poem is doing, with none of that jargon appearing in the verse itself. Then, separate from the poem, write me a short note on where the poem leans on a contested reading of musical expression, and what consideration would make you revise the volta or the couplet.
