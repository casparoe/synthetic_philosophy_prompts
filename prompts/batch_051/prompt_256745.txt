I am putting together a handout of objections to the family resemblance account of concepts that Wittgenstein develops in connection with games in the Philosophical Investigations. The objections I already have are these:

(1) The overgeneration objection: if the criterion for falling under a concept is merely overlapping similarities with paradigm cases, then with enough intermediate links almost anything can be brought into the extension of almost any concept, since some resemblance or other can always be found.

(2) The objection from normative constraint: a list of goings-on tied together by family likeness does not by itself explain why some candidate applications of a word strike competent speakers as wrong or as not merely unusual but excluded by the concept.

(3) The objection from exact concepts: the account seems tailored to open-textured, ordinary-language concepts like game, but it fails to explain formal or mathematical concepts, where boundaries and membership seem exact and not a matter of graded resemblance.

(4) The objection from the role of underlying linguistic rules: the family resemblance picture may simply postpone the problem, because what counts as a relevant resemblance, and how much weight a resemblance has, must itself be governed by something more rule-like than family resemblance.

(5) The objection from communication: if each competent speaker projects a concept along family resemblances in his or her own way, and there is no shared boundary or essence, then it becomes mysterious how speakers manage to mean the same thing by a word often enough to use it successfully.

I want one more objection that is genuinely different from all five of these. It should not be a restatement, special case, or combination of items already on the list. Ideally it should attack something the other five leave untouched, for example the relation between family resemblance and learning, or between family resemblance and what concepts are for, rather than what concepts are. After presenting it, tell me honestly whether it is any good.
