I teach at a big state school and, back in the day, I debated in high school and college. I've been asked to give the opening address at this year's state high-school debate championship in a few weeks. The room will be mixed: maybe four hundred debaters and coaches who live and breathe this stuff, plus a good chunk of administrators and parents who are only there because a kid dragged them. I want this speech to do real work, not just rev the kids up. Our state association is about to vote on whether to classify "competitive argumentation" as an official varsity sport, and I keep going back and forth on whether its a useful recognition or a category mistake that would slowly train kids to be fast, glib winners rather than careful reasoners. I lean yes, but not for the usual boosterish reasons. Please write the speech I should give. Make the case for treating argumentation as a sport, but build in the strongest honest version of the objection and say what would have to be true for the objection to win. The speech has to land for a sixteen-year-old who just broke at nationals and a parent who learned what a flow is twenty minutes ago. Don't make me sound like I've never met a teenager. Then, after the speech text, give me a short private note — not to be read out loud — saying where the speech leans hardest on a contested assumption, and what argument or evidence would change your mind about whether sportifying argumentation improves reasoning rather than just changing it.
