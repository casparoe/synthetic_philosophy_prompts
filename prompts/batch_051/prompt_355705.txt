(2) But hearers routinely accommodate informative presuppositions: if a speaker says they're collecting their sister from the airport and I didn't know they had a sister, I quietly add the sister to the common ground rather than objecting or asking for clarification.

(3) Accommodation is supposed to be a general rule of conversational scorekeeping: whenever the conversational score lacks something an utterance needs, that thing gets added.

(4) So for any utterance presupposing P, P either was already in the common ground or gets accommodated into it.

(5) Therefore no utterance ever has a presupposition that is absent from the common ground. The presupposition is always there before the utterance or put there because of it.

(6) But then the felicity condition in (1) can never be violated, and "presupposition failure" is an empty category.

(7) That seems absurd, because the whole reason we wanted something like (1) was to explain why an utterance like the one about the present king of France being bald is not merely false but somehow defective. If the hearer can just accommodate the existence of the present king on the fly, why isn't it fine?

(8) So the scorekeeping picture seems to imply that conversational accommodation prevents presupposition failure from ever occurring.
