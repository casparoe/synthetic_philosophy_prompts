i have a distinction i keep reaching for and want stress-tested before i lean on it any further. i used to think 'path dependence' was one thing, but it seems to me there are two. soft path dependence is when the road not taken stays open in principle: switching would be ruinously expensive but everyone can still see the alternative and state it's price. vhs over betamax feels like this, maybe imperial vs metric. hard path dependence is when the road not taken stops being an option at all: the system eats the vocabulary, institutions, or skills needed to even name the alternative, so 'switch' stops being a move in the relevant game. the soviet planned economy looks like this to me — at some point 'actually, a market for capital goods' became a category error inside the system rather than a costly reform. my actual claim is that soft vs hard sits on a different axis from contingent vs inevitable at the outset: a choice can be a near coin-flip and still only soft-lock, and a choice can be overdetermined and yet hard-lock the alternative out of thought. is this distinction already around under a better name? is the boundary real or just a matter of degree dressed up as a split? and is the orthogonality claim right, or do the two axes actually pull together in practice? also, if the distinction does work, where does 'costly' end and 'conceptually unavailable' begin — is that a fact about the world or about what the agents can represent? please separate what is settled from what people who know this stuff genuinely contest, and don't present a contested take as the consensus. answer in british english.
