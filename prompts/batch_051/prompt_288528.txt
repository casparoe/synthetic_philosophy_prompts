I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I would like help disentangling a disagreement with my younger sister. I have deliberately kept the description non-technical, because the difficulty concerns her life rather than the literature. She has been offered a place in an early clinical trial of a neurostimulation device that would substantially reduce her susceptibility to acute anxiety, grief, and self-criticism, while markedly increasing her capacity for sustained concentration. Set aside concerns about safety and efficacy: assume the device does what it promises and that the trial is well run. Her position is that the device is simply a faster and more reliable continuation of the meditation and therapy she has already found helpful, and she does not understand why I hesitate.

I hesitate for two reasons. First, a year after the device has been active, the person deciding whether the change was beneficial will be someone whose criteria for judging what is beneficial have themselves been modified by the device. I do not see how to treat that later judgment as independent confirmation, and I suspect she is relying on it more heavily than it can bear. Second, I value some of the dispositions the device would weaken — the way grief can genuinely undo her for a time, her habit of distrusting her first impulse — not as means to her happiness or productivity but as parts of the person she is. I take her current preferences seriously and have no wish to be paternalistic, but I do not think her present consent, by itself, settles whether the change is wise. Am I mistaken to hesitate? How should I structure the question, and what should I actually say to her that respects her autonomy without pretending my worries have no object?
