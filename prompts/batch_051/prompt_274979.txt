Take the dogmatism paradox and replace knowledge with a precise, high credence, then feed the agent a statistically specified stream of counterevidence. In its familiar form, the paradox runs roughly as follows: an agent knows p at t0 on solid grounds, then receives apparently credible evidence against p; since she knows p, she seems entitled to infer that any evidence against p is misleading, and so to dismiss the evidence while retaining her knowledge — a permission that looks indistinguishable from dogmatism, though denying it seems to make knowledge too fragile. The task is to see how much of this paradox survives when the agent's attitude toward p is credence 0.99 rather than knowledge.

Here is the varied case. An agent begins with credence 0.99 in p, where the prior is well calibrated and not itself under challenge. She then observes a sequence of reports r1, r2, ... about p, each generated independently by one process. Conditional on either truth value, each report states the truth with probability 0.6 and states the opposite with probability 0.4; that is, P(ri = p | p) = P(ri = not-p | not-p) = 0.6. The agent knows the 0.6/0.4 accuracy rates and knows that the reports are conditionally independent. She observes a run of m consecutive reports, all saying not-p.

Work through the following: compute the Bayesian update for a run of m not-p reports; find the first m at which the agent's posterior in p should fall below 0.5; then assess whether the agent, at each stage, has available anything properly analogous to the original dogmatist's dismissal of counterevidence as misleading, and what this shows about the original paradox.

1. Setup and modeling choices (150–200 words). Restate the original paradox and the exact parameters of the varied case, and make explicit any modeling choices you are making about how the reports are conditionalized and what the reports assert.

In particular, say whether the agent is entitled, after a single not-p report, to regard that report as probably misleading, and whether the claim that the expected posterior before seeing the report equals the prior gives the dogmatist any real support.
