Consider the following asymmetry in the way people assign responsibility for historical wrongs.

A government issues a formal apology today for grave wrongs carried out by its colonial predecessor roughly a century and a half ago. No current official took part in the original acts, the offending institutions have since been reorganized many times, and most living citizens have no ancestral connection to the colonial administration. Yet the apology is treated as the act of the state itself, and public comment calls it overdue rather than fictitious. By contrast, a private individual who discovers that her great-great-grandfather administered the very same colony, and who declines to apologize for him, is almost universally thought to owe no such apology; the demand strikes most people as perverse or confused. And when a corporation that acquired a firm decades after that firm's management had committed systematic abuse issues an apology for the acquired firm's historical wrongs, it is generally received as a genuine corporate act rather than as nonsense.

What distinction is doing the work here? Candidate principles to test: (a) states and corporations, but not individual persons or families, are temporally extended agents that inherit benefits and liabilities from their past selves, so the present state or firm is the same continuing entity that wronged; (b) an institutional apology is evaluated by what it binds the present institution to do going forward, not by whether it truly confesses the deeds of the dead; (c) what is really being praised is a present change in policy or in official memory, with the apology serving as an announcement of a new stance; (d) the asymmetry is only a legal and diplomatic device with no deeper moral basis. Press each candidate against nearby cases: a revolutionary successor state that repudiated the old regime's debts and identity; a family that still owns land acquired through the wrong; a corporation that committed the wrong in its original form but whose current shareholders bought in afterward knowing nothing; a state that issues the apology while continuing the underlying practice under another description. Then deliver a verdict: is the asymmetry justified, an instrumentally useful fiction, or an inconsistency we quietly live with?
