I retired from a spectroscopy lab a few years back and have been hanging around a philosophy reading group at the state university, mostly to keep my mind busy. One of the regulars there has a very firm position about photography that I keep poking at. She says there is a real metaphysical difference between images you see through and images you merely see. When she looks at some photographs she says she is literally seeing the object itself through the picture, the way you see your face through a mirror, and with others she says no: you are only seeing a representation of it. It is not about how accurate or realistic the image is, and she does not care whether it is art or not.

Over the year I have kept a little record. She has said yes — she sees through to the object — for: an ordinary 35mm color photograph; a live feed from a security camera to a monitor; her own face in a bathroom mirror; a daguerreotype portrait from around 1850; a mass-produced hologram on a credit card; and an auto-HDR photo from a smartphone, because she says the phone just merges exposures mechanically. She has said no for: a photorealistic oil painting copied from one of her own photographs; a deepfake video of a politician saying something he never said; a chest X-ray; an infrared thermal image of a house; an ultrasound sonogram of a fetus; an electron micrograph of a virus; and a false-color Hubble image of a nebula with invisible wavelengths painted into visible colors.

Yesterday I showed her a confocal fluorescence micrograph of a neuron labeled with green fluorescent protein — it is built up from visible light emitted by the dye, scanned by a laser. She would not commit. She said it is the first case that genuinly bothers her and she wants to think it over, but I am not that patient. Can you reverse-engineer the rule or rules she is running from those past verdicts and then predict what she will say about the fluorescence image? I can see at least two candidate rules that fit her record so far, one about visible light and one about natural appearance, and they seem to pull apart on this exact case. Lay out the plausible candidate rules, say what each one predicts for the fluorescence image, and give your best guess about which rule she is actually running and why. Also suggest the single best further case, one she has not ruled on yet, that would most cleanly separate the remaining candidates.
