I'm trying to pin down what counts as a voluntary transfer in the sense Nozick's entitlement theory actually needs. In the Wilt Chamberlain example from Anarchy, State, and Utopia, he imagines fans freely paying an extra twenty-five cents each to watch Chamberlain play basketball, and then challenges egalitarians to say why the resulting distribution is unjust. The whole argument turns on the transfers being voluntary. But "voluntary" is doing a lot of work, and I dont think its obvious what it covers.

Give me a working definition of a voluntary transfer that gets these cases right:

- a fan paying an extra quarter to watch Wilt Chamberlain play
- selling a kidney to a stranger for cash
- buying bottled water after a hurricane when the price has tripled
- taking a dangerous factory job because its the only alternative to unemployment
- signing a consent form for surgery you only partially understood
- handing your wallet to someone who threatens to hurt you otherwise

I think the first is voluntary and the last isnt, and the middle ones I go back and forth on. Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me where it gives an answer that surprises you. If the definition would call something nonvoluntary that Nozick's argument seems to require counting as voluntary, say whether you'd revise the definition or push back on the way the Wilt Chamberlain case is normally read.
