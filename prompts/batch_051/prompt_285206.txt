The question why there is something rather than nothing presupposes that the word "nothing" has a fixed content, but it is far from obvious that it does. I want a working definition of nothing — in the maximal, metaphysical sense relevant to that question, not the everyday sense in which a fridge contains nothing — that sorts the following cases correctly. Supply (a) a definition stated precisely; (b) a verdict of "something" or "nothing" for each case, with the definition applied step by step; (c) an honest note on any case where the definition yields the intuitively wrong verdict, no verdict, or a verdict only under an arbitrary stipulation, together with either a revision or a defense of the surprising verdict; and (d) a closing statement of which case is the "nothing" the original question asks about, and whether the definition makes that question well-posed.

Here are the cases. Some are clear cases, some are clear non-cases, and some are borderline; treat their order as irrelevant.

1. A completely empty region of spacetime: no particles, fields, radiation, or occupants.
2. The quantum vacuum of quantum field theory: a no-particle ground state that nonetheless has field structure, vacuum fluctuations, and, in some cosmological models, nonzero energy density.
3. A way things could be containing exactly one point-particle and absolutely nothing else.
4. A way things could be containing only empty spacetime: spacetime points standing in geometric relations, with no occupants.
5. A way things could be in which no concrete objects exist — no particles, fields, spacetime points, or minds — but in which abstract objects such as numbers, sets, and propositions do exist.
6. The empty set, considered as an object in mathematics.
7. The hole in a brick wall — the local absence of brick through which one could pass a finger.
8. A way things could be with no objects, properties, relations, spacetime points, or abstract objects of any kind.

The definition must give a definite answer for every case; if a verdict depends on a further parameter, name the parameter. It must also do more than offer a synonym, saying what it is for there to be nothing in terms of existence, quantification, or some other notion you take to be more basic. If you conclude that a single maximal sense of "nothing" cannot be made coherent, say so explicitly, then show how far a definition can be made to work against the cases.
