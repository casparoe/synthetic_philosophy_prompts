Give me a working definition of sport: a principled account of what makes an activity a sport, explicit enough that it yields a definite verdict for each case below. Do not just report how the word is used; give the account that best earns the right to govern the hard cases.

Cases that should come out clearly as sports:
- The 100-meter dash at a track meet.

Cases that should come out clearly as not sports:
- A casual argument over dinner about where to go on holiday.

Borderline cases, on which I may or may not have a settled view:
- Chess played in a rated tournament.
- A professional League of Legends match.
- A high school policy-debate round with judges and an elimination bracket.
- A courtroom trial.
- Mountaineering to a summit without a race or judges.
- Competitive eating.

In particular, your definition should take a position on the usual candidate criteria — physical exertion, head-to-head competition, rule-governed scoring, and whether the activity has an aim beyond winning — and say which of them survive contact with the cases.

Then put the finished definition to work on the question I actually care about. Could argumentation be a sport — scored argument leagues, ranked online disputation, televised cross-examination — and would making argumentation more sport-like improve the quality of reasoning, or would it corrupt it by rewarding persuasion over truth?

Format your response exactly as follows:

- Begin with a one-sentence bottom line: your definition of sport and your verdict on whether argumentation could be a sport.
- Proposed definition (about 200 words): state the definition, unpack its key terms, and explain the principle behind it.
- The clear cases (about 100 words): run the 100-meter dash and the dinner argument through the definition and confirm the expected verdicts.
- The hard cases (about 350 words): work through each borderline case; say where the definition yields a surprising answer, a wrong answer, or no answer.
- Revisions and defenses (about 150 words): revise the definition if any hard case broke it, or defend the surprising verdicts if you keep it.
- Argumentation as a sport (about 250 words): apply the final definition to competitive argumentation, and argue for whether sportifying argumentation would improve reasoning or corrupt it.
