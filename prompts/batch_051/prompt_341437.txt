I spent most of my working life as a chemist — physical kinetics, not the exciting kind — and now in retirement I've fallen into philosophy, mostly the aesthetics side. The paradox of tragedy has me hooked: why do people seek out art that reliably makes them feel worse? I've got the standard moves filed away and they all strike me as redescriptions rather than answers. The ones I know: catharsis, where the work stirs up pity and fear and then purges them and the relief feels good; conversion, where the formal pleasure of the artwork converts the unpleasant emotions into something enjoyable; control or mastery, where what we enjoy is the sense of being able to regulate our emotional response; the meta-response account, where we take second-order pleasure in noticing we're the kind of person who feels for the characters; the make-believe and safe-distance accounts, where the emotions are either only quasi-emotions toward a pretense or are threat-free because it's all fictional; the insight or compensation account, where the sadness is the price of a kind of understanding we value more than we dislike the pain; and the rich-experience account, where we want a rich emotional life and the negative emotions are part of a valuable whole. I've also seen the benign-masochism idea drifting around from psychology, where mild negative states are enjoyed the way chili heat is, but that feels like an analogy more than a mechanism.

What bugs me is repetition. I'll reread a tragedy knowing exactly who dies when. Any story that leans on uncertainty, suspense, or the relief of a resolution I didn't see coming is dead on arrivial as far as I'm concerned. My chemical habits make me want an actual causal story, not a label. I keep toying with something like emotional vaccination — small controlled doses of grief keeping the machinery that processes grief in working order. But I can already see that might just be the control account in a lab coat.

So give me an explanation of why we enjoy sad art that is (1) genuinely different from every item in that list and from my vaccination guess, not a rewording or combination of them, (2) survives repeat viewing, and (3) does more than name the phenomenon — it should gesture at a mechanism. And tell me straight whether it's actually any good, or whether I'd be dressing up one of the standard accounts in new vocabulary.
