for the last couple months i've been birding with my mom, and there's this thing about her that keeps bugging me. she can name a warbler from its call at two hundred yards almost every time, but if you ask her how she knows, she can't give you a single field mark, and she's never once been able to teach anyone else to do it. last sunday she called a palm warbler a yellow-rumped with total confidence and the rest of us just shrugged, because what were we supposed to appeal to? she has a track record, not an account of the why. it finally hit me that she might have true belief without knowledge, and that her true beliefs basically work as well as knowledge in practice. so ive been going in circles on the meno problem: why is knowing supposed to be worth more than merely having correct beliefs? anyway, i need a joke about this. the joke has to turn on the actual philosophical content—like, knowledge is supposed to be true belief thats tied down by reasons or an account of the cause, so it cant just wander off, whereas a bare true belief is luck you happen to still have—not on generic oh-they-got-lucky humor. the punchline should only land for someone who gets why plato thought knowledge was more valuable than true belief.
