ive been going back and forth on yablos paradox and its got me stuck between a theory I like and an intuition I cant shake.

Stephen Yablo, in his 1993 Analysis paper Paradox Without Self-Reference, gives this construction. Imagine an infinite list of sentences. S1 says that every sentence after S1 is untrue. S2 says every sentence after S2 is untrue. S3 says every sentence after S3 is untrue. And so on forever. Heres the argument as I understand it. Suppose some Sn is true. Then Sn says all later sentences are untrue, so Sn+1 is untrue. But Sn+1 says all sentences after Sn+1 are untrue. If Sn+1 is untrue, then not all later sentences are untrue, so some sentence later than Sn+1 is true. But that contradicts Sn, which requires all later sentences, including that one, to be untrue. So no Sn can be true. Therefore every sentence in the list is untrue. But if every sentence is untrue, then S1 is true, because S1 says exactly that every later sentence is untrue, and every sentence in the list is later than S1. Contradiction.

The theory Im attracted to is that semantic paradoxes are always self-reference or circularity in disguise. The liar says of itself that it is false. Currys sentence says of itself that if it is true then absurdity follows. On this view, remove self-reference and you remove the paradox. But Yablos sequence seems to break that neat story: each sentence refers only to later sentences, never to itself. Theres no direct cycle. And yet the step-by-step reasoning that shows each sentence cannot be true feels completely solid. My intuition is that the argument is valid, and so this is a genuine paradox without self-reference—which would force me to give up the theory.

But maybe the intuition that the argument works is what should go. Maybe truth values over an infinite descending chain dont behave like ordinary finite reasoning assumes. Or maybe the move from Sn is untrue to some later sentence is true is too quick if bivalence fails. I cant see the flaw though, and every time I try to assign truth values to the sequence I land back in contradiction.

Which should yield? Should I drop the self-reference theory, or drop the intuition that the Yablo reasoning establishes a genuine paradox? Or is there a compatibility move where the theory survives because the sequence is secretly circular in some non-obvious way, for instance because each sentence quantifies over all later sentences and thereby indirectly involves itself? Walk me through the costs of each option. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
