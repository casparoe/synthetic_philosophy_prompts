Im in an early Christianity course and we jumped from Origen's three senses of scripture to fundamentalist hermeneutics, and I've ended up with a half-baked theory I want pressure-tested. I've been calling it the spillway model of allegory. The claim is that nobody is simply literal or simply allegorical. Every community reads literally by default, and non-literal reading kicks in as a relief valve only when the literal sense would make the text fail at whatever job the community most needs it to do at that moment. Origen opens the valve when a literal reading would be unworthy of God or philosophically embarrassing, so Genesis has to be read at least partly spiritually. A fundamentalist also has the valve, but opens it only where the genre is already marked as non-literal (parables, God as a rock), and keeps it shut when the pressure is scientific or moral, like the six days or the conquest narratives. So literal vs allegorical is the symptom, and the real difference is which passages count as blocked and which pressures are allowed to open the valve. Is this a real distinction, am I just relabeling accommodation, or did someone already say this under another name? And what breaks it? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion: (1) is the model coherent, (2) the closest existing view, (3) the strongest point in its favor, (4) the biggest hole, and (5) your verdict on whether it adds anything.
