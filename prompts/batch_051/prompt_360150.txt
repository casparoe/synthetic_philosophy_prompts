I'm an environmental health officer for a port city. Our municipal code basically codifies the precautionary principle: when a proposed activity could plausibly cause harm that is serious or irreversible, we're supposed to act even if the science isn't settled, and our office sees maybe thirty of these a year — new anti-fouling coatings in the marina, algaecides in a drinking-water reservoir, mosquito spraying near wetlands, a pipe-lining epoxy that leaches trace bisphenol A but prevents lead from leaching into drinking water. I need a decision procedure my two-person team can actually run, not a slogan. One of us has toxicology training but neither of us does formal risk assessment, we have ten working days per file, and no outside consultants for routine cases. Please write it as a numbered checklist: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run the procedure on three proposals: (1) the algaecide, where lab fish show thyroid disruption at high doses but field concentrations would be far lower; (2) the pipe-lining epoxy, which leaches trace BPA but cuts lead in drinking water; (3) a mosquito adulticide that is the only affordable option for a neighborhood with rising West Nile cases. I expect at least one of these to come out in a way I won't like, and that's fine. Keep the whole thing under 900 words, lead with the procedure, and finish with a sentence on what the procedure cannot do.
