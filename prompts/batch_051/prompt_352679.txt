Consider the view Karl Popper defended for a substantial part of his career, and later partly retracted, that natural selection is not a testable scientific theory in the way Newtonian mechanics is, but a metaphysical research programme: it supplies a general framework for biological inquiry without itself being refutable, because its central claim—that those who survive are the fittest—can be preserved by defining fitness in terms of actual survival. Suppose you think this metaclassification is broadly correct. Play it straight: what would have to be true for it to be wrong? I want a sorted map of the evidence and arguments that should move me, distinguishing two kinds of defeaters: biological findings or model systems in which evolutionary claims make precise, falsifiable predictions, and methodological results showing that Popper's testability criterion would classify much settled science as metaphysical too. Then assess whether Popper's later concession that natural selection is testable was demanded by the biology, by the philosophy of science, or both, and state honestly whether the original thesis was empty, risky, or useful despite its looseness.
