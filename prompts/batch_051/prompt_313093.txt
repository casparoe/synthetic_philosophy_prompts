I run a small team and am about to make staffing decisions that depend on knowing which kinds of work each person actually enjoys. For the past year I have been trying to infer this from behavior: who volunteers, who procrastinates, who finishes quickly, who says a task is "fine" when asked. But the same conduct keeps turning out to be compatible with very different preferences. One employee volunteered repeatedly for a kind of task and performed it well, so I concluded she liked it; she later told a coworker that she took it on only because she believed declining would hurt her standing, and that she finds the work draining. Another employee never touches a kind of task, which I read as dislike, but he may simply believe he would be bad at it or that it is not his place to take it on. Is there a defensible way to infer what someone actually prefers from what they do, given that behavior is also shaped by beliefs, by what people think is expected of them, and by habit? Is the whole project underdetermined, or does it become workable if I make the right assumptions? What would those assumptions be, and how could I check them rather than taking them on faith?
