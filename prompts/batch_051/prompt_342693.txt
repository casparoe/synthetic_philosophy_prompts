The alignment team I'm on keeps going in circles about how much to trust chain-of-thought. One faction treats reading a model's chain as basically like listening to a person think out loud: they call it a priviledged window into the model's real reasoning, where you get the actual sequence of steps, in order, before the final answer locks in. They lean on that analogy to argue that CoT can work as an oversight channel — if the model were about to do something dangerous, the dangerous reasoning would show up in the chain, and a careful reader could catch it. But I've started to suspect the analogy is doing a lot of unexamined work, because nobody has actually mapped it out; they just say it's like thinking out loud and move on.

So how far does that analogy actually hold? Map it for me: what in the model case is supposed to correspond to the inner thought, the decision to say it out loud, the incentive to be honest or to self-censor, the audience, and the gap (if any) between a person's actual reasoning and what they report. Then tell me where the mapping breaks, and which of those breaks matter for the oversight conclusion. I'm especially stuck on the fact that the model was trained to produce chains that humans rate as good, which sounds like it could turn the whole thing into a press release rather than a window; is that a decisive disanalogy, a fixable one, or a feature the source case actually has too? And if the analogy collapses, is there a better way to think about what chain-of-thought gives us, or does the oversight conclusion survive anyway?
