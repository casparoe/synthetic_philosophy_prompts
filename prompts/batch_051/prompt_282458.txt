In my epistemology seminar we've been on the norms of assertion for three weeks and it feels completely stuck. Everyone accepts the same data points—lottery propositions, Moore-paradoxical conjunctions, warranted false assertions, 'selfless' assertions where someone asserts p while believing not-p—but the knowledge, truth, belief, and justification norm camps each handle some of these easily and treat the others as spoils to be explained away. I'm starting to suspect the fight isn't actually about which norm best explains the data; maybe each side is answering a different question under the heading 'the norm of assertion' (what makes a speaker blameless, what the speech act itself aims at, what a hearer may infer). Is that diagnosis right, and if so, what kind of disagreement is this really? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress—a distinction, a formal model, a new kind of case, a change of question—or should we expect it to stay stuck?
