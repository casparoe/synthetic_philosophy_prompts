Been reading the AI-safety-via-debate stuff and theres a shape of problem I keep noticing that I dont have a decent label for. The setup is usually two arguers in front of a judge with limited time, but it also shows up in regular arguments. Someone can advance a conclusion by a chain where every individual step is plausible on its own, and the judge checks each step locally and signs off, but the chain as a whole is wrong. The arguer doesnt hide the bad step so much as spread it out: a definition that's slightly too broad, a lemma applied just outside the conditions where it was shown to hold, an example that is doing more work than it looks like. Tracking down the load-bearing error ends up costing the judge much more effort than the arguer spent constructing the thing, and crucially the argument feels tuned to this particular judge's checking process, the way an adversarial example is tuned to a classifier. Is there a name for this, and how exactly does it connect to whether the debate setup is a good safety mechanism? I'm not looking for a one-word answer; I want to know how people carve the space up, what the difference is between this and an ordinary invalid argument, and whether the established concept (if there is one) covers exactly what I'm describing or only a nearby thing.
