Consider a Frankfurt-style case with one change. Black wants Jones to vote for candidate A and has implanted a device that monitors Jones for a reliable prior sign of a decision to vote for candidate B; in the original case, the device would have made Jones vote A, and Black never intervenes. In the modified case, the device is fallible: when triggered, it overrides Jones's decision with probability 0.9, and with probability 0.1 it malfunctions and Jones votes B anyway. On the actual occasion Jones deliberates in his ordinary way, decides on his own to vote A, and the device never activates; Jones knows all of this. Work through how this change affects the standard Frankfurt-style argument against the Principle of Alternate Possibilities and what the modified case should do to the compatibilism–incompatibilism debate. Give exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, covering: (1) whether Jones could have done otherwise in the modified case and whether control over the alternative matters; (2) whether he is morally responsible for voting A and how compatibilist and incompatibilist verdicts diverge; (3) whether the anti-PAP argument depends on the device being infallible, and at what reliability it would regain its original force; (4) which successor principle the case pushes us toward—such as a robustness requirement on alternatives or a sourcehood account—and why; (5) a further precisely specified modification of the device that would sharpen rather than dissolve the dispute.
