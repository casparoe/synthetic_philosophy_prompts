I'm in a reading group on free will, and we recently worked through the Sphex wasp example—the digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to the edge of its burrow, goes down alone to inspect the entrance, comes back up, and, if an experimenter has moved the cricket a few inches away, drags it back and runs the entire inspection ritual again without ever "noticing" that the burrow has already been checked. The moral usually drawn from this is that the relevant contrast for freedom is not between caused and uncaused behavior but between rigid and flexible behavior: a free agent, unlike the wasp, can revise its routine when the world changes, and it is this flexibility, rather than some break in the causal order, that makes determinism compatible with freedom.

Lately I have been unable to stop thinking about the example in connection with my work as a machine learning engineer. The systems I train—large language models and reinforcement-learning agents—are, within their domains, vastly more flexible than the wasp: they improvise novel responses, drop one line of attack for another, and adapt to inputs they have never seen. Yet almost no one would say that a language model, however flexible its outputs, has free will in the sense the Sphex example was meant to illuminate. This makes me suspect the argument was doing more work than it advertised, and that "flexibility" was standing in for something further—purposes the system has made its own, a unified point of view, or the capacity to care about whether its projects succeed.

My question is whether the Sphex-based argument—that flexibility, not indeterminism, is the heart of free agency—survives the existence of highly flexible but apparently unfree systems. Does this development refute the argument, or does it reveal that flexibility was always only a necessary surface feature while the real criterion lay elsewhere? I want a diagnosis of which premise the development bears on, and whether the argument can be repaired without circularity; if it cannot, what should the distinction it was trying to draw now be called?
