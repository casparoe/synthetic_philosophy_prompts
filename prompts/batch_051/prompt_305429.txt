I run a monthly reading group at my company for software engineers — about 14 people, one hour over lunch in a conference room. Lately we've been going through a few pieces on climate change and intergenerational fairness, and we keep hitting the same wall: everyone nods along with the idea that future people matter and that discounting their wellbeing just because they happen to be born later seems arbitrary, but then someone says something like "well, we cant negotiate with people who aren't born yet" and the discussion just stalls out. I want to make that wall the session itself, rather than something we keep bumping into and then awkwardly moving past.

I'd like you to design a runnable activity for this group, something in the game/simulation family, not a lecture. The goal is that participants feel the structural asymmetry themselves: the present generation makes the decisions, the future ones just inherit whatever is left, and there's no mechanism for the future people to push back or punish anyone. If possible, the rules should make it locally rational to kick costs down the road, so people can feel the pull of pure time discounting before anyone names it. The debrief should map concrete moments in the game onto the idea that future generations can't sanction us, and onto the thought that this still doesn't settle whether it's okay to treat their interests as less important. Please include rules, materials I can actually pull together in an office with a whiteboard and printer paper, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, a rough timeline for an hour, a debrief, and an honest note on what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and which part of the philosophical idea the game can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. No lecture up front, and nobody should hear the word "discounting" or "intergenerational" until afterwards.
