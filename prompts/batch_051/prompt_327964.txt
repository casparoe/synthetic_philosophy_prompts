I run a small discussion group for people with no academic philosophy background, and we have been trying to work out what a person is on the hook for when they tell someone that something is the case. Some of us think only whether the claim turns out true matters; others think the person has to believe it; others think they need good reasons; still others think they have to know it. I want a handout that sorts people by how they actually answer concrete cases rather than by which slogan they like.

Please build a decision tree whose branch questions are concrete cases or plain-English claims, and whose leaves are named positions. Build it from the logical space first: lay out the combinations of answers the questions allow, place the positions in that space, and flag any combination no known position occupies, with a sentence on whether it is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any two positions the tree cannot cleanly separate.

The following five cases must appear in the tree, and for each the question is whether the person's assertion is in order or whether they have gone wrong. (1) Someone reads a stopped clock that happens to show the correct time and tells another person the time. (2) Someone holding one ticket in a fair lottery with ten million tickets says, before the drawing, that the ticket will lose, and it does lose. (3) Someone with strong but misleading evidence says something false. (4) Someone who does not believe what she is saying asserts it because her job requires it, and what she says is true and well supported. (5) Someone with no evidence at all makes a specific prediction as a pure guess, without believing it, and happens to be right.

The leaves should include at least the knowledge norm, the truth norm, the belief norm, and the justification or reasonable-to-believe norm, plus any other leaf you think these cases force out. Keep it to roughly six or seven leaves, and write it so a member of the group could follow the whole tree in under ten minutes.
