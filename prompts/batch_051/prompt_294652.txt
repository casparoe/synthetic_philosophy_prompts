I've been reasoning with a picture of gratitude for years now: it's what's owed to an agent who intended to benefit you — the fitting warm response to someone doing you a good turn on purpose, with maybe a standing disposition to reciprocate if the chance comes up. The trouble is, that picture gives me nothing to do with the two things that actually produce my most gratitude-shaped feelings: sheer luck (where and when I was born, health, all of it) and the accumulated acts of dead or distant strangers who never had me in mind. The person who ran the trial for a drug I now take didn't intend to benefit me; neither did whichever ancestor moved to a place with better laws. By my own account, gratitude toward luck or the faceless past is a misfire, an emotion aimed at a target that can't receive it. So, taking that account as right, what's the correct way to talk myself out of these feelings — and if it turns out I can't, or shouldn't, am I forced to treat them as irrational residue I shouldn't reason from? Lead with the bottom line in one sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, don't restate the question.
