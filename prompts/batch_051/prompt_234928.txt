I am a novelist, and I have been asked to open a half-day workshop for twelve adult fiction writers on why readers come to care about characters. I want the group to collide, before I offer any craft advice, with a problem that sits at the seam of my profession: readers are moved by people they know perfectly well do not exist, and yet the emotion does not feel counterfeit while it is occurring. I want the participants to supply the problem themselves—to feel something toward a character and then, in the same session, discover that they cannot give a consistent account of what they felt and about whom—before any theory is named.

Design a structured ninety-minute activity, runnable by me alone with twelve participants and no special equipment, that lets them feel the force of the paradox of fiction rather than hear it explained. The first phase should actually elicit, not merely report, an emotional response; the idea should surface in what the participants do, not in anything I say beforehand. They should end able to state, in their own words, why it is hard to hold both that their emotion was real and that the object of it was not. The activity should be self-contained: any passage, card, or prompt used should be invented for the exercise, and it should not require participants to have read philosophy.

Please specify the timeline; any distributed materials, prompts, instructions, or rules; the roles participants take; the likely moment at which the puzzle surfaces; and a debrief that maps what happened onto the main families of reply—roughly, that the emotion is an attenuated or imaginary counterpart of ordinary emotion, that readers in some sense take the fiction as real while engaged, or that the emotion is genuine but aimed at real possibilities or thoughts rather than at the nonexistent person—and that presses participants to say which family costs them least. Also include an honest note on which feature of the paradox the exercise cannot reproduce in a room and must therefore be supplied by the facilitator, and anticipate what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run and how to handle it. Please write the answer in American English.
