I've been trying to pin down what Descartes' cogito actually proves, and I've got a bunch of gut reactions that I can't turn into a single rule. I'm a software engineer, so I can handle formal stuff, but I havent read the secondary literature, so go easy on jargon.

Case 1: I notice that I'm thinking about whether I exist. Verdict: fine, I exist, at least for as long as the thinking is happening.

Case 2: A powerful demon is feeding me every experience and belief, so everything I believe is false. Verdict: still fine, I exist. If I'm being deceived, there's something being deceived.

Case 3: I'm dreaming that I'm walking. Verdict: walking therefore I exist doesn't carry the same certainty. But I seem to be walking therefore I exist does.

Case 4: Someone objects that all Descartes is really entitled to is that thinking is happening, not that an I exists. Verdict: I lean toward thinking this is right, and I don't have a good reply to it. But it seems like it would also sink Case 2, and I really don't want to sink Case 2.

Case 5: A program checks its own internal state and prints "I exist" after a successful self-check. Verdict: that's not the cogito, even if the self-check is correct.

Case 6: I'm under anesthesia and there's no thinking going on. Verdict: the cogito says nothing about whether I exist during that gap. It only covers the moment of thinking.

What bugs me is that Case 2 and Case 4 look like they have the same shape — some mental activity is happening, and from that I infer a subject. But I want to keep Case 2 and I'm half-convinced by Case 4, and I dont know if that's principled or just special pleading. Also Case 1 vs Case 3: why does thinking get me to existence but walking doesn't, when both are things I do?

So the actual question: what exactly does the cogito prove — a momentary subject of this thinking, a persisting thinking thing, or just that some thinking exists? Is there one principle that captures all my verdicts, and if not, which verdicts do I have to overturn? Please also test whatever principle you settle on against a case I didn't list: a demon that isn't just deceiving me but is doing the thinking itself, with me as a passive recipient. Does the cogito go through for me there? And if you can think of another case that would split the candidate principles, run that one too.
