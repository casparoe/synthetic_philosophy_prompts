I've been thinking about Oedipus Rex a lot lately — the Sophocles play — because when I first read it, I assumed it was about fate and how you can't escape it. But the more I sit with it, the more the actual engine of the plot seems to be what happens when people learn something about there own future and then try to do something about it. Laius and Jocasta hear that their son will kill his father and marry his mother, so they arrange to have the baby killed, which is what lands Oedipus with adoptive parents and makes the prophecy possible. Oedipus hears the same thing at Delphi, assumes it's about his adoptive parents, and runs away from Corinth to avoid it — and that's the road that takes him to the crossroads where he kills Laius. Nobody is being passive; everybody is trying like crazy to avoid the prediction, and the avoiding itself is what brings it about. A friend who works in AI stuff recently mentioned an idea he called embedded agency: the thought that a mind is not a spectator standing outside the world and taking notes, it's a part of the world it's trying to model, so its predictions about its own actions feed back into those actions. That clicked with the play for me, and now I can't unsee it.
