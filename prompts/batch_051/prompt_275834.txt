I'm doing a well-being module, and we've been on hedonism — the view that pleasure is the only thing that is ultimately good for a person. My lecturer spent a whole seminar on what she called the heterogeneity problem: once you start listing real pleasures, it stops looking like there is one shared 'pleasure-feeling' underneath them all.

An orgasm is clearly a pleasure, and the mild annoyance of a delayed train is clearly not; no argument there. The difficult cases are the relief when a biopsy comes back clear; scratching an itch that is somehow both sore and delicious; the glow of finishing a spreadsheet you had been dreading; enjoying a film that makes you cry; the small mean buzz of watching someone you dislike trip over a kerb; a runner's high; deep flow while programming; and the background cosiness of sitting under a blanket while it rains. Some of these feel like pleasures, some feel more like moods or attitudes, and I can't find the seam.

I half-believe the definition 'pleasure is whatever you want to be experiencing and would prolong if you could' — it nails the blanket case — but it seems to double-count the relief case (do I want the relief itself, or do I want the fact it represents?) and it wierdly turns pride, satisfaction, and flow into pleasures. So I'd like a working definition that actually sorts the list. Lead with the definition itself as the first sentence — no preamble, no restating the question — then take it through each case above. Say which cases it gets wrong or has no verdict on, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising result. If the honest answer is family-resemblance or a few related senses, say so and keep them as precise as you can.
