Here's the question I set: "Zeno gives several arguments against motion. Pick two, explain how they differ, and then say whether the usual move about an infinite series adding up to a finite total solves the puzzle, or only solves the arithmetic."

Here's his answer: "The two I'll take are the Dichotomy and Achilles. In the Dichotomy, to get across the room you first have to go halfway, then half of what remains, then half again, and since that chain never has a last step you never actually arrive. In Achilles, a fast runner gives a tortoise a head start, and every time he reaches where the tortoise was, the tortoise has moved ahead a little, so he never catches it. Honestly these are the same paradox. Both assume you can split a distance into infinitely many pieces, and Zeno's mistake is thinking you can't complete infinitely many tasks in a finite time. Modern math solves both by showing that 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... converges to 1, so infinitely many pieces don't add up to an infinite distance. Once you accept that, there's no philosophical problem left; Zeno just didn't know calculus. The arrow points at something different, but the two I picked are fully solved."
