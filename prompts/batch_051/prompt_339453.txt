I teach an upper-level seminar on statutory interpretation and I'm writing the final this week. I'd like one essay question on the textualism vs. purposivism debate that cant be answered by reciting the standard definitions ("textualists look at the text, purposivists look at intent"). Could you make it revolve around a short invented statute plus a fact pattern where the text and the most obvious legislative purpose genuinely pull apart, but where a careful textualist and a careful purposivist would partly converge. In particular, I want the question to force students to see that a lot of the fight is downstream of interpretation—it's about what a court should do when the semantic meaning runs out or leads somewhere odd, not about whether to read the words. Both sides can accept purpose as context for pinning down public meaning; they're more likely to split on whether a clear text may be bent to serve the statute's larger purpose, and on whether a multimember legislature can have a single recoverable purpose at all given bargaining and compromise. Please write the question itself (with the statute and facts), a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme for 25 marks that seperates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each one has to make, rather than just by vibes. It would also be great if the fact pattern contained a trap or two, like a committee report sentence that sounds like a broad purpose but is really just post hoc committee noise. And after the model answer, add a short note saying how confident you are in the account of the debate embedded in the answer and singling out the point you're least sure about.
