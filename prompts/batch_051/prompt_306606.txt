Consider a proposal from a consortium of mathematics journals and software vendors to settle what they call the root-labeling problem. They would maintain a centralized Canonical Root Registry. For any polynomial with rational coefficients submitted to the registry, an algorithm first assigns provisional labels to the roots by sorting numerical approximations according to an explicit tie-breaking rule — for example, lexicographic order by real part and then imaginary part at a fixed precision, with a deterministic pseudo-random tiebreak for near-coincidences. The registry then declares the labels stable under perturbation: if a coefficient of the polynomial is varied continuously along a path that avoids polynomials with repeated roots, each label is required to accompany the root continuously connected to the originally labeled one. The consortium proposes that journals require authors to cite roots by registry label and that computer algebra systems return roots only in registry form, so that the phrase "the root r₃ of p" has a fixed meaning across papers and databases.

Treat this as a mechanism rather than as a philosophical thesis, and analyze how it would actually behave. What would it incentivize among the registry's maintainers, among authors who prove identities involving roots, among designers of numerical and symbolic software, and among users comparing roots across sources? How could it be gamed — through the choice of a deformation path, through polynomials engineered to sit near the tie-breaking boundary, or through the timing of submissions? Trace precisely where the continuity rule fails: what happens to labels when two roots collide, or when the coefficients are taken around a closed loop that encircles a configuration with repeated roots? Is the resulting path-dependence a repairable defect of this particular algorithm, or does it reveal that no global path-independent labeling of the roots can exist? What second-order effects would follow for how published identities and numerical tables are read? Finally, identify which failure modes could be fixed by amending the rules and which would persist under any repair short of abandoning the demand for canonical labels — and explain what the answer suggests about whether a root's label records a fact about the root or imposes a choice upon it.
