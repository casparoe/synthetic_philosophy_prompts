Here is an argument that purports to show that no one could have come from a different sperm or egg from the ones they actually came from. It strikes me as suspiciously quick, but I cannot locate the fault. Please go through the steps and tell me whether the argument is valid; if it is not, say exactly which step fails, and state what a corrected version of that step would have to assert in order for the conclusion to follow.

(1) Let z be the particular zygote from which you actually developed, and let s and e be the particular sperm and egg whose fusion formed z.

(2) A zygote is individuated by its gametic origin. The cell formed by the fusion of s and e is the particular cell it is in virtue of being formed from those very gametes; had a different sperm or a different egg been involved, the resulting cell would have been a numerically different zygote rather than z. So z could not have existed while originating from anything other than s and e.

(3) You actually developed from z.

(4) Since z essentially originates from s and e, and since you developed from z, it follows that you essentially originate from s and e: any possible situation in which you exist is a situation in which you came from s and e.

(5) Therefore, you could not have come from a different sperm or a different egg.

I am inclined to accept (1), (2) and (3), and yet I also feel that if my parents had conceived a few seconds earlier or later, I might still have existed, though from a different sperm. Does the argument discredit that feeling, or does it fail at some step? Be precise about any equivocation or suppressed premise.
