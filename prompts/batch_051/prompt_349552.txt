I have spent the past few months reading the dispute between higher-order and first-order/self-representational theories of consciousness, and I find it strangely static. The higher-order side maintains that a mental state is conscious only if it is the object of a distinct higher-order state, such as a thought or perception directed at it, because otherwise the state could occur without the subject being in any way aware of it. The first-order and self-representational sides reject that dependency: some claim a conscious state represents itself in virtue of its own structure rather than through a distinct further state, while others treat the state's phenomenal character as basic and regard awareness of the state as something a conscious state can cause. The debate then loops through a fixed set of cases. Higher-order theorists cite subliminal perception and masked stimuli as evidence that a first-order state can occur without consciousness and so cannot be sufficient for it; their opponents reply that such cases involve degraded or unreportable first-order states rather than the absence of any higher-order state, and add that the higher-order view still owes an account of why the higher-order state is itself conscious, on pain of regress.

I have begun to suspect that the impasse is structural rather than empirical. The higher-order camp seems to me to be trying to explain how a state becomes subjectively available to the person; the first-order/self-representational camp seems to be trying to explain what it is for a state to have a felt character at all, and treats the subject's relational awareness of the state as a downstream effect rather than as a constituent of the consciousness itself. If that is right, the two groups are not really offering rival explanations of one agreed phenomenon; they are answering different questions under the single heading of consciousness, and using the phrase awareness of a state in different senses.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a verbal disagreement, a difference about explanatory priority, a difference about which cases count as data, a difference about what a theory of consciousness must explain, or something else? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress: a sharper distinction, a formal argument that the regress is or is not vicious, a type of experiment, or an argument that the question should be replaced or dissolved? I want the diagnosis supported by what each side actually says, not a survey of the literature.
