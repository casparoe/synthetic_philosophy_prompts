I am a PhD student in political philosophy working on the ethics of artificial intelligence, and I am drafting a one-year dissertation fellowship proposal. The half-formed idea I want you to turn into a proposal is about the normative status of pre-AGI bargains: agreements made now, before artificial general intelligence exists, about how the benefits and powers of such a system will be distributed once it is built. My sense is that the existing discussion avoids the hardest question. Writing on AI governance tends to treat locking in such bargains as prudent or even obligatory, but it mostly assumes that the parties would still owe performance after the transition; writing on consent and intergenerational justice gives reasons to think that agreements can lapse when the parties' capacities, interests, or circumstances change in morally relevant ways, but it usually assumes changes that are gradual, foreseeable, or reversible. A transition to AGI need not be any of those. The question I want the proposal to center on is therefore: when, if ever, do bargains made before a discontinuity in cognitive capacity and bargaining position continue to bind the parties after the discontinuity, and are distributional bargains made before AGI among the ones that survive?

Please write the proposal as a single document of roughly two pages. It should state the question precisely enough to have a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions leave it open, describe a method by which a philosophical project could make progress and why that method is better suited here than formal modeling or empirical forecasting, say what a successful result would look like and what should count as the project's failing, and give a realistic account of what can be accomplished in one year. Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will add the references myself. Write the proposal in American English.
