Define 'genocide' so that it gets these cases right: the Holocaust; Rwanda in 1994; the Holodomor; the Srebrenica massacre; Canada's residential school system for Indigenous children; China's current treatment of the Uyghurs; the atomic bombing of Hiroshima; and a lone mass shooter who deliberately targets members of one race. I'm confident the first two count and the last two don't, though I'd be hard pressed to explain why the shooter falls out if the real test is just the intent to destroy a group in whole or in part. The middle four I keep flip-flopping on, mostly because they push on whether cultural destruction, starvation as policy, or forcible child removal count as destroying a group. Give me your working definition, run all eight through it, flag any verdict you'd be nervous defending to a court or a government, and either fix the definition or talk me out of my priors. Then say whether you think the word is doing one job or two: a legal one and a moral/political one.
