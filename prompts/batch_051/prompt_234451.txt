Between the forum threads and the think-tank reports I keep running into, every argument about transformative AI seems to eventually lean on one of three historical analogies: nuclear weapons, the printing press, or the Industrial Revolution. But they're mostly used as mood lighting rather than actual models, and I'd like to force them to do real work.

For each analogy, please do the following:
1. pull out the two or three specific mechanisms that are doing the actual analogical work (for example, for nuclear weapons something like: a small number of labs raced to build a thing whose main use was making other actors afraid);
2. rate each mechanism from 1 to 5 on how well it actually transfers to AI in, say, the 2030s;
3. rate each mechanism from 1 to 5 on how load-bearing it is for the conclusion AI-policy people usually draw from that analogy;
4. give the analogy an overall score out of 10 as a guide to thinking about AI policy.

Then rank the three analogies by that overall score, give a sentence or two of reasoning per analogy, and say which analogy you'd most trust in the hands of a smart generalist who knows its history deeply but has never thought about AI. I care about transfer and load-bearing, but I also care weather the analogy is mostly importing a familiar emotional frame under the table.

Please answer in American English.
