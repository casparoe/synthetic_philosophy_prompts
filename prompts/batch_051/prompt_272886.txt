I've been chewing on a problem I ran into while thinking about how you infer what some optimizer is doing. From the outside you have to reconstruct two things at once—what it's actually trying to maximize, and what options it can even see or search over. Those two get tangled: a weird move might mean a weird objective or just a narrow search. Then it hit me that I do almost the same thing when I look at an artist's whole body of work: I'm trying to seperate what the painter was after from what the painter was able to do. A flat, awkward painting might be taste, or it might be a skill ceiling, and the work alone doesn't tell you which. Is this the same underlying problem wearing different clothes, or am I just pattern-matching on the fact that both involve guessing hidden stuff from behavior? If it is the same problem, what transfers—does the optimizer case give a clear way to distinguish objective from search space that art talk is missing, or does art give a better sense of when you can't ever pin someone's goal down?
