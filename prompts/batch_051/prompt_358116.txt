Over the past few months I've talked myself into a structuralist view of math, mostly by thinking about code. The puzzle that got me there is this: in ZFC you can build the naturals the von Neumann way, with 0 = ∅, 1 = {∅}, 2 = {0,1}, 3 = {0,1,2}, 4 = {0,1,2,3}, so 2 is literally an element of 4; but you can pick a different encoding where 2 is not an element of 4, and all the arithmetic still works. So I concluded that whether 2 is an element of 4 is just an artifact of an implementation, and that numbers themselves have no non-structural properties: 2 is nothing over and above being the successor of the successor of the initial element in the natural-number structure. Like an object whose whole identity is its interface, not its representation.

I want to stress-test this before I get to comfortable. What would count against it? I don't mean the old arguments about whether numbers *are* sets; my positive view is that numbers are positions in a structure, with no implementation-level facts. I want to know which observations, arguments, edge cases, or results from other areas of math or philosophy should push me off that. Which defeators would a committed structuralist say are just restating the view, and which actually sting? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and try to separate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's still being fought over, so I don't mistake a live dispute for consensus.
