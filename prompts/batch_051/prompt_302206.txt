I'm a PhD student on the philosophy-of-software side of intellectual property, and my two supervisors sit in opposite camps on the question I keep tripping over: for copyleft licences, where does one program end and another begin? The integrationists think the boundary should track how tightly two programs are designed to function as one system — shared data structures, plugins built against a host's internals, dynamic linking into a single address space, that sort of coupling. The modularists think the boundary should track whether anything protectable has actually been copied or adapted, so a separately written program that merely calls another program's interface is its own work however tight the functional integration gets. Could you draft the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between those two camps should produce? I don't want a verdict on which side is right. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can genuinely sign, covering how the licences are drafted, what the derivative-work concept does and doesn't settle, and where the doctrine is simply underdetermined; (2) a table of the remaining disagreements, with each position worded in a way the relevant camp would accept as its own; (3) for each disagreement, the crux if you can find one — the sub-question on which at least one side would actually change its mind; and (4) two or three concrete cases both sides agree bear on those cruxes — say, dynamic linking to a library, a seperately distributed plugin for a GPL host, and two programs passing messages over a socket — with each side's prediction about the right outcome written down in advance. Also flag anything you think is mostly a verbal disagreement or a policy preference dressed up as doctrine. End by saying how confident you are in the conceptual and legal claims you've written, and pick out the single point you're least sure of.
