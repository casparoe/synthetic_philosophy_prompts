After a long career as a field limnologist, I have retired and turned to the philosophy of language and the foundations of probability; the combination has made my part-time service on the Lake Marrowbone Compact Commission considerably more difficult. The Compact, negotiated in 1957, was drafted by people who understood a "substance" as a recognizable pollutant and an "injury" as something observable shortly after a spill. They had no quantitative risk assessment, no concept of a tail probability, and no inkling that compliance would one day be decided by a simulation. Article IV(1) of the Compact reads: "No Party shall cause or permit to be introduced into the waters of the Lake any substance which may be injurious to the fishery, including its spawn and food organisms."

We are now asked to approve a south-shore municipality's plan to discharge treated effluent carrying roughly 0.4 parts per trillion of the degradation product of a synthetic flocculant first manufactured in 2005. The compound is not a recognized fish poison at ordinary environmental concentrations; our best model, built for this permit review, estimates that under the worst-case scenario it produces measurable damage to larval fish with a probability of about one in fifty thousand per year, and that under every other scenario the effect is negligible. We are therefore left with a substance that may injure the fishery only in the far tail of a model the drafters could not have imagined.

I must write the Commission's short opinion explaining what Article IV(1) requires. Please argue the question from the text, especially the word "may"; from the evident purpose of the fishery clause; from what the drafters would have said had they been asked about a one-in-fifty-thousand annual risk; and from what a reasonable Party to the Compact would understand the provision to prohibit. Then say which of these approaches should control and why. I am especially interested in whether a provision written in the language of possibility reaches any event whose probability is merely nonzero, or only those that are probable enough to matter, and where a principled threshold could come from.
