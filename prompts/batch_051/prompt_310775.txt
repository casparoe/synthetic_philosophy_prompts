ok so for a workshop a few of us are writing out our credo on translation and i want mine stress-tested before i circulate it. i have a nagging feeling it doesn't all cohere but i cant find the crack. here are the commitments, roughly as i'd write them:

(1) there's no perfect or final translation; translating is always interpreting.
(2) still, some translations are just wrong, not merely different.
(3) the thing a translator is faithful to is the whole text, so word-by-word fidelity isnt even a coherent target.
(4) a good translation should read the way the author would have written if the target language had been their native one.
(5) when the original is strange, hard, or exploiting the resources of its own language, the translation should keep that strangeness rather than smooth it over.
(6) being faithful to an original doesn't mean recovering a single determinate meaning, because the original doesn't have one in plenty of places; two quite different translations can both be faithful to the same text.
(7) there are texts i'd refuse to translate, on the grounds that any version i produced would be a serious misrepresentation of what the text does.

go through and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the genuine ones by how bad they are, and say what the cheapest fix for each is and what it costs. then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together.
