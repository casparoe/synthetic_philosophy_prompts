I'm a judge on a three-judge panel in our state's intermediate appellate court, and I've been assigned to write the opinion in a case that's tying my two colleages in knots. The city ordinance at issue makes it unlawful to operate a motorized vehicle in any city park, and the municipal code defines "motorized vehicle" as any vehicle with a motor that is capable of carrying a person or a load. A full-time parks department employee was cited for driving the city's electric utility cart into a park at six in the morning to empty trash barrels and clean the restrooms. The trial court found him in violation and fined him $150. He's appealing.

There's no dispute that the cart is a "motorized vehicle" under the definition, so the text plainly reaches exactly what he was doing. One colleague says that's the end of the matter: the council chose broad language, the result may be unfortunate but it isn't our job to fix it, and if the city wants an exemption for its own workers, the council can pass one. The other colleague says no reasonable council adopts a park-traffic ordinance to punish its own trash collectors for doing their jobs, and courts have long refused to apply clear text when the outcome is absurd. Adding a narrow exception for official park-maintenance vehicles, he says, is reading the statute as law, not writing a new one. The city complicates things by pointing out that in two other ordinances—one for the airport service roads and one for the public golf course—the council explicitly exempted official city vehicles, but it included no exemption here. The first colleague says that shows the council knows how to exempt and deliberately declined to; the second says it just shows the council wasn't thinking about its own maintenance crews, and a drafting oversight is exactly the kind of absurdity courts clean up.

Draft the opinion for the panel. I need a concrete holding—does the ordinance apply to this employee or not—plus a general account of when a court may decline to apply clear text on the ground of absurdity, not just a gut call. Please also address the strongest point for whichever side loses. Don't bother with case citations; this is for my own thinking right now.
