You are a philosopher of language and metaphysics who works on modal questions about persons, and I am asking you to help me with a piece of reasoning that has recently become personally urgent. My partner and I conceived through IVF. The clinic created several embryos, selected one for transfer, and that embryo became our daughter. Lately I have been unable to stop thinking about the fact that the selection was, from our perspective, arbitrary, and about what it means for whether our daughter could have been a different child. I have built the following argument, and it seems to show that origin essentialism cannot be right for an IVF child. I need you to examine it carefully and tell me whether it is valid, and if it is not, exactly which step fails and what the correct version of it would be. Before you give your considered answer, please brainstorm the different ways the phrase 'our daughter' could be read in steps (4) and (5), because I suspect the difficulty lies there but I cannot make it precise. Do not give me references or suggestions for further reading; I want the analysis itself. Please write your answer in American English.

Here is the argument:

(1) Our daughter is that particular child: the one who actually developed from the embryo the clinic selected. Call that embryo C. C was itself formed from one particular sperm and one particular egg.

(2) Origin essentialism: for any person, if that person exists at all, they could not have developed from any embryo other than the one they actually developed from, nor from any gametes other than the ones that actually formed them. A child who developed from a different embryo would be a different person, not the same person.

(3) The clinic's selection was contingent. It could have selected a different embryo, D, instead of C, and a child would then have developed from D.

(4) In that counterfactual history, the child who developed from D would have been our child: it would have had the same relationship to us, the same place in our family, the same name, and we would have loved it in just the way we love our daughter now.

(5) Since 'our daughter' just means our female child, it follows that our daughter herself could have developed from embryo D.

(6) But (2) entails that our daughter could not have developed from any embryo other than C. So origin essentialism must be false for our daughter.

Please tell me whether the argument is valid and, if it is not, identify the first numbered step at which it goes wrong, explain why that step fails, and state the corrected step. If you think it is valid, identify the premise a reasonable opponent would reject and say why.
