You are a professional philosopher of mind with a research background in perception, colour science, and the metaphysics of conscious experience. Please treat this as serious academic planning: think through the candidate questions, discard the merely fashionable ones, and explain your reasoning as you go.

I am a doctoral student preparing a one-term research proposal for a seminar on neural interfaces and the limits of experience, and I would like you to draft the proposal for me. My half-formed idea is that the debate about whether a brain-computer interface could ever deliver a genuinely novel colour experience is stuck because the two sides are answering different questions. The sceptical side points to the opponent-process architecture of early colour vision: colour is encoded along two chromatic opponent channels, red-green and blue-yellow, and because a single channel cannot signal both poles at once, combinations such as reddish-green and bluish-yellow are called forbidden colours. From this it is inferred that the dimensionality of colour experience is biologically fixed, so an interface could at most produce an already available hue through a non-standard route. The optimistic side replies that 'forbidden' need not mean absolutely impossible: there are contested reports of observers, under stabilised-image conditions, describing something like reddish-green, and cortical stimulation might in any case bypass the early opponent machinery. From this it is inferred that an appropriately driven neural population could yield a genuinely new colour experience.

I suspect both inferences trade on an unarticulated distinction between an experience that is new for a particular subject and a new kind of experience, and between a new value along an existing colour dimension and a new colour dimension altogether. The question I want the proposal to pose is roughly this: what would brain-computer interface evidence have to show for us to conclude that an experience is a genuinely novel colour, in a sense that matters to the metaphysics of experience, rather than an unfamiliar but in-principle familiar one? Please turn this into a precise and tractable question; explain why the colour-science answer and the philosophical qualia-language answer each leave it open; set out the method, presumably a mix of conceptual clarification, operationalising the idea of a new colour dimension, and sketching a hypothetical implant whose outputs would count as positive or null evidence; say what a positive result and a null result would each establish and what would count as the project failing; and give an honest appraisal of what might make it too large for one term. I will add the scholarly references myself, so do not include a bibliography or literature review.
