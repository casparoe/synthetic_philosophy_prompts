For a graduate seminar on Kant's Critique of Pure Reason, prepare a specification of the requirements a treatment of the antinomies of pure reason must meet in order to count as a genuine resolution of them rather than an evasion or a mere diagnosis. Cover all four antinomies together: (1) whether the world has a beginning in time and limits in space; (2) whether composite substances consist of simple parts; (3) whether natural causality is the only causality or whether freedom is also possible; (4) whether an absolutely necessary being exists, either as part of the world or as its cause. A requirement may be motivated by one antinomy and need not apply uniformly to all four, but the list as a whole should speak to all of them.

For each requirement, give the consideration behind it — the feature of Kant's antinomies that the requirement protects. These considerations should typically be drawn from the dialectical situation itself: the apparent soundness of both the thesis and antithesis proofs, reason's demand for the unconditioned, the cosmological idea of a world-whole, the difference between the mathematical antinomies and the dynamical antinomies, the distinction between appearances and things in themselves, and the difference between a constitutive and a regulative use of reason. Then mark each requirement as non-negotiable for any adequate account, or as a preference of a particular interpretive camp — for instance, a requirement that only a two-worlds reading, a two-aspects reading, or a deflationary reading of transcendental idealism would insist on.

Do not turn this into a scorecard of named commentaries or secondary literature; the desiderata themselves are the deliverable. But I do want one section identifying which requirement or small set of requirements does the most to exclude candidate treatments — for example, treatments that show Kant's proofs to be fallacious without explaining why the conflict is necessary and natural to reason, or treatments that dissolve the antinomies by denying that the conflicting judgments ever appear to be about the same thing. Finally, if any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, or satisfiable only by attributing to Kant a view he does not actually hold in the Critique, say so plainly; that is the part I most want to see.
