i sit on the state's Child and Family Policy Review Board, and we've been asked to weigh in on a pilot proposal to license parents before they can take a newborn home. Under the proposal, before hospital discharge the biological parents would have to complete a 12-hour child development course, submit to one home visit from a licensed social worker, and pass a structured risk screen for abuse and neglect. The license would cover first-time parents, and would only be revisited if there's a later substantiated maltreatment report. Half the board frames it as protecting the child's right to an open future: being raised by someone who can't meet basic caregiving needs closes off a kid's options before the kid even has any, so the state has reason to screen in advance — like a driver's test for the one activity that most shapes a person's life. The other half thinks that's a seperate thing entirely: the open-future idea gives the child a claim against being boxed in by others, not a positive entitlement to a good-enough upbringing, and universal screening treats would-be parents as guilty until proven competent, with predictable discrimination against poor, disabled, and non-traditional families. And the empirics are not on our side either — the risk screens have lousy positive predictive value, so we'd flag a lot of safe families and miss a lot of dangerous ones. Draft the short memo I'll circulate before Friday's vote: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete course of action and a time frame. Engage the strongest point on the side you don't recommend rather than just restating the policy's goals or the legal background. The board needs something we can actually vote on, not a survey of arguments.
