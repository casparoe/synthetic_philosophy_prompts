Last night I got into a long argument with a friend about whether misleading someone is as bad as lying to them. I was defending the view that it can be: a mutual friend told her girlfriend she was "just getting dinner with a coworker" after work, which was technically true, but she carefully let her think it was a group thing when it was actually just her and her ex. I said that was every bit as bad as lying. My friend thought I was being prissy about it, and he hit me with this argument:

(1) If misleading is as bad as lying, it must be because they share the same wrong-making feature. (2) The only serious candidate for that feature is deliberately causing someone to believe something false, or to keep believing it. (3) But if that feature is what makes lying wrong, then anything that has it should be just as wrong as lying. (4) Magic tricks, stage acting, poker bluffs, and letting a coworker keep a harmless false impression all have that feature. (5) But none of those are as bad as lying, and some aren't wrong at all. (6) So deliberately causing false belief isn't what makes lying wrong, and the supposed equivalence between lying and misleading was built on top of it. (7) So misleading isn't as bad as lying; at best it's wrong in some other, lesser way, and I haven't even shown that.

I still think what our mutual friend did was as bad as a lie, but I couldn't say wich step to reject without feeling like I was just digging in my heels. It's driving me up the wall.
