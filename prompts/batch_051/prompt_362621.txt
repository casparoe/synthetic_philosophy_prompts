Me and a friend got into an argument about whether a serial cheater can blame his partner for a one-off affair, and I wound up defending a stronger claim than I actually believe. Taking it as given that the partner's cheating was wrong, I tried to write my reasoning down, and now I cant tell whether its valid or whether I've talked myself into something comfortable. Here is the argument as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Whether someone deserves blame for what they did depends only on their act and their reasons for doing it.
(2) So if B did something blameworthy, B deserves blame no matter who is pointing it out.
(3) Suppose A blames B for cheating, and A is a serial cheater who has never acknowledged or repaired his own cheating—A is a hypocrite in the relevant sense.
(4) We say A has no standing. But standing cant make B undeserving; at most it makes A an awkward messenger.
(5) If B deserves blame, then there is nothing wrong with anyone telling B, truly, that what B did was wrong and that B should repair it.
(6) So either A's lack of standing makes A's saying that wrong—in which case (5) is false—or lack of standing is not about whether A's utterance is wrong at all, just about our distaste for A.
(7) And the only things that could make A's utterance wrong are that it is false, that it is gratuitously harmful, or that it misleads B into taking A for a spotless critic. A's claim is true. It need not be harmful. And A could say it while openly admitting he has done the same, so it need not mislead.
(8) Therefore A's blame is permissible, and the no standing move has no real force.

I keep suspecting (5) treats blame as if it were just a true moral report, but I cant say exactly what else blame involves. Is the argument valid? If not, which step or steps fail, and what is the strongest version of the failing premise?
