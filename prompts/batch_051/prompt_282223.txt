I'm TA-ing an intro epistemology course and I've got a stack of midterm answers on the innate ideas debate to grade. The question students were answering was:

"Leibniz and Locke both discuss whether any ideas or principles are in the mind before experience. First, present one of Leibniz's arguments for innate ideas. Second, present what you take to be Locke's strongest objection to innate principles. Third, say whether that objection refutes the Leibnizian argument you presented, and why or why not."

One student wrote:

"Leibniz argues from necessary truths. The senses only ever tell us how things actually are, in particular circumstances; they never tell us that something must be the case. But we have knowledge of necessary truths, like mathematical truths. Since their necessity could not come from experience, it must come from the mind itself. So the mind must contain some innate ideas or principles that make this knowledge possible.

Locke's strongest objection is that if a principle were really in the mind, the person whose mind it is in would have to be aware of it. To say a proposition is in the mind but nobody is ever conscious of it makes no sense. Yet children and people with serious cognitive disabilities do not assent to the supposed innate principles, and even competent adults only come to know them after instruction. So these principles cannot be innate.

This does refute Leibniz's argument from necessary truths, because necessary truths are not present in a baby's mind either. A baby cannot do arithmetic, so the relevant ideas are not there from birth.

It is worth adding that empiricism does not have to deny all innate structure. Locke himself allows the mind to have innate faculties or capacities, like perception, memory, and abstraction. Empiricism rejects innate content, not innate mechanisms. So the disagreement is narrower than people assume."

I've read enough of these to know the rough shape of a good answer, but I'm not sure how to weight some of the moves this makes. Can you grade this out of 25 and give me the point-by-point comments you'd write in the margins? I want to know which parts are right, which parts are subtly off, and whether the third paragraph actually follows from what comes before it.
