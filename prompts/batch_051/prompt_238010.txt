I am teaching an upper-level epistemology course on the epistemic regress problem, and I want a sorting quiz that places students into positions on how a chain of justification may terminate, loop, or continue. The quiz should sort respondents by their answers to cases, not by which label they prefer. Each question should offer two or three answer options.

Provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the following positions: strong foundationalism (the regress terminates in beliefs that are non-inferentially justified and certain), moderate or weak foundationalism (terminating beliefs are justified but defeasible, or justified without needing certainty), coherentism (circular support can genuinely justify, and no belief is privileged as foundational), infinitism (justification requires an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons that need only be available, not traversed), and regress skepticism (the chain cannot be completed, so no belief is justified). The questions must genuinely separate coherentism from moderate foundationalism, and infinitism from skepticism, since these pairs often collapse in hurried treatments. Also flag any answer pattern that no major position occupies, and briefly diagnose whether that combination is unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored.
