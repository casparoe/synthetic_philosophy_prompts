In a seminar on Abelard's ethics, the maxim 'the sin is in the consent, not the act' was invoked to settle two different disputes. In the first, it was taken to mean that someone who firmly resolves on a grave wrong but is prevented from carrying it out is no less culpable than someone who succeeds. In the second, it was taken to mean that someone who performs the prohibited act under coercion has not sinned. The maxim appears to run several distinct claims together. Distinguish the precise theses it could be asserting, say which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false; then identify which thesis each of the two disputes actually needs, and whether the maxim, on that reading, supplies it.
