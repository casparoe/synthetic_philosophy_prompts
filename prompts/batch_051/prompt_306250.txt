I help run a small discussion society that has recently attracted several members who want to make argument into something measurable, and one of them has circulated a proposal to turn the society into a competitive reasoning league. The proposal, as written, works like this. Each round, a player is assigned to defend a position on a proposition drawn from a pool submitted by players, with the constraint that no one may be assigned a proposition they authored. The players exchange written briefs of at most 900 words. Each brief must: (a) list the claims on which the argument centrally depends, each tagged with a confidence level from 1 to 10; (b) state, under each claim, the observation or argument that would lead the author to retract it; and (c) state what the author considers the strongest argument against their own assigned side. Opponents reply in at most 400 words identifying what they take to be the weakest dependency in the brief. A panel drawn from the league's other members grades the briefs and replies. The unusual part is the scoring. Winning a round earns nothing. Instead, a player gains points when a tagged claim survives an opponent's challenge and loses points when a tagged claim is shown to be false, misleading, or unverifiable, with the loss scaled by the stated confidence; across the season these outcomes are compared with the stated confidence levels to produce a calibration score. Separately, each player's grades of briefs in rounds they are not part of are matched against the panel's eventual grades, and players are scored on how well they forecast the panel. The public leaderboard shows only calibration and forecasting scores, with no win-loss column.

I am trying to work out how this institution would actually behave before we pilot it, and I do not trust my first intuitions. What does the scoring scheme incentivize in the choice of propositions, the drafting of dependency lists, and the assignment of confidence? Where would it be gamed first—through vague or hedged claims, through briefs engineered to make every dependency unchallengeable, through collusion on panel grading, or through something subtler? Would the league select for better reasoners, or for people skilled at the mechanism itself? And which failure modes could be fixed without turning the whole thing back into ordinary debate with its usual incentives?
