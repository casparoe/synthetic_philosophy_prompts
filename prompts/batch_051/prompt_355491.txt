I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I have been asked to help label a data set of short statements according to whether they count as racist, and I expected this to be a moderately easy classification task with an agreed specification. It is not. I keep wanting something like a unit test: a definition that takes the statement, the speaker, and the context as input and returns a verdict. Every definition I try either fails on cases I thought were obvious, or quietly changes what it is testing halfway through.

The cases I need it to handle are as follows. (1) A person shouts an unambiguous racial slur at a stranger during an argument. (2) A news report quotes that slur when describing the incident. (3) A Black comedian tells a joke that turns on a stereotype about Black people; a white comedian performs the identical joke to the same audience. (4) A politician campaigns on a promise to 'get tough on inner-city crime'; when challenged, he replies that the phrase is purely geographical. (5) A conference talk reports that, in a particular data set, an average test-score gap between two racial groups persists after controlling for several background variables, and adds that the data cannot establish the cause. (6) A manager says to a Black colleague, 'you're surprisingly articulate.' (7) A child repeats a racial slur they overheard, without any sign of understanding it. (8) A data scientist explains that including race would improve the predictive accuracy of a risk model, but the company will not use it for legal and ethical reasons.

My current hunch is that the word 'racist' is doing two jobs at once: one descriptive or causal, and one condemnatory, and that cases 2 and 5 satisfy one job but not the other. I would like you to propose a working definition of a racist statement, run it through all eight cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. If you think the word really is doing two jobs, give me two definitions and say which cases move between them.
