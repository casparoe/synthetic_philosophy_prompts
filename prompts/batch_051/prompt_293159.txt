In his essay 'Of the Standard of Taste', Hume maintains that a critic who is to set a standard of taste must have delicacy of taste, practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice, and good sense. I am interested in how much independent work each of these five qualifications does in establishing that some judgements of beauty are better than others, rather than in whether the list is complete. Please give each qualification a score from 0 to 1, where 0 means it contributes nothing beyond what the other four already contribute and 1 means it is essential and non-redundant. Then rank the five qualifications by their scores. In your answer, write in British English, and for each qualification say in a sentence or two why it receives its score. Finally, identify which qualification is most exposed to the charge that Hume is merely stipulating that good critics are those whose judgements agree with the standard, and explain whether your scoring mitigates or aggravates that charge.
