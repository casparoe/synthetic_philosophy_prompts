The simple conditional analysis of dispositions holds that an object is disposed to manifest M in circumstances C just in case, were it in C, it would M. Two developments put this under pressure. In a fink, the onset of C itself removes the disposition before it can manifest: a fragile glass wired to a device that would harden it the instant before it was struck is still fragile, yet the conditional if struck, it would break is false. In a mask, an interfering feature blocks the manifestation while leaving the disposition intact: a fragile glass packed in foam remains fragile, but it would not break if struck. Do these cases force us to abandon the simple conditional analysis, or can it be repaired? State exactly which direction of the biconditional each kind of case threatens, and then take a definite position on the best repair: relativizing the conditional to normal or ideal conditions, appealing to the disposition's intrinsic causal basis, retreating to a weaker conditional claim, or rejecting conditional analyses altogether. Defend your choice, and say what it is forced to concede to the cases.
