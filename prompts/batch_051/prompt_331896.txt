I sit on a hospital committee that reviews death determinations. We have no written definition of death; we have only our prior case rulings, and we need to decide a new case consistently with them. I am not asking for legal citations or a literature review; I want a recommendation reasoned from our own precedents.

Our prior rulings:

- Case A. A patient was pulseless and not breathing for four minutes during a cardiac procedure before being resuscitated, and he recovered fully. We ruled that he was not dead during those four minutes.
- Case B. A patient in a persistent vegetative state (unresponsive wakefulness syndrome), breathing unassisted, with intact brainstem reflexes but no behavioral awareness. We ruled that she was alive; we declined to allow organ procurement before death, though we permitted withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment.
- Case C. A patient on a ventilator after a massive brain injury met whole-brain criteria for death, with absent brainstem reflexes and a positive apnea test. We ruled that he was dead and allowed organ procurement while the ventilator was still running and the heart was still beating.
- Case D. A newborn with anencephaly, with a functioning brainstem and reflex activity but no cerebral hemispheres. We ruled that the newborn was alive and declined to permit organ recovery before death.

The new case: a patient with a valid do-not-resuscitate order has had five minutes of continuous, witnessed asystole (no heartbeat), with no realistic prospect that the heart will restart on its own and no attempt to restart it will be made. No brain-death testing has been performed. The transplant team wants to recover organs now. We must decide whether to declare the patient dead now on circulatory grounds or to withhold the declaration until whole-brain criteria are met.

Give me the recommendation as a JSON object with this schema:

- candidate_rules: an array of objects, one per distinct death-determination rule; include every rule that fits all four prior rulings, and briefly note any rule that a single precedent would exclude. Each object has: label (short name), criterion (precise statement of the rule), fit_with_precedents (one sentence), and verdict_for_new_case (what the rule yields for the new case).
- recommended_verdict: either declare dead now or withhold declaration, with one sentence of reasoning from the precedents.
- closest_precedent: which prior case is the closest analog to the new case and why.
- principle_adopted: what principle the recommended verdict commits the committee to.
- what_is_not_settled: what the recommendation would still leave open.
- separating_future_case: a brief description of a future case that would most cleanly separate the rules that remain live after this decision.
