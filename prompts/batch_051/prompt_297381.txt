I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to write a utopia that doesn't secretly suck. The problem is that a world where every desire is instantly satisfied feels like it would either be a dystopia or just deeply boring, and I want a story that avoids both. The idea I keep coming back to is that a good future might need what I've started calling productive friction: not scarcity or suffering or oppression, but the pushback that comes from doing something with internal difficulty—learning an instrument, tending a garden, staying in a hard conversation, training a body. Something that resists you just enough that your choices still feel like yours. So the prompt I'd give a collabortor is this: write a short story of about 800 words set in a future where material scarcity is bascially solved and no one is coerced, but where the main conflict is someone trying to find or protect a source of meaningful friction in a world that has smoothed away almost all of it. The story should dramatize, rather than lecture about, the difference between getting whatever you want and wanting well. Don't make the utopia secretly evil, and don't have the character want to destroy it. End with a small, earned sense that the friction itself is enough. And please don't cheat by making the friction just self-harm or an artificial struggle—it should be something a reasonable person could see the point of from the inside.
