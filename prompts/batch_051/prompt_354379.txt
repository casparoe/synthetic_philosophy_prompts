In the longstanding dispute over reduction and emergence in the philosophy of physics, I'm struck by the fact that both sides now cite effective field theories and yet neither seems to have moved. The anti-reductionist side points out that an infrared theory is formulated in its own variables, that its couplings can absorb indefinitely messy high-energy physics, and that many different ultraviolet completions flow to the same infrared behavior. The reductionist side replies that this shows only epistemic autonomy: for any particular ultraviolet completion, the infrared theory is still fixed by it, even if we cannot compute the mapping in practice. Both camps accept the same renormalization-group facts and the same physical predictions. My working hypothesis is that they mean different things by derivable — one side means uniquely determined, the other means in principle obtainable — and that separating those senses would dissolve most of the dispute. Is that right, or is there a substantive remainder about what explains what, about whether the world itself is layered, or about what a successful explanation must do? And if my diagnosis is wrong, what would actually count as progress here: a formal result, an experiment, or a sharper distinction?
