I've been going back and forth on a paralell I can't shake and I want someone to either make it rigorous or kill it. In vector semantics, word meanings are points in a high-dimensional space and you can do arithmetic on them: the stock example is king − man + woman ≈ queen. That looks an awful lot like the old componential analysis of word meaning from mid-century linguistics, where a lexeme is a bundle of discrete semantic features and you form a new concept by swapping one feature — bachelor = [+human, +adult, +male, −married], spinster = [+human, +adult, −male, −married], and queen is basically 'king, but female.' The vector arithmetic seems to be doing exactly that: take king, subtract the male bit, add the female bit, get queen.

So my question is wether embedding arithmetic is a genuine vindication of feature-bundle semantics, or whether I'm just pattern-matching. If it's a real identity of structure, what should transfer between the two — for instance, should we expect the dimensions of an embedding space to be interpretable features, or expect the arithmetic to work for all concepts, not just the famous examples? If it's superficial, what's the cleanest thing the feature view predicts that embeddings actually get wrong? I'd rather you pick a side than tell me it's complicated.
