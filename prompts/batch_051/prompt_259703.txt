I run the grantmaking side of a small family foundation, mostly education and community health, and I read maybe thirty letters of inquiry a week. I keep seeing the same move: a LOI spends two pages establishing an empirical fact — absenteeism dropped in a pilot, cash transfers give families more choice, something is "evidence-based" or "what communities already do" — and then slides straight to "therefore you should fund us." I took one metaethics course years ago and remember the is-ought gap (the point that you cant derive a normative conclusion from purely descriptive premises) and Moore's open-question argument (that you can always ask of any natural property, "yes, but is it good?"). I want to turn those into something I can actually run at my desk, not a term paper topic.

So here's what I need: a reusable decision procedure — a checklist or a flowchart in words — that I can run in about twenty minutes on a single LOI. It should catch places where an ought is being smuggled in under descriptive vocabulary ("natural," "effective," "best practice," "what people want or do," "community-led") and places where there is a bridge premise but it's unstated and would need independent defense. It should also keep two jobs separate: the inference problem (are the premises even the right kind to support the conclusion?) and the definition problem (is a word like "good" or "successful" being defined in terms of something empirical without anyone saying so?). Assume the person using it hasn't read any philosophy; my colleague needs to be able to use it too. Say which steps are genuine tests and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and what to do when a LOI gives no clean numbers.

Then apply it to two or three made-up LOIs, including one where the checklist tells me not to fund something I'm otherwise inclined to love — I want to see it work against my own preferences. Finally, tell me what the procedure cannot catch, the kind of normative slide it'll still miss. And write the whole thing in American English.
