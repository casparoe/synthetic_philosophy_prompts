Define the predicate “over-demanding” as it figures in the familiar objection that a moral theory — paradigmatically a maximizing consequentialism — demands too much of agents. The definition should not simply grade how costly a theory’s requirements are in aggregate, and it should not collapse into the separate claim that those requirements are wrong. It should identify a distinct defect: the theory is over-demanding rather than merely mistaken, expensive, or inconvenient. It should get the clear cases below right and should give a definite answer either way for the borderline ones.

The cases:

(1) A theory requiring you to wade into a shallow pond to save a drowning child, ruining a good pair of shoes.

(2) A maximizing beneficence theory requiring every agent to donate all income above what is needed for a minimally decent life, except where doing so would be self-defeating.

(3) A theory forbidding you to pursue a demanding artistic or scholarly career, on the ground that your talents would do more good elsewhere.

(4) A theory requiring you to sacrifice your life in order to save two strangers who would otherwise die.

(5) A deontological theory that absolutely prohibits lying, even to a murderer asking where the intended victim is hiding.

(6) A theory requiring a firefighter, in virtue of that role, to enter a burning building to try to save a child.

(7) A theory imposing only small requirements — daily check-ins on neighbors, modest donations, volunteer shifts — whose aggregate consumes nearly all of the agent’s discretionary time.

I am confident that (1) is not over-demanding and that (2) and (4) are. I have no settled view about (3), (6), and (7). I suspect (5) is demanding in a different sense from the others, if it is demanding at all.

Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. In doing so, address whether you end up needing one notion or two: a descriptive measure of what an agent must give up, and a normative complaint that the theory leaves too little room for the agent’s own projects or distributes the burden unfairly. If you think two notions are needed, state both, and say which cases move between them. Conclude by saying which of your verdicts is least secure.
