I keep noticing what looks like the same structure in two places that rarely get discussed together, and I want to know whether the parallel is deep or just pattern-matching.

In epistemology, the debate over epistemic akrasia asks whether it can ever be rational to hold a first-order doxastic attitude — say, believing that a flight is safe — while simultaneously believing, at the higher order, that one's evidence does not support that attitude, or that one's processing of the evidence was compromised. Anti-akrasia views treat the higher-order judgment as the deliverance of the same faculty one uses to assess first-order evidence and conclude that flouting it is always irrational. Pro-akrasia views allow cases in which the higher-order judgment is the compromised element (fatigue, bias, a misleading signal about one's own reliability) and argue that retaining the first-order attitude can be rational despite the mismatch.

In distributed-systems engineering, a structurally similar conflict arises between a component's own computation and a meta-level signal about that component's reliability. A node may compute that a value is correct while a watchdog or crash detector reports that the node itself is faulty; or a partition may leave a node with a minority view while the majority reports a state of the world inconsistent with what the node observes.

The parallel as I see it: both are cases in which a first-order representation of the world, a second-order indication that the representation was produced unreliably, and a rule about which level to defer to jointly determine behavior. The anti-akrasia norm — never let one's attitude toward P contradict one's own assessment of the evidence — looks formally like the engineering rule that a process should defer to a reliable fault signal over its own output; the pro-akrasia position looks like the rule that a process should sometimes stick with its own output when the fault signal may itself be corrupted.

Is this a real identity of structure, or am I overfitting two superficially similar dilemmas? If it is real, state the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and name at least one thing the parallel would predict that fails on one side. If it is only superficial, say exactly where the structures come apart.
