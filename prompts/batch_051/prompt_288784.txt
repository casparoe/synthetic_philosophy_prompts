I'm putting together a counterfactual "interview the dead" episode for a small philosophy podcast, and the guest is Peter Abelard — well, an Abelard scholar answering in character, defending Abelard's line that the whole sin is in the consent and not in the deed, and not even in the desire itself. I need eight to ten interview questions that don't just ask him to restate that thesis; I want them to poke at the places where the view gets hard. At least one question each on (1) how to draw the line between taking pleasure in an unbidden fantasy and consenting to it; (2) whether its a sin to consent to the likely cause of a sin, like someone who gets deliberately drunk knowing exactly what he'll do, without consenting to the act itself at the time; and (3) whether the completed act really adds nothing to guilt, given that Abelard seems to allow harsher human punishment and maybe harsher penance for worse deeds. For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates the answer he's most likely to give and pushes on it.

The angle I'm after is less the standard "but the consequences matter!" objection and more the internal troubles: I keep tripping over cases where the suggestion-pleasure-consent scheme seems to come apart, as with someone who consents to a misdeed while hating it (akratic consent, if that's even coherent on his view), or a negligent omission where there is no identifiable moment of consent at all. Also I'd like a question about whether God's judgement and the Church's punishment end up measuring two completely different things, which seems intended but also a bit wierd to defend to a modern audience.
