In a seminar on the philosophy of rules, I have been defending the thesis that a change to the rules of a rule-governed practice is a valid amendment exactly when three conditions all hold: the change is adopted by the procedure the rules prescribe for making changes; it is made by a person or body those rules authorize; and it conflicts with no rule the practice marks as unalterable. I would like you to test this thesis against a borderline case. Imagine a game of the Nomic sort, in which the players may amend any rule, including the rule that governs how rules are amended. The amendment rule currently provides that any rule of the game may be amended by a majority vote of the players. The players vote on a proposal to amend that very rule so that it will henceforth provide that any rule may be amended only by a two-thirds vote of the players. The proposal receives fifty-five percent of the votes cast: a clear majority, but short of two-thirds. Is this proposal a valid amendment under my three conditions? Work through the conditions one by one in prose, say whether the case qualifies, and then say whether the case shows that any of the three conditions needs revision. Please write in continuous prose, as in an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
