If two options are exact permutations of each other across states, I'm indifferent between them no matter how flashy one of their best cases is. And I keep feeling that assigning equal probabilities to the states and maximizing expected value is the right move when the choice will be repeated many times, while in one-off cases that same move feels like a con. Is there one coherent principle behind all this? State it precisely, say what it predicts about cases I havent listed (mixed one-off and repeatable stakes, feedback that's only partial, options with identical worst cases but different spreads), and tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep.
