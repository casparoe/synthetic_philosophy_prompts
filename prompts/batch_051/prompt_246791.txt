I work as a journalist covering science and ideas. I've developed a rule for staying sane on a contested beat: once I've spent enough time on a topic and reached a settled view, I stop reading the serious rebuttals that come in afterwards. My reasoning is that the evidence I already trust supports my view, so anything that would overturn it is probably misleading, and reading it would just be adding noise. A philosopher friend says I've turned the dogmatism paradox into a workflow: from my own perspective I've concluded that any counter-evidence to my view must be flawed before I've even looked at it, which is precisely the move that makes the paradox work. He has a point, but I do read the opposing side while I'm forming the view, and I can't spent every week reconsidering settled conclusions. Is my rule a defensible epistemic shortcut, or just dogmatism with a salary? If it's not defensible as it stands, what's the closest policy I could defend?
