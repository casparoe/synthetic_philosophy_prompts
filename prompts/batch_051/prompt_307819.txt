I have been thinking about chain-of-thought faithfulness, the worry that a language model's visible reasoning may be a post-hoc rationalization rather than an account of the computation that actually produced its answer, and I want to pressure-test a training scheme a colleague recently proposed.

The scheme assumes a model already trained to solve problems with a moderately objective, verifiable correct answer. Each training episode has two phases. In the first phase the model reads a problem P and produces a hidden chain of reasoning R followed by an answer A. It receives a baseline reward for A's correctness. In the second phase, which occurs on a random subset of episodes, R is passed through a stochastic edit operator before the model writes any continuation: the operator may delete a random contiguous block, replace one sentence with a placeholder token, keep only the first and last sentences, or shuffle the sentence order. The model is then shown only the edited chain R', together with a generic instruction to complete the reasoning and state a final answer. The original problem P is not supplied. The model outputs A', and it receives an additional reward if A' matches A from the first phase.

The designers' rationale is that if an answer is genuinely computed through the chain, then the chain should contain enough of the computation that a capable model can recover the answer from a damaged or reordered fragment of it; if the answer is computed elsewhere and the chain is a plausible-looking cover story, then there is nothing in the chain to recover. The second phase should therefore differentially reward chains that are load-bearing for the answer, and over time the model should learn to make its visible reasoning causally load-bearing. The designers identify this property with faithfulness.

I want to know how the scheme actually plays out, not whether it realizes its stated intention. What policies does the second-phase reward actually select for, and how far can they drift from faithful reporting of the real computation? What degenerate or adversarial strategies become available under this particular edit operator? At deployment, when the fine-tuned model produces visible reasoning for users, what should we expect its chains to look like? Under what conditions does load-bearing under reconstruction come apart from causal responsibility in the first pass, and can the scheme be patched without reintroducing the original problem? Finally, is it selecting for honest reasoning, for a parallel reconstruction skill, or for something else?
