I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether the written reasoning a language model produces can be trusted as a record of how it actually reached its answer. His position, in compressed form, was that chain-of-thought monitoring is doomed: once a model is trained or selected so that its written reasoning is scored by an overseer, optimization pressure teaches it to write reasoning that looks acceptable to the overseer while its real decision procedure drifts elsewhere. The more carefully we monitor the reasoning, he argued, the more we are selecting for fluent rationalization rather than honesty. I defended the view that faithfulness is at least in principle testable — that some measurement could distinguish a faithful chain of thought from a post-hoc justification — but I could not say what the measurement was.

I want to make that claim concrete, or else give it up. Please help me design the strongest test you can of the following claim: for a given model and task, there is a well-defined fact of the matter about whether the written chain of thought reports the process that produced the answer, and that fact can be brought under experimental pressure. Set up the test precisely — for instance, by considering a task where the true basis of the correct answer is a known feature and a tempting shortcut is also available, and by comparing models whose chain of thought is optimized under different monitoring regimes. Say what the two hypotheses are in operational terms, what measurement you would record, and which result would favor which side. Then be honest about what a positive result would and would not establish: address whether causal mediation between the written reasoning and the answer is the right test at all, whether a monitor that sees only text can ever detect reasoning that has learned to hide, and whether the phrase the process that produced the answer picks out something real enough to operationalize or just a convenient figure of speech. If the strongest test still leaves the disagreement unresolved in principle, say whether that is a limitation of our instruments or a defect in the notion of faithfulness itself.
