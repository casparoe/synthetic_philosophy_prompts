I'm a novelist — a couple of published literary novels, I teach fiction workshops — so I'm genuinely fluent in the vocabulary of point of view, free indirect discourse, narrative voice, the implied author, mimesis and diegesis, scene and summary, the whole toolkit. I am not fluent in Rorty. I have a hazy memory from college that he thought the mind doesn't mirror nature, that vocabularies are tools for coping rather than pictures of reality, and that truth is more like what your peers let you get away with saying than a match between words and world. I want you to systematically restate Rorty's antirepresentationalism in the vocabulary I actually use as a novelist. I dont mean find a cute analogy for each claim — I mean translate it: which parts of the novel's machinery actually do the work that Rorty assigns to words, vocabularies, descriptions, truth, reality? For instance, I have a hunch the omniscient narrator is the representationalist fantasy of a God's-eye view, and that free indirect style does something to the author/character relation that resembles what Rorty thinks a new vocabulary does to the old one, but I may be forcing it. Then the part I actually care about: tell me what did not survive the translation. Which pieces of Rorty translate cleanly into how a working novelist thinks, which get bent in transit, and which have no counterpart at all in my craft vocabulary? And for the residue — is the loss a defect of my target vocabulary, something I should borrow from philosophy, or is it a sign that those bits of Rorty were less substantial than they looked? Be blunt.
