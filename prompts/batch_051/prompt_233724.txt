I keep noticing a structural parallel between the thermodynamics of Maxwell's demon and something in formal epistemology, and I would like help deciding whether it is deep or superficial. In his 1871 book Theory of Heat, James Clerk Maxwell asks us to imagine a being so sharp-eyed that it can follow each individual molecule and operate a frictionless shutter in a partition dividing a gas. The being opens the shutter for fast molecules coming from one side and slow molecules coming from the other, so without doing work it makes one side hotter and the other colder, apparently reversing the entropy increase forbidden by the second law. The standard modern answer is that the demon cannot get the sorting information for free: the demon must acquire and later erase information about molecular velocities, and Landauer's principle says erasing a bit of information dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat, which pays back the entropy reduction.

The parallel is this. In Bayesian epistemology, an idealized agent can always condition on more and more finely discriminated evidence. If she has access to a finer partition of the sample space, she can update to a posterior that is, in expectation, no worse and often better calibrated; and these acquisitions can look free, because Bayesianism does not itself charge any entropy or energy for the evidence. But there is a hidden cost: the finer partition requires the agent to represent, store, and eventually update against a more complex set of possibilities. A boundedly rational agent who wants the same discrimination must do representational work, and old beliefs must be overwritten or marginalized away. So I wonder whether the physics-of-information morals transfer. Is there a Bayesian analogue of Landauer's principle, a lower bound on the representational cost that must be paid to achieve a given reduction in expected posterior entropy? And is the parallel genuine enough that results about reversible computation, Bennett's insight that only erasure is irreversible, or the Kolmogorov complexity of the demon's memory, say something precise about epistemic agents?

If the parallel is only superficial, I want to know exactly where it breaks. In the physical case, the second law is a constraint on a conserved quantity given a coarse-grained state description; in the epistemic case, there is no conserved quantity analogous to energy and no thermal bath that pays the bill. So maybe the analogy is just the word information appearing in both places. But I cannot tell whether that difference is fatal, or whether the deeper structure is the same: both cases involve an agent whose fine-grained access to a system allows it to extract a macroscopic asymmetry, and in both the cost is hidden in the agent's own memory rather than in the process it manipulates. Please give me a precise mapping, state what would have to be true on both sides for the physics results to transfer, and name something the parallel predicts about bounded Bayesian agents that turns out false if the analogy is only loose.
