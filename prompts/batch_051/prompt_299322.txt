I have long followed a rule in reading moral psychology. I do not let a finding about human moral judgment alter my views in either direction unless the sample on which it was established, or a later replication, is not exclusively WEIRD: Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic. I adopted the rule after noticing how often results from American undergraduates and other affluent Western adults fail to generalize to small-scale societies. The rule was meant to protect a larger question I am trying to answer: how much cross-cultural variation in folk morality should reduce my confidence that any of my moral convictions track something objective.

The rule has started to fail me, or I have started to fail it. A friend points out two things. First, my application is selective. I invoke the rule against studies that present moral judgment as a bundle of locally learned conventions, because I do not want that conclusion to force me into treating all morality as local habit; but I let studies that present moral judgment as a shared, roughly universal capacity pass without the same scrutiny, even when their samples are just as WEIRD. Second, she argues that a blanket rule is the wrong instrument, because the proper discount for a WEIRD sample depends on the kind of claim being tested. A claim about basic moral cognition — for instance, that people everywhere distinguish a harm done on purpose from one done by accident — may raise different worries about generalizing from WEIRD subjects than a claim about which particular acts, relationships, or foods a community treats as moralized.

I want the rule itself examined, not just my recent use of it. Is it defensible to embargo WEIRD-only evidence by default? If the blanket rule is not defensible, is there a defensible restatement: a discount rate that tracks the distinction between claims about shared moral cognition and claims about culturally variable moral content? And how should I tell, from the inside, when appealing to the WEIRD critique is genuine methodological caution and when it is a shield for the moral convictions I started with?
