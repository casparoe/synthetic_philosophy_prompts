I work as a programme officer at a small foundation that funds and designs pilot projects in local democratic reform, and I need something I can reuse every time a proposal lands on my desk. Most of our board and staff are converts to sortition — genuine enthusiasts for citizens' assemblies and randomly selected panels — and we all tend to roll our eyes at the phrase career politician. The trouble is, I'm the one who writes the memos, and I get maybe one proposal a month: a city wants us to back replacing a planning committee with a citizen panel, a county association wants us to endorse a two-term limit for councillors, a local party wants help designing a salaried council so non-retired people can afford to stand. I have about half a day per proposal, my colleagues havent read the literature, and the board signs off on whatever I recommend. I'm aware my own pro-sortition instincts make me lazy about the risks.

Write me a decision procedure I can run in order before each memo. It should be built from what political theory and the comparative evidence actually tell us about sortition, term limits, and career politicians, translated into questions I can actually ask without a seminar. For each step, say what question I'm asking, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where the procedure says stop/no or send the proposal back for redesign, and where it hands the decision to the board because it's a judgement of values rather than facts. Be honest about the steps where I'll be tempted to fudge because I like the proposal.

Then run the procedure on these two, and show your working:

1. A city wants us to fund a twelve-person citizens' panel, recruited by open call, with binding decision power over planning and licensing applications for an initial two years. Members would meet monthly, get a small allowance, and be advised by two professional planners. The pitch says this takes decisions away from a committee of career politicians and gives them to ordinary people.

2. A county association wants our endorsement for a change to its member councils' constitutions imposing a two-term lifetime limit on councillors, sold explicitly as a cure for careerism and entrenchment. The proposal says nothing about support for members, and the county has a strong permanent officer corps that already does most of the policy work.

Make sure at least one of the two outcomes is one my board won't want to hear, and end by saying plainly what the procedure can't do for me — what it won't protect me from.
