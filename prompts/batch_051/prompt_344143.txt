In my cognitive science reading group, the dispute between representationalism and enactivism has persisted for months without anyone changing their mind. We agree on the phenomena: an outfielder running to intercept a fly ball, a rat solving a maze, a person reaching for a cup. The representationalist says the system must be constructing and updating an internal model of the environment; the enactivist says the behavior emerges from ongoing agent–environment coupling, with the environment itself serving as the relevant model and no inner reconstruction needed. Neither side will say in advance what observation would force it to revise. I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is: a merely verbal dispute about the word representation, a difference in standards of explanation, a pragmatic bet about which research program will be more fruitful, or something else? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a sharper distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or is there reason to expect none?
