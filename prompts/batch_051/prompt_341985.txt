I'm an undergraduate taking a philosophy of science course on measurement and convention, and we have just finished the section on how far conventionalism can be pushed: the claim that certain choices about simultaneity or congruence are free conventions because no observation settles them, and the counterclaim that this only shows the choices are underdetermined, not arbitrary. I found myself more interested in a case we only touched on, the choice of an epoch for a calendar. I got into a long argument with my roommate about why the new millennium was celebrated in 2000 although the twenty-first century technically began in 2001, and from there I fell down a rabbit hole of year-counting puzzles: the Common Era goes from 1 BCE to 1 CE with no year zero, astronomical year numbering quietly inserts a year zero in between, the French Republican calendar tried to restart the count at the founding of the Republic, and proposals like the Holocene calendar add ten thousand years to the Common Era so that the count begins near the start of the Holocene. Most discussions I have seen treat this as a single question about where to put the origin of the count. I suspect that is a mistake. It seems to run together two things: the choice of a zero point, and the structure of the calendar as a system for numbering and aligning years, including whether zero should appear at all, how negative years behave, and what exactly the unit is supposed to track. My half-formed thought is that many choices are conventional in only one of these respects and not the other, and that separating them would dissolve some of the familiar arguments on both sides.

Could you turn this into a short term-paper proposal, the kind I would submit to my instructor for a roughly six-thousand-word paper due in one semester? I would like the proposal to state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions leave it open, describe the approach and why it is better than treating the paper as a survey of historical calendar reforms, say what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide between them, identify what would count as the project failing, and give an honest sense of what could make it too large for a semester. Please do not include citations or a bibliography; I will add those myself. Please also write the whole proposal in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
