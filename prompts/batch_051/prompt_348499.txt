At our company we're building an internal forecasting tool and someone has proposed a blanket rule for every model submitted: any Bayesian model has to use the maximum entropy prior (the flattest distribution that satisfies whatever constraints the modeller explicitly lists), and the constraints have to be registered in the ticket before the modeller sees the data. Reviewers can reject a model if they think the prior encodes information beyond the listed constraints. The pitch is that this makes priors 'objective' and stops people baking opinions into the model. I'm less interested in whether that pitch is right than in how the rule would actually play out: what it selects for among modellers, reviewers, and the company, how the listing of constraints gets gamed, what reparameterisation does to the whole idea, and whether we end up with rational priors or just priors that are defensible on paper. Are the failure modes fixable without turning it into reviewer discretion? Oh, and could you write your answer in British English please.
