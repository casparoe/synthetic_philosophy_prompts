Consider a criminal statute of limitations: the state extinguishes its power to prosecute a wrong after a fixed period, however strong the evidence and however serious the wrong. Many familiar rationales are epistemic or pragmatic: they show that prosecution becomes unreliable or costly, not that punishment after a lapse of time becomes undeserved or impermissible. Others attempt to locate moral significance in the passage of time itself. Please rank the following seven candidate justifications from strongest to weakest specifically as moral justifications — reasons to think that, after enough time, punishment would no longer be deserved, permissible, or valuable, rather than merely difficult to do well.

1. Memory and evidence deteriorate, so old prosecutions carry a serious risk of punishing the innocent.
2. A person changes over decades, so the person the state now punishes is not, in any sense that matters to desert, the same person who acted.
3. Retributive desert fades with temporal distance: the wrong itself becomes a weaker ground for punishment as it recedes into the past.
4. People have a legitimate interest in living without an open-ended threat of punishment, and that interest only grows with time.
5. Punishment is a form of censure, and censure delivered long after the act no longer expresses the community's judgment in a coherent way.
6. Social peace requires that old claims eventually lapse; otherwise harms are inherited and disputes never settle.
7. Requiring victims and communities to reopen old wrongs imposes costs that, beyond a point, outweigh whatever good prosecution could do.

In your reply, do four things. First, give the ranking and a sentence for each item saying what it does and does not establish. Second, say which items could justify a categorical rule of limitation and which could at best justify extra caution case by case. Third, for each item, indicate whether the bare passage of time itself is doing moral work or is standing in for something else. Fourth, state the two rankings you are least confident about, and identify the strongest argument or consideration that would change your mind about each.
