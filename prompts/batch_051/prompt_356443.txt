I am a PhD student in philosophy of mathematics trying to get clear about the Church-Turing thesis before I evaluate the main accounts of its epistemic status, and I have found that the thesis is made to do several quite different jobs at once. It is used to summarize the fact that several independent 1930s formalisms (Turing machines, the lambda calculus, general recursiveness, Post systems) delimit the same class of functions; it is used in undecidability results to justify the conclusion that no effective procedure exists, not merely that no Turing-machine procedure exists; and it is sometimes extended to claims about what any physical device could in principle compute. I want a specification of the target before I read the accounts against it.

I am asking for the desiderata for an adequate account of the epistemic status of the thesis: what kind of claim it is (a definition, a conceptual analysis, an empirical hypothesis, a convention, or something else) and what kind of support or proof it admits. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is non-negotiable, negotiable, or specific to one camp. I am especially interested in whether some of these demands pull against one another. For example, an account may need to say both that the intuitive notion of effective computability is informal and open-textured enough that the thesis cannot be proved as an ordinary theorem, and that the thesis is nonetheless settled enough to close off the search for an algorithm; and it may need to explain why the convergence of independent formalisms is strong evidence without treating the thesis as an ordinary empirical generalization. But I do not want my own list to prejudice yours.

Please return the result as a JSON object with the following fields: "requirements" (an array of objects, each with "requirement", "motivating_consideration", "status" taking one of "non-negotiable", "negotiable", or "camp-specific", and "excludes_or_pressures" naming the candidate positions the requirement puts pressure on); "jointly_unsatisfiable_subsets" (an array of objects, each with "subset" listing requirement names and "why_they_conflict", or an empty array if you judge the list jointly satisfiable); and "most_exclusionary_requirement" (a string naming one requirement and explaining in one sentence which positions it eliminates).
