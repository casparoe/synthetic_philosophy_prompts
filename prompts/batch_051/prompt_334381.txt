I keep noticing a recurring move in proposed solutions to the Liar paradox, and I do not know whether it has a recognized name or even whether it is a single phenomenon.

A proposed solution typically introduces a new status or category to defuse the original Liar. The sentence that says of itself that it is false is declared to be neither true nor false; or meaningless; or not determinately true; or true and false but somehow quarantined from the usual inferences. The solution then appears to work for that sentence. But as soon as the new status is on the table, someone constructs a new sentence that uses that very status: a sentence that says of itself that it is either false or neither true nor false; a sentence that says of itself that it is meaningless; a sentence that says of itself that it is not determinately true. The new sentence recreates the original problem from inside the solution, and the only obvious response is to introduce a further distinction, which then seems to invite a further sentence.

I have seen the pattern in several settings. In a Tarski-style hierarchy, the Liar is ruled out of the object language, but a sentence about that very ruling is formulable from above. In three-valued or fixed-point treatments, the Liar falls into a gap, and a sentence that says of itself that it falls into the gap becomes the problem. In fuzzy treatments the target is the range of intermediate values; in paraconsistent treatments it is the admission of true-and-false sentences. What unsettles me is that in many of these cases the new sentence cannot be formulated until after the solution has been stated: the solution supplies the vocabulary its own counterexample needs.

I am not asking for a survey of these proposals. I want to know whether this pattern has an established name, whether all its instances are best understood as one logical phenomenon or as a looser family, how the pattern is usually distinguished from the original Liar, and what a solution would have to do to break the cycle rather than merely shift it. A bare label will not satisfy me; I need the carving, including where the edges of the phenomenon are and what lies outside it.
