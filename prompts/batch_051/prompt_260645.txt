I sit on my university's research ethics board. We're reviewing a proposal from a visual-neuroscience group that wants to induce "forbidden colors" in healthy volunteers — reddish-green, blue-yellow — using an eye-tracker that stabilizes small equiluminant red and green patches on the retina so the usual opponent-channel inhibition can't resolve the conflict. In a second arm, they add weak anodal tDCS over visual cortex while people do the same task, on the theory that loosening the inhibitory tuning might push past the canonical forbidden pairs into genuinely novel qualia: experiences with no natural precedent and no name. The protocol includes a practice session, an instant-stop button on every trial, normal exclusion criteria, and a debrief. The lab isn't hiding anything, but the consent form can only say participants may experience "unfamiliar or unnameable color sensations," which, fine, thats the honest version: you can't describe a novel quale to someone who hasn't had it.

My priors are a mess here. Half the board reads this as a standard visual-psychophysics study with modest physical risk and good withdrawal procedures. The other half thinks the tDCS arm at least is asking healthy people to consent to a change—temporary, probably—in the structure of their experience that they can't antecedently understand, and that the consent language is doing approximately zero work. There's also a deeper split about whether reddish-green is a genuinely novel color or just an unusual joint report of two familiar hues, and whether that distinction should matter for an ethics decision at all. I suspect both camps are pattern-matching to something safer: "basically an afterimage" versus "basically a first-in-human device."

I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting. Draft the document I'll send. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with a time frame spelled out, and it should seriously engage the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than restating the regs or punting to "more data." And please write it as continuous prose only, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables; I'm going to paste it straight into our minutes and the formatting gets mangled anyway.
