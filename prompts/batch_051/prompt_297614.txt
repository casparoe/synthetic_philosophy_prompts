I've been putting together a graduate seminar on fakes, forgeries, and the value of originals, and last week a student—a physicist, so bless her, she had no patience for the usual talk—asked the really awkward version of the question: if a copy is so good that no expert and no test can tell it from the original, what exactly does the original have that the copy lacks? I found myself reaching for the same five moves I always reach for, and I'd like to be pushed passed them. Here they are, roughly. (1) The original is historical evidence of the artist's actual choices, materials, self-corrections, and pressures, while the copy tells us only about the copyist. (2) Passing a forgery off as an original is deception, and it harms buyers and erodes the trust that connoisseurship depends on. (3) A Goodman-flavoured point: what we see in a painting is not raw retinal input; once you know which canvas is the original, you genuinely see different aesthetic properties in it—invention, originality for its time, rightness of gesture—so the original and the copy can differ aesthetically even while remaining perceptually indistinguishable. (4) The original is the bearer of a particular artist's achievement at a particular moment, under constraints the copyist did not face; the copyist's achievement is derivative mimicry. (5) There's the aura or contact thought: the original is the very object the artist worked on, and humans care about such contact in the way they care about relics and memorabilia. I know the market-scarcity story as well, so don't give me that one.

What I want is one more reason of the same broad kind: a reason a thoughtful person could give for thinking the original has a kind of value—aesthetic, historical, or whatever else—that the perfect copy lacks. It must not be one of the five I've listed, and not a variant, special case, or combination of them (so no relabeling the achievement point as 'skill', and no spinning the contact point into 'sentimental value'). Then tell me honestly whether it is any good, and in particular whether it collapses back into one of my five under pressure. Oh, and I'd be grateful if the answer were in British English rather than American.
