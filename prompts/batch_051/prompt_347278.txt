I'm on my university's academic integrity board. We're hearing an appeal from a student who admitted he lied to an investigator to protect his older brother, who had actually cheated by using a paper mill. The student says he wasn't just scared; he saw covering for his brother as what a good younger brother does, a role-based duty, and he's asked us to consider that as mitagating when we decide his own sanction for lying during the investigation. Half the board thinks this is irrelevant or even an aggravating attempt to excuse dishonesty, and the other half keeps muttering about Confucian role ethics but none of them can say exactly how that should cash out in a sanction. Could you draft the short decision memo I'd circulate? It should recommend a concrete approach to whether and how role-based family obligations should weigh in our integrity procedures, with reasons we can defend to the student and to the university. Make sure the memo actually engages the strongest version of the case for treating family loyalty as irrelevant.
