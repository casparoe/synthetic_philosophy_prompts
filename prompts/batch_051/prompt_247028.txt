I'm on the clinical ethics committee at a mid-size hospital, and we've been keeping an informal list of how we've handled requests for assisted dying over the last few years. We've got a new case coming up and I honestly don't know how we'll decide. Here are our own past decisions, as best I remember them:

- We approved a 71-year-old with pancreatic cancer, maybe three months left, pain that wasn't fully controlled even on high-dose opioids, and a clear, consistent request.
- We approved a 64-year-old with ALS who couldn't swallow anymore and was starting to lose respiratory function. He'd asked repeatedly and was clearly competent.
- We rejected a 48-year-old with MS that had been stable for four years. She said her pain and fatigue made life unbearable, but she hadn't really engaged with the pain clinic, and the committee felt her suffering was more about losing her old life than about untreatable physical symptoms.
- We rejected a 69-year-old with treatment-resistant depression, physically healthy, competent, who said she was just done. The committee drew a hard line at psychiatric illness alone.
- We approved, with a bunch of conditions, a 77-year-old with early Alzheimer's who'd written an advance directive for this when she was first diagnosed. She was still competent enough now and had been consistent about it.
- We rejected a 59-year-old with end-stage COPD, maybe 8 to 12 months, who'd gone back and forth on the request and whose family was fighting about it.

The new case is a 54-year-old woman with locked-in syndrome after a brainstem stroke. She's cognitively intact, fully competent, communicates with eye movements, and has asked for assisted dying consistently for two years. Her condition is stable and not terminal; she could live decades. She says the total loss of movement and dependence is unbearable, and there's no treatment that will restore function.

I'm less interested in the "right" answer than in what we'd have to be committed to. What are the rules that fit our past cases, which precedent is the closest analog, what would each of those rules say about this new case, and what's your best guess about what we'll decide if we want to stay consistent with ourselves? And what further case would cleanly tell the rules apart?
