I am preparing a philosophy of science seminar presentation and would like my current position audited for consistency before I present it. I hold all of the following commitments, which I take to combine the durable insights of Kuhn and Popper:

(1) Scientific revolutions can be rationally warranted transitions: the old paradigm is sometimes abandoned for good epistemic reasons, not merely because the community changes its mind for sociological or psychological causes.

(2) The paradigms on either side of a revolution are locally incommensurable: some central terms shift in meaning, so that certain claims of the old and new theories cannot be directly compared for truth or falsity.

(3) There is no algorithm or set of neutral methodological rules that uniquely determines theory choice, because any such procedure would require a theory-neutral observation language and shared standards of comparison, which do not exist across revolutionary divides.

(4) Nonetheless, theory choice across a revolution is guided by shared cognitive values — empirical accuracy, internal consistency, scope, simplicity, and fruitfulness — and it is the operation of these values that makes paradigm choice rational even though it is not algorithmic.

(5) Popper is right that the core of scientific rationality is the critical attitude: subjecting theories to severe tests and abandoning those that fail, rather than any positive logic of confirmation or induction.

(6) But Popper's account of rational transition by decisive falsification cannot describe revolutionary change, because a decisive refutation requires a stable theory-neutral observation language, and exactly that is unavailable during the revolution.

Do these hang together? Please audit the set: say which commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each tension specify what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. End by identifying the single revision that would do the most to make the whole package coherent.
