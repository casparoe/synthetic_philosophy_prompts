I have been reading Wang Yangming's recorded conversations and letters in the Chuanxilu, and one passage on the unity of knowledge and action has tied me in knots. He gives the examples of knowing cold and knowing pain: you do not first know cold in the abstract and then later undergo cold; the knowing already is a kind of undergoing. From this he concludes, as I understand him, that someone who knows filial piety but does not act filially does not really know filial piety. I cannot quote the exact wording, so I am paraphrasing. That passage seems to license a much stronger argument, which a classmate pushed me to spell out:

(1) In the genuine case, knowing and acting are one; there is no temporal gap between them.
(2) So if someone genuinely knows that x is right, their action already is x; if someone genuinely knows that x is wrong, their refraining from x is already part of that knowing.
(3) Therefore, whenever someone fails to do what they say they know to be right, they must not have genuinely known it.
(4) But people do report knowing, with full sincerity and sometimes with evident distress, that something is right while not doing it—for example, knowing that a donation would save lives and yet not donating.
(5) If Wang is correct, such reports are self-deceiving or misdescribing something else, and moral akrasia is impossible.
(6) That conclusion seems too strong: it makes an ordinary guilty conscience in full awareness incoherent, and it turns moral education into a matter of transmitting information, with practice, habituation, and emotional transformation becoming at best incidental.

Where does this argument go wrong? I want to know which numbered step I should reject, and what rejecting it costs: does it force us to weaken the unity-of-knowledge-and-action doctrine into something less radical, and if so, which part of Wang Yangming's view survives? Please also separate what is settled in the reading of this material from what is genuinely contested among scholars of Wang Yangming and Chinese philosophy, and do not present one contested interpretation as the consensus.
