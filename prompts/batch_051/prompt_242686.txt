I am currently taking a philosophy of art course, and we have spent several weeks on attempts to define art. The definition I find most compelling, and most troubling, is the institutional theory: an artwork is not something with a distinctive intrinsic or perceptual property, but an artifact that has been put forward for appreciation by a person or persons acting within the artworld — the loose network of artists, curators, critics, and audiences. The theory earned its keep by handling readymades, where a mass-produced object becomes art because the artworld takes it up as something to be appreciated, not because of anything about the object itself.

What I want to know is whether this theory survives a development that postdates it. Text-to-image generative models can now produce an effectively unbounded stream of images. Consider a pipeline in which a model generates images, an automated system posts them to an online gallery or marketplace, and they are displayed and sold without any human having selected, curated, or even viewed the particular images. My intuition is that these images are not artworks, or at least not all of them, but I cannot locate which premise of the institutional theory excludes them. The artifact condition seems satisfied, since the images are made things, and the artworld seems to be treating them as candidates for appreciation, so the theory appears to classify an arbitrarily large number of unseen, unselected outputs as art. Perhaps the real pressure falls on a hidden premise about human intention or agency that the theory never stated, or on what counts as conferral when the conferring is automated.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a survey. Does the institutional theory of art survive this case unchanged, does it need a repaired version that states the agency or conferral condition more carefully, or is the case fatal to it? Identify exactly which premise the automated pipeline bears on, and say whether the theory can be restated so that it handles both readymades and automated images without overgenerating. End with a line beginning "Verdict:" followed by one of "survives unchanged", "survives with repair", or "dead", and a short phrase naming the premise that decides the case.
