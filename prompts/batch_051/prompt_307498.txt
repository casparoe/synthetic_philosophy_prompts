People are always saying that Millennials killed this or Gen Z is like that, but the one that still nags at me is the whole "OK boomer" thing from 2019. Here's the episode as I understand it: the catchphrase blew up online, mostly as a way for teenagers and twenty-somethings to wave off older people as out of touch; at one point a New Zealand MP in her mid-twenties was interrupted by an older MP during a speech on a climate bill and just answered "OK boomer"; and the media spent a few weeks treating it as proof of a generational war. One odd detail I want you to actually sit with: lots of the people being told "OK boomer" were not literal baby boomers. The heckler in the New Zealand parliament was Gen X.

I want you too use this episode as a case study for the question of whether generations—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, the whole lot—are real kinds. Start by laying out the main positions carefully, something like: (1) a generation is a real kind, a group whose members share formative historical conditions or experiences that really do shape them in common ways; (2) a generation is just a conventional label, an arbitrary date range that marketers and journalists use and the rest of us inherit without it cutting nature at the joints; (3) a generation is a looping or self-fulfilling category, where the label starts out sloppy but eventually causes the similarity it claims to describe because enough people treat it as real.

Then run the OK boomer episode through each of those views. What would each view predict or explain well, and which features of the episode does each one handle badly? In particular, how much explanatory work is the "real kind" picture really doing once you notice that "boomer" functioned more like "older person I find condescending" than like an actual birth cohort? Does the backlash—older people calling it ageist, younger people calling it justified pushback—make the generational categories more real over time, or just more grabby? End by saying what the episode should make us think about the real-kind debate as a whole: does it tilt you toward one of the three views, or toward some mixed position? Defend that verdict.
