Been mulling over something about how courts do sentencing and I cant get it to line up. Where I live (England), the same court that says a sentence should reflect how serious the offence was will knock a decent chunk off if you plead guilty early, and give you less of a discount the closer to trial you leave it. So two people who did the same thing, same harm, same record, can end up with noticeably different sentences, with the gap mostly coming down to how much bother they saved the system. People say the discount is there because an early plea shows remorse and spares victims from having to give evidence, but everyone knows plenty of defendants plead guilty just because the deal is on the table, not because they feel bad. So what does that practice actually assume about what punishment is for? Is the system still working on the idea that the sentence should match what someone deserves, or has it quietly switched to treating the sentence like a price you can negotiate to keep the machine running? And if it really believed the deserve-their-punishment story, what would have to change about how guilty pleas are handled — would the discount have to go entirely, or could you keep a bit of it for the ones who genuinely show remorse without the whole thing becoming a fiddle? Answer in British English please.
