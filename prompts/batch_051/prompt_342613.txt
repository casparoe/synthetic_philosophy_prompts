I am a novelist, and the manuscript I am drafting turns on the correspondence between two characters: a Humean about laws of nature, who holds that laws are just an efficient summary of the totality of particular facts, and an anti-Humean, who holds that laws are something over and above the facts and in some sense govern them. They are forced to produce a joint statement for a conference, and I need that document to be the real thing rather than a caricature of either side. Act as a professional philosopher of science and metaphysics. Think carefully, and begin with some brainstorming about what each position genuinely commits itself to and where their dispute is merely verbal. Then draft the joint statement with these elements: a numbered set of non-vacuous claims both can sign; a precise formulation of each doctrine in wording its own adherents would accept; the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — including, in particular, whether laws explain their instances and how a law is distinguished from an accident; and, for each crux, a case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Identify any part of the dispute you judge to be purely verbal. Omit footnotes and reading recommendations. Please answer in British English.
