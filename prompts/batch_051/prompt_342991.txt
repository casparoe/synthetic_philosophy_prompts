Me and a friend just spent an hour arguing about whether 'this glass is fragile' is a testable claim or just talk. I said it's testable: it means the glass would break if you knocked it off the counter, so knock it off and look. He said no, because the phrase hides a bunch of normal-conditions baggage. A fragile thing can fail to break when dropped — wrap it in bubble wrap (a mask: the disposition is there but can't manifest), or rig a gadget that hardens it a split second before impact (a fink: the disposition gets removed exactly when it's triggered). And a non-fragile thing can break when dropped if something makes it brittle just before it lands. So no single outcome settles whether the disposition was really there. I conceeded the sentence carries more than I'd admitted, but I still think it has empirical content.

What I want: take the claim 'this glass is fragile,' understood as something like 'it would break if struck under normal conditions,' and design a concrete test of it. Say what you'd manipulate and hold fixed, and which outcomes count for and against fragility. Then say what any positive result would and wouldn't show: does it confirm the conditional claim itself, or only that it broke this one time plus some uniformity assumptions? And is there any outcome that should make someone give up 'the glass is fragile' outright, or does the notion have enough slack to absorb every failed prediction as a fink or a mask and become unfalsifiable? Be honest about the residue no test can reach.
