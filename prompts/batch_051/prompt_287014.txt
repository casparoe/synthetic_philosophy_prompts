I'm putting together a session on Pascal's mugging for my decision theory grad seminar, and I'd like a parody piece to open with. Could you write me a deadpan direct-mail fundraising appeal, maybe 600 to 700 words, from a charity that has fully accepted the mugging and is trying to raise funds by exploiting it? The letter should be a sustained reductio of the idea that vanishingly small probabilities of astronomically large utilities should dominate our choices. The jokes should come from getting the decision theory right rather than from caricature: set up the threat so that, on an unbounded utility function, the expected disutility of not donating works out to be enormous; then have the letter keep bumping into the real escape routes — the need to bound utility somewhere, the arbitrariness of whichever bound you pick, the worry that a rival charity can always announce twice as many victims at half the probability, and the awkward fact that ignoring small probabilities leaves you unable to say where the threshold is. A line from a 'concerned' donor who has adopted a logarithmic utility function would be lovely. Keep the voice believably charity-sector and the irony legible, and write in British English please.
