I teach philosophy of biology, and for the past few years I've been running a reading group on the question of whether DNA really is a code, or whether "genetic information" is just a metaphor we forgot is a metaphor. The group is about half molecular biologists and half philosophers and historians, and I've watched us loop through the same four or five moves so often that I could play either side from memory.

The literalists say: the genetic code maps nucleotide triplets to amino acids in a systematic, causal way; copying errors propagate across generations; that's no looser than any engineering use of "code." The metaphor people say: where's the sender, where's the receiver, where's the convention or the intention? A sequence of nucleotides doesn't mean anything the way a sentence means something; we borrowed "code" and "information" from human communication and then quietly dropped the scare quotes.

Here's the thing that puzzles me. Nobody disputes any of the molecular facts — transcription, translation, codon tables, mutation — and nobody disputes that information talk has been wildly productive in biology. The fight is entirely over what it takes for something to be a code in the first place. But every time I try to get the group to fix that question, each side accuses the other of slippage. I think what's happening is a slide between information in a causal or correlational sense (A is informative about B, the way smoke is informative about fire) and information in a semantic sense (about something, capable of being right or wrong). My worry is that even after you seperate those two, there's a leftover substantive dispute about whether a code needs a history of selection, or an interpreter, or neither.

I want a diagnosis of the dissagreement itself, not a verdict on who's right about DNA, and not a survey of the positions. Is this verbal, metaphysical, about the explanatory role of "code" talk, or what? And on your diagnosis, what would count as actual progress — a distinction, an experiment, a reformulated question? I'd like something concrete enough that I could take it back to the group and we'd know whether we'd moved.
