I'm taking a seminar on political language, and for the final I have to write a short "where I stand" paper on George Orwell's "Politics and the English Language." I've got a list of things I believe, but I can't tell if they actually fit together. The passage I keep coming back to is this: "As soon as certain topics are raised, the concrete melts into the abstract and no one seems able to think of turns of speech that are not hackneyed: prose consists less and less of words chosen for the sake of their meaning, and more and more of phrases tacked together like the sections of a prefabricated hen-house."

Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I'm basically with Orwell that ready-made phrases in political speech are a real harm, not just an aesthetic one—they let the speaker and the audience skip the step of checking whether the words hook onto anything definite. (2) I also think a lot of what he'd call dying metaphors—"stand shoulder to shoulder with," "level playing field"—do genuine work for ordinary people in public debate, and trying to purge them would just make politics a contest of who can phrase things freshly, which seems elitist. (3) I think technical language in politics is fine when it buys precision—"reproduction number," "adverse selection," "confidence interval"—and isn't just Orwell's pretentious diction. (4) I think democratic legitimacy requires that the main reasons for any major policy be statable in plain language to a reasonable, attentive adult. (5) But I also think that in practice, when experts try to translate those reasons into plain language, they fall back on exactly the kind of prefabricated, evasive prose Orwell is descibing—the plain-language summary is often the hen-house. (6) And I don't think the fix is to demand better plain-language translations, because some precision genuinely doesn't survive translation, and insisting on it just manufactures false clarity.

Can someone police this set for me? I want to know which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it, ordered by how bad the tension is, and for each real tension what the cheapest fix would be. And if you had to make one revision to get the whole set to hang together, which one should it be and what would it cost me?
