In my American history class we read the portion of Benjamin Franklin's autobiography in which he describes his attempt to make himself morally perfect. He chose thirteen virtues, gave each a short rule for himself, and kept a little book with a chart on which he recorded his faults for each virtue, concentrating on one virtue each week until he had cycled through all thirteen. He is also candid that the method did not work as well as he hoped: he kept sliding back, and he remarks that pride in particular kept returning, which was especially annoying to him because he had added humility to the list only after a friend suggested he seemed proud.

Because we were asked to try a shortened version of the chart ourselves, I ended up running his exercise next to an app I have used for several years. The app tracks habits with streaks, badges, weekly summaries, reminders, and the option to share progress with friends. The comparison has left me wondering what Franklin would actually think of present-day habit-tracking and self-improvement culture, which often treats him as a kind of ancestor. I do not want an answer that simply says he would approve because both use charts, since his method was driven by a specific moral inventory and by his own reflections on why each virtue mattered, whereas the app mainly rewards consistency itself. I would like you to reason from his stated views and from the details of his method to a careful prediction: would Franklin adopt something like a habit-tracking app for a modern version of his project, would he recommend it to a student like me, and where would he object to or modify the way such apps and the surrounding self-improvement culture work? Finally, please say how confident you are in this prediction as a whole, and single out the point you are least sure about, since any answer about what a long-dead person would think is partly guesswork.
