A friend and I spent most of yesterday evening arguing about Being John Malkovich and whether its portal into Malkovich's head is a good image for what Metzinger calls phenomenal transparency. By that I mean the idea that the conscious self is a transparent self-model: the organism models itself as a whole, with a first-person point of view and a felt sense that this body and these thoughts are mine, and the model is transparent because we do not experience it as a model—we simply experience ourselves as directly present. I argued that the film works as an intuition pump for transparency and its dissociation: Craig occupies Malkovich's point of view without being Malkovich, pulling apart the perspective from the bodily ownership and agency that usually come with it. My friend replied that the portal smuggles back exactly the Cartesian spectator—a little man behind the eyes who could in principle be swapped for another—that Metzinger's theory is meant to dissolve. We could not settle which of us the film supports.

I would like a reading of the film through the self-model theory that tells us where each of us is right and where the film itself is confused: where the portal genuinely clarifies phenomenal transparency, mineness, and the ownership and control of a body, and where it quietly reinstates the homunculus the theory denies. Stay close to the film's details—the fifteen-minute ejection, Craig's initial passivity and later control of Malkovich, Malkovich entering his own portal—and do not reduce the film to an illustration of the theory. Conclude by saying what the film makes vivid about phenomenal transparency that the theory's own examples do not.
