I'm a high school student writing a short paper for an independent study in the philosophy of artificial intelligence. The paper argues that Coherent Extrapolated Volition (CEV) — roughly, the proposal that a beneficial AI should pursue not what we currently say we want but what we would want after a process of idealization: being substantially better informed, thinking more clearly, becoming more the sort of people we would want to be, and having our preferences formed into a shared, coherent whole — is too underspecified and too contested to serve as a target for AI alignment. I'm compiling a list of objections to CEV as an alignment target. The objections I already have are:

- Operationalization: no one can say how to turn the notion into a procedure an AI could actually follow, even in principle.
- Non-uniqueness: different choices about what counts as "better informed," "more the sort of people we would want to be," or "coherent" yield different endpoints, and nothing in the proposal selects among them.
- Identity loss: idealizing away our flaws and limitations may remove exactly the features that make a set of preferences ours in the first place.
- Convergence assumption: the proposal assumes our idealized preferences would converge, but they may remain conflicting or become more conflicting once ignorance is removed.
- Realism assumption: the proposal presupposes that there is a determinate fact about what our extrapolated volition would be, which a constructivist or antirealist about value can reject.
- Reference class: it is arbitrary where the boundary falls — which humans, whether future people count, and whether nonhuman animals are included — yet the choice matters greatly.

I want one more objection that is not on this list and not a variant of anything on it — not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of items already listed. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good and where it is weakest.

Take the role of a professional philosopher who works on preference aggregation and AI alignment. Before answering, brainstorm at least three candidate objections in your head; then report only the strongest one that satisfies the constraint above.

Return your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- objection_name: a short label for the objection, at most eight words.
- objection: a one-paragraph statement of the objection, written in the first person as though it were a paragraph in my paper.
