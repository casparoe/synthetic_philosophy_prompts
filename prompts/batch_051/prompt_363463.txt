For a draft section arguing that the index of risk attitudes in risk-weighted expected utility (REU) is not merely a redescription of the utility curvature that expected utility (EU) theory already allows, I need a worked case built to order. Construct a decision problem in which EU theory and REU, in Buchak's framework, issue opposite verdicts about the same pair of monetary gambles, with this direction: EU prefers the lower-probability, larger-payoff gamble, while REU prefers the higher-probability, smaller-payoff gamble. Constraints: ordinary, low-stakes dollar payoffs; no probabilities below 0.1 and no certain outcomes; both theories use the same Bernoulli utility function u(x) = sqrt(x); REU uses the probability-weighting function r(p) = p^2, so r(p) < p on (0,1), underweighting the probability of the better outcome; and the reversal must be produced entirely by the interaction of r with u, not by giving the two theories different utilities, by making u risk-seeking, or by relying on near-certainty or infinitesimal-probability effects. After presenting the case, compute each theory's verdict explicitly and explain why each gives the verdict it does. Then isolate the feature of the case that does the work, say what happens to the reversal if that feature is weakened or removed, and give the strongest reply an EU theorist would make to the claim that this case exposes a comparative defect in EU.
