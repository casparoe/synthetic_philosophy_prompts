I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I've developed a rule when I read biology or medical papers, and I want to know if it's defensible or just a bias I've dressed up as hygiene. My rule is: whenever someone says "the function of X is Z," I automatically rewrite it in my head as "X does Z, and Z happens to help the organism." I don't let the word "function" do any extra work unless the author gives evidence that X was actually selected because it did Z. For example, when Harvey writes in On the Motion of the Heart and Blood:

"The chief function of the heart is the transmission and pumping of the blood through the arteries to the extremities of the body."

I treat that as meaning nothing more than "hearts pump blood through the arteries to the extremities, and pumping blood is beneficial to the animal." I thought this was just scientific hygiene, but a friend said I'm quietly taking sides in a philosophy debate between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function without knowing the arguments. He says my rewrite can't handle cases where a heart pumps blood but was never selected for pumping, or where a trait does something helpful but that helpful effect played no role in its evolution. I dont actually know what those accounts are. Is my rule defensible? If not, is there a repaired version that keeps the benefits without the costs? Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
