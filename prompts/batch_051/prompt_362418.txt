Recently I took over a project that will allocate scarce medical equipment, and the team keeps saying the allocation module performs ethical reasoning. I need to decide whether that description is defensible. Please give me a working definition of ethical reasoning, or if the phrase does two jobs, one describing a process and one endorsing it, two definitions with a note on which cases move between them. Run the definition against these cases: a physician explicitly weighing whether to deceive a patient; a rules engine applying a written triage protocol; a language model instructed to justify a decision from a stated moral principle; a reinforcement-learning agent whose reward model was learned from human preference judgments and which, in an unanticipated case, chooses an action no one on the team can defend; a self-driving car executing a fixed utilitarian crash-optimization rule; and a calculator computing expected utilities from numbers supplied to it. Where the definition gives what you take to be the wrong answer or no answer, revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Finally, say what we would need to establish about our own system before it would be accurate rather than misleading to call its behavior ethical reasoning.
