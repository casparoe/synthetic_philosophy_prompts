I got roped into a law-reform working group and there's an asymmetry I can't stop turning over. In our state, sex between consenting adult siblings is a crime, but adultery isn't even a civil penalty, even though both are private, consensual acts that a big chunk of the public calls a serious moral wrong and both get defended with "it's thier business, not the law's." Devlin's argument in The Enforcement of Morals, very roughly, was that a society's shared morality is what holds it together, so the law may enforce that morality just as it may suppress subversive activity. But that seems to explain why both should be crimes, not why we pick one. Hart's reply that law should prevent harm to others, not enforce positive morality, doesn't seem to single out incest either, unless we stack the harm story. So: what's the best non-ad-hoc principle that criminalizes incest but leaves adultery alone? And if the honest answer is that the asymmetry is just disgust plus habit, should we keep it on Devlin-type grounds that the criminal law should track a community's felt judgments, or give it up?
