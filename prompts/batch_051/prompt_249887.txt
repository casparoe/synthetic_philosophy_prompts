Imagine that, starting tomorrow, every human over roughly age five looses the ability to produce or recover conversational implicatures. People still parse sentences literally and get conventional meanings, but anything Grice would call a conversational implicature—irony, metaphor, scalar implicatures like “some” suggesting “not all,” indirect refusals, answers that are only relevant by implication—just doesn’t compute for either speakers or hearers. Memories and institutions stay the same. Walk me through your reasoned prediction for the next twenty years in four domains: law, politics, advertising, and close personal relationships. Use Grice’s distinction between what is said and what is implicated as your framework. Keep your reasoning visible: say which effects you think are robust because they follow from the loss itself, which effects depend on how fast people adapt and make their speech explicit, and what would have to be true for your prediction to be wrong. Also tell me whether any group is better off, and if so why.
