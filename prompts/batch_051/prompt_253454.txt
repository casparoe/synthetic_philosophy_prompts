I’m trying to get clear on what I actually believe about gratitude — not the etiquitte question of when to say thanks, but when gratitude is genuinely owed, and to whom. Going by my gut on cases:

A stranger shoves me out of the path of an oncoming lorry, badly hurting himself. I’d never met him and he didn’t ask first. I obviously owe this man a deep, ongoing gratitude — arguably as much as I owe anyone.

My friend throws me a surprise birthday party even though I’d told her the month before that I hate parties and didn’t want one. She spends a lot of money and effort; the evening is miserable for me. I don’t think I owe her gratitude for the party itself, since I didn’t recieve a benefit from it. I owe her some acknowledgement, maybe, and I can appreciate the intention, but gratitude for a benefit feels wrong.

A wealthy man I never met funds my entire degree anonymously. I later find out he did it mainly for the tax write-off and because his advisor said philanthropy would make him look good. I still think I owe him gratitude — warmer than I’d like, less warm than the lorry case, but real.

A colleague spends hours debugging my code so the launch succeeds and he gets credit for saving the team; he’d have watched me fail if it weren’t tied to his own reputation. I feel roughly no gratitude owed, just a reciprocal professional favour, and even that only sort of.

The donor and the colleague feel completely different to me, though I can’t offhand say why — in both, the person’s motives are mostly about themselves, and in both the benefit to me is real.

My neighbour, while trimming his own tree, accidentally makes the branches block a view from my kitchen window that I’d hated for years. He has no idea he’s done it. I don’t owe him gratitude; I’m pleased, but there’s nothing like a debt.

My great-grandfather worked himself into an early grave so his kids could get out of the pit, and I now enjoy the results of that. I feel something quite like gratitude toward him, but “owed” seems off — he’s gone, I can’t thank him, and the benefit passes down in a way that dilutes it. Still, calling it nothing seems wrong.

A friend, meaning to help, misreads a map and sends me the “wrong” way on a long drive. By pure luck the other road — the one he should have sent me down — is closed that day, so his mistake saves me hours. I don’t think I owe him gratitude, though I might text him a joking thanks.

The case I keep going back and forth on: my stepfather paid for my education and was genuinely warm to me, but I now think he did it partly to keep my mother from leaving, and he spent years quietly undermining me in private. Do I owe him gratitude?

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? I want it stated precisely, with the case or cases behind each clause, and an honest list of which of my gut verdicts it can’t accommodate and which it would overturn. And what does the winning account say about a case I haven’t listed: someone who saves your life while trying to kill you, and only fails because they’re incompetent?
