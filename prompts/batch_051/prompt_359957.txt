Ive been going back through the grue literature after a back-and-forth in an old LessWrong comment section about whether you can get induction out of maximum entropy priors. The thing that bugs me is that Goodman's new riddle seems to show any purely formal criterion over evidence alone undershoots: the data support green and grue equally until we add something about the predicates themselves. But every time I try to state what that something is, it feels like smuggling in a preference for our current vocabulary, and I want to see whether there's a move I haven't found.

The answers I already have on my list are: (1) green is simpler, more natural, or less disjunctive than grue; (2) Quine-style entrenchment, where we project green because green has been successfully projected in the past; (3) Jackson-style counterfactual robustness, where green things would still be green if we changed the time index but grue things wouldn't; and (4) invariance under interventions or symmetry constraints, the kind of thing Sober pushes. Each one seems to me to run into either circularity or an indexicality problem, but maybe I'm missing a different strategy.

Give me another solution to the new riddle that is not on my list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of those four. It can be a dissolution, a constructive proposal, or an argument that the problem is pseudo. Then tell me honestly whether it handles the grue-with-a-different-time-index case and whether it's actually any good.
