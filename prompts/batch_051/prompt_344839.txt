For a theology seminar I have been asked to write down my commitments about how to distinguish transcultural commands in Scripture from culturally particular ones, and I would like a consistency check on the set before I present it. I hold:

(1) Scripture is authoritative for Christian faith and practice, and part of faithful reading is sorting its commands into those that bind all Christians at all times and those that addressed particular situations.

(2) The Old Testament dietary and purity laws were binding on Israel but are not binding on Gentile Christians, because in Christ the purity system has fulfilled its purpose and been set aside. This is not because those laws were misguided, but because they belonged to a stage in the history of redemption.

(3) The New Testament passages instructing slaves to obey their masters are a pastoral accommodation to the social structures of the Roman world, not a timeless endorsement of slavery; one can hold Scripture to be authoritative and still condemn slavery as unjust.

(4) Paul's instruction that women cover their heads when praying or prophesying is culturally particular to first-century Corinth and is not binding today.

(5) I want these distinctions to be principled rather than made case by case on the basis of contemporary moral sentiment. I therefore want an explicit hermeneutical criterion, or a small set of criteria, that classifies commands as culturally particular or transcultural and that yields (2) through (4) without licensing me to relativize the rest of the New Testament's moral teaching.

(6) I am disposed to treat a command as transcultural when its own rationale in the text is transcultural, and as culturally particular when its rationale is tied to local custom, honor, shame, or temporary circumstances.

I would like a careful audit of this set. Which commitments are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each real tension? For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be, and then say which single revision would make the whole set most coherent while keeping it recognizably the same.
