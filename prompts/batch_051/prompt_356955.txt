Every time my reading group tries to settle whether LLMs are agents or simulators, the conversation dies in a fight over definitions, so I want to step back and work out what any adequate answer would have to get right before I commit to one. Give me the desiderata for a theory of what an LLM is — agent, simulator, tool, role-player, or some hybrid — not a scorecard of existing positions. For each requirement, say which case, observation, or distinction forces it. The ones I can already half-see are: (1) the split between what training does and what inference does, since that's where the simulator people put all the weight; (2) the fact that a chat model can be told mid-conversation to drop one goal and adopt another, and it just goes along, which seems different from a standard agent; (3) role-play — a model can produce a first-person narrative without there being a narrator behind it, and I don't know whether that's a decisive point or a red herring; (4) whether scaffolding a model with memory, planning, tools, and a loop changes what the system is or just what it can do; (5) what RLHF is doing when it turns a base model into an assistant, since that's where people start using the language of 'goals.' Treat those as a starting point, not a constraint: I want the full list, including anything I've missed. For each requirement, mark whether it's a non-negotiable adequacy condition or just a camp-specific demand. And the two things I most want: which single criterion does the most work in excluding one of the popular positions, and is there any pair of requirements that can't be jointly satisfied? That last part matters to me more than a summary of who thinks what.
