A colleague of mine is convinced that inference to the best explanation is a reliable route to truth: we're justified in believing the explanation that best balances simplicity, fit with the data, and explanatory scope, because that's the one most likely to be right. I keep going back and forth on whether this is an empirical claim we could actually check or just a dressed-up way of saying "the explanation I find most appealing is the one I'll believe." It seems like the only thing you could test is something weaker, like whether scientists' favourite explantions later get confirmed more often than their rivals — but that wouldn't show the loveliness itself is doing any epistemic work, would it? What's the strongest test of the claim, and what exactly would a positive result settle and not settle?
