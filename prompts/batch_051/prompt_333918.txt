I run a small secondhand book stall. For a few months, a regular customer has been offering me the same kind of exchange: I hand over whichever one of three books I currently own, pay a small fee, and receive one of the other two. Each swap has felt like a genuine improvement. When I hold the large art monograph, the long literary novel seems like the book I actually want, so I pay the fee and switch. When I hold the novel, it sits unread and the compact field guide starts to seem more honest and useful, so I pay the fee and switch. When I hold the guide, it feels too narrow and the monograph seems like the richer book I should have kept, so I pay the fee and switch back. I have now returned to the monograph, down three small payments, owning nothing I did not start with.

Each trade satisfied my preferences at the time I made it, and I cannot point to the single choice that was wrong. My partner says the whole pattern is irrational, but she cannot identify the mistaken trade either. I am unsure how to think about this. The worry is not that I was tricked into wanting a bad book, but that my preferences themselves seem to shift depending on which book I currently have. Am I being irrational? If no individual trade was a mistake, where exactly does the irrationality reside: in the preferences themselves, in my one-at-a-time rule for deciding, or in the assumption that my choices across time should add up as though one agent made them?
