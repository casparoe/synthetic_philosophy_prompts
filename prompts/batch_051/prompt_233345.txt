People sometimes cite the Löbian obstacle as a reason a self-modifying agent can't be made to trust its own modifications: to prove that its successor would only prove true things, the agent would basically have to prove it's own soundness, and the Gödel/Löb results say a consistent formal system can't do that. But that argument was built for an agent whose cognition is basically a proof system that reasons about itself and its successor. Today's frontier systems don't prove anything at all: they're trained networks with no explicit self-model, and when they "check" their own output it's a generated plausibility review, not a soundness proof. Does the Löbian obstacle survive that shift, or does it die? I want to know which premise of the original argument actually breaks, and whether there's a repaired version that still says something weaker but real about systems that train on their own outputs or self-critiques.
