I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether the current Stoic revival has kept the hard center of the ancient ethics or has quietly softened it into a set of resilience and productivity techniques with a Hellenistic veneer. The argument moved through Epictetus on control, the passions, whether a Stoic can be politically active, and how much of the old physics a modern Stoic is allowed to set aside. By the end I had defended a fairly strict reading of the ancient position, but this morning I am not at all sure the position I defended is a coherent whole rather than several independently plausible commitments standing too close together.

The commitments I put forward, more or less as I stated them, are these. (a) The dichotomy of control is exhaustive: the only things genuinely up to me are my judgments, impulses, desires, and aversions, as Epictetus says at the start of the Handbook, and my body, health, property, reputation, career, and the welfare of other people are not up to me. The discipline of desire consists in refusing to stake my peace on anything in the second list. (b) The ancient theory of the passions is not something to renegotiate. Anger and grief are not healthy emotions to be processed and expressed; they are false evaluative judgments, and the correct aim of practice is to approach apatheia, not to keep the passions in moderate shape. (c) Stoicism is not quietist. Justice obliges a person to engage politically—to organize, advocate, protect the vulnerable, and try to make institutions fairer—even though the outcome of that engagement is uncertain and mostly not up to me. (d) The preferred indifferents really are to be preferred: my health, the lives and flourishing of the people I love, and a tolerably just social order are things I should actively pursue, for myself and for others, under the reserve clause, without treating them as genuinely good or bad. (e) The revival is right to detach the ethics from the ancient physics: I do not accept the Stoic account of a providentially ordered cosmos or its universal determinism, and I do not think the dichotomy of control needs that metaphysical backing in order to do its therapeutic work.

I would like an audit of this set rather than a restatement of it. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs actually pull against one another, which pairs only look as though they do and why, and how severe each real tension is. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating every conflict as equally serious, and for each give the cheapest plausible fix and what it would cost me. Finally, tell me which single revision would buy the most coherence and what it would force me to abandon. I am more interested in learning where the position is overextended than in having it confirmed.
