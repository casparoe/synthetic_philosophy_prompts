I keep getting stuck on a mismatch in how people talk about social categories. Ask most people I know whether someone is a woman, and they'll say what settles it is how the person identifies — if she says she's a woman, that's that, and insisting she isn't is treated as a serious wrong. But ask the same people whether someone is Black or Latino, and suddenly self-identification isn't enough: a person who "identifies as" Black without any Black ancestry, and whom nobody treats as Black, gets accused of faking it, and almost nobody thinks their say-so settles the matter. Both gender and race are supposed to be socially constructed rather than biological facts. So why does one kind get to be fixed by the individual's own word while the other dosn't? Is there a real difference between the two kinds that justifies the asymmetry, or is one of the pair being handled inconsistently? I'd rather have a definite verdict than a survey of options: which side would you change, and on what grounds?
