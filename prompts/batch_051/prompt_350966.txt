I'm taking a course on speech acts and performative utterances, and I keep coming back to a question that feels a little dumb at first but gets harder the more I think about it: what actually is an apology? We've covered some Austin and Searle, so I get the idea that some utterances do things just by being said under the right conditions, like christening a ship or pronouncing a couple married. If apologizing is that kind of performative, then maybe the performance itself is the apology, and whether it's sincere is a seperate question about whether it was a good apology rather than whether an apology happened at all. But that feels wrong to me too: a kid forced to say "sorry" to his brother hasn't really apologized, has he?

Here are the cases that are messing me up. (1) A stranger says "I'm sorry" after bumping into me on the bus — clear yes, I'd have said. (2) A friend at a funeral says "I'm sorry for your loss" — same two words, but that's sympathy, not an apology, so a clear no. (3) A friend I confronted says "I'm sorry you feel that way" — this one feels like a no dressed up as a yes. (4) A mother makes her seven-year-old say "sorry" to his brother after hitting him, and the kid says it with zero remorse. (5) A company puts out a statement after a data breach saying it "apologizes to anyone who may have been affected." (6) An actor says "I apologize" in character on stage. (7) I write a letter to my late grandmother apologizing for something I did years ago; she can't read it, and I know that when I write it. (8) A friend spills my drink and says "my bad."

Could you give me a working definition of "apology" that gets these cases right, or at least tells me which of my confident classifications the definition actually forces me to revise? Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should be the definition. Items 2 through 5 should run the definition against the cases, clustering them however makes sense, and item 5 should also name the classification you're least confident in.
