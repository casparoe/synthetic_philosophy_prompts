I've been going back and forth on what I've been calling, half-seriously, experiential finitism: the idea that any mind with finite memory, finite discriminative powers, and a finite lifespan can have at most finitely many distinct total experiences, so that in principle its experience could be used up. My gut responses to cases keep tugging against each other, and I want to know what principle, if any, is actually behind them.

Here's where I land, case by case. A sixty-second loop of white noise on repeat: I've heard it all after maybe ten minutes, and every further listen is just more of the same—exhaustible. A twenty-second recording of my mum laughing: I've listened hundreds of times over the years, and every few months I catch something new in it, a little break or a held breathe; it's just as bounded a sound as the noise loop, yet it feels not exhaustible in the same way. Chess: finitely many board positions, so a strict count says exhaustible, but in thirty years of playing I've never once felt I'd seen the position before—practically inexhaustible, though I'd never say infinite in principle. The same photograph on my desk: after two weeks I stop seeing it, exhaustible, almost trivially. A lava lamp: nothing but slow deterministic fluid flow, and my visual system can almost certainly only discriminate finitely many snapshots, yet I feel I could watch forever and never finish it—inexhaustible in practice, though maybe not in principle. Two arrangements of a deck of cards that differ only by swapping two cards I can't tell apart: those are the same experience for me, so distinct experiences track my powers of discrimination, not facts about the stimulus—which pushes me toward finitism. Taste in wine: my palate sharpens for a while, but there has to be a floor set by my taste receptors, so finitely many possible taste experiences—exhaustible.

I can't tell whether there's a single principle that gets all of these, or close, or whether 'experiential finitism' is just too blunt to be doing any real work. Is there a principle that captures the pattern? What does it say about the case I keep circling: a person with locked-in syndrome whose only changing input is a slowly drifting coloured light—could their experience remain genuinely novel indefinitely, or must it eventually flatten into pure repetition? And wich of my verdicts, if any, does the best principle force me to give up?
