I want to work on the paradox of hedonism, but as a problem in value theory rather than as practical advice about how to be happy. My half-formed idea is this: the standard reply to the paradox is that hedonism can remain true while withdrawing from deliberation — pleasure is the only thing good for a person, but it is best secured by aiming at other things. If the sole good cannot be aimed at directly without undermining itself, what work is the claim that pleasure is the good doing in guiding a person's deliberations?
