I'm a retired biophysicist, and I've spent the last year going down a rabbit hole in philosophy of mind. The thing I keep coming back to is Dennett's two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn. As I remember the setup: both are veteran professional tasters, and both report that the house blend now tastes worse to them than it did years ago. Chase says the experience itself changed for him — his taste apparatus drifted, so the blend now presents a different taste. Sanborn says the experience is precisely the same as it ever was; what changed is him, his standards or liking, so he now rejects the same taste he used to accept. Behaviorally the two are indistinguishable, the "which one is right?" question looks unanswerable from the inside, and Dennett uses that to suggest that "qualia" may be an incoherant posit — a distinction without a difference.

I'd like you to draft the document that a good-faith adversarial collaboration would produce between (a) a qualia realist, who holds that there are intrinsic, introspectively available, non-representational features of experience and that Chase and Sanborn are reporting a genuine inner difference even if neither can articulate it, and (b) an eliminativist/quiner in Dennett's line, who thinks the posit should be dropped rather than reduced.

Format it as:
1. A numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign. Make these non-vacuous: the behavioral facts about Chase and Sanborn, facts about verbal report and discrimination performance, and whatever precise limits on introspection both sides accept.
2. A disagreements table, with each camp's view worded so that camp itself would accept the wording.
3. Cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case, test, or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that test will show.

At least one crux should be Chase-and-Sanborn itself, but don't just restate Dennett; I want the realist's best reply and the quiner's use of the case made exact. You can also invent variants around the coffee tasters — a third taster, a forced-choice discrimination training program, a taster who recalibrates and then reports the blend tastes like it did at the start — but every variant must be one both camps agree, before seeing the outcome, bears on the crux. Keep it to what they'd actually sign, not a literature review.
