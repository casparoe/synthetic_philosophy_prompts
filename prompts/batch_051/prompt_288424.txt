My family has recently had to divide scarce care resources between two relatives whose needs differ in kind — one is in chronic pain, the other is losing the ability to live independently — and I keep hitting the question of whether there is any fact of the matter about how much a given intervention improves one person's wellbeing relative to the other's. I would like to know how one could test the claim that interpersonal comparisons of utility are measurements of something real rather than conventions or projections imposed by the person doing the comparing. Please design a concrete empirical program — experiments, observations, or measurement procedures — that bears on the claim. For the central test, state which result would favour the measurement reading and which would favour the conventionalist reading, and then say exactly what residue no test of this kind can reach.
