Suppose that in 2030 a mid-sized open economy reforms its labor law so that, whenever collective-bargaining negotiations at firms above 200 employees reach an impasse, a state arbitrator must impose the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution rather than the existing backstop, under which arbitrators tend to split the difference between the parties' final positions. The imposed rule is defined as follows. Take the no-agreement outcome as the disagreement point. For each side, compute the best payoff it could obtain anywhere in the feasible set (its utopia payoff). The arbitrator must select a Pareto-efficient agreement at which each side receives the same fraction of the distance from the disagreement point to its utopia payoff. Predict, with explicit mechanisms, how this backstop reshapes bargaining over the next twenty years: wage demands, strike and holdout behavior, contract structure (indexation, contingent pay, duration), investment in firm-specific skills, and the political durability of the rule. Indicate which predictions you would stand behind, which depend on assumptions such as the observability of utilities or the distribution of private information, and what outcome would count as the rule failing on its own terms. Then provide a table with columns: Predicted effect; Mechanism; Confidence; Key assumption; Observable sign by 2050.
