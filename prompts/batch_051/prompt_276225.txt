Say we could wake Locke up and explain seasteading to him: a company builds a floating platform in international waters, residents sign a membership agreement with arbitration clauses, the operator owns the platform and can expel people, there's no elected legislature, and people get there by leaving their old country. I know the lazy answer is "consent, so he'd love it," but I want you to reason from his actual machinery, not vibes. Make the case from both sides on (a) whether his right to exit political society (or just the right not to become a member in the first place) actually covers founding a permanent new jurisdiction in the ocean, or whether that right was mainly about joining an existing society; (b) whether his account of original acquisition, with labor mixing and the proviso that you leave enough and as good for others, would let a corp fence off a patch of open ocean, given that water can't really be mixed with labor the way land can; and (c) whether he'd count a platform where the operator has final say and residents are bound only by private contract as a genuine commonwealth with a common judge, or as closer to the state-of-nature/absolute-monarchy situation he spends so much time attacking. Then give a single number from 0 to 1 for how sympathetic he'd be overall, and say which of (a)-(c) is doing most of the work in your answer.
