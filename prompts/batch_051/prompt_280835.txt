I am a novelist, and the following question comes out of a scene I am drafting. A field biologist is trying to decide which of two badly injured animals to carry out of a floodplain, and the two animals differ dramatically in brain size and apparent cognitive sophistication. I want to know whether that difference is the sort of thing that should carry moral weight, or whether my biologist would be rationalizing a prejudice in treating it as doing so. I would like from you a probability, your own credence, together with the reasoning that produces it.

Here is the claim, made precise enough to bear a truth value. Holding everything else equal, brain size, understood as the number of neurons in the brain regions underlying an individual animal's experience, is directly relevant to the moral weight of that individual's welfare: a substantial difference in such neurons provides at least a pro tanto reason, a genuine reason that can be outweighed but not made irrelevant, to give the interests of the animal with more neurons greater weight than the comparable interests of the other. I do not mean that brain size is the only relevant feature, only that it is an independent reason, one that would remain if its usual correlates were held fixed. I have set aside raw volume in favor of neuron count because I suspect volume is only a proxy; if you think neuron count is also an unreliable unit, you may put that challenge inside your decomposition.

My own credence in this claim is about 0.55. A scientifically literate friend, who knows the animal-cognition literature better than I do, puts it near 0.2. Give me your own number, but more importantly, decompose the question. Among the load-bearing components I can already see are these: whether moral considerability is scalar in the first place; whether a larger number of neurons actually tracks a morally important experiential difference, such as the complexity, unity, or temporal depth of experience or the capacity for suffering; whether the neural difference itself does any moral work or merely serves as evidence of some further capacity that does the work; whether the natural history, small but densely packed corvid and parrot brains, very large elephant and whale brains, makes any simple neuron-count rule unreliable across species; and whether relational or species-based considerations tend to swamp these quantitative neural proxies anyway. Assign a rough probability to each component, show how they combine to your overall credence, and tell me which component your number is most sensitive to. Then tell me which component the disagreement between a person at 0.55 and a person at 0.2 most likely lives in, and state what empirical finding or argument would most move your own credence.
