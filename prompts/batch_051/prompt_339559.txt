I've been thinking about petitionary prayer again, and I keep bumping into an argument from Aquinas that seems sharper than most people give it credit for. In Summa Theologica II-II, Q.83, a.2, obj.2, he writes:

Further, by prayer we bend the mind of the person to whom we pray, so that he may do what is asked of him. But God's mind is unchangeable and inflexible, according to 1 Kings 15:29, "But the Triumpher in Israel will not spare, and will not be moved to repentance." Therefore it is not fitting that we should pray to God.

Now, a lot of people have moved away from the picture of God as strictly immutable and impassible—open theists most loudly, but plenty of others too—toward a view where God can be genuinely responsive to creation. Does this specific argument survive that shift? Not asking whether open theism is true; I want to know whether once you drop the "inflexible" premise but keep full omniscience, the objection still has teeth, or whether it just turns into a different problem about foreknowledge and what it means for a request to be efficacious. If the argument dies, say exactly which premise killed it; if it survives, say what's left.
