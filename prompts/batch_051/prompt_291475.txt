Here is the exam question and the student's answer.

Question: Is free riding on a voluntary cooperative scheme wrong because it harms others, because it is unfair, or both? Explain why a harm-based account has trouble with the free rider whose individual contribution makes no detectable difference, and assess whether a fairness-based account fixes that problem without overreaching.

Student's answer: "Free riding is wrong not because it harms anyone — in a scheme with many contributors, one free rider makes no detectable difference to how much benefit is produced — but because it is unfair. The principle of fair play says that whenever a person knowingly receives the benefits of a cooperative scheme, she thereby acquires an obligation to contribute her share; in enjoying the good while others bear the costs, she makes an exception of herself for no defensible reason. And the objection that her contribution would not make a difference is easy to answer: if everyone reasoned that way, the scheme would collapse, and so her individual non-contribution is unfair after all. The conclusion is that free riding is wrong whenever the benefit it takes is genuinely shared."
