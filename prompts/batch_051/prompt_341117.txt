Take Nozick's experience machine and change what it does. Instead of simulating whatever experiences you want, suppose it directly induces a permanent neural state of complete tranquility: no craving, no suffering, no felt lack. It doesn't simulate a life—you know youre in the tank, and this doesn't bother you. Assume you start with ordinary cravings and ordinary suffering, and the machine removes all of it for good. Now take a tranquilist who holds that the only thing that matters is the absence of craving and suffering. Does the original anti-hedonist argument transfer? The standard thought is we wouldn't plug in because we care about actually doing things and being in contact with reality, not just about how things feel from inside. But the tranquilist might say that objection only cuts against someone who wants positive experiences; they only want the absence of suffering and craving, and this machine delivers exactly that. So does a committed tranquilist have to plug in, or is there a refusal that doesn't smuggle in any value beyond the absence of craving and suffering?
