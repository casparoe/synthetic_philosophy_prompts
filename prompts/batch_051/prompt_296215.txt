ok so after years of reading LessWrong and telling myself I understood exactly what was wrong with everything, I've somehow signed up to give a session at our local reading group on van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and I want the objections pre-mortemed. The version of the thing I'm actually going to defend is what I've been calling expert-qualified Reflection: your current credence should defer to a later credence only when the later self is at least as well-informed as you are now and not compromised, so the drunk-you, forgetful-you, and cognitively-pilled-you cases don't count, and the principle says nothing embarrassing about them. My line is going to be that the usual diachronic Dutch book argument looks like it proves unqualified Reflection but actually smuggles in an assumption that your later self is a better judge, i.e. it smuggles in the expert-clause; strip that out and the argument stops working, which is fine because the unqualified principle really is false and the qualified one is a boring corollary of conditionalization plus deference to well-informed future selves. The place where I know this gets hard is self-locating stuff, Sleeping Beauty in particular, where it's not obvious what at least as well-informed about the same total evidence even means.

The room is a real mix. There are two formal epistemologists who basically think van Fraassen settled this decades ago and want the Dutch book to be enough. There's a decision theorist who thinks diachronic coherence constraints are mostly fake and will say the whole thing is an artifact of a bad model of sequential choice. There's somebody who works on self-locating belief and is definitely going to bring up Sleeping Beauty and some Elga-style cases. And there's a grad student who's read the anti-Reflection stuff and is convinced the principle is just false because you can know in advance that you'll forget or be confused or drunk.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet, but write it as continuous prose like an email, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. I want the objections in roughly the order I'll actually get them from this crowd, each one phrased the way the person who would raise it would put it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note about where that reply is softest. At the end, tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks, why it's worse than everything else, and what I should actually do if it comes up.
