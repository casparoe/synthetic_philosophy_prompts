I write about science and ideas for a living, and for the last few months I've been working on a piece about forgeries, reproductions, and why people care about originals at all. The reporting has left me with a pile of gut reactions I can't unify, and I'd like you to help me find the principle behind them, if there is one.

Here are my verdicts, going by gut. When van Meegeren's Emmaus painting was believed to be a Vermeer, people saw a masterpiece; once it was exposed as a forgery painted in the 1930s, I feel the very same object lost almost all its value, even though not a fleck of paint changed. So I know its not the visual surface doing the work for me. But I'd happily hang a decent, clearly labeled reproduction of a Vermeer in my apartment and not feel I was missing anything, because I wouldn't be pretending it was an original. A photographer I interviewed sometimes prints an image from a negative she shot decades ago and signs it; I'm entirely comfortable calling that later print an original work, even though the paper never saw the inside of the old camera. I'm also mostly comfortable with a sculptor's estate casting a bronze from the original plaster after the sculptor's death, though I'm shakier on that than on the photograph. But if a stranger stole the mold and made a cast that came out indistinguishable, I'd value it far less. An artist who paints the same composition twice by hand, both signed, both from the same materials: I'd call both originals, not an original and a copy. And here's the case that nags at me. Suppose a painting I love burns, and someone makes a replica so exact that no test could distinguish it from the lost canvas. Even honestly labeled, I don't think the replica inherits any of the original's value, and I can't say why, given how much my eyes matter in the other cases.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or at least gets most of them and tells me honestly which of my verdicts I should overturn? I'd especially like to know what it says about a case I keep going back and forth on: the painter himself, years after a canvas burns, rebuilds it from photographs and his own notes, as close to identical as he can manage. Is that a new original, a replica, or something in between? And if the principle splits value-as-something-to-look-at from value-as-a-trace-of-the-act, can you state exactly where the line falls, say for a digital artwork where every copy is bit-for-bit identical but the artist sells one as the original?

For the response, just continuous prose like an email or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
