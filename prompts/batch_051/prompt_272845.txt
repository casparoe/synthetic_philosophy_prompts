For an assignment in my high-school philosophy seminar on the mind, I have to write down my current position on consciousness as a list of commitments and then have it checked for consistency. Here is the list:

(A) I hold physicalism about the mind: conscious experiences are not anything over and above physical processes in the brain.

(B) I find epiphenomenalism about experience plausible: how an experience feels never causes me to say or do anything; the underlying neural processes do all the causal work, including causing my beliefs and reports about my experience.

(C) I take myself to know directly, from introspection, what my experiences are like.

(D) I accept a causal constraint on knowledge: my belief that p can count as knowledge only if the fact that p is appropriately causally involved in producing that belief.

(E) I think there is a real explanatory gap: no account of the brain in purely physical or functional terms would let someone grasp what an experience is like.

I would like an audit of this set: which commitments are actually in tension, in order of how serious the tension is, and which pairs only seem to be in tension. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the rest hang together. I do not need a textbook summary of epiphenomenalism; I want to know whether my own combination is defensible.
