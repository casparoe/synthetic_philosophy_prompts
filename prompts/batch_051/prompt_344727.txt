For the past year a few of us have been meeting to talk about political emotions, and the discussion of envy keeps stalling in the same place. The basic fight is between two camps. The Virtue camp says envy is a vice full stop: it's a painful or hostile response to someone else's having a good, not a response to the injustice of the distribution that gave them that good, so it should be trained away, or at least given no weight in our thinking about justice. The Signal camp says that's too fast: when the gap between rich and poor gets large enough, the envy people actually feel is diagnostic evidence that a distributive arrangement has gone wrong, even if the envious people can't articulate the injustice. A third of us keeps pushing a Rawls-inspired line — that there's a difference between ordinary envy and Rawls's excusable envy, understood roughly as the envy that predictably arises when a society's institutions leave some people so badly off, and so visibly below others, that their self-respect gets damaged; a well-ordered society should reduce the conditions for that kind of envy, but the feeling itself still isn't a moral emotion the way resentment is. Nobody can agree on why that distinction is supposed to help, or whether it carves anything real.

The odd thing is that we don't seem to disagree about any of the standard cases: the worker who resent the boss's bonus is usually seen as having a legitimate grievance of some sort, while the person who hates their coworker's new car is usually seen as just petty. What we disagree about is why those labels track anything. Some members treat introspective confusion as a devastating objection: if ordinary people can't reliably sort "I want what she has" from "the way she got it was wrong," the distinction can't do the work Rawls wants. Others say introspection is beside the point because the distinction is normative, not descriptive — it's about which feelings a just order ought to encourage or extinguish. My working theory is that the debate isn't really about envy at all: it's a downstream symptom of a prior disagreement about whether any emotion can carry justificatory weight, and we've dressed it up as a fight about a vice. But I can also see it being a hidden empirical question about what envy actually does to people's judgments, or a merely verbal dispute about the word resentment. What kind of disagreement is this, really?
