I dont have any philosophy training but ive been reading about whether we can trust the stuff our own mind tells us about itself, and i keep running into Comte being cited as the guy who showed introspection is impossible. A blog post i found sums up his view like this: in his Course of Positive Philosophy Comte says the mind cannot directly observe its own operations, because that would require the self to split into two—one part doing the thinking and another part standing back and watching the thinking. Since the organ being watched and the organ doing the watching are the same organ, no real observation can happen. The blog takes him to mean that introspective psychology is a dead end, that the only reliable facts about mental life come from observing other people from the outside (behavior, physiology, that kind of thing), and that this is also why modern psychology treats self-reports as just more behavior instead of a window into the mind. That last part seems like a stretch to me, and also im not convinced by the splitting argument because I can catch myself in the middle of a thought—like right now im aware that im mentally rehearsing this sentence while im typing it. Is the blog being fair? Where does it get Comte right, where does it overstate him, and does the conclusion about self-reports even follow from what he actually argued?
