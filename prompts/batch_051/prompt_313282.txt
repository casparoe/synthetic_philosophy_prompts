I keep a list of claims about how the world works, because I got tired of contradicting myself in arguments with my brother-in-law, who is a meteorologist and enjoys catching me out. The list reads something like this. I'm a determinist: any state of the universe, together with the laws of nature, fixes every later state, so the future is already settled in the present. I don't think there is any genuine randomness in the world; when we say something is random, we just mean we don't know enough about its causes. I think a perfect intelligence that knew the exact state of everything and all the laws could in principle predict the whole future and retrodict the whole past with no uncertainty. But I also beleive chaos theory shows that some things, the weather being the obvious one, are unpredictable even in principle: tiny differences in how the world starts out get amplified so fast that no amount of better measurements or computing power will ever get forecasts past a few weeks. And I think the butterfly effect is the bit that really breaks the old clockwork picture, because it says small causes can have enormous effects, and the small stuff doesn't just average out.

What I'd like is an audit of these claims. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, and for the genuine ones say how bad the conflict is and what the cheapest fix would be, including which claim I'd have to weaken and how much that would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would make the whole set hang together best. If the problem is that I'm sliding between senses of 'predictable', 'random', or 'deterministic', say that plainly rather than humouring me — I'd rather have the framing corrected than get a polite answer to a badly posed question. Also, be explicit about how confident you are in each verdict and where you're relying on a contested reading of the physics rather than something settled. Please write in British English.
