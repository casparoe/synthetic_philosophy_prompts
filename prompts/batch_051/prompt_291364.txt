I run a 90 minute big-questions night at my local library, usually about 10 to 14 adults, most without much philosophy background, and I try to make everything a do-something-first thing rather than a lecture so the idea lands before I ever name it. Lately ive been stuck on the great filter: the idea that between dead chemistry and a galaxy visibly full of civilizations there has to be at least one step almost nothing gets past, which would explain why the sky looks empty. The part I keep tripping on is that we are the descendants of whatever didn't fail, so using our own history to guess which steps were easy is like interviewing lottery winners to decide whether the lottery is winnable. I want the group to feel that, not just nod along.

Can you design a game or simulation we can actually run in the 90 minutes? I'd like people to steer a few lineages through the rough stages from chemistry to a civilization that lasts: self-copying molecules, complex cells, multicellular bodies, tool-using brains, and then surviving your own technology. Use a cheap random mechanism, dice or cards or tokens. The catch I want built in is this: the true survival odds for each step are set beforehand by the facilitator and hidden from the players, and failed lineages just drop out of view without any post-mortem. So by the end the group is arguing about which steps are hard based mostly on the lineages that happened to survive. Some light scoring so the groups that make it through feel clever is fine, as long as it doesn't leak the real probabilities. Then the debrief reveals the actual odds and lets people compare them to how safe or dangerous each step felt.

I want the group to come away seeing why our own existence doesn't prove the past steps were likely, and why the filter being behind us or ahead of us is something our own record can't settle on its own. Give me the rules, the materials, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto that idea. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be straight about which part of the idea the game can't produce by itself and I'd have to fill in myself.
