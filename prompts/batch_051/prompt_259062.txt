For a novel I'm writing, I've been reading around the Ship of Theseus, and I keep noticing a contrast: the case has been debated for over two thousand years, yet the standard moves — continuity of form against loss of matter, with a third voice saying it depends on how you count — are all on the table and nobody shifts position. The continuity side says the ship survives plank-by-plank replacement because its organization and causal history go unbroken; the material side says once no original part remains the object is numerically new; the sortal side says the question has no answer until you specify whether you are counting a ship or a collection of planks. I suspect the impasse persists because these are different questions wearing one name: a metaphysical question about what persistence consists in, a question about how we actually track and re-identify artifacts, and perhaps a practical question about what continuity justifies us in treating as the same thing. I would like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a ruling on who is right. Is it substantive, verbal, or several disputes under a single heading — and under that diagnosis, what would count as progress, or is the debate rationally stable at a stalemate?
