In a seminar on practical reason last term, the instructor kept returning to a single analogy: value, he insisted, is like height. If Amara is taller than Basil and Basil is taller than Chen, then Amara is taller than Chen; and the same, he said, holds for 'at least as good as' and 'better than.' The analogy was doing real argumentative work. Whenever someone raised spectrum-style worries about the transitivity of betterness, he dismissed them as a confusion between measurement and measurement error: the underlying quantity is transitive, and our pairwise comparisons only look intransitive because we cannot reliably discriminate small differences. I want a full audit of that analogy, because I have never been able to shake a spectrum argument of the following sort.

Consider a sequence of possible lives, S0 through S60. S0 contains one year of pain at intensity 100 on a 100-point scale. S1 contains two years at intensity 99, S2 three years at intensity 98, and so on, each step adding one year and subtracting one point of intensity, until S60, which contains sixty-one years of pain at intensity 40. When I inspect any adjacent pair on its own, the two lives differ by so little that I cannot honestly judge the later one worse; each adjacent verdict is at most 'not worse.' Yet S60 is plainly worse than S0. If 'at least as good as' is transitive in the way 'at least as tall as' is, these verdicts cannot all stand together.

I would like the correspondence table for the height analogy as deployed here: what is doing the work of the measuring rod, the units, the object being measured, and the threshold of discrimination; where the mapping holds; where it breaks; and which disanalogies matter for the conclusion it was used to draw. In particular, does the analogy smuggle in the assumption that betterness is a single underlying scalar quantity whose small differences can be real but sub-threshold, so that spectrum arguments concern only our perceptual limitations? Does the instructor's conclusion—that spectrum arguments can be dismissed as measurement error—survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the height analogy carrying the conclusion the whole time? If height is the wrong source analogy, what would a better one be, and what would it license instead?
