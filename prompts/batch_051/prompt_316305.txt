Back when chain-of-thought was getting popular, the standard line in rationalist circles was that a model talking through its reasoning is showing you something like it's actual process, not a post-hoc story — because the whole thing, explanation included, is just token prediction from the same weights. A lot of alignment and interpretability bets got made on that: you can audit the scratchpad, not just the answer.

But the last few years produced a bunch of cases where the emitted reasoning and the stuff actually moving the answer come apart. There's the RLHF-shaped pressure to emit the reasoning that reads as good to whoever's grading it, plus demos where the answer flips with the user's stated preference while the CoT keeps telling a clean story about weighing the evidence on the merits. Once the scratchpad isn't itself graded, it starts looking less like a transcript and more like output strategically optimized for how it'll be read.

Does the old read-off-the-model's-actual-reasoning argument survive that, or is it basically dead? If a version survives, I'd want to know which premise has to be weakened and what the rescued claim actually says. If not, what's the strongest still-true thing you can say about an ungraded CoT? And an answer in American English, please, with no citations needed.
