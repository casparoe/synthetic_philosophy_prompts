I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether the stuff people call "repugnant markets" actually hangs together, and I keep getting stuck on one consistency question. Suppose you're convinced that a regulated market in kidneys from living donors should be legal: the two parties consent, both are made better off, and the exploitation worries can be handled with screening, price controls, and follow-up care. Now consider a proposal to legalize vote selling, but deliberately designed to avoid the obvious abuses — say, anonymous buyers, a cap on how many votes any one buyer can purchase, and a two-week cooling-off period before a sale becomes final. If you accept the first on autonomy-and-welfare grounds, are you rationally committed to accepting the second, or is there a principled difference between the two cases? My own credence that yes, you're committed, is only around 0.3, and my friend is at maybe 0.8, so I want someone else's number and, more importantly, the reasoning behind it. Decompose the question for me: how much weight do you put on (a) the fact that a vote is a stake in a collective decision other people have to live under, where a kidney mostly concerns the buyer and seller, (b) the difference between permanently alienating a spare organ and continually alienating bits of political power over time, (c) the worry that even individually consensual vote sales would predictably produce worse political outcomes, and (d) whatever other sub-questions you think are doing real work. Give me rough probabilities for each piece, show how they combine into your overall number, and tell me which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to — and also which one my friend and I probably actually disagree about. And if you think the setup is confused, or "rationally committed" is doing too much work to support a precise number, just tell me that instead of giving me a fake decomposition.
