I keep going back and forth on whether practical reason has to speak with one voice. The tidy view says there is a single answer about what I have most reason to do, all things considered: if self-interest pulls one way and impartial morality pulls another, at most one of them can be authoritative, because otherwise I'd be left with two equally binding but contradictory orders and no decision procedure. But here's my strong intuition, and I don't think it's just squeamishness: someone who spends their savings on a year of travel instead of donating it to a charity where it would save several strangers is not irrational, and someone who gives it all away in the same situation is not irrational either. The dualist says that's because practical reason splits into two equally valid standpoints — egoism and impartial benevolence — with no neutral court of appeal to rank them. That sounds either profound or like relabling the conflict and refusing to think. What would I have to give up to keep the one-voice view, what would I have to give up to accept the split, and is there a careful reading where the intuition (neither person is irrational) and the theory (there is a single all-things-considered ought) are compatible after all?
