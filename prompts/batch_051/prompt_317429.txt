Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a cosmic nihilist: the universe has no purpose, and there's no point of view outside a human life from which that life could be said to matter.

(2) But I don't think meaning is just a feeling or a personal choice. Some lives are genuinely more meaningful than others, and a person can be mistaken about whether their own life is meaningful.

(3) The view I find most plausible is fitting fulfillment: a life is meaningful when the person is engaged with, and fulfilled by, things that are objectively worth engaging with. Feeling great about your life isnt enough, and neither is a worthwhile object that leaves you cold.

(4) I also think Camus gets Sisyphus right. The boulder never amounts to anything, there's no product and no audience, but when Sisyphus takes on the task with full lucidity and revolt, his life is meaningful because of the way he lives it.

(5) I don't think there needs to be anything supernatural or Platonist backing the objective worth in (3). I think objective worth is made rather than discovered: it comes into existence once creatures like us start caring about certain activities in a way that sustains and deepens us, and there's no fact of the matter about it beyond that.

(6) Last, I think the occasional striking sense of absurdity—the sudden why-do-any-of-this-at-all feeling—is not itself evidence that one's life is meaningless; it can hit in the middle of projects I'd otherwise count as genuinely worthwhile.

So that's the package. I honestly can't tell where the strain is, so please be specific about which commitments pull against which, and don't just say its all fine or that (1) is the obvious problem. Take your time and work through it step by step.
