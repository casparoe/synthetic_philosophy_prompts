I cover artificial intelligence and the ideas behind it for a magazine aimed at scientifically literate readers, and over the past year I have found myself defending a position I did not originally expect to hold: the step-by-step text a language model produces before answering — its chain of thought — should not be read as a faithful report of how the model actually reached its answer. The model has no privileged introspective access to its own computation; the narrated steps are generated in the same predictive pass as the rest of the output, and their coherence with the final answer looks to me like evidence about the kind of text the model was trained to imitate, not about the causal route by which the answer was settled. And yet researchers whose work I otherwise respect treat this narrated reasoning as evidence for why a model answered as it did, what it knows, or which intermediate operations it used — sometimes in the same conversation in which they concede the narration can be fluent and false. I want to stress-test the position rather than collect further support for it. If I am wrong — if chain-of-thought text is sometimes, or even reliably, a genuine self-report — what exactly would have to be true? I am not looking for the standard case for the rival view, but for a map of what would count against mine: the observations, experiments, formal results, or internal tensions that would damage it, with a sharp line between what would be fatal and what would be merely awkward. I would also like you to say which of these a committed defender of my position should concede counts against it without giving up the core claim, and where the view has been so thoroughly insulated by qualifications that it no longer runs a real risk.

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, ordered from the most damaging defeater to the least. In the fifth item, after the least damaging defeater, state how confident you are in the overall map and identify the single point in it you are least sure of.
