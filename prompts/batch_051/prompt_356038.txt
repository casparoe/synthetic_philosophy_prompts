I'm teaching an intro ethics class for about 18 first- and second-year students, one 75-minute slot, and I want to do moral luck without lecturing it at them first. My half-formed idea is a two-phase trial: everyone reads the same story about a driver who glances at there phone to send a quick text; in phase one they judge the driver purely on the conduct and write down a sentence and a blame rating. Then, only after they've committed to that, each small group rolls a die and gets told the outcome: low rolls, the car swerves and nobody is hurt; high rolls, a pedestrian steps into the road and is killed. Then they re-judge. I want the exercise to let them catch themselves revising the judgment of the same act because of somthing the die did afterwards — and then be forced to say what, if anything, the driver did differently between their two verdicts.
