Someone I read claims that, on balance, habitat destruction reduces wild animal suffering: a cleared or drained landscape contains fewer animals than the intact ecosystem it replaces, and the animals that remain do not live obviously worse lives, whereas the intact system sustains a large population whose lives include predation, parasitism, disease, and gradual starvation. I want to know how far this claim can be made answerable to evidence rather than left as an untestable intuition. Give me the strongest concrete test — a natural experiment, matched comparison, or measurement program — the results that would count for the claim and against it, and the confounds you would have to rule out. Then say what even a decisive result would fail to settle, especially how to compare welfare across species and what to do about the animals that would have existed in the intact habitat but never exist once it is destroyed. Finally, tell me where your verdict is least stable: what finding or argument would be most likely to change your mind.
