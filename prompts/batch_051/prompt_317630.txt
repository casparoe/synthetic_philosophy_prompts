A private hospital turns away a man with a burst appendix because he's uninsured, and he goes into septic shock in the waiting room. A grocer refuses to let a hungry shopper walk out with bread he can't pay for, and the shopper goes home and misses a meal. Most people would say the hospital's refusal is a serious wrong — maybe even a violation of a right — while the grocer's refusal is just business, maybe stingy but not unjust. We hold both verdicts at the same time. Is there an actual principle that seperates the two cases, or are we just reacting to the whiff of blood? Give me the strongest candidate principle you can — need, health as a special good, professional obligation, a background social contract, whatever — and test it against nearby cases: a dentist refusing to drain a painful abscess, a pharmacist refusing insulin to someone who can't pay, a chef refusing to feed a hungry person, a landlord evicting a family in winter. Tell me straight whether the asymmetry survives, and if it doesn't, which of the two judgements we should give up.
