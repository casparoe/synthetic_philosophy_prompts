Consider a line of argument that has done a lot of work in the philosophy of action and normativity. It runs roughly as follows: a reason, in the sense that can justify a belief or an action, is necessarily a reason for a reasoner. It earns that status only within the deliberative perspective of an agent who can weigh it against alternatives, revise under pressure from counterevidence or criticism, and be answerable for it. On this view, a consideration that looks like a reason but is generated without any such perspective—a free-floating rationale—is at most a cause or a cue, not a reason. The load-bearing assumption is that the functional role of a reason—guiding deliberation, licensing an inference, making an action intelligible—can be realized only by something with the right kind of agential backing: commitment, responsiveness to counterevidence, liability to being asked for justification.

Now consider a large language model. It emits fluent and often persuasive rationales for conclusions—diagnoses, arguments, ethical justifications—without deliberating, without committing itself to the considerations it cites, and without the kind of answerability the old view required, because it is trained to reproduce the surface of reasoning by next-token prediction. Some of these rationales track truth well enough that people in practice treat them as reasons.

Does the old argument survive this development, or does it mistake a contingent feature of the only known generators of reasons for a necessary feature of reasons themselves? I am asking about the argument, not recommending AI policy. Say exactly which premise the language-model case puts pressure on—the requirement of weighing, the requirement of answerability, or the assumption that the justifying role cannot be realized by statistical text generation—and whether the argument can be repaired. If it can, give the repaired criterion for when a free-floating rationale counts as a reason, and state the strongest thing the repaired view still rules out. If it cannot, say what should replace the old principle so that we are not left accepting any persuasive text as a reason. No literature review; give me your own analysis.
