Constitutions commonly make some of their provisions harder to change than ordinary legislation: amendment may require supermajorities, repeated votes, ratification by subnational units, or may place certain provisions beyond amendment altogether. These devices are usually described as precommitments, on the model of Ulysses binding himself to the mast so that a later self cannot act on a foreseeable weakness. Reconstruct what this practice presupposes about agency and time. Does it presuppose that a political community is a single temporally extended agent, so that an earlier majority and a later one are the same agent binding itself? Or does it presuppose that later majorities are distinct agents whose predictable failures the earlier ones may anticipate and constrain from outside? Show which features of actual entrenchment each reading explains and which it misdescribes or leaves silent, and say whether the Ulysses analogy itself imports a view of personal identity over time that may not carry over to a collective. Finally, indicate what would change in the justification or design of entrenchment if the presupposition you favor were replaced by the rival one.
