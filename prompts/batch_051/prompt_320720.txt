I'm a retired experimental physicist — spent most of my career around detectors, noise, and unobservables we posited to make sense of the data — and I've only recently started reading philosophy seriously. I keep trying to think about the moral realism and anti-realism debate the way I think about neutrinos or dark matter: you never see the thing itself, you see traces of it, and you ask whether positing it earns its keep. I've picked up the usual labels — robust realism, error theory, expressivism, constructivism — but the arguments still feel slippy to me, and I suspect it's because I keep reaching for the wrong vocabularly.

What I'd like is this. Restate the moral realism versus anti-realism debate in the vocabulary I actually used as a physicist: posits and observables, saving the phenomena, underdetermination, inference to the best explanation, instrumentalism about theoretical entities, empirical equivalence, calibration, signal and noise, model selection. Do each of the main positions as faithfully as you can in that language.

Then give me the residue report — the bit I care about most. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which parts did you have to bend or fudge to make them fit? And which parts have no expression at all in the physicist's vocabulary, for instance the idea that a moral judgement motivates by itself, or that moral facts are supposed to be reason-giving in a way electrons never are? For each bit of residue, tell me whether it is a sign that my physics vocabulary is too poor to express something real, or a sign that the philosophers' claim was less clear than it looked. I want you to take a definite view on that, not hand me a menu of possible interpretations. If you think the whole translation was misguided and the moral debate is radically unlike positing unobservables, say so plainly and say why.

Also, please don't quietly assume the physicist's frame is neutral and then call whatever drops out a defect of philosophy. I want an honest translation, including the places where my vocabulary is the thing doing the distorting.

Write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables — just an email-like essay. Go ahead and be technical; you won't lose me on the physics.
