Sanity check on something I can't stop thinking about. Dennett's ladder of minds lines up, as far as I can tell, almost exactly with the ways we build learning agents. Darwinian creatures get better only by natural selection across generations; Skinnerian creatures do trial-and-error in their own lifetime and keep what gets reinforced; Popperian creatures can test hypotheses internally and let them die in their heads instead of out in the world; Gregorian creatures then import other minds' already-tested ideas through language and tools. Now read that alongside: evolutionary algorithms, model-free reinforcement learning, model-based RL and planning inside a learned world model, and LLMs pretrained on a huge corpus of human text. It feels like the same ladder, but I cant tell if this is a deep structural fact or just a loose analogy generated by the word selection. If it is deep, things should transfer. For instance, Popperian control only beats Skinnerian control when your world model is accurate enough and fast enough relative to the actual environment; otherwise trial-and-error wins. I think that transfers, roughly. But where does the mapping snap? Is it a mistake to call LLMs Gregorian creatures, given they dont import tested ideas so much as model the distribution of language? Or is the whole ladder too clean in both cases?
