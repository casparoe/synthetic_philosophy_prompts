I teach a final-year seminar on theology and evolution, and I'm setting the final essay. I want one question that turns on this bit of Darwin, from his letter to Asa Gray of 22 May 1860. Just before this he's been saying he finds it hard to believe a perfectly good and all-powerful God would have purposefully made parasitic wasps so that their young eat a caterpillar alive from the inside. Then he writes:

"On the other hand I cannot anyhow be contented to view this wonderful universe & especially the nature of man, & to conclude that everything is the result of brute force. I am inclined to look at everything as resulting from designed laws, with the details, whether good or bad, left to the working out of what we may call chance. Not that this notion at all satisfies me. I feel most deeply that the whole subject is too profound for the human intellect."

I want the question to force students to work out wether this "designed laws, details left to chance" move actually dissolves Darwin's own wasp problem, and whether a defender of the doctrine of a good creation (creation was good before the Fall, with death and suffering arriving as a corruption after sin) can take over Darwin's move without quietly giving up the doctrine. Please write the question, a model answer of around 700 words, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves an A answer must make and the specific mistakes that should cost marks, so it seperates students who get the crux from those who just recite "death before the Fall is a problem". 50 minutes, 20 marks.
