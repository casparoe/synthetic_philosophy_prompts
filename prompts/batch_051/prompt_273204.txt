I spent my career in behavioral ecology, mostly on parental care trade-offs in fish and birds, and I've only been reading philosophy seriously since I retired, so I'm still trying to keep my concepts from sliding around. Lately our reading group has been stuck on whether it ever makes more than metaphorical sense to say one AI raises another, and what guardianship would amount to between artificial agents.

One friend in the group has been staggeringly consistent about a series of adjacent cases. She says adoptive parents are obviously real parents, so she isn't hung up on shared genes or shared code. She says a human coach who trains one narrow skill in a child for years is not raising the child, because the coach is answerable only for that slice of the child's life. She says a team that trains a model end-to-end on a fixed objective and ships it has built a tool, not raised anything. But she accepts that a human who acts as a long-term guardian of an AI—setting and revising it's goals, correcting behavior, granting autonomy stage by stage—is doing something she would call raising in a stretched but real sense. She says an AI that copies its weights into a new system is replicating or forking, not raising, and two AIs that co-adapt with neither in charge are peers rather than parent and child.

The case we are now fighting over is this. A capable AI is assigned to raise a successor that starts out mostly unstructured. The senior AI chooses the curriculum, adjusts the reward schedule as the junior improves, opens and locks away capabilities, and has to vouch for the junior before each new stage of autonomy. The junior doesn't inherit the senior's weights, and the senior's choices measurably shape what the junior becomes. The senior, being an AI, can't be fined or punished, and nobody thinks it has a subjective stake in the junior's welfare; it just does the job it was assigned.

Given her track record, what should we expect her to say about this case? I don't actually care that much about the yes or no. I want to know what principle she's running, which other principles fit her earlier calls just as well, where they disagree about this case, and what further small variation would sort them out. And tell me whether the fact that the senior AI can't be held responsible is doing all the work here, or whether that's just my old field-biology habits talking.
