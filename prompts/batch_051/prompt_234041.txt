Write a research proposal for a one-semester term paper on whether the distinction between original and derived intentionality can survive Dennett's two-bitser challenge. That challenge, in brief, is that every proposed mark of an originally intentional state is either also present in some cheaply engineered system whose intentionality Searle would call merely derived, or else amounts to an unexplained appeal to the observer's own conscious case. The proposal should pose a determinate version of this question that a semester's work could actually settle. It should identify which existing moves leave the question open and why—for instance, appeals to the state's causal history, to its biological function, or to what it feels like from the inside. It should then specify an approach that could make progress, such as characterizing original intentionality by a causal role that a behaviorally equivalent copy could still lack, and defend that approach against the obvious alternatives. Finally, it should say what a successful result and a failed result would look like and give an honest account of what could make the project too large for one semester. Describe positions and arguments in your own words; do not provide a bibliography or citations, since those can be filled in later.
