I'm a software engineer and don't have any real philosophy background, but I've been reading about the problem of evil and got stuck on the specific case of Satan's fall. I've ended up holding something like the free-will defense: God creates free creatures because a freely chosen good is worth the risk; freedom has to include the real possibility of choosing evil; so even a creature that starts out good, in God's presence, could freely bring about evil. That's roughly how I make sense of how evil could arise in a good creation with free angels.

I want to know what would genuinely count against that position. Not just the standard response that God maybe shouldn't have taken the risk, but the strongest cases, intuitions, or arguments that would show the story is false or incoherent. The step I'm least sure about is how a perfectly good will can first choose evil without already having some defect or evil inclination—because if it needs a defect, then freedom alone doesn't explain the first sin. Is that as serious a problem as I think? What else should make me drop the view? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and it would help to know which ones a committed defender of the free-will defense would themselves concede count against it. If the view turns out to be unfalsifiable, does that make it a bad explanation here, and is that different from unfalsifiability in other areas?
