Analyze the March 2018 Uber autonomous-vehicle fatality in Tempe, Arizona as a case study for the responsibility gap problem for autonomous systems.

Here are the facts to work from. At roughly 9:58 pm on 18 March 2018, an Uber prototype self-driving car based on a Volvo XC90, operating in autonomous mode, struck Elaine Herzberg while she was pushing a bicycle across Mill Avenue outside the designated crosswalk. A human safety driver was in the driver's seat. The vehicle was traveling at 43 mph. Telemetry showed that it detected Herzberg about six seconds before impact but for about 4.7 seconds did not infer that emergency braking was needed; its automated braking had been disabled that day. The safety driver did not intervene before impact and, according to a police report, had been streaming a television program shortly before the crash. Prosecutors did not charge Uber criminally. The safety driver was charged with negligent homicide and later pleaded guilty to endangerment, receiving three years' probation.

The concept I want you to use is the responsibility gap: the concern that when an autonomous system causes harm, there may be no natural locus of moral or legal responsibility because no one fully controlled the harmful act, foresaw it, intended it, or is obviously answerable for it in the way we expect a responsible agent to be.

Please do not just explain the concept. Work through the episode in both directions: use the theory to analyze the case, and use the case to test and refine the theory.

Format:

- The theory setup: about 200 words. State the conditions you take to be necessary for a person or organization to be morally responsible, and separately legally liable, for a harmful outcome. Then say exactly why the involvement of an autonomous system threatens to prevent these conditions from being satisfied.
- Mapping the candidates: about 500 words. Go through at least these four candidate responsible parties — the safety driver, Uber as a corporate entity, the engineers and designers whose decisions configured the vehicle's braking and perception systems, and the vehicle/software system itself. For each one, identify what they controlled, knew, and intended, and say whether they meet the conditions from your setup.
- Locating the gap: about 250 words. State precisely where the responsibility gap is sharpest in this episode, and distinguish a genuine responsibility gap from a mere institutional or evidential failure to allocate blame.
- Closing or widening the gap: about 250 words. Consider the legal and organizational mechanisms that already exist, such as corporate liability and supervisory duties. Is Tempe best described as an actual responsibility gap, or as a failure to use those mechanisms?
- Lesson for the theory: about 200 words. What does this episode show about the responsibility gap concept? Is it confirmed, refuted, or shown to be too coarse? If it needs refinement, state the refinement.

Do not include footnotes, references, or a bibliography. Work only from the facts supplied and your own reasoning.
