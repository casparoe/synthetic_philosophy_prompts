Here's a brief for a thought experiment. The background frame: most people who think about whether AIs could 'raise' other AIs end up saying the relationship is really just training, or ongoing fine-tuning, or a developer using one model to generate data for another — and therefore the 'parent' AI has no genuine guardianship role and no real partial responsibility for what the 'child' becomes. I want the opposite case built.

Construct a scenario in which an AI stands in a genuine guardianship relation to another AI, such that the guardian can reasonably be held partly responsible for the child's values or dispositions, even though the guardian did not design the child's architechture or write its initial weights.

Constraints. (1) Near-term and boring: foundation models, tool use, scaffolding, corporate incentives — no superintelligence, no exotic physics, no agent with unbounded self-modification. (2) The child must not be a fork or copy of the guardian: different weights at the start, and not a child in the trivial sense of 'an earlier checkpoint of the same run'. (3) The guardianship has to fall out of the structure of the situation, not out of a human simply declaring the first AI to be the second one's parent.

Then show the design works: state what the guardian actually controls and how that control is exercised; say why this is guardianship rather than training, and what a hard-line training-only reader would say in reply; identify the single structural feature that is doing the classifying work; and say what the case collapses into if that feature is removed. Write the whole thing in British English, cheers.
