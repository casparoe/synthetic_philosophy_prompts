I keep going back and forth on whether physics could have a final theory, because I've absorbed a lot of the effective field theory picture and it pulls me one way, but I also have a very basic intuition that pulls me the other.

The theory side goes like this. Every quantum field theory we actually use looks like an effective theory: you coarse-grain, the high-energy degrees of freedom get integrated out, the couplings run with scale, and the details above the cutoff are hidden from the low-energy physics, which is fixed by a handful of relevant and marginal operators. The Standard Model is probably just the EFT of something else, and that something else will probably look like an EFT of something else again. On this picture there is no natural endpoint: physics is a tower of descriptions, each valid relative to a scale, and asking what the final theory is might be a category mistake. I find that compelling for clean systematic reasons — it explains why we can do excellent physics while ignorant of the deep UV, and why renormalizability and universality look the way they do, without positing a metaphysically privileged bottom layer.

Then the intuition side. But the world is just some way. Our theories get more accurate, they unify previously separate domains, and they stop changing in regimes where earlier theories kept changing. That doesn't feel like we're just finding better coarse-grainings; it feels like convergence to something. If there's no final theory, I want to say, then what are all these approximations approximating? And even if we could never test below some scale, it still seems true that the universe is doing something determinate down there, and the idea that there's just no fact of the matter about it is one I can't let go off.

I don't want a verdict. I want to know what would have to happen for the intuition to be the thing you give up, what would have to happen for the theory to be the thing you give up, and wether there's a reading on which both survive — maybe the final theory just is the fixed point of the renormalization group, or the whole tower, or a limiting object the sequence approaches. And then: if there were such a thing, how would we ever recognize that we'd found it, given that every test we can perform is at some finite scale?
