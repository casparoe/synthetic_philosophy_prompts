I've been reading about the appraisal/bestowal distinction in the love-and-reasons literature, and I'm drawn to the bestowal view: loving someone doesn't just detect value that's already there, it creates reasons and partiality that wouldn't exist for a non-lover. That explains why the beloved's wellbeing can matter to me in a way a stranger's doesn't, without me pretending the beloved is objectively the most excellent person alive. But I also can't shake the intuition that love can be mistaken. Loving someone because you've projected qualities onto them they don't actually have feels like an error rather than just an odd bestowal, and a love that's wholy indifferent to who the person is seems defective. So which gives? I'm not asking for the one-paragraph verdict. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be debunked and dropped, what it would take for the bestowal view to be amended or abandoned, and whether there's a reading on which both survive — say, by seperating the grounds of love from the reasons love creates.
