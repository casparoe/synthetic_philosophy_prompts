For a workshop on Mill’s The Subjection of Women I have been asked to circulate my philosophical commitments so the other participants can test them. I would like an audit of the set rather than a lecture. Here it is.

I accept Mill’s central claim that the appeal to women’s “nature” in defense of their subordination is circular: the traits offered as natural are largely produced by the very institutions and customs they are used to justify. I also think Mill’s diagnosis in Chapter I—where he writes, “So long as an opinion is strongly rooted in the feelings, it gains rather than loses in stability by having a preponderating weight of argument against it”—explains why this move survives refutation and why reasoned argument alone rarely dislodges it.

But I hold several other commitments that may pull awkwardly against this:

(1) I accept a standard Humean prohibition: no ought from an is, and no biological difference can directly justify a legal or social inequality.
(2) I also think that long-standing customs, including many gendered customs, have a mild initial presumption of serving some real function simply because they have persisted, though the presumption can be defeated.
(3) I think Mill’s case for women’s equality does not require denying that there might be biologically based average differences between the sexes; his argument would be no weaker if such differences were confirmed.
(4) Yet I think that reliable cross-cultural evidence of biological sex differences would matter for policy, because it would constrain predictions about which reforms are feasible and what costs they would impose.
(5) I suspect that in political argument the most effective way to defeat an entrenched nature-claim is often to advance a rival nature-claim, for example that women’s capacities as mothers give them distinctive moral insight.

Please go through these commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which conflicts are only apparent. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would most improve the overall coherence of the set.
