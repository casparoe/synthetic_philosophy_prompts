I'm a PhD student in political sociology and I keep coming back to the tight-loose culture framework—the idea that tight societies have strong norms and low tolerance for deviance, loose ones the opposite. I want to stress-test it against Singapore's 2020 migrant worker dormitory COVID outbreak. Here's the version of the story I have in mind: through early 2020, Singapore's general population got a very strict, very compliance-heavy response—aggressive contact tracing, quarantine orders, mask rules, fines for even small violations—and community transmission stayed pretty low. Then in the spring the virus exploded in the cramped dormitories where migrant workers live, and by summer the vast majority of Singapore's confirmed cases were in that population. The government responded by quarantining entire dormitories, with movement restrictions inside the buildings themselves, and there was a fair bit of criticism that workers were being treated as vectors rather than as a population the rules were supposed to protect.

I want a careful two-direction analysis. First, actually map the episode in tight-loose terms: who is the "group" whose norms are being described, what were the norms and enforcement mechanisms for the citizen population versus the workers seperately, and what does the theory predict each would do under a collective threat. Then tell me where the mapping strains. E.g., Singapore looks tight if you look at its citizens, but was it tight for the migrant workers before the outbreak, or was that population just outside the norm-enforcement system? Does the framework have room for a society that is tight toward insiders and effectively treats a large resident group as an exception, or for norms imposed on a group without any real buy-in? And what should the framework say about how much rule-following a society should demand when the rules apply so unequally? I'd rather get an honest account of what the episode shows about the theory's limits than a forced "the theory explains everything" essay. Be specific about how you'd code it for real too: what would count as evidence for tightness here, and where would you look to decide between a segmentation story and a genuine looseness story.
