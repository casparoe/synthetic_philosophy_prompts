I'm writing a novel built around "gradual replacement" uploading: a clinic replaces each of a person's neurons, over the course of many months, with a synthetic unit that reproduces that neuron's signaling behavior, until no biological neurons remain and the patient's memories, habits, and temperament appear untouched. In a scene I keep revising, my protagonist—a cellist, not a philosopher—asks whether the person who wakes up at the end of the procedure is really the same individual who walked into the clinic. Her engineer brother answers, "You are the pattern, not the matter," and treats the question as closed. I want her reply to be more than mere anxiety, so I would like to know exactly what that sentence could be claiming.

Please lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan might express—for example, that ordinary survival already involves continuous material replacement; that what matters in survival is psychological continuity rather than substrate; that personal identity consists in some preserved informational pattern; or that a pattern, wherever it is realized, is numerically the same person. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive but contestable, or probably false. Then tell me which reading or readings would actually do the work the brother needs—establishing that the upload is literally the same person as his sister, rather than merely a similar conscious successor with copies of her memories—and which readings merely sound as if they support him. I would also like the readings tested against a branching case: suppose two uploads are made from a single scan, or the original body survives the procedure alongside the synthetic one. Which readings would then imply that both resulting beings are the same person as the pre-procedure cellist, and which would avoid that conclusion?

Finally, say where your verdicts are shakiest: which further case or consideration would be most likely to change your mind about which reading of the slogan deserves to be left standing in the engineer's mouth.
