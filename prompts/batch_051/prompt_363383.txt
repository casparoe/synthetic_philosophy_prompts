I cover science and ideas, and I'm writing a piece about whether an AI could ever know what pain is like without feeling it. Philosophers keep reaching for Mary's room: a neuroscientist who's lived her whole life in a black-and-white room and knows every physical fact about colour, then steps out and sees red for the first time. I need a working definition of 'knowing what it's like' — or of understanding, if that's the better word — that I can apply without getting lost in jargon. Test it against these cases, some of which feel clear-cut to me and a couple of which I genuinely can't settle: knowing the wavelength of red; having seen red once, years ago, and being able to describe it accurately; having seen red once but now unable to picture it; a colour-blind physicist who knows all the physics of colour; an AI that can correctly answer any question about pain but has no pain receptors and no felt states; and reading a perfect description of the taste of a fruit you've never eaten. Then tell me: on your definition, does Mary learn something new when she leaves the room, and what should I say about the AI case?
