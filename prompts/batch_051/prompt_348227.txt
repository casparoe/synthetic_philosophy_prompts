I keep going round in circles on a tension between a view I'm increasingly attracted to and an intuition I can't shake, and I'd like you to help me work out which should give way. On one side, the Daoist notion of wu-wei — roughly, acting without forcing, a kind of absorbed ease — strikes me as the only thing that makes sense of a bunch of ordinary cases: the harder you try to fall asleep, the more awake you are; over-rehearsing a joke kills it; and being told to just be natural immediately makes you less natural. There seems to be a general principle here: effortfully aiming at certain states tends to undercut them; the attempt to grab ease or spontaneity pushes them further off. Call it the paradox of aiming at effortlessness. On the other side, I've spent enough years doing things that reward sheer grind — I rowed competitively, and I'm now learing the piano — and there the lesson runs the other way. Progress comes from deliberate, effortful, self-monitored repetition; anyone who has learnt a hard skill knows that the advice to just relax and let it happen is exactly the advice that keeps a beginner bad. So my firm intuition is that trying works, even for the very things that later become fluid.

I don't want a survey of Daoism or a self-help list. I want you to present the strongest version of both sides and then tell me where the pressure should go. Is my intuition just a misreading of the wu-wei idea, because that idea was never about skill acquisition in the first place? Is the theory overgeneralising from a few quirky cases? Or is there a genuine reading on which both survive — where effortful practice builds the conditions for later spontaneity, but I can't aim at the spontaneity itself — and if so, is that a real distinction or just a comforting fudge? Say what would justify each move: dropping the intuition, amending the theory, or accepting the compatibility claim. Please write your answer in British English.
