ok so im writing this paper for my gov class about what a fair society actually looks like and ive probaly landed on five views but i cant tell if they hang together. here they are:

(1) fairness is mostly about equal opportunity, not equal outcomes. if everyone had a real shot, then whatever happens after is basically fine and we shouldnt force everyone to end up in the same place.

(2) but i also think equal opportunity cant just mean nobody technically discriminated against you. like if a city says a wheelchair user and an able-bodied person both have the same opportunity to get a job downtown, thats bogus if the buses arent wheelchair accessible and the office only has stairs. so real equal opportunity means spending extra on some people so they actually have the same options. treating people differently is sometimes the only way to treat them equally.

(3) i think people should be compensated for bad luck (being born poor, getting sick, natural disasters) but not for bad choices (dropping out, gambling). but honestly i cant find the line, because alot of bad choices come from a bad background, which was itself bad luck.

(4) i also think aiming for everyone being equally happy is a mistake. if someone has super expensive tastes and needs a yacht to feel satisfied, thats on them, not a problem society has to fix. what matters is giving people enough resources and real opportunities, not equal satisfaction.

(5) and in practice that means i support high inheritance taxes and heavy public school funding, not to equalize outcomes, but because if rich parents can buy their kids ahead forever, equal opportunity stops meaning anything after one generation.

so does this hang together? which ones are actually pulling against each other and which just look like they conflict? and if they dont all fit, whats the cheapest one to change and what would i be giving up? rank the tensions by how serious they are, dont just say everything is fine or everything is broken.
