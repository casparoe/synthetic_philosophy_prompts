My friend is really into identity thought experiments and I've kept a running list of her verdicts because they've always seemed weirdly consistent. In roughly the order they came up:

- Star Trek style transporter: scan you, disassemble you, rebuild you at the destination from the blueprint. She says the original dies and the person who steps out is a duplicate with your memories, not you.
- The ship with every plank swapped out one at a time over a long voyage until no original plank remains. She says it's the same ship the whole time.
- A perfect brain scan run as a digital simulation while your biological body goes on living. She says the simulation is a copy and the real you is the biological one still walking around.
- A brain transplant into a fresh body, original body destroyed. She says you go with the brain and wake up in the new body, still you.
- Complete irreversible loss of every autobiographical memory plus a drastic personality change after a head injury. She says it's still the same person, just badly changed.
- Both hemispheres transplanted into two separate brainless bodies, each resulting person getting roughly half your memories and half your personality. She says neither of them is you; the original is gone.

Now the case I want to ask her about next. A surgeon replaces a person's brain neuron by neuron with microscopic silicon units that mimic each neuron's input-output role, over about eighteen months. The patient is awake and conversing normally throughout, and at the end there isn't a single biological neuron left but memories, personality, and behavior are unchanged and the patient reports no break or jump. Does the original person survive?

The wildcard is that she's never said wheter she'd extend the ship intuition to a person, since all her person cases so far involve a copy, a whole-brain transfer, or a split, never replacing the brain's parts while the person keeps going. So I'm less interested in the bare yes or no than in the rule or rules that fit everything she's already said, wich of her earlier cases is the closest analog, what her answer would be under each rule, and what further case would cleanly separate the rival rules that survive.
