Write a satirical environmental-impact assessment, roughly 650 words, for a fictional project: the "Welfare-Rationalized Habitat Conversion" of a coastal marsh into a compacted gravel lot, on the grounds that the marsh currently supports a large population of short-lived, high-mortality invertebrates, amphibians, and small vertebrates whose aggregate lifetime welfare the assessing consultancy models as strongly negative. Write the report in the deadpan, jargon-heavy voice of a sustainability consultancy, with numbered sections such as Project Description, Baseline Sentient-Stakeholder Assessment, Net Welfare Projection, Transition Costs, and Consultation and Objections.

The satire should target two things at once: (1) the philosophical position that destroying habitat can be a welfare-improving intervention because it reduces the number of suffering wild animals, and (2) the quantified cost-benefit style of environmental assessment that lets that conclusion be written down with a straight face. The humor must come from the content rather than from winking. Land the following objections without ever stating them in the authorial voice: the deep uncertainty of cross-species and within-life welfare comparisons; the contested empirical claim that wild animal lives are on balance negative; the non-welfarist value people attach to biodiversity and ecosystem integrity; the moral difference between letting existing animals die and actively eliminating them; and the sheer epistemic imprudence of a metric confident enough to round all of this to a single net-positive figure. A specialist in wild-animal-suffering debates should recognize the satire as fair, and a newcomer to the debate should still understand what is being ironized.

Jokes should be generated by taking the view's own logic seriously—for example, amortizing the deaths of currently living animals as a one-time "transition cost," or listing "non-sentient biodiversity enthusiasts" among the stakeholders whose concerns are recorded but not monetized.

Do not break the ironic register. The report itself must not read as an endorsement of the view; the irony should remain legible throughout. After the report, include an italicized note of up to 150 words, outside the word count, that separates what is reasonably settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on wild animal welfare and habitat destruction, and identifies where the satire leans on a contested reading.
