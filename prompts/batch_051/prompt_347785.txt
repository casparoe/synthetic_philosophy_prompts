My friend keeps a running set of Lockean acquisition cases and records how she adjudicates them, mostly to test whether the Lockean proviso can be made precise without collapsing into either libertarian unilateralism or common-ownership egalitarianism. Her recorded verdicts are as follows. Gathering wild fruit from an unowned tree is legitimate. Clearing and farming an unowned meadow is legitimate when adjacent meadowland of similar quality remains unclaimed. Enclosing a portion of a common pasture is legitimate when the rest remains open and enough for the villagers' current herds. Enclosing an unowned hillside that contains the only spring that downstream villagers rely on in dry years is illegitimate, even though the encloser improved the area by terracing it. Damming an unowned side channel of a river to build a mill is legitimate when downstream users are left with exactly their current irrigation supply. Seizing control of the last remaining wild stand of timber that several households use occasionally for building repairs is illegitimate, even though the claimant would manage the forest more efficiently.

The new case is this. An outside group claims ownership of the only unclaimed deep aquifer in a dry basin and begins extracting water for export. No one in the basin currently pumps from that aquifer, and the current water supplies of the three nearby communities will be unaffected for roughly fifty years. After that, their springs, which recharge from the same aquifer, will decline sharply. The outside group proposes to fund desalination plants that will supply the communities with their current water volumes in perpetuity, but will not make any allowance for population growth or new agricultural use. Assume the Lockean state of nature applies and the aquifer is genuinely unowned.

I want a prediction, not a bare answer. What should my friend's verdict be if she wants to be consistent with her previous cases? Which of her precedents is the closest analog? Weigh the candidate rules that fit her record, especially a current-use no-harm reading of the Lockean proviso and a future-opportunity or enough-and-as-good reading, and say which one her new verdict would commit her to. If her precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, say exactly what principle she would be adopting with each possible ruling, and specify one further case that would cleanly discriminate between the candidate rules.
