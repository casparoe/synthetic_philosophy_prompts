Had a long argument with a friend last night about how to update on evidence that isn't a plain proposition, and I still can't shake the feeling we're missing something. Imagine you're dropped into a region divided into four equal-probability zones: Red HQ, Red Second, Blue HQ, and Blue Second. You get a radio message that says, roughly, if you're in Red, you're three times more likely to be at HQ than at Second. The message says nothing about whether you're actually in Red. We tried to update by conditionalization but couldn't, because the message isn't true/false in the straightforward way—it's a conditional. We then tried two other methods: one just set the Red odds to 3:1 and renormalized, the other minimized cross-entropy from the original uniform probabilities, and they gave different answers for the final probability of being in Red at all. That last bit bothered me, because the evidence only seemed to constrain the split inside Red. My friend said this exact puzzle has a name in Bayesian epistemology. Does it? And if so, what is the thing called, and is there actually a principled reason to let P(Red) move when all you've learned is a conditional about Red's internal odds, or is that a sign the update rules are underdetermined?
