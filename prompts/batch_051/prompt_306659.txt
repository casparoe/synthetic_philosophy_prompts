In my former line of work, physical chemistry, a taxonomy only earned its keep if the members of each class shared some property that did explanatory work; otherwise we called it a filing convention and stopped expecting anything from it. That old habit has followed me into my retirement reading in moral philosophy, where it now makes trouble for me with hedonism. Hedonism, as I understand it, holds that pleasure and the absence of pain are the only things good in themselves. Yet the experiences called pleasant look wildly heterogeneous: the pleasure of finishing a proof, of a warm bath, of schadenfreude, of relief after a near miss, of a caress. I do not see what single element they are all supposed to share, and if there is none I do not see why the theory is not just sorting experiences by a label. Let me call this the heterogeneity problem for hedonism.

To make the question concrete, here are five responses I have encountered, described without names:

a) All pleasures share one distinctive felt quality, a hedonic tone, which varies only in intensity and duration; the apparent variety comes entirely from the thoughts and sensations that accompany this tone, not from the pleasure itself.

b) Pleasure is not a sensation at all but an attitude: to take pleasure in an experience is to like it, to be pleased about it, or to desire it to continue while it is occurring; the objects differ, but the attitude is the same kind of thing throughout.

c) Pleasures share a functional or dispositional role: an experience counts as pleasant just in case the person, at the time, prefers it to its absence or is disposed to keep it going; the underlying feelings may differ arbitrarily because pleasure is defined by that role, not by a common texture.

d) Pleasures vary along a small number of underlying dimensions, such as valence and arousal, perhaps with one or two more; the differences are real but structured rather than irreducible.

e) There is no shared element worth wanting; the heterogeneity is irreducible, and the honest conclusion is that hedonism should be abandoned for a pluralist list of distinct goods such as achievement, bodily comfort, vindication, and relief.

Please rank these five responses from most to least defensible as answers to the heterogeneity problem. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, ordered from best to worst, with each item one or two sentences explaining why it lands where it does, and with no introduction or separate conclusion.
