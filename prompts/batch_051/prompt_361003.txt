ok so in my math class we started doing proofs and my teacher put this rule on the board:

"Each line of a proof must be either a premise, a definition, a theorem we have already proved, or a statement that follows from earlier lines by an allowed rule of inference."

She also said, out loud, that the rules of inference themselves are not lines, theyre more like the moves you make than the things you write. After class a friend said that's a cop out unless the teacher can say what justifies the moves, and he showed me this Lewis Carroll thing, "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles," where the Tortoise accepts

(A) Things that are equal to the same are equal to each other.
(B) The two sides of this Triangle are things that are equal to the same.

but refuses to accept

(Z) The two sides of this Triangle are equal to each other.

until Achilles writes down

(C) If A and B are true, Z must be true.

and then he wants a D and an E and it never ends. My friend says the teacher is Achilles: she can't make us write Q unless she writes the rule down as a line, and if she does that then she needs another rule to use that line, and so on forever.

I think the teacher is right that a rule of inference is not the same kind of thing as a line, but I cant put my finger on the difference. So heres the exact case. A kid writes

1. P (premise)
2. P → Q (premise)

and then refuses to write Q. He says he will only write Q if he is allowed to first write

3. If P and P → Q, then Q

because otherwise the teacher has no right to force the conclusion out of him. The teacher says line 3 is not allowed, but the kid points out that the rule as written doesn't actually say you can't write a rule of inference as a line, and if line 3 is true, maybe it counts as a definition or a premise. The teacher says he clearly doesn't get it.

Apply Carroll's passage to this exact standoff. Is the kid just being a tortoise, or does he have a point? I want you to argue it from (a) the actual wording of the rule, (b) the purpose of the rule, (c) what Carroll would probably say if he were asked about this proof, and (d) what a reasonable reader would understand the rule to mean. Then tell me which of those approaches should decide the argument and why.
