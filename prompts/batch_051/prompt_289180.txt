Friend of mine keeps pushing a very Aunt Hillary line: an ant colony is basically a brain, with each ant as a neuron, the pheromone trails as the connections, and nest-building, raiding, and relocating as cognition. His conclusion is that if brains get to have minds, the colony has one too—just slower, smellier, and made of ants instead of cells. How far does this actually hold? I want the mapping laid out in both directions: what's the ant version of a synapse, a firing rate, a cortical module, a unified subject? Then where it breaks: ants are a lot more autonomous than neurons, they each eat and reproduce on their own, the colony has no skin or skull, and pheromone signals are slow, broadcast, and evaporative rather than private and point-to-point. What does the analogy quietly import from brains—especially the idea of one subject with one experience—and once the disanalogies are on the table, does his conclusion still follow, or is the analogy doing the argumentative work? If the brain is the wrong comparison, what's a better one for what a colony actually is? And keep separated what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on minds and emergence; don't make a contested take sound like consensus.
