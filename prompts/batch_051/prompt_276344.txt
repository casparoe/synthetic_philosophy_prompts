Write a pantoum in which the form's mechanism of interlocking repetition stages the problem of universals rather than merely describing it. The speaker should be a realist about universals, with a nominalist objection voiced somewhere in the weave. Use the pattern precisely (each quatrain's second and fourth lines becoming the next quatrain's first and third): let lines naming a quality shared by many particular things be the ones that recur verbatim in new stanzaic contexts, as the realist says one and the same universal recurs in numerically distinct particulars, while lines naming bare particulars occur once and stay anchored to their objects. Have the nominalist interrupt to object that each recurrence is only a different token, a different line on a different page, resembling the others without anything identical being carried over; then have the closing quatrain, where the poem's first and third lines return, reply by enacting the one-over-many argument through the form's own return rather than by stating it. A philosopher working on universals should recognise the position from what the poem does, and a reader new to the problem should feel the pull of the question as well as the argument. Write in British English.
