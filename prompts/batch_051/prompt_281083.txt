Two people in my lab — a comparative psychologist who treats Morgan's canon as basically sound, and a cognitive ethologist who thinks the canon has done real damage to the field — have finaly agreed to stop arguing in the comments and do an adversarial collaboration instead. The first one, call him the minimalist, holds that you shouldn't explain an animal behavior with a fancy psychological capacity if a lower, simpler one is available, and that this is just ordinary scientific parsimony applied to minds. He brings up Clever Hans every time. The second one, call her the attributionist, holds that the canon as actually used is a stacked deck: the "lower" explanation is assumed to be simpler even when spelling it out takes a huge list of brittle rules, the line between higher and lower keeps moving because once an ability is accepted it gets quietly reclassified as lower, so the canon never has to lose a case. She also thinks the asymmetry is the scandal: nobody demands that the lower explanation show its own evidence — it just gets the benefit of the doubt. They've burned months on the same jay experiment, the one where a bird re-hides food only when another bird watched it hide it the first time.

They've agreed the deliverable is a joint statement both will sign. Draft it. I want:

1. Agreed claims (~150 words): the things both can sign about what parsimony requires, about the history of animal cognition research, and about what would count as progress. Push these past platitudes.

2. Disagreement table (~200 words): the remaining disputes, each phrased in neutral wording both accept, with a column for each side's exact wording of their own view. No slanted verbs.

3. Cruxes (~120 words): the sub-questions such that, if one side changed its mind on one, they'd have to move on the main dispute. I suspect one crux is whether there's a principled scale of higher-to-lower faculties, and another is whether false attributions and false denials are equally bad. Tell me if there are others.

4. Tests and predictions (~250 words): for each crux, a concrete case or kind of evidence both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction written down in advance. Clever Hans is one obvious case; give me at least one more that isn't a horse.

5. Verbal vs real (~100 words): which parts of their long argument were really about words, and which are substantive, and why.

Don't bother with citations; they want the document, not the bibliography.
