I have a policy with evolutionary psychology: unless the person explaining a behavior by natural selection can tell me, before seeing the data, what observation would have killed the story, I don't update. Not even a little. So when someone tells me men are wired to fear sexual infidelity more than emotional infidelity, or that we're fat because sugar was scarce on the savanna, I nod and move on. My friend says this is unfair to a historical science—you can't run an experiment on the Pleistocene, and I dont hold geology or cosmology to the same standard even though nobody watched plate tectonics happen either. He also says some evo psych hypotheses do stick their neck out, with checkable predictions about things like spatial cognition or hormone changes and mate preferences, so my rule is both too strict and factually wrong about the field. Is my policy defensible? If it isnt, what's the closest policy that is? I want to keep the real worry—that an adaptive story can feel satisfying, fit everything, and still be a post-hoc just-so story—without giving myself a permanent excuse to dismiss a whole science.
