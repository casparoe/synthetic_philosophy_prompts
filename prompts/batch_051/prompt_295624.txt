Right, I'm new to philosophy of religion proper—most of my working life was in materials science, so I keep wanting a specification rather than a survey. I've started reading around the old dispute about whether God can suffer (divine impassibility vs. passibility, as I now know to call it), and what bothers me is that everyone argues for one side by leaning on a single favourite case: the flood story where God regrets making humankind, the golden calf episode where God is said to change his mind, the crucifixion, the cry of dereliction, the problem of evil, whatever. Before I read another article, I want the full spec.

So: what would an adequate account of whether, and in what sense, God suffers have to get right? Give me the requirements list, not a survey. For each requirement, say what the case or consideration behind it is, and mark whether it is non-negotiable for any serious account or just a commitment of one camp (classical theism's immutability, the passibilist's pastoral worry that an impassible God can't love in any way I recognise, and so on). I especialy want you to flag any subset of the requirements that can't be jointly satisfied as far as you can tell—in my old line of work an inconsistent spec is often the most informative thing you can have, because it tells you the problem was misdescribed.

There are some distinctions I don't want a theory to be allowed to collapse without an argument: between suffering and merely being affected, between a passive disturbance and a settled disposition of love or will, between "cannot suffer" and "chooses not to", and between the divine nature and the human nature in Christ—or, if a theory denies that last distinction does the work, it has to say so and take the consequences, not just ignore the cross. And then, briefly: which single requirement does the most to exclude the usual positions—classical theism, open theism, process thought, and whatever the passibilist-but-not-process crowd get called? I don't need a scorecard, and I don't need footnotes or a reading list; the criteria are what interest me.
