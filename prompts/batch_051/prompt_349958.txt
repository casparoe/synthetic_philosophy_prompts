Analyze the question of who started the Cold War as a case study in how historians assign blame. I already know the rough timeline, so don't summarize it for me: the wartime alliance breaks down, the Soviets turn Eastern Europe into a bloc, the US drops the atomic bombs and then rolls out the Marshall Plan and NATO, Germany gets divided. What I want is for you to treat this as a problem in the theory of blame, not a history quiz. Pick two or three rival accounts of how to assign responsibility for a big messy outcome — for instance, a counterfactual account (you blame the actor whose different choice would most plausibly have avoided the break), an intentionalist account (you blame whoever most deliberately pursued the split), and a structural account (you blame the distribution of power, not any particular decision). Run each account over the period from roughly 1945 to 1950 and say who or what it blames, and what in the record it has to emphasize to get that answer. Then turn around and say where each account distorts or flattens the history: what it has to downplay, ignore, or stretch. Finally, take a stand on whether "who started it" is even a well-posed question for this case — if it is, say which account you think gets closest and why; if it isnt, say what the better question would be and why that question does more work. Don't end on the fence.
