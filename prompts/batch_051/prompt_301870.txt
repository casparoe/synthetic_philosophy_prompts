I am a philosopher of mind whose first career was in distributed systems, and I still find that older vocabulary more precise than the standard philosophical idiom when I think about identity over time. I would like the debate over mind uploading and gradual replacement restated in that vocabulary. By that debate I mean the question whether you would survive destructive whole-brain emulation—a scan of the brain, destruction of the original, and then execution of the emulation—and, separately, whether you would survive the gradual replacement of your neurons by functionally equivalent artificial units. The standard positions appeal to psychological continuity, biological continuity, or the preservation of a pattern. Restate those positions one by one in the vocabulary of processes, state, execution histories, live migration, checkpoint and restore, forking, and host substitution. I am fluent in that vocabulary, so use it precisely, but do not let it quietly import its own answers. For each position, say what translates cleanly, what is distorted in the translation, and what has no expression at all in systems language. Then give me the residue report: is there anything the philosophers are arguing about that has no counterpart in that vocabulary—phenomenal consciousness is my working example—and is the reason that the vocabulary is missing something the debate genuinely needs, or that the dispute looks less substantive once translated? Finally, tell me what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would most plausibly lead you to reverse the verdict you give on the residue, and where in your translation you are least confident.
