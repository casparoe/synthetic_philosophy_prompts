Veterans' organizations, above all the Air Force Association and the American Legion, attacked the planned script as biased and unpatriotic, objecting especially to its attention to Japanese casualties and to its treatment of the decision to use the bomb. Congress held hearings and members threatened the museum's funding. The museum revised the script repeatedly, its director Martin Harwit resigned under pressure in 1995, and the exhibition that finally opened that summer displayed the aircraft's forward fuselage with little more than an identifying label and almost no historical interpretation.
