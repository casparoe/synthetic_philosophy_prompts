I read old maths and physics papers for fun, and I keep catching myself doing the same thing without having a word for it. When I pick up, say, Maxwell's electromagnetic papers or Euler's work on mechanics, I automatically rewrite the argument in modern notation before I try to follow it. Some of that is obviously a convenience; nobody wants to work through 18th-century algebra for its own sake. But I've come to suspect the translation isn't neutral. After I've rewritten an argument, I find myself criticising the author for not seeing the structure that my own rewriting just imposed on the thing, and I stop noticing the questions the author was actually trying to answer, because in modern notation those questions hardly look like questions at all. I know 'presentism' and 'Whig history' are somewhere nearby, but they both seem too broad: I'm after the specific practice of silently modernising an old argument and then judging the original by the modernised version. Does that have an established name of its own? If not, what would you call it, and how would you separate it from plain presentism on one side and from the harmless use of modern notation on the other? I'm not after just a label — I want to know where the edges of the thing are.
