I've been reading Aristotle's On the Soul in a small class, and the bit that keeps nagging me is his claim that thinking never happens without a phantasma—roughly, an image—and that phantasia is an intermediary capacity between perception and thought. Now I keep wondering what Aristotle would have made of people with congenital aphantasia, who report no conscious mental imagery and yet can reason, do math, and plan without any obvious deficit. Would he bite the bullet and say they must have phantasmata they cant introspect? Would he dismiss the condition as misreported? Or would he have to weaken the no-image thesis, and if so, how much of the surrounding machinery in On the Soul has to move with it? Give me your careful guess, not a summary of what he actually said.
