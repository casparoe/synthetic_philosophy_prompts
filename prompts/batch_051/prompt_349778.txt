A working group in the philosophy of cognitive science has asked each member to write down, as candidly as possible, the set of commitments that structure their views on mental modularity, so the group can test whether those commitments hang together. Here is my current set.

I accept Fodor's criteria for what makes a system genuinely modular—informational encapsulation, domain specificity, mandatoriness, speed, and fixed neural realization—and I take input systems such as early vision and language parsing to satisfy them.

I regard the deepest argument for modularity as computational: a domain-general, unencapsulated reasoner would have to survey everything it knows to determine what bears on the present task, and would be paralyzed by the problem of relevance, often called the frame problem. Only by restricting what information a mechanism can access does real-time cognition become tractable.

For this reason I am drawn to massive modularity about much of so-called higher cognition. Capacities such as mindreading, cheater detection, intuitive physics, and folk biology are, I believe, best modeled as content-specific computational systems rather than as applications of a single general-purpose reasoner.

At the same time, I cannot see how to extend this to mature scientific and philosophical reasoning. Theory choice in these domains looks irreducibly isotropic and Quinean to me: there is no domain-bounded list of what might turn out to be relevant, and revision can in principle propagate anywhere in the belief network.

Finally, I hold that perception is cognitively penetrated in ways that matter. Expectation, motivation, and prior knowledge alter what is seen before anything I would call central judgment, and these effects are more than noise in the output.

I would like an audit of this set rather than a survey of the debate. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and how severe is each conflict? Which pairs merely appear to conflict and can be reconciled by drawing distinctions or dividing cognitive labor? For each genuine tension, what is the cheapest repair, and what independent cost would it carry? I would also like to know which single revision would leave the whole set most coherent.
