I am a novelist, not a philosopher, but I have run into a philosophical problem while building a character and would be grateful for help with it. The character spends years persuading herself that she is content in a marriage she in fact wants to leave, and I want the mechanism to be psychologically credible rather than melodramatic. While trying to understand what such a person is doing, I keep coming back to an argument, partly my own and partly from a philosopher I once asked, that seems to show the thing itself is impossible. I would like you to tell me whether the argument goes through, and if it does not, exactly which step fails and how that step should be corrected.

The argument runs as follows.

(1) To deceive someone is intentionally to bring it about that the other person acquires, or continues to hold, a belief that the deceiver regards as false.

(2) In self-deception the deceiver and the deceived are one and the same person. If I deceive myself into believing that the marriage is sound, then I intentionally bring it about that I acquire a belief that I myself regard as false.

(3) But I cannot intentionally bring it about that I acquire a belief I regard as false. Deliberately bringing a belief about would require me to adopt the proposition as true at the very time I treat it as false, and a person cannot accept and reject one and the same proposition in a single episode of mind. No one can simply decide to believe what she judges not to be so.

(4) Therefore self-deception is impossible, and what we call self-deception must in fact be something else: non-intentional wishful thinking, biased handling of evidence, a change of mind, or the illicit splitting of one person into two.

The conclusion strikes me as too strong, since people plainly manage something that deserves the name. Yet each premise, taken on its own, seems nearly unavoidable, and I cannot see how to break the chain. If there is a failure I suspect it is in step (3), but that is little more than a hunch. Have I been tricked? Say precisely which step, if any, fails, and provide the corrected version of that step; if the argument is sound as stated, tell me exactly what it establishes and what it leaves untouched.

Please write your answer in continuous prose, as you would a careful letter or email, without headings, bullet points, bold type, or tables.
