I work for a state sentencing commission, and we keep hitting the same fight every time we revise the guidelines: two people do the same dangerous thing—drive drunk, carry a gun, throw a punch—but one causes a death and the other doesn't. The current ranges treat one as a minor offense and the other as a major felony, and I can't tell whether that's a defensible use of outcome information or just moral luck dressed up in a sentencing table. I want a decision procedure my working group can actually run when we set a range: when, if ever, may resulting harm change the sentence, and when must we ignore it? It should separate severity of the conduct from severity of the outcome, it should be usable by non-philosophers who don't care about the control principle, and it should mark the steps where the procedure stops being a procedure and becomes a moral judgment call. Then apply it to three cases: attempted murder versus completed murder, a DUI near-miss versus a fatal DUI, and a reckless punch that happens to kill someone. Make sure at least one application gives an answer the commission will hate, and be honest about what the procedure can't do.
