Courts routinely treat a person who clicks a button labelled 'I agree' on a website as bound by the provider's standard terms, even though it is common knowledge that almost no one reads those terms and even though the same person, asked in advance, would have refused several of the clauses. Yet the same courts maintain that contractual obligation requires mutual assent, and that no one is bound by terms to which they did not agree. What theory of agreement does this practice presuppose? Does it presuppose that agreement is an act one can perform by a suitably conspicuous gesture, such as clicking a clearly labelled button, rather than a mental state whose content tracks the terms; or that a person is reasonably deemed to have read and accepted whatever was made available; or something else? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguishing what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and state what would have to change in the law of standard-form online contracts if agreement were instead taken to require actual reading or comprehension of every term.
