I've been poking at the old argument about whether DNA is literally a code or whether calling the genome "information" is just a metaphor that got way out of hand, and what strikes me is how little the debate has moved in fifty years even though the molecular facts aren't in dispute. Everyone agrees about base pairing, transcription, translation, the codon table, the fact that a single nucleotide change sometimes wrecks a protein and sometimes does nothing. Yet one camp says DNA carries semantic information in something like the way a blueprint or a sentence does, another says "information" here is at most a statistical measure of covariation — the kind of thing you could apply just as easily to smoke and fire — and a third says DNA isn't informational in any interesting sense at all, just a set of causes with particular effects. My working hunch is that the impasse isn't a disagreement about biology. I suspect the sides are using "information" for different things and each one thinks the other is either sneaking in intentional language or draining the concept of everything that made it interesting.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Diagnose it rather than adjudicate it: is it verbal, is it substantive but about something other than molecules (say, about what a code has to be before the word applies), or are the sides really asking different questions — one about the actual causal mechanics of gene expression, another about how biologists talk, model, and reason with sequence data? Be concrete about what each side is committed to. And then say what would count as progress: a distinction that reframes the question, a definitional fix, an empirical result, or an honest case that the question was ill-posed from the start.

1. The positions, compressed (about 150 words) — restate the causal/statistical, semantic, and deflationary takes on DNA as code or information, and what each one is really trying to preserve.
2. Diagnosis (about 250 words) — what kind of disagreement this is and why it has stalled; if several kinds of disagreement are tangled together, say which one is doing the real work.
3. What would move it (about 200 words) — the distinctions, reframings, or findings that would count as progress given the diagnosis, or why none should be expected.
