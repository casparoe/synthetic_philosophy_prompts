I have recently joined a small volunteer committee charged with allocating a modest fund among several community programs, and the experience has made vivid a pattern I had previously noticed only in family arguments. In both settings, when two people hold conflicting moral views, each tends to describe the other's conviction as a bias while reserving words like value, considered judgment, or legitimate preference for their own. For instance, one committee member argues that we should fund the program he has seen succeed with his own eyes; another calls this an availability bias. He replies that her insistence on formal outcome metrics is itself a bias toward what is easily measured, and that she is ignoring the kind of knowledge only direct involvement provides. A similar exchange occurs in my family: one relative holds that we owe far more to those near us and to our own community; another calls this parochialism or in-group bias; the first replies that the cosmopolitan outlook is itself a bias against the particular and the local. Each side applies the vocabulary of bias to the other with ease, and each side believes its own view merely reflects what matters.

What troubles me is that the word bias seems to do no genuine work in these exchanges. To call a moral judgment biased seems to presuppose some baseline of what an unbiased person would judge, but in a genuinely contested moral question there is no agreed baseline, so the accusation can be mirrored indefinitely. Yet I cannot bring myself to conclude that the distinction is empty, because some judgments plainly are distortions and some plainly are legitimate moral priorities. I would like to know whether this phenomenon — the ready conversion of moral disagreement into mutual accusations of bias, together with the difficulty of saying what makes a moral judgment a bias rather than a preference — has a name, or whether it is discussed under some established concept I do not know. If it is, I want more than the label: I want to know precisely how the nearest concept carves the boundary between a moral bias and a moral preference, and where my description fits it or fails to fit it. If no established concept captures it, I would like a candidate name and definition, with its boundary cases made explicit.
