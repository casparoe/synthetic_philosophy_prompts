i'm on the board of our city arts commission, and next week we decide whether to fund a proposal from a Black artist collective for a mural on the side of the municipal services building. the application says right on the first page that the mural is propaganda: it depicts a specific ongoing fight over a housing development and police budget line-items, and it names two sitting councilmembers as the villains. the board is split. half of us say the commission's mandate is to fund art, not advocacy, that an explicitly propagandistic project turns us into a political organ, and that the mayor will defund us if we aprove. the other half, led by a member who has been rereading Du Bois's 1920s writings on art, argues that the 'art for art's sake' standard is itself a political position, that Du Bois's whole point was that art always propagandizes for something—either for the existing order or against it—and that a commission that has quietly funded public art celebrating city history and business development was never neutral to begin with. i need to circulate a short writen opinion before thursday's meeting. draft it: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with reasons both factions can actually respond to. engage Du Bois's position seriously, not as a curiosity from the archive, and make the bottom line actionable—if you recommend conditions, say exactly what they are and who enforces them.
