So me and my friend spent the whole afternoon going back and forth about Boethius on divine foreknowledge, and I want a second opinion. You know the standard move. God doesn't know tomorrow from inside time; he's outside time entirely, so he sees your whole life at once, the way someone standing on a hill sees the whole road while a traveler only sees one bend at a time. And seeing someone stuck at a bend doesn't force them to be there, so God seeing your choices doesn't force them either. Free will is saved. Fine.

But then my friend pulled this on me. If God sees the whole road at once, doesn't that mean the whole road is already there for him to see? Not that the future is open and God just has a magic telescope, but that every moment of time is equally real, all the points on the line already laid out. That's the block universe picture, not a theistic add-on. And if tomorrow is already real, sitting up ahead on the line, then it's already fixed what I'll do tomorrow — and now it isn't God's knowing that does the fixing, it's the block itself. So maybe Boethius' solution doesn't rescue freedom at all; it just swaps one threat for another. Replace God's foreknowledge with the future already being there anyway, and you still can't do otherwise.

So my question is wether that swap actually matters. Does the Boethian have to sign onto the block universe picture, or could an atemporal God somehow see an open future? And if all times are equally real, is that by itself enough to give you fatalism even with God never mentioned? I'm trying to figure out whether this variation just relocates the original problem or actually dissolves it.
