I’ve been reading Popper’s The Poverty of Historicism because I’m outlining a novel set fifteen years from now, when a tech company claims its model can predict the collapse of nation-states from historical data, and I want the characters to argue about it properly. Popper thought there couldn’t be laws of history in the same way there are laws of physics, because the growth of knowledge itself changes the course of history in ways we can’t predict before they happen. But he was writing in the 1940s and 1950s, before machine learning and all this. If he were alive today and saw a large language model trained on centuries of economic, social, and political data, and the company was selling probabilistic forecasts—not iron laws, just conditional probabilities—how do you think he would react? Would he call that historicism? Or would he say it’s fine as long as it doesn’t pretend to be a law of historical development? I’m trying to figure out where his line actually was, because my villian wants to say Popper’s argument only applies to grand teleological theories like Marxism, and modern predictive models are a different thing entirely. Is that a fair reading of him, or would he be just as hostile to the AI forecasts?
