I edit a general-interest online magazine, and every other month a writer submits an essay that reaches for Gödel's incompletness theorems to settle something about AI or the mind: machines can't be conscious, AI will never do real mathematics, no formal system can be trusted to catch its own errors, that kind of thing. Our copyeditors are sharp but not trained in logic, and they swing between letting howlers through and forwarding everything to me, which annoys the writers. I want a one-page checklist-and-decision-tree they can run every such piece through. It should catch the standard misuses—mixing up the first and second theorems, confusing unprovable in a given system with false or unknowable, skipping the conditions on the theorem (a formal system with a checkable list of axioms, enough arithmetic, consistency), acting as if a Gödel sentence for one system is a Gödel sentence for all possible systems—but it should also say when a Gödel appeal is actually doing real work, so we're not just banning the topic. Give three or four short fake passages from submissions, with a worked verdict on each using the procedure. Make sure one of the worked cases is one where the procedure tells us to keep a Gödel argument I personally find fishy, or to cut one I find impressive—I want the copyeditors following the checklist, not my gut. Finally, mark the cases the checklist can't decide, where it has to come to me. The copyeditor gets about ten minutes per piece and no more than a page. And please don't bother with outside sources or a reading list; the guide should stand on what the theorems actually say.
