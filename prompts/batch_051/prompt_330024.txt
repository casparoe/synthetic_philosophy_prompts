I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so bear with me if this is either obvious or hopeless. For years I've plotted my books on a distinction I made up and never tried to defend out loud. I call it the two ledgers (my other name for it was "door-space vs odds," which is clunkier but maybe clearer).

Here's the theory. At any point in a story, a reader is keeping two separate accounts of what might happen next. One is the probability ledger: how likely each outcome is, given what she knows about real life, the genre, the characters, whatever. The other is the possibility ledger: which outcomes she can actually hold in her head as live continuations — which doors are actually open. My claim is that the two ledgers dont reduce to each other, and in fact they can move almost independently.

Two examples. In a rom-com, it's overwhelmingly probable by genre logic that the protagonist's wisecracking best friend is in love with her; the reader has that at about 0.9 from page one. But a good writer can keep "they end up together" off the possibility ledger for most of the novel — not as an unlikely outcome, but as a non-option, a door that isn't even in the room — and then flip it open in one scene without adding any new evidence that raises its probability. Probability stays around 0.9; what changes is pure possibility. Conversely, a sudden fatal aneurysm in chapter three is statistically possible and has a nonzero real-world chance, but in most stories it's narratively impossible — not because it's unlikely, but because it would make the story stop being the same story.

So far that's just noticing. The part I actually want checked is a stronger principle I've been writing by. I call it the Independence Principle: in a well-built story, the two ledgers can be edited separately. A scene can reorder which doors are open without changing the odds on any door that's already open, and it can shift the odds without opening or closing anything. The only constraint is they can't contradict each other forever: an event kept at high probability but never admitted to the possibility ledger eventually makes the reader feel cheated (she knows X, but she's never allowed to actually entertain X), and an event kept open as possible while its probability collapses makes everything feel arbitrary.

I've started to suspect this isn't just about fiction — that fiction is a clean laboratory where possibility and probability come apart in a way they don't in ordinary deliberation. So: is the distinction coherent, or am I smuggling in a third thing? Does it already exist under a better name (it feels like it must), and where would mine differ from it? And is the Independence Principle actually true — can you think of well-built stories where the two ledgers are locked together, or where my constraint on them is wrong?
