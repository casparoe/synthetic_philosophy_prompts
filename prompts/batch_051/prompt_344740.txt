An adversarial collaboration is being convened between two parties to the debate over philosophical zombies. One party, the conceivabilist, holds that the ideal positive conceivability of zombies—beings physically identical to conscious creatures yet lacking all phenomenal consciousness—entails their metaphysical possibility, and that since physicalism requires the physical truths to necessitate the phenomenal truths, physicalism is false. The other party is a type-B physicalist who grants both the conceivability of zombies and an epistemic gap between physical and phenomenal truths, but denies that this conceivability carries modal weight: phenomenal concepts, which pick out experiences under a first-personal mode of presentation unavailable to physical-functional descriptions, explain why a genuine metaphysical entailment is cognitively opaque. Draft the joint statement this collaboration would issue. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about how zombies are defined, what a physical duplicate fixes, and what each side must show; (2) a statement of the residual disagreement, worded so that each side would accept the description of its own position, with any merely verbal portion of the dispute identified as such; and (3) a list of cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—each paired with the arguments, thought experiments, or theoretical results both sides agree would bear on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Pay particular attention to whether the type-B physicalist's appeal to phenomenal concepts can handle the conceivabilist's two-dimensional semantics arguments without becoming ad hoc, and to what would actually count as evidence against each position. Do not pad the document with declarations about the value of collaboration or with appeals to authority.
