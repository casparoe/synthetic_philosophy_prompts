I have been assisting a colleague who works on the ethics of complicity, and over several months I have collected her offhand verdicts on hypothetical cases. The pattern still escapes me. In every case, the person carrying out the principal wrongdoing is clearly guilty; the only question is whether the third party described is complicit in that wrongdoing or is better described as an unconnected bystander, customer, or coincidental facilitator. Her rulings so far are these:

- A driver who joined a planned bank robbery and drove the robbers away afterward is complicit, even though the robbers had parked their own car two blocks away and would have escaped exactly as well without him.
- A bystander who shouts encouragement during a street assault is not complicit, even if the attacker later reports that the shouting kept him going.
- A locksmith who opens a warehouse door at night for a burglar at his usual fee, knowing perfectly well what the burglar means to do, is complicit.
- An engineer who designed a generic acoustic component that was later incorporated into a device used for torture is not complicit, since she neither knew nor had reason to suspect that downstream use.

Next week she will be asked about a translator who accepts a job rendering a forged financial document into another language, knowing the document is forged and that it will be used to defraud a particular investor, and who genuinely wants the fraud to succeed. As it happens, the target reads the original language fluently and never looks at the translation, which therefore plays no role in the fraud. I want to predict what my colleague will say. Please set out the strongest candidate rules or principles that fit her record so far (there are at least two that survive it), state what each candidate would say about the translator, give a best guess with the reasons for preferring one rule over the other, and then specify the cleanest further case that would separate the surviving candidates. I am less interested in the translator's verdict itself than in the rule she is implicitly applying and in what would settle which one it is.
