I'm in a grad seminar on scientific change and I've built an argument that I suspect proves too much, because it seems to show Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating research programmes is just a way of telling stories, not a rational standard. I really don't believe that, so something's off, but I can't locate the step.

1. Lakatos says a research programme is progressive when each new theory has excess empirical content over the one before it: it predicts some facts the previous theory didn't, and some of those novel predictions get corroborated. It's degenerating when new theories mostly just digest known anomalies by bolting on extra clauses after the fact.

2. But applying that criterion requires deciding whether a fact was "newly predicted" or "accommodated after the fact," and that requires deciding what the earlier version of the theory actually implied — which in turn means deciding which auxiliary assumptions were legitimately in play at the time.

3. That decision isn't forced by the formal content of the theories. Give me any two successive theories and any fact, and with enough ingenuity I can reconstruct the older theory, by quietly adding auxiliaries, so it already implied the fact; or reconstruct the newer theory, by selecting the right revisions, so the fact was just bolted on afterward. So excess empirical content isn't a formal property of theories; it's a property of how we narrate them.

4. Since there's no principled constraint, beyond narrative plausibility, on where we draw that boundary, every research programme can be made to look progressive and every one can be made to look degenerating.

5. Therefore Lakatos hasn't given us a rational standard for when to abandon a programme; he's given us a flexible vocabulary for telling dramatic histories of science.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd like to hang onto the basic Lakatos picture, so I especially want to know which premise a Lakatosian has to reject and what it costs to reject it.

Please give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
