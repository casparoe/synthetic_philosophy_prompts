For a social ontology reading group I need to write a short "where I stand" statement, and I'd like a consistancy check before I share it. Here are the commitments I've ended up with:

(1) Race, gender, and money are all socially constructed in the same basic sense — none of them would exist without our collective attitudes and practices.
(2) Being socially constructed in that way doesn't make something less real, or less objective.
(3) There is no biological or ancestral condition that is necessary or sufficient for being, say, Black or a woman.
(4) For race and gender there is still a fact of the matter about who falls under the kind, fixed by prevailing social classifications rather than any individual's judgment.
(5) I also think, on the strength of passing cases and cases where someone's gender assignment shifts, that what someone is can depend on who is reading them and what they know — and sometimes there just isn't a single answer.
(6) I think racial and gender categories should be eliminated, but I'd still keep money.

Does this package hang together? I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest fix would cost. Please rank the tensions rather than saying it's all equally bad, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most.
