I've been poking around in Durkheim's Elementary Forms of Religious Life, and I keep bumping into his claim that religious thought divides the world into two domains, the sacred and the profane, and treats them as radically seperate in kind — not just a graded difference of intensity. He says the sacred is what the profane must not touch without ritual precautions, and that contamination across the boundary is a big deal. Now compare that to the moral-psychology literature: people often list purity/sanctity as one of the foundations of moral judgment, but a skeptic could say all those judgments are just disgust with a moral gloss. I used to be a physicist, so I want the experimental version, not just more definitions. How would one test the claim that purity/sanctity is a distinct moral value, rather than a post-hoc rationalization of a raw disgust response? Give me a concrete design, what result pattern would count for each side, and what part of the claim no test could settle. Present the final answer as a table with columns: test, operationalization, result favoring 'purity is a moral value', result favoring 'disgust-only', and what it leaves unsettled.
