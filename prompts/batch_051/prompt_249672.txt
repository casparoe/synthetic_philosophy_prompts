Ok so I just started my first job this summer and our employee handbook has this exact line: "Employees are not required to work outside their scheduled hours, and managers may not assign duties during rest periods. Rest periods must be completely free of duties, and any time an employee is required to be available for work counts as working time." The rule was clearly written back when work meant physically being in the building. But everything at my job runs through a group chat now. Every Sunday night around 9:45 my manager posts the new schedule with a message like "everyone confirm you saw this or I'll assume you're not available and give your shifts away." Replying is just a thumbs-up, two seconds, but you basically have to be watching your phone all evening to catch it. A coworker missed it by an hour and lost her shifts.

So I'm not only asking whether the thumbs-up is "work" (though I want to know that too). I want to know whether being expected to monitor the chat during off time means she was "required to be available for work" under the rule. And then what the usual ways of reading a rule actually give here: the plain words, the purpose of the rest-period protection (which is the same impulse as the 35-hour week, that off time should be actually off), what the people who wrote it before smartphones would have said if you asked them, and what a reasonable worker reading it today would think it means. Which reading should win? Also my manager keeps saying its just common sense that good employees reply, and I can't tell if that's a real argument about the rule or just the protestant work ethic sneaking in to make rest sound like laziness. Am I overthinking this?
