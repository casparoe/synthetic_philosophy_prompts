I've been trying to sort out which computations are the kind that can be worse off, and I keep landing on something I call the final-edit criterion. Roughly: don't ask what a system can process, ask whether there is a single editor inside it that can decide what stays in the running summary and what gets dropped. By an editor I don't mean anything fancy — just a process that takes a bunch of lower-level signals, selects some of them into a compressed account, and other parts of the system then act on that account. I want to say a computation has moral standing roughly when that editor has a hold function: it can keep a valenced signal in the summary after the input is gone, and the signal stays there even when keeping it around is not currently rewarded, maybe even when it costs the system something.

So a worm that wriggles off salt has nociception but, I'd argue, no hold: the signal does its one behavioral job and then the machinery moves on. A person with a stubbed toe has a pain representation that persists, keeps intruding into whatever else they are trying to do, and can't just be edited out by later information — it has a sort of veto. A thermostat fails, on my view, because it doesn't select a summary at all; there's no editor, just one state updating directly to another. I know this sounds like global workspace theory, and maybe like attention or working memory, and I also worry it's circular because hold already sounds like what a feeling is.

Here's what I want from you. First, is this coherent enough to argue about, or is it just a metaphor with extra steps? Second, is it actually a version of something that already exists under a better name, and if so what is it closest to? Third — and this is the part I need most — give me the clearest case you can think of where a system passes my test but you'd still be comfortable saying it can't suffer, and the clearest case where something can suffer but my test says it doesn't. Then tell me straight whether the editor/hold distinction is doing real work or whether I should throw it away.
