I keep noticing a kind of idea that nobody argues with and nobody ever passes on, and I don't know if there's a word for it. It isn't secret, false, or useless—often it's a safety insight or a maintenance rule—but it has no hook, no status payoff for repeating it, and it tends to evaporate between the moment someone hears it and the moment they'd actually need it. The stranger cases are worse: the content of the idea is exactly the sort of thing that makes people stop thinking about it, like a finding about how groups underreact to a slowly building risk. I can point to times this happened in a literal meeting—everyone agreed, and two weeks later everyone acted like the discussion never happened—but I can't tell whether that's one phenomenon or two, and whether either has an established name. I know the memetics side has terms for why ideas spread; I'm after the other end. Rather than a bare label, I want the boundaries: what separates an idea that's merely forgettable from one that somehow suppress its own transmission, and is there such a thing as a self-effacing meme that works by lowering its own salience?
