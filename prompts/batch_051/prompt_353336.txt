I was arguing with a friend who does visual neuroscience about whether a brain interface could ever produce a genuinely new colour, and not just some new combination of colours you already have. We kept talking past each other, so I wrote down my working critiera for calling an experience a genuinely novel quale: (a) it has its own qualitative character, not merely a new piece of information or a new behavioural capacity; (b) that character can't be built by mixing, rotating, or reweighting qualia the person already had; (c) the person can notice it and identify it as new rather than inferring it after the fact; and (d) it recurs in a stable way across contexts instead of being a one-off fluke.

Now here's the borderline case he described. Imagine an implant that drives a cell population in the visual system with a pattern no ordinary stimulus ever produces — think of the reddish-green or bluish-yellow "forbidden colours" people sometimes report under carefully arranged afterimage setups, but done chronically and in isolation. The subject starts with normal trichromatic vision, goes through a long calibration period, and then reports an experience she refuses to call red, green, blue, or any mixture she knows. Her phrase is that it is "like seeing in a new direction," not like seeing a new shade. She can sort chips by it reliably, can somewhat imagine it later without the implant running, and the same character shows up for weeks whenever a particular protocol is on. Is this a genuinely new quale on my four criteria, or is she just learning to label a combination of familiar colour signals in a new way? You're a philosopher of mind who works on the qualia debate. Start by brainstorming the strongest deflationary readings of the case, then think carefully and go criterion by criterion, being honest about the places where my criteria simply don't decide the matter. Then say whether the harder problem lies in the criteria themselves — especially (b), which assumes qualia come in units you can mix at all — and finish by telling me what evidence would make you confident in the deflationary story instead.
