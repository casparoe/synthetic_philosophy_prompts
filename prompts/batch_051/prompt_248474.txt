here is where i landed:

(1) death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness, and i think of that capacity as something the whole brain sustains, not just the cortex.
(2) brain death is death. a warm body on a ventilator with a dead brain is a corpse being ventilated.
(3) someone in a permanent vegetative state (cortex gone, brainstem still running breathing and sleep-wake cycles) is alive, and i would be horrified to call them dead.
(4) the dead donor rule is right: you dont take vital organs out of a person who is still alive, even if they consented.
(5) standard donation-after-circulatory-death practice is fine: withdraw life support, wait until the heart has stopped for a few minutes, declare death, then recover the organs. at the moment of recovery, the donor is dead.
(6) death is a biological fact, not something hospital policy or society gets to define.

my friend kept insisting that (5) turns death into a policy call because the wait time is just a number somebody picked, which i dont accept, but thats his objection more than mine.
