I run an incident-review program at a mid-sized software company, and I am weighing a proposal to drop the term "root cause" from our postmortem template. The people behind the proposal argue that "root cause" is not merely vague but actively defective: it frames an incident as having a single initiating source, stops inquiry once one plausible early link is found, and pushes remediation toward a single pointed fix with one owning team. Their suggested replacement is a required "contributing conditions" section that captures the preconditions, failed controls, and amplifying factors that were jointly sufficient for the outage. Their claim is that this is conceptual fixing, not relabeling: the new concept would change what counts as an acceptable explanation, would change how teams act, and that change would be an improvement against the actual purpose of postmortems — surfacing more actionable preconditions, reducing recurrence, and distributing remediation across the system. I've been told this is what philosophers call conceptual engineering, but I have no background in that literature and I don't want to settle it by theory.

My prior is that "this concept is better" is the kind of claim people assert rather than test. I want you to show me otherwise, or show me exactly where the test gives out. Treat the conceptual claim as a live hypothesis about practice and design the strongest empirical test I could plausibly run inside an engineering organization.

I need four things in the prose before the summary:

1. the outcome measures and why they operationalize "improvement" without smuggling in the conclusion;
2. the comparison design, including how to avoid the obvious confound that the replacement also adds structure and process, not just a concept;
3. the result pattern that would support genuine conceptual improvement and the pattern that would support mere relabeling;
4. what the test cannot settle, including any part of the original claim that stays non-empirical no matter how clean the study is.

Then give a concise JSON object with exactly these fields:

{
  "testable_reading_of_the_claim": "restate the claim so that part of it makes contact with observable practice, and flag the part that cannot",
  "test_design": "population, comparison, and procedure in enough detail that I could run it",
  "operational_measures": ["measure and why it tracks the target"],
  "pattern_if_genuine_conceptual_improvement": "the results that would favor the proposal",
  "pattern_if_mere_relabeling": "the results that would favor the skeptical reading",
  "what_a_positive_result_would_show": "the strongest conclusion a positive result licenses",
  "what_a_positive_result_would_not_show": "the part of the original claim a positive result still leaves open",
  "residue_no_test_reaches": ["the conceptual or normative residue that no arrangement of incident data reaches"]
}

Keep the prose tight; the value is in the operationalization and the honesty about its limits.
