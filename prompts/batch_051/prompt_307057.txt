Here's an argument a friend keeps making whenever one of those "I dreamt it and then it happened" stories goes round. He says one solid anecdote of the right kind should rationally convert a total stranger to belief in premonitions. Every step looks fair on its own, but my nose says something goes wrong and I can't pin down where. Walk me through it.

(1) For any particular person and any particular night, the chance that they have a vivid dream about a neighbour dying in a fire, and the neighbour actually dies in a fire the next day, is about one in a million.

(2) If a single outcome has a one-in-a-million chance under the chance hypothesis, and that very outcome then occurs, the chance hypothesis is in serious trouble: a low-probability outcome, specified in advance, is strong evidence against mere chance.

(3) So whenever such a dream-death pair actualy occurs, we should reject the chance hypothesis and conclude that something more than coincidence is going on.

(4) The only non-chance explanation on offer is that the dream was a premonition, since the idea that the dream caused the death is absurd.

(5) Therefore, from the dream-death pair alone, we should very probably believe in a premonition.

(6) A third party who merely reads about the pair in the newspaper has exactly the same facts before them as the dreamer did, so the argument goes through just the same for them. A single reliable anecdote of this kind should therefore convince a rational sceptic.

Tell me whether the argument is valid, and if not, exactly which step fails and under what reading. I have a suspicion the birthday paradox is relevant here, so say whether it actually is and how it bears on the case. Answer in British English, please.
