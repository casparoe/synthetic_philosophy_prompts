Act as a philosopher of science who also knows ML systems; think carefully and start by brainstorming a few candidate tests before committing to one.

I'm a backend engineer who works on anomaly detection, and there's a distinction we toss around constantly but never pin down: two alerts firing because of the same underlying mechanism, versus two alerts that just produce the same symptoms. As if "same mechanism" is a real feature of the system and not just a modeling choice. Example: a memory leak and a misconfigured cache can both look like latency goes up and then plateaus — same signature, but is the mechanism the same?

Design the best test you can of the claim that two anomalies genuinely share a mechanism rather than merely sharing a symptom/signature. Be concrete: what data do you collect, what intervention or experiment do you run, and what result would count as evidence for "same mechanism" versus "different mechanisms that happen to look alike"? Then say what the test could never settle even in principle — e.g., whether sameness of mechanism is just a choice of abstraction level, or whether the same bug running on two services counts as one mechanism or two. Dont just gesture at causality; make the test operational.
