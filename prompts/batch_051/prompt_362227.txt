So I got into it with a friend last night about sex work laws — he's very into the Nordic model, clients criminalised and sellers left alone, because he thinks it shrinks demand without punishing the vulnerable party; I was arguing that in practice it still pushes everything into dingy cars and hotel rooms and makes workers afraid to call the cops, and that New Zealand-style full decriminalisation is the only honest option. He kept bringing up Germany and the Netherlands as evidence that "legalization" means big pimp-run mega-brothels, and I kept saying that's a different animal from decrim. We went in circles.

After he left I kept chewing on it and came up with a scheme I want someone to stress-test. Suppose a jurisdiction fully decriminalises the sale and purchase of sex between adults (including street solicitation, as in New Zealand), but instead of leaving third parties to the ordinary market it says: any advertising platform, booking app, or payment processor whose main revenue is from sexual services must be structured as a nonprofit "safety trust" run by sex workers. The trust can host ads, process payments, and rent rooms, but it can't take profits beyond cost recovery and staff salaries, it has to let workers delete or edit their own ads instantly, and it holds client identity data in escrow: the data is destroyed after 60 days and only ever turned over to police with a warrant tied to a specific reported assault, robbery, or trafficking complaint. Individuals who operate entirely cash-only and off-platform are free to do so, and a bank or landlord can't close an account or evict someone soley on the grounds that they do sex work.

What I'm trying to figure out is how this would actually behave in practice, not whether it sounds nice. Who ends up on-platform vs. off-platform, what does the escrow do to screening and to a worker's willingness to report a violent client, where do the clients who refuse to be ID'd go, does "nonprofit" just mean people launder the surpluses through salaries and consultancies, and can the failure modes be fixed without turning the trust into a de facto police surveillance arm? Also, does the escrow even help, or does it just become a honeypot of prosecutable clients under some future Nordic-style law this place explicitly didn't adopt?
