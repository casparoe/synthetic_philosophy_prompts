Recently I was reading about Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, and I noticed a parallel that I cannot assess on my own. As I understand the view, created things have no genuine causal powers: when fire touches cotton, God produces the burning at that moment, and the fire itself does not cause the burning. Creation is sustained moment by moment, and what we call a causal connection is really just God's habitual way of arranging successive states. This reminded me of a discrete-time computer simulation. Inside the simulation, a ball does not persist between frames in any substantive way, and a collision does not cause the next state; the program simply computes each frame from the previous one. Apparent causal laws are patterns in a sequence that something external is generating step by step.

I am trying to decide whether this parallel is deep or superficial. The obvious problem is that, in the simulation, the next state follows from the previous state by a fixed deterministic rule, whereas Al-Ghazālī denies that the order of nature binds God or follows necessarily from any created thing; God always could have done otherwise. Is that difference fatal, or could a deterministic update rule simply play the role of a freely chosen, revocable habit? Are there other places where the mapping breaks, and does anything transfer from one side to the other? If the comparison is confused or artificially stretched, I would rather be told that plainly than have it polished into a false parallel.
