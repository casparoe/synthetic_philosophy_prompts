I am a novelist, and I want a working definition of resilience — precise enough to tell me, across the characters I invent, when the word is doing honest descriptive work and when it is doing something else. Please define it so that it correctly classifies the following:

- A soldier who advances under fire because drill has made the action reflexive and no thought of danger occurs.
- A bereaved parent who keeps working and raising children and slowly regains enjoyment without ever getting over the loss.
- Someone who bounces back quickly from every setback only because she never revises her self-image and always blames someone else.
- A community under occupation that keeps its language, weddings, and festivals partly as an act of refusal.
- A founder who persists for years in a failing venture, piling up debt, because quitting feels like self-betrayal.
- A worker who absorbs constant overwork and describes herself as resilient in a review that treats the workload as unchangeable.
- Someone who weathers a crisis well mainly because of money, family, and luck rather than any quality of his own.
- A child in a chaotic household who shows no distress at the time but later says he was not resilient, only numb.

To anchor the exercise: the bereaved parent strikes me as plainly resilient and the reflexive soldier as plainly not; I have no stable view about the child.

Then say which of your verdicts is least secure.
