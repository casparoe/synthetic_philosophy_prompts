I'm presenting at a well-being workshop next month and I'm defending a version of the paradox of hedonism that I think people get wrong. My thesis is that the paradox, as it shows up in Sidgwick's The Methods of Ethics, is a real problem only for what I call directive hedonism — the idea that you should aim directly at maximizing your own pleasant experience — and not for criterion hedonism, where pleasure is just the standard by which we evaluate outcomes. I'm hanging a lot on a passage in Book One, Chapter Four where Sidgwick says, roughly, that the moment you make your own maximum pleasure the conscious object of your pursuit, you alter the conditions under which pleasure arises and tend to lose it. I want to say that passage isn't making a merely empirical claim about human psychology — it's showing that happiness is a byproduct of engagement with things we value for their own sake, and that aiming at it directly changes the intentional structure in a way that undercuts itself. The audience is a philosophy department workshop, mostly people who work on well-being and moral psychology. There's a cluster of neo-Aristotelians who will think I should just be a eudaimonist and stop trying to save hedonism at any level; two committed experientialists who'll say the paradox is overstated and that you can aim at happiness indirectly by focusing on activities you enjoy without giving up directive hedonism; a desire-satisfaction theorist who'll say all of this is at the wrong level and the real issue is whether hedonism is the correct criterion, not whether it's a good rule of thumb; and one hard-nosed utilitarian who'll say the paradox doesn't touch hedonism as a criterion of rightness and that's all a utilitarian needs. Give me a prep sheet, not a defense: the objections I'll actually get, ordered by likelihood and damage, each phrased the way that person would phrase it, with a short reply for each and a note on where the reply is weakest. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. I want you to commit: pick a definate view about which objection is most likely to sink the paper and what I should concede up front, rather than just laying out options.
