I run my high school’s philosophy club, which has about fourteen junior and senior members and meets for one hour. We are about to take up Dennett’s argument that qualia — the supposed private “what it is like” of experience — are an incoherent posit. The part I find hardest to convey is the thought experiment with the two coffee tasters: each used to like the same coffee and now dislikes it, but one says the flavor itself has changed while the other says the flavor is exactly the same and only his preferences changed, and there seems to be no evidence, even from the taster’s own introspection, that could decide between them. I want the club to run into that problem themselves before “qualia” or Dennett’s conclusion are named, and to come away seeing that the distinction between a changed experience and a changed reaction is underdetermined by everything they can observe or introspect. Please design a runnable activity for this group — real tasting if it helps, but the format is up to you — that forces participants to confront “did my experience change, or only my reaction to it?” from the inside. Include the setup and rules (or roles), a timeline for the hour, a debrief that maps specific moments of the activity onto Dennett’s argument, what will predictably go wrong when it is run and how to handle it, and an honest note on which part of the argument the activity cannot reproduce and that I would have to supply.
