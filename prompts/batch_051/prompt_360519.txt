I keep returning to a configuration in the aftermath of the Second World War and I do not have a precise term for it. At Nuremberg and Tokyo the victorious powers established tribunals to try the defeated leadership for conduct — launching aggressive war, atrocities against civilian populations, and related acts — that was not, or at least not clearly, criminal under the law in force when the acts were committed. The tribunals answered the defense's objection that there can be no punishment without a prior penal law partly by arguing that earlier treaties and custom had already made the conduct unlawful; but the charters that defined the offenses were drafted after the events, by the victors themselves, and the courts' jurisdiction stopped neatly at the losing side. The same powers did not subject their own comparable wartime conduct to the same standards.

I have heard this configuration called victor's justice, but I suspect that phrase lumps together several distinct defects and that using it too quickly obscures more than it captures. I want three things. First, is there an established concept or name for the configuration, and does that concept name one defect or a family of them? Second, how exactly should one separate the retroactivity objection — the law applied was not in force at the time of the acts — from the selectivity objection — the law, whatever its content, was applied only to the defeated side? Are these two independent defects, or does each draw its force only from the other? Put sharply: if a tribunal applied a fully retroactive criminal law but applied it evenhandedly, convicting its own side's comparable acts alongside the enemy's, would the retroactivity objection still have force? And conversely, if a tribunal applied only non-retroactive positive law but was staffed by one side's officials and reached only the other side's defendants, would anything still be defective in it? Third, is there a residual complaint — about the victors sitting in judgment at all — that survives even a complete cure of both the retroactivity and the selectivity problems? I want the phenomenon carved so that each of these considerations finds its proper weight, whatever the labels turn out to be.
