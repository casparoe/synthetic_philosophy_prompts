Write me a dialogue between an Orwellian and a Stalinesque about what's actually happening in the color phi setup: a red flash, then a green flash a few degrees away, and the subject reports seeing one moving dot that turns green about halfway across. The Orwellian thinks consciousness plays out in real time and the report gets overwritten afterward in memory; the Stalinesque thinks the brain edits the stream before it ever reaches consciousness, so the subject accurately reports an already-reconstructed experience. Have them actually argue with each other about whether any conceivable neural data could pull these two stories apart, and make each one say what they mean by the time of the experience once both stories predict exactly the same report. Neither should be a pushover, and each should restate the other's strongest objection before answering it. Keep the back-and-forth going until they hit the point where the distinction either collapses or turns on a hidden assumption one of them is smuggling in.
