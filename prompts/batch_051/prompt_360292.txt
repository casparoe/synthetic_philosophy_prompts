After retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have been reading philosophy of time, and I keep returning to the long-running dispute between presentists and eternalists over the block universe. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument, as I understand it, runs roughly like this. Special relativity denies absolute simultaneity: for any two spacelike-separated events, some inertial frame will judge them simultaneous. Different frames therefore identify different distant events as occurring "now." If presentism is right, then what exists is whatever is simultaneous with me; but because simultaneity is frame-relative, presentism would make existence itself frame-relative. Since existence cannot plausibly depend on the choice of an inertial frame, the argument concludes that existence should not be tied to any observer's present — and the eternalists take this to be the entering wedge of the block universe. The presentists reply that the argument quietly assumes that frame-relative simultaneity settles what exists, an assumption they reject; some also hold that a future physical theory may supply a preferred foliation and an objective present after all. Yet both sides accept the same physics — Lorentz symmetry, the absence of superluminal signaling, and the same experimental outcomes — and neither camp has moved in decades.

This strikes me as a dispute in which the empirical facts are settled and the real disagreement lies elsewhere. My working hypothesis is that it is ultimately a disagreement about what a physical theory must show before it commits us to the existence of something: the presentist refuses to let a symmetry argument force an ontology, while the eternalist regards that refusal as special pleading. But I can also see it as a verbal dispute over the word "real," or a bedrock difference of intuition about time that no formal result could touch. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this, exactly? Is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing genuine work — for example, whether a viable quantum theory of gravity will supply a preferred foliation — or is that a red herring? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a theorem, an experiment, a change of question, or no progress at all?
