The view I'm tempted by instead says a virtual object can be a vehicle for perfectly real interests — labor, self-expression, reputation, attachment — and a virtual harm is real exactly when it sets back one of those real-world interests through the object. Call that the interest-vehicle view.

In Case 2, another player does something similar, and the interest-vehicle view says it's not a moral harm — just an in-game setback.
