I'm a PhD student in media studies, and I sit on the trust & safety advisory board for a mid-sized video platform. We're reviewing an appeal from a researcher in disinformation studies whose account was suspended after she posted a 40-second clip from a state-run disinformation campaign — the video falsely claims the opposition party bussed in fake voters during last year's election. She didn't post it raw: she added a commentary overlay, a visible "manipulated media" warning, and a link to her peer-reviewed debunk in the caption. Our policy has a flat ban on "reproducing media produced by known disinformation networks," with no exception for critique, and the automated system flagged it before any human looked. Some of us think we're punishing exactly the people who could inoculate viewers against the original. Others say an unblurred copy is still an unblurred copy, our classifiers can't tell the difference between her post and the propaganda, and the board has never had to handle a case quiet like this.

Draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday: recommend one concrete course of action — uphold, restore unconditionally, or restore with conditions (blurring, audio-muting, viewer limits, labeling, whatever you think fits) — with a time frame for any conditions, and give a fair hearing to the strongest argument for the side you're recommending against rather than just restating our policy. Format it as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
