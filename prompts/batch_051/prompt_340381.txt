Suppose that next year a cheap, legal machine becomes widely available that can make a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of any physical object, with one constraint: the duplication process destroys the original. The duplicate is indistinguishable from the original by every physical test, and the only difference between the two is causal history; the machine also leaves a tamper-proof public record that this object is a duplicate and that the original was destroyed. Predict, with a visible chain of reasoning, how this changes, over fifty years, the market for unique artworks and historical artifacts, the collecting and display practices of museums and archives, and the law of authenticity and fraud. For each major prediction, say how robust you take it to be, what factual or behavioral assumption it leans on, and what observation would overturn it.
