Two members of my reading group have spent the last term locked in an argument about the proviso in Locke's account of property, and we'd like to write an adversarial-collaboration document so we stop rehearsing the same moves. One of us is a left-libertarian: on her view the earth's resources are owned in common in a fairly strong sense, so whoever fences off a plot of land or monopolises a natural resource has to leave everyone else with a per-capita-equal share of the value of the unimproved resource itself, or something that compensates them for it at competitive market value. The other is a right-libertarian in the Nozickian mould: appropriation is fine as long as it does not leave others worse off than they would have been under a system of no private appropriation at all, and paying people to work the land can count as satisfying the proviso. Both of us accept the Lockean picture of original acquisition via mixing one's labour with unowned things, and both agree the proviso — that one must leave others with resources as plentiful and as good — is important, but we parse it completely differently.

Draft the document we should have produced. I want: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, including claims about what the proviso governs (land and natural resources only, or the full improved value), what the labour-mixing actually establishes, and which baseline matters for saying someone has been made worse off; (2) a disagreements table giving the precise wording of each residual disagreement, with each side's wording acceptable to its own camp; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance, and a note on what each camp would count as evidence or argument against its own position. The case we keep getting stuck on is the single waterhole one: someone appropriates the only waterhole and then offers everyone else jobs carrying its water at a wage better than they managed before the appropriation. One of us thinks this plainly satisfies the proviso and the other thinks it plainly does not, and we can't tell whether that's a real disagreement or two different baselines talking past one another. Also say if you think any part of our dispute is merely verbal. Answer in British English, please.
