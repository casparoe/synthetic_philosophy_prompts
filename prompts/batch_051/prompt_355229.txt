I am a journalist covering the science of expertise. For months I have heard rationality trainers argue that clear thinking is not a body of knowledge but a trained capacity: something closer to a martial art than to a course of study. They say it requires drilled moves, artificial “katas,” and supervised sparring, and that once developed it transfers from, say, medical diagnosis to financial analysis. A friend who teaches this method points to William James on plasticity and habit, but what sticks with me is a different James line, from the preface to The Principles of Psychology: “All attempts to explain our phenomenally given thoughts as products of deeper-lying entities (whether the latter be named ‘Soul,’ ‘Transcendental Ego,’ ‘Ideas,’ or ‘Elementary Units of Consciousness’) are metaphysical.” That sentence seems to draw a line between empirical psychology and something else. I want to know whether the martial-art view of rationality is empirical. Design the strongest realistic test you can imagine for the claim that rationality can be trained like a martial art, with transfer across domains. Be concrete about what the training condition and control would be, what result would count for the claim, what result would count against it, and then say exactly what a positive result would and would not settle. Assume adult subjects with similar education and no prior formal training in the method.
