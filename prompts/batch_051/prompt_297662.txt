I've been keeping a running list of my friend's verdicts on whether a bunch of mental states count as experiences—that is, states with a felt, subjective character, not just states that cause behavior. So far she has said yes to: watching a movie while fully awake; a daydream she got shaken out of and could describe; a lucid dream where the person signaled with pre-arranged eye movements while still asleep; a brief image of a face that flashed just as she was falling asleep, which she was jolted awake by and could describe right then; and a patient who was awake and aware during surgery despite the anesthetic and later could describe actual events from it. She has said no to: a person woken from deep slow-wave sleep who, after some leading questions, produced a story about dreaming; and a sleepwalker who navigated obstacles and afterward remembered nothing. Next week she has to rule on a new case: a man woken from REM sleep gives a detailed report of being chased earlier in the night, but he says he had no awareness of it at the time—when the alarm went off the whole dream seemed to arrive at once as he woke. What should I predict she will say, what priniciple is she actually running, and which further case would cleanly separate the live options?
