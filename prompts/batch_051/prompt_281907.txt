I have been arguing with my older brother about one sharply stated claim: that pure time preference, by which I mean valuing a benefit less solely because it arrives later in time, with its size, probability of occurring, and recipient all held fixed, is never rationally justified. My brother puts his credence at about 0.95. I put mine at about 0.45, mainly because I am unsure whether rational has a determinate meaning here and because I suspect that some things that feel like time preference are actually judgments about how connected my present self is to my future self.

I would like your actual credence, but I mainly want the reasoning decomposed into the sub-questions the number depends on. Please assign a rough probability and a one-line justification to each of the following, and add any further sub-question that you think carries real weight:

- whether later wellbeing is just as important as earlier wellbeing for the same person, so that no point in time is intrinsically privileged;
- how much of what passes for time preference would disappear once uncertainty about the benefit actually arriving and the opportunity cost of forgoing present investment are controlled for;
- whether a person’s discounting of their own far-future self is better explained by weakened psychological connectedness over time than by time itself, which would make it something other than pure time preference;
- whether there is a determinate fact of the matter about what pure time preference would be rational or irrational, as opposed to rational here being a matter of which criterion one stipulates.

Please show how these terms combine to produce your overall credence in the claim, tell me which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and end by saying what evidence or argument would move your credence the most. I would also like a sentence on which sub-question my brother and I most likely disagree about.
