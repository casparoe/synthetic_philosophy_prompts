A couple of people in a rationalist-adjacent Discord got into it about whether an LLM hallucinating a fake court case is lying, bullshit, or just error, and I ended up wanting a referee's ruling. The exchange, abridged:

(1) A: It can't be lying—there's no intent to deceive. And it can't be Frankfurt-bullshit either, because Frankfurt's bullshitter is an agent trying to get away with something. The model is just predicting tokens and the tokens came out false. That's plain error.

(2) B: Too thin. Frankfurt's point is that the bullshitter doesn't need to believe his claim is false; he just doesn't care whether it's true, so long as the speech does its job. That's exactly what the model has been trained to do: produce fluent, confident text that looks like an answer, with no mechanism checking it against the world. It's a bullshit generator even if no single output is "intended."

(3) A: "Doesn't care" is an attitude. A model has no attitudes, so you're anthropomorphizing. By your standard a broken calculator that prints 2+2=5 is bullshitting.

(4) B: No, because a calculator isn't in the business of producing accepted-as-competent prose. If you want, locate the bullshit in the whole system, or in the people who ship a tool that emits confident falsehoods while presenting it as a research assistant.

(5) A: Now you've moved the target. If the designer is the bullshitter, then the hallucination itself is just error and the bullshit is in the marketing. Those are seperable claims and you're blurring them.

(6) B: I think that's the wrong line. Frankfurt's kicker is that bullshit is worse for truth than lying is, because it doesn't even answer to the truth. That's what we have here: a system that never even tries to be answerable to the facts. Calling it "mere error" makes it sound like a typo, which undersells the failure.

Please adjudicate this. Lead with your overall bottom line in the first sentence, then go through the six moves and rate each one from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a sentence or two of justification per move. Point out anything both of them are missing.
