ok so ive been trying to sort out what i actually believe about automation and it feels like every time i settle on one thing another one pulls. heres where ive landed:

(1) the scary thing about a world without jobs isnt the money, its that people get purpose, an identity, a feeling of being depended on, and most of their friends from work.
(2) but i also think automating away most jobs would be a huge win, because most jobs are boring, exhausting, degrading, or just busywork.
(3) a UBI would fix the income side, but i dont think it can fix the meaning side, because a lot of the meaning of a job comes from being needed and from having to show up even when you dont feel like it.
(4) at the same time i think people shouldnt get their self-worth from paid work, and we should be building a culture where you dont have to be economically useful to count, instead of inventing make-work to keep people feeling important.
(5) if work mostly vanished i think people would eventually find enough meaning in family, crafts, games, volunteering, clubs, local politics, etc., so the meaning problem is mostly a transition problem.
(6) and then i remember that earlier rounds of automation (farmers to factory workers, typists to word processors) destroyed whole ways of working and people adjusted fine, so i cant see what makes this round different in principle.

Does this set hang together? go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are. rank the real tensions by how severe they are, say what the cheapest fix for each would be, and then tell me which single change would clean up the most.
