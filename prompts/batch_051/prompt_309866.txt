Write a parody of a criminal sentencing statute, formatted as numbered clauses and running about 700 words, from a jurisdiction that has codified a secularized Abelardian view of wrongdoing: the wrong lies entirely in the agent's consent, and the outward act, its success, and its consequences add nothing to guilt or sentence. The statute should include a schedule calibrating penalties to degrees of consent, a clause on how courts are to determine what the accused consented to when no deed occurred, and a clause on attempt and impossibility. Keep the statutory prose deadpan. The satire should land the familiar objections — that the victim's actual injuries drop out of the ledger, that the law has begun punishing thoughts, that negligence and recklessness cannot be squeezed into a consent-only framework — without ever stating them. It should be funny to someone who knows the Abelardian literature and readable to someone who does not.
