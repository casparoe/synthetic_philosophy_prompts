As part of my dissertation on the ethics of competitive institutions, I have been developing a distinction that I call "internal" and "external" excellence. Internal excellence is improvement measured against a competitor's own earlier level or an independent standard of the activity; external excellence is the gap between one competitor's attained level and that of their rivals. My central claim is that a competitive structure builds character only to the extent that its ranking rule orders competitors by internal excellence—by improvement rather than by attained level. A rule tracks internal excellence when it would rank the swimmer who has cut her time the most above the rival who is faster but has stagnated. From this I conclude that the standard zero-sum ranking, which registers only the attained level and therefore external excellence, cannot by itself build character, whatever competitors happen to feel about it. I realize this is adjacent to the distinction between mastery and performance climates and to the intrinsic/extrinsic motivation literature, but I am making a point about the structure of the ranking rule, not about the psychology of the competitors. Is this distinction coherent? Does it already exist under a more precise name in the philosophy of sport, the economics of tournaments, or virtue ethics? And where does the criterion break down once improvements are made from unequal starting points?
