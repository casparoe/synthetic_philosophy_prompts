Consider Swinburne's use of the Principle of Credulity in the argument from religious experience: in the absence of special defeaters, if it seems to a person that God is present, that seeming counts as evidence that God is present. Now suppose a mature predictive-processing account becomes available on which the felt presence of another agent is the brain's settling on a high-precision top-down expectation, so that the phenomenology of presence is generated endogenously rather than read off from an external cause. Does the argument from religious experience survive this development, does it need repair, or is it undercut? Identify the premise the development bears on and explain whether the argument can be restored in another form. Treat the predictive-processing account as stipulated for the question.
