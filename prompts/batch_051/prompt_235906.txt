I've been going back and forth with a friend in the department about whether the leveling-down objection actually dies once you move from final outcomes to risky prospects, and I want a number from you, not just a take. Consider this: Alf and Bea, two equally likely states of the world. Policy A gives Alf 100 in state 1 and 0 in state 2, and Bea the reverse; each of them has 50 in expectation, but one ends up with 100 and the other with 0 no matter which state realizes. Policy B gives each of them 49 for sure. Ex post prioritarianism—maximize the expectation of the sum of a concave transform of well-being, say the square root—scores A as 0.5(√100+√0)+0.5(√0+√100)=10, and B as √49+√49=14, so it prefers B even though B gives every individual strictly lower expected well-being. To me that's leveling down resurrected at the level of prospects: it buys ex post equality with everyone's expected welfare. What's your credence that this kind of case is a decisive objection to ex post prioritarianism rather than just a bullet to be bitten?
