I'm one of the mods of a forum with a pretty active debate section, maybe 30k users. A proposal on the table is to turn the section into a scored competition: volunteer judges, a season ladder, win/loss records, an end-of-year bracket. The pro side says competition would improve the quality of argument, because people prepare when there's a record and sloppy claims cost you points. The con side says that in a sport you score the performance, but in a debate the thing we actually care about is whether someone is right, and on most of our threads nobody can tell who's right, so the judges would end up scoring confidence, style, and crowd-pleasing and calling it quality. The proposer came back with a tweak: for debates about checkable stuff, you also earn points months later if your factual predictions pan out, so it isn't just vibes. The judges would just be long-time users, not experts. I got asked to write a short memo for the mod team with a clear recommendation — adopt, reject, or adopt with changes — and if we adopt, what two or three rules would keep the scoring from rewarding the wrong things. The memo needs a bottom line we can vote on and should take the strongest version of the losing side seriously instead of strawmanning it.
