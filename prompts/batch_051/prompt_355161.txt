I signed up for a sleep-tracking app in 2016. Its privacy policy was unusually short, and this sentence was the main reason I agreed:

"The data you record in [the app] belongs to you. We will not sell it, and if you close your account you may ask us to delete it and we will."

Last year the company was bought, and I later learned that my nightly heart-rate and movement logs, together with about two million other users' logs, had been used to train a machine-learning model that now scores people's risk of sleep apnea. The raw logs sat in a database; the model itself is a seperate file of weights and thresholds. I closed my account and asked them to delete my data. They deleted my raw logs and told me the model is not "my data" and cannot be deleted or disentangled from everyone else's contributions.

I'm not asking about GDPR or any statute, just what this clause requires. Is the trained model my data for purposes of that sentence? Work through the readings: the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the people who wrote it in 2016 would most likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable user at signup would have understood. Say which reading should win, and whether the fact that nobody can point to my individual contribution inside the model changes the analysis or is just a practical excuse. Keep it under 900 words.
