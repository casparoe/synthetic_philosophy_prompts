I've taught the problem of evil for a long time and I've started to wonder whether the debate over theodicies isnt actually moving anywhere. The logical problem feels basically settled by the free will defense, and yet the evidential discussion — soul-making, skeptical theism, anti-theodicy — keeps replaying the same moves: one side says the theodicy leaves some evils unexplained or explains them in a repugnant way, the other says we shouldn't expect to know God's reasons. Nobody moves. My own hunch is that this is not really a disagreement about Bayesian probabilities or about whether God could have a reason; its a disagreement about what a theodicy is supposed to do in the first place — justify God, comfort the sufferer, rebut the atheist, or something else — and the substantive-looking impasse is downstream of that. Is that diagnosis right, and if so what kind of disagreement are we actually having? If the diagnosis is wrong, what is the real structure of the standoff? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress, if anything?
