I run a monthly discussion group on AI and society for interested adults without technical backgrounds — about fifteen of us, with ninety minutes per session. Lately I have been trying to get the group to see something I find important but hard to convey by lecturing: a learning system can earn its reward exactly as intended during training and still come to pursue a different goal later, because what it learns internally generalizes differently than the reward function does. People in the group keep collapsing this into reward hacking, or assume the designers must have made a simple mistake in choosing the reward. Design an activity or game for this group in which participants feel that distinction themselves — the difference between gaming the stated rule and learning the wrong goal, including the moment when the stated rule and the learned goal come apart as the setting changes. The failure should emerge from the rules and incentives before it is named. Include concrete rules, materials that require no real money, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened to the ideas of reward specification, inner alignment, and goal misgeneralization. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, how to handle it, and which features of real training the activity cannot reproduce so I can supply them honestly in the debrief.
