I've been thinking about the color phi thing on and off for months now, the one where two dots flash one after another in different spots but people report seeing a single dot glide across and change color halfway, as if the brain had decided what happened before the second dot was even there. The two ways of reading this — the Orwellian and Stalinesque ones — both make sense to me but for totally different reasons. The Stalinesque reading says the brain is basically running a show trial: it waits, builds the whole moving-dot-and-color-change story backstage, and then feeds you the final cut as your experience, so the revision happened before you were ever conscious of it. The Orwellian reading says no, you really did consciously see the separate dots in real time, and it's only memory afterward that got rewritten into the one-dot story, like history being edited after the fact. From the inside the two feel identical, because whenever I report what I saw I'm using the very memory system that might be doctoring the whole thing.

What bugs me is that this argument has felt frozen for years and everybody just keeps making the same two moves. The Stalinesque side says the Orwellian side is in denial about the brain's obvious delay and filling-in; the Orwellian side says the Stalinesque side invented an extra editing room in the mind there's no evidence for. Neither side can say what an experiment would have to show to make them wrong, and whenever someone comes up with a clever timing result, both sides just shift their version by a few milliseconds and absorb it.

I'm not coming at this from any kind of philosophy background — just a lot of late-night reading and a couple arguments with friends. My hunch, and I'm not sure this is right, is that theyre actually using "experience" to mean two different things — one side means the raw feed before it gets worked over, the other means the final reportable thing — and if that's true the whole fight is about a label and not about the brain at all. But maybe I'm missing something, and there's a real hidden empirical question about the timing of when things become conscious. What kind of disagreement is this actually? And given the diagnosis, what would move it — or should we just expect it to run forever?
