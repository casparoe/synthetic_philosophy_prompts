I've been going back and forth with a friend about how much explanatory work symmetries are actually doing in physics, and I've ended up defending a view I want to see attacked properly. The view is this: when we ask why momentum is conserved, the real answer is translation invariance. The symmetry explains the conservation law, not the other way around, and not just in the sense that the two show up in the same derivation. Same for energy and time-translation, angular momentum and rotation, charge and phase, and so on. Everyone reaches for Noether's theorem at this point, but I know the theorem is closer to a two-way correspondence under the right assumptions (smooth symmetry of the action, etc.), so its not obvious that the arrow of explanation has to point from symmetry to conservation. Still, my intuition is that symmetry is what makes a conservation law non-mysterious, and conservation laws are just downstream of that.

What I want is an honest map of where this view is exposed. If a smart critic wanted to break it, where would they push? The routes I can half-see are: the equivalence point (the math doesn't care which side explains which, so the explanatory priority is something we project onto it), the approximation point (real symmetries are often only approximate or broken, so the "explanation" explains an idealization, not the messy world), cases where a system has a first integral or hidden conserved quantity without any symmetry I can point to, anomalies where a classical symmetry doesn't survive quantization and yet something close to the conservation law persists, and the more deflationary worry that this isn't a physical claim at all but an aesthetic preference for pretty derivations. I want to know which of these, or which other things, would count decisively against the view as opposed to just forcing me to add a qualification, and what a defender of symmetry-explanation should themselves admit is the hardest kind of case. In particular, what would a real observation or a clean derivation actually have to show for "symmetry explains conservation" to come out false rather than just "symmetry is one nice way to package it"? I'd rather find out I'm wrong now than keep pattern-matching to the pretty proof.
