At my sixth-form college there is a student-run film society which invites outside speakers each week. It is funded by a small contribution from the students who attend, and members take it in turns to set out chairs and clear up afterwards. One student never pays and never helps, yet he attends almost every talk, takes a handout, and joins in the discussion. When I challenged him, he replied that his presence costs the society nothing extra: the room and the speaker's fee are fixed, and there are always spare handouts. He added that the society would carry on even if a few more people behaved as he does, because enough of us will keep contributing. I believe that what he is doing is wrong, but I cannot say exactly why without falling back on the claim that it is simply unfair. I would like help in working out whether his behaviour is actually wrong, and, if it is, what the wrong consists in. Should I raise the matter with the society's organiser, or would that be an overreaction?
