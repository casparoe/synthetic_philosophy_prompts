I'm fairly convinced by the Bayesian picture of evidence—that confirmation is just a change in probability, and that a piece of evidence you already know can't confirm a theory because its probability is already 1. But I can't shake the sense that some theories really are confirmed by facts we've had sitting around for decades: when someone finally explains an anomoly everyone already knew about, that feels like evidence for the new theory, not just a nice post hoc story. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the Bayesian framework to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading of 'knowledge', 'evidence', or 'confirmation' on which the two can live together. If the intuition is just a temporal confusion about when I learnt something, say that; if the theory needs something like counterfactual priors, say that too.
