I'm a grad student taking a seminar on Schopenhauer's aesthetics, and I'm also TA-ing the undergrad course on him that this exam essay comes from. The question we set was: "In his account of the arts, Schopenhauer claims that music is unlike every other art because it copies the will itself rather than the Platonic Ideas. Explain why he thinks this, and why he takes this to make music the highest of the arts." The student wrote:

"Schopenhauer's hierarchy of the arts is keyed to how directly each art presents the Platonic Ideas. Architecture presents the lowest grades of the will's objectification, like gravity and rigidity; tragedy presents the highest, the will in conflict with itself. Music doesn't fit this ladder because it doesn't copy the Ideas at all, it copies the will directly. The other arts give us only the shadow of things, but music gives the inner nature of the world itself. Instead of depicting a particular joy or sorrow, music gives joy or sorrow in the abstract, which is why it works as a universal language. And since the will is the most universal of the Platonic Ideas, music stands above the other arts, higher even than tragedy. Moreover, by putting us in direct contact with the will, music gives us the deepest insight into our own essence, which for Schopenhauer is what finally quiets the will, since you can't renounce what you don't understand."
