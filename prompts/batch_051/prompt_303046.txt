Rorty's neopragmatist antirepresentationalism starts from the idea that language doesn't mirror nature; descriptions are tools for coping with the world, and calling a claim true is basically the social commendation it earns by surviving in the conversation of people you take seriously. That view tacitly assumes there is something like one ongoing conversation. But now that conversation runs through engagement-optimizing recommender systems and has splintered into self-reinforcing vocabularies: a claim can be exactly what my peers let me get away with saying in one feed while being laughed out of the other, with no shared forum where the two ever meet. Does Rorty's position survive this? I'm trying to pin down which premise the development actually puts pressure on, whether there's a way for him to absorb it without turning truth into whatever your filter bubble amplifies, and what a honest repaired version would look like.
