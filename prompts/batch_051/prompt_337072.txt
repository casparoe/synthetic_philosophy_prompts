I keep going back and forth on whether to be a realist or an instrumentalist about all this talk of neural networks having representations, so I figured I'd sort through my gut calls on other cases first and see if there's a principle underneath. Here are my honest verdicts.

A paper road map: obviously a representation — it's about a place, I can misread it, and a wrong map is wrong in a way that feels different from just being inaccurate. A mercury thermometer: no, or at most it indicates; it's just a physical regularity, and if it reads high that's miscalibration, not misrepresentation. But a car's fuel gauge: yes, and here's the annoying part — when the gauge sticks at full while the tank is empty, I really do want to say it misrepresents. So why does the gauge get to misrepresent but the thermometer doesn't? I don't know, that's part of the job. A trail of footprints on a beach: I lean yes, they're about a walker, but I'd be okay-ish giving this one up if the principle forces it. A variable named 'pressure' in a climate model: yes, trivially, because we stipulated that it stands for pressure.

Now the neural cases. A neuron in a trained image classifier that reliably fires on pictures of cats and almost nothing else: my gut says yes, that's a representation. A neuron that fires on a jumble — cats plus a certain texture plus the absence of dogs: I lean no, it isn't representing anything in particular, its just whatever the training happened to leave behind. The word-embedding thing where 'king' minus 'man' plus 'woman' lands near 'queen': I used to call that a representation of gender, but now I think it's just a geometric regularity, not something the model uses. A direction in a language model's internal numbers that researchers found by training a classifier to predict whether the model is about to answer honestly or deceptively: I flip-flop. If all they did was read it off the probe, that feels like the probe is doing the representing, not the model. But if you can nudge the model along that same direction and it becomes more or less honest, then it feels like the model had the honesty thing in there. A coefficient in a linear regression that predicts house prices from square footage: no, just a fitted parameter, nobody would call it a representation.

So: is there one account of representation that gets all or nearly all of these? In particular, what distinguishes the cat neuron from the thermometer and the regression coefficient, since all three are just reliable causal covariation? And the pair I actually lose sleep over: is the fuel gauge different from the thermometer only because we built the one for a purpose and not the other, and does that mean trained networks earn representations by being trained toward a task? What does the account say about the honesty direction, and does interpretability need the things it finds to literally be representations, or is it fine if they're just useful probes? Finally, tell me which of my verdicts the principle makes me give up and what it predicts for a case I haven't listed but suspect matters: a direction in a model that, when you turn it up, reliably makes the model emit text about, say, deception — is that a representation or just a control knob?
