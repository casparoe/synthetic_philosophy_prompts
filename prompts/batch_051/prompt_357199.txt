Exam question: Set out Bradley's regress as it arises for a relational account of the unity of the proposition, e.g., the view that the proposition Fa is a complex in which a and F are bound by an instantiation relation. Then evaluate whether Frege's saturated/unsaturated distinction escapes the regress. Be explicit about what would be needed to show the regress is vicious.

Student's answer: Bradley's regress is the infinite regress that arises when one tries to explain the unity of a proposition by means of a relation. Take the proposition Fa. On the relational picture, it is a complex containing a and F together with an instantiation relation binding them. But if that relation is what makes a and F cohere into one proposition, then we need something further to cohere a, F, and the instantiation relation into a complex, and so on without end. Because the regress never terminates, a relational account never succeeds in explaining unity. Bradley concluded that relations are therefore not ultimately real and that the apparent unity of the proposition is only an appearance of a deeper non-relational whole. Frege avoids this by refusing to treat the constituents of the proposition as all of one kind. The object a is saturated, while the concept F is unsaturated; the concept takes the object as argument, and this application yields a complete proposition with no additional relation required. Frege thereby shows that the regress is not vicious.
