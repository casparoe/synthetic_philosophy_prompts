I'm in a practical reason seminar and there's one debate that has completely stalled after like six weeks. The question is whether a person can have a reason to do something when nothing in their current motives, cares, or commitments would connect up to doing it, even after thinking things through. The internalists say no: if the reason can't in principle get any grip on the person as they actually are, then calling it "her reason" is just a fancy way of saying we wish she were different. The externalists say yes: there are things a person just has reason to do — not wreck her own future, not be pointlessly cruel — and the fact that she happens not to care doesn't cancel the reason; it shows something is wrong with her. What's weird is that in my group everyone agrees on nearly every concrete case we bring up. We all agree that someone who wants a cool drink and grabs the bottle that actually contains paint thinner is making a mistake, because there's a path from what he already wants to not drinking it. We also all agree that a person with no concern at all for his future self is doing something wrong and isn't going to be talked around. The split is only on whether "he has a reason to care about his future self" is a true thing to say about him. My professor calls this the internal/external reasons debate, and apparently it has been stuck in more or less this shape for a long time.

My working theory, which I want you to pressure-test, is that we've got two different jobs the word "reason" does — explaining why someone would be moved to act, versus assessing whether they fall short from some outside standpoint — and the debate is what happens when one word is forced to do both. So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it verbal, a clash of bedrock intuitions, or a real question underneath about whether reasons are the sort of thing that have to be able to get a grip on the person? And supposing your diagnosis is right, is there anything that would actually move the debate, or should we dissolve it and stop asking? I'm honestly not sure if were even arguing about the same thing anymore.
