The core claim is that translating phantasia as imagination or mental imagery is a mistake, because Aristotle seems to need the faculty even when there is no picture-like content—like thinking of a vacuum, a mathematical point, or an indefinite future, where there isn't really anything that can be imaged in a visual way. So I argue phantasia should be read more generally as the capacity to have something present to mind as absent, and that this reading changes how we should use Aristotle in contemporary debates about aphantasia and imagistic versus propositional thought. The bit I'm least confident about is my handling of the part in III.3 where Aristotle says belief isn't up to us but bringing an image before our eyes is up to us whenever we wish; I treat that as evidence that phantasia is an active power of presentation, not an inner picture gallery. Could you comment on whether that's a fair reading and whether the argument is interesting rather than just a verbal quibble?
