I keep seeing people debate the torture-vs-dust-specks thing — one person tortured for fifty years vs some astronomically large number of people (3^^^3 in the original) each getting a single, barely noticeable speck of dust in the eye — by just insisting on a verdict, and I'd like the full spec before anyone gets to pick a side. Write the desiderata a theory of aggregating tiny harms has to meet. Cases it has to handle beyond the headline one, distinctions it cant collapse, and the formal results it has to respect or reject on the record. For each requirement, say what case or consideration forces it. Then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular candidate views, and mark which requirements you think are negotiable and which are load-bearing. You can briefly score the usual contenders against the list at the end, but most of the work should go into the requirements.

Things I suspect belong somewhere in the list: the difference between a harm that's small but noticeable and one that's below conscious threshold; same-person accumulation of many specks vs spreading them across many people; whether it matters if the number of specks is fixed or selected by a sequence you could stop at any point; whether transitivity and dominance-style constraints are non-negotiable or fair game; and whether attention or adaptation changes a speck's moral weight (does a speck you forget in five seconds count the same as one you dwell on?). Assume the point of the theory is action guidance, not just having a vibe.
