In the First Dialogue of Berkeley's *Three Dialogues between Hylas and Philonous*, Hylas says:

"What I can anything be more fantastical, more repugnant to Common Sense, or a more manifest piece of Scepticism, than to believe there is no such thing as MATTER?"

And Philonous replies:

"Softly, good Hylas. What if it should prove that you, who hold there is, are, by virtue of that opinion, a greater sceptic, and maintain more paradoxes and repugnances to Common Sense, than I who believe no such thing?"

Here's a case Berkeley couldn't have foreseen. Suppose a VR world runs on a server that only simulates the room a player is currently in. The rest of the world is generated on demand when someone turns a corner. The developers insist the world is fully real and persistent, but nobody can ever catch an unperceived part existing, because the act of checking forces the server to generate it. One of my friends says Berkeley was right at least about VR: there simply is no unperceived world there. Another says no, VR objects are ordinary physical server states—magnetic charges, voltages—so the unperceived parts exist anyway.

Treating Philonous's early reply as a strategy rather than a settled conclusion, how would it apply to their disagreement? Is the friend who denies the unperceived VR world in the position of Philonous or of Hylas here? And does the VR case show anything about what Berkeley's master argument actually needs in order to work, when the person on the other side can point to a physical substrate Berkeley never imagined?
