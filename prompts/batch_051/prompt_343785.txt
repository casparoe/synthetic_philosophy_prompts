Benjamin Franklin described in his autobiography a scheme for reaching moral perfection by means of a catalogue of thirteen virtues. The apparatus was strikingly systematic: he kept a small ruled notebook with a page for each virtue and seven columns for the days of the week, entering a mark whenever his evening self-examination found a fault; he concentrated on one virtue at a time in a fixed rotation, so that the entire list was worked through in thirteen weeks and the course could be repeated four times a year. His expressed rationale was that trying to reform everything at once would be discouraging, whereas attending to one virtue at a time would allow a habit to form, and that a written record would make each lapse conspicuous rather than easily forgotten. He admitted that the program succeeded incompletely: order, in particular, defeated him, and he observed that pride was the most stubborn of the vices, since it reappeared even in attempts to appear humble.

Now suppose Franklin were shown today's digital self-improvement devices: habit-tracking apps that award streaks and badges, social platforms that publish a user's progress to others, and adaptive systems that detect lapses and schedule reminders. Suppose he also encountered the quantified-self movement, with its ideal of logging and optimizing life as a stream of data, and the designers' claim that gamification works partly by enlisting a user's vanity and desire for social approval.

Would Franklin see these tools as a continuation and refinement of his method, or as a distortion of something essential to it? In your answer, address three tensions: (a) his private paper chart versus social or shared accountability; (b) his aim of changing settled character through repeated courses versus the measurement of discrete behaviors; and (c) his practice of deliberate evening self-examination versus automatically generated metrics and reminders. Reason from Franklin's own rationale for his method, and be specific about both where he would likely be sympathetic and where he would likely be skeptical.
