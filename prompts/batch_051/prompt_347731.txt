I'm a graduate student in cognitive science, and I've spent the last few months reading both sides of the "is the brain a computer?" debate. What strikes me is that neither side has moved much in decades, even though both seem to grant the same underlying neuroscience. Computationalists insist they are not speaking metaphorically: they claim neural processes literally implement computations over structured representations, and that this is what makes cognitive explanation different from mere behavioral description or dynamical modeling of the nervous system. Critics reply that the brain has no stored program, no central processing unit, no discrete symbol strings, and no principled hardware/software split; on their view the brain is a self-organizing dynamical system, and since almost anything can be simulated by a sufficiently liberal computational model, the claim that the brain "computes" becomes trivial. The computationalists answer that simulation is not implementation — the question is whether the brain's causal structure realizes computational states, not whether someone can write code that mimics its outputs. And there the exchange stalls.

What puzzles me is that the two sides seem to disagree about what kind of question they are even asking. Is this an empirical question about, say, whether we can decode neural representations or whether neural dynamics are Turing-computable? Is it a question about which level of description does the real explanatory work? Or is it a verbal dispute in which "computer" slides between a weak sense (describable by a computational model) and a strong one (literally an artifact with stored-program, digital architecture)? My working hypothesis is the last one — that the impasse is largely an equivocation on how literally "is" is meant — but I can also see coherent versions of the other diagnoses, and I don't trust my own preference here.

Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using the considerations the two sides really advance, and say what would count as progress given that diagnosis — or whether progress should not be expected. Please begin with your diagnosis in one or two sentences, then argue for it, and keep the whole response under 900 words.
