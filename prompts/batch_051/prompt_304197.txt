In the classic Libet experiment, subjects watched a rotating spot on a clock and reported the moment they first felt the urge to flex their wrist, while EEG recorded a gradual readiness potential beginning roughly a third of a second before that reported moment. Now modify the setup. A real-time decoder can detect, within a few milliseconds and with high reliability, the moment the readiness potential crosses the threshold that predicts an upcoming movement. The subject watches a progress bar that begins to fill at that threshold—before they would normally report any urge to move—and is told that, whenever the bar starts to fill, they may either allow the movement or veto it within a short window. Work through how this modification changes the standard philosophical reading of Libet. Does the visible, pre-conscious signal strengthen the skeptical conclusion, or does it give the subject a kind of control the original setup lacked? A skeptic will say that the veto itself is preceded by its own readiness potential, so the same problem returns one level earlier. Determine whether that regress is genuine. Then spell out how a libertarian, a compatibilist, and a hard determinist would each analyze the modified experiment, and identify the exact point at which their accounts genuinely diverge.
