I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, mostly working on consciousness, and I've been putting together a short position statement for a seminar. I'd like a consistency check before I send it around, because I have a feel that some of this hangs together better than it looks, but I can't tell exactly where the problems are. Here's the set of things I currently want to hold:

1. Physicalism: every token mental state is identical with or realized by a physical state of the brain.

2. Epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness. I'm drawn to the Huxley line from On the Hypothesis that Animals are Automata, and its History (1874). He compares consciousness to the steam-whistle of a locomotive: it accompanies the working of the engine but has no influence on the machinery. On my version, qualitative experiences are caused by certain neural processes but have no downstream causal powers themselves.

3. Introspection is a generally reliable, if fallible, way to know what one is currently experiencing. When I form the belief that I'm in pain, it's normally true, and it isn't a wild guess.

4. Sincere introspective reports, like saying "I have a headache," are generated by neural events in the speech-production system, not by the headache itself.

5. The content of a thought or belief is fixed at least partly by its causal or informational relations to what it is about. A thought about water, for example, is about water partly because water tokens have played the right sort of causal role in forming water-thoughts.

6. I think the paradox of phenomenal judgment is a real problem and not merely verbal: there is something genuinely puzzling about how our introspective beliefs can be reliably about qualia if qualia themselves are causally inert.

The thing is, I can't tell which of these are actually in tension and which only look that way. I'd like an audit: go through the pairs, say where the real tensions are, rank them by how severe they are, and tell me which single revision would resolve the most at the lowest cost. Don't assume I already know which one I'd give up.
