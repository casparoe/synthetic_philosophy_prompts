Write a deadpan parody of a hospital informed-consent form for a clinic that administers a single, irreversible treatment guaranteed to be a transformative experience in the philosophical sense: afterward the patient's core preferences, values, and sense of self will have altered, and no description of the change can tell the patient in advance what it will be like from the inside. Keep it roughly 550–650 words, in the measured, bureaucratic voice of a real consent document, and scrupulously maintain the fiction that meaningful informed consent is being obtained. The comedy should come from the structural features of the problem rather than from winking or jokes: the person who signs cannot evaluate or bind the person who wakes up; the clinic's descriptions are either empty or already presuppose the values the treatment will replace; and the notions of risk, benefit, capacity, and voluntariness all assume the stable evaluator this very treatment destroys. Never state this thesis outright. Write the piece in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — use sentence-level transitions where a real form would use section headings.
