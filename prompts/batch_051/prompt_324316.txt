After two years of visiting my grandmother — advanced dementia, mostly content, humming along to songs and eating yogurt — I finally filled out an advance directive for myself, and I was surprised how convinced I am by the idea behind it. The argument for binding my later self goes roughly like this: I am the one whose life it is; my values, projects, and considered judgments now are the most authentic expression of me; the later person, whatever she wants, won't have the capacities that made those judgments worth protecting. So the earlier self should have authority over the later one, the way Ulysses had himself tied to the mast precisely because he knew he'd later scream to be untied. I don't want some smily content later-me kept alive in a diaper for years just because she's forgotten what mattered.

But then I picture the woman in the chair — happy, or frightened and pleading, wanting her hand held — and the binding starts to feel almost cruel. It starts to look like I'd be sacrificing a real person in front of me for the sake of a person who no longer exists, and that's the intuition I can't shake. So here's the clash: the theory (precedent autonomy, I think it's called) says the earlier self's directive should win; the intuition says the later self is the one who's actually there and shouldn't be railroaded. Which should give? Should I debunk the intuition as soft-hearted confusion about 'a different person,' or amend the theory so the earlier self has some but not total authority, or is there a reading on which they're compatible? And tell me honestly: is the 'different person' talk doing the real work here, or is it just a way of dressing up the intuition to make it sound principled?
