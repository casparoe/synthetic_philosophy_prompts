At my shop two people got into it over a line in a feature we were running: "the world is becoming less violent." I cover science and ideas, so I ended up as the de facto referee, and I honestly couldn't settle it. The back and forth went roughly like this.

(1) Rashid, the data editor, said the line is fine. Homicide rates per 100,000 have fallen over the long run, and war deaths per capita too. He said per capita is obviously the right lens, because absolute deaths mostly track population: more people means more murders even if the world isn't any more violent, so raw counts don't capture what the phrase means.

(2) Elena, the reporter, said per capita isn't a neutral correction, it's a choice about what the headline is about. A rate describes an individual's risk, not the total amount of violent death in the world. If you went from 10 murders among 100 people to 120 among a million, the rate says the world got safer, but there are 110 more grieving families. So calling the world less violent smuggles in the idea that only an average persons risk matters.

(3) Rashid said that is what the phrase means in ordinary use: the typical person's exposure to violence. Absolute counts confound violence with population size; if the population doubles and the murder rate halves, saying there's "more violence" because there are more bodies is misleading about what actually changed.

(4) Elena: Fair, but then you've shrunk the claim to "an average person is at less risk of interpersonal violence," and even that hides a numerator choice. Which harms get counted? Put in famine deaths, deaths from pollution, police killings, or structural violence, and the long-run picture shifts. And the further back you go the spottier the record-keeping gets, so some of the "decline" might be the bureaucratization of counting, not a decline in violence.

(5) Rashid: That's the everything-bad-is-violence move, and it dissolves the word. The standard meaning is assault, murder, war, maybe genocide. Under that line the decline is real. And the bad old records actually help my side: undercounting in, say, 1400 makes the past look calmer than it was, so correcting for it would steepen the decline, not erase it.

(6) Elena: I'm not saying the trend is invented. I'm saying "the world is becoming less violent" can only come out true or false after you've picked a measure of violence, a denominator, and a stretch of time, and there isn't a value-neutral way to pick. So the headline pretends there's a fact of the matter where there's really a menu of defensible numbers pointing different directions. That was my whole point, and you never answered the absolute-suffering version of it.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then say who is more right overall, and what, if anything, both of them are missing.
