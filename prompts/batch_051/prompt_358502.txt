OK, I've been going back and forth about what we ordinary mean by 'possible' — not the modal-logic diamond/box sense, just the everyday 'could' in sentences like 'it could have landed the other way.' I'd like a working definition that earns its keep against a list of cases I keep tripping over. Here they are:

(a) A fair coin is about to be flipped. Could it land heads? I say obviously yes.
(b) A die is weighted so six comes up 90% of the time and each other side 2%. Before a roll, could it come up one? I honestly don't know what I think.
(c) A dart is thrown at a continuous board and lands on an exact point p. Before the throw, the probability of hitting exactly p was zero under any ordinary continuous distribution — but it hit p. So was hitting p possible?
(d) A deck has been shuffled and the top card is now fixed, but nobody has looked. Is it true that 'the top card could be the seven of hearts'? The card either is or isn't.
(e) My car wouldn't start this morning; the battery was dead, so in the mechanic's sense it couldn't have started. Before anyone knew, 'it could have started' seemed equally fine. Are those two different senses?
(f) A lottery has 300 million tickets and one is drawn. Before the drawing, could the eventual winner win? One in 300 million isn't zero, but it sure doesn't feel like 'could.'
(g) A glass slips off the table and falls. Could it have fallen upward instead? I want to say no, even though 'falling upward' isn't a contradiction like a square circle.

Propose a definition of 'possible' in this everyday could-sense, run it through all seven cases, and tell me where it gives a verdict you don't share or no verdict at all — then revise it or defend the surprising verdict. Also keep two things seperate: what's actually settled here (for instance, that probability zero doesn't by itself settle impossibility) and what's genuinely contested among people who work on modality and probability, and don't make a contested take sound like the consensus. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing your one-sentence best definition.
