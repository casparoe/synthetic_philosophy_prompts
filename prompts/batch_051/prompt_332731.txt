I sit on the undergraduate admissions policy committee of a selective private university that receives federal funds. We vote in two weeks on the policy that will replace our former race-conscious holistic review, which current law no longer permits. The dean's working group has recommended a package of race-neutral measures: a percentage plan for in-state applicants, a school-level socioeconomic disadvantage index, a test-optional review that reads each file in the context of the applicant's high school, and expanded recruitment in low-income and predominantly minority school districts. The committee is split. One faction argues that the package is a racial quota by proxy: its evident purpose and predictable effect are to reproduce the racial composition the old policy produced, and adopting it would launder an intention the law now forbids. The other faction argues that there is a principled difference between selecting on race and selecting on structural disadvantage, that each measure can be justified on non-racial grounds, and that a university committed to equal opportunity should adopt them precisely because they are race-neutral.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete policy, specifying which measures to adopt, modify, or reject and the metrics by which the committee should evaluate the policy over four admission cycles; take a definite position on whether and when race-neutral proxies collapse into quotas; distinguish among aiming at a particular racial outcome, adopting criteria that merely correlate with race, and pursuing educational diversity for its own sake; and treat the strongest argument for the losing side honestly rather than as a straw man. Do not include legal citations — the committee already has counsel's briefing and needs your substantive reasoning. The document must be actionable, written so the committee can amend it and vote on it, not a general essay on the ethics of affirmative action. Aim for about 1,500 words.
