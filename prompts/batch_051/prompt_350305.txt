I'm writing a term paper on the epistemology of testimony, and I've hit the point where I can't tell wether reductionists and anti-reductionists are even answering the same question. One side seems to be explaining what makes a particular act of trusting a speaker justified, and the other seems to be asking if there's a default entitlement to believe what people say. Before I pick a side I want the actual spec sheet: what does a theory of testimonial justification have to get right to count as adequate?

Give me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration behind it. At minimum it should handle: the child who comes to know things from her caregivers without being able to run any serious check on their reliability; the hearer who trusts a speaker who is sincere but has an unlucky track record; the hearer who has strong independent evidence the speaker is lying and ignores it; the way we routinely accept what strangers tell us (directions, labels, announcements) without verifying; the fact that three independent strangers saying the same weird thing intuitively moves me more than any one of them does alone (though maybe that's a constraint on a theory of coherence rather than testimony, so tell me if it belongs on the list); and the sense in which a hearer can be blamed for gullibility even when the speaker happens to be right. And don't let it collapse the distinction between a speaker being trustworthy (something about their character or their reasons) and a speaker merely being reliable (something about their track record), since that collapse is how both sides end up talking past each other.

For each, say why it's a requirement rather than an optional extra. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable for any view and which are just one camp's hobbyhorses, and say whether any pair is jointly unsatisfiable. Finally, briefly: which single requirement causes the most trouble for a flat-footed reductionism, and which for a flat-footed anti-reductionism? I care more about the criteria than the scorecard.
