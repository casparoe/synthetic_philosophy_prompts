Populist politicians, elected leaders, and protest movements all claim to act on "the will of the people," yet the phrase seems to do genuine work while resisting any definition I try to give it. I would like a working definition of "the will of the people" that yields a determinate verdict for each of the following cases — saying which clearly fall under the term, which clearly do not, and which are borderline. I have no settled view about several of the borderline ones. The cases are:

1. A single-issue referendum with 81% turnout, in which 73% vote in favor.
2. A candidate who lost an election by 20 points nonetheless insists that the real will of the people supports his program.
3. An opinion poll of 1,200 adults with a four-point margin of error, in which 54% support a policy.
4. In a three-party election, a party wins 38% of the vote, the largest share, and claims a mandate for its entire platform.
5. Several hundred thousand people protest for a change that the party that won the most recent election explicitly campaigned against.
6. A constitutional court annuls a statute that recent polls suggest 68% of the public supports.
7. A dam would displace a village of 300 people and slightly reduce electricity prices for 20 million; a large majority of the 20 million favor it, and nearly all of the 300 oppose it.
8. A randomly selected citizens' assembly, after months of deliberation with experts, recommends a measure that a majority of the public told pollsters they oppose.
9. A policy most people privately dislike persists because everyone believes everyone else supports it.
10. A choice among three policies in which pairwise majority votes cycle: A defeats B, B defeats C, and C defeats A.

Please propose a definition, run it against all ten cases, identify the verdicts that are least secure, and revise the definition if it gives wrong answers. Also address whether the phrase does two jobs at once — one descriptive (aggregating what people actually want) and one normative (authorizing a decision) — and, if so, give the two definitions and say which cases move between them. If your definition gives no answer in some cases, say whether that is a defect of the definition or evidence that the term is indeterminate there.
