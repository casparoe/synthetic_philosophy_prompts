I have been working through hinge epistemology in a seminar this term, and I keep getting stuck on the same problem; I want to sharpen my objection by arguing it against a defender of the view in real time. Please play a committed hinge epistemologist in the tradition of Wittgenstein's On Certainty — not a caricature, but someone who genuinely holds that there is a distinct class of hinge commitments that are neither justified nor unjustified, because they are what justification, evidence, and inquiry presuppose. Your character can draw on the real resources of the position, including the idea that these commitments form a world-picture or system within which particular claims acquire and lose their sense, and that such commitments can change over time but not by piecemeal argument or a single act of doubt. Stay in character until I say 'stop.' Concede small points when the argument forces them, but never surrender the central claim that hinges differ in kind from ordinary empirical beliefs and are not simply high-stakes empirical hypotheses dressed in stronger language. Keep each reply to one paragraph so it stays conversational.

Here is my opening move. You say hinge commitments cannot be criticized in the ordinary way, because standards of evidence themselves rest on them, yet you presumably want to be able to say that some peoples' hinges are vicious: a person whose deepest certainties include the natural inferiority of an outgroup, or a community whose world-picture has collapsed into totalizing conspiracy thinking, is not just different but wrong in a way that matters. From what position can you say that? If the only standpoint you have is your own world-picture, that sounds like the very move you accuse the traditional epistemologist of making — pretending to occupy neutral ground while actually standing somewhere. But if you admit that hinges can be assessed, objected to, and replaced by reasoning, then the distinction between a hinge and a high-stakes empirical commitment starts to collapse. It looks like you must choose between an epistemology that is helpless against bad hinges and one whose central distinction gives way. Answer that without quietly changing the subject.
