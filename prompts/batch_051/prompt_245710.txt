I'm in a philosophy of criminal law reading group, and we've been spinning our wheels since October on the paradox of blackmail. The setup is simple: I have a true, embarrassing fact about you that I'm legally free to publish, and it would be legal for me to just ask you for money. But if I put the two together — if I say I'll publish unless you pay me — that's blackmail. Why does the combination become a crime when each ingredient is legal on its own?

Our group has split into two camps that can't seem to make contact. One side thinks the combination is genuinely coercive: by making the payment a condition of staying quiet, I'm using your vulnerability to extract a benefit, and that exploitation is a new wrong — the conditional structure of the proposal itself leaves you worse off than before I made it. The other side thinks that's question-begging. Since I was free to publish regardless, an offer to stay quiet in exchange for money actually improves your position compared to what you were entitled to expect, so it looks like a sale rather then a threat. On that view the criminal prohibition is a purely pragmatic rule about the downstream incentives — discouraging secret-hunting and keeping the legal system from becoming a marketplace for reputations — not a reflection of something intrinsically wrong with the transaction itself.

We've agreed on the legal atoms and even on the downstream social harms, and still nobody moves. My hunch is that the real disagreement is about baselines: the first camp is using a descriptive baseline (compared to before the proposal, the target is worse off), while the second is using a moralized, rights-based baseline (compared to what the target was entitled to, they're better off). So maybe we're not actually disagreeing about blackmail at all — we're disagreeing about what makes a proposal coercive, and blackmail is just the case that exposes it. Is that diagnosis right? What kind of disagreement is this — verbal, a hidden formal question about how to define threats and offers, or a bedrock difference about what people are entitled to expect from each other? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress, or should we expect it to stay stalled?
