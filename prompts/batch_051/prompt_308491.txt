I'm a software engineer, and I do my clearest thinking in the vocabulary of version control and build pipelines: source, spec, interface, implementation, commit, branch, build, runtime instance, image, snapshot, deterministic and nondeterministic builds, port, fork. I want to understand a debate in the ontology of musical works well enough to restate it in that vocabulary rather than grope through philosophical terms.

The debate, as it has been described to me, is roughly this. Philosophers of music disagree about what sort of thing a musical work is. Some treat a work as an abstract type whose tokens are its performances and recordings, fixed by a score. Some treat it as a historical lineage of concrete performances and artifacts. Some treat it as reducible to the score or specification itself. They test these views against hard cases: whether a work exists before its first performance, whether two composers could compose the same work, what makes a badly played rendition still a performance of the work, what a recording of a performance is and what it is of, what an electronic track with no score is, and how loose templates such as jazz standards or partially indeterminate scores fit.

Restate the debate in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can, and make the mapping you are assuming explicit at each step. Then tell me what did not survive the translation. For each thing that failed to survive, say whether my vocabulary is missing a distinction the musical-ontology debate genuinely needs, or whether that part of the debate was doing less work than it looked. Where the translation would quietly import assumptions from version control or build systems, flag that rather than letting it pass as a neutral translation.
