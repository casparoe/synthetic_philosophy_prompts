I am the best man at my brother's wedding. He is Dutch, his bride is Japanese, and the two families bring sharply different norms about directness and indirectness: the Dutch guests tend to hear white lies and tactful omissions as a kind of disrespect, while the Japanese guests tend to hear unvarnished bluntness as rudeness. I have about five minutes for the toast. Write it so that this difference in norms is the speech's organizing tension, not something it either smooths over or mocks. The toast should take a genuine position on whether a white lie is ever the more respectful or loving act, without lecturing either family or celebrating both styles as mere decoration, and it should land as affection for the couple in language both families can accept.
