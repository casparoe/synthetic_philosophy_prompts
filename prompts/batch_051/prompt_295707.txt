I cover science and ideas, and I've been reporting a piece on how seafood certification schemes decide what counts as humane slaughter. I've got a source — a welfare scientist who sits on one of these boards — and I've been tracking her past calls. I need to predict how she'll vote next month, and I want a serious read on it.

Her track record, as best I can reconstruct from my notes:
- Farmed Atlantic salmon: she pushed hard to require electrical or percussive stunning before slaughter, and called CO2 stunning unacceptable.
- Farmed rainbow trout: she backed mandatory electrical stunning, and said killing in an ice slurry wasn't good enough.
- American lobster: she would not support a rule requiring stunning before boiling. Her reasoning was that the evidence for decapod pain is too contested and the equipment isn't there for small boats.
- Farmed whiteleg shrimp: she voted against mandatory stunning at slaughter, on the grounds that there was no validated commercial stunner and the evidence was too thin to justify the costs, but she pushed the board to fund crustacean-welfare research.

Now the new case. The board is voting on whether farmed whiteleg shrimp must be electrically stunned before slaughter. Two things have changed since her earlier shrimp vote: a validated in-line electrical stunner is now commercially available and would add only about 2–3% to processing cost, and a new scientific review concludes that decapod crustaceans meet the usual criteria for pain. If she's consistent with her own record, how does she vote?

Don't give me a menu of possibilities. Commit: give me your predicted vote, the rule you think she's actually running, the other rule(s) that fit her record nearly as well and what they would predict, which of her earlier calls is the closest analog, and the one further case you'd most want to see to settle whether you've got her right. Lead with the prediction, and keep the whole thing under 350 words.
