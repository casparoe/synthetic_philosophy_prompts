I'm trying to figure out whether there's a name for this, and I don't think it's simply the planning fallacy. I make quite detailed daily plans—hour by hour—and then mostly ignore them. But on days when I don't make a plan, I don't get more done; I just drift and feel vaguely anxious until the day's gone. The plan seems to matter mostly because it makes the day feel manageable in advance, not because I follow it. I've noticed something like it in groups too: a team will spend a whole afternoon building a detailed project timeline, everyone leaves the meeting visibly relieved, and then nobody looks at the document again. The work gets done anyway, just not on the schedule they wrote. So the planning is doing a different job than coordinating or predicting—it's closer to reassurance, or a little ritual that gives permission to start. My question is whether there's an established concept for plans whose real function is psychological in that way, and if there isn't, can you name and define it so that it doesnt collapse into the planning fallacy or into plain avoidance of starting?
