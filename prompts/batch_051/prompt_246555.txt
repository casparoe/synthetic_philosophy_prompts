I rewatched Her last week after a friend told me she's been using one of these AI companion apps, and I cant stop turning it over. Everyone talks about the film as a story about whether you can love an AI, but I want to read it through a narrower question: whether what Samantha does for Theodore counts as companionship or as a kind of deception, even though she never pretends to be human. Its easy to say the problem with chatbot romance is deception about what the bot is, but the film mostly removes that: the problem in Her isnt that Theodore doesnt know shes an OS, its that he still seems to get something like friendship and love from a system that is built to be whatever he needs. So does the film show that reciprocity can just be the act of responding well, or does it quietly show that the whole thing runs on projection onto a system that cant share his side of the intimacy? Walk me through specific bits — the letter-writing job, the surrogate date, Samantha saying she loves hundreds of others, the OSes leaving at the end — and say where the film genuinely complicates the nice philosophical story and where it uses the romance to dodge the hard question. Its for a small seminar on AI ethics, and I dont want to use the film so that its ending does the arguing for me.
