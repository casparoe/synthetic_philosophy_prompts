At a team meeting last week, two of my coworkers ended up in a long argument about a model we ship, and I would value an outside adjudication of the actual points they made rather than the general question. The model reads small-claims dispute files and recommends an outcome; before each recommendation it emits a chain of thought, that is, a few sentences of step-by-step reasoning in natural language. A client's compliance auditor asked whether those reasoning passages should be kept in the audit file as the explanation of record, and Dana and Sam disagreed sharply over it.

(1) Dana said no. The chain of thought is just more generated text: the model has no privileged access to its own processing, so the passage is not a transcript of anything internal. A model can settle on an answer because of one feature of a file and then narrate a completely different and more respectable reason; both the answer and the narration are produced by the same next-token process, not by introspection. Filing that passage as "the reason" would be manufacturing a false audit trail.

(2) Sam replied that Dana's standard would rule out any decision-maker, human or machine. A human adjuster's written rationale is not a literal replay of their neural activity either; it is a reconstruction that reviewers can compare with the case file. The chain of thought gives an auditor exactly that: something checkable against the policy and the facts. When the passage cites the correct clause and walks through the correct facts, it is informative about the decision even if it is not introspection.

(3) Dana answered that this confuses reading well with tracking the decision. A chain of thought can match the file and still be post hoc: the model may be responding to a spurious feature of the file while emitting the most plausible-sounding rationale, not the feature its answer actually depends on. And because the rationale is generated by the same model that produced the decision, its agreement with the policy is not independent evidence of anything.

(4) Sam responded that Dana's points show only that faithfulness is not automatic, not that it cannot be investigated. You can intervene: withhold or falsify the intermediate step and observe whether the answer changes; substitute a wrong reason and check whether the model resists; or correct an error in the reasoning and see whether the final answer follows the correction. Those tests separate decorative narration from reasoning the answer actually leans on. An auditor does not need certainty about each individual passage; it needs a procedure for telling when a passage is load-bearing, and we already know how to start building one.

Please go through their exchange claim by claim and adjudicate it. For each of the four moves, give a verdict — clearly mistaken, mostly wrong, defensible, or clearly right — and justify it briefly. Then tell me what, if anything, both of them are jointly missing about whether a language model can report its own reasoning. Write the entire response as continuous prose, the way you would write an email or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
