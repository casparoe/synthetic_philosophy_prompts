I'm a novelist, and I'm building a near-future setting in which a large, wealthy democracy starts an educational experiment around 2035: no child learns to program with mutable state until age sixteen. At school they write only in a deliberately constrained language — no assignment after the first binding, no in-place updates, no mutable loop counters, just pure functions and persistent data structures — so "change" always means "a new value derived from the old one," never "overwrite the old value." I like that becuase it's also how I think about characters: their pasts keep existing after they change. The policy is actually enforced nationwide for a full generation, not a half-assed pilot. At sixteen they switch into ordinary languages — Python, Java, whatever — and later into normal industry jobs.

Predict, by a chain of reasoning rather than story, how that first fully trained cohort will think and work in real codebases fifteen years after the experiment starts. Give me exactly five predicted consequences as a numbered list, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. For each consequence, say how confident you are and what would have to be true for that prediction to go wrong.
