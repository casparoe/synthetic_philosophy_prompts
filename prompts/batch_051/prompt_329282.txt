I write a small column on science and ideas — one week it's exoplanets, the next it's whether a Dyson sphere counts as art. I want to make a "which view of civilizational advancement do you actually hold?" quiz for readers. Every time I mention the Kardashev scale someone writes in to say energy is the wrong metric, and someone else writes in to say John Barrow's rival scale — rank a civilization by the smallest thing it can engineer, from genes down to Planck-scale physics — is just as arbitrary. I'd like the quiz to force readers to sort themselves by the cases they care about, not by which label sounds good.

Roughly eight to ten questions, two or three options each. Each question should be a concrete case, and I want at least these ones used: a Dyson swarm built only for crypto-mining; a nanotech civilization that never leaves its home planet; a civilization that deliberately caps its energy use to keep its night sky untouched; a galaxy-spanning civilization whose culture hasn't changed in a million years; and a matrioshka brain running uploaded minds. You can add cases, but no question should just ask "do you think energy use is what matters?" My readers are smart laypeople, so keep the cases vivid and don't lean on jargon you can't explain in a clause.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto positions roughly like these, but I want you to build from the distinctions first and only then named the positions: (1) energy maximalism, Kardashev-style; (2) miniaturization, Barrow-style; (3) information or computation; (4) autonomy or wise restraint — capability you choose not to use counts for more, not less; (5) pluralism or anti-ranking — there's no single scale and "advancement" is a projection or a loose family resemblance.

Also identify at least one answer pattern that would correspond to a position nobody seems to defend, with a one-line guess at whether it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any pair of positions your quiz can't reliably separate instead of pretending their distinct.

After the quiz and key, say how confident you are that the questions actually sort readers the way the key says, and single out the question you're least sure of.
