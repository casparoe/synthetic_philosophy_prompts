I teach metaphysics, and I keep having the origin essentialism discussion go sideways because students answer by labels (Kripkean, whatever) instead of by what they'd say about cases. So I want to make a handout: a "which origin essentialism are you actually committed to?" quiz. The underlying question is whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg than the one you actually came from. I'd like the quiz to have eight to ten questions, each one a very concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto positions rather than the other way around. No question should just ask students which label sounds right to them.

The positions I want it to sort into, as leaves:
- strict gamete essentialism (you're tied to that particular sperm and that particular egg, individually)
- zygote essentialism (the conception event / the zygote is the origin, so any change before fertilization gives a different person, but changes after don't)
- genetic essentialism (same genome is enough; numerically distinct gametes that happen to produce the same DNA would still give you)
- a psychological-continuity view (what matters is your mental life continuing, so your origin matters only insofar as it normally secures that)
- a deflationary / no-determinate-answer view (the question just doesnt have a well-defined answer across these cases)

Please build it from the logical space: first work out the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they actually allow, then locate the positions in that space. Flag any combinations of answers that are logically open but that nobody (as far as you know) defends, and say briefly why: incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored. And be honest about where the quiz can't separate positions. I suspect strict gamete essentialism and zygote essentialism may be nearly impossible to tell apart with the cases I have in mind, except maybe via twinning cases, and even then I'm not sure.

Cases I'd like you to at least consider using:
- a different sperm fertilizing the same egg (same act)
- the same sperm fertilizing a different egg
- numerically different sperm and egg that are chosen in vitro because they'd give the same DNA as yours
- the actual zygote splitting into monozygotic twins
- a clone made from the person's adult cell, no gametes involved
- the actual zygote implanted in a different womb
- the same sperm and egg, but fertilization happens in a lab dish a year later than it actually did
- a person produced from different gametes but with your full psychology imposed somehow (a science-fiction transplant at birth, say)

Some of those might not earn their keep; you can swap in better ones if you say why. The audience is bright undergrads who've done one week on the necessity of origin, so dont assume they know more than that.

Finally, the deliverable should say how confident you are in the claims it makes, and single out the point you're least sure about: a particular branch, a scoring assignment, or the claim that two positions can't be separated.
