I'm a novelist trying to write an essay on Borges's "The Library of Babel" through the lens of algorithmic randomness, and I'm going in circles. The library is a finite collection of books — every possible arrangement of 25 symbols over 410 pages, more or less. The idea I'm working with is that a finite string is random when the shortest program that prints it is no shorter than the string itself (the Kolmogorov–Solomonoff–Chaitin line). That makes the library both random and not: nearly every individual book is incompressible gibberish, but the whole collection has a very short description ("print every possible book"). I want a reading that takes both the story and that idea seriously — where the lens illuminates the story (the search for the Man of the Book, the cults, the narrators hope), where the story pushes back against it, and whether Borges is dramatizing something the formalism misses about randomness depending on the level you describe it from. Start with the reading itself, no preface or commentary.
