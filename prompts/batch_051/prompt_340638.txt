Ok, so I'm working on a novel where the whole ending turns on the main character realizing something during a long dream — the dream is what finally makes her leave her husband, or at least that's the current draft — and I keep going back and forth on whether I'm actually allowed to do that, given what I think dreams are. Before I build a plot on it I wrote down my standing views on dreams, and I want someone to poke holes in the set. Not to tell me what's true, just whether it all hangs togther.

So here's me:

(1) Dreams are experiences, full stop. While I'm dreaming there's something it's like, just as much as when I'm awake.

(2) But a dream that leaves no trace at all — I never remember it even once, it changes nothing in me — is not part of my mental life. I don't count it as something that happened to me.

(3) My sincere dream report is authoritative in a very strong way: if I wake up and say I dreamed of my father's house, then that's what I dreamed. No sleep scientist with a brain scan gets to tell me I'm wrong about that.

(4) Dream memory is fallible, like ordinary memory. I trust my memory of last night's dream about as much as I trust my memory of what I had for lunch on Tuesday — which is to say, roughly but not perfectly. I can definitely get details wrong.

(5) The dream is still richer than the report. When I describe a dream I'm summarizing; the dream had more specific detail, more rooms and faces, than I can ever get into words, the way a recap of a film leaves out most of the shots.

(6) And I'm a materialist about the mind. No extra mental stuff; the brain doing things is the whole story.

The question: which of these are actually in tension with each other, and which only look that way? For the real tensions, rank them by severity and tell me what the cheapest fix is for each, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
