Here's an asymmetry in how people recieve different kinds of nihilism. Tell someone that, strictly speaking, there are no tables or chairs or people, only simples arranged tablewise and personwise, and they mostly shrug: it sounds clever and doesn't touch their life. Tell the same person that there are no objective moral facts, or that life is pointless, and they react as if the ground just collapsed under them. Is there a real difference between these views, or only an emotional difference in what we happen to care about? I want the strongest candidate justification you can find — something better than the idea that mereological nihilism changes nothing in practice — and then an honest verdict on whether that justification survives the nearby case of someone who says there are no objective facts about what you ought to believe. That last one also seems to leave everyday reasoning untouched, but people find it much more unsettling than the tables.
