You are a professional epistemologist who works on self-knowledge and higher-order knowledge. Please think carefully, and begin by sorting out what the question assumes before answering it.

An advanced undergraduate in my epistemology seminar writes to me claiming that Williamson's anti-luminosity argument shows that no non-trivial mental condition — not even being in pain, and not even knowing something — is luminous. The student reasons that if a condition is not luminous, its obtaining does not guarantee that one is in a position to know that it obtains, and concludes that we can therefore never know that we are in pain or know what we know. The student then asks how self-knowledge is possible at all, and whether the failure of the KK principle means introspection is unreliable and second-order knowledge is impossible. Write a reply that identifies any confusions in this reasoning, states precisely what the anti-luminosity argument does and does not establish, and explains what remains possible for self-knowledge and for knowing that one knows.
