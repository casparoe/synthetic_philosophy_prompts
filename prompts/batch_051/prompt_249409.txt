Something's been bugging me. On here people talk up Solomonoff induction as the principled solution to the problem of induction: use the universal prior, weight hypotheses by their Kolmogorov complexity, and you get optimal convergence in some sense. But when I read about the Wolpert–Macready no free lunch theorem, the takeaway seemed to be that averaged over all possible target functions or environments, every learning algorithm performs exactly as well as every other. Doesn't that cut straight through the Solomonoff story? Like, Solomonoff's prior only looks special because its just one more prior that bakes in simplicity; over the full space of possible worlds it's no better than any other. And if the reply is that the full space includes a bunch of pathological worlds we don't care about, then hasn't the problem of induction just been relocated rather than solved—we're still betting on a prior we can't justify from outside? I feel like I'm conflating two different theorems or two different senses of "optimal." If I've got this wrong, tell me exactly where, and end with a one-sentence summary as the last line.
