I'm a software engineer and I somehow got roped into a philosophy reading group at work. We've been reading Boethius, The Consolation of Philosophy, and we're stuck on Book V, the part about divine foreknowledge. I don't have any formal philosophy background, so a lot of this is over my head. Last week the facilitator made us read the passage where Philosophy distinguishes between two kinds of necessity. In the translation we're using, the examples are roughly: when a man is walking, it is necessary that he is walking while he walks, but that is only the weaker kind of necessity; it doesn't mean his walking was forced or inevitable in the stronger sense. I'm probably not doing justice to it, but that's the passage.

Our facilitator has been giving us verdicts on cases. From my notes: God foreknows the sun will rise tomorrow — she said that's simple necessity. God knows a person is walking right now while he is walking — conditional necessity only. God foreknows a person will be startled by a sudden loud noise and jump — she said that's simple necessity because the response is automatic, not willed. God foreknows a person will choose tea over coffee after a long, messy deliberation — conditional necessity only, still free.

Next week's case is this: God foreknows that a person will make a decision after genuinely deliberating about it, but the decision is so firmly rooted in her settled character that she would make the same one in every nearby possible world. It isn't automatic, it isn't externally compelled, and God doesn't cause the decision. Our facilitator wants us to predict her verdict before she gives it. Act as an expert on Boethius, medieval philosophy, and action theory. Think carefully: start by brainstorming several distinct rules that fit her prior verdicts, including the tension between the startle case and the tea case. Then tell me which rule you think she is implicitly running, what that rule predicts about the new case, and whether the new case would be simply necessary or only conditionally necessary in her view. Finally, give me the one further hypothetical case that would best cleanly split the remaining candidate rules apart.
