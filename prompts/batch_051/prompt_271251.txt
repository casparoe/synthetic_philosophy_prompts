I've been defending what I've always taken to be Hofstadter's picture of creativity: that making something new is mostly a matter of finding the right knobs on an existing thing and turning them a lot, in combination, until the result feels fresh. Not genius from nothing, just high-dimensional knob-twiddling with good taste. My friend who paints says this is basically vacuous, because it doesn't tell you which knobs count or why turning one knob a little counts as creative while turning another one all the way is just a cover version of the same idea. I still think the picture can do real work, and I want to see wether it can at least rank cases.

So here's a batch of variations on existing works:

- Transposing a Bach two-part invention from C major to C-sharp major and leaving everything else identical.
- Rewriting a five-page proof with every variable renamed and the lemmas in reverse order, with no change to the actual argument.
- Taking a joke and swapping the priest for a chatbot but keeping the logic of the setup and punchline untouched.
- Changing a story's setting from Vienna in 1890 to a generation ship in the year 2300, keeping the same plot beats and scene order.
- Changing the protagonist of a novel from a man to a woman and giving her one new trait, while every scene she appears in otherwise remains the same.
- Rendering the same composition twice, once in a pointillist style built from small dots of color and once in flat vector shapes.
- Translating a sonnet while preserving meter and rhyme scheme.
- Replacing every second chord in a jazz standard with its tritone substitution, the chord rooted a tritone away, but keeping the melody and form.
- Performing a Chopin nocturne at half tempo with heavy rubato.
- Flipping a twist so the narrator turns out to be the killer instead of the butler, without changing any of the preceding scenes.

Sort these into four tiers, from basicaly clerical substitution to genuinely creative, under the knob-twiddling model, and say in a sentence why each lands where it does. Then tell me what your tiers are actually tracking: the number of knobs turned, how far downstream the effects ripple, how much the identity of the theme changes, surprise, or something else. Because whichever dimensions do the work are the dimensions a better version of the model has to name, and I want to know if my friend is right that the model as I stated it cannot name them.
