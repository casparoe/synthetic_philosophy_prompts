I recently had a long argument with a friend about whether the idea of eternal bliss is coherent, and specifically about Williams's Makropulos case. I want to write out where I landed before a reading group pushes back on it. Here are the commitments I left the argument with:

1. For endless temporal life, I accept Williams's point: a person with a finite human character and a finite stock of categorical desires — projects and attachments that give her reasons to go on living — cannot extend through infinite time without those desires eventually being exhausted, repeated until they lose their purchase, or replaced by ones that no longer express the same person.
2. But I do not think this threatens heaven as traditionally understood, because heaven is not an endless sequence of days; it is an atemporal state, participation in a timeless good rather than an unending series of satisfying moments.
3. I also hold that an afterlife is worth hoping for only if it is mine: for the blessed person to be me, there must be substantial continuity of memory, character, and desire with the person I am now.
4. Yet I also hold that one can rationally want to be radically transformed, to be made capable of a form of life one cannot now find attractive, without thereby wanting someone else rewarded in one's place.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest single revision would be to make the set consistent. I would rather not revise any of them, but I also do not want to present a muddle.
