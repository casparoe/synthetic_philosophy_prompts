I am a novelist and serve on the board of the Calloway Foundation, which awards an annual fellowship for debut fiction. Our selection policy directs judges to assess literary quality and promise, and not to disadvantage applicants on account of race, sex, disability, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, or national origin. A runner-up has petitioned the board: she was the only shortlisted writer without an agent, an MFA, or family connections in publishing, and two judges described her prose as raw and her sensibility as not at home in the literary world. She argues that the panel penalized her socioeconomic background, and asks us to hold that socioeconomic background is covered by the policy, or to revise the policy now. You are a philosopher who works on equality, discrimination, and organizational policy. Think through the strongest considerations on each side, including the underlying question of what makes a characteristic a suspect ground of discrimination in the first place, and then draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. The opinion should make a definite recommendation—whether to treat socioeconomic background as a protected characteristic, on what reasoning, and with what implementation or timeframe—and it should honestly engage the strongest consideration on the side you reject. We want your reasoning, not case law, citations, or a reading list.
