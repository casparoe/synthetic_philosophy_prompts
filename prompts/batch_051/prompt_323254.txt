I am preparing a film-and-philosophy unit on why human extinction matters beyond the deaths it involves, and I want to pair Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture with Alfonso Cuarón's Children of Men. Scheffler's conjecture, as I understand it, is that our confidence that humanity will continue after our own deaths is a hidden precondition for much of what we value: if we came to believe the human race would end, many of our projects, relationships, institutions, and reasons would lose their grip even though our own deaths are not what is at issue. The film can be read as working through one of the standard ways this conjecture is made vivid — not the doomsday variant in which the species is destroyed shortly after one's own death, but the infertility variant, in which everyone alive lives out a normal life knowing that no further generations will follow.

I want a reading that treats the film as a working-through of the conjecture. Where does Children of Men dramatize the claim — for instance, in what has already happened to ordinary ambition, long-term institutions, the state, art, or care in a world without successors? Where does the film complicate or resist the claim? Several things seem to resist a tidy fit: the characters are not facing their own premature end, only the end of the human serial line after them; the film's political violence is driven by migration, fear, and an authoritarian state rather than by explicit grief over the future; and the response to the baby carried by Kee seems aimed at something more immediate than the backward-looking confidence Scheffler emphasizes — perhaps natality, hope, or the future as such. Does the conjecture need to be revised so that it distinguishes caring that someone will come after me from caring that the human story is not finished at all, even if I will have no direct successors? And what does the film add to the argument that argument alone cannot capture, and where should an honest reading say the two come apart? I want a reading that is faithful in both directions: do not reduce the film to an illustration of the theory, and do not distort the theory to fit the scenes.
