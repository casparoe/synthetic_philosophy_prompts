I work as a backend engineer on a voice-commerce feature that lets users verbally accept purchase offers. When a buyer says yes to a seller's offer, our system passes the audio through a voice-activity detector to isolate the segment, transcribes it, and writes an acceptance event to the database; until that write commits, nothing else in the product treats the buyer as bound. I have been reading about the idea that some utterances are performative: in the right circumstances, saying the words I accept does not merely report acceptance but is itself the act of accepting. If that is right, it sounds as though the buyer accepts at the moment the words are spoken. But my product manager insists the true moment of acceptance is the database commit, since that is when the system begins treating the buyer as bound, and the legal team seems comfortable with that view. One of our ML engineers thinks the relevant moment is when the speech-to-text model emits the transcript, because that is when the words first enter the system as text. I have been asked to choose a timestamp event for dispute resolution. At which point in this pipeline does the speech act actually occur—the acoustic event, the detector boundary, the transcription, or the database commit—and what should I tell the team about the relationship between the utterance itself and the system state that records it?
