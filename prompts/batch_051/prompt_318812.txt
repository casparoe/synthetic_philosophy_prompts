I’ve been having this endless arguement in a group chat about whether “just being honest” and “being rude” are actually the same thing, and it’s made me think about the cliché of Dutch directness versus Japanese indirectness. One friend insists Dutch-style bluntness is honesty with the sugarcoating removed, and that every white lie or polite fiction is a small moral failure even when it’s kind. Another friend says that’s ridiculous: Japanese-style indirectness isn’t lying, it’s a more sophisticated way to transmit the same information while preserving face, and what we call a “white lie” is usually not a lie at all but a social signal about care. I have some half-remembered philosophy floating around about whether lying to spare someone’s feelings is always wrong, but I don’t trust my memory on it, so don’t treat that as a citation.

I want you to write an ironic piece in the form of a glossy “cross-cultural honesty app” product page / self-assessment FAQ, around 700 words. The app, let’s call it Candour, sells modules like “Rotterdam Mode” and “Osaka Mode” for calibrating how directly you say what you mean. White lies should appear as a premium subscription tier. The deadpan startup voice should stay consistent the whole way through. The real target should be a pair of lazy positions: that bluntness is pure truth and indirectness is just deception. The humour should come from actually taking those positions too seriously and letting them collide with the idea of selling a cultural style as a personal authenticity product. Try to make it funny to someone who has thought about pragmatics and face-saving, but still readable to someone who just thinks it’s about their Dutch friend who says your haircut looks bad.

Don’t reduce Japanese politeness to lying or Dutch bluntness to cruelty—those would be the cheap versions of the joke. The piece should leave the reader with the feeling that both styles are doing communicative work, and that the real puzzle is why we call some of that work “honesty” and some of it “lying.” Maybe end with an absurd user testimonial.
