Sorry this is long, I've been turning it over alot. My brother runs a small restaurant, and three weeks ago a local activist with a pretty big following did a live Instagram video calling him a slumlord, a wage thief, and a fraud, none of which is true as near as I can tell. She didn't tell anyone to do anything — just laid out the story and said "make of it what you will." Within hours the video was clipped and posted to Nextdoor and a local subreddit, someone dropped his home address in a comment, and about thirty people showed up outside the restaurant that night shouting and filming. He's fine, but he's scared and his rating got destroyed. I keep hearing people say that if a tolerant society tolerates intolerant speech, the intolerant speech eventually eats the tolerance, and I get the fear, but I also don't want to hand anyone the power to decide which criticism counts as too much.

I keep coming back to this passage from Mill's On Liberty, Chapter III, where he tries to describe where even opinion stops being protected:

"No one pretends that actions should be as free as opinions. On the contrary, even opinions lose their immunity, when the circumstances in which they are expressed are such as to constitute their expression a positive instigation to some mischievous act. An opinion that corn-dealers are starvers of the poor, or that private property is robbery, ought to be unmolested when simply circulated through the press, but may justly incur punishment when delivered orally to an excited mob assembled before the house of a corn-dealer, or when handed about among the same mob in the form of a placard."

Mill obviously never imagined a livestream that a mob assembles itself around after the fact. So my question is: apply the passage to this case. Does the livestream count as "simply circulated through the press" (protected) or as delivered to an excited mob (punishable)? What exactly is the feature that moves a case from one side of his line to the other, and does that feature show up in what happened to my brother? I want you to run it through each of the usual approaches — the literal words, the purpose of the distinction, and what Mill would probably say if he watched the clip — and tell me which approach should win and why. And if none of them cleanly handles a distributed online mob, say whether that means Mill's line itself was dirty or just that our examples need updating.
