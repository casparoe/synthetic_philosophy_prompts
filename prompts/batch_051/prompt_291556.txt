I had a long argument with a friend last night about whether the legal reasoning at Nuremberg was anything more than victors’ justice with nicer clothes, and I keep turning over one sentence from the Court’s judgment. The Court is responding to the defense claim that the defendants were being punished under ex post facto law. It says:

"the maxim nullum crimen sine lege is not a limitation of sovereignty, but is in general a principle of justice. To assert that it is unjust to punish those who in defiance of treaties and assurances have attacked neighbouring states without warning is obviously untrue, for in such circumstances the attacker must know that he is doing wrong, and so far from it being unjust to punish him, it would be unjust if his wrong were allowed to go unpunished."

That’s from the Judgment of the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg, 1946.

My friend’s whole point was that the phrase “not a limitation of sovereignty, but is in general a principle of justice” is basically the Court admitting the game: when sovereigns want to punish enemies, the principle gives way; when it suits them to invoke it, they call it justice. I don’t know if he’s being too cynical. So here’s the thing I actually want help with. I don’t just want another moral verdict on Nuremberg. I want to know whether that sentence makes a claim about how legal systems actually treat nullum crimen that could be tested. What would you count as evidence for or against the claim that the maxim functions as a real constraint on power rather than as a flexible legitimating story that gets cited when convenient? Try to design the best empirical test you can, using things like how later tribunals and courts invoke or avoid the principle, what institutional arrangements would show it constraining governments, and what result would convince you either way. And then be honest: what exactly would even a clean positive result fail to settle, since the Tribunal itself seems to run together a descriptive point about how law works and a normative point about when punishment is just? Oh, and please write your answer in American English.
