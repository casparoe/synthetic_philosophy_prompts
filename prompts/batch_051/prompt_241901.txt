At a summer math program, I read about a heuristic model of the primes that treats "n is prime" as if it were a random event with probability 1/ln n near n, and assumes that the events "n is prime" and "n+2 is prime" are independent. This independence assumption is doing a lot of work: it is what makes the twin prime conjecture look as though infinitely many prime pairs should exist. But the primes are a fixed, deterministic sequence, so there is no actual coin to inspect. I would like to know whether the claim that primality behaves as if these events were independent is something that can be tested. What measurement or computation could bear on it, what would a positive or negative result show, and which part of the claim would no finite test ever settle?
