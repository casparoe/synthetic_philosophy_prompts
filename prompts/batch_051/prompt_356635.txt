i teach an applied ethics seminar (second-year undergrads, about 18 people, one 80-minute session) and we're about to start a unit on sweatshops. I want an in-class role-play or game where students actually feel the pull of both sides: the factory owner isn't forcing anyone, and the workers are better off taking the job than their next-best option, but the deal still strikes us as exploitative. I don't want the difference between 'it harmed them,' 'it was coercive,' and 'it took unfair advantage of them' to be lectured up front — the setup should make students run into it through roles, choices, and payoffs. Please give me concrete roles, two or three rounds, and include at least one round with asymmetric information (like the buyers know the true value of the order or what other workers are being paid) so the class has to confront the line between tough bargaining and exploiting someone's ignorance. Use a points-based payoff scheme, no real money. Then a debrief that maps what happened in the game to the idea that exploitation can be mutually beneficial while still counting as a wrong. Also say what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about witch part of real sweatshop exploitation the game just can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself.
