I'm a novelist, and I'm kicking around a idea for a near-future story that I could use a hand with. The seed is this: the protagonist wears a small device — an earbud, maybe, or a contact-lens display — that continuously predicts her own choices a few seconds out. It's not magic, just a unnervingly good learned model of her. At first she uses it for low-stakes stuff, like what she'll order for lunch or whether she'll hit snooze, and it's spooky-correct. Then she starts leaning on it for bigger things: whether to quit her job, whether to answer a message from her ex. But a choice isn't a weather forecast. Once she sees the prediction, she can't just update on it and move on, because she's the one deciding. Sometimes she goes along with it, sometimes she rebels just to prove it wrong, and the more she treats the device as a guide, the more her own deliberation starts to feel like a spectator sport. The prediction surfaces, she watches herself either obey or resist, and it nags at her whether there's a real "her" in there making a call, or just a loop of forecast and reaction.

What I'd like from you is a short story, maybe 900–1200 words, that sits inside that experience rather than explaining it. I'm less interested in the gadget's mechanics than in the phenomenology: the sense of your own deciding hollowing out when a prediction gets in ahead of it, and the way probability assignments to your own next act go unstable, even self-undermining, once you try to use them to settle what to do. The ending can go where you like — maybe she smashes the thing, maybe she finds some fragile way to keep choosing. Just begin with the story itself, no preface or commentary.
