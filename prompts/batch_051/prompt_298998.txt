Here’s a thing I keep going in circles on. People get genuinely angry about governments “printing money” — they say it’s fake wealth, its bound to destroy savings, etc. But when a commercial bank gives someone a mortgage and just credits their account with a deposit, nobody seem to treat that as money printing, even though from what I can tell that’s how most money actually gets created: banks make loans and deposits appear. So both are, in some sense, creating money, but one gets treated as reckless counterfeiting of wealth and the other as normal boring banking. Is there a real distinction that justifies the asymmetry — who gets to spend the new money, whether it’s a loan versus a purchase, what the money is backed by, something about the bank having to get it back later — or are people just angrier at the more visible one? Then tell me honestly whether that distinction survives, and also: when people say printing money “creates wealth,” are they making a category mistake, just wrong, or right in a sense I’m missing? Start with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating the question.
