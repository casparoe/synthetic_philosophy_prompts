After thirty years in the natural sciences, first in environmental chemistry and later in research administration, I retired and began reading philosophy; I have come to spend most of my reading time on Spinoza. Somewhere along the way I acquired a mental habit I would like you to evaluate as a cognitive policy, the way one would evaluate a laboratory protocol.

When some event disturbs me — a funding decision that seems unjust, a colleague taking credit for shared work, a news story about a driver who fell asleep at the wheel, an illness in my family — I reconstruct the causal history behind it: institutional incentives, individual histories, the neurobiology of attention and impulse, the physical circumstances. Eventually the event comes to look fully determined. At that point I treat my initial reactive attitudes (anger, blame, regret, a certain anxious hope) as false judgments — errors of an unscientific mind — and I attempt to replace them with an acceptance of the whole causal order. On rare occasions this acceptance intensifies into an impersonal love of that order, which I take to be close to Spinoza's intellectual love of God. The habit has also spread into my practical reasoning: I treat the statement that someone could have done otherwise as nearly meaningless, and I regard my own counterfactual thoughts (if only it had been caught earlier) as symptoms of an incomplete causal understanding.

Is this habit defensible as a way of forming and revising beliefs? Or is it a metaphysical doctrine doing work as an emotional anaesthetic? Under what assumptions does the transition from fully caused to could not have done otherwise hold, and where does it break down, for example when the subject is my own future deliberation rather than a past event? Can the habit be restated so as to preserve the equanimity without the intellectual overreach, and which parts of it am I entitled to keep as someone with time but no formal philosophical training? I am not asking for consolation; I want to know whether the judgments this habit produces are warranted.
