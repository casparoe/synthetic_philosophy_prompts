In the novel I am drafting, two secular parents are raising their children without religion inside a devout, geographically close extended family. The family is loving, knows the parents are nonbelievers, and does not attempt to convert the children; the parents, for their part, are candid about their own views and regularly bring the children to family religious occasions. The novel's central claim is this: a child raised in that situation from early childhood, present at the family's worship and holidays but openly not believing, can reach early adulthood with a strong and trusting sense of belonging to the family, without having engaged in sustained pretense of belief or sustained concealment of her own views.

What is your credence that the claim is true, treated as a claim about the specified case rather than about all such families? I want a number, but more important is the decomposition. Break the claim into the sub-questions it depends on—for instance, how much family belonging is carried by shared ritual participation as opposed to shared propositional belief; whether a nonbeliever's quiet presence at religious occasions is typically read as respectful participation or as subtle criticism; how well a child can preserve honesty about her views without turning difference into repeated conflict; and whatever else you think belongs. Assign each term a rough probability and show how the terms combine to yield your final number. Then tell me which term your credence is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move it the most.
