I have been thinking for years about why I deliberately seek out books and films that make me cry, and why the crying itself feels strangely welcome. I have no training in philosophy or psychology, so I have worked out my own explanation, and I would like someone to pressure-test it honestly rather than just reassure me that it is plausible.

I call it the rehearsal account. Sad stories, on my view, give us exercise in being undone. When I watch a character lose someone, something in me produces a close cousin of real grief, but in a cooled-down form. I distinguish between hot sadness, which is attached to a real loss that is mine and cannot be switched off, and cold sadness, which is vivid but knows somewhere in the background that the loss is not mine and that I can stop the film or shut the book. The enjoyment is not the sadness itself. It is the repeated discovery that I can let a feeling wreck me and still come out intact by the end. As I put it to myself, each sad story is a rehearsal for actual grief: I practice being destroyed in safe conditions, and the pleasure is the pleasure of discovering that I am still the kind of creature who can care about things enough to be hurt and still survive being hurt. The view makes a prediction, too: people who have not yet undergone much real loss should be the hungriest for sad fiction, because they have the most need to rehearse, while people in the middle of real grief should avoid it, because the safety has been removed.

I know there are older ideas about tragedy purging or cleansing the emotions, and about art letting us process feelings, and my account may simply collapse into one of them. What I want is a candid appraisal. Is the hot/cold distinction doing real work or is it just a metaphor with labels on it? Is the rehearsal account genuinely different from the purging/cleansing idea, and if it is, where does it break? In particular, how well does it handle the fact that people rewatch the exact scenes that hurt them most, or the people who seem drawn to sad art precisely because it is not safe?
