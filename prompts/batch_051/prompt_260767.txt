I help run a standing seminar for graduate students working on AI safety and the philosophy of mind, and our next session is on chain-of-thought faithfulness: the question whether the step-by-step text a language model produces when prompted to "think step by step" can be read as a report of how it actually reached its answer. The group keeps talking past itself because different members mean different things by "faithful." Some mean that the intermediate text plays the causal role in producing the answer; some mean that it accurately describes the considerations or features the model really used; some think faithfulness is entirely a matter of how the model was trained; and some think the very phrase "reporting its own reasoning" smuggles in an introspection the system does not have.

I would like you to write a sorting instrument for this group: eight to ten multiple-choice questions, each built around a concrete case, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions in the debate. Build the questions from cases such as the following: a superficial feature of the input wording flips the model's answer though the model's stated rationale never mentions it; paraphrasing the chain of thought while preserving its content leaves the final answer unchanged, but inserting a false intermediate step shifts it; an intervention on the model's internals shows the verdict is computed before the explanatory tokens are produced; a model is fine-tuned with process supervision to verbalize each step; a model's confident self-reports vary from one sample to the next while the final answer stays fixed. Each question should offer two or three choices, and no question may ask respondents directly whether chain of thought "reflects inner computation" or to pick a label they like.

Your scoring key must include at least four leaves: (1) transparency—the chain of thought is, or can be, a genuine first-person report of the model's reasoning; (2) fallible realism—the chain of thought can both carry and report the reasoning, but it is also subject to post-hoc rationalization and confabulation; (3) training-dependence—faithfulness is not a general capacity of language models but a property of particular training signals and conditions; and (4) behaviorism—there is no further inner fact of the matter, so "faithful" can only describe reliable input–output regularities. Add further leaves only where the logical space forces them. After the key, flag any pairs of leaves the instrument cannot honestly separate, and identify any answer combinations that are logically possible but empty, with a one-sentence guess about why nobody occupies them. The audience is technically literate but new to this debate, so keep every scenario self-contained.
