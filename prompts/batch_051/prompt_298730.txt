In my decision theory course we have been discussing Buridan's ass. In the familiar version, an ass exactly halfway between two identical bales of hay is indifferent between them and therefore, it is said, cannot choose and starves. Consider this modification. The ass is an expected-utility maximizer: eating from either bale yields utility 1, starving yields utility 0, and the only two pure actions are going left and going right. The ass can also adopt a mixed strategy, committing in advance to go left with probability p and right with probability 1−p, and its utility is linear in probabilities in the standard expected-utility sense. Work through how expected-utility theory evaluates the two pure acts and the full set of mixed strategies. Then discuss whether allowing mixed strategies dissolves the original problem or merely relocates the problem of choice without preference to the choice of p, and give your own diagnosis of whether the ass is still in a decision-theoretically interesting predicament.
