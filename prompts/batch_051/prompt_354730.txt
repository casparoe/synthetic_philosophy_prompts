I keep noticing a bit of conversational policing that I want you to unpack. Suppose someone says flat-out, "Priya's moved to Manchester." The usual response isn't "Why do you believe that?" or "Is that true?" It's "How do you know?" And if the answer is something like "I think she mentioned it once, but I might be misremembering," the reply tends to be "well you shouldn't of said it like that, then." Treat that practice—the way we challenge and correct assertions—as a piece of data, and reconstruct what it presupposes about the norm governing assertion. Does it strictly presuppose a knowledge norm, or could a truth norm, a belief norm, or a justification norm license the same practice? I want you to distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say what would actually change in everyday conversational practice if we swapped in one of the rival norms. When you answer, please seperate what's settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
