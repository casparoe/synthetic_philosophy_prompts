My uncle, who is an engineer and way too confident about a few youtube channels, sent me a long message this week about the "gender equality paradox" and I can't tell how much of it to take seriously. His summary, roughly: researchers looked at countries scored on gender-equality indexes and found that the more equal a country is, the bigger the sex differences in what people end up studying and working in. Scandinavia is supposedly the star case — Norwegian and Swedish women cluster in nursing and men in engineering — while in countries like Algeria or the UAE a higher share of STEM graduates are women. He says the lesson is obvious: once a society stops pushing men and women into roles, people's real natures finally get to express themselves, and it turns out they are more different than anyone thought. From there he concludes that the STEM gap must be substantially biological, that social-constructionist accounts are basically dead, and that programs pushing girls into coding are trying to force people into careers they don't actually want, which he thinks is illiberal or even unjust. I have an easier time believing the underlying data than the part where he leaps from equal countries showing bigger sex differences in choice to so those differences are innate and equal-outcomes programs are unjust, and I keep going back and forth on whether he's distorting the science or just drawing conclusions I don't like. Can you separate for me which parts of his account a careful researcher in that field would nod along with, where exactly he's overreading, and whether any of his bigger social or political conclusions survive once the finding is stated carefully?
