I'm on the executive committee of my university's senate, and we're deciding whether to co-sign an administration statement that begins "We deeply regret that last year's tuition increase forced low-income students into debt." Half the committee says we can't sign: the sentence presupposes a causal claim the senate never established, and co-signing would place that claim in the public record. The other half says that's confused, since presuppositions aren't assertions and you can sign an apology without vouching for every background proposition embeded in it. One member just wants to amend it to "We regret the stress students experienced," which drops the causal claim. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's vote: recommend a concrete course of action, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side, including some account of what it even means for a committee to "concede" something by not challenging its presuppositions.
