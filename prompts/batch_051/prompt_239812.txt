So my brother has spent the last couple months going down a rabbit hole about hell — he was always the fire-and-brimstone one, and now after our nan died he's swung all the way to annihilationism and he's definately not going to drop it. His main argument is an analogy, and he wheels it out constantly: he says if a parent grounded their kid for a year over one stolen biscuit, we'd all say the parent was cruel and the punishment was wildly out of proportion, so how is a finite life of ordinary sin getting *infinite* punishment any different? It's basically the parking-ticket version of hell. He says the doctrine of eternal torment treats God like exactly that parent — a judge handing out sentences where the length is supposed to match the size of the crime, except the crime is finite and the sentence isn't, so the only fair options are annihilation or universal salvation. I keep feeling the analogy is doing way too much work but I can't articulate exactly where it breaks.

Can you lay out what maps onto what in the parent-and-biscuit picture — who's the child, who's the parent, what's the biscuit, what's the grounding — and where the mapping quietly imports things from parenting that don't carry over to God punishing souls? Like, a parent grounds a kid partly to correct them, a parent has limited authority, a parent is dealing with someone who can't fully understand what they did... none of that obviously applies. And maybe the crime isn't really finite because it's against an infinite being, or maybe hell isn't a "sentence length" at all but a settled state you stay in. Then tell me whether his conclusion actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table — does the analogy genuinely force you toward annihilationism or universalism, or does it only work by quietly smuggling in a retributive, criminal-sentencing picture of hell that the other options could reject too? I don't want bible verses, I want to know if the argument itself is any good.
