I've been putting together a little position statement on responsibility gaps for autonomous systems for a workshop, and I want someone who knows this stuff to stress-test it before I circulate it. Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in moral responsibility and the ethics of technology. Think through each pair of commitments carefully; start by brainstorming the possible tensions, then sort out which are real, which are merely apparent, and what the cheapest fix would be for each.

Here's where I've landed:

1. Moral responsibility in the full sense—the kind that licenses resentment, indignation, and punishment—requires both the capacity to recognize the relevant reasons and a fair opportunity to act on them. I'm a compatibilist, so I don't think determinism matters here.

2. No current or near-term AI system has either capacity, so no such system is a morally responsible agent.

3. When a self-driving car or an autonomous weapon causes a serious harm and the designers, operators, and users all behaved reasonably (no one negligent or reckless), there is a genuine responsibility gap: the harm is caused by the system, but there is no human to who full moral blame attaches.

4. Still, I think the law can and should respond to those harms through strict liability and organizational penalties, and that this isn't merely a pragmatic cost-shifting exercise—it can express a real moral judgment about an organization's failure to ensure safety.

5. At the same time, I think the responsibility gap is overblown as a moral problem. Tort law's main functions are compensation and deterrence, not tracking moral desert, so the absence of a culpable individual doesn't really threaten the justifiability of liability.

6. I also believe we shouldn't deploy systems whose behavior no one can explain, and that manufacturers have a moral duty to keep a meaningful human in the loop for high-stakes decisions.

7. But I also think it's wrong to resent or punish a corporation the way we resent or punish a person; organizations aren't the kind of thing that can be the target of the reactive attitudes.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each tension, say whether it's real or only apparent, what the cheapest fix is, and which single revision would leave me with the most coherent overall position.
