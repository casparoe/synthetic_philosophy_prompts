I'm an engineer on a small team that builds a scoring model for a temp-staffing app. We have the same fight every time someone proposes a new input: commute distance, years of experience, gaps in work history, college attended, once even a vector embedding of the applicant's name. Legal tells us not to discriminate on race, sex, age, disability and the other protected characteristics, but that gives us nothing for features that aren't a protected category on paper and still feel like one underneath. Our head of product keeps saying if it predicts, it ships, and I've got no philosophy background to push back with. I've heard the usual candidate ideas — immutability, a history of the trait being used against a group, whether the trait is even relevant to the actual job — but I can't turn those into a step-by-step. I want a written procedure we can run on any proposed feature to decide weather it's suspect in the same way a protected characteristic is, including proxies, interactions of two innocent-looking fields, and behaviors that mostly act as stand-ins for a protected class. Legal will review the procedure once and after that we're on our own, so it has to be something a PM or a new engineer can run in under an hour. It should mark clearly where it stops with a no, don't use this, where it passes, and where it hands the decision to a human because its a values question the procedure can't settle. Then run the procedure on these three candidate features: (1) years of direct experience, with a minimum of three; (2) commute distance in miles; (3) total months of employment gaps over the last five years. I especially want to see it flag one that our team currently uses and thinks is obviously fine, and be honest about where the procedure will misfire.
