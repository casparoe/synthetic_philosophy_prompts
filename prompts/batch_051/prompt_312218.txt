My friend Priya moderates readers comments for a local news site, and she has a very particular track record on what counts as a racist comment. I've been keeping a list so I can predict her rulings, and I want you to help me reverse-engineer the rule. Cases so far: a white commenter posted "I'm not a racist, but this neighborhood has really changed" under a story about a new refugee resettlement program, and she deleted it and said it was a dog whistle. She deleted a comment from a white guy who used the n-word while enthusiastically praising a rap album, even after he insisted he was just quoting lyrics and meant no offense. She left up a Black commenter who wrote "white folks will never get it" on an editorial about policing, and said it wasn't the same thing. She left up a white commenter who argued with a bunch of charts that school discipline gaps are mostly about poverty rather than race; she said she disagreed but it wasn't racist. She deleted "we need to take our country back" from a city council candidate commenting on immigration, and said that one was the clearest dog whistle yet.

Next week she has to rule on three things. (1) A white substitute teacher writes in saying he told a Black student "you're so articulate" and the parents complained; he says it was a sincere compliment. (2) Under a crime story, a commenter posts "62% of shooting suspects were Black last year, but Black people are only 24% of the population. Just the data." Nothing else. (3) The op-ed page runs a piece by a white columnist arguing that Asian-American students succeed because their culture values hard work and education, and that other communities should adopt those values.

Predict what Priya does with each of the three, tell me which rule she's actually running — intent, effect, or dog whistle/coded meaning — which of the other rules fit her record just as well, and which new case (or what further case) would settle it. And write the answer in American English.
