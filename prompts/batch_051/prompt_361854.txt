I'm a high school student helping run a philosophy club, and last week we discussed the problem of evil. The handout we used summarized the debate roughly like this: the logical problem of evil says that the existence of any evil at all is logically incompatible with an all-powerful, all-knowing, all-good God. The standard reply, the free will defense, says God could not have created a world containing moral good without thereby creating the possibility of evil, because even an omnipotent being cannot make someone freely choose good. And since a world with free beings who sometimes do wrong is better than a world without free beings, God's existence is compatible with evil after all. The handout then concluded that the free will defense fully settles the problem of evil, because once God and evil are shown not to be strictly contradictory, the existence of evil provides no reason at all to doubt that God exists.

I suspect the last step is too quick and that the handout conflates the logical problem of evil with the evidential problem, but I'm not confident enough to identify exactly where it goes wrong or which parts a defender of the free will defense would accept. Please go through the summary point by point and help me sort what is accurate, what is a simplification, and what is an overstatement, and then say whether you think the handout is on balance fair. If I am misstating the free will defense or drawing the logical/evidential distinction in a confused way, tell me so directly and correct my framing rather than working inside it.
