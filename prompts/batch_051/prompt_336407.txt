I am a novelist at work on a book about a woman who takes a job in a garment factory knowing that the conditions are degrading, because every alternative open to her is worse, and I have returned to Upton Sinclair's The Jungle as a moral and structural touchstone. I do not want the familiar muckraking reading of it, but something more philosophically exact. The debate I have in mind is the one over sweatshops and mutually beneficial exploitation: the claim that a labour bargain can be fully voluntary and can leave the worker better off than under her next-best alternative, yet still be exploitative in a way that should trouble a consumer or an employer. The interesting question then is what property of the exchange carries the wrong-making weight, whether the disparity of power, the level or unfairness of the wage, the background poverty that shaped the alternatives, the division of the value the worker produces, or something else.

Could you write a substantial critical reading of The Jungle through that lens, one that treats the novel as more than an illustration of a philosophical position? I am especially interested in the places where the novel and the philosophy pull apart. Which episodes invite the reading that the stockyards jobs were a genuine, possibly rational improvement over the family's alternatives in Lithuania, and where does Sinclair quietly undercut that reading by multiplying fraud, wage cuts, credit traps, blacklists, and physical danger until the arrangement stops looking mutually beneficial at all? Does he ever depict a bargain that is on balance beneficial by the worker's own lights and nonetheless exploitative, or does his condemnation always secretly depend on the job having turned into catastrophe? And what, in your view, does the novel contribute to the philosophical debate that a cleaner analytic treatment of sweatshop labour tends to miss? Please ground the reading in particular episodes without quoting from the text, and be explicit about where the novel resists the analytic categories rather than conforming to them.
