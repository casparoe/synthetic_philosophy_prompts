I'm in an upper-level seminar on well-being and we've been stuck on the heterogeneity objection to hedonism for a couple weeks now, and from what I can tell the literature has been circling the same point forever. The debate in our group goes like this. Hedonism says well-being is pleasure minus pain, which only works if "pleasure" picks out one kind of thing. The objectors point out that the pleasure of eating when you're starving, the pleasure of finally getting a proof to work, the pleasure of a hot shower, and the pleasure of seeing someone you dislike fail all feel completely different — they have nothing in common as sensations. So either hedonism is treating a gerrymandered disjunction as a single value, or the word "pleasure" is too heterogeneous to do the job. One camp replies that beneath all the differences there's a single hedonic tone, a common felt quality that makes each of them pleasant; the differences are in the content of the experience, not in the pleasantness itself. The other camp says there's no such felt quality to be found — you can introspect as long as you like and you won't locate it — and that pleasure is better understood as an attitude of liking or favoring an experience, not a sensation at all. Everyone in the group agrees about the descriptions of the experiences, and agrees that the experiences are good for the person, but the two camps haven't moved an inch, and I honestly cant tell whether they're even talking about the same question.

My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about how experiences feel. It's about what "pleasure" has to be for hedonism to stay interesting: one side thinks hedonism needs a single felt quality to be a real monistic view, and the other thinks it only needs whatever property all these good experiences share in virtue of being good for the person — which might turn out to be an attitude, or even a family-resemblance category. That would make it a partly verbal, partly theoretical disagreement about what the theory is supposed to do, not a dispute about introspection. But I'm not sure, and it could be that there's a genuine introspective or empirical question hiding underneath that I'm just not seeing.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, is it substantive-about-phenomenology, or are the two camps answering different questions under the single word "pleasure"? Hidden in your diagnosis should be a view about what would count as progress: a distinction that dissolves it, a claim about introspection that could be checked, a reformulation of hedonism, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why. And please write the answer in American English.
