Consider a research community that adopts the following norm as an institutional substitute for individual intellectual modesty. Before anyone may criticize a colleague's argument, they must first state, in the same venue, what they take to be the strongest objection to their own current position, and the colleague is entitled to ignore any criticism that does not begin this way. Replies to criticism are exempt, and the self-critical preface is signed rather than anonymous. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral ideal. What behavior would it select for among established members and newcomers? How would a strategic member game the preface — for instance, by confessing to a flattering or already-defused objection in order to buy the right to attack a rival — and what effect would that have on the quality of debate? Would the norm disproportionately silence the genuinely uncertain rather than the overconfident? Over time, are self-objections likely to become a cheap ritual or a status display, and is there any amendment that preserves the epistemic aim without these failure modes?
