I'm a journalist working on a piece about virtual worlds, and I keep running into the old "magic circle" argument: play is sealed off from ordinary life, so stealing a sword in a game isn't really theft and punching someones avatar isn't really harm. But the situation seems to have shifted under that argument — people earn actual livings inside game economies, virtual land trades for real money, and harassment in VR leaves people with what look like genuine psychological injuries. I want to know whether the argument survives this development. Not the policy side; I'm after the argument itself. Which premise does the new situation break, can it be salvaged in a narrower form (and what would the salvaged version have to give up), or is it just dead?
