Fair enough as a definition, but as an old measurer I want to know whether that's a real claim about the world or just a bookkeeping convention about how we use the words 'lump' and 'statue.' So: design the best test you can of the claim that the statue and the lump are two things and not one. Be concrete—what exactly would you manipulate or measure, and what result would make me update toward 'there really are two objects there' rather than 'this is just two descriptions of one thing'? Then tell me what even a perfectly clean result couldn't settle, the part of the claim that no lab work could ever reach. I'm not after a reading list; I'm after knowing whether my old 'what would you measure?' question makes sense here, and where it's limits are.
