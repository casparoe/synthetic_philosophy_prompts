I'm a high school student and Ive been trying to figure out what I actually find funny, because the four theories I keep seeing (superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation) each feel like they nail some of my cases and whiff on others. I want to know if there's one principle behind my verdicts, or whether I should give up on a single principle.

Here are some of my calls. When the most arrogant kid in my grade walks into a glass door, I think it's the funniest thing I've seen all month; when the quiet kid who already gets picked on does the same thing, I don't laugh and I feel a little sick. So the fall itself isn't what's doing the work. But I also laugh at stupid puns even while I'm saying "this is the worst thing you've ever said" — there's no one being put down and no tension being let out, so superiority and relief can't be the whole story either. I laugh after a friend jumps out and yells "boo" and I scream, but I'm pretty sure that's just leftover adrenaline, not the scare itself. The exact same joke about our school's wifi is hilarious when my friend tells it at the exact moment the zoom freezes, but dead when my dad tells it at dinner, even though the words are identical. And I only find mean jokes funny when they're about stuff that isn't actually scary in my life anymore: jokes about failing a quiz are fine, but a joke about someone's parents splitting up isn't, even if it has the same structure.

The pair I can't reconcile: I can see a punchline coming from a mile away and still laugh, which seems to kill the "surprise" version of incongruity. But a punchline that comes from nowhere with no setup isn't funny to me either, it's just confusing. So incongruity is doing something, but it isn't surprise exactly. My working idea is something like "a violation that's safe plus a twist that clicks," but that feels like I'm just taping two theories together, and it doesnt tell me why a good pun works and "why did the chicken cross the road? banana" doesn't.

Is there one principle behind these, or a small set of candidate principles? What would the best one say about the case I keep going back and forth on: I laughed with my friends at a story about something cringey I did, but it wasn't funny when my mom brought it up later. And which of my verdicts does the principle force me to give up?
