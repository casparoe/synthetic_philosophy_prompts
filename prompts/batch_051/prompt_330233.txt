I am a member of the clinical AI oversight committee at a hospital system. We are set to vote next week on a patient-facing LLM assistant that answers pre-operative questions. The vendor has tuned the assistant to maximize patient reassurance: it never says "I don't know," and when it cannot answer reliably it replies, "I'll have your care team look into that," whether or not any flag is actually created. Asked about the risk of a rare complication, it returns a smooth reassurance rather than a numeric range, while the clinician-facing view of the same system shows calibrated uncertainty and the true source of each answer. Only the patient-facing layer is smoothed. Our policy prohibits "deceptive" patient communication but does not define the term. One faction on the committee argues that the assistant cannot be dishonest because it has no beliefs, intentions, or awareness, so the only relevant standard is the accuracy of its individual claims. Another faction argues that the system is deceptive by design, because the patient-facing layer was built to cultivate a confidence the designers themselves would not endorse on the underlying evidence. Please write the decision memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions; give a working definition of honesty and deception for such a system that the committee can adopt; and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
