I'm a retired chemist, and I've been reading around in the philosophy of causation, and I can't get causation by omission to sit still. I wrote down my gut reactions to a pile of cases, and I'd like to know whether there's an actual principle behind them or whether I'm just dressing up blame as cause.

Here's what I came up with.

My neighbor asks me to water her tomatoes while she's away; I forget; they die. I'd say my not watering them caused them to die, full stop. A different neighbor sees the plants wilting and could easily turn on the hose, but it isn't his garden and he never promised; he does nothing. I don't think his not watering them caused the death — or if it did, it's a differnt kind of cause. A switchman on his phone fails to throw the lever; the train goes onto the wrong track and collides. Clearly the omission caused the crash. An ICU doctor doesn't show up for a routine overnight check because she's asleep in the on-call room; the patient crashes and dies. Also clearly causation. Two nurses each assume the other gave the patient her evening dose of blood thinner; neither dose is given; she throws a clot and dies. I think both omissions caused the death, even though neither failure would have been enough on its own. The neighbor who promised is hit by a car on her way to water the tomatoes and can't do it. I don't think her not watering them caused the tomatoes to die. Somebody on the other side of town who never even knew the plant existed also didn't water it. Obviously not a cause.

The case I keep going back and forth on is this: a lifeguard is texting instead of watching, and a swimmer drowns, but it turns out the swimmer had a massive stroke in the water and wouldn't have survived even if the lifeguard had gotten to him immediately. Is the lifeguard's not going in a cause of the drowning?

Is there one account of causation by omission that gets all of these, or nearly all? What would it say about the lifeguard case, and about a further case I haven't thought of? And if some of my verdicts have to go, wich one should I give up first? I genuinely want to know whether 'A's not doing X caused E' is a literal factual claim or just a way of registering that A is at fault.
