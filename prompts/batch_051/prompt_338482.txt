I'm putting together a short statement of my views in the philosophy of mind for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. I currently hold all of the following:

1. To be in pain is to be in a state that occupies a certain causal role: it is typically caused by tissue damage or similar bodily disturbance, and it typically causes withdrawal or avoidance, signs of distress, and changes in subsequent behavior.
2. Mental states are multiply realizable. Any physical substrate that implements the pain role is in pain; the substrate—carbon-based neurons, hydraulic tubes, silicon circuits—is irrelevant to whether the state is pain.
3. But I reject the overly liberal form of functionalism. A lookup table that stored the right input-output mapping, or a whole population wired together to mimic the role, would not literally be in pain. To count as a realizer of the role, the implementation must be integrated in the right way, not merely produce the right input-output profile.
4. Mental causation is real. When I withdraw from a hot stove, my pain—the mental state itself—does causal work; it is not epiphenomenal relative to the underlying neural activity.
5. Psychology is an autonomous special science. Psychological generalizations hold at the functional level, and a mature psychology should not be expected to reduce to, or be replaced by, neuroscience.
6. This carries over to other species: an octopus feels genuine pain, not merely acts as if it does, even though its nervous system is organized very differently from ours. If it implements the pain role, it feels pain.
7. I am neither an eliminativist nor an illusionist about phenomenal character. There is something it is like to be in pain, and any acceptable view must keep that.

Please go through the set and tell me which of these commitments are actually in tension with one another, which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance dissolves on inspection, and how severe each genuine tension is. For each genuine tension, say exactly what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Then say whether there is a single revision that would do the most cleanup, and what it would cost. I would rather have the tensions ranked by severity than a list that treats every pair as equally problematic.
