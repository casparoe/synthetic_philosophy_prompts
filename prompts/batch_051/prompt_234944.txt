Take Sen's liberal paradox, the classic one-copy version with Lewd and Prude deciding about a copy of Lady Chatterley's Lover. Lewd's ranking is: Prude reads > Lewd reads > nobody reads. Prude's ranking is: nobody reads > Prude reads > Lewd reads. Each person controls only their own reading: Lewd can decide between Lewd-reads and nobody-reads, Prude can decide between Prude-reads and nobody-reads, and together with Pareto (if both prefer one alternative to another, the social ranking does too) you get the usual cycle.

Now suppose there are enough copies that both can read, so the outcomes are N (nobody reads), L (only Lewd reads), P (only Prude reads), and B (both read). Extend the preferences in the natural way: Lewd ranks B > P > L > N, and Prude ranks N > P > L > B. Keep minimal liberalism but apply it to each person's own reading while holding the other person's reading fixed: Lewd dictates L vs N and B vs P, Prude dictates P vs N and B vs L.

Does the cycle still arise? If it survives, does that show the original paradox was never really about there being only one copy?
