A piece I'm working on keeps circling around the Zhuangzi passage in "The Full Understanding of Life" (Legge's translation, chapter 19) where the archer's skill collapses when the stakes go from practice trinkets to gold. Please argue the position that this is a real paradox and not just a fuzzy self-help cliché: that trying to implement wu-wei in high-stakes settings is self-defeating because the higher-order attempt to control spontaneity inherits the same concern with outcomes it is supposed to eliminate. Also give me your best counterargument, and tell me if you think the passage actually supports the paradox or is better read as warning against greed rather than caring. Keep it under 350 words.
