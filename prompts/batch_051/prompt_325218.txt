Ok here's a design brief for a Frege-puzzle case I want for a philosophy of language class. (Quick gloss: by "Millian" I mean the view that a name's only semantic contribution is its referent, and by "Fregean" I mean the view that names also contribute a sense, or mode of presentation.) I need an identity sentence 'Alpha is Beta' that meets all of these at once: (i) an object is officially given both names at the very same dubbing ceremony, (ii) the two names travel to a competent speaker by two seperate chains of use, so the speaker has both names in their vocabulary without yet knowing they co-refer, and (iii) the speaker would report learning something when told 'Alpha is Beta'. The names can't be a disguise, a stage name, or an alias, and no Hesperus/Phosphorus, Twain/Clemens, or Clark Kent/Superman. Take the names to be ordinary rigid proper names with no descriptive meaning. Then do the check: say what the referent is, what proposition a Millian says the sentence expresses and why that seems to miss the speaker's epistemic gain, and what distinct senses a Fregean would assign the two names and how their senses can differ when both were fixed by the same ceremony. Finish by saying (a) which feature of the case does the actual work, (b) what breaks if you fuse the two chains of transmission into one, and (c) the strongest Millian reply that accounts for the speaker's feeling of discovery without positing senses, and whether you find it convincing. Keep the scenario realistic and under a paragraph.
