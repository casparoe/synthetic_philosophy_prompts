ok so here's roughly where i've landed on moral dilemmas and i want to know if it all hangs together. (1) i think there are genuine dilemmas: sometimes you face two obligations that conflict, neither overrides the other, and whatever you choose you've done something wrong — not just "this feels bad," but you've actually violated a real obligation. (2) i accept ought-implies-can: you can't be obligated to do the impossible. (3) i'm a pluralist about where these obligations come from — fidelity, beneficence, honesty, whatever — they're irreducibly different and can't always be ranked or traded off against each other, and sometimes nothing determinately outweighs anything else. (4) i think the obligation you don't fullfil leaves a moral remainder: agent-regret, or a duty to apologize and make amends, remains fitting, and it would be a mistake to treat the unchosen obligation as simply cancelled. (5) i also think someone who faces a genuine dilemma, deliberates honestly, and picks one horn hasn't shown a character flaw and isn't blameworthy. (6) and i'd have said that if you ought to do A and you ought to do B, then it follows that you ought to do A and B.

Is this a consistent package? I want a committed audit, not a menu: lead with your bottom line, then reason it through, without preamble or restating the question. Tell me which of these genuinely pull against each other, order the tensions by severity, flag any that are only apparent, and say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs — which commitment i should give up or revise and what i lose by doing it.
