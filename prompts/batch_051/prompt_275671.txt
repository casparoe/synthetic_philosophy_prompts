I've been going back and forth with my hiking-group chat about a logistics problem that's turned into a weird self-trust problem, and I'd like to know if there's a principled way out. Next month I'm doing a solo traverse of a long ridgeline in a fairly remote range. The route starts and ends at different trailheads about two hours apart, there's no shuttle, and the one thing everyone on the local forums agrees on is that drivers no longer stop for hikers who need a ride back to their car. Too many people promise to venmo them when they get home, and then never do, because by then they're showered, fed, and the driver is basically untraceable. I get it. If I imagine the version of me who is already home, I think he wont pay either: the money's gone, the need is over, and the driver is a stranger he'll never see again. The whole ride will have been a pure windfall, and it'll be really tempting to just... not.

The thing I can't shake is that the promise I'd make at the trailhead would be sincere. Cold and tired and wanting a ride, I genuinely mean it when I say "I'll venmo you when I get home." But I can also see that its sincerity counts for almost nothing, because the actual decision to pay gets made by a different version of me in a different situation. My buddy keeps saying the answer is just to be the kind of person who pays, as if I can settle that now and have it stick on its own. But that feels like the exact move I can't make by an act of will, since the person deciding later won't be the one who made the decision now. I keep trying to model it as a bargaining game between trailhead-me and home-me, except home-me holds all the cards and no longer cares about the deal.

So I have two questions, maybe three. Is there anything I can do before the trip, or in how the promise is set up, that would make the promise credible to a skeptical driver and actually get home-me to pay? If such a thing exists, is it rational to take it, given that it'll force me to act against my own interest at the moment of paying? And the small one: if I can already predict that I won't pay when I get home, am I lying when I promise anyway, or can a promise be sincere even when the person making it knows the later self will break it? The obvious patch is to carry cash and hand it over up front, but that somehow doesn't get at the part that's actually bothering me: whether I can rationally make myself less rational later, and whether a sincere promise by one "version" of me binds the later one in any way that matters.
