I keep meeting the same four families of theory about humour — superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation — and each one is built around one vivid kind of case and then stretched to cover the rest. Before I read any further, I want the specification of what an adequate theory of humour must actually deliver. Write me the desiderata.

In particular, I want:

(1) The phenomena and cases an adequate theory has to get right, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Go beyond the stock examples. Include at least these hard cases: a joke explained is no longer funny, even though the incongruity in it is now better understood; the same pratfall reads as comedy when it happens to a pompous executive and as cruelty when it happens to a frail, harmless person; a scuffle that looked real turns to laughter when it is revealed as staged, though nothing physical changed; a punchline dies with repetition while an in-joke grows funnier with repetition among insiders; canned laughter makes a joke funnier for most viewers but kills it for some; and tickling produces something very like mirth with no content to get at all.

(2) The distinctions a theory must respect and not collapse — for example, between the laughter of tickling and the amusement of a joke, between something being funny and something being morally acceptable, and whatever distinctions you think are real among wit, absurdity, the comic, and irony. Don't take my list as authoritative; tell me which of these are real distinctions a theory must draw and which are not.

(3) For each desideratum, a verdict on whether it is non-negotiable or is a local demand of one family of theory (such as incongruity theories insisting on a cognitive mismatch, or relief theories insisting on pent-up arousal), with your reasoning.

(4) Whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether a theory can be content-neutral enough to explain wordplay and social enough to explain why mockery tracks hierarchy, or whether the demand for a single mechanism conflicts with the demand to cover both surprise-dependent and familiarity-dependent funniness. If some subset forces a choice, say so and say which demand should give way.

(5) Finally, a brief scorecard for the four families of theory against your list — brief because the criteria themselves are the deliverable.

Treat the target as explanatory adequacy for the psychology of humour, not a theory of the comic as a high art form alone and not a moral theory of when laughter is appropriate; if a proposed desideratum turns out to belong to ethics or aesthetics rather than to humour, say so and demote it.
