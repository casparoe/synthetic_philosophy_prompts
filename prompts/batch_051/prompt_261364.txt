I recently read Galen Strawson's basic argument and its been eating at me, because I sit on my county's drug court team and our policies suddenly look incoherent. The argument, as I understand it, goes like this: you can't be ultimately morally responsible for what you do unless you're responsible for the kind of person you are — your temperament, your self-control, your values — and you can't be responsible for that unless you chose it, but you could only choose to be that way if you already were the sort of person who would choose it, and so on back forever.

The practice that's bugging me is this. Our court treats an actual relapse as a symptom of addiction: the person gets sent back to treatment, no jail time. But it treats 'failure to engage in treatment' — skipping groups, being combative, not showing what the judge calls genuine commitment to recovery — as a willful violation, and people catch real jail time for it. On Strawson's argument I can't see why the second is any more up to the person then the first; being the kind of person who can will themselves into engaging with treatment is itself a trait they didn't choose.

What view of responsibility does this distinction presuppose? Does it presuppose that people can be responsible for their attitudes or character, not just their actions, and is that coherent given the argument? I'd also like you to separate what the court strictly has to assume from what it merely happens to fit well with. If I take the argument seriously, what would the court have to change — could it still sanction people at all, and on what basis?
