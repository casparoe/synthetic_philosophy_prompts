I am a novelist, and while researching a character who works in the philosophy of biology, I came across a distinction I had previously only met, in different clothing, in arguments about how to read fiction. I want to know whether the resemblance is real or merely verbal.

In the philosophy of biology, two broad accounts of what it is for a trait to have a function stand opposed. The etiological (or selected-effects) account locates function in history: roughly, the function of the heart is to pump blood because pumping blood is the effect for which hearts were favored by natural selection in the past. The causal-role account locates function in present organization: the heart's function is to pump blood because pumping blood is the contribution the heart makes to the circulatory capacities of the organism in which it now sits, whatever its selection history.

I am familiar with a structurally similar divide in thinking about fiction. One school treats the function of a device, a scene, or a recurring image as a matter of what the author put it there to do—its origin in authorial intention. Another school treats its function as a matter of what it actually does within the finished work—the role it plays in the structure as it stands, apart from authorial history or the reader's biography. One side looks backward to genesis to settle function; the other looks to current systemic contribution.

My question is whether this is a deep structural parallel or only a loose analogy. Does a commitment in one domain carry over to the corresponding position in the other, or do features specific to biology and features specific to literature break the alignment? If the parallel were deep, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer from the biological debate to the literary one? And what is something the parallel predicts that turns out false?

Please take the role of a professional philosopher with expertise in both philosophy of biology and aesthetics. Start by brainstorming the points of similarity and difference before committing to a verdict. Then give a structured assessment of whether the parallel is deep, superficial, or a mixture, stating precisely where the mapping holds and where it comes apart. Write your answer in American English.
