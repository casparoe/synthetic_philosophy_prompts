I'm a novelist and I keep turning over what it means for a language, an art form, or a tradition to be lost—whether the loss is only to the people who held it, or something more.

My gut calls, for what they're worth. (1) The dialect of the valley I grew up in is down to a few hundred mostly elderly speakers. I think it would be a real loss if it disappeared, real enough that public money should go to recording it properly and running evening classes even if the next generation is mostly indifferent. (2) A neighbouring valley had a masked midwinter play that mostly served to humiliate one family as the "scapegoat." The younger people quietly let it lapse; the older ones mourn it. I think the younger people did right, and a heritage grant shouldn't revive it. (3) A friend translates poems from a language with about fifty speakers left. There's now a small audience for them in translation, far from the community, and the community itself is lukewarm. She says the poems have value for people who will never live the language, and I mostly agree. (4) I once saw a court dance reconstructed from notation two centuries after anyone last performed it. It was scrupulous and a little dead. I'm glad the notation was preserved, but I'd rather the money had gone to a living, scrappy dance company. (5) Here's the one that bothers me. The last man who can build a certain kind of wooden boat says he won't teach anyone; the boats were for a way of life that's finished, and he wants the knowledge to end with him. Part of me says that's his right, that the knowledge is his life's meaning. But I also think I'd back someone getting him to talk through the technique into a camera even if he didnt really want to. And I don't see how that squares with what I said about the masked play.

Is there one principle behind all these, or close to one? If a simple principle fits, tell me which of my calls has to go; if the only principle that covers them is complicated, say that too. And I can never settle the case of a community that stages a rather fake "heritage" version of a ritual for tourists because the income lets them stay on their land. What does your principle say there? Keep it under 700 words.
