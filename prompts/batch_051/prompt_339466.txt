I'm on a working group at my company that has to recommend how to distribute a one-time $300k remote-work setup budget to roughly 600 employees. Last year we gave everyone a flat $500, which was simple, but it caused grumbling: our one blind engineer ended up paying about $1,400 out of pocket on top of the grant for a screen reader setup, while plenty of people used the money to upgrade a monitor they didn't strictly need. So this year we have to hand HR an actual rule.

Inside the group there are three camps. One says give a flat amount again: equal money for everyone is the only defensible kind of fairness, and any other policy is just us picking winners. Another says spend each dollar where it buys the largest reported gain in wellbeing, which means some people get a lot and some get nothing, because $500 is worth a lot more to someone who is struggling than to someone who is comfortable. The third says fund people up to a baseline of being able to do their jobs from home competently; some people will need six grand and some need zero, and the thing that matters is what people can actually do, not the money itself or how happy it makes them.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursdays meeting. It should recommend one of those three rules, or a well-defined hybrid, with a bottom line specific enough that HR could implement it without another meeting to argue about first principles. It should also handle the strongest objection to whichever rule I'm recommending honestly, not as a strawman. I'm a staff engineer and this has gotten more philosophical than I expected, so keep it in plain language.
