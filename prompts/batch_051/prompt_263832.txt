I spent thirty-odd years as a physical chemist running Monte Carlo simulations, and now that I'm retired I've drifted into philosophy of probability. A puzzle keeps stubbing my toe, and I suscpect it has a recognized name that I just haven't stumbled on.

Take any finite string of 0s and 1s, no matter how disorderly it looks. I can always write a small deterministic program that prints exactly that string and stops—at worst the program simply contains the string in quotes as data. If I'm also allowed to choose my notion of "short" after seeing the string, I can make the thing come out looking either trivially simple or hopelessly complex. So there seems to be no fact of the matter about whether a particular finite string is random: any finite string could have been produced on purpose by some rigged source, and none of them wear their chance history on their face. For an infinite sequence, by contrast, there does seem to be a stable question: does the sequence escape every mechanical test you can actually run that tries to pick out a bias or pattern in it? A genuinely fair coin looks like it escapes; 01010101... obviously doesn't; and the digits of a computable number don't either, even though they look wild to the eye. So randomness appears to be a property of sources and infinite sequences, never of a finite string—and yet scientists talk constantly about "a random data set."

Someone will object that 010101... repeated fifty times is clearly not random while a messy-looking string at least could be, so finite strings can't all be on a par; there are better and worse candidates. I agree, but then I want to know what "better candidate" amounts to, given that no finite string is excluded from having been produced deterministically. I have a vague sense I'm near algorithmic randomness, but the things I've read jump straight to measuring how compressible a string is, and that feels like a different question. Does the asymmetry I'm describing—randomness as a property of the source or the infinite sequence, never the finite string—have an established name, and how do the formal notions around it carve the concept? What I'd really like is a taxonomy: what exactly is the claim, what is it not, where are its edges, and whether "no finite sequence is random in itself" is a settled slogan or a live dispute.
