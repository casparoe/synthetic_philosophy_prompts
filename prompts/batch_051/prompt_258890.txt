I keep noticing a structural similarity between the Humean best-system account of laws of nature and the way statisticians select models by trading off fit against complexity. In both cases one begins with a body of data — the mosaic of particular facts for the Humean, a sample for the statistician — and instead of reading the pattern directly off the data, one chooses the simplest hypothesis or model that still captures the data well, with an apparently similar judgment call about how much simplicity is worth relative to fit, as in AIC, BIC, or regularization. Is this more than a loose analogy? In particular, could the selection of the best system be understood as a model-selection problem, and which disanalogies — for instance, that laws are meant to be strictly true universal generalizations while statistical models may be merely approximate or probabilistic — would prevent results from transferring between the two settings?
