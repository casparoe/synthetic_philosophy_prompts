Some background on me: I have spent years reading rationalist blogs and forums, and the vocabulary I actually think in is signaling, status, costly punishment, and game-theoretic accounts of fairness and cooperation; I have no formal training in political philosophy. I want to know whether a philosophical position I keep running into survives translation into that vocabulary: the position that envy is not simply a vice, that it can be a signal of injustice, and that at least some envy is excusable.

The position, as I understand it from Rawls, runs roughly as follows. Envy proper is pain at another's advantage that would persist even if their advantage took nothing away from you; it includes a willingness to lower your own position in order to lower theirs. Resentment is different: it is a moral reaction to an actual wrong. On the Rawlsian picture I have in mind, envy proper is a vice because it is negative-sum and would make everyone worse off, but it can nevertheless be excusable rather than blameworthy when a society's inequalities become so deep that they undermine people's self-respect.

Please restate this position as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Translate envy proper into something like a spite or inequity-aversion term in a utility function, translate resentment into something like third-party punishment or norm enforcement, and translate excusable envy into a claim about the incentives or signals that arise when a cooperative scheme comes to be seen as rigged. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the Rawlsian picture translate cleanly, and which parts did you have to fudge or force? Are there parts that simply have no counterpart in a signaling-and-status framework — in particular, the idea that self-respect matters as something more than a strategic asset? For each piece of residue, tell me whether it exposes a genuine blind spot in my home vocabulary or whether it shows that the philosophical position was looser than its defenders admit. My prior is that the distinction can be captured almost entirely by payoff functions plus signaling, and that self-respect is where it will strain; test that prior rather than flattering it. Keep the translation honest: do not quietly assume that normativity reduces to games and then treat the translation as complete.
