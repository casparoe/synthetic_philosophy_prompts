Here's an exercise I want you to do. Take two camps that read the feeling of existential emptiness differently. Camp A holds that the felt void is basically veridical: the self really is empty in roughly the Buddhist sense, and the suffering comes from clinging to a substantial 'me' rather than from the void itself. Camp B holds that the same feeling discloses the absence of any pregiven essence or external meaning—a condition of radical freedom you can't fill and shouldn't try to fill. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration these two camps would produce if they were forced to stop talking past each other. Give me (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign that aren't vacuous, (2) a disagreements table where each row states the residual difference in terms each side would accept for its own view, and (3) a short verdict on wheter any of this is just a fight over the words 'void' and 'empty,' and which part isn't. Also make each side specify what evidence, case, or argument would actually move them.
