I am a novelist rather than a trained philosopher, and I am drafting a near-future novel in which the economy has shifted largely to informational goods: designs, software, datasets, texts, engineered organisms, and similar things that can be copied at negligible cost and whose use by one person does not diminish what remains for others. I want to get the philosophical bones of the book right, which means working through a question about Locke's account of property.

The argument I want stress-tested is the Lockean labour theory of acquisition: roughly, a person makes an unowned thing her own by mixing her labour with it, provided she leaves others no worse off, under the proviso that enough and as good is left in common for others. That argument was built for a world of scarce, rivalrous, perishable resources such as land, acorns, or fish. My novel's world is one in which the most valuable things people produce are non-rival and non-perishable. Copying a design takes nothing from the person who already has it.

My question is whether Locke's labour argument survives this development. When the thing appropriated is non-rival, the proviso seems to be satisfied almost automatically, because one person's taking leaves literally as much and as good for everyone else. Yet Locke also tied property to avoiding waste and spoilage. Does the argument still ground exclusive private ownership of informational goods, or does it now ground only weaker, non-exclusive claims such as attribution, compensation for the labour, or open access? Or does it simply stop applying, leaving my world's property relations without a Lockean foundation?

Please handle this as a professional philosopher would. Begin by brainstorming the different ways the Lockean argument could be reconstructed, then state the version whose premises you will test. Identify precisely which premises of the argument depend on rivalry, scarcity, or perishability, and say what happens to each when those assumptions are removed. Then consider what the strongest Lockean position in such a world would say, and what the original theory would have to give up to reach it. I care less about which conclusion you reach than about seeing which premise actually does the work and whether that premise can be repaired in a weaker but still meaningful form.
