I'm compiling a list of examples of Stigler's law (the pattern where a scientific result or object gets named for someone other than its original discoverer). So far I've got: Stigler's law itself, which Stigler credited to Merton; Halley's comet; the Gaussian distribution; L'Hôpital's rule; Gresham's law; and Snell's law. They all seem to me to share one underlying structure: an earlier person found it quietly, and a later person got the name attached by publishing or promoting it more effectively. I want one more that is not a variant of that structure. Give me a case where the eponym came about through a genuinely different mechanism, and then say honestly whether your example is still an instance of Stigler's law or a different kind of naming failure, and what it tells us about how eponyms actually get assigned.
