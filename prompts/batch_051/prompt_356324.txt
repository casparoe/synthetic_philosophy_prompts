Here's a question I've never been able to settle about Schrödinger. Suppose he could be brought back this afternoon and made to read the current literature on integrated information theory—the view that consciousness is identical to integrated information, measured by Φ, so any physical system with the right causal structure (including certain non-biological systems) has at least a little bit of consciousness. In his own writings he kept insisting, on the strength of Schopenhauer and the Upanishads, that the apparent manyness of minds is an illusion: consciousness is one, and the individual self is just a limited perspective on that one thing. Now, IIT is usually pitched as a scientific theory that wants to measure consciousness rather than settle whether it is ultimately one or many, but in practice it says there are many subjects, one for every system that locally maximizes integrated information. Would Schrödinger have seen IIT as friendly to his picture (because it dissolves the separate Cartesian self into causal architecture), hostile to it (becuase it multiplies little centers of consciousness exactly where he wanted unity), or would he have said the whole Φ business was asking the wrong kind of question altogether? Reconstruct his likely take, and tell me which parts of your answer follow fairly directly from the view I described and which parts are guesswork. Hard limit: 250 words, no citations.
