Write a satirical onboarding letter, in the deadpan, oily voice of a government "Office of Democratic Renewal," informing a randomly selected citizen that the national lottery has chosen them to serve a single, non-renewable two-year term in the legislature under a new full sortition system. All career politicians have been "released from service," and the letter should make clear this is for everyone's own good: a second term, or any relevant experience, would only corrupt them. Include a short FAQ box (What if I don't know the issues? Who writes the actual bills? Can I decline?). The letter should quietly reveale that the real drafting is handled by a Continuity Council of former politicians and permanent staff, who are definitely not just the old class with worse PR. The satire should land the standard objections to lottocracy without ever stating them: the competence objection, the accountability objection, the capture objection, the way term limits just hand power to unelected staff, and the gap between descriptive representation and substantive representation. It should reward someone who knows the sortition literature and still be readable to someone who doesnt. Around 700 words.
