I'm a junior person on a grant panel that ranks proposals one by one, going around the table in a fixed order, and I speak near the end. I've drifted into a policy: when the first few people all recommend funding a proposal and my own read of the file left me uneasy, I recommend funding anyway. I tell myself a handful of independent readers probably knows more than my one muddled impression, and I'd rather not drag the meeting out over a half-formed worry. Lately I've started to suspect the people ahead of me might be deferring to each other just like I do, so the independant-readers premise isn't guaranteed; and when I bury my worry, the people after me never get to use it. My worry's also the kind of thing I can't always put a page number to — it's more a sense I get from reading a lot of these proposals. Is this policy a defensible way to pool private information, or am I just free-riding on the group and dressing up herd behaviour as judgement? When would a rule like this be reliable, and when would it fail? Is there a version of it that keeps the good parts while fixing the buried-information problem — and how would I tell the difference, from the inside, between responsibly deferring and lazily going with the flow?
