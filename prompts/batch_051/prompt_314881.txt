I cover health and science for a national publication, and I am trying to make sense of a dispute between two urgent-care chains whose results reverse depending on how they are divided. In the most recent reporting year, the state health department's dashboard shows Chain Alpha with an 82 percent thirty-day full recovery rate for the condition in question, versus 63 percent for Chain Beta. But when a university researcher reanalyzed the same records after dividing patients into mild and severe cases, Beta did better within each category: among mild cases, Beta recovered 95 percent of patients to Alpha's 90 percent; among severe cases, Beta recovered 55 percent to Alpha's 50 percent. The aggregate favors Alpha only because roughly four-fifths of Alpha's patients were classified as mild, while roughly four-fifths of Beta's patients were classified as severe.

Alpha's medical director contests the stratified comparison. The two chains, he says, do not assign severity the same way. Alpha records severity on arrival using a short screening form, while Beta labels the case only after the first day of treatment, after which some patients have already improved and may be reclassified; Beta's physicians, who see a sicker referral base, may also set the threshold for calling a case severe in a different place. On this account, the subgroup comparison is an artifact of inconsistent measurement and timing, and the aggregate dashboard is the only comparable summary. Beta's director counters that the aggregate ignores obvious differences in case mix and penalizes the chain that takes on the harder patients.

I need to write a story about which chain should be preferred, or at least about what these numbers do and do not support. Is the aggregate comparison the safer guide in this situation, is the stratified comparison the right one, or do neither comparisons yet warrant a conclusion? What would have to be true about severity measurement, timing, referral patterns, and case mix for each interpretation to be correct? What additional information or analysis would most cleanly separate the competing explanations for the reversal, and what would count against each interpretation if found? I want the final piece to avoid both false precision and false equivalence.
