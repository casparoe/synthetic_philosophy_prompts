I'm a PhD student in legal philosophy, and I've somehow ended up running the two-hour interactive slot in our Law and Philosophy MA seminar. There are about twelve students, and they are a good group but they've started to glaze over whenever I bring up abstract rule-systems. I want to get them to feel the wierdness of self-amending rules before I put a name to it. The thing I have in mind is this: a rule that sets out how the other rules may be changed is itself one of the rules, so do its procedures apply to changing it? If yes, the amendment clause can be used to amend the amendment clause, and then the whole system can be made trivial; if no, then some rule stands outside the game of rule-changing, and I dont see where its authority comes from. I've read a little about Nomic, but I don't want to just hand them the game; I want them to blunder into the problem themselves and leave the room seeing why an amendment clause is not just one more rule.

The students are playful and argumentative, fine with small stakes and a bit of procedure, but they get bored by long set-ups. I'll have a whiteboard, index cards, and about ninety minutes before we debrief. Design a runnable activity — rules, roles, a starting state, a way of scoring or winning so that changing the rules actually matters — that reliably produces the moment where someone proposes to amend the amendment rule itself, and maybe a follow-up where they notice that the same problem shows up in constitutions, club by-laws, and parliamentary standing orders.
