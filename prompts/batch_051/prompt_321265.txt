In several European countries you can be paid decent money to donate eggs—clinics advertise compensation for your time and discomfort—but in several of those same countries, paying a woman to carry a pregnancy is a crime and any surrogacy contract is simply void. In the US, gestational surrogacy is a more or less normal market: agencies, advertised base compensation, the works. I keep trying to figure out what picture of bodies, labor, and parenthood actually explains that split. Write me an account of the philosophical commitments each regime presupposes. In particular: can the European position be made internally coherent, or does it have to claim something specific about gestation—that it isn't work, that it is a kind of work that has to stay outside the market, or that carrying a child creates a relationship that selling eggs does not—to justify treating the two cases so differently? Reconstruct the implicit theory you think is doing the actual work, be clear about which parts the policy strictly presupposes versus which parts it merely fits well with, and then say what would have to change in the European rules if the underlying view were replaced by a straight labor-market view of pregnancy.
