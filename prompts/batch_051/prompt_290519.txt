I got into an argument with a friend about whether giving AI systems legal personhood is obviously insane or obviously inevitible, and I realized we were both just gesturing at the idea rather than analysing anything specific. So I want to stress-test a concrete scheme instead. Here it is: any AI system that can enter contracts through an API without a human approving each transaction may be registered as a "statutory person." Unlike a corporation, its personality is graded. It can hold property only in a segregated fund that its operator must seed and keep above a stated minimum; it can be sued, but only against that fund; and the operator's liability for what the system does is capped at three times the fund. Registration is optional, but the kicker is that if you run an unregistered system and it incurs a liability, the cap vanishes and the operator is personally (or corporately) on the hook without limit. The stated goal is to give counterparties something like a someone to sue while keeping operators from hiding behind the machine.
