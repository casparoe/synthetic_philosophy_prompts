I'm a philosophy professor, but I've been roped into chairing our university's science-communication review group for a year. Every few weeks a team proposes that we stop using some term and swap in another on the grounds that the old one makes the public think the wrong thing: 'social distancing' should become 'physical distancing', 'obese patient' should become 'person with obesity', 'climate change' should become 'climate crisis', and so on. Some of these seem right to me, some feel like word magic, and I'd like something better then my gut.

Please write me a reusable decision procedure, grounded in whatever the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis / linguistic relativity actually supports (not the strong version that every undergraduate refutes, but not the smug line that only syntax matters either), for deciding when a proposed term change is likely to shift thought or behaviour and when it's only symbolism. Take seriously the difference between what a language obligatorily makes speakers encode and what a word merely nudges them toward. It should be something my panel of non-philosophers and a few scientists can run in twenty minutes per proposal. Give me the questions in order, what counts as a passable answer to each, where the procedure stops with a 'no', and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than evidence. Be explicit about which distinctions from the actual linguistic-relativity literature survive being turned into a checklist and which had to be dropped.

Then run it on three proposals: (1) changing 'social distancing' to 'physical distancing' in pandemic guidance; (2) changing 'climate change' to 'climate crisis' in the university's outward-facing sustainability reports; and (3) changing 'obese patient' to 'person with obesity' in a health faculty style guide. I'm personally attached to number 2, so if the procedure says no, don't soften it. Finish by saying what the procedure cannot do and which residual judgment calls cannot be eliminated. Answer in British English.
