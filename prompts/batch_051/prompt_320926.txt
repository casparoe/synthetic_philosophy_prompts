I am a novelist drafting a book in which two characters, without ever using philosophical terminology, embody a disagreement about whether the past is gone. I would like a thought experiment constructed to my specifications, which I will then adapt into a scene.

Construct a thought experiment in which presentism and eternalism about time give different accounts of what makes a single, ordinary past-tensed statement true, even though both theories agree that the statement is true.

Specifications:

- The scenario must be realistic and contemporary—one paragraph, set in an ordinary place such as an apartment, a shop, or a street—with no time travel, no speculative technology, and no supernatural elements.
- The target statement must be something an unpretentious character might actually say, and the scenario itself must not use logical notation or technical terms.
- The truth of the statement must be intuitively independent of any presently existing trace: if every physical record, object, and fallible memory connected with the event were destroyed after the scene, the statement would still have been true. Do not plant a photograph, diary, recording, or other surviving trace that carries the statement's truth; if the example depends on one, it fails.
- Both presentism and eternalism must classify the statement as true and must agree on all observable facts in the scenario. They must diverge only over the truthmaker—what, in reality, grounds or makes the statement true. The presentist is restricted to presently existing things or presently obtaining facts; the eternalist may also appeal to past objects, events, or times.

After the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification: state exactly what presentism says about the case and what eternalism says, and spell out why they diverge. Then identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and say what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed. Finally, explain how a defender of whichever theory the case leaves worse off would attack it—for example, by enriching the present with sparse metaphysical facts or by rejecting the demand for truthmakers—and assess whether that reply is ad hoc.

Please write the entire answer in American English.
