In my social epistemology and technology course, we have been discussing whether reputation systems such as Reddit karma or Stack Exchange reputation are anything more than popularity contests. I would like to think through one testable version of that question. Consider the claim that a user's karma score—built from other participants' upvotes and downvotes, usually before any independent expert assessment—tracks the epistemic quality of that user's contributions, where epistemic quality means something like accuracy, clarity, and responsiveness to reasons, rather than merely tracking popularity, wit, or agreement with the audience's prior beliefs. Please design the strongest concrete study you can to test this claim. Specify how you would operationalize epistemic quality independently of the karma system, what pattern of results would support the epistemic-quality reading, and what pattern would support the social-approval reading. Then explain what a successful test would and would not settle—including whether some part of the claim about epistemic quality inevitably resists operationalization.
