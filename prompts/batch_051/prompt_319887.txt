I've been trying to get clear on what I actually think about believing other people, because my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases won't line up under any single rule. When I look lost in an unfamiliar city and a random pedestrian points and says the station is two blocks over, I just go — no checking. Fine. A stranger at the airport tells me my gate has changed, and I walk to the other gate without asking the desk. Fine. My kid tells me he did all his homework at school and left it in his locker, and I don't believe him, but that's because he's lied about exactly this twice before. Then the other side: a stranger at a bus stop tells me the fare machine is broken and I can ride free, and I still try the machine rather than take his word. A used-car salesman tells me a car was never in an accident, and I don't accept it without a report even though I have no particular reason to think he's lying. A single anonymous Reddit comment says the city council just raised property taxes, and I won't repeat it until a second source backs it up, even though the commenter sounds like they know the issue cold.

The two verdicts that really bother me are these. My friend tells me she met Tom Hanks at the grocery store, and I believe her instantly. The same friend tells me she saw a glowing orb that spoke to her in the parking lot, and I immediately think she's unwell or dreaming, not that it happened. Same person, same general honesty, and I can't say what changed except that the second one is weirder. My working theory is something like: believe people by default unless I have a specific reason not to, except that when money, danger, or surprising content is involved I need evidence. But that's just a list of patches, not a principle.

Is there one principle that captures these verdicts, or close? If I have to pick between "we're entitled to believe others by default unless something defeats it" and "we always need positive reason to trust a speaker, like a track record or corroboration," which does my gut actually hold, and which verdicts would each option force me to abandon? And what would the principle you land on say about the case I keep going back and forth on: my first day in a new country, before I've learned anything about how truthful the locals are, a person on the street tells me the tap water is safe to drink — am I rational to just believe them? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
