At work, a discussion about whether a colleague's offhand comment should count as racist was ended by someone saying that when it comes to racism, "it's impact, not intent." I want to handle that move better next time it comes up. Take that sentence and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be used to make, running from the nearly trivial to the very strong. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude almost everyone should grant, a substantive and contestable thesis, or something implausible or overreaching, and note what would have to be true for it to hold. I especially want readings distinguished in the places where appeals to intent do real work: the difference between harm and blame, the question of what makes an act racist as opposed to merely ill-received, the role of the speaker's social position, the knowability of inner states, the function of plausible deniability in dog whistles, and the question of how a conversation should proceed once someone reports that a remark landed badly. Then say which reading or readings the person was actually relying on if they meant it to rule out "but he didn't mean it that way," and which readings would not support that ruling at all.
