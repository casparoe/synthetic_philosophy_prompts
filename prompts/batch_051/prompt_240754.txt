Here's the setup. There are two common ways labs supervise language models on tasks that require multi-step reasoning. Outcome supervision gives the model feedback only on the final answer: the code passes the tests, the number matches the key, the yes/no is right. Process supervision instead scores each intermediate step as it goes, so the model gets a signal about whether the chain itself is good, not just whether the last line is right.

People usually discuss this as an engineering tradeoff: process supervision costs more to annotate, but it tends to improve sample efficiency and makes it harder for the model to game the reward by producing the right answer through garbage reasoning. But I suspect there's a deeper issue, because the two methods quietly seperate notions we normally run together — reasoning well and getting the answer right. I want to know what each side is actually committing to.

So my question is: what theory of the relationship between reasoning well and getting the right answer does each scheme presuppose? In particular, does outcome supervision presuppose that reasoning is only valuable as a cause of correct outputs — a purely instrumental view — or is it compatible with the thought that the path itself has epistemic value that just isn't being measured? And does process supervision really presuppose that each step has a truth value, or could an engineer pick it for purely practical reasons while still believing that only the final answer matters? I'd also like to know where the choice is philosophically underdetermined — the places where a lab's pick doesn't actually commit them to an epistemology at all. Finally, imagine a lab that really adopted the strict view that only the final output matters and reasoning is just whatever reliably gets you there. What would have to change in how they evaluate models, what they reward, and what they say about the reasoning the model shows users? And is the fact that labs still care about whether the displayed chain of thought is faithful, even when they only supervised the outcome, best read as an inconsistency with that strict view, or just a pragmatic safeguard?
