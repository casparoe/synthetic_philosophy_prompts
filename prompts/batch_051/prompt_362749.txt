Please give me your credence in the following claim: Fitch's knowability argument decisively refutes verificationist anti-realism about truth—the view that every truth is in principle knowable, but not every truth is actually known. Let the knowability principle (KP) be the formalization p → ◇Kp for unrestricted p, where K is factive knowledge and ◇ is the relevant kind of possibility. Fitch's derivation, using classical logic, minimal normal modal principles, the distribution of K over conjunction, and the instance p ∧ ¬Kp, appears to show that KP entails that every truth is actually known. Decompose your credence into at least the following terms: (1) the probability that unrestricted KP faithfully captures the verificationist's knowability claim, rather than a strawman; (2) the probability, conditional on (1), that the derivation is valid under the intended reading of ◇ and K; and (3) the probability, conditional on (1) and (2), that every available revision that blocks the derivation—such as an intuitionistic logic or a restriction on admissible p—either lacks independent motivation or abandons the core verificationist claim. Give a number and a one-line justification for each term, add any further terms you think are needed, then show how you combine them. Finally, identify the term you are least confident in and describe what evidence or argument would most shift your overall credence.
