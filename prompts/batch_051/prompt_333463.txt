I've been going back and forth on the Sleeping Beauty problem for ages and I'm starting to think the real issue isn't that people are bad at conditional probability. The set-up is the usual one: Beauty goes to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is tossed, and if it comes up heads she's woken once on Monday, while if it comes up tails she's woken Monday and Tuesday with her memory wiped between, and each time she's asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. Halfers say you stick at a half because the coin is fair and she knew all along she'd wake up at least once, so waking teaches her nothing new; thirders say a third because there are three possible awakening episodes and only one of them is a heads-awakening. Both sides have a neat argument that ends up making the other side look like it's saying something silly about bets or about what probability means.

I'm after a diagnosis rather than a survey. My working theory is that the two sides are answering different questions under the same word 'credence': one side is asking what Beauty should think about the coin as a physical chance, and the other is asking what she should assign to this awakening, here and now, being a heads-awakening. I've also heard its a verbal dispute about whether self-locating evidence is evidence at all. Which is it: a substantive disagreement with a right answer, a verbal one, or two different questions? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a sharper account of what credence is for, a variant case, a betting rule, or is the whole thing just ill-posed?
