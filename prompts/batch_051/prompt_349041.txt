My background is in software engineering rather than philosophy, so I am prepared to discover that I am only pattern-matching on the word "random." Here is the parallel. In distributed systems we deliberately introduce randomness as a design tool: jitter on timers, randomized backoff after contention, randomized election timeouts in consensus protocols to keep nodes from repeatedly tying for leadership, and load balancing in which you probe two randomly chosen queues and assign the incoming task to the shorter. In these cases randomness is not a concession to ignorance; it is a technique for breaking symmetry, avoiding correlated failures, and making the system hard for an adversary, or for unlucky timing, to steer into a pathological state. I recently read about lottocracy, the proposal that a legislature should be selected by lottery instead of by election. It looked to me like the same move in politics: replace a deterministic, optimizable selection process with random sampling from the population, on the theory that any organized search for the best representative can be captured, polarized, or gamed.

What I cannot decide is whether this similarity is deep or superficial. On the algorithmic side the payoff of randomization is often provable once you state assumptions: you can bound expected queue lengths, tail latency, or the probability of a long leader-election deadlock. Does lottocracy have anything analogous, or does the analogy break because machines in a cluster share an objective while voters and legislators have opposed interests, unequal competence, and careers that change them after selection? Please state the mapping as precisely as you can, say what would have to be true on both sides for any result to transfer, and name at least one prediction the analogy makes that you think is false in real politics. Please also tell me where your verdict is most fragile: which empirical finding about real legislatures, or which formal fact about randomized algorithms, would most likely change your mind. I would appreciate an answer in American English.
