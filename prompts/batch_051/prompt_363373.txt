Before I read another empirical study on whether religion makes people more moral, I want the specification the whole debate keeps skipping. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of religion's relationship to morality must satisfy. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. The list should at minimum handle: moral behavior versus moral judgment versus moral character; in-group versus out-group obligations; individual-level versus society-level effects, including why sustaining social trust is not automatically the same thing as making individuals better; and the fact that 'religion' covers traditions with sharply different moral contents, not one variable. Then address what 'more moral' could even mean when the standard of moral evaluation is itself contested — say whether the desiderata can settle that or must instead flag it as a limit on the whole empirical debate. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate the popular one-line positions (e.g., 'religion is a net force for good' or 'religion only produces in-group loyalty'), and briefly say how the main camps fare against the list. A table is fine for the requirements. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
