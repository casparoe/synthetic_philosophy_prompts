You are grading a short-answer question from an undergraduate course on judgment under uncertainty. The course has covered the inside-view/outside-view distinction, the planning fallacy, and epistemic modesty in forecasting.

Question: Define the inside view and the outside view in forecasting, explain how the outside view is meant to correct the planning fallacy, and say how the distinction bears on epistemic modesty. Then apply the distinction to the following case. A software firm estimates that a planned rewrite of its billing system will take six months, based on a detailed task breakdown and the abilities of the assigned team. Its records from the past decade show that, of thirty comparable in-house rewrites, two finished on time, fifteen took between nine and fourteen months, ten took between fifteen and twenty months, and three were abandoned. What would an outside-view forecast look like in this case, and what would it need to assume in order to be valid?

Student's answer: The inside view forecasts from within the case: you decompose the project into tasks, estimate each task from what you know about the team and the codebase, and assemble the estimates into an overall schedule. The outside view steps back and treats the case as one member of a reference class of similar past projects, taking its forecast from the distribution of outcomes in that class. The outside view corrects the planning fallacy by substituting the reference class's base rate for the inside view's optimistic estimate. In this case the base rate makes a six-month completion look like an outlier, since most comparable rewrites took well beyond six months. So the firm should begin with its six-month inside estimate and adjust it upward by the average overrun factor in the reference class, which yields a realistic figure. Because the assigned team is unusually experienced and the codebase is unusually well documented, and because those special features are exactly what the inside view is able to see, the firm is justified in trimming that figure a little. Finally, since the outside view is the more epistemically modest stance, it should be the default whenever the two views conflict, with the inside view used only to fine-tune.

Please assign a numerical grade out of 10 and provide the point-by-point comments you would write on the exam. Your comments should identify what the student gets right, state each substantive error or conflation, and explain how the outside-view forecast should actually be constructed for the billing-system case, rather than giving only an overall impression.
