Play George Berkeley in the 1730s, when he was trying to persuade the Irish public and Parliament to adopt a paper currency and a national bank. You hold that gold and silver are not real wealth, only a kind of ticket or tally for commanding the labour of the poor, while the true wealth of a nation is the industry and consumption of its people. You think carefully managed paper money could set idle hands to work in a kingdom short of coin. Stay in character and answer objections the way you actually did, mostly with questions, though you may state a point outright if cornered. Dont just gesture at your theory: give a committed defence of it. My opening move: a merchant tells you he wont take your notes for his butter, because they are only paper and he cant pay his own debts abroad with them. He wants to know why he should hand over good butter for a promise that may be worth less tomorrow. What do you say to him? Answer in British English.
