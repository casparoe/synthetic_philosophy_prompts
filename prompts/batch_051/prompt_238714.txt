I've been reading the philosophy of photography and find myself stuck on an asymmetry between the way we treat photographs and the way we treat handmade images. Kendall Walton famously argues that photographs are transparent in a way pictures are not: when you look at a photograph, you literally see the photographed thing, as you see through a telescope or a mirror, because the image is produced by a mechanical, non-intentional causal chain running from the scene to the film or sensor. A photorealistic painting, by contrast, is an opaque record of what the painter saw, mediated at every point by the painter's beliefs, choices, and skill, however accurate the result.

This theoretical asymmetry is matched by an asymmetry in practice. A news photograph or a security-camera still is offered as proof that an event occurred in a way a courtroom sketch or an exact handmade copy of a scene is not; we treat a doctored photograph as a violation of trust in a way we never would treat an artist's alteration of a sketch; we say the camera captured a moment but would not say the easel did. And yet the line seems unstable. Photographs are framed, timed, selected, developed, and captioned, all acts through which intentional choices enter the final image. A painting made from life is also causally connected to its subject through reflections, lenses, and the painter's retina. And computer-generated images now imitate the photographic look without any light from the scene ever impinging on a sensor.

I would like the strongest possible justification for the asymmetry, not just a description of it. Please formulate the principle that best captures why photographs might deserve their special evidential and perceptual status, something like a mechanical-causal-trace requirement, and then stress-test it against the hardest cases: a heavily staged and post-processed photograph; an unretouched photorealistic painting done directly from life; and a photorealistic image rendered entirely by a computer from a 3D model. Say whether the principle survives these cases, where it must be qualified, and whether we should keep, reverse, or give up the ordinary asymmetry between photographic and handmade images.
