I teach an undergrad seminar called Minds and Machines, sixteen students, eighty minutes per session. We're about to do two sessions on the Ant Fugue from GEB (the Aunt Hillary bit), and I want the first session to be a game before anybody has done the reading, so they meet the idea in their bodies before they get Hofstadter's terms for it.

Design an activity where the students are the ants. The room (and maybe a hallway) is the foraging ground. The hard constraint is that no ant can have a plan, no ant can see the whole space, and nobody is told what the colony is trying to acheive — the interesting behavior has to fall out of local rules plus stigmergic signals (paper scraps, chalk marks, cups as pheromone). I want a moment where the facilitator points at something the colony did — found a shorter path, routed around an obstacle, relocated food after some ants "forgot" where it was — and asks whether the colony did it, and whether that "it" is a mind, and then the group argues about it before any labels get applied.

Then a debrief that maps specific game moments onto the ideas they'll meet in the chapter — whether the trail is a memory, whether a pheromone is a signal or a symbol, whether it matters that each ant is executing rules it doesn't understand, what "the whole is greater than the sum" could even mean here — while keeping the name "Aunt Hillary" out of it until they've read it.

Include exact rules and materials, a timetable for 80 minutes, the debrief questions, a note on what predictably goes wrong when students run it (they'll try to coordinate from above, they'll look at the whole board, they'll smuggle in intentions) and how the facilitator should handle each, and a frank note on what the game cannot reproduce about real ant colonies — the scale, the timescales, the fact that students are self-aware in a way ants aren't — and which part of the idea the facilitator has to just assert.

Make sure the idea is felt during play, not announced afterwards; the debrief should be where the fight happens, not where the view is revealed. I want them to come away seeing two things at once: the pull of saying the colony is a thinking system even though no ant is, and the worry that we're just seeing a face in the pattern.
