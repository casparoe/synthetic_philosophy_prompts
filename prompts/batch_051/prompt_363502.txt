I sit on the planning commission of a midsized coastal city, and I've been stuck with drafting the recommendation memo for Thursday's vote on a storm-surge barrier. The state climatologist's office gave us a 22-member downscaled ensemble projecting sea-level rise and storm intensity out to 2070, and the members disagree more than anyone on the commission expected. A couple of them imply the small barrier we've been discussing would be plenty; two imply we'd be idiots not to build the big one now; the multi-model mean sits in the middle, even though no individual model actually looks like the mean. One faction wants to design to that mean because, as one of them keeps saying, each model is one plausible future and the average is the best single guess. Another faction wants to design to the upper end because the downside is asymmetric and we can't rebuild the whole city if we're wrong. A third faction wants to build the cheap, reversible option now and keep our options open, on the grounds that the spread itself is the real information and our successors can watch what actually happens. I know how to average numbers, but I don't know how to think about the disagreement itself, especially since many of the ensemble members share code and tuning choices, so they aren't independent opinions. The engineers also keep treating the multi-model mean as if it was a physically real climate, which I half-suspect is a mistake but I can't say exactly why. Draft me the memo. It needs a clear recommendation, with a ten-year trigger for revisiting the decision, and it has to take the losing positions seriously; two of the smartest people on the commission are in those other camps and I need them to vote for this.
