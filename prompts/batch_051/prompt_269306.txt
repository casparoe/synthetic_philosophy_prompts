A paratrooper, Judy Benjamin, is dropped in a region divided into four equal sectors: Red territory is split into Red-HQ and Red-Second-Company, and Blue territory is split into Blue-HQ and Blue-Second-Company. She starts with equal credence in all four, so P(Red)=1/2. Her radio picks up a message from base, which she trusts completely: "If you are in Red territory, then you are in Headquarters Company area."

Here's an argument that she should drop her confidence that she's in Red:

(1) The message says exactly that the material conditional R ⊃ H (i.e., not-R or H) is true.
(2) Learning a proposition with certainty means conditioning on it.
(3) P(R ⊃ H) = P(not-R) + P(R and H) = 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4.
(4) So P_new(R) = P(R | R ⊃ H) = P(R and (R ⊃ H)) / P(R ⊃ H) = P(R and H) / P(R ⊃ H) = (1/4)/(3/4) = 1/3.
(5) So after a message that only told her what would be true if she were in Red, her probability of being in Red falls from 1/2 to 1/3.

I can't decide wether this argument is valid with a weird conclusion, or whether one of the steps is doing something sneaky.
