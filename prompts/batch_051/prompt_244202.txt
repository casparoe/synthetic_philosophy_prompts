I cover science and ideas for a living, and the fight over mandatory arbitration is one of those arguments I've watched go nowhere for over a decade. On one side, people say arbitration is just private dispute resolution that the parties choose: cheaper, faster, more specialized, keeps small claims out of clogged courts. On the other side, people say that's a polite description of a system where a company buries a clause in the fine print, consumers and employees never meaningfully consent, the same arbitrators get hired again and again by the same companies, there's basically no appeal, the awards are secret, and no precedent gets made—so the law stops developing for whole categories of cases. The weird thing is both sides cite the same empirical work and nobody moves. Some studies find consumers and employees do worse in arbitration on certain measures; defenders say those studies don't control for the right things, and that cases that end up in arbitration aren't compareable to court cases in the first place.

I'm not asking you to pick a side. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. My working theory is that it's only partly empirical. Underneath it, I suspect, is a difference about what a court is for: is adjudication a public good—like clean air or a shared language—whose value includes precedent, public reason, and the development of law, or is it just a service for resolving individual disputes, which private parties should be free to contract around? If that's the real disagreement, then no amount of regression analysis settles it, and progress would have to look like something else—a distinction, a reframing, a narrower question. But I also wonder whether there's a hidden factual question inside it, maybe about repeat-player bias or how often arbitration actually tracks what a court would have done, that could move at least some people if it got nailed down.

So: what kind of disagreement is this—verbal, factual, conceptual, or several of those at once? What would count as progress given your diagnosis? And please seperate what is actually settled among people who know the arbitration literature from what is genuinely contested, so you don't present one camp's findings as the consensus. Keep the answer to 600 words, tops.
