I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and my working language is UI and systems architecture. I habitually distinguish between what a system is actually doing internally and what it renders for display; I think in terms of dashboards, status indicators, HUDs, synthetic summaries, debugging views, and the fact that a coherent interface can be presented by machinery in which no single component corresponds to that interface. I am also used to affordances and skeuomorphism: the trash can icon and the desktop metaphor let a user manipulate real state even though there is no literal trash can or desktop inside the machine. I am fluent in this vocabulary and only passingly familiar with academic philosophy of mind.

Restate the debate between realists and illusionists about phenomenal consciousness in that vocabulary, as faithfully and systematically as you can. By realism I mean the position that there is a real, private, qualitative aspect to experience — the idea, associated with Nagel, that an organism is conscious when there is something it is like to be that organism, and that this something-it-is-like cannot be fully captured by any description of function or information. By illusionism I mean the position associated with Dennett's deflationary picture of consciousness and Frankish's explicit illusionism: introspection presents a subject with a simplified, unified, high-level representation of its own processing, but there is no inner theater, no inner observer, and no phenomenal ingredient over and above the representational activity itself. Consciousness on this view is more like a dashboard than an object standing behind the dashboard.

Do the translation side by side. I especially want you to work through three contrasts: privacy (qualia seem accessible only to their owner) versus state that is scoped to a process and not visible to an outside observer; ineffability and intrinsic feel (an experience seems to have a character that resists description and is not just what it does) versus the gap between a rendered summary and the implementation it hides; and the realist's something-it-is-like versus the difference between a system's display of its own condition and a thing that the display is about. For each contrast, say whether it maps cleanly, had to be bent to fit, or has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then give me the residue report: what failed to survive the translation. Finally, give me the verdict I actually want. Is the residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing something — that I am disposed to mistake an interface for the thing it renders, or to assume there is always a user on the other side of the display — or is it a sign that the philosophical claims in question were less clear than they looked, and my vocabulary exposes that? Keep the translation honest: a UI engineer's vocabulary makes it easy to declare the realist wrong by definition, so push back against that temptation and tell me where the translation would be cheating.
