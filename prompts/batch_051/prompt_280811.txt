I think there's a real taxonomy in here and i want something that would have stopped the argument from going in circles.

So build me a decision tree or a which-position-do-you-actually-hold quiz on self-prediction. Start at the question whether a person who is currently deciding between two options can coherently assign credences (probabilities or degrees of belief, not just gut odds) to the acts themselves. The leaves i want are at least these four: (1) no, deliberation crowds out prediction, a deliberating agent assigns no credences to the acts she is choosing between; (2) yes, because you are a statistical mechanism and deliberation is just the stream of evidence updating those credences until one act becomes probable enough to perform; (3) the numbers you assign while deciding are actually intentions or plans, not predictions, so they are fine but shouldn't be read as beliefs; (4) act-probabilities are only coherent from outside or for future actions, never from the first-person present standpoint of the chooser.

The branch cases have to include the following three, phrased so someone can answer without knowing which leaf they are headed for: a friend who knows you extremely well offers even odds on which of two jobs you will accept while you are still undecided, a poker player assigns probabilities to bluffing as part of a mixed strategy, and a brain scanner says with 80 percent confidence which of two buttons you will press next while you are trying to decide. Flag any pairs of leaves the tree truly can't separate instead of pretending, then go through the empty boxes in the logical space and say for each whether nobody is there because its incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And open with the tree itself, no preface or commentary.
