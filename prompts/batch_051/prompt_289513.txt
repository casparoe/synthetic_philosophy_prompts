I keep getting tangled up on what it even means to say the state itself did a wrong, as opposed to it caused a harm, or that one of its employees did a wrong while acting in their official role. Im trying to build a working definition of state wrongdoing — not legal liability and not just "bad outcome," but something like: the state is the sort of entity that can wrong a person.

Give me the definition, run it through every case below, and say which ones it gets right, which ones it gets wrong, and where it just goes quiet. Then either patch the definition or defend the weird verdicts. At the end, tell me which verdict you're least secure about, and say whether your definition makes the old common-law claim that the sovereign can do no wrong a moral thesis, a legal thesis, or a category mistake.

Cases:
- A corrections officer beats an inmate in clear violation of written policy. The state fires him, prosecutes him, and settles the civil suit. (Im pretty sure the state did wrong here, but I can already hear the objection that it policed its own bad apple.)
- A legislature, after open debate, passes a law that strips one racial group of the vote. The state supreme court upholds it under a deferential rational-basis standard. Unjust, but lawful inside that system.
- A public hospital botches a routine surgery because a nurse is on the fourteenth hour of a double shift, and the hospital has been chronically understaffed for years. Did the state do wrong, did the nurse, or is it just an accident with a cause?
- A state agency denies a patient's request for permission to sue the state over a bungled diagnosis, citing sovereign immunity, even though the same claim could have gone forward against a private clinic. Did the denial itself wrong the patient, or just leave an independent wrong unremedied?
- After years of specific warnings from its own engineers, a state keeps deferring levee repairs to fund tax cuts. A flood kills dozens. Did the state wrong the people who died, or is this just bad governance?
- A public health official lies in a press conference about how far a disease has spread, sincerely predicting that the lie will save lives by preventing panic. It causes more deaths than the truth would have, though fewer than the official feared. Wrongdoing, or a bad bet?
- A state executes someone who is later exonerated by DNA, after a trial that followed every rule in force at the time. No prosecutor, judge, or lab tech is later shown to have broken any rule.
- A state raises its gas tax and spends the money on suburban highways. A low-income commuter says the state has wronged him by making him materially worse off. (My gut says this is just not a wrong; say so if you disagree.)
