I'm trying to get clear on whether "democratic backsliding" can function as a non-partisan description rather than a label people apply to governments they dislike. My difficulty is this. Two observers can agree on every relevant fact about a country and still disagree about whether backsliding is occurring. When an elected executive weakens the courts, one observer sees decline from a liberal-democratic baseline, while the other sees a legitimate democratic correction of a judiciary that was itself captured under the previous regime. They differ only in their baseline, and the baseline seems to depend on which conception of democracy one already holds — majoritarian, liberal, deliberative, and so on. If the baseline against which decline is measured is contested in this way, how is "backsliding" anything more than a partisan verdict expressed in technical vocabulary? Is there a criterion that could show one of these observers is wrong without begging the question, and if there is not, should we give up on the concept? After giving your answer, please say what would change your mind about it: which argument or consideration would lead you to a different conclusion, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
