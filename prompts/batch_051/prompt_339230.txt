I run a ninety minute whiteboard session for first-year philosophy of science PhDs who are comfortable with Bayesian updating and Occam's razor but have never met Kolmogorov complexity or a Turing machine. The goal is for them to leave actually understanding why Solomonoff induction's universal prior (2 to the minus Kolmogorov complexity) is supposed to make Occam's razor fall out of probability theory, not be a thing you add to it. Give me a plan for the session: the order the pieces have to be introduced in and what depends on what, the one running example I should use so the prior feels inevitable rather then arbitrary, the specific misconceptions this audience will bring or form (e.g. that Kolmogorov complexity is just gzip size, that the whole thing is arbitrary because it depends on the programming language, that uncomputable means normatively useless) and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off, a check for understanding that would show they actually got it, and what I should openly refuse to cover at this level.
