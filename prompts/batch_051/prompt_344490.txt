I spent most of my working life in the natural sciences, and since retiring I have been reading in the philosophy of obligation. The puzzle I keep coming back to concerns oaths, and in particular the old idea of swearing by the Styx. The story seems designed to force a hard question: the gods, who by hypothesis have no superior to compel them, can nevertheless be bound by an oath on the Styx. If even the highest beings can bind themselves by swearing, then an oath cannot owe its force to a higher authority standing over the swearer. But what, then, could the binding consist in?

I have come to the following argument, which appears to show that an oath can never add any binding force at all.

(1) Either the swearer already had sufficient reason to perform the sworn act, or the swearer did not.
(2) If the swearer already had sufficient reason, then the oath adds no binding force; it merely announces, dramatizes, or records a reason that was already in force.
(3) If the swearer did not already have sufficient reason, then the oath cannot create such a reason out of nothing. A solemn utterance is an event in the world, and an utterance alone cannot make an act required that was not required before.
(4) Therefore an oath never adds binding force; it is either redundant or inert.

This conclusion cannot be right. It would make a marriage vow, an oath of office, a treaty, and a promise made under oath before a court all, in strict terms, mere noise. And yet I cannot locate the fault in the argument. I also do not want to purchase binding force back at the price of a hidden higher authority that enforces every oath from outside, since the Styx story precisely denies that such an authority is necessary.

Tell me which step, if any, goes wrong, and what one gives up by rejecting it. In particular, is it coherent to say that an oath changes something outside the swearer—the legitimate expectations of others, the structure of a shared practice, the actions that would now count as betrayal—without thereby reducing the oath's force either to social pressure or to the very external-enforcer model the Styx case was meant to set aside?
