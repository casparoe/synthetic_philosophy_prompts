Postmodernism is constantly summarized in my department by the slogan that the postmodern condition is one of incredulity toward metanarratives, and yet the slogan is made to do very different kinds of work. A cultural historian uses it descriptively, to observe that people in wealthy late-modern societies no longer believe the grand stories about inevitable scientific progress, national destiny, or the forward march of the working class. A political theorist uses it to argue that Marxism, Christianity, and Enlightenment liberalism are all just stories, and that we are no longer entitled to believe any of them because the postmodern condition has dissolved their authority. I want to know what that slogan is actually asserting. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making—descriptive claims about what people do or do not believe, normative claims about what we ought to stop believing, epistemological claims about whether such systems can be justified, definitional claims about the word postmodern itself, and any others you can distinguish. For each reading, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and then say which of these readings, if any, would actually support the political theorist's dismissal of an entire tradition.
