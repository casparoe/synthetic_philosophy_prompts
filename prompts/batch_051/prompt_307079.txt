Question: Peirce and James are both pragmatists about truth, but their accounts come apart. In a paragraph, explain the main difference and why it matters.

Student answer: Both of them reject the copy theory, where a belief is true if it pictures reality. For Peirce, truth is the opinion the community of inquirers would settle on if inquiry went on forever; it's the belief that no further experience or argument would overturn. James instead defines truth by its practical effects in a person's life: a true belief is one that, when acted on, fits with the rest of your experience and lets you get along. So the core difference is that Peirce cares about consensus among scientists while James cares about usefulness for the individual. This is why James, unlike Peirce, thinks truth is relative: if something works for you, it's true for you, even if it doesn't work for someone else.
