Take the ship of Theseus puzzle and replace the ship with a people that claims a right to self-determination, and the planks with persons. Over enough generations the entire membership is replaced through birth, death, emigration, and immigration, while the group keeps a name, a territory, and continuous institutions. The continuity solution says the ship endures through piecemeal replacement; does the parallel argument show that the later population is the same demos, and so inherits the standing to hold a vote on secession? Or does the substitution change the analysis, because a people persists only through something a ship lacks—shared memory, a joint project, or a practice of mutual recognition—which can fail even while the population is continually renewed? Give exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; one point should identify the single most important disanalogy between the two cases.
