Last night a friend and I got into one of those arguments that just wouldn't die, and I want to see it written up cleanly. I kept saying that what makes a pain a pain isn't the chemistry, it's the causal role — the kind of internal state that's typically caused by tissue damage and typically leads to wincing, guarding the injury, avoidance, and so on. So if an octopus has a state with that same causal profile, it literally has the same mental state I have when I'm in pain, even if the wetware is completely different. My friend said I was confusing the job with the employee. He said 'pain' in a human names the physical state that actually does that job in humans (C-fibers firing, roughly), while 'pain' in an octopus names a different physical state; so it isn't one mental-state type, it's two states that happen to fill the same role. Then he brought up weird edge cases — a human whose C-fibers are hooked up to the wrong functional role, a creature with the role but totally alien chemistry, all of that — and I had to admit my side got awkward in places. I'm pretty sure we were rehashing the role-functionalist vs. realizer-functionalist debate, though I might be misremebering the labels.

Write a dialogue between a role functionalist and a realizer functionalist about whether two systems with the same functional profile literally share one mental-state type, or merely share a role that gets filled differently in each kind of system. Make them respond to each other's actual strongest points, and keep both positions serious — neither one should just be a foil. Have at least one turn where a character genuinely concedes a point, and one turn where they find an unexpected agreement. After the dialogue, make the very last line a one-sentence summary of the deepest remaining disagreement, beginning 'Summary:'.
