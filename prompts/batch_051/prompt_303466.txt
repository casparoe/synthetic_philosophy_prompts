Write an essay on the following controversy. Cicero’s On Old Age (De Senectute) contains a passage, in Cato’s reply to the complaint that old age weakens the body, where he concedes that the aged lack the raw strength of youth, but insists that this is not the right standard: an old man should be judged by the qualities proper to his time of life, especially wisdom, self-command, and the authority that others rightly grant to age. He compares it to judging an old horse by reliability rather than speed, and says that those who mourn lost bodily force are aiming at the wrong object, because what matters in a person is the deliberate use of reason, not muscle. I have paraphrased the passage rather than quoting it from memory. The question I want you to take up is whether this reply succeeds. Does Cato answer the objection about bodily weakness, or does he change the subject from bodily capacity to social respect and mental virtue? Is compensation the right framework in the first place, or does it assume that the good life is a fixed quantity of powers that can be swapped one for another? Weigh the strongest arguments for and against Cato’s position, including at least one modern objection from disability, loss of physical autonomy, or cognitive decline, and then defend a clear verdict rather than ending in mere ambivalence. Throughout, be explicit about how confident you are in your interpretation of the passage and your conclusion, and single out the point you are least sure of.
