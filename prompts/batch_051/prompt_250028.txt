Ok, here's one I've always wanted to see done properly. You know Carroll's little dialogue with Achilles and the Tortoise—the Tortoise accepts the premises but refuses to take the step to the conclusion, and every time Achilles turns the inference into a new premise the Tortoise just asks for another one, so they end up in an infinite regress. For this prompt, imagine two philosophers get put into an adversarial collaboration about what that dialogue actually shows about the justification of deduction. Call the two camps (you can define them more carefully): the regress-skeptic camp, who thinks the dialogue exposes a real problem, namely that basic rules of inference like modus ponens cant be justified without either circularity or a vicious regress; and the constitutivist camp, who thinks the Tortoise's demand rests on a category mistake—rules of inference are not premises, they're what you follow in moving from premises to a conclusion, so the regress is generated by the demand itself and shows nothing wrong with deduction.

Draft the joint statement they would co-sign. I want the usual adversarial-collaboration structure. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, stated precisely enough that neither one is hiding anything: about what the dialogue minimally establishes, about the difference between a rule of inference and a premise, about what a non-circular justification of modus ponens would have to look like if there were one, whatever you think they can genuinely share. Then a disagreements table, one column per camp, with each entry phrased so that side would say "yes, that's exactly my view." Then the cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side—for instance, is the regress vicious or merely benign? Is a circular justification of modus ponens automatically worthless, or could there be an acceptable kind of circularity? Is the demand for a justification of deduction even coherent? Finally, for each crux, say what evidence or argument both sides would agree in advance would bear on it, and record each side's prediction of how it comes out.

And please, throughout, keep the difference between what's settled and what's genuinely contested among people who work on this clear. Dont make a live dispute sound settled, and don't make a contested reading sound like the consensus.
