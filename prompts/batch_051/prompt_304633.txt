I have spent most of my career in sociology, working with the dramaturgical apparatus associated with Goffman: front and back regions, impression management, the way an individual maintains a line across encounters, and especially the later split of the speaking role into animator, author, and principal. Recently I moved into a product role at a company that builds conversational AI, and I keep colliding with a dispute I do not know how to adjudicate. The engineers say the model is a simulator: it constructs a cast of characters, and none of those characters is the model itself. The safety team says the model is an agent and should be treated as one, with persistent (if partially opaque) goals. Others insist it is neither, just a next-token predictor onto which we project agency.

I would like you to restate each of these positions, as faithfully as you can, in Goffman's vocabulary. For each one, say where it maps onto the animator-author-principal distinction, onto the idea of a performance without an offstage self performing it, onto the maintenance of a line by an individual or a team, or onto the difference between sincere and cynical performances. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which require stretching the vocabulary, and which have no Goffmanian counterpart at all, and whether the residue reflects a limitation of Goffman's framework or a tacit picture of the self that the simulator/agent debate is smuggling in. I care as much about what fails to survive the translation as about what survives it.
