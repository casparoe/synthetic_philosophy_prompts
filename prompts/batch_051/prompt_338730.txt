I've been playing piano again for the first time since my mother died, mostly just scales and the saddest things I can find, which may have been the wrong move but whatever. And I've worked out something I've been calling the Unfinished Cadence Theory of musical emotion, and I want someone to tell me wether it actually holds up.

Here's the idea. Real-world sadness, the grief kind, isn't really a feeling sitting in your head; it's an intention that can't complete its motion. When you lose someone you keep half-forming the act of telling them something or reaching for them, and the act gets cancelled before it lands, and the pain is the repeated cancellation, not some separate inner glow of sorrow. Kind of like a chord that keeps trying to resolve and keeps getting pulled back. Music is sad because it can do precisely this to a listener: a phrase sets up a strong tendency, then delays, deflects, or refuses the landing, and the listener's own expectation-and-movement system gets stuck in an unfinished gesture. That stuck gesture is what we then report as sadness. This is why a deceptive cadence, V to vi instead of V to I, sounds wistful even with no words, and why the most gutting moments in pieces are usually the near-resolutions, not the slow or low passages.

I know this is close to the older expectation theory of musical emotion and maybe to motor-mimicry accounts. But I think my version is more specific and more literal: sadness just is incomplete action, and music is one of the only things that can cause incomplete action on purpose and make it feel beautiful instead of merely agitating. So my questions are: is this an actual theory or just a dressed-up metaphor? If it's real, has someone already said this under a name I should know? And give me the strongest counterexample, a piece or a chord or a kind of sadness the theory can't handle, and say wether any of it is fatal. Don't do headings or bullet points or any of that, just write me back like a normal email with paragraphs. And honestly I don't want a reading list, I want to know if I've talked myself into something.
