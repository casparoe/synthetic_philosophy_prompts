my friend and I got into a thing about the judge who turns down the bribe, and he's convinced that "incorruptibility" is a word we should retire because the concept can't be made coherent. He gave me this cleaned-up version of his argument, and I cant find the exit.

(1) A person is corrupted when she accepts a private benefit for doing what, by her own lights, her institutional role forbids. (2) A private benefit is just an incentive, and incentives are everywhere: salary, bonus, promotion, re-election, collegial goodwill, a free lunch. All of them get people to do things they wouldn't otherwise do. (3) So the line between a corrupting incentive and a legitimate one has to be drawn by institutional duty itself: a bribe is an incentive to violate duty, a salary is an incentive to perform it. (4) But institutional duty is just whatever the role's rules and settled practices make it, so the concept can't be used to criticize the institution, only to police people against those rules. (5) The trouble is, roles and rules can themselves be corrupt. A functionary in a rigged system can follow every procedure exactly while selling out the public, and we want to say the office is corrupt even though no individual violated a rule. (6) So "incorruptibility" collapses into one of three things: a factual claim about where someone's price is, blind obedience to whatever the rules happen to say, or just the general claim that someone does the right thing under temptation. (7) None of those is a distinctive virtue, so there's no such thing as incorruptibility.

I refuse to swallow (7). It feels like the same moves would let you retire "courage" by saying it's just a fact about someone's fear threshold, or retire "honesty" by saying it's just following the norms of assertion. But I dont know which step to blame: I'd like to keep (1), because it nails the paradigm case, but maybe that's exactly what I have to pay for keeping the concept. So, which premise do you reject, or is the argument valid with a conclusion I should learn to live with? If I have to give something up, tell me what rejecting it costs and what the strongest version of my friend's position looks like after that move.
