I've been taking a philosophy of science course and we keep circling around the idea that the sciences carve nature at its joints. I get the general vibe—some categories feel like they're tracking something real, and others feel like filing conventions—but I've never seen an actual criterion for telling them apart. My roommate and I got into an argument about whether "planet" is a real kind or just a label astronomers voted on, and we realized neither of us could say what "real kind" even means without waving our hands.

Here's the project. Give me a working definition of a natural kind, one I could apply to a new case without already knowing the answer. It should count the electron and the element gold as natural kinds, and it should count the class of things heavier than a duck as not a natural kind. Then run your definition against each of the following and give a definite verdict—natural kind, not a natural kind, or the definition refuses to answer: biological species, thinking especially about ring species; jade, given that one gemstone name covers two different minerals, jadeite and nephrite; psychiatric categories like schizophrenia or major depressive disorder; planet, after the Pluto reclassification; and the racial categories used in some medical risk calculators. For each, tell me what your definition says, whether you actually endorse that verdict, and why. If your definition spits out a verdict you don't want to stand behind, revise it or defend the surprising answer. Also flag which of the final verdicts you're least confidant about.

After the prose, add a table summarizing everything, with columns: Case, Verdict under your definition, Do you endorse it, and Why. Dont worry if some of the cells are short.
