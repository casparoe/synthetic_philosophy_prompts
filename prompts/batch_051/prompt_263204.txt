Here are my verdicts about when gratitude is owed. A stranger returns my wallet, at no real cost to herself; I feel gratitude and think I owe it. A coworker covers my shift only because the manager is watching and it will help his promotion. The help is exactly what I needed, but I feel no gratitude and think none is owed, though I will return the favor. A friend drives an hour to help me after an accident and, through no fault of his, arrives after everything is already resolved; I owe him gratitude as fully as if he had arrived in time. Someone who dislikes me tries to get me fired; the attempt backfires and gets me promoted; I do not owe that person gratitude. A falling branch misses me by inches because of a gust of wind; I feel a rush of thankfulness toward no one, but I do not call it gratitude. A sanitation official who died before I was born made the decision that kept my grandparents alive and in that real way made my existence possible; I feel something very close to gratitude toward her and am inclined to say it is gratitude, even though there is no one alive to thank. My working guess is that gratitude tracks a will directed at my good rather than the benefit I receive or the cost to the benefactor, but I cannot tell why the dead official and the wind come apart if neither can receive my thanks, or why the coworker's real help does not count. What principle, if any, captures all of these verdicts? Which of my verdicts, if any, does it force me to overturn? Also tell me what your principle says about a case I am torn about: someone bestows a real benefit out of genuine concern for me but against my stated wishes. Please give a committed answer rather than a survey of possibilities.
