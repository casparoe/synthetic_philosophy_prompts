I teach a political philosophy seminar — 18 students, one 75-minute session — and I want an in-class game that makes them feel Pascal's point in the Pensées about justice and force. The rough idea is that justice without power to enforce it is pretty impotent, and force without any claim to justice feels tyrannical; Pascal's line, if I remember it right, is that we can't make what is just strong, so we make what is strong just. I'm not hung up on exact wording.

Design a structured role-play or bargaining game where different players have different kinds of leverage: some have enforcement power (they can punish non-compliance), some have a recognized claim to speak for what's fair (but no muscle), and some just have numbers (a majority but no rule-making authority). They have to agree on rules for distributing a shared resource or assigning roles, and the payoffs are points toward a participation bonus, not real money. The kicker is that the rules and incentives should be set up so participants actually run into the problem themselves — where they see appeals to fairness get brushed aside, or where the powerful side starts describing its threats as legitimate — before anyone names the idea.

Tell me the rules, materials, a point scheme that doesn't need cash, and a debrief that maps what happened back to Pascal's claim about making the strong just. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of Pascal's point the game can't reproduce and I'll have to supply in discussion.

Keep the whole answer under 900 words, not counting any handout text or diagrams.
