ok so in a science/philosophy club I've been going to, we read the structural realist argument: across theory changes, the equations and relationships usually survive even when the picture of the entities gets tossed out, so realists should put their confidence in structure rather than stuff. The usual defense is that all those successful predictions would be a miracle unless some structure is genuinely preserved. But lately I've been reading about parts of science where the best predictor is a giant trained neural net, with no compact equation or interpretable feature list anyone can pull out of it. Does the structural realist argument survive that? If the thing doing the predicting has no transportable structure you can point to, is the realist position just turning into instrumentalism, where the model works but nobody can say why? Which premise actually buckles first: the no-miracle intuition, or the assumption that a theory has a separable predictive core that can outlive its entity-talk? And is there a repaired weaker version that still deserves to be called realism, or was structural realism built for a kind of science that's getting less central? i'm asking about the argument itself, not about whether black-box models are good or bad.
