My background is in statistics rather than philosophy, and after a long pub argument where I defended the line that if two models fit the same data equally well you just pick whichever predicts better out of sample, a philosopher friend told me I was missing the real problem. She described something she called Duhem–Quine underdetermination. As I understood her: for any finite body of evidence, many (maybe indefinitely many) theories can fit it equally well, so the evidence never forces a single theory. And worse, when a prediction fails you are never testing a theory on its own — only the theory plus a pile of auxiliary assumptions about instruments, background conditions and the experiment being sound — so a failed prediction never tells you unambiguously which part to reject.
