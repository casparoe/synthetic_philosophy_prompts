Before I retired I spent thirty years doing statistical mechanics and dynamical systems — renormalization group, order parameters, broken symmetry, attractors, effective theories, that whole toolbox — so that's the vocabulary I still think in. I've recently gotten into the philosophy of large language models, and the debate that keeps bugging me is whether they're agents, simulators, or just fancy pattern-rollers. As I understand the three options: the agent view says the trained model itself has something like goals and beliefs and does means-end reasoning; the simulator view says all the goal-directedness lives in the characters it simulates, while the model is more like a machine for running any persona you request; the parrot-ish view says there's nothing but next-token statistics and the rest is anthropomorphism. I want you to restate each of those three positions in my physics vocabulary — coarse-graining, effective description, order parameters and broken symmetry, attractors, ergodicity, maybe free-energy minimization if it earns its keep. For each one: what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what has no expression at all. Then the part I actually care about, a residue report: is what doesn't survive just a gap in the physics vocabulary, or is it a giveaway that one of the positions wasn't as precise as it looked? Is the difference between saying the model is an agent and saying it simulates an agent the kind of thing that shows up as a real difference in a physical description, or does it wash out as the same effective theory? I know the physics cold but the philsopphy only from a few books and podcasts, so don't assume I'm up on the jargon.
