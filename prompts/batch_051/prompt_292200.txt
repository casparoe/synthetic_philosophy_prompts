I am a PhD student in philosophy working on the ontology of musical works, and I have hit a wall in the early chapters of my dissertation. The theories I encounter all seem built around one paradigm — typically a fully notated work in the Western classical tradition, individuated by a score — and are then stretched, with varying degrees of strain, to cover everything else. Before I commit to any of the standard accounts (Platonism about works, type–token views, nominalism, or performance- and action-centered alternatives), I want the specification of what an adequate theory actually has to deliver, without assuming from the start that the symphonic case is the central one.

Please write the desiderata for a theory of what a musical work is. The list should be able to say what is going on in at least these cases: (i) a Beethoven symphony, whose identity is fixed across many performances by a score; (ii) an electronic dance track assembled in the studio from sequenced and synthesized elements, where the canonical object is the recording and there appears to be no performance of which the track is a record; (iii) a jazz standard that persists across radically different improvisations, arrangements, and instrumentations; (iv) a rock album built from dozens of separate takes by players who were never in a room together; and (v) covers, remixes, and authorized alternate versions, where the theory has to say what is an instance or performance of the same work and what is a new work. For each desideratum, give the motivating case and say whether it is a hard requirement or a negotiable aspiration. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude the standard type–token and Platonist accounts, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied under any one metaphysics. I want the criteria themselves and the reasoning behind them, not a survey of positions.
