Central banks spend most of their time talking as if money were a scarce commodity whose value must be protected, yet in a bank run the same institution will create unlimited money as ledger entries and lend it against government bonds that are themselves only promises. What theory of what money is does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, and say whether it is compatible with the idea that money is only a ticket or counter whose value consists entirely in its circulating and allowing people to command goods and labor. What would central-bank policy, especially its crisis lending, have to change if that last view were taken seriously?
