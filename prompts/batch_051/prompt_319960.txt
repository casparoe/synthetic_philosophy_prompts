You are an expert on the philosophy of free speech and its application to platform content moderation. Think step by step before giving me the procedure, and write your answer in American English.

I work in trust and safety policy at a large video platform. We have a rule that propaganda from designated terrorist and state media accounts gets removed, but we keep a "critique or education" exception. Moderaters are inconsistent, and the exception now gets used for almost any post that adds a critical caption. I want a reusable decision procedure for our reviewers — they have about ninety seconds per item, the stakes are real (removal suppresses speech, leaving it up can amplify coordinated influence operations), and our policy team has to sign off on the final checklist.

I want the procedure grounded in what John Stuart Mill actually says in Chapter II of On Liberty, especially this passage, not just in the slogan "marketplace of ideas":

"But the peculiar evil of silencing the expression of an opinion is, that it is robbing the human race; posterity as well as the existing generation; those who dissent from the opinion, still more than those who hold it. If the opinion is right, they are deprived of the opportunity of exchanging error for truth: if wrong, they lose, what is almost as great a benefit, the clearer perception and livelier impression of truth, produced by its collision with error."

Mill is talking about a government that silences outright; our case is a private platform deciding whether to keep up reproduced propaganda that is being used for critique. Build the checklist so it distinguishes posts where the reproduced material is doing the cognitive work Mill describes — producing clearer perception of truth through collision — from posts where it is just laundering the propaganda for reach. Then apply the procedure to three cases: a 45-second clip of a designated state media broadcast with a detailed debunking voiceover; a 15-minute uncut speech by a designated leader with the caption "this is evil"; and a user who posts the same propaganda clip weekly with "see what they're saying" and no analysis. Mark the steps where a reviewer's judgment cannot be eliminated, and tell me one case where the procedure would probably frustrate someone on my team.
