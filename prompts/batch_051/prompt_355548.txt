Not sure if this is philosophy or psychology, but I keep noticing a pattern in arguments where someone reconstructs the history of the disagreement in a way that quietly advantages them. They'll say "you said earlier that X," or "you always frame it as Y," and I genuinely can't tell whether I said it — not because it's blatantly false, but because it's the kind of thing I might have said in passing. It's never one big lie; it's dozens of small, plausible-sounding attributions over months, and because I can't flatly deny any single one, I end up conceding that their memory of the whole arc is better then mine. That then makes them the authority on what "we" actually agreed to. I don't think they're doing it deliberately — that's what makes it feel different from gaslighting as I understand it, which requires intent to make someone doubt their sanity — but the cumulative effect is similar. Is there an established name for this, and if not, what would you call it? More importantly, I want a careful carve: how does it differ from gaslighting, from plain biased memory, and from someone legitimately having better recall and using it in an argument? And is it manipulation even when there's no intent to deceive? Keep the answer under 300 words.
