I am a third-year undergraduate currently enrolled in a seminar in the philosophy department called Testimony, Evidence, and New Media. The seminar has been working through the argument that cheap synthetic media undermines the epistemic backstop once provided by audio and video recordings. As I understand it, the position has four steps. (i) Photorealistic recordings have functioned as a corrective to false statements because the content of a recording is caused by the depicted event through a chain that, once fixed, no longer depends on the intentions of the person whose claim is in dispute. (ii) The practice of appealing to recordings as a shared, decisive arbiter rests on a standing background assumption that any particular recording is very likely an authentic trace of what it depicts. (iii) Cheap generative models weaken that assumption. They do not merely enable forgeries; they make it permanently plausible that any given recording is synthesized, which hands dishonest parties a deniability benefit: even a genuine recording can be waved away as likely fake. (iv) Consequently, the distinctive epistemic work recordings used to do, namely breaking deadlocks between conflicting testimonies by supplying evidence whose provenance lies outside either party's control, is not recoverable just by adopting stricter scrutiny, because skepticism becomes cheap while authentication remains expensive.
