I spent most of my career in the natural sciences, where a hypothesis earns its keep by telling you in advance what observation would count against it. Since retiring I have been reading social theory, and I keep returning to the controversy over Weber's Protestant ethic thesis: the claim that an ascetic form of Protestantism, one that treated disciplined worldly work as a calling and saw deferred gratification as a sign of a properly ordered life, was causally implicated in the rise of the commercial habits we now call capitalism, rather than merely serving as a later rationalization of them. The thesis is usually discussed as an interpretive claim, but I want to see how much of it can be treated as a testable one.

Please give me the strongest concrete empirical design you can for the claim that such a religious ethic can be a cause of economic behavior and institutional change, and not just an accompaniment or a post hoc justification. Be specific about what you would compare: the units or sites, the measures you would use to capture ethic and capitalistic behavior separately enough that one is not defined in terms of the other, and the confounding factors your design rules out — literacy, congregational discipline, minority status, trade networks, geography, legal institutions, or whatever you take to be the real threats. Then tell me what pattern in the data would count as evidence for a cultural cause.

Now do the harder part. Tell me what even a favorable result would fail to settle: whether the theological content did the causal work or merely marked communities that also happened to have stronger discipline or denser networks; whether establishing that values and behavior travel together shows that values cause behavior rather than that both are maintained by some third factor; and whether a mechanism found in one historical case licenses any general statement of the form that culture can be a cause. I am trying to decide whether that general statement is an empirical hypothesis at all, or a standing interpretive commitment that no single study could move.
