I spent most of last Saturday arguing with a friend about a wasp. The natural history, as I understand it: a female Sphex has stung and paralyzed a cricket, and she drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it at the threshold, goes inside to inspect the chamber, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If an observer moves the cricket a few inches while she is inside, she returns to find it displaced, drags it back to the threshold, and goes inside to inspect once more — and she will repeat this whole sequence, without apparent limit, every time the cricket is moved. The component behaviors are all intact, yet she never notices that the loop has stopped making progress.

By the end of the evening I had come to hold something like this: the difference between the wasp's rigidity and human freedom is one of degree, not kind. Free action is the far end of a continuum of behavioral self-revision — the capacity to notice that one's current routine is failing in its own terms and to change course — and the wasp sits near the other end. On this view, 'could have done otherwise' does not name a further metaphysical fact about alternative causal powers; it summarizes what it is for an agent's behavior to be governed by revisable reasons rather than fixed routines. There is nothing else to freedom.

I want to know what would count against that view. Tell me which observations, arguments, intuitions, results about animal cognition or human automatism, or conceptual considerations should move me away from it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish what a committed defender of the degree view would themselves concede counts against their position from what they would dismiss as a mere re-description of it. I am especially interested in cases where flexibility and rigidity come apart — a creature or machine that is highly flexible in the relevant sense but intuitively not free, or one that is rigid in the wasp's manner but still intuitively free. My friend kept pressing the thought that the wasp's loop only looks stupid because the experimenter keeps undoing its progress, something no wasp evolved to expect, and I am unsure how seriously that should lower my confidence. Finally, say whether the view has quietly redefined freedom so that it cannot fail to come out true, and whether that insulation is a defect or a feature for a view of this kind.
