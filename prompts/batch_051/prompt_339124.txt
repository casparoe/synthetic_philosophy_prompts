I'm a novelist, and I'm on the board of a small translation grant—we give one award a year to get a single novel into English. We're deadlocked on two submissions of the same book, a short, difficult novel from a language that has almost no presence in English. One translator has gone hard in the direction of fidelity: she keeps the syntax of the original, refuses to explain cultural terms, drops footnotes on the page, and lets the reader feel lost. The other has produced something that reads beautifully in English, but only because it keeps reassuring the reader that the foreign book is really just like an English book; whole layers of strangeness are gone. Half the board says the first translation honors the novel and treats the reader like an adult; the other half says its unreadable and betrays the author's plain wish to actually reach people. Friday I have to circulate a short written opinion recommending what we should do—fund one, fund neither, fund something in between—with criteria we could actually use for future years, and it has to take the strongest version of the losing side seriously rather than just bury it. Underneath I think the real question is whether a translation can be faithful by being difficult, or whether fidelity has to include fidelity to the experience of reading, and what a translator owes the parts of a book that won't cross a language barrier at all. Draft me the memo, formal enough to circulate, maybe 700 words, with a clear bottom line.
