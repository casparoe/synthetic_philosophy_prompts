I've been going back and forth with a friend about the user-illusion view of consciousness—the idea that conscious experience stands to the brain roughly the way the icons, windows, and little trash can on a computer desktop stand to the underlying machine code: a simplified, heavily edited interface, not a faithful picture of what's actually going on underneath. My friend keeps saying this is just eliminativism. If consciousness is a user illusion, then there's no phenomenal quality, no what-it's-like to anything—its all made up and nothing is really felt. A desktop is a real interface; you do real work with it, and the interface has real properties even though it doesn't match the hardware. So maybe the user-illusion view is aimed at the picture of a little inner spectator watching a private mental screen, not at the existence of experience itself.
