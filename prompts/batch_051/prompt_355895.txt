I'm a philosophy of biology PhD student and I've been going round in circles with my supervisor about what the word 'function' even means in biology. I can't keep the two main accounts seperate in my head: on one, the selected-effects or etiological account, a trait's function is whatever it was selected for over its evolutionary history; on the other, the causal-role account, its function is whatever the trait contributes now to the working of the organism or some subsystem of it. The first account says a heart that has stopped still has the function of pumping blood, because its history is unchanged; the second seems to say it has lost that function, which strikes me as wrong. But the second account handles cases like a useful side-effect that was never selected for, which the first account has to dismiss as mere luck. I'd rather have one honest working definition than a survey, and I'm fine with being told that my starting verdicts are wrong.

Here are the cases I want the definition run through: (1) a healthy human heart — clearly functions to pump blood; (2) a pebble on a hillside — clearly no function; (3) a heart in cardiogenic shock that cannot pump at all — I lean towards saying it still has the function of pumping, just is not fulfilling it; (4) the human appendix — genuinely no idea, and I'd rather the definition forced a verdict than hid behind the empirical debate; (5) a new mutation in a slow-breeding animal that, as an unselected side-effect, lets it detoxify a synthetic poison that only appeared this generation, and that side-effect is the only reason the animal survives; (6) feathers, if it's right that they first evolved for thermoregulation and were only later co-opted for flight; (7) the nose's ability to hold up spectacles — people sometimes call this a function of the nose, but to me it sounds like a use rather than a function; (8) a pseudogene that hasn't produced anything for millions of years — clearly no function.

What I'd like: propose a working definition of biological function, run it through all eight cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict it commits you to. Please also say how confident you are in the definition you end up with, and single out the one case or point you're least sure about.

Could you structure the answer in these sections, in this order: Working definition (roughly 100–150 words); The cases, run through (a sentence or two each); Where the definition breaks (brief); Revision or defence (brief); Confidence and weakest point (two or three sentences). And write it in British English?
