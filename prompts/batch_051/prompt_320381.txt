You are an expert in the philosophy of cognitive science who is also technically fluent in machine learning. I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I want your help thinking through whether a claim about creativity is testable.

Some context. I work on a generative music system. It learns a high-dimensional latent space that captures the style of a corpus of pieces. We have found that moving along certain learned directions changes musically interpretable features: melodic contour, harmonic rhythm, density, ornamentation, how strongly each next note is expected. The team has started calling these directions knobs. A colleague, influenced by Hofstadter's writing on creativity as variation on a theme, now insists that this is not just a description of our model but the right account of human creativity: a creative act is twiddling a finite set of underlying conceptual parameters on an existing theme. The difference between a routine variation and a celebrated original is which knobs get turned, how far, and in which combinations, not a difference in kind. He adds that the introspective sense of a radical leap or a genuinely new idea is an illusion: we simply lack conscious access to the knobs we are turning.

I do not know whether his claim is false, merely metaphorical, or true but only in a trivial sense. I would like to know whether it can be brought into contact with evidence, and what kind of evidence.

Please do the following:

1. Brainstorm three or four candidate operationalizations of the claim that human creativity reduces to variation along a finite set of underlying parameters. For each, briefly note what would count as evidence for it and what would count against it.
2. Pick the strongest candidate and turn it into a concrete experimental design or measurement program. Be precise about what data would be collected, what comparisons would be made, and what decision rule you would use: which result would favor the knob-twiddling reduction, and which result would favor the rival view that some creative acts require something over and above continuous parameter variation, such as adding a new dimension, changing the underlying grammar, or making a move the old space cannot represent.
3. Say clearly what even a clean result would fail to settle. I am especially worried that any test will have to stipulate in advance what counts as a knob, and that this stipulation quietly assumes part of the answer. Address that worry head-on: does the proposed test test the claim, or does it only test whether a chosen formalism can compress the history of a creative field? Where exactly is the residue, and is it a limitation of the experiment or a defect in the claim?
