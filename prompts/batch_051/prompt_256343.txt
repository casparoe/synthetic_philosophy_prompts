I'm putting together a short statement of my working views on how to update on uncertain evidence for a formal epistemology workshop, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package as I'd defend it:

(1) Full certainty about one's evidence is rare, so ordinary Bayesian conditionalization is at best a limiting case. The general rule is Jeffrey conditionalization: when my evidence shifts my probabilities over a partition, I adopt the new probabilities for its cells, keep my probabilities conditional on each cell fixed, and reweight everything else accordingly.

(2) When the evidence consists in a single proposition E becoming more or less likely, the entire input is the move of my credence in E from p to q. I should update on the two-cell partition {E, ¬E}, and redescribing the same evidence with a finer partition, or distributing credence differently within ¬E, is a modeling artifact that should not move the posterior.

(3) The order in which I receive successive pieces of uncertain evidence should not matter, at least when the updates are about different subject matters: if I first learn something about an election and then something about the economy, my final opinions should not depend on which piece of news I happened to see first.

(4) Rigidity is what does the real normative work in Jeffrey's rule. What I learn never, by itself, tells me to change my conditional opinions given E or given ¬E; it only tells me to change how much weight those conditional perspectives receive.

I'd like you to go through the commitments pairwise. Say which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank any genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be. Finally, say whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most.
