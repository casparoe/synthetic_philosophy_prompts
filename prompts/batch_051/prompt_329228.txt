In teaching the metaphysics of origins, I keep finding the following argument pressed on me, and I have not yet found a stable response.

(1) You originated from a particular zygote, formed by a particular sperm S and a particular egg E.
(2) Necessity of origin: you could not have originated from a different sperm or egg. Had another sperm S′ fertilized E, the resulting person would have been your sibling, not you.
(3) Before fertilization, nothing about S marked it out as yours; S and the millions of sperm produced alongside it were all on a par, and which one succeeded was a chancy microscopic race.
(4) From (2) it seems to follow that for each sperm that might instead have fertilized E, there is a distinct possible person, the one who would have existed just in case that sperm had won.
(5) So, prior to your conception, the realm of possibility already contained millions of distinct possible persons, differing from you and from one another only in which gamete won a microscopic race.
(6) That is an intolerable ontology of possible persons, and it makes the difference between you and your possible siblings turn on a bare lottery that does not reflect anything internal to the persons it distinguishes.
(7) Therefore either origin essentialism is false, or anyone who keeps it must accept the ontology of pre-existing possible persons in (5). Both horns seem unacceptable to me.

I would most like to keep premise (2), but (5) strikes me as absurd, and (3) seems to be only the obvious point that sperm do not arrive with our names on them. So I am stuck.
