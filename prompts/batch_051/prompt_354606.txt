I am currently taking a graduate seminar on Bayesian epistemology and formal learning theory, and over the past few weeks we have been working through the convergence and merging-of-opinions results: Doob's consistency theorem, the Blackwell–Dubins theorem, and the associated claim that, under the right conditions, two agents with different priors who observe the same sequence of data will end up with predictive distributions that agree in total variation almost surely. I find the advertised philosophical moral genuinely attractive. It seems to give Bayesianism a way to concede that priors are subjective while denying that this subjectivity is ultimately pernicious: choose whatever prior you like, and the data will eventually dominate it. That is a tidy and unifying resolution of a standing worry, and I am inclined to believe it.

But I also have a firm intuition that priors do not, and should not be expected to, simply wash out. At any finite sample size the posterior is still a compromise between prior and likelihood, and every actual inquiry is finite; the limiting regime is a mathematically well-defined idealization rather than anything an inquirer ever inhabits. Moreover, the conditions on convergence look less innocent than the slogan suggests. If two agents' priors are mutually singular, or if the model is misspecified or non-identified, they need not agree even in the limit, and the posterior can end up wherever the support of the prior directs it. My intuition is that the prior does something more than temporarily fill a gap that the data will eventually close: it sets the interpretative frame within which evidence is weighed, and this remains true in the only regimes we actually occupy.

I am not looking for a verdict about which side wins. I want to understand what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives, what it would take for the theory to be amended instead — for instance, by restricting its claims to well-specified, identified, absolutely continuous cases, or by reinterpreting the sense in which priors wash out so that it means something weaker than consensus — and whether there is a reading on which both the theorem and the intuition are preserved.
