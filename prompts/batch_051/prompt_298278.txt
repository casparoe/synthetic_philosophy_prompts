Sorry if this is half-baked, but it got personal recently. A few months back I sat in a meeting where my team decided to quietly push through a policy I'm fairly sure will screw over a client. I said nothing. Nobody asked me. What keeps me up isn't that my objection would have changed the outcome — my boss would have done it regardless. It's that my silence, plus five other people's silence, made the room look like it had signed off, and that's part of the machinery that let it happen. I've been calling this "signal complicity": complicity that lives in what your non-objection communicates to a group, rather than in whether your individual act moved the causal needle. The outcome didn't need me; but it needed a room that looked on board, and I helped build that look. My question is whether this is a real distinct kind of complicity or just ordinary complicity described in fancier words. Is there already a name for it? And where does it break — does it make you complicit every time you don't speak up, even in groups that arent really deliberating or when the stakes are tiny? Also, is it even coherent to call an absence a "signal" when nobody in the room was trying to send one?
