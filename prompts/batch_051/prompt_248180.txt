I serve on the Survey Governance Committee at a large public university, which must approve the wording of every survey instrument before it is administered to students. We are reviewing the next Campus Experience Survey, a university-wide questionnaire sent to all enrolled students; its results are used to allocate student-services funding and appear in accreditation reports.

The draft instrument includes the following item, with a five-point response scale from "not at all" to "a great deal":

"How much has the discrimination you have experienced at this university interfered with your ability to succeed in your courses?"

The survey contains no earlier question asking whether the respondent has experienced discrimination, and no response option such as "I have not experienced discrimination." Some committee members regard this as a harmless wording choice: respondents who have not experienced discrimination can choose "not at all" or skip the item, and adding a filter question before every such item would lengthen the survey and depress completion rates. Others reply that the item presupposes that the respondent has experienced discrimination, and that respondents who do not accept a presupposition tend to accommodate it—quietly adding it to the common ground rather than challenging it. On this view, the questionnaire is a language game with a running scoreboard, and a question's presuppositions get entered on that scoreboard just as surely as its answers do; the item would therefore manufacture evidence about the incidence of discrimination while appearing only to measure its effects.

I have been asked to draft the committee's decision memo. Please recommend whether we should approve the item as written, reject it, or approve it with revised wording (for example, a filter question, a "not applicable" option, or an explicitly conditional formulation). The memo should state an actionable bottom line, explain the presupposition-and-accommodation mechanism in terms the rest of the committee can follow, and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
