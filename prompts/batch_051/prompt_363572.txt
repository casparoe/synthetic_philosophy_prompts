"Stigler's law of eponymy was formulated by the statistician Stephen Stigler, and it says that no scientific discovery is named after the person who first made it. The Pythagorean theorem is a classic case: it was known in Babylonian mathematics long before Pythagoras, but we still call it the Pythagorean theorem. Halley's comet is similar, since it had been seen for centuries before Halley, yet it is named after him because he worked out its orbit. The Matthew effect is essentially the same thing: Merton's point that credit in science flows to the already famous. So if Stigler's law says credit goes to the wrong person, and the Matthew effect says credit goes to the already well-known, then Stigler's law is just a special case of the Matthew effect as applied to naming. The law clearly applies to itself, since Stigler didn't originate the idea but credited it to Merton. That makes it a paradox rather than a joke, because the law predicts its own misattribution, and if any eponym might be misattributed then we can never know whether any named discovery is correctly credited."
