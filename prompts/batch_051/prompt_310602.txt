On reflection, I find the arguments for a counterpart-theoretic treatment of de re modal claims close to sound. On this view, when I say that I could have become a mathematician, the sentence is not made true by one and the same me inhabiting multiple possible worlds. It is made true by a counterpart: the inhabitant of some alternative world who is more similar to me, in the respects selected by the context, than anything else in that world. The theory earns its keep: it supplies a compositional semantics with no primitive cross-world identity; it dissolves the puzzle of how a single thing can carry incompatible features in different worlds; it replaces the demand for transworld identification with context-relative similarity, so that cases with several equally good candidates — or none — are treated as vagueness rather than metaphysical crisis; and it does all this without hidden haecceities. I can state these virtues plainly, and I find them hard to resist. Yet there is one intuition I cannot get behind reflection. When I actually consider the possibility that I might have trained as a mathematician, I take myself to be considering a possibility for me — for this very subject, under an alternative history — not sorting some different, similar person into a career. The counterpart paraphrase feels as if it has swapped the object of my modal concern for a stand-in, however useful that swap may be for the semantics.

I do not want a verdict that merely selects a side. Present each position in its most plausible form, then work toward a negotiated outcome. What would have to be true for the intuition to be debunked as an artefact of how the imagination must represent possibilities first-personally? What would have to be true for the theory to be amended or abandoned? And is there a stable middle position on which counterpart semantics is retained as a formal instrument while the metaphysics remains one of genuine transworld identity, or does that position merely refuse the question? Close by saying how one would tell whether the dispute is substantive or merely verbal.
