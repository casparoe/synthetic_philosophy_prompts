I write the kind of near-future literary fiction where a lot of the characters are AIs — not godlike ones, mostly household agents, delivery vans, hospital scheduling systems, that sort of thing. My recurring problem is inconsistency in how they think. Sometimes a machine reads a scene as if it has the full outline open: it notices exactly the detail the plot needs, revises exactly the beliefs the plot needs it to revise, and stays conveniently blind to everything that would complicate the scene. Other times it misses something a person would catch in half a second, and a beta reader says "its supposed to be smart, why was it so stupid?" I have a vague sense this has to do with the frame problem — the old AI problem about how a system knows which facts change and which stay the same when something happens in the world, and which of a million true facts are even relevant to what it's doing. Teach me the version of that problem a working novelist can actually use. I want a reusable drafting procedure, grounded in what's genuinely hard about the frame problem, that I can run when planning or revising any scene with an AI character. It shouldn't assume I know CS. It should fit a ninety-minute revision pass per key scene. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with "this is a writer's judgment call," and where it should warn me that I'm feeding the AI information it has no right too. Then run the procedure on two cases I keep fumbling: (1) a household robot carrying a tray of drinks while two people argue about a third person's secret, and (2) an autonomous grocery-delivery van that, after a minor collision, has to "decide" whether to finish its route or stop. At least one of the applications should produce an answer I won't like. Structure the response as: The frame problem for a working novelist (about one paragraph), The procedure (roughly half the response), Two scenes run through it (roughly a third), Where this procedure will misfire (the rest).
