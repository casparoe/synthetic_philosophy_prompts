I keep going back and forth on a parallel between Adorno's idea of "pseudo-individualization" and modern recommender systems, and I can't tell whether it's a real structural point or just pattern-matching.

Adorno's claim, as I understand it, was that the culture industry produces standardized cultural commodities but disguises the standardization with small surface variations — a novel guitar fill, a singer's affectation, a plot detail — so that each product feels like it's for you even though everyone is being sold the same underlying structure. The variation is real, but it's part of the machinary, not a break from it.

A recommender feed looks like the opposite of standardization: mine is visibly different from yours. But it's generated by one model minimizing one objective across every user, so every feed is in a substantive sense the same thing — the output that maximizes some engagement metric given a user vector. The apparent uniqueness is the product; the feeling of "this is made for me" is exactly what keeps the loop running. So the structural claim would be: a centralized uniform process produces surface uniqueness as a way of managing consumers.

If the parallel is deep, results should transfer. For instance, Adorno thought pseudo-individualization was regressive not because the variation was fake but because it made the underlying sameness harder to notice and so harder to want anything else from. Does that carry over to feeds, where fine-grained personalization makes it hard to see that you and everyone else are being optimized by the same loop? Or does it break because a recommender can genuinely surface niche culture that no broadcast-era industry could profitably produce, so the variation in content is not cosmetic in the way it was in pop songs?

Where exactly does the mapping hold and where does it come apart? Is it one logic of "mass-produced individuality," or are the two cases only superficially alike because one standardizes form while the other standardizes selection and leaves content genuinely heterogeneous? Is there anything the parallel predicts that turns out false?
