I find teleosemantics the most compelling naturalistic account of mental content: a state has the content it does because of what the state's type has been selected—by evolution or by learning—to indicate or to do, and this is supposed to explain both content and the possibility of misrepresentation without irreducible semantic properties. But I also have a firm intuition that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me, assembled by chance with no evolutionary or learning history, would still have determinate beliefs: placed in my immediate environment, it would believe the same things I believe about it, even though there is no history of selection to ground any content at all. Teleosemantics seems committed to saying the duplicate has no non-derivative content, or that its content is indeterminate, and I cannot see how to resist that without giving up the theory's main virtue. Give me the strongest case that this intuition should be debunked as a confusion between original content and an interpreter's useful ascription, the strongest case that teleosemantics should be amended or replaced to accommodate the duplicate, and then a negotiated verdict: which should yield, or is there a reading on which both survive? I want the moves justified, not just chosen.
