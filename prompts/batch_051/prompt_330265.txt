I'm assembling a taxonomy of responses to the discursive dilemma for a seminar on collective decision-making, and I want a response that is not already on my list. For background: the discursive dilemma arises when a group of individually consistent voters decides by majority on logically connected propositions and ends up with a majority for each premise but a majority against the conclusion that follows from them. The responses I already have are these:

1. Premise-based voting: the group votes only on the premises and takes the conclusion to be whatever follows from the majority premises, even if a majority of members would reject that conclusion.
2. Conclusion-based voting: the group votes only on the conclusion and never aggregates the premises.
3. The deflationary response: report the separate majority positions and abandon the requirement that the group itself hold a consistent judgment set.
4. Deliberation-first views: treat the dilemma as an artifact of aggregating fixed judgments, and hold that prior deliberation, by changing individual judgments, can dissolve the inconsistency before aggregation.
5. Distance-based aggregation: select the consistent collective judgment set that minimizes total distance from the members' individual judgment sets.

I suspect these already cover the standard moves, so I am asking for something beyond the received menu. Give me a sixth response that is genuinely different from these — not a rewording, special case, or combination of anything on the list. It should identify which feature of the dilemma it targets (the aggregation rule, the notion of group judgment, the agenda, the inputs, or something else). Then tell me honestly whether the response is any good as a solution and where it is weakest. Finally, say how confident you are in your assessment, and single out the point you are least sure of.
