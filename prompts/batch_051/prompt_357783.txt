I've never taken a philosophy class, but I fell down an Aristotle rabbit hole from a podcast and now I can't shake a problem his account of thinking gave me. As I understand him, he says the soul never thinks without a phantasm, and a phantasm is a kind of mental image, a leftover trace from perception. But I'm a backend engineer, and I don't think in pictures at all — I'm one of those people with aphantasia, and what I actually do all day is reason about type parameters, whether a function is total, whether two monads compose, stuff like that. There's no image attached to any of it.

So I wrote out the argument that's been bugging me:

(1) Every act of thinking requires a phantasm.
(2) A phantasm is a particular mental image, the trace of some particular perception.
(3) A particular image can only represent a particular thing — this triangle, that red patch, that one chipped mug.
(4) But thoughts like "triangle" or "pure function" or "type" are universal; they apply to infinitely many cases.
(5) If thought has to run on particular images, and particular images only pick out particulars, then no thought can ever be about a universal.
(6) But most of my thinking is about universals, unless I'm radically misdescribing my own job.
(7) So either Aristotle's no-thought-without-images claim is false, or I have never actually thought about a universal.

This feels like it proves to much, and I'd rather keep (6) than Aristotle, but I don't know which step is doing the sneaky work or what I'd have to bite the bullet on if I kept Aristotle. Which premise should I give up, and what does rejecting it cost?
