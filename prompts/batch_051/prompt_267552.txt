Im an engineer, not a philosophy person, so I keep wanting to pin this down like a spec. In science-vs-religion threads I keep seeing people quote "the Bible tells you how to go to heaven, not how the heavens go," usually credited to Galileo, and use it as if it obviously settles the whole thing — scripture was never in the astronomy business, so there cant even be a conflict. I got curious and actually read part of Galileo's Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, and I hit this passage where he quotes Augustine:

"Now keeping always our respect for moderation in grave piety, we ought not to believe anything inadvisedly on a dubious point, lest in favor to our error we conceive a prejudice against something that truth hereafter may reveal to be not contrary in any way to the sacred books of either the Old or the New Testament."

That sounds way more hedged than the slogan. It says dont be hasty about an unresolved physical question, not that scripture has no jurisdiction over nature at all. So what precise claims could the slogan actually be making — is it that scripture errs on physical matters, that scripture is only about morals/salvation, that scripture speaks loosely to common minds, that science and religion answer disjoint questions, or what? — and for each reading, does this passage actually support it, or is the slogan doing more work than the text? If none of the readings match, what does the slogan add over what Galileo actually says here? I dont want "non-overlapping magisteria" repeated back at me; I want the readings separated and a verdict on each.
