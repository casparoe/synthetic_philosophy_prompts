In a course on distributive justice, we have been working through the equality-of-what debate, and I find myself attracted to resourcism: the view that egalitarian justice should equalize the all-purpose means people command, not the welfare they manage to derive from them. The pull is that it supplies a single currency with a principled account of responsibility. It refuses to compensate expensive tastes, since preferences are something a person can be expected to manage against a fair bundle of means, and it leaves the results of chosen gambles where they fall. But I have a contrary intuition I cannot integrate. Consider someone born with a serious mobility impairment, who needs costly equipment, adapted housing, and daily assistance simply to move through the same spaces I do. Equal resources seem to leave her with less real freedom than me through no choice or taste of her own. I know resourcists have tried to accommodate this by asking what insurance a rational person would purchase, not knowing whether she would be born disabled; but the answer seems to depend on how badly the disability affects her life, which looks like welfare rather than resources. Rather than a verdict, I need an account of which should give way. Walk me through what it would cost to treat my intuition as already covered by resourcism, what it would cost to amend or abandon resourcism in response to it, and whether there is a stable interpretation of the view on which both survive. If resourcism must make special welfare-like adjustments for disability, has it already ceased to be resourcism?
