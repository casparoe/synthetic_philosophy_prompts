The weird part is that after about half an hour in there, with no light, no sound, and the water at skin temperature so I can't really feel my body, I still have this very clear sense that I exist—but I genuinely can't locate my arms or legs or anything. A friend of mine who studied philosophy says this is basically Avicenna's flying man thought experiment, and he has an argument that he thinks shows the self is immaterial. He put it like this:

(1) Imagine someone is created fully formed, floating in a total void, with no sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch, or bodily sensation, limbs spread so they don't touch each other, and no memory of ever having a body.
(2) This person would still be directly aware that they themselves exist.
(3) But they would not be aware of any body, or any bodily feature at all.
(4) Therefore, the thing they are directly aware of as existing is not a body or a bodily thing.
(5) The thing they are directly aware of as existing is just what we call their self.
(6) So their self is not their body.

I think there's a jump in there, but I can't tell if its step (4) or if I'm just not wanting to accept the conclusion. Is the argument valid? If it is, say so. If it isn't, which step fails, and what exactly goes wrong in that step?
