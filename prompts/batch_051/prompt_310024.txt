Ever since a friend of mine got into Sam Harris's stuff, he's been citing the Libet experiments as the experimental knockout blow against free will, and I keep going back and forth on whether I should be updating or just annoyed at how tidily it all fits. Here's roughly how he summarized it:

Libet had subjects watch a fast-moving clock and flex there wrist whenever they felt like it, noting the clock position at the moment they first became aware of the urge to move. The readiness potential, a slow buildup of electrical activity in the motor areas, started about 550 ms before the movement, while the reported moment of conscious intention was only about 200 ms before it. So the brain had already been preparing the movement for ~350 ms before the subject consciously "decided" to move. Libet concluded that conscious intention doesn't initiate action—it just observes and rationalizes what the brain was already doing—so the everyday idea that our conscious choices cause our actions is an illusion. He adds that a 2008 fMRI study, where researchers could decode left-versus-right button choices several seconds before the conscious choice, just makes the case stronger: the choice is made nonconsciously, and the feeling of deciding is a post-hoc story.

I'm inclined to accept the timing part, but I keep tripping over the slide from "the brain was preparing the movement" to "the brain had already made the decision." And I vaguely remember that Libet himself drew a much more modest conclusion—something about consciousness being able to veto an action it didn't start, in the final fraction of a second. I don't want to just pattern-match my way out of an uncomfortable conclusion either. Could you go through the summary piece by piece and tell me which parts a careful neuroscientist or philosopher would accept, which are legitimate simplifications, and which are just wrong? And does the final conclusion—free will is an illusion—actually survive the corrections, or is the summary doing the thing where it swaps in a much stronger claim halfway through?
