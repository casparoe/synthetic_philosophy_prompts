Write a sestina from the point of view of a single simmball inside the Careenium, as it gradually realizes that its trajectory is being steered not by any additional force but by the shape of the pattern it is part of. The six end-words are: ball, collide, pattern, symbol, push, cause. In the early stanzas, "push" should mean the local physics of elastic collisions between balls; by the later stanzas, it should mean the apparent high-level pressure of a symbol-pattern on its own substrate. The envoi must contain all six end-words and should bring the two senses together without choosing between them, so that the poem itself dramatizes why the question whether symbols push the substrate around can be answered both no and yes. Do not make the symbol a ghost or an extra force, and do not append a prose explanation after the poem.
