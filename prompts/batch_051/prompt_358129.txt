At our tenants' meeting last week two neighbors got into it over a plan to replace the parking lot across the street with a nine-story mixed-use apartment building. I kept out of it, and I still can't tell who was right, so I want someone to go through it move by move.

Maya was against the project and said something like: (1) this street has a real character — low-rise, walkable, shopkeepers who know your name. A nine-story box doesn't just add housing, it erases that. Character is worth protecting, and calling it a "vibe" is how you make sure no one protects anything.

Dan supported it and came back with: (2) "character" is doing all the work there. When you say the street's character, you mean the people already here get to decide who's allowed to arrive. A new building changes the character; it doesn't erase it. And you don't get to freeze the street at the exact moment you happened to move in.

Maya: (3) that's not fair. I've lived here twenty-two years and the street is still recognizable from when I arrived. Change is fine when it's gradual enough that the people who made the place can still recognize it. Saying newcomers should fit in rather than replace the street — its not exclusion.

Dan: (4) "fit in" is exactly where it becomes exclusion. The people in the new building won't have a seat at the meeting where we decide what the character is. You're defining a character that includes you and excludes them, then presenting that as neutral. If we'd applied the same logic when you moved in, most of the people at tonight's meeting wouldn't have passed the test.
