After I retired from a career in experimental physics I got into the philosophical literature on causation, and I keep coming back to the classic two-rock bottle case. You know the one: Suzy and Billy both throw, Suzy's rock gets there first and shatters the bottle, and Billy's would have shattered it a split second later if Suzy had missed. The simple counterfactual account says Suzy's throw didn't cause the shattering, since the bottle would have shattered anyway, while the interventionist/structural-equations side recovers Suzy as an actual cause by holding fixed something like 'Billy's rock never hits' and then making the outcome depend on Suzy.

Here's the variant I've been puzzling over, and I'd like you to actually run it through both frameworks rather then just paraphrase the standard verdicts. Suppose the bottle is instrumented—strain gauges and high-speed cameras—and we individuate the effect finely. Suzy's rock hits at t=0, produces a particular fracture network, and the pieces land in a specific scatter pattern. Billy's rock, had Suzy missed, would have hit at t=80 ms from a different angle and with lower momentum, so the bottle would have come apart along a different set of fracture lines and the pieces would have scattered differently. So the bottle shatters either way, but the actual shattering—the one with this crack pattern, this timing, this piece distribution—would not have occurred without Suzy's throw. Does the counterfactual account now say Suzy did cause the shattering after all, because the effect is being individuated more finely? And does the interventionist account's verdict shift if we enrich the causal model from a coarse 'bottle shatters or not' variable to include timing and fracture pattern? Or is that a cheat—does treating 'the shattering with this exact crack pattern' as the effect quietly smuggle in the cause? I'm trying to figure out whether the two accounts genuinely disagree about preemption, or whether both just inherit the same unargued choice about how finely to slice events and variables.
