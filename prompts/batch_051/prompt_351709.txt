I've been in a small discussion group for a couple years now — nobody in it has a philosophy background, we just like to argue after dinner — and for the last year or so we've kept circling the same fight. It's about whether caring more about your own people counts as a moral preference or as a bias. One regular says, look, of course you should care more about your own kid than a stranger's kid: that's not a flaw, it's a real preference, and any decent moral outlook has to respect it. Another regular says that preference is just kin selection plus tribalism — an inherited gut reflex that makes us hoard for our in-group — and that the whole point of morality is to catch and correct that kind of bias, not dignify it. The strange part is they agree on almost all the facts: the feeling is natural, it evolved, it shows up everywhere, and it's fine when it's just a parent feeding their own kid first. They only get stuck on whether that same leaning, when it shows up in bigger decisions like immigration or donations or hiring, is something to honor or something to fight. The first one says the second is just slapping the word "bias" on any preference he doesn't share; the second says "preference" is just a polite word for an urge we inherited and never chose. I'm less interested in who's right about loyalty than in what is actually going on between them.

I've got a hunch this isnt really a fight about loyalty at all — it might be a fight about what counts as a moral reason, or about whether there's any fact of the matter about which feelings are the good ones. But that could be too neat, so I wanted an outside view. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: a hidden factual disagreement, a difference about standards of evidence, a verbal dispute over the words "preference" and "bias", or just bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, be honest about whether anything could actually move it — a distinction, a different way of asking the question, a thought experiment — or whether this is just the sort of argument people are going to keep having forever.
