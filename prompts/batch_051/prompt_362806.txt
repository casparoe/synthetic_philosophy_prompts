Consider two elementary questions. A working mathematician answers "Is 2 less than 4?" without hesitation, and treats "Is 2 an element of {0, 1, 2, 3}?" as settled trivially by the definition of the set. Yet many balk at "Is 2 an element of 4?" even after noting that, in the standard von Neumann construction of arithmetic, 4 just is the set {0, 1, 2, 3}, so the third question is no harder than the second. The asymmetry is that the same people treat "2 < 4" as a determinate truth about the number 2, while treating "2 ∈ 4" as a category mistake, a mere matter of convention, or a question with no determinate answer. Is anything principled behind that difference in attitude? Give the strongest structuralist justification for holding that the less-than relation belongs to the number 2 itself while set-membership does not, the strongest case for saying the asymmetry is mere habit that should be abandoned, and a clear verdict on how a mathematician ought to regard the two questions.
