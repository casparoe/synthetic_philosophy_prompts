My small group has spent six months with two people who keep talking past each other about Paul and James. One holds the classic Reformation position: a person is justified by faith apart from works, and the deeds a believer does are the fruit or evidence of that faith, never part of what grounds the justification. The other insists James means what he says — a faith with no deeds doing anything is dead, and the usual faith-alone formula quietly turns James into a ventriloquist for Paul. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and they want a document they could both sign rather than another arguement.

Write that document for them. It should be a joint statement, not a verdict on who is right. Don't make it a bibliography; spell the reasoning and evidence out in your own words. Include:

(1) a numbered list of agreed claims about what Paul and James are actually saying and the pastoral situations they're writing into, phrased so neither side flinches;
(2) the exact residual disagreement, with each side's own view worded the way that side would put it;
(3) the cruxes — in particular what Paul means by works of the law, what James means by justified when he uses it of Abraham and Rahab, and whether James is correcting a garbled version of Paul's slogan about faith or an independent current in early Jewish piety;
(4) for each crux, the textual or historical evidence both sides agree would bear on it, and what each side predicts that evidence will show.

If part of their fight has been verbal or about two senses of a word, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
