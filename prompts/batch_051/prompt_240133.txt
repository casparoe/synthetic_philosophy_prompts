Ever since my daughter turned six and found the tooth fairy's note under her pillow, my partner and I have been re-litigating whether we should have done the whole Santa and tooth fairy thing at all, and it's become the same argument every December. My line is that it's harmless shared make-believe, that kids are more in on the pretense than adults give them credit for, and that calling it a lie is already begging the question. My partner's line is that it's deliberate deception by the people a child trusts most, and the fact that most kids turn out fine doesnt make it respectful, it just means children are resilient. Our friends add a third line: the real problem isn't the falsehood, it's the be-good-for-Santa apparatus, the gifts and the surveillance tied to behavior. We've gone round on this for years and nobody moves, which is why my working theory is that it's not one debate to begin with. I think there are three disagreements bolted together: a factual one about how kids actually experience the discovery, a moral one about what makes lying wrong, and a conceptual one about wether a child's half-participation in the pretense changes whether it counts as lying at all. What I want is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a verdict on Santa and not a survey of parenting takes. Is my three-debates theory roughly right, or is this one substantive disagreement wearing different vocabularies? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work, say whether kids who figure it out early go on to trust their parents less in any measurable way, and would a decent longitudinal study actually move anyone, or would each side just re-read the results through its starting intuition? And if the real impasse is bedrock intuitions, like whether parental authority includes a right to curate a child's reality, or whether respect requires full honesty even in play, then say that straight and tell me what would count as progress rather than telling me to agree to disagree. If the whole framing is off, maybe there is no single Santa question, or the debate is confused rather than stalled, please tell me that plainly instead of humoring me.
