I am designing a seminar exercise on Wittgenstein’s 1938 lectures on religious belief, centered on his discussion of the Last Judgment. In those remarks he distinguishes religious belief from ordinary empirical hypotheses and suggests that the believer’s use of certain pictures shapes how she lives, evaluates evidence, and treats contrary occurrences. Please rank the following five criticisms of this account into four tiers—S: decisive, A: serious but answerable, B: minor pressure, C: misdirected—and for each give one sentence of justification plus one sentence indicating the strongest Wittgensteinian reply available from the lectures. The criticisms are: (1) the view cannot distinguish firm faith from unreasonable dogmatism, since both resist counterevidence; (2) it treats religious belief as too non-cognitive to explain doctrinal disagreement and the exclusion of heresy; (3) it overgeneralizes from a Christian eschatological example to religions organized around ritual practice rather than belief; (4) it cannot accommodate the fact that some believers adduce historical or scientific evidence for their faith; (5) it imposes a sharp category difference between religious and ordinary beliefs, whereas actual cognition varies by degree. End with a ranking summary table.
