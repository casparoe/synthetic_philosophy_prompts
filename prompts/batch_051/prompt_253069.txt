Consider a binary risk score S used to make a consequential decision about individuals — for concreteness, predicting whether a loan applicant will default — and let G be a protected group variable. Four candidate constraints on S are:

1. Group calibration: for every score value s, the proportion of individuals assigned score s who actually have the outcome is the same across groups, so the score means the same thing regardless of group membership.
2. Equalized odds: the false-positive rate and the false-negative rate are each equal across groups.
3. Statistical parity: the proportion of individuals selected is equal across groups.
4. Individual fairness: individuals who are similar with respect to the predictive task receive similar scores.

Take as established the impossibility result that a binary risk score cannot in general satisfy both group calibration and equalized odds; the two conditions are compatible only in the degenerate cases of equal outcome base rates across groups or a perfect predictor. Note also that statistical parity can be satisfied by a constant, uninformative score, and that individual fairness presupposes a chosen metric of task-relevant similarity rather than being a single operational condition.

Sort these four constraints into tiers according to how defensible each is as a hard requirement in a high-stakes decision system, once these incompatibilities are on the table. For each constraint, give a single sentence stating its core normative rationale and saying what about that rationale survives, or collapses, when a system must choose among the four.
