At my physics department journal club, two postdocs got into it over Maxwell's demon, and specifically over this passage from Maxwell's *Theory of Heat*:

"... if we conceive of a being whose faculties are so sharpened that he can follow every molecule in its course, such a being, whose attributes are as essentially finite as our own, would be able to do what is impossible to us. For we have seen that molecules in a vessel full of air at uniform temperature are moving with velocities by no means uniform, though the mean velocity of any great number of them, arbitrarily selected, is almost exactly uniform. Now let us suppose that such a vessel is divided into two portions, A and B, by a division in which there is a small hole, and that a being, who can see the individual molecules, opens and closes this hole, so as to allow only the swifter molecules to pass from A to B, and only the slower molecules to pass from B to A. He will thus, without expenditure of work, raise the temperature of B and lower that of A, in contradiction to the second law of thermodynamics."

Anja said the modern lesson is that the contradiction only looks real if you fail to charge the demon for the information work: sorting molecules requires measurement and either storing or erasing results, and erasure costs at least \(k_B T \ln 2\). Evan said that's a confused reading of what Maxwell actually wrote, because Maxwell wasn't making a claim about computation; he was making a point about the statistical status of the second law, and the real resolution is just that the demon's own entropy has to be included in the full isolated system. Anja shot back that including the demon's entropy *is* the information point: if the demon's mind becomes more correlated with the gas, that isn't costless, and a cyclic demon eventually has to erase. Evan replied that a non-cyclic demon that just accumulates bits still seems to violate the second law for the gas, and saying "the demon's memory has entropy" doesn't by itself show that workless sorting is physically impossible; you also have to ask whether Landauer's principle is a law of physics or just an empirical engineering constraint. I want a committed ruling, not a menu of possibilities.
