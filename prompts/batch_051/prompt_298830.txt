A pattern keeps recurring in arguments I have read or overheard in several different fields, and I cannot find a name for it. On the surface, the parties appear to be having a factual disagreement. But if you press them, each side will admit that every observation either of them could ever make would come out exactly the same on both views; what they disagree about is only the unobserved structure that supposedly lies behind those observations. So far this is just the familiar situation of two theories that fit the same facts. The part I find peculiar is what happens next. Neither side concludes that the disagreement is merely verbal, or that the two descriptions are interchangeable ways of carving up the same evidence. Instead, each side treats the other as making an error about what exists, and begins to offer non-observational considerations — one view is simpler, or more economical, or fits better with what is already believed elsewhere — and to offer them with the weight of evidence, even though, by their own admission, evidence cannot reach the point in dispute. I have encountered this in discussions of how to interpret a well-confirmed physical theory, where everyone accepts the same equations and the argument concerns only whether some element of the formalism corresponds to something real or is a convenient bookkeeping device; and I have encountered a quieter version of it in a dispute about whether a certain mental state is a real discrete kind or a summary we impose on a continuum, with no proposed measurement on either side.

My question is whether this specific argument pattern has an established name. I am not asking for the name of the underlying situation, the fact that two rival accounts can be observationally alike; that, I take it, is well known. I am asking about the subsequent move and the resulting standoff: the move of promoting simplicity, coherence, economy, or naturalness to the status of evidence precisely in the one setting where observational evidence is, by hypothesis, unavailable, and the peculiar fact that the disagreement remains substantive and factual while being in principle unresolvable. If there is no exact concept, I would like to know the nearest ones, how my description differs from each, and no bare label: I want a definition with clear edges, plus the boundary cases where the pattern stops being an instance of itself.
