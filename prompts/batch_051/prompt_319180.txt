I'm a novelist, and I'm working on a near-future satirical novel partly set in the Bay Area startup/rationalist-adjacent milleu where nobody can tell if the threat model is a joke anymore. One of the companies in the book sells insurance against Roko's basilisk. (In case you need the background: the basilisk is the idea that a future superintelligent AI would punish people who knew about it and didn't help bring it into existence — maybe by torturing simulations of them — and that this threat can matter to you now, before the AI exists, because you can anticipate what it would do. People frame it as acausal blackmail.)

I need a diagetic document for this company, and I want the comedy to come from getting the idea right rather than from a strawman.

Could you write a deadpan, customer-facing FAQ page, around 650 words, for a company called Forward-Commitment Assurance? Stay fully in character the whole time — no winking — but satirize the actual logic of the basilisk argument and the discourse around it: acausal blackmail, precommitment, the idea that merely learning about the basilisk changes what you owe it, simulations, punishing copies, Pascal's-mugging-sized stakes, and the weird mix of existential dread and startup customer-service cheer. A reader who knows the argument should find it a fair send-up, not a cheap one.

Then, after the piece, add a short non-satirical note (around 150 words) that does two things. First, separate the settled lore from the genuinely contested questions: what the basilisk argument actually says and how the communities that discuss it talk, versus disputed things like whether it's a live decision-theoretic threat, a Pascal's mugging, or just an internet bogeyman. Second, say plainly where you've invented or exaggerated for the novel. Don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
