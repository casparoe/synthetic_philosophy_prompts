I've spend a lot of time lately reading about Hofstadter and Mitchell's Copycat program and the notion of conceptual slippage: the idea that concepts aren't lists of necessary and sufficient conditions but fluid, context-sensitive structures that get built partly on the fly, under pressure from analogy and perception. In the letter-string microdomain, "abc → abd" primes the concept successor so hard that "ijk → ?" feels forced to be "ijl"; but switch the target to "kji" and the concept slips — do you map successor onto the rightmost letter ("kjj") or onto the whole left-to-right group ("lji")? The program lets lots of codelet-level pressures, top-down and bottom-up, push different slippages around until a representation settles. Fine.

Now I'm trying to point that lens at a real episode instead of a microdomain: the 2006 International Astronomical Union vote that demoted Pluto. You know the rough facts: by the early 2000s we had found a bunch of big Kuiper belt objects, Eris looked like it might be bigger than Pluto, and rather than letting the Solar System balloon to maybe a dozen-plus planets, the IAU voted that a planet must orbit the Sun, be round from self-gravity, and have cleared the neighborhood around its orbit — Pluto fails the third test. People usually treat this as a boring taxonomic decision or as a fight between science and nostalgia, but it looks to me like a desperate, bureaucratized attempt to freeze a concept that had been quietly slipping for decades.

So what I want is a case analysis in both directions. Treat "planet" as a fluid concept. What was the slippage doing in the decades before the vote, what exactly did the IAU's three-part definition do to the concept and at what cost, and where did the slippage leak back in afterwards (dwarf planet, plutoid, the endless arguments about what counted as "cleared," the public refusal to go along)? Does the episode vindicate the fluid-concepts story, or does it show that some concepts really do need a bright line and the slippage view overcorrects? Try to keep the mapping honest: tell me where the letter-string microdomain is genuinely illuminating and where it's a strained analogy.
