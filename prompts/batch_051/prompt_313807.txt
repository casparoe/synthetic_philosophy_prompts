Before I look at any more claims that a language could lack pronouns or other indexicals, I want the standards fixed in advance. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of an indexical-free language must meet: which facts about ordinary indexical use it must explain away or reproduce; whether it must show that all indexical sentences have equivalent non-indexical paraphrases, or only that a community could get by without dedicated indexical forms; which distinctions it must respect (for example, context-dependent content versus context-dependent truth conditions, deictic gesture versus grammatical person, first-person reference versus first-person thought); what it must say about the peculiar failure of self-identification and about error through misidentification; and what kinds of evidence about a possible or an actual language would count for or against the view. Then briefly score the two main candidate positions—a replacement view on which indexicals are eliminable in principle, and a deflationary view on which indexicals are just shorthand for shared contextual descriptions—against your list.
