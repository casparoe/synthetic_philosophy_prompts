Everyone learns the Descartes move: I can doubt my body, the external world, even math, but I can't doubt that I'm thinking right now, because doubting is itself a kind of thinking. What I've never fully gotten is what, exactly, this is supposed to prove. Does it prove that a persisting thing (me) exists, or only that some episode of thinking is going on? Is the move an inference from a premise like "thinking is happening" to a conclusion like "something exists," or is it more like a report that can't be false while it's being made? And can the bare fact that there's thinking even get you to an "I"? I'd like you to lay out, as strictly as possible, what the cogito licenses if you refuse to import any assumptions beyond what the scenario itself guarantees, and then say where the usual version slips in more then that, if it does.
