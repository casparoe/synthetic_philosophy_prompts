Somewhere along the way I ran into the Ellsberg paradox and the surrounding debate about ambiguity aversion, and I've ended up defending a position I'd like you to stress-test: that Ellsberg-type choices are not a mistake to be debiased, but a reasonable response to uncertainty about probabilities, in roughly the way robust-control or maxmin-expected-utility reasoning treats a set of possible distributions rather than a single one. I mean this as a normative claim about rationality, not just a descriptive claim about what people happen to do.

I already know the standard objections, so I don't need an introduction to them; I want to know where the view is genuinely exposed.
