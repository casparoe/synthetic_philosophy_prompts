I chair the board of a small foundation whose charter commits it to promoting frugality. Our history is one of modest, hands-on work: repair cafés, tool libraries, and small grants to community groups. Our executive director now brings us a proposal for a national campaign against wasteful household consumption, with a seven-figure media budget. It would involve professionally produced advertisements, paid celebrity endorsements, first-class travel for staff, and a launch event at an expensive hotel. The staff concede the apparent contradiction, but their analysis, which our outside evaluator regards as competent though necessarily uncertain, indicates that a campaign with this production quality would reduce household consumption far more than our usual programming would, and by more than enough to offset the consumption the campaign itself would create. The staff argue that the foundation's purpose is to make the world more frugal, not to live frugally as an end in itself, and that refusing to spend visibly would be self-congratulation at the expense of measurable results.

Several trustees disagree sharply. They say frugality is not merely an outcome we chase but a discipline that must govern our own conduct. If we preach restraint from a luxury ballroom, they argue, we have already contradicted what we are saying, because the public reads character from conduct, and an organization that will not practice the restraint it asks of others has no standing to ask for it. They add that the foundation's integrity is not for sale, whatever the projected results.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend one course of action — approve the campaign, reject it, or approve a modified version with specific conditions — and it should present the strongest version of each side's position before taking its own. Do not paper over the tension with a vague compromise; if you propose conditions, explain the principle that makes those conditions something other than splitting the difference.
