I'm working through a biology textbook that summarizes Darwin on morality and religion like this: "Darwin showed that conscience is just another evolved instinct—like hunger or fear—installed by natural selection because tribes whose members were more cooperative and self-sacrificing out-competed other tribes. Darwin was upfront about the consequence, namely that an evolved moral sense has no more authority than any other impulse, so there are no objective moral facts. And by the same logic, since religion spread because it glued early societies together, belief in God is a useful fiction we evolved to tell ourselves." This feels to strong to me—I'd always heard Darwin was careful not to say evolution debunks morality, and I'm not sure he'd have called religious belief a "useful fiction." But the textbook is by an actual biologist and I'm no Darwin scholar. Is this a fair summary of what Darwin actually says in The Descent of Man?
