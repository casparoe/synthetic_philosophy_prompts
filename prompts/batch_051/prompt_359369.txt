Something I think about a lot but can't get to the bottom of is the Sleeping Beauty problem, specifically why the two sides are stuck. The setup: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped. Heads, she's woken only Monday; tails, she's woken Monday and Tuesday, but an amnesia drug erases the memory of any earlier waking, so each waking feels like the only one. She knows all of this. At each waking she's asked for the probability the coin landed heads. The halfers say 1/2: waking up gives her no new information, since she knew she'd be woken at least once no matter what, so her credence should stay at the coin's 50/50. The thirders say 1/3: there are three possible wakings, only one belongs to heads, and since she can't tell them apart she should spread her credence over all three. Same experiment, same rules, smart people deadlocked for decades. Part of me thinks the two sides are really answering different questions about the word 'probability'—one about the coin itself, one about which waking she's caught in—but that might just be a verbal dodge. So I'm asking for a diagnosis, not a verdict: what kind of disagreement is this? Is it verbal, or substantive about evidence or self-location, or a hidden disagreement about what counts as a fair sample, or is the question just ill-posed? And if your diagnosis is right, what would actually count as progress—a distinction, a reformulation, or is it the kind of thing that never settles? Use each side's actual reasoning only to locate the impasse, don't give me a survey of the arguments. And write it as continuous prose only, no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, like an email.
