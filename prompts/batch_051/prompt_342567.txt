I keep going back and forth on whether the standard dig at Leibniz is actually fair. A professor in a lecture laid it out like this: "Leibniz's whole position is that God, being perfect, had to create the best possible world — so everything that happens in it, wars, diseases, whatever, is for the best once you see it properly, and we should basically accept it. Voltaire skewered this already with Pangloss in Candide: if this really is the best possible world, there's no reason to try to improve it." Something about that feels off to me, but i can't put my finger on it. Where exactly does the real Leibniz agree, where does this slide into caricature, and is the last step — that his view makes trying to improve things pointless — something Leibniz would actually have to grant?
