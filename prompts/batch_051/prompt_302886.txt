Ancient accounts of Persephone's abduction pull in different directions. The fullest early version is the Homeric Hymn to Demeter, quoted below in Hugh G. Evelyn-White's translation:

"she was playing with the deep-bosomed daughters of Oceanus and gathering flowers over a soft meadow, roses and crocuses and beautiful violets, irises also and hyacinths and the narcissus, which Earth made to grow at the will of Zeus and to please the Host of Many, to be a snare for the bloom-like girl — a marvellous, radiant flower. It was a thing of awe whether for deathless gods or mortal men to see: from its root grew a hundred blooms and it smelled most sweetly, so that all wide heaven above and the whole earth and the sea's salt swell laughed for joy. And the girl was amazed and reached out with both hands to take the lovely toy; but the wide-pathed earth yawned there in the plain of Nysa, and the lord, Host of Many, with his immortal horses sprang out upon her —the Son of Cronos, He who has many names. He caught her up reluctant on his golden car and bare her away lamenting."

In this telling, the seizure is no accident: Earth grows the narcissus at Zeus's will as a snare for the girl, and the plain itself opens so that Hades can spring out. Other tellings shrink that apparatus. Ovid's Metamorphoses reduces the episode to something like a sudden act of Pluto's desire, with no planted flower-snare or gaping earth, and Apollodorus' Library gives only a bare abduction. Yet the Hymn's account is routinely treated as the canonical version of the myth.

That word "canonical" is the issue. On one reading it is a historical claim—that a culture settled on a standard or authoritative version—and on another it is an honorific we retroactively attach to the earliest surviving or most detailed text. How could you test the claim that some version, the Hymn's for concreteness, is canonical rather than merely earliest, fullest, or most famous? What evidence or measurements would you gather, and what result would count as positive? Then state what that positive result would and would not settle—specifically, whether it could show that a canon exists, or only that one variant dominated the surviving record. Please give your answer in American English.
