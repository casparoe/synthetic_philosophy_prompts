I spent most of my career in the physical sciences, where I made heavy use of optimization and, toward the end, of machine-learning methods; the no-free-lunch theorems have always struck me as among the cleanest results in that field. As I understand them, they say that if one averages over all possible objective functions, every optimization algorithm performs exactly as well as every other, and that an analogous result holds in supervised learning: averaged over all possible target functions, no learner generalizes better than any other. An algorithm can outperform chance on one well-behaved class of problems only by doing correspondingly worse on some other class, so the advantage is purchased entirely by the prior assumptions built into the algorithm. In retirement I have begun reading the classics of epistemology, particularly Hume and Goodman, and I cannot shake the impression that I am looking at the same theorem written in a different notation. Hume, as I read him, argues that any inference from observed regularities to unobserved cases presupposes that nature is uniform, and that this presupposition cannot itself be justified except circularly. Goodman adds that unless we restrict the class of admissible hypotheses, the very same observations confirm indefinitely many incompatible projections. Lining the two up—learning algorithms with inductive strategies, objective functions with ways the world might be, and search or predictive performance with epistemic success—both seem to say that without a substantive prior, no course is better than any other.

What I would like to understand is whether this resemblance is deep or superficial. If it is deep, does the no-free-lunch theorem amount to a formal vindication of inductive skepticism, or does it instead show that the skeptic is demanding a presuppositionless justification for a task that the mathematics proves cannot be performed? If it is superficial, where exactly does the analogy break down, and what would have to be added to the formal setting to recover the genuine structure of induction as science actually practices it?
