My rationalist-adjacent reading group has been stuck for something like a year on whether the golden rule can be fixed, and Ive started to think the object-level impasse is the interesting part. One camp says the standard formulation is just a reciprocity heuristic and the obvious patch is the so-called platinum rule: treat people as they want to be treated. The other camp says that patch destroys the point, because the whole point of the golden rule is to get some distance from the other persons actual preferences and apply a symmetry test: would I endorse this action if I didnt know which side of it I occupied. We all agree on the stock cases, the person who wants brutal honesty and the person who wants comfort, the domineering parent and the self-neglecting friend, but nobody updates. I cant tell whether this is a verbal dispute about what the golden rule names, a substantive dispute about whether first-person preferences are morally authoritative or just data, or a dispute about whether a moral rule should be a decision procedure at all rather than a fairness check. My hypothesis is that were really disagreeing about whether the golden rule is supposed to remove special pleading or to track other peoples interests, and those two goals come apart in the hard cases. Diagnose the structure of the disagreement and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress.
