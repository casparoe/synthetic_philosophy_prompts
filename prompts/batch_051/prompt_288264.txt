Construct a decision tree that sorts people into positions on why promises bind: in virtue of what does a promise obligate the promisor? Work from the logical space rather than from a list of proper names. First formulate the diagnostic questions, each a concrete case or claim on which the main positions come apart, phrased so that someone can answer it without already knowing the labels, and then locate the actual positions as descriptively named leaves. Flag any combinations of answers that no serious position occupies, and for each say whether it is empty because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I need this for a short course module on whether promises to strangers online bind in the same way as face-to-face promises, so the tree should especially test whether the obligation survives: a promisee wholly indifferent to performance; a promisee who could not have relied; a promise made without any backing social practice; and a promise whose performance is trivially easy. The leaves should include at minimum an assurance/expectation view, a convention/practice view, a reliance/harm view, a consent/voluntary-transfer view, and a relationship/trust view; add more if the logical space requires them. Keep the tree to roughly six to nine branch questions, and flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot actually separate, stating what further question would separate them.
