I am a doctoral student working on the metaphysics and epistemology of reasons, and I would like you to write an essay on a focused controversy in this area: whether there can be reasons without reasoners, sometimes called free-floating rationales. The essay should reach and defend a definite conclusion rather than merely surveying the debate. Please do not include a bibliography or citations; the essay should stand on its own argument.

One side holds that reasons are constitutively tied to reasoners. On this view, a consideration counts as a reason only in virtue of its relation to some agent's psychology or inferential practice, and talk of a reason existing while no one has it is at best an elliptical way of speaking. The opposing side holds that reasons are, in the first instance, facts or propositions that count in favour of conclusions or actions; on this view, a reason can exist and be normatively in force before anyone notices or articulates it. The pressure points are cases in which a rationale appears to be genuinely operative in a collective practice even though no individual member of the collective can state it. Consider a research consortium that follows a statistical protocol whose real justification is that it controls a particular selection bias, although the protocol was copied from a neighbouring field and no current member could say why it survives; or consider a market whose allocation of resources is responsive to information dispersed across thousands of traders, none of whom holds the overall rationale.

In your essay, weigh the principal arguments on each side and engage in depth with at least one such extended case. It may help to distinguish between a reason's being in force for a collective and a reason's being possessed by any individual, or between a reason that is merely unarticulated and a reason that is no one's reason; but these distinctions should not become a substitute for a verdict. By the end, you should either defend the coherence and usefulness of the idea of a free-floating rationale, defend the stronger claim that all genuine reasons depend on reasoners, or defend a clearly delimited hybrid position, and you should justify that verdict against the strongest objection to it.
