I am a high school student preparing a twenty-minute presentation for my school's Philosophy and Debate Society, and I would like you to act as an expert epistemologist helping me prepare for the question-and-answer session. In the talk I will defend a moderate conciliationist view about peer disagreement: when I learn that someone I have good reason to treat as my epistemic equal—roughly as familiar with the relevant evidence and as competent at evaluating it—holds a substantially different view about some non-trivial factual or philosophical question, I should significantly reduce my confidence in my own view, though I am not committed to splitting the difference exactly and I need not defer in cases of ordinary perception or simple arithmetic. My outline of the argument is this: independent evaluations of shared evidence by equals should carry evidential weight; my merely having reached a judgement gives no special reason to prefer it; and partial rather than full suspension is enough to respect my peer without letting disagreement act as a veto.

The audience will be around twenty-five students aged sixteen to eighteen, mostly from the debating and philosophy clubs, plus the supervising teacher, who has graduate training in philosophy with an analytic orientation. The debating students tend to prize defending a position under pressure and often say that everyone has a right to their own opinion; several others suspect that changing your mind when challenged is intellectually weak. I expect the objections that actually arise to reflect those commitments.

Before drafting, please think carefully through this audience's likely background assumptions. Then produce a question-and-answer preparation sheet, in British English, that includes: the eight or so objections I am most likely to receive from this specific audience rather than from professional epistemologists, ordered by a judgement of how likely and how damaging each one is; each objection phrased in the vocabulary a member of this audience would actually use, with a note on who is likely to raise it and whether the underlying concern has real force; a reply I can deliver in under two minutes; and an honest note on where each reply is weakest. Please also finish with the question I should most hope nobody asks, and explain briefly why it would unsettle my position.
