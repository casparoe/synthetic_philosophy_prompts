The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem is standardly glossed as showing that, once there are at least three alternatives and no domain restrictions, every non-dictatorial social choice function is manipulable via strategic misreport. Yet the field that teaches this theorem also advertises a substantial family of mechanisms as strategy-proof. I realize I must be misreading the theorem's scope, but I cannot see exactly where. If non-dictatorial strategy-proofness is impossible in the setting the theorem describes, what precisely do these strategy-proof mechanisms achieve? Is the term strategy-proof being used to mean something weaker than what the theorem rules out, or is the achievement confined to preference environments and institutional assumptions that the theorem does not cover? And if the latter, why should the theorem be treated as a central impossibility result for social choice rather than merely as a boundary condition marking where strategy-proof design is possible?
