I keep noticing an asymmetry in my own judgment and I want to know whether it can be justified. My friend Dave has eaten terribly and never exercised for as long as I've known him. When he tells me I should take my cholesterol seriously and change my diet, I immediately think, you're a fine one to talk, and the advice lands with no force at all — even though he's right, and he probably knows more about my bloodwork than I do. But when the same Dave tells me that my statin interacts with grapefruit and walks me through the evidence, I don't discount it one bit; his own cheeseburger habit seem irrelevant to whether that's true. So I treat hypocrisy as undercutting blame and advice, but not factual testimony. Is there a real principle behind this, or am I just being defensive? The usual explanations don't obviously work: his reliability as a source is the same in both cases, his sincerity is the same, and he stands to benefit the same either way. I want the strongest principle you can build for why a hypocrite can inform but not admonish, and then I want it pushed against nearby cases: a doctor who smokes telling a patient to quit, a convicted fraudster warning me not to lie on my taxes, a corrupt politician telling me I should vote in local elections. Do those come out right? And if no principle does both, which side of my asymmetry should I give up?
