Question (10 marks): State the Condorcet jury theorem in its simplest form and explain the role of its independence assumption. In your answer, discuss the case where the individual competence p is below one half, and say whether the theorem by itself gives a general reason to prefer larger decision-making groups.

Student's answer:
"The Condorcet jury theorem says that if an odd number n of voters choose between two alternatives, one of which is the correct one, and each voter has the same probability p of choosing the correct alternative, with p > 1/2, and their votes are independent of one another, then the probability that a majority picks the correct alternative is greater than p, and as n goes to infinity this probability approaches 1. So even a large group of only slightly-better-than-chance voters is virtually infallible. For instance, with 500 voters each 51% likely to be right, the majority is right more than 99% of the time.

The independence assumption matters because otherwise the result breaks down. If all the voters read the same misleading source, their errors are correlated, and the majority can be wrong just as often as a single voter. This means the theorem only applies when the voters have formed their views completley independently of one another — for example, they haven't deliberated together or shared evidence. That is why the theorem is mathematically elegant but not a real justification for majority rule, since real voters always deliberate and share evidence. Finally, if p < 1/2 the theorem reverses: larger groups make things worse, and the majority converges to being wrong with certainty as n grows."
