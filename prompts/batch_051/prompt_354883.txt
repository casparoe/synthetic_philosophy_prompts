I've got a policy for reading forums and comment sections that I'm starting to suspect is just prestige bias with a number attached. The rule: when I don't know the people involved and the topic is the kind where being articulate isn't the same as being right, I mostly ignore posts from accounts below some karma threshhold, and I treat disagreement from several low-karma accounts as not really counting against one high-karma account. Worse, I do it across topics — a high-karma account that mostly posts cat pictures gets the benefit of the doubt from me on a vaccinology question. Last week I trusted a 60k-karma account's claim that a certain drug interaction was overblown over two low-karma accounts who actually cited something, and I couldn't have restated the argument myself.

I know karma is measuring something, but I don't know exactly what, and it might be measuring 'posts a lot and tells people what they want to hear.' So: is this defensible as a time-saving policy, or is it a way of protecting whatever the high-status users already believe? When does it fail in ways I wouldn't notice from the inside? Is there a principled version — say a discount rate instead of ignoring low-karma accounts entirely, and topic-relative reputation instead of global karma — and how would I tell that the policy is serving my epistemic goals rather than just my comfort?
