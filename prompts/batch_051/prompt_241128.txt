I've been stuck on the brute luck / option luck distinction ever since my insurer denied my appeal: according to them my condition traces back to choices I made, so it counts as option luck and I'm the one who bears it. Could you write me a Petrarchan sonnet about that line? Let the octave set up the distinction at its most confident, with the hypothetical-insurance intuition that makes it look workable — brute bad luck is what we'd have insured against before knowing how things fell out, option luck is the gamble you knowingly took. Then the volta should be the moment the line starts sliding: the temperament, the genes, the social lottery that set the odds, the very capacity to choose well, all of it brute luck smuggled inside the "choice." I dont want it to resolve into "so nobody is ever responsible"; I want the final line to leave open whether the line can be drawn at all, because that's honestly where I've landed.
