After retiring from a research career in the physical sciences, I have been reading systematically in moral philosophy, and I would like an audit of a position I intend to defend at an interdisciplinary seminar. The position concerns the possibility of genuine moral dilemmas. I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in being told whether my own commitments jointly hold.

I hold the following:

(1) Moral realism: there are stance-independent moral facts, at least about a core class of deontic considerations such as promise-keeping, truthfulness, and avoiding serious harm.

(2) Rossian pluralism: these considerations are prima facie duties; when they conflict, the all-things-considered obligation is determined by whichever duty is weightiest in the circumstances, and the overridden duty ceases to be a live requirement.

(3) Incommensurability: some conflicts involve considerations that cannot be completely ranked against one another, and in such cases there is no fact of the matter about which duty is weightiest.

(4) Genuine dilemmas: in some of those incommensurable conflicts, both requirements remain genuinely binding; the agent cannot satisfy both; and whatever she does she violates a genuine obligation, leaving a moral remainder for which she is not necessarily blameworthy.

(5) Ought implies can: an agent cannot be under an all-things-considered obligation to do something impossible.

(6) No residual duties after right action: if an agent has done what she was all-things-considered required to do, then any overridden prima facie duty has lapsed, and no unmet moral demand remains; any remaining discomfort is not the response to a real obligation.

I want to know whether this set is consistent, and if not, where the genuine fault lines are. I suspect a tension between (3) and (1), but I may be wrong; and I am unsure whether (2) and (6) simply repeat one another, or whether they say different things that jointly rule out (4). Please sort the tensions by severity, distinguish the real from the merely apparent, and identify the least costly revision and what it costs.
