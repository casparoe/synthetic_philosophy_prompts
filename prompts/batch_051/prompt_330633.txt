I recently joined the rules committee of a municipal council after two years of watching badly drafted ordinances cycle back to us because residents, enforcement officers, and even the courts cannot agree on what they require. A colleague has circulated a proposal that I find difficult to evaluate on the merits, because my instinct is to like anything that promises clearer law. I would like you to tell me how it would actually behave if adopted, not whether it is admirable in the abstract.

The proposal is this. No ordinance may take effect until a standing Law Quality Panel has issued a public certificate. The Panel would consist of two legislative drafters borrowed from other municipalities, one retired judge, and five residents drawn by lot from the voter rolls. For each proposed ordinance, the Panel would publicly rate it on eight standards: generality, so that it binds classes rather than named persons; prospectivity, so that it avoids retroactive effect; clarity; consistency, both within the text and with the existing body of ordinances; possibility of compliance, so that it does not command what ordinary people cannot do; stability, so that it does not casually unsettle arrangements people have built around recent law; congruence, meaning that the text matches how the agencies and officers say they will enforce it; and lay accessibility, meaning that an attentive nonspecialist can restate the main requirements after one reading. An ordinance takes effect only if the Panel certifies all eight standards as met, and the certificate and its reasons are published alongside the ordinance. Finally, if a court later finds that a sponsor obtained the certificate by misdescribing how the law would be enforced, the ordinance is void from the outset, even as to conduct that occurred before the finding.

Walk me through a plausible ten-year arc for this institution. What would it incentivize among the drafting office, council members, the agencies that must describe their enforcement plans for the certificate, and the residents who rotate through the Panel? Where would it be gamed first, and what would that gaming look like—staged enforcement promises, capture of the Panel by repeat players, routing of governance through instruments that escape the audit, or box-ticking ceremonies that leave the text untouched in practice? Which of the eight standards would degenerate from a substantive test into a performance, and what would that degeneration look like on the ground? I also want to know what this does to the rule of law as an ideal: some traditions treat these formal virtues as themselves morally structured, others treat the rule of law as a politically neutral instrument that good and bad regimes can each use, and still others locate its value in the way legal processes address people as capable reason-givers. Please separate what would follow robustly from the design from what is merely speculative, and do not present one of those traditions as the settled view.
