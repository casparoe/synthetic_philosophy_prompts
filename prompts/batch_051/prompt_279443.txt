I work in a social science department, and as part of a methods workshop each of us has been asked to set down our standing commitments on the individualism–holism question so the rest of the group can stress-test them. Here is where I currently am, stated as plainly as I can.

(1) I am an ontological individualist about the social world. Social facts are nothing over and above facts about individuals: their properties, their actions, their beliefs, and, crucially, their relations to one another. If two worlds match individual by individual in all those respects, they match socially.

(2) I am also, in at least one respect, an explanatory holist. Many of our best social-scientific explanations are irreducibly macro: an account of why a norm persists, or of what an institution does to the distribution of life chances, can be a fully satisfactory explanation without being translatable into a story about named individuals and their choices.

(3) Nonetheless, I think the microfoundations demand is usually sound. An aggregate-level regularity or mechanism that cannot be connected to any intelligible individual-level process is a black box, and I distrust black boxes as explanations.

(4) Yet I am skeptical of rational-choice reconstructions in particular. When a norm, an institution, or a pattern of collective behavior is explained as the equilibrium of optimizing individuals, I usually find the story false or beside the point; I would sooner appeal to structural mechanisms such as network position, path dependence, or the way an institution makes some courses of action costlier than others.

(5) I also believe social structure is causally efficacious. Changing an institutional rule can redirect what people do without changing anyone's individual psychology: no one has to become more self-interested, more biased, or better informed for the new rule to shift behavior.

(6) Finally, I take methodological individualism to be a regulative ideal rather than an ontological doctrine. It directs me to keep looking for individual-level mechanisms, but it does not entitle me to assume in advance that every sound macro-level explanation has one.

Please audit this set. Tell me whether the six commitments form a coherent package; which pairs are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity, and what would have to give to remove each tension; which pairs are only apparently in tension and dissolve under careful reading; and which single revision would buy the most coherence at the least theoretical cost. Treat me as someone who does not already know where the weak points are.
