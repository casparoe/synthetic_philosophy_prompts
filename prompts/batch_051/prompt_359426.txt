I chair the governance working group for a large open-source project. Our steering committee has seven seats: five are filled each year by a community-wide election among active maintainers, and two are held by the lead maintainers of the largest components. A proposal is on the table to replace three of the five elected seats with members drawn by lot from a self-nominated pool of maintainers who have made some minimum level of merged contributions in the past year. Supporters say the elections keep returning the same small set of visible, well-connected maintainers, reward campaigning over judgment, and load all of the governance work onto a few people until they burn out; random selection would break that incumbent advantage, reduce capture by factions and corporate sponsors, and pull in less-visible maintainers with good judgment. Opponents say the committee makes consequential decisions about licensing exceptions, trademark policy, code-of-conduct enforcement, and sponsorship money, and someone must be answerable for those calls; a lottery installs people with no mandate, no demonstrated interest in governance, and no clean way to remove them mid-term if they're bad at it. I find this harder than either camp admits, and I've been asked to circulate a short decision memo before next month's vote. Please draft the memo recommending that we adopt the lottery proposal, reject it, or adopt a modified version. Write it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; put the recommendation in the first item, and make sure the strongest point on the losing side is stated fairly rather than knocked down as a strawman.
