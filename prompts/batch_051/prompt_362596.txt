John Dewey spent much of his career arguing that inquiry is not a private mental act but a transaction with a problematic situation: a felt difficulty provokes the location and definition of a problem, the formation of hypotheses, and their testing in action, with conclusions warranted only provisionally, since every settlement exposes further problems. He further argued that democracy is experimental intelligence organized socially. On his account, a public forms when the indirect consequences of shared activities affect people who were not parties to the original transaction, and democratic government requires that such publics be able to identify themselves, articulate the problems those consequences create, and test proposed remedies through open inquiry and communication, with those affected taking part in forming and judging the ends as well as the means.

Now suppose Dewey could be shown a contemporary social-media company that governs its platform through continuous, low-cost randomized experiments. Every interface change, recommendation policy, and pricing rule is tested on randomly selected groups of users, and any change that moves aggregate metrics—retention, session length, engagement, revenue—is rolled out to everyone. The company describes its method as empirical, data-driven decision making: it learns what works and iterates rapidly.

Would Dewey recognize this as the experimentalism he defended, now applied to the governance of a large social environment? Give a careful reconstruction of what he would welcome, what he would regard as technique masquerading as inquiry, and on what grounds. Be specific about the distinctions he would insist on: between experiments whose ends are fixed in advance by one party and experiments in which the ends are themselves formulated and revised by the affected public; between users acting as passive sources of data and users acting as participants in defining problems, interpreting results, and judging success; between control of variables and control by a public; and between iteration that accumulates private knowledge and iteration that enlarges the shared capacity for further inquiry. What institutional changes would he demand before such a system could honestly call itself democratic experimentalism, and is there a reading of his position on which a well-designed profit-governed platform could satisfy him, or would he see the two as incompatible in principle?
