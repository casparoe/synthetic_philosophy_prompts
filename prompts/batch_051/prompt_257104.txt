Suppose a panel proposes a new diagnostic category — call it vocational dysphoria — for persistent (six months or more) dissatisfaction with one's occupation that causes marked distress or impairment in important areas of functioning, accompanied by rumination about work, reduced enjoyment of non-work activities, and a felt inability to change jobs, when no other condition better explains the presentation. What is your credence that vocational dysphoria, so defined, falls under the correct concept of disease — the one that best captures central cases such as pneumonia or type 1 diabetes — rather than being a normal variation, an intelligible response to a bad environment, or a problem that belongs outside medicine? I am not asking whether it would appear in any actual manual. Give a number, but decompose it into the sub-questions your credence depends on. At minimum, assign a rough probability to each of the following: that a broadly naturalist account of disease, on which disease is value-free biological or psychological dysfunction, is correct; that, conditional on such an account, this condition involves a genuine dysfunction rather than an intelligible response to circumstances; that the condition involves the right kind and degree of harm to count as a disease on a normativist account; and that the term disease has a determinate extension in a borderline case like this at all. Show how these terms combine to yield your credence, and tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. My own credence is around 0.3 and a colleague's is around 0.75; say which sub-question you think our disagreement most likely lives in, and what evidence or argument would move your own number most.
