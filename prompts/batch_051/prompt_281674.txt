I keep seeing people say that training a model with process-based supervision—feedback on each step of its chain of thought, rather than just on whether the final answer is right—teaches it to reason, while outcome-based supervision only teaches it to emit the right-looking answer. I honestly can't tell whether that's a claim about the world or just a way of talking. Suppose I hand you two models with the same architecture and data, one trained with step-level feedback and one with only final-answer feedback, and they end up equally accurate. Design the best test you can of whether the process-trained model is genuinely using its intermediate steps to reach its answers, rather than producing steps that are epiphenomenal—correlated with the correct answer but doing none of the causal work. (I've seen people corrupt a middle step and check whether the final answer changes, but that seems too easy to game.) Tell me what result would convince you each way, and what even a clean result could never settle about whether the model "really reasons."
