You are an expert in philosophy of biology, specializing in biological individuality and the organism concept, and you are designing a diagnostic instrument for a graduate seminar on holobionts and superorganisms. Think carefully before you write the instrument: first work through the logical space of positions and the commitments that actually separate them, then build the instrument from that space rather than from a list of named views.

Write a decision tree that takes a participant from a single fixed starting admission to a named position on the question: under what conditions, if any, is a holobiont — a host together with its persistent, coevolved microbial symbionts — one organism?

The fixed starting admission is this: a host and its persistent symbionts form a real, causally integrated system in which host traits depend partly on symbiont genes; take the pea aphid and its Buchnera as the fixed illustration. Every subsequent branch node must be a question the participant can answer from a short natural-history description of a case, without already knowing any philosophical label. Build the branch nodes around the candidate criteria for organismhood — genetic homogeneity, physiological integration, being a reproductive unit, being a developmental unit, being a unit of selection — but phrase each as a concrete question about one of the cases. Draw the node questions from at least the following cases: a Portuguese man o' war, a pea aphid with Buchnera, a lichen, a human with its gut microbiome, and a coral with its zooxanthellae. Phrase the questions so that they sort participants by what they believe, not by what they would call themselves.

The following positions must appear as leaves, named and briefly characterized:

- The physiological-individual view: an organism is one body with a largely shared genome and a single, integrated physiology; holobionts are ecosystems or communities, not organisms.
- The evolutionary-individual view: an organism is a unit of selection; a holobiont counts when host and symbionts are transmitted and selected together as a package.
- The developmental-individual view: an organism is a developmental system; a holobiont counts when symbionts are vertically transmitted and required for normal development.
- The integrationist view: an organism is a system that passes a threshold of metabolic and functional integration; some holobionts qualify, others do not.
- The pluralist view: "organism" is question-relative; holobionts are organisms for some biological questions and not for others, and there is no fact of the matter beyond that.

Also do the following. First, identify any logically available combination of answers that no live position occupies, and diagnose it in one line as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Second, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot in fact distinguish, rather than papering over the difference. Keep the tree to at most seven branching questions on any path, and make it usable in a twenty-minute opening segment.
