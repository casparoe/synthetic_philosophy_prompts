I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about whether Goodhart-style gaming can be told apart from real improvement. The claim I keep hearing is: once a measure becomes a target, any subsequent improvement is mostly specification gaming, not real change in the thing measured. Take A&E four-hour waits. The target goes in and numbers improve. One camp says real — attention was focused, bottlenecks were cleared. The other says gaming — clock-stopping, corridor care, patients reclassified as admitted on paper. Could data you could plausibly get for one metric seperate these readings? Design the strongest test: what to measure, what result favours real improvement, what favours gaming, and where the two stories become observationally equivalent. Then be honest about what even a clean result would not settle — e.g., whether the metric is still worth keeping once it's become a target.
