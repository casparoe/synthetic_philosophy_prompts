I've got an argument that Hume is committed to denying real necessary connections in nature, not just to saying we can't know about them. Here it is.

(1) For Hume, every idea is a copy of some prior impression.
(2) The only impression we ever get of necessary connection is the feeling of the mind being carried from cause to effect after we've seen a constant conjunction.
(3) So our idea of necessary connection just is the idea of that internal feeling.
(4) When New Humeans say Hume believed in a necessary connection in the objects themselves, they're using the idea of necessary connection to reach beyond that feeling.
(5) An idea can't legitimately be applied beyond the impression it was copied from.
(6) So either this objective necessary connection is something we can't think at all, or Hume can't have believed in it in any straightforward way.

I accept (1) and (2), and the conclusion seems to follow, but something about (3) or (5) feels like a cheat. Can you tell me whether the argument is valid, and if not exactly which step fails? Give the version of (5) you'd need to make the argument work and say whether Hume should accept it. Also say how confident you are in your reading and which point you're least sure of.
