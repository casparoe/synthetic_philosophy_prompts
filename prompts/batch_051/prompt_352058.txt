I was writing a unit test the other day and got the feeling that mocking an object is the same problem as truth in fiction — the Sherlock Holmes problem — just wearing a different hat.

Here's the parallel as I see it. In a test I make a mock user. Inside the harness, "the user's email is test@example.com" is true even though no real user stands behind it; it's true-in-the-test and false or senseless in production. If a method I didn't stub gets called, the framework either throws (no answer), returns a zero value, or — if it's a spy — delegates to the real object. The philosophical menu looks almost identical. "Holmes is a detective" is true-in-the-stories and false or truth-valueless outside them. For the questions the stories dont settle (was Holmes left-handed?), a gappy view says there's no answer, whereas possible-worlds accounts of fiction import actual-world facts wherever the story is silent, which is the spy move. And the mock itself — a real object in the test that has no production referent — looks like the view that Holmes exists as an abstract artifact created by the stories.

I can't tell whether this is a genuine identity of structure or just me pattern-matching on the phrase "true in." If it's real, does anything transfer? Would the stub/mock/spy/fake taxonomy actually carve the theories at the joints, or force them into a shape they don't have? What would have to be true of both sides for the transfer to go through? And where's the first break — maybe already in the spy analogy, since a spy falls back to the same object and there is no actual Holmes to fall back to?

Be blunt if the mapping is confused or if "true in the test" is doing different work than "true in the fiction." Also tell me which parts of your answer are solid and which are speculative. No citations or reading lists needed.
