hey, so i've been going down an epistemology rabbit hole in between deploys and i wrote up where i've landed because i'm supposed to present it to a reading group, and i want a consistency check before i show anyone. full disclosure, i'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so if i'm butchering a term just read around it. roughly my position is this. i think knowledge requires tracking: you only know p if, had p been false, you wouldn't have believed p via the same method you actually used. i like this because it explains why a lucky guess isn't knowledge and it gives a neat answer to the brain in a vat stuff (i think the view is from nozick, not sure). i also think knowledge is closed under known implication: if i know p and i know p implies q, then deducing q from p gives me knowledge of q. that just seems obvious to me, like if i know the deploy failed and i know a failed deploy blocks the release, then i come to know the release is blocked. i think i know plenty of ordinary things, e.g. i know the deploy failed this morning because i watched the pipeline go red, and i know i have hands. i do not think i know that i'm not a brain in a vat being fed a fake version of all this. and i think testimony gives knowledge: when the on-call person tells me an outage is a database deadlock, i know that's what it is, even though she could in principle have been wrong. so do these all hang together? what i want is an audit, not a lecture: go through them and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other and which only seem to, rank the real tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix for each would be, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. and please, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables in your reply, just continuous prose like a normal email.
