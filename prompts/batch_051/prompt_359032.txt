I'm writing a novel whose protagonist is a philosophy PhD student, and I want his central private obsession to be causation. He's come to think the regularity theory is basically right — all we ever observe is constant conjunction, contiguity, and temporal priority; there's no impression of a necessary connection, so necessity is something we project onto the world from our own habits of expectation. It's simple, and it explains a lot. But he also can't shake the feeling that when a hammer cracks a stone, the blow genuinely produces the crack — that the necessity isn't just in his head. And lately he's been reading the New Hume stuff, which half-convinces him Hume himself believed in a real connection we just can't know about. I want the chapter to actually argue this out rather than resolve it cheap. So which should yield: the theory, the intuition, or is there a coherent reading — the New Hume reading, a projectivist story, whatever — on which the regularity theory and the intuition both survive because they're about different things? Give me the strongest version of each move and say what would justify it. And if I'm treating 'necessity' as one thing when it's really two or three different things, tell me that straight instead of playing along.
