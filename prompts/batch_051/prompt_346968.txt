I am working on an essay about causation by omission, and I keep returning to Ursula K. Le Guin's "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" as a test case, so I would like a careful philosophical reading of the story through that lens. As you likely know, Omelas is a city whose prosperity, beauty, and general happiness are said to depend on the abject misery of a single child kept locked away in a cellar; everyone learns of this arrangement upon coming of age, most accept it and remain, and a few, after seeing the child, simply walk away from the city. The metaphysical question I want to press onto this setup is whether the citizens' ongoing failure to free or comfort the child is itself a cause of the child's suffering, or merely a failure to prevent a harm that is caused by something else — the original arrangement, the institutions, the rule that the child must be kept there. The philosophical terrain is disputed: some positions deny that omissions are causes at all, since an absence is not an event and nothing is transmitted from a non-happening; others treat omissions as causes relative to a contrast class or a background of expectations, the way a gardener's not watering the flowers can be said to cause their death.

Read the story against these positions. Does it implicitly treat the citizens' silence and acceptance as sustaining the child's misery, or as inheriting a condition they merely decline to undo? Does the story license the claim that every citizen who stays is causally implicated in the harm, and does it distinguish their position from that of whoever locked the child there in the first place? What, philosophically, do the ones who walk away contribute — is their leaving an action or itself another omission, and does the story care about the difference? Finally, where does the story resist being reduced to a tidy illustration of any causal theory, and what does its interest in complicity, knowledge, and accepted dependence add to the philosophical debate that a bare example of an omission would not? I would value a reading that is faithful in both directions: using the philosophical debates to sharpen what the story dramatizes, while letting the story push back where the theories become too clean.
