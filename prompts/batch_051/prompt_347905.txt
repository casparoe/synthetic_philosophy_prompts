In my philosophy of mind reading group we've spent a year stuck on gradual-replacement uploading: replace neurons one at a time with functionally identical prosthetics until the whole brain is synthetic. We all agree on the procedure and the end state, yet we keep splitting three ways. One camp says you clearly survive, because no single step crosses a threshold where you stop existing. Another says you clearly die, just at no particular moment, and what's left is a duplicate. A third says the question is empty, because 'same person' isn't the sort of thing that needs a determinate answer across a slow replacement. My working theory is that the real disagreement isn't about the thought experiment at all: it's about whether 'same person' tracks biological continuity, psychological continuity, or something more like practical/narrative identity, and maybe about whether the word 'death' is doing disguised moral work. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a hidden formal point—like whether the survival relation must be transitive—doing the actual work? And given your diagnosis, what could move this: a distinction, a different case, a result about transitivity, a change of question, or should we expect no convergence?
