ok here's one that keeps bugging me. i find the cantorian apparatus for the infinite genuinely compelling as theory: you define 'just as many' by one-to-one correspondence and the whole murky question of how to compare infinite collections gets a crisp, non-arbitrary answer. from there its a short step to the claim that an infinite set can be paired with a proper subset of itself — the naturals with the evens, the naturals with the squares, whatever — and hilbert's hotel is just the vivid version. when i actually run the hotel i can't find a contradiction: every guest gets a room, nobody is left out, the instructions are finite and clear. as mathematics it feels basically unimpeachable.

but the intuition refuses to shut up. a proper part of a collection should be smaller than the collection. the even numbers are every other natural number, so how can there be exactly as many evens as naturals? the hotel is full — full means every room occupied — so how does moving everyone down one open up a room when nobody has checked out? i get that the move works only because there is no last room, so my finite mental picture of 'the person at the end walks off the map' has no analogue. i get it. and yet it still feels like the theory has quietly changed what 'full' and 'more' mean into technical senses that no longer answer the question i actually asked. i know this is basically the old galileo puzzle with the square numbers, and part of me suspects the right lesson is that finite notions of greater and less dont automatically transfer to the infinite.

so walk me through the strongest version of each side. on one side is a tidy, unifying theory with genuine mathematical work behind it; on the other is the principle that the whole is always bigger than a proper part of it, which i can't make myself drop even while i watch the theory violate it. then tell me, concretely, what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes — am i just projecting my experience with finite piles onto the infinite, and should i debunk it as a scope-of-experience artifact? what would it take for the theory to go instead — would i have to go finitist, deny actual infinities, and what would that cost me in analysis or number theory or anything that uses limits? and is there a stable reading where both survive, for instance by separating two notions of size (cardinality versus something like numerosity, or a pre-formal relation of 'having more'), or by diagnosing an equivocation on what 'full' means for a collection with no last member? i don't want a cheerleader for one side; i want a negotiated verdict with the costs named.
