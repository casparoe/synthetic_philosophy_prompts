Counterfactual: a country of about 10 million with a generous welfare state passes a Brute Luck Compensation Act, designed to finally implement the option luck / brute luck distinction. Any loss you suffer is fully compensated, tax-funded and with no deductible, if a tribunal decides it came from unchosen circumstances — a congenital illness, a drunk driver, the family you were born into. If the tribunal decides it came from a chosen gamble — starting a restaurant, downhill skiing, moving to a floodplain because the rent is cheap — you get nothing. The line is drawn case by case, with appeals to a Luck Court. Predict, with reasoning, how this plays out over 25 years. How quickly do people start re-describing choices as unchosen, and where do borderline cases land (smoking, dangerous jobs, having children)? What happens to risk-taking and entrepreneurship, and does the scheme stay a strict desert scheme or quietly turn into universal insurance? Be explicit about which predictions are robust and which hinge on assumptions about how people game the system.
