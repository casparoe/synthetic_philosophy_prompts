I teach philosophy of mind and comparative cognition, and I have been invited to submit a one-page pitch for a workshop on parsimony in the study of other minds. I want the pitch built around a question I think has not been posed sharply enough: when we choose between a lower and a higher psychological explanation of an animal's behaviour, are we applying a neutral epistemic rule, or are we smuggling in a substantive ranking of minds? Morgan's canon — the familiar rule, attributed to C. Lloyd Morgan, that we should not explain an animal's behaviour in terms of a higher psychological faculty if it can be adequately explained by one lower on the scale — is usually treated either as sound methodological caution against anthropomorphism or as a nineteenth-century prejudice that biases the field against animal minds. I want the proposal to argue that both readings are too coarse, because the canon is really a family of distinct principles: parsimony about mechanisms, conservatism about unobservable mental states, and a bet about the relative costs of over- and under-attributing cognition — and these principles come apart when pressed.

Please write the proposal. It should state the question precisely, explain what the standard readings leave open, justify a philosophical reconstruction of the canon rather than the obvious alternatives (a historical study of Morgan's texts, or an empirical survey of how researchers actually invoke the rule), and say what a successful outcome would look like. I imagine a taxonomy of the distinct principles and a defensible recommendation about which, if any, should govern comparative cognition; a fair account of failure would be that the principles cannot be cleanly separated, or that the recommendation collapses back into one of the standard readings. Please also give an honest sense of scope for a one-year project, and, since the funder is not a philosophy shop, a short answer to why this matters for people actually designing and interpreting animal-cognition experiments. No bibliography is needed; I will fill in citations where the funder expects them.
