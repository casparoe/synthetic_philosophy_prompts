Suppose a country classifies personal data as property in which the data subject retains a non-waivable residual interest: any firm that uses data about a person to earn revenue must pay that person a small fixed percentage of the revenue attributable to their data, with payments administered by a public clearinghouse. Attribution is taken from the firm's own records and checked by random audit. Amounts below a minimum threshold are pooled into a civic data fund rather than paid out. The royalty cannot be sold for cash, but firms may offer capped, standardized non-monetary benefits in exchange for a partial waiver. Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral verdict: what would it incentivize among platforms, data brokers, advertisers, and individual users? How would each group game it, what second-order effects would it have on what data is collected and on the price and quality of ad-supported services, and where would it break down first? Then assess whether the main failure modes could be repaired without abandoning the data-as-property frame. Finally, state how confident you are in your analysis and identify the single point you are least sure of.
