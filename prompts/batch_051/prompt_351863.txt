After a long argument with a friend about whether the handicap principle applies to human status displays, I pulled out this passage from Thorstein Veblen’s *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899), from the opening of chapter one:

> The institution of a leisure class is found in its best development at the higher stages of the barbarian culture; as, for instance, in feudal Europe or feudal Japan. In such communities the distinction between classes is very rigorously observed; and the feature of most striking economic significance in these class differences is the distinction maintained between the employments proper to the several classes. The upper classes are by custom exempt or excluded from industrial occupations, and are reserved for certain employments to which a degree of honour attaches. Chief among the honourable employments in any feudal community is warfare; and priestly service is commonly second to warfare. If the barbarian community is not notably warlike, the priestly office may take the precedence, with that of the warrior second. But the rule holds with but slight exceptions that, whether warriors or priests, the upper classes are exempt from industrial employments, and this exemption is the economic expression of their superior rank.

My friend read it and said it was exactly not a costly signal: Veblen is describing a social rule that forbids industrial work to the upper classes, not a voluntary handicap they take on to advertise quality. But I cannot see how the distinction holds, because the exemption itself looks like the costly display—the upper classes are the ones who can afford to be unproductive, and the fact that their rank is expressed economically by that exemption seems to treat the unproductiveness as a signal of the underlying superiority. I would like help sorting out what Veblen is actually claiming here. Is he saying that exemption from productive work merely manifests an already accepted rank, or is he saying that the exemption is part of what produces and stabilizes that rank? And if the second, is the difference from the handicap-principle logic substantive, or only a difference of vocabulary?
