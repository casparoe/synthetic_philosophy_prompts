A friend and I recently argued late into the night about whether the reasons one has for loving a particular person are discovered in that person's qualities or created by the love itself. I was defending what I'll call the bestowal view: love confers a distinctive kind of value on the beloved, and the reasons love gives are made, not found. He was defending what I'll call the appraisal view: love is a response to a value the beloved already has, and the beloved's qualities ground our reasons for loving. We kept talking past each other, largely because we could not agree on what would count as evidence either way.

I would like you to draft the document of the adversarial collaboration we might have produced. The document should contain:

1. A list of substantive claims both camps can sign. These should go beyond platitudes and should include claims about ordinary cases: love at first sight, love that survives the loss of the qualities that initially attracted the lover, love for difficult people, love that ends, and love that never begins despite the presence of apparently lovable qualities. The section should also say what each view is not committed to, so that neither side is attacking a caricature.

2. A precise statement of the residual disagreement, worded so that an appraisal theorist would accept the appraisal-side formulation, a bestowal theorist would accept the bestowal-side formulation, and both would accept a neutral statement of the contrast between them.

3. Two or three cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one camp to revise its view. For each crux, give the concrete case that both camps agree bears on it, and state in advance what each camp predicts about that case.

4. An honest note on how much of the disagreement is verbal rather than substantive, and which parts of it no feasible case or observation could settle.

In choosing the cruxes, consider at least these three questions: whether a shared history or relationship can itself be a reason for love; whether the beloved's value can be specified independently of the lover's perspective; and whether love's reasons are conditional on the beloved's retaining whatever qualities the lover first loved.

Do not write a bibliography or attribute the positions to named philosophers. Develop the document from the positions as stated.
