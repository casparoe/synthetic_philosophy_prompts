I'm putting together a unit on anthropic reasoning, and I want a clean toy case for the Boltzmann brain worry. Here's what I want you to build. Give me a finite, urn-style model with two kinds of beings: ordinary people, who each persist through many conscious moments, and Boltzmann brains, who each persist through exactly one. Treat every conscious moment in the model as subjectively just like the one you're having now, so the only differences the two sampling rules can see are structural. Define two rules. PERSON: reason as if you're a random draw from all people, each person equally likely. MOMENT: reason as if you're a random draw from all conscious moment-slices, each slice equally likely. I want the counts set so that PERSON says your almost certainly a Boltzmann brain, while MOMENT says you're almost certainly an ordinary person. Every number has to be finite and explicit, and nothing can do sneaky work through a vague definition of an observer.

Then do the check: state the counts, compute the verdict each rule gives, and show the reversal isn't just noted but falls out of the arithmetic. After that, tell me which feature of the population structure is doing the work, what happens if you remove it, and how a defender of PERSON would push back and try to stop the case from counting. The point of the exercise is to see whether the Boltzmann brain conclusion is robust to changing the unit you sample, so stipulating the verdict or feeding the two rules different evidence would be cheating.
