Please argue that epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness can perfectly well explain why we go around judging, talking, and writing about our own qualia, and that the so-called paradox of phenomenal judgment is therefore not a real refutation. By epiphenomenalism I mean the view that qualia — the felt, what-it's-likeness of experience — are caused by physical goings-on, but never themselves cause anything at all: not a word, not a wince, not a belief. The standard complaint is this. If my qualia are causally inert, then my judgment that I'm in pain isn't caused by my pain, and my judgment that pain feels a certain way isn't caused by the feel of it. The physics has to do all the causal work by itself, whilst the qualia just sit there. So the fact that the physics reliably produces judgments whose content matches the qualia I actually have looks like a staggering coincidence, and worse, it's unclear how a judgment even manages to be about epiphenomenal qualia in the first place. Please argue that this complaint fails. A good answer will spell out a 'tracking' or 'information-carrying' account of how a physical state can be about an epiphenomenal property, and then defend that account against the obvious comeback that mere covariation isn't representation: a thermometer tracks temperature without believing anything.
