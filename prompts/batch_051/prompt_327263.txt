In a seminar on free will someone kept deflecting every move with a line he called the pragmatic maxim: if two positions don't differ in any practical consequences we could ever observe or act on, then the dispute between them is empty. He used it to wave away both the hard determinist and the libertarian, and to declare the whole compatibilism debate dissolved. I have a vauge sense this flattens something Peirce actually meant into a much cruder rule, but I can't articulate the alternatives. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that one-liner could amount to — as a claim about meaning, as a claim about truth, as a claim about how inquiry should fix belief — and say for each whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or false? Which reading did my seminar-mate's move actually need, and does the best version of that reading get him his conclusion?
