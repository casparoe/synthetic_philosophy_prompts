I was an entomologist, so I spent my working life with dichotomous keys, and now that I'm retired and getting into philosophy I keep wanting the same tool for arguments. I'm deep into the vagueness debate at the moment, and I'd like a dichotomous key that starts from the shared starting point that vague terms have borderline cases — cases where you know all the ordinary non-linguistic facts and still dont know what to say — and keys out the main positions, so I can sort my reading group by what they actually say about cases. The leaves have to include at least epistemicism and supervaluationism, plus any others the key genuinely seperates (a degrees view, a nihilist option, contextualism — your call), but dont pad it: aim for five or six leaves and no more than eight couplets. Each couplet should be a question about a concrete case that someone can answer without already knowing the labels, so no couplet may just ask 'are you an epistemicist?' or 'do you accept bivalence?'. I'd also like at least one case to use a scientific predicate (a wetland, a seedling, that sort of thing) rather than the usual heap and bald. Then finish with a short note saying which pairs of leaves your key cant pull apart, and whether any combination of answers has no occupant — and if a combination is empty, whether that's because the position is incoherent, just unattractive, or simply hasn't been defended.
