Consider the Ring of Gyges as Glaucon presents it in Republic II. In the original story, the ring makes its wearer invisible at will, so unjust actions carry no risk of detection; Glaucon's challenge is that, with discovery removed, the just person and the unjust person would behave identically, and that justice is therefore valued only for its consequences. Now alter one parameter of the case: invisibility is imperfect and probabilistic. Suppose each unjust act is observed with a known, independent probability p—the ring occasionally "flickers," and this is common knowledge. For concreteness, let a particular unjust act yield a private benefit of 100 units if unobserved and a penalty of −1,000 units if observed, and take the agent to be risk-neutral.

Work through the case under two positions. First, psychological egoism, understood as the descriptive claim that agents in fact always act so as to maximize their own expected self-interest. Second, ethical egoism, understood as the normative claim that agents ought so to act. Compute the critical value of p at which each position ceases to predict (or sanction) the unjust act; for the purposes of the calculation, treat both positions as applying the same decision-theoretic criterion, so that any difference between them must show up elsewhere than in the arithmetic. Say whether the two positions still make distinct behavioral predictions in this modified case, or whether they collapse into one another; if they collapse, identify what remaining resource, if any, still distinguishes them.

Finally, assess whether the modified ring still poses Glaucon's challenge. Since any nonzero p permits just behavior to be explained as prudence rather than the intrinsic value of justice, does the variation retain the power the original story was meant to have—namely, to isolate justice-for-its-own-sake by eliminating the prudential motive? Which further changes to the case—the size of the gain, the severity of the penalty, the agent's attitude to risk, or uncertainty about p itself—would be needed to recover a version that can actually distinguish a person who is just for its own sake from a prudent egoist?
