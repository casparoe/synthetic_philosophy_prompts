Okay so I'm trying to pin down what "understanding a proof" actually amounts to, as opposed to just being fluent at the moves. I want a working definition of "S understands proof P," and I want you to run it against these cases, then revise it if it gives dumb verdicts. Don't just tell me the word is vague.

(a) A student can write out Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes word for word, but when you ask why you add one to the product of the primes, she shrugs and says that's just the move that makes the contradiction show up.

(b) A student can't reproduce the proof cleanly, but she can explain the actual idea: from any finite list of primes you can build a number whose prime factors can't all be on the list. She only fumbles when she tries to turn that thought into tidy notation.

(c) A student goes through an induction proof line by line and confirms that each step follows from the previous one. Asked whether she now sees why the theorem is true, she says no, she just sees that the proof is valid.

(d) A different student has a diagram that makes it obvious to him why a statement is true, and he can explain the diagram well, but he can't turn it into a rigorous proof and doesn't see what's missing when you push him.

(e) A person can immediately rederive the standard proof that √2 is irrational after five years, and can explain exactly where the same argument would break down if you tried it on √4, but she has to struggle to write the proof out as a fully formal lemma with all the quantifiers and assumptions spelled out. Does she understand the proof?

(f) An AI can generate flawless proofs, answer standard questions about each step, and adjust a proof when you change the assumptions slightly. Should your definition count it as understanding? If yes, say why you're okay with that. If no, name the specific ingredient you think is missing.

(g) A person reads a proof, can paraphrase each inference, but can't give a one-sentence summary of the overall strategy and can't get started reconstructing it a week later. Should we say they understood it at the time?

I suspect you'll need to separate at least three strands: checking validity, grasping the explanatory idea, and having a mental model that supports reconstruction and transfer. If you think they collapse into one thing, defend that; if you keep them apart, say which one deserves the word "understanding."
