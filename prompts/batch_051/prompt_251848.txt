Right, I'm giving a twenty minute talk to my local science-and-ideas meetup next month and I need help preparing for the questions after. It's a pub group, proper mixed, no academics. My thesis is that whilst a science is going through a proper revolution, the old Popperian idea that you just test for falsifications and that sorts it can't actually decide between the two sides, because what counts as a clean test or a good reason is part of what's being fought over. But I also want to say it doesn't follow that the change is irrational: what changes is what 'rational' has to mean, more like exercising judgement under shared but vague values than following a fixed set of rules. My rough argument is (1) in a revolution the two sides aren't just disagreeing about facts, they're disagreeing about which facts matter and how to weigh them; (2) so the standards you'd use to referee the fight are themselves part of the fight — you can't seperate standards from theories cleanly; (3) but it's still not a free-for-all, because there are shared values like accuracy, consistency, simplicity and fruitfulness, they're just vague and can pull in opposite directions; (4) so switching paradigms can be rational without being forced by the rules. The audience includes Graham, a retired electrical engineer who's read a fair bit of Popper and basically thinks a claim is scientific only if you can say what would falsify it; Sharon, a former nurse who watched her ward change its whole approach to a condition over about ten years and now reckons most of that was fashion and a couple of strong personalities rather than neat falsification; and a handful of undecideds who'll get fed up if I sound like I'm saying science is just politics. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way Graham, Sharon and the undecideds would actually phrase them, with how serious each one is, what I should concede, what I should hold my ground on, and where my own position is weakest so I defend that rather than hoping they don't notice. And include the question I'm quietly praying nobody asks.
