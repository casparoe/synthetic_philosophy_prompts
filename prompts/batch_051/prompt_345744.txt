Heres a claim I want a number on: there is a non-circular justification for modus ponens — or for the basic rules of deductive inference generally — where a justification counts as circular if it draws any inference of the very kind whose entitlement is in question. By "justification" I mean something that would show a rule-following agent is rationally entitled to draw that inference, not just that the rule is truth-preserving or useful. (Truth-preservation is cheating if you had to use the rule to prove it.)

Give me your actual credence, 0 to 1, and the decomposition. How much of your credence rides on each of these?

- Whether inference rules need the same kind of justification as premises, or whether the Tortoise's demand is just a category mistake. I suspect this is doing most of the work for people who answer high.
- Whether a "rule-circular" justification (you use modus ponens in the course of arguing that modus ponens is truth-preserving) is vicious or harmless. I want the weight, not just the label.
- Whether the meanings of "if" and "then" make the rule constitutive of the conditional, so that outside justification is impossible because the rule is already assumed in having the concepts.
- Whether there is a determinate fact about what follows from what, independent of any practice of inferring, that could make the rule correct or incorrect.
