My friend in an MA program was explaining why she thinks simplicity is overrated in ethical theory, and her summary of the debate went like this: "Occam's razor makes sense in science because we assume the world is simple and parsimony tracks truth, but moral reality isn't a physical system and could be highly complex, so there's no reason to prefer one master principle like maximize utility or the categorical imperative over a pluralist deontology with a half dozen independent duties. If ethics has data at all, it's our considered moral intuitions, and a pluralist theory that fits more of them is ceteris paribus better than a simpler theory that has to torture them into shape. So preferring parsimony in ethics is really just an aesthetic prejudice, a category mistake imported from physics." I agree moral reality probably isn't physical, but I suspect she's conflating a few things. Is this a fair statement of the case against simplicity as a theoretical virtue in ethics? Please audit it claim by claim: what would a careful defender of parsimony accept, what would they say is overstated or a subtle misrepresentation, and whether the conclusion follows from the accurate parts.
