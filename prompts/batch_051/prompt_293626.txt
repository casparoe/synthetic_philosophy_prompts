I've got a belief-rule I follow and I'm starting to wonder if it's too convenient. Whenever someone tells me a story about an amazing coincidence -- a friend thought about an old classmate and then the classmate called an hour later, my uncle dreamed about a car crash the night before he got rear-ended, a horoscope was weirdly specific -- I file it under noise and don't update at all. My grounds for this is the birthday paradox: in a room of 23 people it's already more likely than not that two share a birthday, so things that feel unlikely on the surface turn out to be near-guarantees once you count all the chances for them to happen. I take that as a general lesson that personal "what are the odds" stories are worthless evidence. I've had this rule for years and its made me feel like the rational one in the room. But recently my wife has pointed out that I use it as a blanket excuse: she'll tell me something genuinely weird, like she was reaching for her phone to text her sister and the phone lit up with a call from that exact sister while she was holding it, and I'll say "birthday paradox" and move on. She says I'm using a party trick as a shield. So I want to know whether my rule is actually defensible or just a bias with a math-sounding slogan. Is the birthday paradox even the right analogy for a one-off personal story, where it's a specific event rather than any two people matching something? If some coincidence stories should move me even a little, what has to be true of them -- and can I keep a fixed-up version of the rule that still lets me ignore horoscopes but doesn't just automatically wave away everything my wife brings me?
