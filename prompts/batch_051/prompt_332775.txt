I keep noticing a pattern and I want to know if it has a name, or whether its actually two different things I'm conflating. The first kind of case: a country treats some custom as ancient national heritage—the tartans-and-kilts image of Scotland, a 'traditional' dish that turns out to have been invented for a world fair, a solemn coronation ritual whose choreography was written out by a committee around 1902—and even when people are shown the dates, they still talk about it as proof of how old the nation is. The second kind of case: millions of people who will never meet nonetheless feel like one people, and this seems to ride on mass newspapers, maps with a border drawn around 'us,' novels written in a standardized language, an anthem everyone learns in school. So the question isn't just 'what are these called,' it's how to carve the space: is a genuinely old custom that got reworked in the 1800s the same kind of case as one invented from scratch? Does the second phenomenon need the shared material to be new, or can it be genuinely old?
