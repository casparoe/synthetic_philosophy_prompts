Ok so in my decision theory class we did Death in Damascus and its been bugging me. The case: you're in Damascus, and Death (a perfect predictor) says he'll meet you tomorrow in whichever city he predicted you'd be in — Damascus or Aleppo — and if you're in that city, you die. I've always found causal decision theory compelling: only causal consequences matter, so two-box in Newcomb and move on. Applied here, your choice can't causally affect where Death already is, so you should estimate where he is and go to the other city. But the moment you settle on Aleppo, that's evidence he predicted Aleppo, so Damascus looks better, and it loops. Evidential decision theory avoids the loop but only by saying both acts are equally doomed, which feels right and useless at the same time. I'm convinced rational advice shouldn't flip-flop like that, but I'm not ready to give up causal decision theory. I don't want a verdict handed to me. I want to know what it would take for the causal-only intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to get amended (ratifiability? something else?), and whether there's a reading where both survive.
