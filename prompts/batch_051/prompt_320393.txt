Here's a puzzle I want you to run Nozick's tracking theory on. Just so we're using the same machinery: Nozick says a subject S knows p when p is true, S believes it, and S's belief tracks it — if p had been false S wouldn't have believed p (holding the method fixed), and if p had been true S would have believed it. The famous upshot is that knowledge isn't closed under known implication. The usual exhibit is the zoo case: someone looking at a zebra in a zoo knows the animal is a zebra, but doesn't know it's not a cleverly disguised mule, because if it were a disguised mule they'd still think it was a zebra.

Now vary it. Suppose the zoo installs a certification system: every morning each animal in the zebra enclosure gets a DNA check, and only if every animal is confirmed a zebra does a green light on the enclosure switch on. Dana visits, understands the system, sees the green light, and on that basis forms the belief "that animal is not a mule." The animal actually is a zebra.

Walk me through what the tracking view says about Dana. Does it say she now knows the animal is not a mule, even though the original visitor didn't? Does the view still deny her an obvious-sounding implication of "that's a zebra," and if so which one? Then run the skeptical follow-up. Consider the further claim: "that animal is not a mule that was injected with zebra DNA earlier, so the scanner reads zebra and the green light stays on." Is Dana's belief in that further claim sensitive? If it isn't, does this variation fix closure, or does it just push the closure failure up one level? Finally, say where your verdict is most fragile — I'd guess it's the bit about what counts as her method and which counterfactual worlds are nearest — and tell me what would have to be true instead for the verdict to flip.
