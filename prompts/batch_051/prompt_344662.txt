Lately I've been going back and forth on evidential decision theory, and the thing I can't shake is Death in Damascus. On paper I'm basically sold on EDT. Newcomb makes one-boxing feel almost forced, and I like that it gives me a single clean rule: do whatever you'd be glad to learn you'd done — more precisely, maximise expected utility with the probabilities you'd have conditional on the act. That rule snaps a lot of cases into place that causal decision theory handles rather akwardly.

But here's where I get stuck. As I understand the story, I'm in Damascus and Death tells me in a dream that he'll meet me tomorrow. He's an almost infallible predictor of what I'll do, and he goes to whichever city he predicted I'd be in. I can either stay in Damascus or travel to Aleppo. If he predicted I'd stay, then staying means death and fleeing means life; if he predicted I'd flee, then fleeing means death and staying means life. So there's a plain fact about which city is the death trap, and exactly one of my options makes the prediction false. But when I run EDT on it, conditional on me staying, the probability that he predicted staying is enormous, so staying is terrible news; conditional on me fleeing, the probability that he predicted fleeing is enormous, so fleeing is also terrible news. EDT seems to say I should be indifferent — both acts come out as "you learn you're doomed." And that doesn't sit right, because from outside the choice one of staying or fleeing is the saving act. I even catch myself wanting to flip a coin to break the predictor's grip, but if he can predict the coin too, I'm back where I started.

I don't want to be told that EDT just wins or just dies. I want to know: if I keep the intuition, what exactly has to give in EDT — the rule itself, a way of applying it, or something about how I'm modelling the predictor? And if I keep EDT, what exactly has to give in the intuition — is "one of the acts makes the prediction false" a mistake, and if so what kind? Or is there a reading on which EDT and the intuition are both fine? Please write the reply in British English.
