I am an undergraduate in a philosophy of science seminar on laws of nature, and I am writing a short position paper in defense of a Humean account. Before I submit it, I would like a consistency check on the package I have ended up defending. Here are the commitments I currently hold.

(1) Laws supervene on the mosaic: there is no difference in the laws without a difference in the local, non-modal particular facts. More specifically, I take the laws to be the axioms or theorems of the best systematization of the mosaic, where best is fixed solely by the familiar trade-off among simplicity, strength, and fit.

(2) Laws explain their instances. When I say that this particular piece of salt dissolved because salt dissolves, I mean that as a genuine explanation, not as a restatement of what happened or a circular redescription.

(3) Sparse worlds have determinate, non-arbitrary lawhood facts. If the entire history of a world consisted of a single particle moving uniformly forever, I take it that the law of inertia would hold in that world. If the entire history were empty, I take it that the world would have no laws. I treat both verdicts as forced by the view, not as convenient stipulations.

(4) I will not fall back on primitive modality. There are no governing relations, no nomic necessity as a further ingredient, no dispositional essences, and I will not complicate the notion of best just to make my preferred verdicts come out right.

I am asking for an audit rather than a general discussion. Are these four commitments jointly tenable? Please begin with a one-sentence bottom line, then rank the tensions from most to least severe, including tensions I may not have noticed. For each pair that is merely apparently in tension, say why it dissolves; for each genuine tension, say exactly which commitment would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost; and close by identifying the single revision that would do the most to make the package coherent. Keep the entire response under 1,000 words, and write in American English.
