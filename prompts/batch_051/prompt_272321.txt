I run a small online journal that publishes critiques of propaganda, coordinated disinformation campaigns, and hyperpartisan media. I have a strict editorial rule: we do not reproduce the propaganda itself. We do not embed the videos, quote the slogans, display the memes, or link to the primary documents, because even a refutation gives the material another circulation, launders it into contexts where it looks discussable, and hands the propagandists the attention they wanted. Over time this has become more than an editorial rule; it has become a personal epistemic policy. I mostly refuse to read or watch primary propaganda myself, working instead from transcripts and descriptions prepared by researchers I trust. My reason is that the material is engineered to move an audience before it argues with one, and I do not see why I should assume I am immune. I am not protecting my readers' attention from the material so much as protecting my own judgment from it.

A younger editor on the staff has started pushing back. She says the rule makes our criticism unaccountable: readers cannot check whether our paraphrase is fair, and we ourselves are critiquing a mediated description rather than the thing. She argues that the entire point of critical analysis is to expose how the text does what it does, and you cannot expose what you refuse to display. I find her position plausible, but it also sounds like a recipe for a publication that endlessly recirculates the very content it claims to oppose, while congratulating itself for doing so critically. I want to know whether my rule is defensible, not merely as a reputation-management strategy but as an epistemic and moral policy. Is there a principled distinction between the reproduction that criticizes and the reproduction that amplifies? When is critique that never displays its object a failure of intellectual honesty rather than a refusal to be exploited, and is my personal habit of avoiding primary sources a reasonable division of epistemic labor or a way of shielding my beliefs from the strongest version of the view I claim to oppose?
