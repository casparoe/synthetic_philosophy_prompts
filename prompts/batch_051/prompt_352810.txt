We just read William James's The Varieties of Religious Experience in my philosophy of religion course, and I keep coming back to his chapter on mysticism. He names four marks of mystical states: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, and passivity. The one I'm stuck on is noetic quality. As I understand James, he means that during the experience the person feels they have been given access to a deep truth or knowledge, not merely an emotion, even though they usually cannot state what that truth is. James treats this as one reason mystical experiences carry authority for the person who has them and why outsiders should not just dismiss them as mere feeling.

Here's my worry. Since James wrote in 1902, we have learned a lot about states that feel like revelation but seem to have no actual content. I'm thinking of psychedelic experiences where someone is convinced they have grasped the secret of the universe and later says it was inexpressible, and also about temporal lobe seizures that produce a sudden feeling of significance, sometimes interpreted as a message from God, without any proposition attached. If the feeling of insight can be induced chemically or electrically and doesnt seem to track any real insight, does that undercut James's noetic-quality argument for treating mystical states as evidence for anything beyond the subject's own brain? Or can James say he never claimed the noetic quality proves the content, only that it marks a distinct kind of experience? I want to know which premise the new development attacks, and whether the argument survives in some repaired form.
