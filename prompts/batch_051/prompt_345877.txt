I'm not an academic, just someone who keeps coming back to this because my employer auto-enrolled me last year. When an airlines booking site pre-ticks the box for travel insurance or seat selection, so you end up paying extra unless you un-tick it, everyone calls it a dark pattern, and in a lot of places it's flat out against the rules. But when the government made my employer put me into the workplace pension by default, taking money out of my wages unless I filled in a form to opt out, nobody said it was manipulative — people told me it was good policy and I'd be glad of it later. Its the same move either way: whoever sets the default knows most people won't change it, so the default does the choosing for us. The easy escape is that the pension is for my own good and the airline is for theirs. But then what do I do with a company that auto-enrols its staff into a decent pension unless they opt out? Thats the company acting for my own good too, and a lot of people would still feel queasy because the company picked the fund and the worker never really chose. Flip it round, and a council lottery that signs you up for a £5-a-month ticket unless you opt out would be called a scam even if every penny went to libraries. I want the strongest principle you can find that draws the line in the right place across all four cases, or an honest verdict that there isn't one and we are just going on who we trust and whether we like the outcome. Write the whole answer in British English, please.
