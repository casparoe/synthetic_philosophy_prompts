I run a small robotics team that builds manipulation systems for warehouse picking. We are stuck on a task that ought to be easy: a human can pick up an unfamiliar, oddly shaped object and reorient it in the hand without ever computing a grasp point, while our best system either needs an explicit model of the object or fails the moment the object deviates from its training distribution. A colleague with a philosophy background suggested I look at Merleau-Ponty on the lived body and motor intentionality, and I was surprised to find him describing, from a completely different starting point, exactly the capacity our robots lack. As I understand him, the core claims are roughly these: that the body has a pre-reflective, practical sense of its own posture and possibilities — a body schema — that is not an explicit representation in the head; that the world shows up first as a field of possible actions rather than as neutral, objective geometry, so that a handle is already something graspable and a gap already something passable before any planning begins; that skilled movement is a modulation of this practical sense rather than the execution of a plan over a representation of space; and that explicit, objective representations of space and objects are derivative achievements that presuppose this more basic motor intentionality. I may be getting some of this wrong, and I want you to be the referee.

I would like two things, in this order. First, a faithful translation of these claims into my working vocabulary — policies, value functions, world models, forward and inverse models, state estimation, affordances, end-to-end learning, priors, sim-to-real transfer, planning under uncertainty — which I am fluent in, though I have no philosophical training. For example: is the body schema best understood as a learned prior over body configuration, as a forward model, or as something with no clean counterpart? Is motor intentionality a policy, and if so, a policy over what state and action space? Is the way an object solicits a grip closer to an affordance, or is that already a distortion? Second, a loss report: tell me which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all, and for each, whether the failure is a defect of the engineering vocabulary — something it is missing — or a sign that the philosophical claim is less precise than it looks. Be especially honest about places where the translation would smuggle in assumptions my field takes for granted, such as that intentionality just is computation over representations, or that a body is just a system with a state. Please write the answer in British English.
