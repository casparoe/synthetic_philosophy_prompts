In our metaphysics of propositions seminar we have been working through Bradley's regress as it arises for the unity of the proposition. The standard presentation is roughly this: if a proposition is a complex in which an n-place relation universal binds its terms together, then the binding relation itself must bear some further connection to each term, and that further connection must in turn be connected to what it relates, and so on. I am trying to think through a variation in which the underlying tie is not represented as a universal constituent at all, but as a generating rule: at each stage, the same rule takes whatever entities are available at that stage and yields a new complex, without itself appearing among the entities to which the rule is next applied. Does this actually stop the regress, or does it merely relocate it from the level of ontology to the level of rules? In the original puzzle the regress is often taken to show that relations cannot do the work of unifying a content, but if the rule is not a constituent, the original argument no longer directly applies. How should defenders of the original argument respond, and what does this variant reveal about which assumption the original puzzle really depends on?
