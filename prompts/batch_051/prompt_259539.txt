Since Quinn's self-torturer shows that rational preferences can be intransitive — each step of the dial is worth taking for the money, but the final state isn't perferable to the starting point — why do decision theorists still keep transitivity as a non-negotiable axiom of rational choice? The setup is: a guy is paid to turn up an electric shock by imperceptible increments, each one too small to notice against the last, but the cumulative change is awful. He takes every step, and ends up in a state he'd have paid to avoid if it were offered all at once. If that's a clean counterexample, what's left for transitivity to be an axiom of?
