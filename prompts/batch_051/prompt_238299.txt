I've been trying to reconstruct an argument a lecturer gave against the coherence of the Chalcedonian two-natures doctrine, and I can't locate the failure. I half-suspect step (3), but I don't see how adding "qua divine" or "qua human" blocks a contradiction if there is still just one person. Here is the argument:

(1) One and the same subject, Christ, is fully divine and fully human.
(2) To be fully divine is to have every essential divine property, including omniscience; to be fully human is to have every essential human property, including lack of omniscience.
(3) By the communication of idioms, whatever belongs to either nature belongs to the person.
(4) So Christ is both omniscient and not omniscient.
(5) Nothing can be both; therefore the two-natures doctrine is incoherent.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what is the most defensible revision of it? If adding "qua" clauses is the fix, explain whether they genuinely avoid the contradiction or merely relabel it.
