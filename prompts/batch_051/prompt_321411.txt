I chair the Neurotechnology Ethics and Review Board at a research institute that has been asked to approve a pilot program in whole-brain emulation. The investigators propose to recruit up to three terminally ill volunteers; each would undergo non-destructive high-resolution structural and functional scanning over several sessions, and after natural death the donated brain would be preserved and scanned with electron microscopy to complete a software reconstruction. The emulation would then be activated on local computing infrastructure. The volunteers’ advance directives would specify whether the emulation is to be treated as the same individual for the purposes of continued consent, withdrawal from the study, and communication with family.

The central dispute on the board is not the imaging technology but the metaphysics and ethics of identity. One faction insists that the emulation is at most a high-fidelity behavioral and structural model, not the person who signed the consent form; therefore, the original consent cannot authorize running or experimenting on it. The other faction argues that identity is preserved if the emulation retains the causal-functional organization of the donor’s brain, and that refusing to recognize the emulation would deny the volunteer’s own expressed values. I need a decision memo for Thursday’s meeting that recommends approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. The memo should state a concrete course of action with a time frame, specify the criteria the board would use in future cases to determine whether an emulation counts as the same mind as the donor, and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write it in American English.
