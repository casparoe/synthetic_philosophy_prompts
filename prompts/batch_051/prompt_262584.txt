Write the joint statement that a proponent of the content-based account of verisimilitude and a proponent of the similarity-based account would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The content-based account analyzes closeness to truth as a function of the true and false claims a theory entails; the similarity-based account analyzes it as degree of similarity between the possible world the theory selects and the actual world. Both sides accept that some false theories are closer to the truth than others, and both reject the simple definition of verisimilitude as truth content minus falsity content in light of the standard trivialization results. The statement should include: (1) numbered substantive claims both sides can sign, including what the trivialization results do and do not show; (2) the residual disagreement, stated in wording each side would accept for its own position; (3) the cruxes, framed as sub-questions whose answers would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete comparative case both agree would test it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Format the residual disagreements as a table, and say explicitly if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal.
