Take Pascal's wager and make everything finite and explicit. Say there are n mutually exclusive gods, you assign each god credence 1/(n+1), and you assign the no-god possibility the same credence 1/(n+1). Each god, if real, rewards its exclusive worshippers with V units of utility in the afeterlife and punishes everyone else—atheists and wrong-god worshippers alike—with W units of disutility. If no god exists, your utility is just the baseline worldly utility (normalize it to 0), and worshipping any god costs you c in this life. Worshipping more than one god counts as worshipping none, so no hedging. Work out the expected utility of (a) atheism and (b) worshipping an arbitrary single god, and say wich option wins for n=1000, V=10^12, W=10^9, c=10. Then give the general condition on n, V, W, and c for worship to beat atheism. Finally: does making the payoffs finite dissolve the many-gods objection, or does it just turn it into a numerical condition, and why?
