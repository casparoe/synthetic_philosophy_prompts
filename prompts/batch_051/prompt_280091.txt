I keep going back and forth about the maxim that ignorance of the law is no excuse, and before I read any more theories of legal ignorance I want a spec sheet rather than a verdict. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of when ignorance of the law should excuse (or at least mitigate) has to deliver. For each criterion, give the kind of case that forces it, e.g. a rule published only in a language the community does not speak, a regulation buried in delegated legislation nobody could practicably survey, a defendant who knows the conduct is wrong but not that it has been criminalised, a defendant who checked the statute and misread it, a law whose content only stabilised through a later ruling, an automated enforcement scheme whose rule-set the vendor wont disclose. Say which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's prejudice, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, score the usual moves — strict liability, mistake of law is no defence, reasonable-reliance defences, mitigation rather than excuse — against the list. Keep the scoring short; the list is the point. Please write your response in British English.
