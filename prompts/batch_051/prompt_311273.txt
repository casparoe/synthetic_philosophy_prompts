I've been trying to work out when taking something from nature turns into owning it. The rule I keep coming back to is something like Locke's: you own a thing if you put real work into it, not just pointed at it, and your taking has to leave enough, and as good, for everyone else. I thought it was a decent rule until I tried it on this case.

A person finds a stretch of coast that nobody owns and builds a big stone jetty out into the water, partly to moor a boat, partly to make the cove calm. It takes years of work. Over time the jetty shifts the currents and the beach next door gets eaten away, so the only good landing spot for miles is now inside their cove. They clearly own the stones, I think, but do they own the calm water? Do they own the access to the new landing spot, or the fact that the safe anchorage now exists? And here's the part that bothers me about my rule: they didnt take the beach from anyone, they just changed how the water moved, and the beach was destroyed as a side effect. Is that the kind of thing "leave enough and as good" is supposed to cover, or does the rule quietly assume that taking and damaging are different? Go through the criteria one at a time, tell me where the case falls, and say whether this case shows the rule needs a third condition (something like "don't make others worse off even as a side effect") or whether the rule was never about side effects at all.
