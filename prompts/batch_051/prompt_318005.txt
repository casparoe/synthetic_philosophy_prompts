I'm a journalist on the science and ideas desk, though my actual beat for years has been how countries metabolize ugly chapters of their own history — official apologies, reparations, truth commissions, all of it. Over time I've built up a set of commitments on state apologies, and a political theorist I interview regularily told me she thinks they might not hang together. I can't tell where the problem is, so I want an audit rather than a lecture.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) States are real corporate agents. A state apology isn't just a press release from whichever official happens to hold office; it's an act of an institution that persists over time, which is what lets a state apologize for things done generations before anyone now in office was born.

(2) Temporal distance doesn't dissolve the obligation. A state can owe — and meaningfully give — an apology for a wrong committed in, say, the 1870s, even though every direct victim and every direct wrongdoer is dead.

(3) But when it comes to reparations, my intuition flips. I don't think you should tax random citizens today for a historical wrong unless those citizens have inherited some identifiable benefit from that wrong. Insitutional continuity by itself isn't enough to open individual wallets.

(4) I'm also allergic to purely symbolic apologies. An apology counts only if it changes something ongoing — a relationship, a policy, legal standing, an institution — not if it's just ceremonial regret about the past.

(5) And I dislike presentism. A state should apologize only for things that were wrong, or at least seriously contested, by the standards available at the time, not merely by ours.

The concrete case I keep coming back to: a state in the late 1800s took land under treaties it then broke, used forced labor on that land, and its current institutions descend more or less directly from that period. I'm convinced that state owes an apology today. Do commitments (1)–(5) actually deliver that, or do some of them cut against each other? Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity instead of giving me a flat list, and tell me what each fix costs.
