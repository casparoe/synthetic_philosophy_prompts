I keep noticing a pattern in arguments about whether machines could ever genuinely think, feel, or matter morally, and I'd like to know whether it has an established name. When a person writes a kind message or improvises a tune, nobody offers "that's just neurons firing" or "that's just learned associations" as an objection; taking the thing apart into smaller processes is treated as beside the point. But when a computer does something of the same apparent kind, the same move—"it's just statistics," "it's just predicting the next word," "it's just running a program"—is offered as if it settles the question, and nobody seems to notice that the identical move would sound strange, even insulting, aimed at a human being. I don't mean ordinary doubt about whether the machine is faking, and I don't mean a bare preference for biology over silicon. I mean the asymmetry itself: a taking-apart explanation is allowed to deflate artificial performances but is never allowed to deflate human ones. Does this asymmetry have a name? If so, how exactly does it differ from the general problem of knowing another mind from the outside, and from the bare refusal to extend concern beyond biological creatures? If not, what would you call it, and where would you draw its boundaries?
