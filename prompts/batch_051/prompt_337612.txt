I administer the performance-pay program at a mid-sized home health agency, and I need help reasoning through a provision in our internal policy.

Section 4.2 of our Performance Incentive Policy states:

"A performance indicator may be used in compensation, promotion, or renewal decisions only if the indicator tracks the outcome it is intended to approximate. No indicator shall be used as a target if an employee can raise the indicator without producing a corresponding improvement in the outcome that the indicator exists to measure."

The policy is about ten years old and was written before we used any AI tools. Last year we introduced an AI scribe that drafts contact notes, follow-up messages, and routine check-in texts. Our "successful outreach" indicator counts documented outreach attempts in the case-management system. Until now the indicator was safe because documenting an attempt required actually making one. With the scribe, a care coordinator can have dozens of individualized check-in messages generated, reviewed, and sent in the time it used to take to make a few phone calls, and every one is logged as successful outreach. The logged number has gone up sharply. But our independently tracked outcome — actual client engagement, measured by reply rates, completed intake steps, and kept appointments — has not improved, and for some client groups it has fallen.

Our coordinators argue they are not manipulating anything: real messages are sent, no logs are false, and clients can ignore the messages just as they could ignore a call. They also point out that the policy never says what "the outcome that the indicator exists to measure" is, and that the indicator exists to measure completed outreach, which really did increase. I think everyone always understood the indicator to be a stand-in for genuine engagement, and that the scribe has quietly cut the link between the stand-in and the thing it stood for.

I have been reading about Goodhart's law on my own, and this looks to me like the measure becoming the target. But I want a careful answer to whether Section 4.2 covers the case. Work through it three ways: the plain words of the clause, the purpose of the provision, and what the people who wrote it would have said if they had been asked about AI-generated outreach. Tell me which approach should decide the matter and why. Then tell me what the clause actually requires of me: should I exclude the scribe-assisted outreach from bonus calculations, allow it, or treat the clause as not speaking to the case and amend it? Also address whether reading the clause as a specific rule or as the policy's version of Goodhart's law changes the answer. Please write the answer in American English.
