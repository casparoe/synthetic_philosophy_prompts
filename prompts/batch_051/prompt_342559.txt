Before I read yet another paper on mathematical explanation in science I want the actual spec, not the usual two-sides dance. Write me the desiderata a theory of mathematical explanation has to meet — the cases it's got to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict — with the consideration behind each one. At minimum cover the cicada-prime case, the honeycomb case, and one more case of your choosing where the math looks explanatory but probably isnt. For each desideratum say whether it's non-negotiable or just a camp preference (platonist, nominalist, causal-mechanist about explanation, whatever side you think it comes from). Then flag any pair or triple of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement knocks out the most of the existing views. I also want to know wether a theory of mathematical explanation is forced to take a stand on the indispensability argument, or whether the two projects can be kept apart — that's the part people keep assuming and I don't think it's settled. Don't score the main contenders in detail; I'll do that myself.
