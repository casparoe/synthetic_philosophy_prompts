A friend and I recently got into an argument about Simone Weil's old polemic against political parties. The way I understand her, she thought parties are inherently in the business of manufacturing collective passions and that they inevitably grind down independent thought, because the party has to have a line on everything and its members have to defend that line whether or not they actually agree with it. She also thought this wasn't a bug of particular parties but of the party form itself: the overriding goal is to grow it's own power, not to discover what is true or just. My friend says she was too pessimistic, and that parties are just a useful coordination technology that can be tamed.

So we came up with a mechanism to test the disagreement. Imagine a country amends its constitution as follows. No candidate for the national legislature may appear on a ballot with any party label, and anyone who currently belongs to a political party is ineligible to run. Elected legislators are barred from holding membership in any party, or in any private association whose main purpose is to influence legislation or elections; a sitting member found to hold such a membership is automatically removed. Parties themselves can keep existing as private clubs, but they may not raise or spend money on campaigns, endorse candidates, or communicate with sitting legislators about legislative business. Inside the chamber, members may not agree to vote as a bloc, trade votes, or take instructions from any outside body; violations, if proved, mean expulsion. The idea is to force politics down into individual judgments while still leaving freedom of association in civil society. Treat this as a mechanism to analyze, not a position to defend.

Please act as a professional political theorist with expertise in institutional design and the history of political thought. Start by brainstorming the main incentives this creates, then give a careful analysis of how it would actually play out: who would benefit, where party-like coordination would reappear despite the rules, what it would do to accountability and to voters' ability to make choices, and whether the failure modes could be fixed without gutting the scheme. Write your answer in British English.
