People sometimes describe arms races, regulatory races to the bottom, and AI labs cutting safety corners as multipolar traps: each actor would prefer that everyone, itself included, remain restrained, but each also prefers to defect if it thinks the others might, so the group converges on an outcome nobody wanted. My background is in software engineering rather than philosophy, so I read this as a race condition across agents, and I keep trying to design a synchronization primitive for it, then finding holes in my own designs.

Here is a mechanism I keep returning to. Several firms face the same decision: ship a product now, or first run a costly safety audit that delays launch. The participating firms each post a large bond with a neutral escrow agent for a fixed window. If a firm ships unaudited while at least one other participant has not yet shipped, it forfeits its bond; half is split among the participants that have not yet shipped, and half is kept by the escrow agent. If all participants ship unaudited in one coordinated move, the forfeiture clause does not apply, all bonds are returned, and the scheme dissolves. If everyone refrains until the deadline, all bonds are returned. No other rules apply, and firms outside the scheme are unaffected.

Analyze how this mechanism would actually behave if serious money were at stake. What does it incentivize for the participating firms, for the escrow agent, and for non-participants? Where does it break, including entry by new firms, gaming the definition or detection of shipping unaudited, side payments, collusion, bond sizing, and the credibility of the escrow agent? Which of these failures can be patched rather than merely moved? Overall, is the mechanism most likely to dampen the race, relocate it, or make it worse?
