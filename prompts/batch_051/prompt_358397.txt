I am a high school student, and I have noticed something in how I read fiction that I do not have a word for. When a story stipulates that in its world gravity reverses at noon, or that certain people are born already grown, I accept these as true within the story without strain. But when a story treats something I consider deeply wrong as though it were perfectly fine—and does not argue for this, but simply assumes it—I cannot seem to accept even the fictional truth of it, although I know it is only a story. The effect is not confined to morality. If a story declares that a character is a brilliant composer and then describes music that sounds ordinary to me, or if it says a joke is hilarious when I do not find it funny, I balk in a similar way. Is there an established name for this pattern? I am not asking only for a label. I want to know whether the moral and non-moral cases are one phenomenon or two, whether the resistance lies in my imagination or in my judgment, and how it differs from the ordinary suspension of disbelief that lets me accept reversed gravity in the first place.
