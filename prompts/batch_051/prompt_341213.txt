I keep going in circles on the word "fitness." I need a working definition of biological fitness that gets these cases right: a healthy antelope in a good herd (clearly fit); an antelope with a congenital leg defect that keeps getting caught (clearly unfit); a healthy antelope struck by lightning a week before mating season; a plant genotype that produces twice as many seeds in good years but dies in bad years while another genotype just chugs along; a sterile worker ant; and a rare morph whose advantage vanishes as it becomes common. The constraint is this: if fitness just means "actual reproductive success," then survival-of-the-fittest is a truism, so the definition has to make fitness explanatory rather than a redescription of what actualy happened. I lean toward the propensity interpretation—fitness as a dispositional probability, more like a half-life than an actual number of offspring—but I'm not sure it yields a single number for the context-dependent and frequency-dependent cases, and I don't know what to do with the worker ant. Propose the best definition you can, run it through all six, and flag where it breaks or forces a counterintuitive verdict. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning.
