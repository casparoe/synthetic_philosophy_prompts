So I've been chewing on an argument from special relativity that seems to prove way too much — that the future is already fully real, sitting out there, and that the difference between "settled" and "still open" is just an artifact of which frame you happen to be in. It runs like this.

(1) Special relativity has no privileged inertial frame. So one frame's "now" slice through spacetime is as legitimate as any other's.

(2) Relativity of simultaneity says frames genuinely disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with a given here-and-now. The Andromeda example makes it vivid: Andromeda is about 2.5 million light-years away, so two observers passing each other at walking-speed relative motion, one heading toward it and one away, can disagree by several days about what's happening there "right now."

(3) Now take an event I'd naturally call future: a flare on some star in Andromeda that, in my rest frame, won't happen until next Tuesday. Since Andromeda is 2.5 million light-years off and next Tuesday is a week away, my typing this sentence and that flare are spacelike seperated. That means there's an inertial frame (moving toward Andromeda at a brisk walk relative to me, I think) in which the flare is simultaneous with my typing.

(4) If no frame is privileged, then the slice of spacetime that makes the flare simultaneous with my typing is just as real as the slice I call "now." So the flare is, in that perfectly legitimate frame, happening at the same time as my typing. The non-arbitrary thing to say seems to be that it's real now — my typing and the flare are equally part of what exists.

(5) But then at least some of what I'd call "the future" is already there. And the usual next move is to chain frames: if being simultaneous with something real in any frame is enough to make a thing real, then I can run the same trick through a third event spacelike separated from both, and reach even my own timelike future. Then the whole block is real and the open future is cooked.

The conclusion is hard to accept: it says past and future don't differ in what exists, only in perspective, like foreshortening. But each premise on its own looks awfully innocent.

So where does this actually go wrong, if it goes wrong? I want a diagnosis, not vibes. Pick the premise or step you'd reject, explain why it looked innocent, and tell me exactly what giving it up costs. Is there a hidden equivocation buried in the argument, or is it valid and I'm stuck choosing which precious assumption is least bad to throw away? And price the main options honestly. The two things I'd most like to keep are: no hidden preferred frame, and a real difference in kind between what's already fixed and what's still open. Tell me whether I can actually have both here, or whether the argument shows exactly why I can't.
