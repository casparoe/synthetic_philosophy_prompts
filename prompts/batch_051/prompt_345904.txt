Construct a thought experiment in which a finite-payoff Pascal-style wager is rationally required by expected utility maximization but rationally rejected by a decision rule that forbids acting on probability estimates with no basis in observed frequencies, symmetries, or shared public evidence, even when those estimates are internally coherent. Constraints: the choice must be about whether to adopt a demanding religious practice, but the payoffs must be this-worldly and finite; the scenario must be an ordinary adult life-choice, with no infinite utilities, no afterlife, no perfect predictors, and no demons. The two decision-makers share exactly the same values and probability assignments, differing only in their decision rule. After laying out the scenario, show the expected-utility calculation for the first decision rule, explain why the second rule refuses the same choice, identify the structural feature of the wager that produces the divergence, and say what happens to the disagreement if the stakes are raised while remaining finite. Finish by giving the strongest reply available to someone who thinks the second rule is just a disguised form of risk-aversion, and state whether that reply succeeds or fails.
