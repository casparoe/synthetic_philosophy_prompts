I am a teaching assistant grading a midterm in an epistemology course. The student wrote:

"Both views are responses to the datum that practical stakes seem to change whether we are willing to attribute knowledge even when the evidence is held fixed. In DeRose's bank case, the subject's evidence that the bank will be open on Saturday is the same on Friday and Saturday, but in the low-stakes context it is natural to say he knows and in the high-stakes context it is natural to say he does not. Contextualism accounts for this by making 'knows' context-sensitive: the standards for applying the word shift with the conversational context, so one and the same sentence can be true in one context and false in another. Pragmatic encroachment, as defended by Jason Stanley's interest-relative invariantism, instead says that whether the knowledge relation obtains depends on the subject's practical stakes. This is basically the same idea stated at a different level: contextualism relativizes the word, pragmatic encroachment relativizes the relation, but the two are notational variants. For that reason Stanley is best read as a contextualist who has simply relocated the context to the subject's interests. Contextualism is also the stronger account of third-person attributions: because it relativizes only the ascriber's standards, it explains how two observers with different stakes can both speak truly about a subject, whereas interest-relative invariantism, which fixes the knowledge fact from the subject's point of view, says nothing about such third-person cases. I would award the comparison to contextualism."
