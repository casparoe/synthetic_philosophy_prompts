I've been tinkering with a variation on Hilbert's hotel and I want to know whether it actually breaks the standard arguments or just makes them look different.

Standard setup: countably infinite rooms numbered 1, 2, 3, ..., all full. One new guest: the manager shifts every guest from n to n+1. A countably infinite tour bus: the manager shifts guest n to 2n, opening all the odd rooms. Everyone moves simultaneously, so both are supposed to work.

Now the variation. The hotel is a physical hallway, rooms one room-length apart, and every guest walks at exactly one room per minute, so a guest who moves d rooms takes d minutes. All guests start at the same time. The manager has two rules: nobody may be moved to a smaller-numbered room, and any rearrangment must be finished within T minutes (where T is some fixed finite deadline), meaning every guest has arrived at her new room by then. Under those rules the single-new-guest trick still works for any T at least 1: everyone shifts up one room and arrives within a minute. But the tour-bus trick sends guest n on a walk of n rooms, so for any fixed T some guests haven't arrived by the deadline. I can't tell whether that means the tour group now genuinely can't be fit, or just that the standard scheme was the wrong scheme.

So: can the manager still fit the countably infinite tour group under the deadline rules? If yes, give the scheme. If no, give the impossibility argument and make it isolate the exact step the original Hilbert-hotel story was getting for free. Then say what this version reveals about the gap between 'every guest has a finite walk' and 'there's a single finite bound on all the walks', and whether a finitist would be right that the original setup quietly assumes something like actual infinity that this concrete version refuses to grant. I care less about the label 'actual infinity' than about which specific step stops being licensed.

After the prose, give a JSON summary with these fields:
- one_new_guest_still_works: true/false, with a one-line reason.
- infinite_coach_still_works: true/false.
- impossibility_or_scheme: the key reasoning, kept tight.
- load_bearing_assumption: the exact infinitary assumption the deadline version exposes.
- finitist_moral: what this shows about potential vs actual infinity, if anything.
- edge_cases: an array of 2-4 objects, each with "case" and "verdict" (for example: a finite group of five, the infinite coach with no deadline, dropping the no-smaller-room rule).
