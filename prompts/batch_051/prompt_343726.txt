I've been trying to sort out what I think it takes for something to count as a given piece of music, and my verdicts on cases are pulling apart. A symphony performance where the first horn cracks a note: same work. A note-perfect rendering of the same score by notation software, with no human performer in the loop: I still want to count it as an instance of the work, though "performance" feels wrong. A jazz quartet playing a standard's chord changes for eight minutes without ever stating the melody: not the same work — a new piece over the same changes. A singer who re-harmonizes a pop song in a new key but keeps the melody and lyrics: same song, different arrangement. A producer who chops and pitch-shifts that same vocal, keeps the melody and only a few words, and sets it over a new beat and unrelated chords: different work. The pair I can't reconcile is this: I count the MIDI playback as an instance of the work, but I don't count playing the original studio recording through a loudspeaker as a new instance of the work — only as a reproduction of a past instance. The two cases are acoustically similar, so my principle had better say what the difference is. Give me the principle you'd actually defend (or a small set of candidates, if it takes that), stated precisely, with each clause motivated; take a firm position on the MIDI/loudspeaker pair; and tell me what your principle says about a further case — a composer writes a score whose only instruction is that the performer press play on that studio recording and stand back. Which of my verdicts, if any, do I have to abandon?
