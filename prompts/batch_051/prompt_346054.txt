I direct the quality-measurement analytics team at a large integrated health system. Every quarter we publish comparisons of primary care practices and hospitals on outcomes such as unplanned readmissions, surgical complications, and revisits to the emergency department after discharge. Our current default is to report a single risk-adjusted rate for each unit and rank them. But I keep hitting Simpson’s-paradox situations: the aggregate rate says clinic A is worse than clinic B, yet within each patient-risk band A is better, or the ranking reverses depending on whether we look at acute and chronic patients separately. The board wants a standard, defensible procedure that analysts can run before we publish any comparison. Please write a reusable decision procedure for deciding when a single aggregate comparison is adequate and when we are obligated to break the comparison into subgroups built from patient mix, condition severity, age band, or similar unit characteristics. The procedure should be one that a master’s-level analyst can complete in under an hour with the data we have, and it should be honest about which steps are genuinely statistical and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure. It should include: the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops and says the aggregate comparison is not publishable as a ranking, and where it hands off to a human because the decision is about values rather than arithmetic. Then apply the procedure to three cases: one where the aggregate and all standard subgroups agree; one where the ranking reverses between two comorbidity bands and there is no obvious causal story; and one where the ranking reverses across racial groups but the underlying sample sizes are small. In at least one of these the procedure should give an answer my leadership will not like.
