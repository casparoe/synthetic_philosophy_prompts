I've been trying to get my head around Fitch's paradox of knowability and keep geting stuck. Here's the argument as I understand it. Suppose every truth is knowable. Now pick any truth p that noone currently knows. Then the combined truth — p is true and noone knows p — is itself a truth, so by the knowability principle it must be possible for someone to know it. But it seems impossible to know that conjunction: if someone knows p, then someone does know p, so the claim noone knows p would be false, and you can't know something false. So we have a truth that can't be known, which contradicts the starting principle. Fine, maybe the principle has to go. But doesn't the same line of reasoning show something much stronger? For any undiscovered truth p, the claim p is true and noone knows p is an unknowable truth, and if that whole claim is unknowable, doesn't that mean p itself is unknowable? That can't be right, since people discover new things all the time. What exactly am I misreading, and what does the paradox actually force an anti-realist to give up?
