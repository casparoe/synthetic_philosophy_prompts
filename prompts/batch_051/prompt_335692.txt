Consider these five criteria often proposed for organismality: genetic homogeneity across the body; physiological integration strong enough that the parts cannot survive or reproduce on their own; a single-cell bottleneck in the life cycle, so that each new whole develops from one founding cell; germ–soma separation, with a division of labor between reproduction and bodily maintenance; and shared evolutionary fate, with the parts' fitness interests sufficiently aligned that the whole functions as a unit of selection. Now take an aphid and the bacteria living inside specialized cells in its body: the bacteria produce amino acids the aphid cannot synthesize on its own, they cannot survive outside the aphid, and they are transmitted through the mother's eggs, so aphid and bacteria reproduce as a packaged whole with a single-cell bottleneck in the aphid's life cycle. The aphid's own tissues carry one genome, and the bacteria carry another. Go through the five criteria one by one and make the strongest case you can both for and against counting this aphid–bacteria package as a single organism. Then say which criterion, if any, ends up doing the real work for your verdict, and whether the case shows that organismality is graded, that the criteria need revising, or both.
