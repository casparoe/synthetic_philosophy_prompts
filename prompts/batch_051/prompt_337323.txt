I've been going back and forth with a friend about when a number in scripture counts as symbolically meaningful and when it's just pattern-hunting. In one of my theology modules we were given three rough tests for a symbolic-number reading. (1) The text itself flags the number as meaningful: it tells you to count, repeats the number, or gives a number so strange in context that a literal reading can't sit with it. (2) The symbolism could have occured to the original audience using the arithmetic and numerology available then, rather than needing a modern spreadsheet. (3) The proposed meaning explains why that number and not a near neighbour, and it actually does something for the point of the passage instead of just being a pretty fact. The tests are vague, I know; that's sort of the issue.

Take John 21:11, the post-resurrection fishing scene. The King James Version reads: 'Simon Peter went up, and drew the net to land full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken.' The precision of 153 has always attracted interpreters. It's the 17th triangular number (1+2+...+17 = 153), and 17 can be taken as 10 for the commandments plus 7 for the Spirit or completeness, which feels like something a first-century reader could do; Augustine reads it along those lines, and other early commentators link 153 to an ancient catalogue of 153 kinds of fish, making the catch a figure for the universal mission. The trouble is that the text itself never flags the number: no one is told to count, the number doesn't recur, and the sentence's obvious point is that the net didn't break. So apply the three tests one by one and say whether the symbolic reading of 153 is legitimate interpretation or pattern-hunting. Which criterion is doing the real work, and should the criteria be revised in light of this case? Please answer in British English.
