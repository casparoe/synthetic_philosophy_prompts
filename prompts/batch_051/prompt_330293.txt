Question:

In The Future of an Illusion (1927), Freud claims that religious ideas are illusions. Explain what he means by calling them illusions. Your answer should: (a) define "illusion" in Freud's technical sense and distinguish it from an error and from a delusion; (b) explain why religious ideas, on his account, count as illusions rather than as mere errors or delusions; and (c) explain the role of wish-fulfilment and infantile helplessness in his account of religion.

Student answer:

Freud's central claim in The Future of an Illusion is that religious beliefs are illusions. An illusion, for Freud, is a belief that is false but held because it satisfies a wish. Religious doctrines — the existence of a providential God, survival after death, and a moral order in the universe — do not derive from experience or from rational argument, so they lack evidential support. Because they cannot be confirmed, Freud holds that science has in effect refuted them, and this is what makes them false. The distinction between an illusion and a delusion is mainly a matter of scope: illusions are shared by an entire culture and are not signs of mental illness, whereas delusions are held by individual patients and are pathological. In both cases, however, the belief conflicts with reality. Religious ideas count as illusions rather than as ordinary errors because they are not simply mistakes about the world: they satisfy some of the deepest and most early-forming human wishes — the wish for protection, for justice, and for continued existence after death. Freud traces these wishes back to infantile helplessness. The young child, weak before the world, turns to its father for protection; the adult, still helpless before nature and fate, projects an enlarged and idealized father-like figure onto the cosmos. Because religion is rooted in these wishes rather than in evidence, Freud thinks it should be given up as science and civilization mature, since it keeps people in a state of consoling untruth. A limitation of the account is that Freud cannot prove that God does not exist, so strictly he has explained why people believe without demonstrating that the beliefs are false.
