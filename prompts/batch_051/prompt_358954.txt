ok so ive been thinking about why we trust simple explanations over complicated ones. everyone gest wrong about occams razor and just says simpler is better, but nobody ive read online actually explains why simple should track the truth. so i made up a thing i call "patch debt." the idea is: every explanation has a complexity budget, like how long the shortest rule is that would generate the pattern. when the explanation gets surprised by something it didnt predict, you have to patch it, add an exception or a hidden thing or a special case. each patch makes the rule longer. but my actual claim is that patches you add AFTER being wrong should count more than the complexity you started with, like interest on a loan. a theory that starts simple and needs five patches is worse than a theory that was born already that complicated, because the simple one was confidently wrong each time and the complicated one was at least honest from day one. so the measure would be: starting length plus interest on every patch, and the interest rate is how badly the prediction failed. i think this explains why conspiracy theories feel so bad even when each added patch is small, they rack up huge patch debt. ive read a little about kolmogorov complexity and solomonoff induction so i know this is half baked. is this coherent? is it already a real thing under a name i dont know? and where does it break, like does it punish a theory for making predictions at all, or is it just solomonoff induction with extra steps?
