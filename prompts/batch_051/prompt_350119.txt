i've got a theory about the endowment effect i've been calling 'anchor realism,' and i want someone to beat it up. the usual story says loss aversion and the WTA/WTP gap are biases, because there's supposed to be a neutral, reference-free price for a mug or whatever. my view is that the neutral baseline is the part that's fake. every valuation is made from inside a situation, and your reference point — owning the thing, expecting it, having already budgeted around it — is itself evidence about what it's worth to you, because being an owner rather than a renter changes you're actual circumstances. so the gap between what owners demand and what non-owners will pay isn't a distortion to be corrected; it's what valuation looks like once you stop pretending you're standing nowhere. i know there's work on expectations-based reference points in behavioral econ, so it's possible i've just reinvented that. is anchor realism a real idea, a version of something existing, or just a metaphor? and where exactly does it break — i'm already worried about the fact that people show loss aversion for things they only expected to get and never had, which sounds like my reference point is doing more work than it's earned.
