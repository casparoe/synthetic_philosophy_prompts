I am a retired atmospheric physicist. For most of my career I built and evaluated ensemble forecast models, and I still think most naturally in that idiom: state spaces and attractors, sensitivity to initial conditions and Lyapunov times, ensemble spread and forecast horizons, structural versus parameter uncertainty, model verification and skill scores, and decision rules that take an expectation over an ensemble of plausible futures. Since retiring I have been reading moral philosophy, and the problem of consequentialist cluelessness — the worry that the long-run consequences of ordinary actions are too complex and too sensitive to intervening events for consequentialism to provide usable guidance — immediately reminded me of the systems I used to model. A resemblance, however, is not an argument, and I want to know how far the parallel can be pushed before it breaks.

I would like you to restate the central claims and standard replies in the cluelessness debate in that modeling vocabulary, systematically rather than impressionistically. For each item — the consequentialist criterion itself, the cluelessness objection, the reply that ordinary expected-utility reasoning over available evidence is sufficient for action guidance, and the further worry that this reply quietly replaces a theory about actual consequences with a theory about expected consequences — say what translates cleanly, what carries over only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in the target vocabulary at all. Please be scrupulous about not importing modeling assumptions into the translation: do not assume without argument that all value-relevant outcomes are in principle forecastable, or that uncertainty is always representable by a probability distribution over a fixed outcome space. Then give me a residue report. Does what fails to translate indicate that my vocabulary is missing distinctions the philosophers are right to insist on, or that some strands of the debate are less precise than they appear once the vivid examples are stripped away? I am still a novice in the philosophical literature, though I can follow precise technical argument, and I am not asking for a survey or a list of sources; I want the honest accounting of what the translation carries and what it loses.
