I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have spent a good deal of time thinking about hedonism and its troubles. The view, as I understand it, is that pleasure is the only thing good for its own sake, and the standard support for that view has been the thought that all pleasant experiences share a single felt quality: the pleasantness itself. What has begun to worry me is a development in the study of the brain and motivation. It now appears that the liking component of a reward can be separated from the wanting component, and that pleasures arising from food, relief, social contact, curiosity, and aesthetic experience are not all underwritten by one common system. There may be no single thing that all pleasant states feel together. If that is right, does the standard argument for hedonism survive? I would like to know exactly which premise the development puts pressure on, whether the argument can be repaired once the common-quality claim is given up, and whether what remains would still be recognizably hedonistic rather than a different theory altogether.
