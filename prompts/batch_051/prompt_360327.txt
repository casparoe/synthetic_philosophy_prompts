In my seminar on behavioral economics and welfare policy, we have been arguing about whether the endowment effect should be treated as a bias to be debunked or as a legitimate feature of people's preferences, and one line of reasoning has left me stuck because its conclusion seems plainly unacceptable.

(1) The standard account of the endowment effect is that ownership moves a good into the owner's reference point, and losses measured from that point weigh more than equal-sized gains. (2) In the classic mug experiment, that reference point is assigned arbitrarily: mugs are distributed at random, so who becomes an owner and who remains a buyer carries no information about anyone's tastes or about the mug itself. (3) If a valuation is driven solely by an arbitrary reference point, it reflects no real feature of the person or the good, and so it is a framing artifact rather than a genuine preference. (4) Therefore the willingness-to-accept/willingness-to-pay gap measured in endowment experiments should be discounted or ignored for purposes of welfare and policy. (5) But the same form of explanation applies to valuations we would never think of ignoring: the premium a longtime resident places on remaining in her own home, the sum someone would demand before surrendering a legal entitlement she currently holds, and the extra value she places on her own health simply because it is already hers. Each of these is likewise a product of the arbitrary fact that the item currently sits on her side of the reference point. (6) By parity of reasoning, those valuations are also framing artifacts and should be ignored in welfare and policy. (7) That conclusion is absurd; taking it seriously would mean treating ordinary attachments and entitlements as mistakes to be corrected away.

Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? If I have to choose, I want to keep (7), but I also do not want to give up the idea that at least some reference-dependent valuations really are biases rather than legitimate preferences. If the mistake is in drawing a line between the mug and the home, what is the principled line, and what does drawing it cost? Or if a premise must be rejected, which one, and what does rejecting it commit me to?
