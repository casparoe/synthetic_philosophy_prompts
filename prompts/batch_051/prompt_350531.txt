I'm a software engineer, and I've been offered a senior position at a company that is building a privately run city inside a special economic zone in a developing country. Residents would sign a long-term residency contract: a flat monthly fee replaces most taxes, and the developer provides roads, utilities, security, schools, and private arbitration instead of the host country's courts. The company's pitch is that the project is clean because no one is forced to move there, everyone chooses the rules they live under, and anyone can leave. My father, who is a lawyer, says that is a trick: I would remain a citizen of the host country no matter what I sign, the city would depend on the host country's military, currency, and trade agreements, and whatever autonomy it has is a permission the government could revoke, not a property anyone bought. I am drawn to the idea that people should be governed only by rules they have agreed to, but his point about depending on the state has unsettled me. I have a few weeks to decide. Is he right that you cannot really exit a country by contract, and if he is only half right, which half should guide my decision?
