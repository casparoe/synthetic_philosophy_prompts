I keep getting hit with "what if everyone did that?" Most recently my brother used it on me when I said I wasn't going to bother voting in a local runoff, and before that a friend used it when I admitted I take the occasional extra ketchup packet. I have a feeling the sentence is doing more then one job: sometimes it sounds like a prediction that the world would be worse if everybody did the thing, sometimes like an accusation that I'm carving out an unfair exception for myself, sometimes like a consistency test on the rule I'm acting from. I know Kant, Hare, and Singer are all supposed to have versions of this move, but I can't keep them straight. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be expressing, from trivial to radical: for each, say whether it's a platitude, substantive and defensible, or just false, and flag the readings people tend to run together. Then say which reading, if any, actually supports my brother's voting argument, and whether the voting case and the ketchup case should get different readings.
