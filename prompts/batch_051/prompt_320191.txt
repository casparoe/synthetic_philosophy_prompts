ok so I'm in an experimental philosophy seminar and we've been stuck on the Knobe effect for two weeks, and I want to check whether the position I've slid into actually hangs together. Here's roughly where I am:

(1) The Knobe effect is real. In the chairman vignettes, people overwhelmingly say he intentionally harmed the environment but not that he intentionally helped it, even though in both versions he says he only cares about profits.

(2) I read the asymmetry as telling us something about the folk concept of intentional action itself, not just a performance error people make when the question feels loaded. So a philosophical account of intentional action has to either accommodate the asymmetry or say explicitly why it's allowed to ignore it.

(3) The explanation I find most convincing is norm-based rather than purely evaluative: what drives the folk verdict is whether the agent knowingly violated a salient norm, not whether the outcome was morally bad as such. So I'd expect the asymmetry to show up even for helpful outcomes, as long as the agent flouted some norm to produce them (an employee who breaks company policy to cover for a coworker, that kind of thing).

(4) I also think this is just one instance of a broader pattern in folk judgments, not something special to the word 'intentional.' I'd expect the same norm sensitivity to leak into judgments about causation, knowledge, and doing versus allowing.

(5) At the same time, I'm sympathetic to a clean knowledge account in the philsophy of action: a side effect is intentional iff the agent knew it would happen or it was part of their plan, and praise/blame are simply irrelevant.

So: are these five jointly tenable? I can tell something is pulling against something else but I can't sort out which pair is actually fatal and which is just the vibes being off. Is (1) in tension with (3), since the original effect looks like it's all about good vs bad? And is the real problem maybe between (2) and (5), or somewhere I haven't looked? Rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on inspection, and tell me what the cheapest revision is — or whether there's a distinction I can draw that lets me keep the whole package.
