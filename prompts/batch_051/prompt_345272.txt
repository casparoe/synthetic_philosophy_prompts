I teach an advanced undergrad seminar on forgiveness, and last week a student said — in response to the question whether victims can ever be obligated to forgive — that "forgiveness is a gift, not a duty," as if that settled things. It's been nagging me, because I think the slogan is doing several differnt jobs at once, and the student then helped themselves to a further claim: that if forgiveness is a gift, it can't be unconditional either, since a gift is something you choose to give and can attach whatever conditions you like. I want to map this before next week's class. What are the distinct precise claims "forgiveness is a gift" could be making, from the pretty trivial to the pretty radical? For each reading: is it plausible, does it rule out forgiveness being obligatory (and in what sense of obligatory — a duty owed to the wrongdoer, a general ethical requirement, a condition of continued membership in a community), and does it let in unconditional forgiveness? And specifically: the student's slide from "gift" to "not a duty" to "therefore not unconditional" — which readings actually license it, and on which readings is it a bait-and-switch? I want a menu I can talk from, not one settled answer.
