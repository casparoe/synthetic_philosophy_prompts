I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about population size, and the argument ended in a genuine impasse. She began by defending a total view—that, other things equal, a larger population with more aggregate well-being is better—but then tried to resist the Repugnant Conclusion, the claim that any population of lives at a very high level of welfare can be matched or exceeded in value by some sufficiently large population of lives each barely worth living. Her fallback was a threshold view: she wanted to say there is a discontinuity in the value of individual well-being between lives that are seriously good and lives that are merely not bad, so that no number of the latter can compensate for a sufficient loss among the former. I pressed her on whether that threshold could be made non-arbitrary, and on whether she was underestimating what a life just above the neutral level can be like from the inside; she pressed me on whether I would actually rank a world of billions of barely tolerable lives above a world of a million flourishing lives, or whether my acceptance of the conclusion was just a failure to take the comparison seriously. By the end I was at roughly 0.65 that the Repugnant Conclusion, construed purely axiologically, is true; she was at roughly 0.3.

Please give me your credence in that same proposition: that for any population of lives at very high welfare, there is some much larger population of lives each barely worth living that is at least as good, all else equal. Decompose the estimate into the sub-claims your credence depends on—for example, whether betterness can be represented by an additive function across lives, whether there is any non-arbitrary lexical discontinuity or critical level in individual well-being, whether an outcome can be better without being better for any particular person, and whether the felt repugnance of the conclusion is tracking a real axiological fact or a bias of perspective. Assign a probability to each term with a one-line justification, then show how they combine. Finally, identify the term you are least confident in, say what evidence or argument would move it most, and say where you think the disagreement between my friend and me most likely lives.
