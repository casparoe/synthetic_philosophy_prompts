i've been asked to give the leavers' speech at our end of year assembly, which is slightly terrifying because our year group has sat through about six thousand assemblies and i don't want mine to be the usual follow your dreams thing with a few in-jokes. we did the Grand Inquisitor chapter from The Brothers Karamazov in English this term and it's honestly taken over my brain. the way i read it, the old cardinal tells Jesus he got it wrong by giving people free choice, because most of us actually can't cope with freedom and would definately swap it for bread and someone else making the decisions, and then Jesus doesn't even argue — he just kisses him and walks off.

it feels wierdly like the exact decision we're all about to make: do you pick your own path and risk getting it badly wrong, or do you take the safe route with a clear ladder that someone else has already built. i want the whole speech to be built around that dilemma, not just have a fancy literary reference tacked on at the end. the audience is mostly sixth formers who haven't read it, plus a few English and philosophy teachers who definitely have. can you write me the speech, around four or five minutes read aloud, that makes the Grand Inquisitor's argument feel real to people who've never heard of it, and that lets him be wrong without making the safe-route people sound stupid? half my mates are going to do accountancy or law because their parents did, and i don't want to sound like i think i'm better than them. i'd rather it came across as honest and a bit unsure than as a motivational poster.
