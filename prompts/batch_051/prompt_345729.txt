In my own work and in seminar I have a policy about aggregative arguments: when a paper tries to get a serious moral conclusion by summing an astronmically huge number of tiny harms across many people—one person tortured versus dust specks in enough eyes, say—I treat the result as a reductio of the aggregation step and don't take the bottom-line conclusion seriously. I've got no worked-out theory of why I do this; basically I act as if there's a lexical floor below which harms don't simply pile up across persons, and I'd be embarrassed if a student pressed me on where the floor is. Is the policy defensible, or is it just an intuition dressed up as a rule? Where does it break down, and is there a version I could keep that doesn't secretly commit me to a threshold I can't justify?
