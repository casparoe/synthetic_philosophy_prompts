I've been reading mechanistic interpretability work on the alignment forums and keep noticing a move for which I don't have a word. Someone claims that a second model has explained a target model because the second model's probes track the target's internal states, or because the target's activations decompose into labeled features. Pressed on why those labels or features should be taken at face value, they point to a third system: the human labelers behind the training data, the pretrained model whose representations anchor the features, or the internal structure of the second model itself. The third system's transparency is then left unexamined, even though it is just as much an object of interpretation as the original model. The chain does not go on forever; it stops wherever it becomes convenient to treat some interpreter as foundational, and that stopping point is rarely defended as more transparent than the thing being interpreted. Is there an established name for this specific pattern? I am not after a bare label; I want the boundaries drawn: what are the nearest named concepts, how does this pattern differ from each of them, and if nothing established fits, what would you call it and what edge cases would separate genuine instances from a harmless division of interpretive labor?
