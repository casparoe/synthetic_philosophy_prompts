Two people I know got into it after a documentary about scapegoat rituals, where the comunity puts its sins on a goat and drives it off into the desert. The first said that's pure theater: the goat didn't do any of those things, so the guilt doesn't actually transfer. The second said the first is using "guilt" for two different things and sliding between them — there's the plain fact that you did the deed, which can't move, and then there's the social standing of being blamed and owing the group, which is exactly what the ritual shifts; and in everyday life, the second said, that second kind is most of what we mean by guilt. The first shot back that this makes it worse: a village could rob a traveler, put the robbery on a goat, and now nobody in the village is guilty, so "justice" becomes a pressure valve. The second said that's only an absurdity if you insist guilt is some untransferable stain; if guilt is a social standing the community can set aside, then transferring it is coherent, and calling the result a pressure valve just names what restoring the community is. The first said then the goat itself is pointless — if it all comes down to what the community accepts, a signed piece of paper would do the same job, so the second is smuggling real symbolism back in while pretending it's all social standing.
