Below are the criteria I teach for what makes something the solution to a Bongard problem. A Bongard problem consists of two panels of six simple figures each; the solver's task is to state the rule that all six figures on the left satisfy and all six figures on the right violate.

(C1) Separation: the proposed rule must classify all six left figures as satisfying it and all six right figures as violating it.

(C2) Conciseness: the rule must be expressible without enumerating the twelve figures individually, and without an ad hoc disjunction whose disjuncts merely list what appears on each side.

(C3) Projectibility: the rule must use a vocabulary that would continue to separate new figures generated by the same underlying contrast, rather than a gerrymandered predicate in the way grue is gerrymandered, even if it happens to cover the twelve displayed figures.

Now consider the following case. The six left figures are small equilateral triangles; the six right figures are large circles. The triangles vary among themselves in color, orientation, and fill pattern, and the circles vary among themselves in the same colors, orientations, and fill patterns, so those dimensions do not track the left–right split.

At least three rules satisfy C1 and appear to satisfy C2 and C3:

(R1) Left figures are triangles; right figures are circles.

(R2) Left figures are angular polygons; right figures are curved figures.

(R3) Left figures are small; right figures are large.

The three rules diverge on new figures: a small circle would be classified as left by R3 but as right by R1 and R2; a large triangle would be classified as left by R1 and R2 but as right by R3.

Is this case a well-posed Bongard problem? Does it have one solution, three legitimate solutions, or no determinate solution until something further is supplied, such as the constructor's intention, a prior over dimensions, or a preference ranking among the rules? Work through the case criterion by criterion, say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the case shows that the criteria need a fourth condition, such as uniqueness or intended contrast, and what such a condition would have to do in order to help.
