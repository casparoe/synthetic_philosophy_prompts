I'm a software engineer, and I have a rule about changing my mind that I want stress-tested. I never revise an important view in the same conversation or reading session where I first encounter a serious objection; instead, I log the argument and schedule a review at least a week out. I adopted the rule because, from a distance, I can see that I overweight the discomfort of revising when it is immediate and underweight it when it is a week away, even though the reasoning task is the same; in the moment I either cave to whatever I just read or dig in reflexively, and in both cases I usually regret it. The delay gives the objection a calmer hearing, but it also means I knowingly go on asserting views while already holding an unanswered objection to them. Is this a defensible response to my own predictable weakness of will about hard thinking, or a sophisticated status-quo bias? If it is partly both, is there a version of the rule that keeps the benefit without that cost, and how would I tell from the inside which version I am actually running? Please answer in American English.
