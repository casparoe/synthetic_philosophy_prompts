Friends in my online reading group have been fighting for months about whether the left/right political axis is real, and I've ended up as the moderator. One camp says there's a real underlying dimension: if you know someone's position on taxes you can make a decent guess at their position on immigration or gun control. The other camp says the thing is a historical accident, a bag of positions glued together by parties and media, and a single left-right number hides more than it reveals. I keep coming back to a compression analogy (I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher): you've got a high-dimensional space of policy positions and you're summarizing it with one scalar. Is the scalar extracting real signal, like a first principal component, or is it mostly noise plus a bunch of historical contingencies?

Draft the adversarial-collaboration document these two camps should have produced instead of arguing past each other. Format it like this:

1. A numbered list of claims both camps can sign, as specific and non-vacuous as you can make them.
2. A disagreements table. Each row states one residual disagreement, phrased so that each side reads their own position and says "yes, that's exactly what I think, not a strawman."
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would make at least one side actually update. For each crux, give one concrete kind of evidence, data, or test both sides would agree bears on it, and say what each side predicts.
4. A short note on which parts of the fight are probably just about words.

Also include a section on what it would take for each side to lose. If the single-axis camp is right that the axis tracks a real psychological or structural cause holding the bundle together, what would show instead that the bundle is held together by coalition politics and would fall apart if the coalitions changed? And for the bundle camp, what would show there is a common cause doing real predictive work, so a one-number summary isnt just re-describing the last few decades? dont worry about citations or named theories; use invented but plausible concrete examples if you need them.
