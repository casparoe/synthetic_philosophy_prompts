I cover science and ideas for a living, and one thing that's been bugging me on the AI safety beat is how a particular argument just runs in place year after year. On one side are people who say the profit motive is the root cause of existential risk from AI: competitive pressure makes labs ship faster than is safe, treat safety as a cost to be minimized, and externalize the downside, so if you don't change the economic incentives you're just rearranging deck chairs. On the other side are people who say that's mistaking the delivery system for the disease. Misalignment, they argue, is a technical problem that would show up just as badly in a nonprofit, a university lab, or a Manhattan Project-style state program, and a totalizing state racing to AGI might be worse because it has fewer off-switches and even less accountability.

The two camps seem to accept a lot of the same local facts — labs do cut corners under pressure, alignment is unsolved, the current ecosystem looks like an arms race. Maybe there's a hidden factual disagreement underneath that I'm missing. But my working hypothesis is that they're answering different questions under one heading: one side is explaining why the current world is dangerous, and the other is doing a counterfactual check on whether removing the profit motive would actually make the danger go away. Both can be right on their own question, so the debate never moves.

What I'd like from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is the disagreement verbal or substantive? Do the two sides differ about facts, about standards of evidence, about which cases count as data, or about bedrock intuitions? Are they answering different questions under one heading? Is a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work, or is the whole thing just ill-posed? Please engage the seperate positions concretely, say which parts of the dispute are substantive and which are just about words, and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a specific experiment, a formal distinction, a better-posed question, or an argument that no progress should be expected here and why. And write it in American English, please.
