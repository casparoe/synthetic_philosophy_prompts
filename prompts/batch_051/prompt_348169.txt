I'm not a philosophy person at all - I mostly write backend services in Go. But I read Tolstoy's What Is Art? over the holidays and there's this one passage that got stuck in my head. In chapter 5 he gives his actual definition of art, and the Maude translation says something like: art is the activity where one person deliberately uses external signs to transmit feelings they have lived through to another person, and the other person gets infected by those feelings and lives them too. He leans really hard on infection as the test.

I wrote a short essay for my tech blog comparing this to code. The idea is that a codebase is kind of like Tolstoy's external sign, and the feeling being transmitted is the mental state of the original engineer. When I read a well-written codebase, I don't just understand the logic, I catch some of the emotional state of the person who wrote it: frustration with a weird API, satisfaction from a clean abstraction, anxiety about edge cases. So code review would be a transmission problem: did the author's mental state actually get transmitted, or did the signal get lost in cleverness and noise? The uncomfortable part is that Tolstoy thinks art is only good when it transmits morally edifying feelings, which maps weirdly: should we only write code that transmits calm competent vibes, not panic or cleverness for its own sake?

I'm not trying to publish this. Could you give comments as if you were a friend who actually knows philosophy? Specifically: where am I misreading Tolstoy, is the code-as-art analogy doing real work or is it just a stretched analogy, and should I engage with the good-art-transmits-good-feelings part or cut it to keep the essay shorter? I have a bad habit of starting essays and abandoning them so honest feedback helps.
