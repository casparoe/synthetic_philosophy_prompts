ok so I'm in a comparative drug policy course and we just spent two weeks on Portugal, and I've realized I can't actually say what "decriminalization" means even though I use it all the time. I want a working definition I could use to sort a policy into decriminalized / not decriminalized / genuinely borderline, with reasons. Here are the cases I want it to get right, or at least handle honestly:

1. Portugal post-2001: using or possessing small amounts of any drug is not a criminal offense, but it's still illegal — you get sent to a dissuasion commission that can impose fines or order someone into treatment. I'd always treated this as the textbook case of decriminalization, but my professor keeps saying it's "depenalization" (penalties removed, prohibition kept) rather than decriminalization proper. So this is now my hardest case.

2. A country that removes criminal penalties for personal possession but keeps sale and manufacture fully criminal. In my head this is just the definition of decriminalization, but maybe I've actually been using the word to mean "decriminalization of use" and silently treating sale as a seperate thing.

3. A city where possession is still a criminal offence on the books, but police are instructed not to arrest or ticket for small amounts. De facto decriminalization, right? But does the "de facto" mean it is a kind of decriminalization, or that it isn't but it looks like one from outside? This seems important because a government can get most of the same results without passing any law.

4. A supervised injection site operating somewhere that possession is technically still criminal but enforcement is suspended inside the site. Similar to 3 but now the non-enforcement is spatial and official.

5. A jurisdiction with a legal, taxed, regulated retail market for cannabis. I want to call this legalization and not decriminalization — so it's definately my clear "no" — but I can't say exactly what makes it a no: the commercial sale, the regulation, the tax, the fact that production is licensed?

6. Drug courts that offer charges dropped after completing treatment. The offence stays criminal the whole time and nobody does time if they comply. Is that decriminalization, or just lenient sentencing wearing a trench coat?

I think the word might be doing at least two jobs at once — one about what the statute says is a crime and one about what the state actually does to people — and that's why the cases won't line up. Also, I'm not sure whether decriminalization is a property of an act, a substance, a person, or a whole policy regime, and maybe it flips depending on which. If you think no single definition can handle all six without being useless, say that and give me the better carving instead. I'd rather be told my question is built on a confusion than get a neat definition that quietly fails on case 3 or 4.
