So I've been put in charge of building some kind of karma/reputation layer for our company's internal wiki, which is currently a graveyard where nobody trusts anything. The idea I keep coming back to is that a vote shouldnt count until the voter actually got value out of the page. Specifically: when you upvote a doc, its just a promise. If within 90 days you actually lean on the doc — you link it in a PR, you click "this saved me during an incident," or it gets surfaced by our internal answer bot and you give the answer a thumbs up — the promise vests and the author gets karma, plus you get a small backer bonus, bigger if you were one of the first five to promise. If you never use it the promise expires and nothing happens. And if a doc piles up a lot of expired promises, I'm thinking that should count against the author's signal ratio somehow. Karma then decides whose docs rank high in search and who gets edit-approval rights on the most-read pages. I'm a backend engineer, not a designer or anything, so I want someone to walk me through what this thing actually does. What does it incentivize authors to write, voters to promise, and people to pretend to use? Where does it get gamed first? Does it actually select for docs that turn out to be useful later, or for docs that are just easy to make look useful later? And if the failure modes are bad, is there a tweak that keeps the "karma only from realized value" core without gutting it?
