I am a doctoral student in political theory, and I have been working on the way ideals of self-sufficiency are invoked at different scales. I would like you to write an essay on the following controversy.

The ideal of autarky has been defended both for persons and for nations, but the parallel is rarely examined closely enough. For persons, the ideal is roughly that a flourishing or virtuous life is one in which one depends as little as possible on others; for nations, it is roughly that a political community should aim to meet its material and political needs from its own resources rather than through trade, aid, or alliance. One view is that these two ideals fail for quite distinct reasons: personal autarky fails because human agency is constitutively dependent on nurture, language, and social recognition, while national autarky fails for more contingent economic and geopolitical reasons concerning comparative advantage, the costs of closure, and the brittleness of isolated economies. The opposing view is that this is too neat a division. On this view, personal and national autarky share a deeper common flaw: perhaps a fantasy of control, a denial of vulnerability, a confusion between independence and self-governance, or a mistaken identification of dependence with domination.

Weigh these positions carefully and defend a conclusion about whether personal and national autarky are best understood as one ideal with one characteristic failure, two ideals with two different failures, or something in between. Do not remain agnostic: the essay should reach a definite verdict and justify it by considering the strongest case for the opposing view.

After the essay, briefly state how confident you are in the overall conclusion and identify the single point you are least sure about.
