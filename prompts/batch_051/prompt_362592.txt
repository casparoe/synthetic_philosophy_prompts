Okay so I've been reading Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's Respuesta a Sor Filotea, and theres this bit where she describes how, as a kid, she would cut off her hair every time she failed to learn something in the time she'd set for herself, because she thought it didnt make sense for her head to have hair if it lacked knowledge. She treats learning almost like a moral duty, like being ignorant is something you should feel ashamed of. I'm a volunteer tutor at an after-school program, and one of my students, Marisol, is fifteen. Her parents keep pulling her out of activities whenever she gets a B, saying if she's not naturally brilliant she should spend her time helping at home instead. My co-tutor says Sor Juana's self-discipline is the right model: you push yourself hard, you treat intellectual failure as shameful, and thats exactly what girls like Marisol need to hear. I dont know though. It seems to me Sor Juana's strategy worked for her but might just be internalizing the same brutal standard thats already crushing Marisol. Is there a defensible line between taking your education seriously the way Sor Juana did, and just making yourself miserable over grades? I keep going back and forth.
