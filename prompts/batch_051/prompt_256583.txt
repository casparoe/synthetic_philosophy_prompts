So I just got out of a two-hour argument with my friend about this, and I'm stuck. I've been attracted to a view he calls lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. The idea is: there's some threshold of suffering intensity, and anything above it has lexical priority over everything below it. That means no amount of below-threshold pain relief, and no amount of pleasure, can justify allowing even a small amount of above-threshold suffering. I like the view because it gives a clean answer to the classic cases: it says you can't torture one person to give a billion people a nice afternoon, but it also doesn't say a pinprick outweighs a lifetime of mild contentment, because the pinprick sits below the line and can be traded off normally.

But here's the thing my friend kept pushing, and it genuinely got to me. The threshold has to fall somewhere. Suppose it's at intensity 10, whatever that means. Then a person at 9.9 is in the commensurable zone: their pain can be weighed against pleasures and lesser pains. A person at 10.1 is in the lexical zone: their pain outranks literally everything below the line. I have a very strong intuition that this is wrong. The difference between 9.9 and 10.1 just can't carry that much moral weight. It feels like a sorites line: shave one unit off and suddenly we're in a completely different moral universe. I cant make myself believe that one person at 10.1 should outweigh a billion people at 9.9, but the view seems to say exactly that (or at least, it says the 10.1 has priority, full stop).

I dont want someone to just tell me the threshold is vague and call it a day, because that feels like it gives up the clean decision procedure I liked. Walk me through what it would actually cost to keep the lexical idea but make the line vague, what it would cost to abandon the lexical structure and replace the threshold with a steep continuous weighting instead, and what it would cost to keep the sharp threshold and explain away my 9.9/10.1 intuition as a bias I should stop trusting. Give each move its strongest version, then tell me which I should do and whether there's a version where the theory and the intuition both survive. Keep it under 500 words.
