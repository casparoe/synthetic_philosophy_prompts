I keep running into this thing with hidden tendencies or capacities and I dont know what to call it. Example one: a live wire hooked to a safety cut-out that switches the current off the instant anything touches it. The wire is live right now — it has the capacity to deliver a shock — but if you touched it, it wouldnt shock you, because the touch itself would cut the power before any shock could happen. So the very test that should reveal the capacity would remove the capacity it is testing for. Example two: a fragile glass packed in foam. Drop the box and the glass doesn't break, but the fragility hasn't gone anywhere — the foam is just in the way, absorbing the impact. Here the capacity is still there during the test, but something else stops it showing up.

I suspect these are two different ways a capacity can fail to show itself, and that someone in philosophy has carved them apart before. But I don't just want the names. I want to know how you tell them apart in a hard case: has the capacity been abolished by its own trigger, or is it still there but blocked? Does the answer shift depending on where you draw the boundary of the object or system (is the cut-out switch part of the wire's setup or not)? Can one and the same case be correctly described either way just by changing that boundary? If the two cases have established names, give me those and then do the cutting; if not, tell me how you'd mark the line.
