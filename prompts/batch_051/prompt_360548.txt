I was reading one of those "your brain is bad at money" explainers, and its summary of the Allais paradox set off a small fight in my friend group. The article said: "Expected utility theory says people pick between gambles by multiplying each outcome's probability by its utility and taking the highest total. Allais showed this is wrong as a description of how people actually decide. Offered a guaranteed $1 million versus an 89% chance of $1 million, a 10% chance of $5 million, and a 1% chance of nothing, most people take the guarantee. But offered a 10% chance of $5 million versus an 11% chance of $1 million, the same people mostly take the 10% shot at $5 million. That's the paradox: those two choices violate the independence axiom, which says an 89% chance of $1 million is an 89% chance of $1 million and should cancel out when it's common to both options. Because people only flip once the sure thing gets swapped for an 89% chance of nothing, Allais proved that people are irrational about small-probability events, overweighting the 1% chance of going home with zero."
