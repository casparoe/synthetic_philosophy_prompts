Epistemic status: I hold the following view with moderate confidence, and I want it pressure-tested rather than defended.

The view starts from computational irreducibility — the idea, most associated with Stephen Wolfram, that for many systems there is no shortcut to predicting their behavior other than running the system itself step by step. I take this to imply that a computationally irreducible system cannot be given a scientific explanation in the strong sense: no theory substantially shorter than the system's own evolution can derive its future states, so the best we can do is simulate the system and watch. Explanation, on my view, is compression, and irreducibility is the point where compression fails.

I want to know what would count against this position. Which observations, formal results, or methodological developments should make me abandon it, weaken it, or redraw its boundaries? Please sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of the position would themselves concede as counting against it rather than dismiss as beside the point.

In particular, give me your honest assessment of the following pressure points. Does the success of coarse-grained descriptions — statistical mechanics, renormalization-group methods, effective theories — already refute the strong version, or does it only show that many systems are reducible at some scales while leaving the core claim intact? Would a machine-learning model that predicts a system's behavior many steps ahead, without running the system and without yielding an interpretable compressed theory, count as a defeat, or does it merely relocate the question to whether the trained model is itself a shorter computation? Is the view in danger of being insulated into unfalsifiability — for example, if every successful compression can be dismissed by saying the system was not really irreducible? If it is so insulated, say whether that is a problem for a view of this kind or a defensible boundary condition. Finally, say which escape routes from the view are live rather than merely logically possible, and what the world would have to look like for each.
