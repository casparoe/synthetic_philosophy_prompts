I keep having this argument with a friend who says survival of the fittest is an empty slogan: it just means the survivors survive, because the only way to tell who was fit is looking at who's left. He says evolution therefore doesnt explain anything; it just tells a story after the fact. I know this is the kind of thing Popper apparently worried about, though I'm fuzzy on the details. But it seems to me you could predict beforehand which organisms will do well from their traits and the environment, and that would show fitness isnt just defined by survival. So I want to know how to actually test this. Give me the strongest concrete test or observation you can think of that would distinguish natural selection from a tautology. Maybe a controlled population with a new stressor, where we write down in advance which traits should spread and why. Tell me what result would push each way, and then be honest about what even the best possible result would leave unsettled.
