I've been trying to sort statements into three boxes—lying, bullshitting, and honest error—where by bullshitting I mean roughly: saying things without regard for whether they're true, not caring about the truth the way a liar has to. My gut rulings are coming out inconsistent and I want a principle, or a few candidate principles.

A politician who knows the election was fair and says it was stolen: lying, full stop. A pundit who says the same thing because its what his audience wants to hear and who has never checked and would rather not know: bullshitting, not lying. A student who writes "Williams (2019)" with a made-up citation because the sentence needs a reference and she figures no one will check: I want to call that bullshitting too, but she did know there was no Williams paper, so shouldn't that just be lying? It doesnt feel like lying though—she wasn't trying to get anyone to believe something about Williams, she just didn't care whether the citation was real. A doctor who tells a patient "your odds are excellent" without knowing, because he believes hope improves outcomes: bullshit, but I don't resent him for it. A friend who says the party's at 8 when he has no idea and just guessed: also bullshit, but that barely seems blameworthy. An LLM that confidently invents a drug interaction and fake journal names when asked a medical question: I don't want to call it lying, because there's no intention, but "honest error" feels too clean, since the model didn't check and miss—it seems structurally indifferent to whether the answer says anything true. But bullshitting in the ordinary sense is about the speaker not caring, and a language model can't care about anything. So maybe the machine case isn't bullshit, just error with a confident tone.

Can you give me a precise way of defining lying, bullshitting, and error that captures most of these verdicts, and tell me which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and has to overturn? Then apply it to the LLM case: should we say LLM hallucination is lying, bullshit, error, or a fourth thing—something that produces text with no regard for truth but has no mind that could be indifferent?
