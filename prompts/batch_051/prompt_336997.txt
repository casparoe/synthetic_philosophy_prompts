Since I retired from a career in physical chemistry and began reading moral philosophy, I have become preoccupied with a pattern I cannot yet name. In the first of two incidents, a manufacturer ships a device that is dangerous under foreseeable misuse. The warning label was drafted by a regulatory affairs employee on instructions from a lawyer; the risk estimate was produced by analysts who never saw the finished design; and the decision to ship was made by a committee whose members each approved only the fragment relevant to their own office. No single person held the whole picture, and each person seems, from inside the role, to have done exactly what the role required. After the accident, the company issues a statement of collective regret and sets up a compensation fund. In the second incident, a bank breaches regulations across many branches, but the practices grow up as local workarounds that senior management never explicitly authorized and that junior staff believe were tolerated. The common thread is this: responsibility is spread across an organizational structure so that it cannot be attributed to any individual, and then, at the moment when blame must land somewhere, the organization itself acquires a voice—regret, acceptance of liability, promises of reform—precisely because no individual voice can plausibly be blamed.

I am trying to determine whether this configuration has an established name. I suspect there is a known phrase for the idea that many people each doing their job can jointly produce wrongdoing, but the phenomenon I am after is narrower: it is the way the collective becomes the bearer of blame only after the structure has made individual blame impossible, as if the corporation were a reservoir for responsibility rather than an agent. What are the nearest established concepts, how exactly does what I am describing differ from each, and which fits best? Does this pattern strengthen the case for treating a company as a blameworthy moral agent, or does it instead show that corporate blame is largely a device for protecting individuals from blame? I am not after a bare label; I want the boundaries between this phenomenon and its neighbors drawn carefully.
