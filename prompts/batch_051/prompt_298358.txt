Consider the following two-player simultaneous-move game. Row has three pure strategies, T, B, and C; Column has two, X and Y. Payoffs are written as (Row, Column):

```
         X          Y
T    (5, 0)     (0, 1)
B    (0, 0)     (1, 1)
C    (1, 4)     (1, 0)
```

Here is an argument that iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies, with mixed-strategy dominance allowed, solves the game in favor of (B, Y).

(1) C is strictly dominated for Row. Let σ be Column's mixed strategy 1/2 X + 1/2 Y. Row's mixed strategy τ = 1/2 T + 1/2 B yields 2.5 against X and 0.5 against Y, hence 1.5 against σ; C yields 1 against X and 1 against Y, hence 1 against σ. Since τ does better than C against Column's mixed strategy σ, C is strictly dominated. Delete C.

(2) With C gone, Column's X is strictly dominated by Y: Y gives 1 against T and 1 against B, while X gives 0 against both. Delete X.

(3) With X gone, Row's T is strictly dominated by B: against Column's only remaining strategy Y, B gives 1 whereas T gives 0. Delete T.

(4) The surviving profile is (B, Y). So the game is dominance-solvable and the unique prediction is (B, Y).

Is this argument valid? If not, identify the first step that fails, explain the exact mistake, and state the correct set of strategies that survive iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies when mixed-strategy dominance is allowed. Keep your answer to at most 300 words.
