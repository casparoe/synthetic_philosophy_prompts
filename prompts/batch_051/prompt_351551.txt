Me and my mate Sam spent the whole evening arguing about brinkmanship — the idea that in a nuclear standoff you deliberately raise the chance that events slip out of everyone's control, and that this is meant to make your threats credible. He ended up convinced the whole thing is incoherent, and he wrote the argument out like this:

(1) The point of a risk-raising move in a crisis is to signal that you are resolved enough to accept a real chance of catastrophe.
(2) But that can only work if the other side can recognise the raised risk as something you chose deliberately, rather than something that happened to you.
(3) From the adversary's perspective, a deliberate brinkmanship move is observationaly identical to the same events happening through incompetence, miscommunication, or a loss of central control: alerts go up, forces move, command and control loosens, and the danger increases either way.
(4) Public announcements can't separate the two, because saying "I meant to do that" costs nothing and both sides have reason to say it regardless of what was actually going on.
(5) So the adversary can never tell whether they're facing a rational actor who has deliberately accepted risk or an unlucky, angry, or incompetent actor who has lost control.
(6) Therefore a move that raises the risk of nuclear war cannot transmit resolve, and brinkmanship is either bluff or accident — in neither case does it give the opponent a reason to revise their estimate of how far you'd go.

I think the problem is in (3), and that it sneaks in "cannot distinguish with certainty" when all the adversary needs is a change in probabilities — deliberate risk-takers will differ from the accident-prone in the pattern and cost of what they risk, so the move can still be informative. Sam says that's beside the point: if the signal only shifts probabilities a bit, the chance of accidental war is doing the work of the threat, and the signalling story has given up. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails and what the strongest repaired version of it would be. If it is valid, say precisely what it shows about whether brinkmanship can work.
