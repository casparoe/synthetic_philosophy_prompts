I'm a philosophy professor, and this semester I've been rereading Augustine's On Lying (De mendacio) for a graduate seminar on deception. It started as a way to get past the usual Kant-vs.-Mill haze, but a student of mine who works in NLP keeps telling me that people in her lab say language models "lie" whenever they hallucinate, and the standard answer is that they don't lie, they just make stuff up. I'm not sure Augustine would let that distinction do the work people want it to do.

The passage I keep coming back to is from On Lying (De mendacio):

Wherefore, that man lies, who has one thing in his mind and utters another in words, or by signs of whatever kind. Whence also the heart of him who lies is said to be double; that is, there is a double thought: the one, of that thing which he either knows or thinks to be true and does not produce; the other, of that thing which he produces instead thereof, knowing or thinking it to be false. Whence it comes to pass, that he may say a false thing and yet not lie, if he thinks it to be so as he says although it be not so; and, that he may say a true thing, and yet lie, if he thinks it to be false and utters it for true, although in reality it be so as he utters it. For from the sense of his own mind, not from the verity or falsity of the things themselves, is he to be judged to lie or not to lie.

So here's what I'd like to know. If Augustine were dropped into a conversation with my engineering collegues in 2026, how would he analyze a deployed chatbot whose designers know it will sometimes confidently state falsehoods as true? I'm less interested in the obvious point that a language model has no mind in his sense. I want the full taxonomy: would he say the model doesn't lie but the engineers lie through it? Or that the user who passes the output off as knowledge lies? Or wether the whole setup is more like a falsehood he'd classify as error rather than lying, because nobody has the doubled mind he describes? And does his line about someone saying a true thing while lying, judged by the mind rather than the truth of the statement, have a weird payoff here? Please lay out what Augustine, taking the quoted definition seriously, would most likely say about the model, its builders, and its users.
