I run a trust and safety team at a platform that handles both text and nested media. We are drafting a reusable policy for determining what a piece of content actually says when it combines layers that can conflict: a screenshot of a post, a caption added to an image, a video with overlaid commentary, a forwarded message with a new introduction. I want to use the frame/outer/inner message distinction that I associate with Hofstadter's GEB. In that vocabulary, the frame message tells a recipient what kind of thing is being decoded (a joke, a threat, a quotation, a forgery), the outer message tells the recipient how to decode the inner message (for example, read the following as satire, or treat this as verbatim), and the inner message is the apparent content. Write a procedure our reviewers can use to assign operative meaning to a specific layer when applying our rules. The reviewers are not trained in the theory and have about five minutes per item; the stakes include account suspension and content removal. The procedure should say which layer controls by default, what to do when an outer message is absent or only implicit, when a frame is intentionally misleading, and how to handle a case where the inner message conflicts with the outer message. Then run the procedure on three flagged examples: a real photograph with overlaid text asserting the opposite of what the image shows; a screenshot of a deleted post embedded in a new post whose caption supplies the only context; and a manipulated video that would count as satire if introduced as fiction but is posted as if it were documentary. Mark the points where a reviewer must make a judgment call that the procedure cannot make.
