I hold a position I call the symmetry view about physics and free will: indeterminism is no more hospitable to free action than determinism is. The deterministic picture threatens authorship because the outcome was fixed before the agent decided; the indeterministic picture threatens authorship because the outcome is partly a matter of chance. Since neither prior fixity nor chance looks like the agent's own doing, I conclude that settling whether the relevant neural processes are deterministic or genuinely chancy cannot settle the free will debate, and that libertarians who invoke quantum indeterminacy are trading inevitability for arbitrariness. What would count against this position? I would like defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I want them to include, for example: an account on which indeterminism supplies open alternatives that the agent then selects without the chancy event entering into the explanation of the selection; a reason to think chancy neural events can be integrated into authorship by a stable deliberative system rather than amounting to intrusions; and any argument that the luck objection would cut equally against deterministic compatibilism and thereby dissolve the contrast I am relying on. Please distinguish what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers who work on free will, do not present a contested view as the consensus, and flag places where my own formulation may be smuggling in a particular conception of control.
