I've been going in circles on an argument and can't find the bad step. It runs:

(1) The gender-equality paradox: countries that score higher on gender-equality indicators tend not to have more women in STEM; often they have fewer.
(2) The natural reading is that egalitarian countries have removed the economic and legal pressures that pushed women into STEM elsewhere, so the remaining difference reflects what men and women actually want.
(3) If a choice is made under egalitarian institutions, it reflects what the chooser actually wants.
(4) If an outcome reflects what people actually want, a society has no reason of justice to change it.
(5) So persistent gender imbalance in fields like engineering or nursing is not something an egalitarian society should try to correct.

The trouble is the same argument seems fully general: whatever group inequality survives the latest round of egalitarian reform gets reclassified as "what people actually want," and saying "we haven't equalized conditions enough yet" becomes unfalsifiable. I most want to keep both (a) that people's actual choices deserve some respect and (b) that a society can still have reasons to worry about unequal outcomes, but those two pull apart here. Which step breaks, and what do we give up by rejecting it?

Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Also say how confident you are in the diagnosis and which point you're least sure of.
