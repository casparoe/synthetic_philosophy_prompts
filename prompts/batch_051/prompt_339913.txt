I'm in a food ethics class this semester and I'm kind of stuck, and I want to see if my gut reactions on concrete cases actually add up to anything. We've been reading these arguments that if animal suffering matters then a lot of normal eating is hard to justify, and that the convenience-based defense is pretty weak. I dont totally disagree, but I grew up somewhere where "just eat something else" isn't always a real option, and my reactions to cases are all over the place. Here they are.

- Chicken strips at the dining hall when there's a vegetable curry: this feels not fine, or at best barely fine, because I could of had the curry and I just felt like chicken.

- My grandmother cooking a goat for the family when I visit home: fine. I could have eaten beans, but refusing would genuinely hurt her and she already spent money she doesn't really have.

- Buying the cheap battery-cage eggs near campus because the free-range ones are twice the price: I think it's fine, but I feel gross about it, because I could afford the free-range ones if I gave up something else.

- Fish from the guy who fishes the lake near my hometown and sells it out of a cooler: fine. The same fish in a supermarket here, farmed in crowded tanks: not fine, even though the fish probably suffers either way. I can't fully explain why these feel different.

- Beef at a wedding in my home country where the hosts aren't well off and the cow was the center of the meal: fine. Buying a steak at a restaurant here when the pasta is right there: not fine.

- I feed my dog meat-based food and don't feel bad, or not much, even though that means a lot of animals are raised and killed for him.

- The one I keep going back and forth on: the free chicken wrap day at the student center. Same factory-farm supply as the dining hall, it gets thrown away if no one takes it, and it's free. My gut says eating it is fine — but that seems to wreck whatever principle gets the first case, since this is even more avoidable than the dining hall strips and I'm still eating it.

My half-baked theory is something like: the harm is okay when avoiding it would cost someone else something real (my gran's effort, the wedding hosts' money, the fisher's livelihood) or when I'd be giving up something I actually need, but not when it's just about my convenience or taste. I don't know if that's just me rationalizing. The dog case doesnt fit it at all, and maybe the cheap eggs don't either.

I'm not asking you to tell me the correct ethics of eating animals. What I want is: is there a principle, or a small list of candidate principles, that captures most of my verdicts? State it precisely and say which case motivates each clause. Then tell me what it says about the free wrap and about a case I didn't list: buying cow's-milk formula for a baby when breastfeeding isn't possible and soy formula is hard to get and expensive, plus any other cases you think would test it. And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate and would have to go.
