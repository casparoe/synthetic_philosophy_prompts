Here's a homemade distinction I keep reaching for and want stress-tested. People usually talk about temperance as one of two things: prudential, where you override a desire because it would cost you something you want more (skip the third drink so tomorrow's run goes well), or repressive, where you override a desire because the desire itself feels bad or shameful (skip the third drink because wanting it seems ugly). But I think there's a third thing being praised most of the time, and I've been calling it 'agenda temperance.' In agenda temperance the desire never becomes a live option at all. You don't refuse the third drink; by the time it would have been possible, the question isn't open. It's like how a longtime vegetarian at a barbecue isn't resisting the ribs — they just arent on the menu in some prior sense. No episode of self-control happens, because there's no felt clash.

The central claim: this isnt just quick prudence or inner repression. It's a standing shape to what presents itself to you as an option. That makes it a third kind, because prudence and repression both presuppose the desire showed up and had to be dealt with; agenda temperance is what keeps it from showing up with that weight in the first place.

Where I worry it breaks: if no clash and no moment of control ever occurs, why is it a virtue instead of just a lucky temperament or an environment you drifted into? A person whose desires never misbehave seems to have good luck, not good character. But the opposite move — insisting the temperate person has to feel the pull and refuse it — just turns temperance back into continence, struggling and winning, which I'd say is a different and lesser thing.

So: is 'agenda temperance' a real third kind, or does it collapse into precommitment, situationism-style self-management, or simply having agreeable desires? What does it wrongly count as temperance? And what's the strongest single objection to treating it as virtuous at all — is it fatal, or can the idea be patched?
