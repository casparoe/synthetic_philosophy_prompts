I teach a seminar on consciousness and philosophy of mind, and we keep getting stuck on one old question, so I want to think it through a bit more carefully. The question is wether dreams are experiences — that is, whether there's something it is like to be dreaming while you're asleep, or whether a dream is just a narrative you find yourself with on waking, with nothing actually experienced during the night. A colleague of mine is a Malcolm-type skeptic: he says the only evidence we ever have is the waking report, nobody can report a dream while it is happening, and so there's no way to tell the difference between a dream being genuinely experienced in sleep and a story being generated at the moment of waking. His favorite version is Dennett's cassette theory, where the whole narrative gets loaded into memory at waking, like inserting a cassette, and then we mistake it for a memory of an experience.

I'm inclined to think it's an empirical question and can be tested, while he thinks it's a pseudo-question. Suppose I wanted to actually test whether at least some dream content is being experienced during sleep rather than confabulated at waking. What would the best test look like, and what result would count as evidence for the during-sleep view and what would count against? I know about lucid dreamers who can signal with eye movements, and about REM sleep correlating with reported content; how far do those get us, and what exactly would a positive result settle? Could a positive result still be consistent with the cassette story, and if so what does the test actually test? And how much of the dispute is really about what we mean by experience, so that no test could settle that residue?
