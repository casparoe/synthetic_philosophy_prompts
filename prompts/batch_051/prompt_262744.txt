Me and my friend spent most of last night arguing about what makes a ground of discrimination suspect, and somewhere in there I realized I’ve been running a private shortcut I never put into words. When someone says group X should be protected (or should’nt be), I immediately check whether X is basically immutable or at least really costly to change. Race, sex, disability, age: presumptively suspect, full stop. Accent, citizenship, fashion sense, sports fandom: not immutable, so discrimination there doesn’t automatically feel as serious to me, even when it’s ugly. My friend tore into this. He pointed out religion is a paradigm protected characteristic even though people do convert, while some pretty immutable traits (handedness, blood type, being tone-deaf) have never been protected and probably shouldn’t be. He also said my test is smuggling in a norm—that nobody should have to alter a central part of their identity to get equal treatment—and that norm, not immutability per se, is doing all the actual work. I half buy it, but I don’t want to abandon a heuristic that works most of the time just because it has edge cases.

So: is my immutability shortcut defensible as a rough screen for which discrimination claims deserve suspicion, or is it a bias dressed up as a rule? Please don’t just answer yes or no. Treat it as a policy: say where it’s reliable, where it breaks, whether it can be restated to keep the benefits without the costs, and whether someone like me (a non-lawyer forming everyday judgments, not a court) is entitled to use it. I’m not asking for case law or a reading list, just the underlying reasoning.
