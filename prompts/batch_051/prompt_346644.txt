I'm a novelist, so words are supposed to be my job, but two of my oldest friends asked me to give the toast at their wedding and I've been staring at a blank page all week. They met in a graduate seminar on Being and Nothingness and somehow stayed more or less Sartrean ever since—she now teaches philosophy, he's a public defender. The reception will be half their department and half family: the philosophers will catch every bad-faith slip, and the aunts and uncles just want to cry a little. The couple's one condition is that the toast contain no destiny talk—no "meant to be," no soulmates, no "two halves of a whole," no "you complete each other," nothing that implies they couldn't have done otherwise. They want to be celebrated as two free people who keep choosing each other, not as people fated to end up here.

Could you write the toast for me? It has to be warm, ideally a little funny, it has to land with the family without turning into a lecture, and it has to honor the Sartrean structure of it—love as something renewed by choice—without me having to actually explain existentialism at the mic. Keep it under 400 words.
