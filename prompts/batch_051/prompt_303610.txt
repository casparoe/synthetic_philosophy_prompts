Was up too late last night argueing with a friend about whether karma works as a theory of moral desert, and I can't shake a parallel I noticed. Karma seems to require a conservation law for moral value: no good or bad act is ever just lost; its stored as merit or demerit, carried across lifetimes, and paid out later. That's the same shape as a conserved quantity in physics — the total stays fixed because the laws have symmetries and the system is closed. Is this a real structural identity, or just an accounting metaphor? If it's real, doesn't it force a karma-believer to say the moral universe is closed the way a physical system has to be, and wouldn't it predict things like moral value being additive or transferable that karma theorists actually deny?
