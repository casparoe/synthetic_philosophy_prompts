I'm a philosophy PhD student working on consciousness, and I keep coming back to the Malcolm/Dennett exchange on whether dreams are experiences because it feels stuck in a distinctive way. Malcolm's line is that all we ever have are waking reports, so there's nothing that licenses reading a conscious episode back into the sleeping brain; Dennett's cassette thought seems to get him to a similar place by a different route — if the whole narrative could be laid down at the moment of waking, you never need to posit that anything was actually experienced during the night — and then the two positions just kinda sit there. My working diagnosis is that the disagreement isn't really about dreams or memory storage at all, but about what could ever count as evidence for phenomenal consciousness when the only access is retrospective and verbal. Is that right, or is there a substantive empirical question buried in it (e.g. about when dream memories get laid down or what sleep-stage data show)? And given the diagnosis, is there anything that would actually move the debate, or is the honest upshot that the question should be dissolved rather than answered? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the literature.
