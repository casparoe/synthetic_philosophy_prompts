I'm in a world history class and we just finished a unit on the causes of World War I. The teacher mentioned almost as an aside that Franz Ferdinand's driver took a wrong turn and stopped the car basically right in front of Gavrilo Princip, and Princip only got that chance because the earlier assassination attempt that morning had already failed. That sent me down a rabbit hole about chaos theory and the butterfly effect, and I ended up staying up way to late typing this argument into my notes app. The conclusion seems to strong, but I can't find exactly where the argument breaks.

Here it is:

(1) In a chaotic system, a tiny difference in how things start can produce a completely different outcome later (the butterfly effect).
(2) Human history is full of moments like that wrong turn in Sarajevo, or a delayed order, or an unexpected storm, where something tiny decided something enormous.
(3) In those cases, if the tiny thing had gone differently, the big event would have gone differently, or maybe not happened at all.
(4) The tiny things involved (a wrong turn, a delayed message, a storm) are the kind of thing that could easily have gone the other way; nothing made them inevitable.
(5) So the big events were extremely fragile.
(6) But if a big event hangs on a tiny, easily-different trigger like that, then the big structural explanations in textbooks—alliances, nationalism, economic rivalry—don't actually explain why the event happened; they just describe the background the event happened against.
(7) So a lot of the standard "why did X happen" explanations in history are basically bogus.

I actually want to keep (1) through (5). The chaos theory part seems right, and the Sarajevo example really does look like it hinged on one wrong turn. But (7) would make the whole causes-of-WWI unit kind of pointless, and that seems like too high a price. So I'm stuck. Can you tell me exactly where the argument goes wrong, if it goes wrong? And if I have to choose between "big historical events are fragile" and "structural explanations still genuinely explain things," what am I actually choosing between?
