I'm in a seminar on obligations to future people, and the professor never states a theory, she just hands down verdicts, so I've been keeping a running log. So far the log is: (1) a chemical company dumps effluent into a river; a girl, Lina, is born nearby a year later and develops a chronic kidney problem traceable to the dumping. If the company had never dumped, Lina herself would have been conceived a couple years later and born healthy. Professor's verdict: the company wronged her. (2) A couple decides not to have a second child because it would wreck their careers; the child would have had a good but fairly ordinary life. Verdict: not wrong. (3) A city has to choose between two flood-control plans, same cost and same safety, but the plans affect migration and family formation differently, so the people who exist in forty years under Plan A are not the same individuals as under Plan B. Under Plan A their lives would be somewhat better, on average, than under Plan B. The city picks Plan A. Verdict: the city did nothing wrong — though she said she'd have been disappointed if it had picked B, since the future residents would have had worse lives even though they'd have been different people. (4) A parent repeatedly skips a needed vaccination for an existing child, and the child contracts a serious but nonfatal illness. Verdict: wrong. Next week she rules on this one: a woman has a temporary infection; if she conceives this month, the child will be born with a moderate cognitive impairment but will still have a life well worth living, and if she waits two months until the infection clears she will conceive a different child without the impairment. I genuinely can't tell wether she will say wrong or not wrong — cases (1) and (4) make her sound like she only cares about some determinate person being made worse off, but her aside in (3) makes me think she might also treat a lower-quality future as a wrong in itself. Predict her verdict; lay out the rules that would each make sense of her record so far; say which one you'd bet on and why; and tell me what further case, different from this one, would most cleanly settle whether she really is running the rule you think she is.
