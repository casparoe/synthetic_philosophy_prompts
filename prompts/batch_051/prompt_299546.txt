I follow a strict rule when assigning priors. If my information constrains only aggregate features of a problem — for instance, last week I knew only that a bounded quantity had a particular mean — I choose the probability distribution that maximizes Shannon entropy among those consistent with the constraints, reasoning that any distribution with lower entropy would encode information I do not actually have. I continue to apply the rule even after noticing that a non-linear redescription of the same variable generally changes which distribution maximizes entropy, so the prior appears to be tracking my choice of coordinates rather than my evidence. Is this policy defensible, or is it a heuristic that mistakes the absence of information for a license to invent a uniquely rational prior? If some version of it is defensible, state the exact principle that would justify treating maximum entropy as rationally mandatory, and say where the policy fails.
