I'm preparing a staged interview with John Stuart Mill for a series on nineteenth-century liberal thought and empire. The centerpiece will be chapter XVIII of Considerations on Representative Government, where Mill defends the government of dependencies by a free state. I want to move past the familiar summaries—the distinction between settler colonies and non-settler dependencies, the claim that representative institutions would be premature for India—and press him on a particular step in the argument. Mill maintains there that a free state may govern a dependency despotically and still be justified, provided it governs in the interests of the governed and orients its rule toward their eventual improvement. Write me eight to ten interview questions that treat this not as a peripheral embarrassment but as a serious application of his theory of government. In particular, I want the questions to probe: how this sits with the harm principle in On Liberty; what observable conditions, if any, would have counted for him as evidence that India either was or was not ready; how he distinguishes paternal imperial rule from the despotism he otherwise condemns; and whether his criterion of improvement gives the imperial power an incentive to keep the standard of readiness perpetually receding rather than to declare success. For each question, include the follow-up keyed to the answer he most likely gives, so that he cannot retreat into the claim that this is merely a special case.
