I'm putting together a discussion section on distributive justice, and I want to hand my students something that makes Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument stick without the usual libertarian-vs-egalitarian shouting match. Could you write a deadpan parody of a terms-of-service agreement for a made-up startup, maybe called ChainTitle or whatever you like, whose business is certifying that a person's wealth is just under Nozick's entitlement theory. Onboarding walks you through justice in acquisition and justice in transfer, and then their's a rectification section. Around 650 words, formatted as numbered clauses.

The humor has to come from the actual doctrine, not from the startup premise alone. So lean into the difference between historical and patterned principles, the fact that free transfers keep messing up whatever pattern you try to lock in, and above all the thinness of Nozick's account of rectification: he is willing to grant that past injustice can poison current holdings, but the cleanup story is famously vague about how far back you have to look and what you do whent he records don't exist anymore. The rectification clause might promise "good-faith efforts" but cap the lookback at something suspiciously small, and the service should quietly refuse to certify holdings whose chain passes through, say, a conquered kingdom before a certain date. A Nozick reader should find the jokes fair and aimed at a genuine weak spot, not a strawman; a newcomer should still get that something is being ironized. Keep it deadpan all the way—no final paragraph explaining the joke.
