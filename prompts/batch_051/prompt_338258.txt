I cover the governance of digital speech for a publication aimed at a scientifically literate general audience, and I want to work through a clean institutional counterfactual rather than survey the law as it stands. At present, a single post can be lawful where it was posted and unlawful where it is read, and states assert criminal, civil, and administrative jurisdiction over foreign speech under several different doctrines. Suppose that in 2030, after a bruising round of reciprocal blocking and takedown orders, a treaty with broad adherence among the major digital states enters into force: the Speech Location Protocol. Its rule is that the lawfulness of any piece of digital expression—a post, a comment, a video, a livestream—is determined exclusively by the law of the state in which the speaker is physically present at the moment of first publication. States parties may not apply their own content rules to someone located abroad at the time of posting, and a platform is immune from liability and from state removal orders for content that was lawful where the speaker was located, provided the platform verifies and records the speaker's location at posting.

Walk me through the next fifteen years. Over that period, how does the online speech ecosystem reorganize? I need an argued prediction, not a story: which jurisdictions become havens for which kinds of speech, and what makes some countries likely net exporters or net importers of legal risk; how platforms redesign moderation, terms of service, and the verification of a poster's physical location; how the race between location spoofing and location attestation plays out, including VPNs, GPS spoofing, rented or borrowed physical presence, and markets for verifiable speech residence; which categories of speech—defamation, hate speech, intimate-image abuse, incitement, copyright-adjacent expression, state-secrets material—become practically unregulable and which remain reachable through other doctrines, private platform rules, or charges about distribution rather than publication; and how states that currently police foreign content adapt their enforcement outside the protocol.

Make your confidence explicit at each link in the chain. Which outcomes are robust consequences of the incentives, and which depend on contestable assumptions about platform behavior, user migration, or enforcement capacity? Finish by describing two or three conditions that, if they failed, would cause the prediction to unravel.
