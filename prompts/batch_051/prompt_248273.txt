I work in the planetary protection office at a space agency, and roughly twice a month we get an instrument readout or a returned sample that makes us ask whether we're looking at something alive. A wrong call either triggers a containment scare or lets something through quarantine, so the stakes are high and we usually have to decide in a day or two with incomplete data. The textbook lists of criteria (metabolism, growth, reproduction, response, heredity, etc.) aren't enough because real candidates fail one or two of them, and half my team just defaults to the definition of life as a self-sustaining chemical system capable of evolving by natural selection, which sounds good but is hard to apply at 3pm on a Tuesday. I want a reusable decision procedure I can actually run: questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure returns a definite no, and where it hands the decision to a human because the issue is values rather than facts. Then run it on these three candidates: (a) an encapsidated RNA sequence that can't metabolize outside a host cell but mutates fast inside one, (b) a bacterial endospore that is metabolically inert and not reproducing but will revive under the right conditions, and (c) a synthetic droplet that maintains an ion gradient and grows but has no hereditary material at all. I especially want to see the procedure give an answer my office wont like in one of these cases, and I want you to say honestly what the procedure cant settle — e.g. whether something gets quarantined even if it doesn't count as alive.
