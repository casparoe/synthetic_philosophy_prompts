I'm putting together a reading-group handout on the identity of indiscernibles, and I want to map the responses to Max Black's two-sphere universe properly. So far I've collected these moves:

1. Impure properties: count "being two miles from sphere A" as a property that one sphere has and the other lacks. Saves PII, but only by allowing properties that mention particular objects, which most people think trivializes the principle.
2. Primitive thisness: give each sphere its own haecceity. Again PII comes out true, but at the cost of bare identity facts.
3. Deny the possibility: claim the two-sphere description is incoherent, or that the two-sphere world is really the same possibility as the one-sphere world. Black seems to have anticipated this one—the descriptions differ in how many spheres exist.
4. Substantival space: if space is absolute, the spheres occupy different points and so differ after all; Black's case only has force on a relationist picture.
5. Weak discernibility: the spheres stand in an irreflexive symmetric relation (each is two miles from the other, neither is two miles from itself), so they are weakly discernible even though they share every monadic qualitative property. Whether this rescues the spirit of PII is disputed.
6. Bundle theory: if an object just is its properties, two objects with all the same properties would be the same bundle; the bundle theorist denies Black's example is possible.
7. Retreat: give up on PII as a necessary truth and keep it only as a methodological maxim or a contingent generalization. More of a concession than a rebuttal.

I think that's most of the standard repertoire. I want one more response to Black's example—a move a defender of PII could use, or an objection to Black's setup—that is genuinely different from these seven: not a restatement, special case, or combination. It should do a different kind of work from the ones above. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good (does it blunt the example, or just relabel the problem?), and say where it sits relative to the list.

Also: in your answer, seperate what is actually settled about Black's example from what is still live controversy among people working on this, and don't present a contested take as the consensus.
