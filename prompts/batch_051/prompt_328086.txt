I want to think carefully about adequacy conditions before comparing formal systems. Let a paraconsistent logic be one in which contradictory premises do not entail every sentence: A, ¬A ⊬ B. A dialetheist needs such a logic because she holds that at least one contradiction is true—the Liar sentence, for example—and she does not want that commitment to trivialize her theory. But that is only the beginning. I want the full list of desiderata for a paraconsistent logic adequate to serve dialetheism: what the logic must get right, and why each item is a requirement rather than a nice feature.

Make sure your list addresses at least these pressures, and for each entry say whether it is a non-negotiable condition of adequacy or merely a preference of one school. (i) Explosion must fail, yet as much ordinary reasoning as possible should survive; since any logic with addition (A ⊢ A ∨ B) and disjunctive syllogism will explode, something familiar has to go—what is the least costly sacrifice, and what would preserving modus ponens for a genuine conditional cost? (ii) The logic should admit a transparent truth predicate: T⟨A⟩ and A should be freely intersubstitutable. (iii) It must give a non-trivial treatment of the Curry sentence—"If this very sentence is true, then 0 = 1"—given that Curry threatens to derive an arbitrary conclusion from the truth schema, a detachable conditional, and the structural rule of contraction, all without ever using explosion; a candidate logic must block Curry without crippling ordinary mathematical proof. (iv) It should say how much classical reasoning is recoverable for consistent domains, sometimes called the recapture problem. (v) It should not drain negation of content: it must say what it is for a contradiction to be true and explain why one true contradiction does not license every inconsistency as acceptable.
