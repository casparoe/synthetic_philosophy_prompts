I'm a novelist, not a ML researcher, so forgive me if this is uses terms sloppily. I'm drafting a novel where a research group builds a language model and trains it on every scientific and technical text published up to midnight on December 31, 1904. Nothing after. Then they feed it the state of physics as of that date—Michelson-Morley, Lorentz's local time, Fitzgerald's contraction, Poincare's relativity principle, the lot—and start interrogating it. The plot hinges on whether it does something genuinely new rather than just rearranging what it has seen, so I want to get the mechanisms right instead of handwaving a eureka.

I've been reading Henri Poincare's Science and Method, the bit in the chapter on mathematical creation where he talks about the unconscious self not merely throwing up heaps of random combinations but somehow sorting them before they surface, selecting the ones with the right harmony and usefulness. He describes it almost as if there's a hidden aesthetic filter doing part of the work below awareness, and the conscious mind only gets the few candidates worth checking. That passage is the lens I want to use.

So the counterfactual is this: suppose the model gets only that pre-1905 corpus and is then asked, from the raw ingredients, whether there might be a single principle from which the contraction, the local-time transformation, and the Michelson-Morley null result all follow without an ether. Predict, with reasoning, what it actually does. Does it mostly summarize Lorentz and Poincare and then stall? Could it articulate something like the special theory of relativity—not necessarily in Einstein's form, but the core idea that the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames and light speed is invariant—even though no training text has put all the pieces together that way? Where in Poincare's account would a token-predicting network need an analogue of that subconscious preselection, and is there any reason to think the geometry of its embedding space could play that role? I want your chain of reasoning, not a yes/no: which moves from Poincare's account transfer to a system that has only text, which do not, and what would have to be true of the training distribution (or the token space) for a genuine leap to occur. Which predictions are you most confident of, and what would a false positive look like in a version where it does not actually have new insight but produces something that looks that way?
