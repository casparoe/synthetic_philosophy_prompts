I've ended up a pretty committed deflationist about truth, more than I ever set out to be. The way I'd put it: 'true' doesn't name a robust property, and 'it is true that p' doesn't ascribe anything to the proposition p—the whole content is just p. The truth predicate is an expressive device: it lets me generalize ('whatever the referee says is true'), endorse a batch of claims at once, or refer back without restating everything. I don't think truth explains anything, and I don't think there's a distinctive norm of truth over and above believing and asserting what's the case. And yes, I've already met the stock objections—the correspondence intuition, the normativity worry, the generalization problem, blind ascriptions, the liar, the conservativeness results, the charge that deflationism smuggles in propositions or satisfaction to do the real work—so they won't shock me, though I do want them in the map anyway.

What I want is a deflationist's exposure map. What would have to be the case—what argument, formal result, observation, or development elsewhere—for this view to be false? Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and flag which ones a sophisticated deflationist would herself concede count against the position, rather than brush off as question-begging. In particular I'd be moved by a place where deflationism can't do a job that truth is supposedly needed for, or a result that shows it collapses into a rival theory. And at the end be honest: has the view been insulated into unfalsifiability, and if so, wether that's a problem for a view of this kind.
