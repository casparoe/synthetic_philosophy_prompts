I keep coming back to a structural resemblance and cannot decide whether it is deep or superficial. Dennett's Multiple Drafts model is built around rejecting what he calls the Cartesian theatre: the idea that consciousness occurs at a privileged site and a privileged instant, where a representation is finally presented to an inner audience. On Dennett's picture, content is fixed in parallel by many specialised processes, there is no single moment at which a candidate representation becomes the conscious one, and which candidate gets promoted depends on what probe the system is subsequently given—a question, a task, a report. Asking exactly when a subject became conscious of a stimulus may therefore be an ill-posed question rather than a question with a hidden answer.

I am struck by how closely this matches distributed revision control and eventual consistency in computing. A Git repository has no master copy whose state changes at one well-defined instant. Each replica carries its own causal history of commits, and what counts as the document's current content is settled only relative to a checkout, merge, or read operation. Vector clocks can recover a partial causal order among updates, but no device recovers the single instant at which the shared document changed, because there is no such instant independent of a chosen read.

So the mapping I would defend is: drafts map to replicas or commits; the Cartesian theatre maps to a centralised master branch; probing maps to reading or merging; and the denial of a well-defined time of consciousness maps to the denial of a global clock that would total-order all updates.

I would like an assessment of whether this is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion. Give the strongest precise version of the mapping, including what the brain's representations and operations would have to be—their analogues of timestamps, causal histories, merge bases, and conflict resolution—for results such as causal consistency, vector clocks, or conflict-free replication to transfer. Then name one prediction the analogy makes that appears to be false on the consciousness side, and say whether that failure is fatal or merely limits the analogy.
