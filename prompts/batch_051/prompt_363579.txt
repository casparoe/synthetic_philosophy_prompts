A student I tutor, L, has so far given the following settled answers about how biological traits arise. She defends all four with confidence.

1. Giraffes. Asked how giraffes came to have long necks, L says that the ancestors kept stretching to reach leaves higher in the trees; the stretching made each individual's neck slightly longer, and this acquired extra length was inherited, so the necks lengthened bit by bit across generations.

2. Bacteria and antibiotics. Asked why a bacterial population becomes resistant after exposure to an antibiotic, L says the bacteria worked out how to withstand the drug and then passed this acquired trick on to their offspring.

3. Cave fish. Asked why a species living in a lightless cave is blind, L says the fish never used their eyes, so the eyes wasted away, and this degeneration was inherited, so later generations were born with increasingly reduced eyes until they were blind.

4. Moths on sooty bark. Asked how a moth population became dark after the tree trunks in its habitat became sooty, L says the individual moths changed colour to match the darkened bark, and their offspring were then born dark.

I ask L whether, over many generations, the population will come to have deeper beaks, and why.
