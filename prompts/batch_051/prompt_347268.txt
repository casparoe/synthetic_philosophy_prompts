Here is a step-by-step argument that the classical theist cannot consistently hold both that God is perfectly loving and that God is impassible.

1. An impassible being is one that cannot be passive with respect to anything outside itself: nothing another being does can bring about an affect in it, and it cannot be pained, grieved, or made sorrowful by another's state.
2. To love someone perfectly is to be affected by that person's wellbeing: when the beloved suffers, perfect love brings about real sorrow or grief in the lover for the beloved's sake.
3. God is perfectly loving by nature. Hence, if any creature whom God loves suffers, God is sorrowful or grieved on account of that creature's suffering.
4. Being sorrowful or grieved on account of another's state is an affect brought about by another, and so entails passibility.
5. Therefore, God is passible.
6. But the classical theist also says that God is impassible.
7. So a perfectly loving and impassible God cannot exist.
