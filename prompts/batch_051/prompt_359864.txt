I'm in a medieval philosophy course and we spent a whole session on Avicenna's flying man, and I left with the feeling that the argument only has bite because one background feature of the subject never gets stated. Quick setup: Avicenna has you imagine a fully formed adult person created all at once, suspended in a void, eyes covered, limbs spread so that nothing touches anything else, so there is no sensory input whatsoever. He says this person would still be aware of their own existence, while not being aware of a body, a limb, or anything external; from that he concludes self-awareness doesn't come through the senses and the self isn't identical to the body. I want a thought experiment built to specification that stress-tests that last step.

Here are the specs. The case has to keep the original created-out-of-nothing, no-sensory-history setup, and keep the deprivation conditions exactly: no current sensation, no access to body or world. You may change exactly one feature of the subject's constitution at the moment of creation — not their past (there is no past), and not by having them grow up embodied; Avicenna would rightly say that changes the test rather than exposing a hidden assumption. The subject must still affirm their own existence in the same thin way Avicenna describes, but the Avincennian can no longer infer from this that the self is distinct from the body. And the reason they can't infer it has to fall out of the feature you changed, not from you stipulating the conclusion or quietly weakening what counts as self-awareness. The case is for isolating whether Avicenna's inference leans on an unargued assumption about how the created subject is constituted.

Then show your work. Describe the subject you create and their self-report. Run Avicenna's inference through the case step by step and say exactly which step the modified feature breaks. Give the deflationary explanation of why the subject still affirms their existence. Identify the feature doing the work, and say what happens if you remove it — does the case collapse back into the original flying man? End with the strongest reply an Avincennian could make against your case. I care less about whether Avicenna was right overall than about whether the case earns its verdict instead of smuggling them in.
