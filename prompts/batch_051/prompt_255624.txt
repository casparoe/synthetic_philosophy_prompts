I'm a philosophy PhD student TAing an undergraduate ethics course, and I'm grading a midterm. The question was: "Kant's Formula of Universal Law is usually said to rule out maxims in two different ways, a contradiction in conception and a contradiction in willing. Explain the distinction, and illustrate each with one of Kant's own examples." Here is one student's answer:

"The two ways match up with perfect and imperfect duties. A contradiction in conception happens when the maxim cannot even be conceived as a universal law of nature. Kant's example is the false promise: if everyone made false promises whenever they needed money, no one would trust a promise, the practice of promising would collapse, and the consequences would be terrible, so the maxim fails. Maxims that fail in this way ground perfect duties, like the duty not to make false promises. A contradiction in willing happens when the maxim can be conceived as a universal law but still cannot be willed. Kant's example is the maxim of never helping others in distress. We can conceive a world in which no one helps anyone, but we cannot will such a world, since we would then be willing away any help we might ourselves need. Such maxims ground imperfect duties, which leave us latitude about whether and how to help."

Please grade this answer out of 20, and give the point-by-point comments that justify the grade.
