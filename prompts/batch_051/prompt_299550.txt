I keep going in circles on a dispute I got into with a friend in ML safety: he says anything short of a worst-case guarantee is morally unserious, and I keep thinking he is demanding something no learning system could ever provide. Here is the position I find myself defending, though I am not at all sure I am right. I think the demand for worst-case guarantees in AI alignment is quietly imported from a kind of pre-theoretical safety ideal, like wanting a bridge that cannot collapse under any conceivable load. But learned systems are not bridges. They are trained on a distribution, their generalization is statistical, and the theoretical guarantees we do have, PAC bounds and the like, are of the form with high probability over the draw of the training data rather than for every possible input and environment. So my working view is that alignment arguments should mostly aim at average-case or high-probability guarantees, explicitly relativized to a specified distribution over tasks, humans, and deployment conditions, and that worst-case talk should be demoted to a stress test you use to expose hidden assumptions, not treated as a genuine success criterion.

What bothers me is that the obvious counterexamples are genuinely strong: adversarial examples, distribution shift, and the fact that for an existential-risk claim the single weirdest failure can be the only one that matters. I can also see a version of my view that is just average-case over the wrong distribution is worthless, which is true but unhelpful, and I do not want to end up with a position that survives only because I get to pick the distribution each time someone pushes back. So what would count against it? I want a commited answer, not a survey: a real map of where this view is most exposed, sorted by how decisive the considerations would be, fatal versus merely awkward versus absorbed with a qualification. And give me an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that is a problem for this kind of view specifically. If you think it is just wrong, say so and rank the reasons.
