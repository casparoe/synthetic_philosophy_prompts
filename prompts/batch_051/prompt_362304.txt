Since I retired from chemistry I've been going along to a local philosophy reading group, and one regular uses 'taxation is theft' as a get-out for any tax policy question. Last week the discussion was whether the top rate of income tax should go up, and he produced the line as if it settled the matter. The phrase seems to slide around depending on what 'theft' is being made to do — sometimes it sounds like a near-definition, sometimes like a serious moral accusation. Could you lay out the distinct precise readings of 'taxation is theft' as a menu, from the almost trivial to the genuinely radical, and say for each whether it's defensible and whether it would actually get him from the slogan to 'so this tax rise is wrong'? I'd rather have a menu than one official interpetation, plus a note on which reading the tax argument actually needs.
