I teach a final-year undergraduate seminar jointly with a colleague, and our sessions on Parfit's teletransporter have reached the same impasse each year. Over the past few terms I have moved towards the Parfitian view that what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, whatever its cause, and that personal identity is not the thing a rational person should care about when stepping into the machine; my colleague holds the further-fact view, on which whether the traveller survives is an all-or-nothing further fact, irreducible to facts about overlapping memories, intentions, and character, and on which the standard teletransporter kills the traveller and produces a replica. We have decided to stop staging the usual debate and instead write a short adversarial-collaboration document that both of us could sign, and I would like you to draft it, with no citations or bibliography, and without adjudicating which of us is right.

Please structure the document in three parts. First, a set of numbered claims about the teletransporter that both camps can accept without hedging, covering the destructive case, the branch-line case in which the original survives the scan but is told they will die shortly afterwards, and a delayed case in which the scan is stored and the replica is assembled only after the original has died of natural causes; the claims should say what each camp predicts the other will say about these cases and should flag any apparent disagreement that seems merely verbal. The agreed claims should be substantive rather than vacuous. Second, a disagreements table in which each side states, in wording it would accept for itself, what it asserts about whether the traveller survives, whether survival can come in degrees, and what a rational traveller ought to care about; separate the rows that mark genuine cruxes from those that dissolve once terms are fixed. Third, a list of cruxes, with the sub-questions that would move at least one side, and, wherever possible, a further case or empirical finding that both camps would accept as bearing on each crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Because the point is a document we can both sign, please also tell me where the draft is most likely to be rejected by one of us, and which argument or consideration would most lead you to move a claim from the agreed section to the disagreement table or the reverse. I would also like to know where your reconstruction of either view is most likely to fail, so that we can read the draft critically.
