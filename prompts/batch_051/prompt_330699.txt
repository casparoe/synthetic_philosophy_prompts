The two camps are (1) the Jeffrey camp, which says that when you glance at a scarf in dim light and your credence that it's green goes from .5 to .7, the rational update is Jeffrey conditionalization over the partition {green, not green}: P_new(H) = sum_i P_old(H | E_i) P_new(E_i). You never become certain of any worldly proposition at all. And (2) the strict-conditionalization camp, which says rational updating is always P_new(H) = P_old(H | E) for some proposition E you become certain of — here, maybe, that you had a visual experience as of a greenish scarf — and that a shift in the partition weights with no certain proposition behind it is a redistribution without a reason.
