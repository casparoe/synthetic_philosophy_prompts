Start from a familiar pattern: with a subject's evidence held fixed, people ascribe knowledge less readily when more practically depends on whether the proposition is true. Pragmatic encroachers, including subject-sensitive invariantists, say that whether one knows really does depend on the subject's stakes; contextualists say the content of the word knows shifts with the attributor's practical context; invariantists say the stakes change only what is assertable or which error possibilities are salient, never whether the subject knows. A persistent obstacle is that raising the stakes in a vignette tends also to affect subjects' sense of the evidence or the relevance of ways things could go wrong. Design the best experimental paradigm you can to pull these three views apart despite that obstacle. Specify your conditions or manipulations, what you would measure, and exactly which pattern of results would favor each view. Conclude by saying what even the most diagnostic possible outcome would leave unsettled about knowledge itself.
