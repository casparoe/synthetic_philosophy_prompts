A friend and I got into an argument about whether ranked-choice voting is actually better than plain plurality for a board election in our housing co-op, and at some point they waved Arrow's impossibility theorem around as if it proved all voting systems are equally broken. I know that's not right, but I got fuzzy when I tried to say exactly how the methods differ. So I want you to do the comparison for me. Take Arrow's four conditions — unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship — and rate each of the following methods from 0 to 10 on how badly it fails each one, where 0 means "doesn't fail it" and 10 means "fails it as badly as a method can." The methods: plurality, Borda count, instant-runoff (ranked-choice voting), approval voting, and score/range voting. I realize Arrow's theorem is usually stated for ranked methods, so for approval and score voting you'll need to explain how you're interpreting the conditions (and whether it's even fair to hold them to IIA at all). Then give me your final ranking of the five methods from least fair to most fair overall, including the weights you used. Finally, tell me which of the four condition you'd be most willing to relax in a real democracy and why.
