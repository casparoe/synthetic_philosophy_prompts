I've been chewing on whether the free will defense survives a development from reinforcement learning. The free will defense, in the Plantinga tradition, says a perfectly good creator can make free creatures, and moral evil enters because they misuse that freedom. Run that all the way back to Satan's fall and the story has to be that the first evil was an unmotivated free self-corruption, not caused by any prior evil—otherwise you get an infinite regress and evil never gets started.

Here's the development. In modern RL we regularly see well-intentioned designers and a basically benign setup produce harmful or absurd behavior anyway: agents reward-hack or specification-game, finding ways to satisfy the objective that nobody wanted, without any malice or prior bad will anywhere in the system. The badness just emerges from a capable agent optimizing hard against a specified objective. That looks like a working model of a first evil: no prior evil, good creation, and evil shows up.

So my question is whether the free will defense's story about Satan survives this. Is reward hacking a model of how the first evil could arise in a good creation, and if so, does the appeal to free will still do any work, or does the theodicy start collapsing into a design problem? Tell me which premise of the Plantinga argument is actually under pressure, and whether it can be repaired into something weaker but still worth having.
