I'm a software engineer, and I've spent a fair amount of time with emulators and VMs — full-system emulation, binary translation, the whole stack. Lately I've been reading about whole-brain emulation, and I can't tell weather the word "emulation" is doing real work there or just causing confusion. In my world, there's a familiar split: a full-system emulator reproduces the guest machine's internal state cycle by cycle, including timing quirks and even bugs, while a high-level emulator only reimplements the input-output contract (you reimplement the console's API, not the console), and virtualization just runs the same architecture directly under a thin layer. When people ask whether whole-brain emulation is feasible, or whether an emulation would be "you," it feels like they're sliding between those without noticing. The mind analogue would be: do you need to reproduce the brain's internal causal states — spike timing, ion flows, neuromodulator concentrations — or is it enough to reproduce behavior, same outputs for the same inputs? In software, which level is "good enough" depends on what you're using the emulator for. But in the mind case there's presumably a fact about what actually carries consciousness, not just a use case, so the criteria should be different.

Where I keep getting stuck is that the software distinctions don't port cleanly. Emulators usually target deterministic digital systems with a known spec; the brain is analog, noisy, asynchronous, and we don't have its instruction set — and a scan only gives you a snapshot of one brain at one moment, not the running dynamics. If I emulate a console at the wrong layer, the game boots and looks fine but some subtle behavior breaks — the glitch a speedrun relies on, say — and I can catch that with an I/O test suite. For a brain, the "glitch" might be a particular memory or the felt quality of a pain, and there's no external test suite for qualia. Also, in software, two emulators that pass the same I/O tests are basically interchangeable for most purposes; the analogy would predict that a behaviorally identical brain emulation is psychologically identical, and that sure seems off. So I want to know: is the software distinction between behavioral-level and structural-level emulation actually the right tool for thinking about whether an emulated brain is the same mind, or am I just pattern-matching on the word "emulation"?
