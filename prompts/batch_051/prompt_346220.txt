My spouse and I are expecting our first child, and the question of baptism has stopped being a debatable topic and become a decision we have to make together. I was raised in a credobaptist tradition: baptism is an ordinance for professing believers, administered by immersion after a credible profession of faith, and it is fundamentally a public sign of what God has already done in the believer. My spouse was raised in a paedobaptist tradition: the infant children of believers belong to the covenant community, baptism is the New Testament counterpart of circumcision, and it is a means of grace that God genuinely uses without our being able to bind him to it mechanically. We can state each other's positions accurately, but we keep finding that the real disagreement slips away from us: sometimes it seems to be about who may be baptized, sometimes about whether baptism is a symbol, a sacrament, or something stronger, and sometimes about whether the church should be understood as the regenerate or as the covenant community. We want to stop talking past each other.

Write the document we should be able to produce together after a good-faith adversarial collaboration between the paedobaptist and credobaptist positions. Begin with a numbered list of agreed claims: what both sides can affirm about baptism, about the biblical data (including the household baptisms in Acts, the Pentecost promise to the hearers and their children, the Colossians passage linking circumcision and baptism, and the passage in First Peter about baptism saving), about the relationship between baptism and faith, and about what any adequate account must explain. Then, in a disagreements table, state precisely where the two positions part, wording each side's view as that side would accept it rather than as the other would caricature it. Treat the symbol-versus-sacrament-versus-cause question as a cross-cutting disagreement, and say where it lines up with, and where it comes apart from, the infant-versus-believer question. Finally, identify the cruxes of the dispute — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side — and for each crux, specify the evidence or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Treat the assignment as drafting a statement two honest parties could both sign, not as adjudicating which side is correct; we are interested in where the disagreement is real, where it is merely verbal, and where it is really a disagreement about something else, such as the nature of the church.

Format the output as: Numbered Agreed Claims; a Disagreements Table; then Cruxes and Predictions.
