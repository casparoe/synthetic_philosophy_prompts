At my school's philosophy club last week two people got into it and I was just sitting there feeling genuinely stuck about who to agree with, so I'm putting it to you. Call them M and J. M opened by saying he's a physicalist: the mind is nothing over and above the brain, and everything that exists is physical, full stop. J said she can never get a straight answer about what "physical" means, and she thinks that's the whole problem. She said: if "physical" means whatever current physics says exists — particles, fields, forces, stuff like that — then physicalism is probably false already, because physics is unfinished (it can't even unify general relativity and quantum mechanics) and it's list of posits keeps changing; a physicalist in 1900 would have balked at spacetime curvature or quantum fields, so tying the doctrine to today's physics is a bad bet. M came back and said he means "physical" in the open-ended way, as whatever a completed, final physics would say exists. J replied that this makes things worse: either the view is empty or it's just trivially true, because if you don't know what final physics will include, then "everything is physical" rules nothing out — the final theory could contain bizarre or mental-ish things and the physicalist would just relabel them as physical, so the doctrine becomes a promissory note with no content. M said that's unfair, because it does rule something out right now: it rules out spooky dualism — souls, ectoplasm, irreducible mental forces, anything not integrated with the rest of how science explains the world — and that's a substantive bet even if the details are open. J finished by saying that's not a thesis, it's just an attitude or a ban on certain explanations wearing a thesis's clothes, and the original definitional problem is still sitting there untouched.

I want to know who was actually right about what here, because they both seemed to score points but also talk past each other. Could you go through their moves one by one and rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a quick justification for each rating? And then say what both of them are missing?
