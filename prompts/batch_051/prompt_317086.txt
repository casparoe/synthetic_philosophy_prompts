Write me a satirical, deadpan FAQ page, no more than 750 words, from the fictional Ministry of the Long-Term Future of a small country that just passed an Intergenerational Sovereignty Act giving the ministry a veto over all legislation. The tone should be helpful, boring, and bureaucratic, but it should quietly descibe what is basically a mild totalitarian system. Include entries like: do citizens still vote (yes, but your vote is weighted by the expected size of your future lineage); what present-bias correction is (a mandatory phone app that flags high-time-preference purchases); why the local library got defunded (its budget was reallocated through a cosmic-endowment model to a particle-collider safety study); how the ministry knows what future people want (a Simulated Posterity Panel maintained by a committee of longtermist philosophers); and why the discount rate for future wellbeing is now zero by law. Throughout, assume the staff are sincere longtermists who lean on familiar ideas — that future people vastly outnumber present people, that existential risk is the main moral issue, and that present-biased preferences should be corrected rather than respected — and lean on them so hard they become instruments of control. The humor has to come from the fact that nobody in the text thinks of themselves as authoritarian; each new restriction just follows rationally from how much bigger the far future is. After the FAQ, add a separate one-paragraph note, 150 words max, that separates what's settled about longtermism from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and says where the parody exaggerates a real commitment versus where it leans on a contested reading; dont present the contested reading as the consensus.
