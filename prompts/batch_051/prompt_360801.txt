I'm a novelist, not a scientist, so go easy, but I've been down a chaos-theory rabbit hole for my next book (the main character runs a long-range weather forecasting startup) and I want the science to be more than set dressing. My writing group asked me to write down everything I currently believe about chaos and prediction, and now I'm worried the list doesn't quite hang together. I don't know where the problem is, so tear it apart.

Here are my commitments:

— The atmosphere, at the scale weather forecasting cares about, is deterministic: the same exact state plus the same equations gives the same future, full stop.

— So Laplace's demon, if it really could know every variable exactly, could in principle predict a chaotic system forever. The unpredictability isn't built into the world itself.

— But I also believe the butterfly effect: any real forecast has to round or miss something, and in a chaotic system those tiny differences grow exponentially, so beyond some horizon a specific weather forecast is useless. For weather I've seen people throw around ten days to two weeks.

— That horizon is a fact about us, not about the weather. When I say a chaotic system is unpredictable, I mean our instruments and finite brains can't pin down the initial state well enough, not that the system is doing something non-deterministic.

— Still, I can't shake the feeling that there's real randomness in the system too, not just measurement error. On a strange attractor the trajectories are dense and the thing is fractal, so I'm not sure the exact initial state even means anything physically. Maybe determinism is a mathematical idealization and actual weather is effectively stochastic.

— I do think strange attractors are real patterns that the system settles onto, and you can predict their shape and statistical behavior even when you can't predict which trajectory the system takes. That's how climatology can say something about averages a century out while a ten-day forecast is a coin flip.

— And I think emergent large-scale things like the jet stream or El Niño are real causes in they're own right, pushing the small scales around, and not just summaries of what the little eddies are up to. The big patterns can't be reduced to the small equations.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only seem to be. For each real tension, rank how bad it is and say what the cheapest repair would be. Then tell me which single change would leave the whole set as coherent as possible.
