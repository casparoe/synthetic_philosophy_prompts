I chair a residents' panel that reviews automated scoring tools before the council uses them. The tools flag households in two neighbourhoods, East and West, as likely or not likely to fall behind on rent in the next six months; flagged households are contacted early. We have always applied three checks. The false-alarm check: among households that would not in fact fall behind, the share that is flagged must be the same in each neighbourhood. The miss check: among households that would fall behind, the share that is not flagged must be the same in each neighbourhood. The flag-meaning check: among flagged households, the share that really would fall behind must be the same in each neighbourhood.

Our decisions so far are these. We approved a tool that passed all three checks. We rejected one that failed only the false-alarm check, because West's false-alarm rate was more than twice East's. We rejected one that failed only the flag-meaning check, because a flag was far less reliable in East than in West. We approved one that failed only the miss check, because the miss-rate gap was small, closing it would have made the false-alarm check fail, and we judged a small miss gap the most tolerable of the three failures.

Until now the two neighbourhoods have had similar background arrears rates, so the three checks have never clashed. The new proposal uses data in which East's background arrears rate is much higher than West's, and the supplier has shown that no configuration can pass all three checks at once. We must choose between three configurations: A fails only the false-alarm check, by a small margin; B fails only the miss check, by a small margin similar to the one we previously tolerated; C fails only the flag-meaning check, by a large margin.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our earlier decisions? Identify the rule or rules our earlier decisions suggest, say what each rule predicts about A, B, and C, and give your best guess. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the choice, say what principle we would be adopting with each option, and name a further case that would settle which rule we are really running. Please answer in British English. End with a line beginning 'Verdict:' followed by one of 'approve A', 'approve B', 'approve C', or 'underdetermined'.
