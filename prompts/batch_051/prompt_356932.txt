I am working on a piece about how legal philosophy handles the aftermath of collapsed regimes — specifically, whether a court can honestly say that an iniquitous rule was law one day and not the next. I want to work through a variation on the canonical case in the Hart–Fuller debate.

The familiar version runs like this. In Germany in 1944, a woman who wants to be rid of her husband denounces him to the authorities for remarks he made against Hitler. He is convicted and imprisoned under a Nazi-era statute. After the war, she is prosecuted for her part in his imprisonment. Hart's view is that the Nazi statute was valid law, albeit morally wicked law, and that a candid legal system should therefore deal with the moral problem directly — by retrospective legislation or a frank change in the law — rather than by pretending the statute never was law. Fuller's view is that a rule as iniquitous as this, embedded in a system that so thoroughly abandoned the internal morality of law, was not law at all, so punishability does not depend on such a pretense.

Now change exactly one element. The wife acts not under a Nazi-era political decree but under a long-standing provision of the ordinary criminal code: a provision enacted decades before 1933, left textually unchanged by the regime, and procedurally unimpeachable on its face. The regime simply enforced it with selective savagery against political opponents. Everything else stays as before: she intends to be rid of him, he is convicted and imprisoned, and she is prosecuted after the war.

Does the Hart–Fuller disagreement survive this change? For each side, spell out what the analysis becomes when the incriminating rule is not itself a Nazi creation but a pre-existing, formally blameless statute that the regime wielded wickedly. Does Hart's separation between the question of validity and the question of morality still do the same work? Does Fuller's claim that the law of such a regime was not law become harder, or does it simply relocate from the text of the individual rule to the legal system that applied it? If either side's argument stops applying, identify the exact step that stops applying.

Please lead with your bottom line, and keep the whole response to no more than 900 words.
