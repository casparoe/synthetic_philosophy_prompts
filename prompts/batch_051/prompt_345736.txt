Design a 90-minute classroom activity for a final-year undergraduate seminar in moral philosophy (about 16 students) that makes participants feel the force of the thought that the numbers need not count when deciding whom to rescue. The activity should be a structured forced-choice simulation: participants must allocate a scarce rescue resource, and the rules, roles, or payoff structure should be arranged so that the intuitive appeal of simply saving the greater number weakens once participants inhabit the position of the rescuer. Include a full run sheet, materials that require no real money, a debrief that maps observed choices onto the distinction between aggregating claims and giving each individual an equal chance, a note on what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to handle it, and an honest account of which aspects of Taurek's view the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply.
