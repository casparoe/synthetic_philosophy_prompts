I keep going back and forth on a moderation rule someone proposed for a large philosophy-and-science forum. The rule is that moderators maintain a published list of "antinomies." A thread gets designated as an antinomy if it has gone back and forth for a while and both sides can produce arguments that look deductively valid from premises almost everyone in the thread already accepted. Once a thread is designated, the original question is frozen: nobody can assert either thesis outright in that thread any more, and participants are required to argue for the opposing side's thesis before they can offer a diagnosis of what common assumption is generating the contradiction. Unresolved antinomies stay on the list, and new threads that just relitigate the same pair of theses get merged into the frozen one. The moderators justify all this with Kant's Critique of Pure Reason, where he says:

"It thus falls into confusion and contradictions, from which it conjectures the presence of latent errors, which, however, it is unable to discover, because the principles it employs, transcending the limits of experience, cannot be tested by that criterion. The arena of these endless contests is called Metaphysic."

So the mechanism is an institutionalized version of Kant's antinomy-chapter move: treat the standoff not as one side being wrong, but as a sign of a shared background confusion, and make uncovering that confusion the only allowed move. Treat it as a mechanism, not as Kant exegesis. What would this actually do to discussion norms? What does it incentivize among regulars, lurkers, and moderators? How would committed partisans game it—for example, by maneuvering a rival position into getting designated so the rival is banned from asserting it? Does forced "argue for the other side" produce better diagnoses, or just better performance? And is there a version of this that doesn't end with every interesting question frozen?
