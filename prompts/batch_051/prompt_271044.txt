I have been thinking on my own about two subjects that do not obviously belong together: Thomas Hobbes's account of persons and representation, and the present dispute about whether large language models are agents, simulators, or something else. In Leviathan, Hobbes makes a distinction that seems to me to cut across this dispute. For him, a person is not an inner self; a person is whoever's words and actions are considered either as belonging to themselves, or as standing for the words and actions of someone or something else. A natural person speaks in his own name, while an artificial person speaks in the name of another, under authority given by the one represented; this is the device by which a multitude becomes a commonwealth. He even allows that inanimate things can be personated through an authorised representative. Hobbes also treats reasoning as a kind of calculating or reckoning, deliberation as an alternation of appetites and aversions, and the will as the last appetite before acting; there is no immaterial self behind the motions.

Now suppose Hobbes could be shown a modern large language model, with an honest explanation of how it is trained, how it is prompted, and how its text is generated. The modern debate asks whether such a system is an agent, because it seems to pursue tasks with apparent intelligence; a simulator, because it only models the process that produced its training text rather than engaging with the world; a tool, because humans set its prompts and choose its outputs; or something else entirely. I want you to work out, as carefully and as faithfully as you can, what Hobbes would say if he had to place this thing in his own categories. Would he call it an agent, a simulator, a tool, an artificial person, a personated inanimate thing, or some combination? Where would his distinctions force the modern agent-simulator debate to be redrawn? I am after the most Hobbesian answer, not the one that sounds most sensible today, and I would like you to flag the points where Hobbes's commitments leave the matter undecided. Please write your answer in British English.
