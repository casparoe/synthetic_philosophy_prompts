Please design a case, set in an ordinary family, in which a parent tells a child a pleasant falsehood of the Santa Claus or tooth-fairy kind, engineered so that two rival justifications of such falsehoods diverge. The first justification is trust-based: it asks whether the fiction, taken as a whole, preserves and deepens the child's trust in the parent, so that discovery becomes a lesson in benevolent pretense rather than in being lied to. The second is autonomy-based: it asks whether the child is deliberately kept in a false view of the world that he or she is in no position to correct, and whether later gratitude for the fiction would itself be a product of the manipulation it is meant to excuse. Build the case so that the trust-based account finds the lie permissible and the autonomy-based account finds it impermissible. Constraints: a single paragraph and a single exchange; the child is old enough to have asked the prompting question but still young enough for the fiction to take hold; the parent fabricates no evidence, extracts no promise, and stages nothing beyond the bare falsehood; and the parent is prepared to correct the falsehood at the child's first pressing, so the autonomy-based verdict cannot rest on a refusal to come clean. After the scenario, state precisely what each account treats as decisive and why it yields its verdict. Then identify the one feature of the case doing the work, explain how the case would collapse if that feature were removed, and offer the strongest reply available to a defender of the trust-based account.
