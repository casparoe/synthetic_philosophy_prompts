Last year I returned to the piano after more than a decade away, and my teacher keeps saying I am trying too hard to sound unforced — every effort to manufacture effortlessness makes the playing stiffer. She pointed me toward the Daoist notion of wu-wei, and I want to close our next lesson with a poem about it.

Write a villanelle on the paradox of wu-wei. Let one refrain speak in the voice of deliberate effort, the attempt to make spontaneity happen; let the other refrain speak for what that effort cannot reach: action that flows only once the grasping stops. The repeating refrains should do the philosophical work — no matter how the intervening stanzas turn, the effortful refrain keeps reasserting itself, and the two voices should meet only in the closing two lines, where the form finally lets repetition and ease coincide. Do not flatten wu-wei into passivity or into the advice to simply relax: the craftsmen of the Zhuangzi are skilled through long practice, yet the text also suggests that the skill must recede into unselfconsciousness before it becomes effortless. After the poem, add a brief note separating what is well established about wu-wei from what is genuinely contested — especially whether effortless action is best understood as trained automaticity or as unlearning deliberate control — and flag where the poem leans on one reading rather than presenting it as settled.
