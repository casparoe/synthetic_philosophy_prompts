A friend in my dept summarized Turing's ESP bit from Computing Machinery and Intelligence for me like this: "Turing thought the statistical evidence for telepathy was overwhelming, so he took ESP seriously as a real phenomenon that science had to accommodate. He argued that if telepathy is real, the imitation game is unfair — a telepathic human could just read the other participant's mind, or the interrogator could sense who is who — so the whole test collapses. His fix was to put the competitors in a shielded room, but he admitted the problem was deep enough that the test can't really be saved." I always assumed Turing was just preempting an objection, not actually endorsing telepathy or claiming the evidence was decisive. Which parts of that summery are accurate, which are overstatements, and does the conclusion that the imitation game can't be saved follow from what Turing actually said?
