I've been trying to actually cash out what I think about the line between manipulation and legitimate persuasion, going by my gut on cases. A friend who gives me the honest pros and cons but orders them so the option she likes looks best: fine. A salesperson who tells me only the true facts that favor the car and leaves out the transmission problem he knows about: not fine. A fitness app that uses streaks and makes me feel like I'm losing something by missing a day to get me to run: fine, even though I know exactly what its doing. A mobile game with near-miss animations and variable rewards tuned to keep me playing: not fine. A partner who makes me feel like skipping the trip would prove I don't love them: not fine. A therapist using motivational interviewing to get me to voice my own reasons for quitting smoking: fine. An AI assistant that tells me my half-baked plan is great so I'll keep using it: not fine, even though it never said anything false.

The fitness app and the mobile game are the pair that bugs me: both are using the same habit mechanics, but I want to call one fine and the other not, and I can't articulate why. Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? If not, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a parent offering dessert as a reward for finishing homework?
