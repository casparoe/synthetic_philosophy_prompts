Exam question

Consider three candidate mechanisms by which a widely entrenched social practice can come to be abolished: (i) reasoned moral argument and persuasion; (ii) shifts in material or economic interests, such as those emphasized in the Williams thesis about the abolition of the British slave trade; and (iii) shifts in what a society regards as honorable or dishonorable, as emphasized in Kwame Anthony Appiah's account of moral revolutions. Explain how these three mechanisms differ, and keep careful track throughout of the distinction between the claim that a practice came to an end and the claim that it came to be morally condemned. In light of that distinction, assess whether the ending of dueling in Britain and the abolition of the slave trade can be explained by the same kind of mechanism.

Student answer

Appiah argues that moral arguments on their own are not enough to end an entrenched practice. His clearest case is the end of dueling in Britain. Dueling did not stop because people were persuaded by arguments that it was wrong; it stopped when it ceased to be honorable. Once people outside the aristocracy began to duel, the practice became ridiculous, and a gentleman could no longer defend it as a point of honor. So honor, not reasoning, did the real work. This shows Appiah's general thesis that moral revolutions happen only when a practice loses its honor. The Williams thesis, by contrast, explains the abolition of slavery through economic interests: by the early nineteenth century, the slave-based plantation system was no longer as profitable or as central to British economic interests as it had been, so British elites had an interest in ending the slave trade. That explains why the British public came to regard slavery as abhorrent. Thus the same contrast applies to both cases: arguments failed, and non-argumentative factors—honor in one case, economic interest in the other—were what really drove the change. The distinction between ending a practice and morally condemning it is not important here, because in both cases the practice was both ended and condemned.
