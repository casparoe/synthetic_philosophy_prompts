I'm putting together a classification exercise for a seminar on the Dennett–Searle debate over intentionality, and I want the instrument to sort participants by their pre-theoretic reactions to concrete systems rather than by which philosopher they already take themselves to agree with.

Write a decision tree that takes a reader from answers about those concrete systems to a position in the debate over whether the distinction between original (intrinsic) and derived intentionality marks a real difference or is a confusion — Dennett's charge being that positing original intentionality is two-bitsing, buying a second kind of aboutness with a mysterious extra ingredient.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names. Every branch node should be a question about a concrete case or a claim exemplified by a case, worded so the reader can answer without already knowing the labels "interpretivist," "teleosemantic," "Searlean," and the like. Use at least the following five systems or artifacts somewhere among the nodes:

- a person multiplying 23 × 47 entirely in their head;
- a pocket calculator's internal register after computing 2 + 2;
- a honeybee's waggle dance;
- a map drawn by a castaway and sealed in a bottle that no one ever opens;
- the intermediate states of a large language model while it produces a correct answer to a factual question.

The terminal leaves must include at least these positions, each with a one-sentence note on its most awkward commitment: a Searle-style view on which only certain biological processes have original intentionality and everything else is merely derived; a Dennett-style intentional-stance view on which all aboutness is relative to an interpretive stance and nothing has intrinsic intentionality; a function-based naturalist view on which a state means what it has been selected, wired, or trained to indicate, independently of any present interpreter; and an eliminativist view on which literal aboutness is nowhere to be found. Aim for 6–10 leaves overall.

For any region of the logical space that your questions open up but no developed position occupies, mark it and say briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, and note which single diagnostic question ends up doing most of the sorting work.
