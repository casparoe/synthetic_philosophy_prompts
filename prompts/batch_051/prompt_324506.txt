I'm the designated "bring your own commitments" person for a reading group on interpretability, and I'd like an outside audit before I have to present. I've ended up with these five views, and I honestly can't tell weather they form a package or a pile of contradictions.

1. Chain-of-thought tokens are causally in the loop: when a model writes out its reasoning and then answers, it conditions on those tokens, so they can genuinely shape the final answer.
2. But a chain of thought is not a reliable introspective report. Models are trained to produce plausible continuations, not to accurately describe their own hidden computations, so a sentence like "I chose A because B" is often confabulated.
3. The only real evidence that a model "used" a particular consideration is interventional: you knock out or edit the relevant activations or inputs and see whether the behavior changes. A model's say-so doesn't count as evidence.
4. Still, I think there is a fact of the matter about whether a model genuinely reasoned a certain way, over and above what it would tell you or how it behaves on any finite battery of tests.
5. In practice I catch myself trusting a chain of thought when it flags a subtle constraint, says the answer changes as a result, and then changes it — even when I have no ablation to back that up.

I want to know which of these are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be, and how bad each tension really is. Please rank the tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give, and then tell me which single revision would make the whole set cohere — and what it costs me.
