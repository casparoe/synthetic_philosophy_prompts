I keep going back and forth on what should count as deplatforming, and I want a working defintion I can use while writing a paper on whether deplatforming is really a form of punishment. Give me a definition that handles these cases, and actually test it against each one: a social media site suspending someone's account for posting death threats; a conference rescinding a keynote invitation after an old article by the speaker resurfaces; a bookshop declining to stock someone's book because the owner finds the politics disgusting; a payment processor cutting off a website that hosts legal adult content; a search engine removing a site from its index under its content policies; an audience walking out of a comedy set in protest; a university blocking a student group from bringing a speaker because it says security costs will be enormous; a network cancelling a show after its lead actor is charged with assault; a person deleting their own account because they find the platform toxic; and a newspaper spiking an op-ed it had already commissioned after members of the staff threaten to resign. The definition should tell me what the "platform" condition is doing—do a bookshop, a university lecture series, or a newspaper op-ed page count as platforms?—and whether deplatforming has to be done by whoever controls the platform, or whether an outside crowd, a payment processor, or an advertiser can deplatform someone too. Finally say whether deplatforming, on your definition, comes out as punishment or just a refusal to keep dealing with someone, and tell me which of the ten verdicts is the shakiest.
