I keep turning over a parallel and can't decide if it's real. In software, an abstract data type is defined by its operations and the promises they make, and we're taught that any implementation satisfying the spec counts as the same data type — whether the top of a stack is stored at a high or low address is an implementation detail you're not supposed to care about. That seems to me exactly what structuralists say about numbers: whether 2 is an element of 4 is not a real question, because on the von Neumann encoding it comes out true and on the Zermelo encoding it comes out false, so it's just an artifact of the encoding. Is this a genuine identity of structure between structuralism and information hiding, or am I pattern-matching on the word 'implementation'? If it's real, does anything transfer from software to philosophy of math — say, the point that implementation details leak back in when you actually have to compute, or that two representations can be behaviorally equivalent but pragmatically very different? And where does it come apart, if it does: maybe in software there's a real fact being hidden, whereas structuralism says there's no fact about 2 and 4 there to hide at all?
