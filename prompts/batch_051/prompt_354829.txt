In my course on future generations, we have been working through Parfit's nonidentity problem. The usual argument is that a child born with a given condition cannot have been harmed by being brought into existence, because the only alternative for that child was not existing, and a life worth living is better than no life. We have recently discussed germline gene editing, which seems to change the relevant counterfactual: the same embryo is held fixed and its traits are altered, so apparently the same child could be born either with or without the condition, rather than a different child being selected. Does Parfit's argument survive this development, or does it simply not cover editing cases? I am trying to see which premise is under pressure, whether the argument can be repaired or only narrowly restricted, and how a harm-based objection to choosing one editing outcome over another should be formulated when the alternative for a particular child is a better life rather than nonexistence.
