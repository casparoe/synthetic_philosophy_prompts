You are a philosopher of cognitive science who works on mental representation and follows debates about neural network interpretability. I want your careful, criterion-by-criterion judgment, not a survey.

Here are the four criteria I use for saying a component of a system genuinely represents a property P rather than merely encoding it loosely. (1) Covariation: the component tracks P across diverse inputs, reliably above chance. (2) Functional consumption: downstream computation actually uses the component in ways systematically sensitive to P, so intervening on the component changes behavior in P-appropriate ways. (3) Misrepresentation: the component can be active when P is absent, or inactive when P is present, and the system then goes wrong in the way one would expect if the representation had misfired. (4) Stability: the component–content link survives changes of context and readout and is not an artifact of one particular decoder.

Now test the following case. A large language model, trained only on next-token prediction, is examined for a sentiment axis. Researchers fit a linear projection of its hidden states that classifies text valence as positive or negative, and it generalizes reasonably to held-out text. They also find a steering vector such that adding it makes continuations more positive and subtracting it makes them more negative. But the steering mildly degrades fluency; it works far better on English product reviews than on other genres; there are many near-equivalent directions that fit the probe just as well but steer less reliably; and the model never received a supervised signal that valence is a category.

Does this sentiment axis qualify as a genuine representation of sentiment, by my four criteria? Start by brainstorming the strongest case that it is a genuine representation and the strongest case that it is only a projective summary. Then work through the four criteria one by one, saying where each is satisfied, partly satisfied, or underdetermined by the facts as given, and what further evidence would settle the residue. Then turn to the criteria themselves: does this case show that one of them is doing all the real work, or that one is too strong or too weak, or that realism about interpretability needs a different criterion than covariation plus steering? End with a definite verdict.
