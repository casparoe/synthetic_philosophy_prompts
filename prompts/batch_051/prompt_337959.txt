I facilitate consensus meetings for a tenant union, weekly while we're in a rent dispute. After each meeting we write down what "we decided," but I've learned that phrase covers at least three things: a real group commitment, a bunch of people who individually happen to lean the same way, and people going quiet to get out of the room. I want a reusable procedure for the last fifteen minutes of a meeting, grounded in whatever the philosophical work on joint commitment versus overlapping personal intentions actually says, that tells me which kind of thing has just happened and what follows if someone later doesn't do their part. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops being mechanical and becomes a judgment call, and one case where the procedure will tell me something I don't want to hear. Then run it on two cases: a rent-strike vote where everyone says yes but half the room won't meet anyone's eyes, and a plan to confront our landlord where two people stay silent but don't object and later claim they never signed on.
