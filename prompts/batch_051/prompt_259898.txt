For a departmental workshop on causation I've been asked to circulate my current view so people can poke holes before the paper. I'm a bit to close to it, so I'd like a consistency audit. Here are the commitments as they stand:

(1) I'm convinced by Russell's point that the notion of cause doesn't do much work in mature physics. At the fundamental level I'd say there are no causal relations at all—just fields, symmetries, and differential equations. So I'm some kind of eliminativist about fundamental causation.

(2) But I'm not willing to say that ordinary causal claims are false or merely useful fictions. Claims like "the heat wave caused the crop failure" or "smoking caused her lung cancer" can be literally true.

(3) I think the best account of the literal truth of such claims is interventionist: X is a cause of Y just in case there's a stable relation between X and Y under some appropriate range of interventions on X.

(4) For token-level causal claims, though, I'm drawn to a Lewis-style counterfactual account: c caused e roughly because e counterfactually depends on c.

(5) I hold a broadly Humean view of laws: laws are just summaries of regularities in the mosaic, not governors of events, and there are no irreducible causal powers.

(6) I'm a physicalist in the sense that all facts supervene on the fundamental physical facts.

(7) I think the causal exclusion problem is a genuine thorn: if a physical event has a sufficient physical cause, then a mental cause of the same event looks redundant. But I don't want to be an epiphenomenalist about higher-level causes. I think a decision can be a real cause of a bodily movement, or that selection pressure can really cause a trait to spread, even if the lower-level physical story is complete.

(8) I think the modal force in causal claims matters: causal claims aren't just records of what actually happened; they carry commitments about what would have happened if things had been different.

Which of these are actually in tension, which only look that way? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give. Also tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the worst problem is, so don't assume I'm attached to any one commitment.
