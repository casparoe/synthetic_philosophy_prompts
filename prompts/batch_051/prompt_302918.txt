Here's an asymmetry that's been bugging me. Historians treat "why did industrialization happen in Britain rather than in Qing China?" as a genuine puzzle—the Needham question is a cousin, and the Great Divergence literature is enormous. But I basically never see anyone treat "why didn't industrialization happen in Qing China rather than in Britain?" as a puzzle of its own, even though it's the same contrast with just the default swapped. Both questions are asking why the world went down one branch rather than the other, and only one direction gets a research program. Is there a defensible principle that makes that asymmetry legitimate—something about baselines or which outcome counts as surprising given prior expectations—or is it Europe quietly getting written in as the null hypothesis? Give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no introduction and no conclusion: (1) the strongest justification you can build for the asymmetry, with its limits; (2) the strongest argument that it's outcome bias or Eurocentrism; (3) a parallel case from outside economic history where the same asymmetry would clearly be fine or clearly be absurd; (4) whether "why Britain and not China?" is well posed once the asymmetry is out in the open; (5) a one-line final verdict.
