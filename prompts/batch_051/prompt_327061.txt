Suppose a cheap, side-effect-free pill becomes available that makes a person's time preferences exponential rather than hyperbolic for the next six hours: they still care about the future, but the near future is no longer discounted at a steeper rate than the far future, so the preference reversals that usually come with hyperbolic discounting stop for the duration. Nothing else about their desires, beliefs, or self-control changes. Predict how the next decade plays out, and give me the chain of reasoning rather than a story. Work through three cases: a student who can't finish their dissertation, a person early in recovery from an addiction, and a government committee setting climate or pension policy. For each: would people take the pill voluntarily, would they take it once or repeatedly, and how would behaviour differ after it wears off? Don't just assume the pill makes people more patient — separate the effect of time-consistency from any change in how much they care about the future. Also say which predictions are robust, which are guesses, and what would have to be true for the whole intervention to backfire.
