I am a novelist, and I teach a small interdisciplinary seminar on theories of mind, which I designed while researching a novel about an artificial companion. On the final exam I set this question:

'A familiar taxonomy distinguishes four kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures, whose behavioral variants are selected only across evolutionary generations; Skinnerian creatures, whose behavioral variants are selected within a lifetime by reinforcement; Popperian creatures, which can test candidate actions against an inner model before acting; and Gregorian creatures, which can acquire and revise cultural tools, above all language, for improving their own generation and testing of possibilities. Characterize each rung in your own words, and then explain in what sense each remains a selection machine even as the locus of selection changes.'

One student wrote:

'Darwinian creatures are the baseline: they do not adjust during their own lifetime, because the environment selects among prewired behavioral variants across generations. Skinnerian creatures adjust within a lifetime, because their overt behavior is selected by reinforcement. Popperian creatures move the selection inside: they try candidate actions against an inner model, so a mistaken hypothesis can be rejected without the creature paying the full cost. Gregorian creatures go a step further: culture, and especially language, supplies thinking tools that can themselves be revised, tested, and passed on. Across the ladder, then, one selectionist logic remains constant, and only the locus of selection changes—genome, overt behavior, inner model, cultural tool-kit. But this is just why the later rungs are not really Darwinian: once variants are selected inside a head or inside a culture, natural selection is no longer doing the selecting, and the word Darwinian is only a metaphor. Popperian and Gregorian creatures have therefore escaped the Darwinian logic altogether.'

Please grade this answer. Give me the grade and the point-by-point comments that justify it, since those comments are what I will hand back to the student.
