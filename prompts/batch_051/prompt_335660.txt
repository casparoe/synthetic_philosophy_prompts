I keep running into an asymmetry I can't get to cohere. If a stranger read your mail, kept notes on where you went and who you met, and sold the notebook to advertisers, you'd call it stalking and the law would mostly back you up. But if a data broker gets the same stuff from you're phone and purchase history—often yielding a profile more revealing than your diary—we treat it as the price of having a phone. The usual move is to say you consented via terms of service, but nobody reads them and there's no real opt-out, so that feels like consent theater doing a lot of work. Is there a genuine principle separating the stalker's notebook from the broker's profile, or is the difference just scale, invisibility, and the fact that you can't punch an app? And even setting that aside: does information about you actually belong to you the way your stuff does, or is "your data" a sloppy ownership metaphor? Give me the strongest justification you can construct, stress-test it against a nearby case (raw clickstream vs. inferences about your personality), and end with a verdict: keep, reverse, or drop the asymmetry.
