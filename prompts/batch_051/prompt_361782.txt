Exam question: Explain the difference between epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment about knowledge, and then say how each view would treat the following case. Dev is deciding whether a flight has a short enough connection at O'Hare, and he has carefully read the same airline itinerary in two situations. In the low-stakes situation, missing the connection would only mean taking a later flight and arriving home late. In the high-stakes situation, missing it would mean failing to attend a job interview that afternoon. His evidence about the connection is identical and quite good in both situations, though not infallible.

Student answer: Epistemic contextualism says that the truth of knowledge ascriptions depends on the context of the person using the word 'knows,' since different contexts set different epistemic standards; in this way one person can truly say that Dev knows and another can truly say that Dev does not know without contradiction. Pragmatic encroachment says instead that whether Dev knows depends on what is at stake for Dev: with the same evidence, higher practical stakes make it harder for him to know. In the case, a contextualist will say that the two situations differ because Dev's practical stakes have changed, and that these raised stakes make 'knows' express a stricter standard in the high-stakes situation; so the contextualist agrees with the pragmatic encroacher that Dev knows in the low-stakes situation and does not know simpliciter in the high-stakes situation. The pragmatic encroacher, for her part, holds that the semantics of 'knows' is fixed, while the subject's stakes enter into the truth-conditions of the knowledge relation itself. Thus one speaker who says that Dev knows in the first situation and another who says that Dev does not know in the second can both be right, and this shows that whether a subject knows is itself sensitive to practical stakes.
