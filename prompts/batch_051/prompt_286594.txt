Write the closing joint statement of a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a Du Boisian — in the tradition of W. E. B. Du Bois — and an aesthetic autonomist, the document each side can sign. The Du Boisian holds that art inevitably functions as propaganda and that Black artists’ work should be judged partly by how well it advances racial justice; the aesthetic autonomist holds that art’s value lies in its formal and experiential achievements and that judging art by political usefulness corrupts criticism. Open with a numbered agreed-claims section that is substantive rather than vacuous: what both can affirm about how art shapes perception, about the real pressures on Black artists, about craft and excellence, and about the danger of reductive or patronizing political standards. Then give a disagreements table with each side’s residual view worded as its own proponents would accept, flagging any part of the dispute that is merely verbal — in particular, distinguishing a descriptive claim about art’s inevitable influence from a normative claim about the proper standard for evaluating art. Identify the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, and for each crux give a concrete case or hypothetical both would accept as bearing on it, with each side’s prediction recorded in advance. Begin with the document itself, with no preface or commentary. After the document, add a brief note from each side saying what would change their mind: which finding against their own view would make them concede ground, and where they judge their own wording most vulnerable.
