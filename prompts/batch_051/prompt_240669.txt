I'm preparing a statement of theoretical commitments for a working group on the juristic character of the state, and I'd like an audit of whether my position hangs together before I present it. I hold the following:

(1) The state just is a corporation in the juridical sense: an artificial person, distinct from the natural persons who occupy its offices, capable of bearing rights and duties, and able to act only through representatives.

(2) Sovereignty, once constituted, is inalienable and indivisible; a commonwealth cannot lawfully transfer its sovereign power to another body or share it between two bodies without ceasing to be the same commonwealth.

(3) The early modern chartered trading companies, such as the English East India Company and the Dutch VOC, were not legitimate sovereigns in the territories they came to govern, notwithstanding their charters, because a body organized ultimately for the private profit of its shareholders is the wrong kind of body to hold the public powers of war, peace, coinage, and criminal jurisdiction.

(4) The corporate personality of an ordinary business corporation is a thin legal fiction, dispensable and easily redescribed, whereas the personality of the state is a real body politic; this is not just a difference of degree.

(5) All corporate action is action by a representative authorized to act in the corporation's name; even a democratic people acts as a people only through some authorized representative, procedure, or institutional form.

(6) Whatever sovereign-looking powers a chartered company exercises are always delegated powers, held under and from a pre-existing sovereign, and never an original title to rule.

Please go through these and identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and state for each what would have to be revised and what the revision would cost. Then tell me whether there is a single revision that would resolve the worst tensions at once, or whether I'd have to choose which of two commitments to sacrifice. I'd rather not say in advance which of these I am most attached to; I want the audit to be unguided.
