Okay so I'm a senior and the student rep on my school district's naming and symbols review comittee. We have to get the board a recommendation by the end of the month about Columbus Middle School: rename it, keep the name, or keep it but do something smaller like a plaque or a new history unit. The chair asked me to draft the short opinion the committee will actually vote on, and I honestly don't know how to write it.

The committee is split. The keep-the-name people keep saying Columbus was a man of his time, every 15th-century explorer did brutal stuff, and if you apply today's standards backwards you'll end up renaming everything, which is its own kind of arrogance. The rename people say that misses the point: a school name isn't a grade we're giving Columbus as a person, it's us deciding who to honor right now, so the man-of-his-time defense is kind of a dodge. One of them also pointed out he never actually set foot in the mainland US (he landed in the Caribbean and later along the South American coast), so the local-heritage argument is confused too. And someone else said she'd looked into it and there were Spanish priests not long after Columbus got there who were already writing horrified reports about how the colony treated the Taino, so saying nobody back then saw a problem just isn't true either.

Write the memo for me. Give one clear recommendation with a timeline, explain the reasoning in a way the board will actually read (they hate culture-war essays), and make sure it seriously answers the strongest point from the side you're recommending against, not a straw man version of it.
