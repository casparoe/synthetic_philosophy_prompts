I'm putting together a handout for a study group on the old tautology objection—the worry that if fitness just means "leaves more offspring," then "the fittest survive" says nothing—and on the Mills–Beatty propensity interpretation that's supposed to answer it. The idea there is that fitness isn't actual reproductive success but a probabilistic disposition (a "propensity") to leave offspring, like how a biased die has a disposition to land six even if it doesn't on any given throw. I'm collecting objections to the propensity interpretation, and so far I have these five:

(1) The tautology worry reappears higher up: if fitness is expected reproductive success, but we can only estimate expectations from actual reproduction in a finite sample, then testing the claim that the fitter reproduce more is circular again.
(2) Reference-class trouble: a genotype's propensity is only defined relative to an environment, a population, maybe even a time-slice, and there's no principled rule for picking which one.
(3) Propensities are metaphysically suspicious: they're supposed to be irreducible dispositions over and above the observed frequencies, which sounds to many people like an occult power.
(4) Expected value loses variance information: two types can have the same expected number of offspring but different variance, and in small populations lower variance can be favored; so defining fitness as the expectation seems to strip out information that matters.
(5) Component problem: fitness has to lump together survival at different ages and fecundity into one number, and the choice of how to average them seems arbitrary, so the "propensity" is partly an artifact of how we do the lumping.

I feel like all five are, in the end, about how you specify or estimate the propensity. What I want is one more objection that is genuinely different in kind—ideally an objection to the core idea that fitness is a real propensity of organisms or genotypes at all, rather than a statistical summary of what in fact happened. It shouldn't be a restatement, a special case, or a combination of the five above. What would you add, do you think a Mills–Beatty-style defender could just shrug it off, and how does it cut differently from the ones on my list?
