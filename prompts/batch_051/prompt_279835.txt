I'm in a course this term called Why Rome Fell and Whether It Did, and we've been reading through all the usual candidate causes: Gibbon-style moral and institutional decay, barbarian pressure from the Huns, fiscal and manpower exhaustion, overextension, lead in the water supply, climate stress, Christianity sapping civic spirit, and so on. One thing keeps bugging me though. Almost all of these get pitched as empire-wide or even Mediterranean-wide causes, but the empire didn't fall empire-wide: the eastern half kept going strong and Constantinople only fell in 1453, while the western half was bascially done by 476. So a cause that hits east and west about equally should predict both halves falling, and it's hard for me to give it much credit unless someone shows why the east was insulated from it.

Could you rank the usual candidate causes into tiers by how much of the asymmetric western collapse they can actually carry on their own? Top tier would be causes that were clearly worse in the west or that naturally explain eastern resilience; middle tiers are causes that help but mostly in combination with something else; bottom tier is stuff that looks roughly symmetric and so is suspicious as a main explanation. For each cause give me a sentence or two on where the east-west asymmetry comes from, or why it's missing, and say which single cause you'd be most willing to defend as doing the most work in the top tier. I'm less interested in a one-off ranking than in how you'd hold the line on the asymmetry point when the prof pushes back on it.
