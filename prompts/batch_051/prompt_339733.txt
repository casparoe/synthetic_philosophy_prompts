I'm a novelist, and I'm applying for a six-month residency that funds a short philosophical research project to run alongside a novel-in-progress. I need to submit a one-page research proposal, and I'd like help turning a half-baked idea into something fundable.

The novel: a woman in her late thirties who has optimized every corner of her life—diet, dating, career, friendships, inbox—until the whole thing cracks. In the second half she tries to figure out whether "good enough" is a mature stance or a rationalization. I keep reading about satisficing and bounded rationality, and I've gotten suspicious that the self-help version of "good enough" blends two different things: (a) a standard you set before you start looking, an aspiration level; and (b) a running judgment that further search isn't worth the cost. I want the proposal to get me clear on whether satisficing is a genuine norm of rationality for bounded agents, or just maximizing with the costs of thinking quietly folded in. And in particular wether my two senses of "good enough" actually come apart, and which of them a person can be praised or blamed for.

So the proposal should: state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; say why the obvious answers each leave something open (satisficing as rational-but-irreducible, satisficing as optimal stopping in disguise, satisficing as just what tired agents do); explain the approach and why it beats the obvious alternatives—I expect mostly conceptual work with small toy cases or decision matrices, not empirical work; say what a result would look like, what would count as the project failing, and what a useful deliverable looks like for someone actually writing a novel; and give an honest account of what could make the project to big for six months. Don't bother with citations, I'll add those.

Also, if I've got the whole frame wrong—say the satisficing/maximizing distinction isn't doing real work, or my two senses of "good enough" is a distinction without a difference—tell me that plainly and write the proposal for the better question instead of making mine look good.

After the proposal itself, give me a brief structured summary so I can skim it for the application. Use a JSON object with these fields:

- "research_question": the question in one or two sentences.
- "gap": what the obvious positions leave open, in a sentence or two.
- "method": how I'd actually work, in a sentence or two.
- "possible_conclusions": an array of the two or three main ways the project could come out.
- "failure_mode": what would count as the project failing.
- "scope_risk": the thing most likely to blow the six-month timeline.
