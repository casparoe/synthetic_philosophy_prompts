At a town-hall meeting about a proposal to expand police access to smart-doorbell and street-camera footage, a council member dismissed a resident's concern about being recorded in the course of ordinary life by saying, "If you have nothing to hide, you have nothing to fear." The sentence did real argumentative work: it made the resident's own unease look like evidence against her, and it shifted the debate from whether the surveillance is justified to what objectors might be concealing. I want to understand exactly what that move assumes. Treat the slogan as a family of distinct claims and lay the readings out as a menu. In separating them, attend at least to (a) what "nothing to hide" can range over — never having broken the law, never having done anything seriously wrong, never having done anything that would bring social shame, never having any fact you would prefer not be broadcast, never having any information whose disclosure could cost you or those close to you; (b) what "fear" can range over — fear of legal punishment, fear of public humiliation, fear of being watched and altered by the watching itself, fear of how stored footage might later be used by the state or private parties against you or your associates, fear of living as if under suspicion; and (c) whether the sentence is best read as descriptive (the innocent will in fact be left unharmed), normative (the innocent ought not object because no legitimate interest of theirs is at stake), or diagnostic (anyone who does object thereby reveals that she has something to hide). For each reading that constitutes a coherent position, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false, and briefly justify the verdict. Then say which reading or readings the council member's conclusion actually needs — that a law-abiding person has no valid reason to resist this surveillance — and whether that reading survives scrutiny. I am less interested in a single correct interpretation than in a usable map of what the slogan can be made to do.
