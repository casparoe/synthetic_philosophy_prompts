I've been trying to figure out whether raw brain size—just the sheer amount of neural tissue or number of neurons, setting aside relative brain size and wiring—has any moral weight on its own. My gut keeps giving me a set of verdicts that don't obviously fit a single rule. If two otherwise healthy adults are both conscious and capable, and one just happens to have a brain around 1,000 cubic centimeters while the other has one around 1,500, I don't think the bigger-brained one has any more moral standing; I'd be appalled if someone treated the smaller-brained one as less important. But if it's a chimp versus a mouse, I'd save the chimp, and I'd probably say the chimp's larger, more complex brain supports a richer mental life, so the size seems to be doing something there. Then again, an elephant's brain is several times heavier than a human's, and I don't think elephants outrank humans—though I'd probably still put the elephant well above the mouse.

The cases get weirder from there. An adult human whose brain never grew past roughly the size of a chimp's (around 400 cc) but who is conscious, responsive, and can communicate a little still seems to me to have full human standing, not some intermediate standing between human and chimp. At the same time, I'd have no hesitation treating a silicon system with as many artificial neurons or parameters as a human brain as morally empty if it was designed only to predict text and had no consciousness. And in the other direction, a hypothetical creature with a brain the size of a walnut that talked, formed attachments, and reported suffering like a person would strike me as a person; the small size wouldn't bother me at all.

I can't tell whether there's a single principle here. Maybe brain size is just a proxy for something like the richness of the mental life a brain can support, and the human with the chimp-sized brain gets full status for a different reason? But then I don't know why the chimp with the same-sized brain gets less, and I don't know whether that's coherent or just speciesist. What principle, stated precisely, captures as many of these verdicts as possible? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop, if any, and what does it predict for cases I haven't listed—like a human-sized brain with very simple internal structure, or two otherwise similar creatures whose brain sizes differ but whose behavior looks equally complex?
