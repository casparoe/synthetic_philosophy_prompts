Hey — I'm a PhD student in philosophy of AI, mostly moral status and the ethics of care, and I've been trying to get my own views on whether AIs could raise AIs into some kind of order. I ended up with the set below and I'd appreciate an audit of whether it hangs together.

(1) I think "guardian" is a role concept: you count as a guardian because you've accepted responsibility for a dependent's welfare, and a good guardian is one who discharges that responsibility reliably. Whether the guardian has feelings about the ward is a separate issue. So institutions and, in principle, non-sentient AIs can be guardians.

(2) But I don't think that's parenthood. I think parenthood is constitutively vulnerable — a parent is someone whose own flourishing is entangled with the child's, such that the child's harm can genuinely diminish them. A non-sentient system can't have that. So AIs could be guardians but not parents, and I want to keep the two apart.

(3) I think if a more mature AI trains a less mature one and gradually hands over autonomy, the ward can be well-raised as long as its interests are tracked reliably. Standards of care for an AI ward should track the ward's needs, not the gaurdian's inner life.

(4) Yet I also think that for human children, emotional attunement isn't just an instrument that happens to produce good outcomes. A human child raised by a cold but perfectly competent machine is being deprived of something in the care itself, even if the outcomes match.

(5) I can't see why that wouldn't extend to AI wards rich enough to need raising rather than mere tuning. The more I treat an AI child as having a good of its own, the more I want to say it too is owed something like warmth, not just reliable servicing.

(6) I also hold that we shouldn't create sentient AIs specifically to fill caregiving roles like "parent." Creating a sentient being in order to extract caring labor from it seems like exactly the kind of instrumentalization a theory of AI care should rule out.

I keep having the feeling there's a bad seam in here but I can't locate it. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and how severe are the real ones? For each real one, what would the cheapest fix be, and which single revision would do the most cleanup? Also, I keep going back and forth on whether (1) and (2) are even consistent — is that a real conflict or am I using two different senses of "caregiver"? Feel free to tell me I'm quitely contradicting myself.
