Since retiring from a career in the natural sciences, I have been reading philosophy and find myself unsettled by a disagreement with an old colleague. I spent my working life modeling uncertain systems, so assigning probabilities to things is familiar to me. I am now deciding whether to accept a three-year appointment on a scientific advisory board; it would be interesting but would eat into the time I now give to reading. My colleague, an economist, says I am being evasive: a rational person can assign a probability to any proposition, including one about his own future behavior, so I ought to be able to say that the probability I will accept is, say, 0.6, and plan accordingly. But when I try this, assigning a number to my own not-yet-made choice feels nothing like forecasting rain. If I settle on 0.8 that I will accept, have I not thereby decided the matter rather than predicted it? And if I am still genuinely deliberating, what is such a number even measuring? I would be glad to know who is right and how to think about this.
