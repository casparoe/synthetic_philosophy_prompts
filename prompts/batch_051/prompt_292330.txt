I'm a science and ideas journalist doing a feature on an adverserial collaboration between two Hume scholars who have spent a decade sniping at each other in footnotes and were finally paid by a foundation to sit in a room for two days and produce one joint statement on whether Hume thought necessary connection is in the world or only in the mind. One holds the regularity/projectivist reading: for Hume a cause is just constant conjunction plus the mind's habit of expecting the effect, and talk of objective necessity is something he disolves. The other holds the New Hume reading: Hume accepts that real causal powers exist in nature but says we can never know them or form an adequate idea of them, so he is a sceptic about our grasp of necessity rather than an eliminativist about it. Their moderator, a third philosopher they both respect, set a required format: first a numbered list of agreed claims, then a disagreements table.

I'd like you to draft the statement they would actually be able to sign, since I'll quote from it in the piece. Make the agreed claims substantive and non-vacuous: what the Treatise and the first Enquiry can be taken to say, what the copy principle does and doesn't rule out, what Hume's two definitions of cause actually define, and what each side is genuinely committed to rather than what it merely finds embarrassing. For the table, use columns something like: the issue; how the regularity camp would phrase its own view; how the New Humean would phrase its own view; the crux; and an argument, case, or textual test both sides agree would bear on that crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. These two are prickly and will refuse to sign anything that makes their own position look like a strawman, so each side's wording has to be one its own defender would accept in front of their grad students. If part of the fight looks merely verbal to you, say which part and why. I want a genuinely negotiated document rather than a textbook both-sides summary—so include the places where the New Humean thinks the regularity reading is quietly doing metaphysics, and where the regularity camp thinks the New Humean has helped himself to a notion of unknowable necessity that Hume's principles cannot pay for.
