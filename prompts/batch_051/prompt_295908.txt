I've got a homemade diagnosis of akrasia I want stress-tested. I call it the "merged-self fallacy." The standard line says weak-willed behaviour is what you get when a single agent has hyperbolic discounting: near rewards get overweighted, so Sunday-me prefers the diet that starts Monday and Monday-me prefers cake. But I think that's mistaking a summary statistic for a mental state. No time-slice actually has the hyperbolic curve the model attributes to "me": Sunday-me discounts next-Monday's goods in one way, Monday-me doesn't discount right-now cake at all, and the pretty reversal curve only shows up after you merge two evaluative standpoints into one graph and call them a single agent. So hyperbolic discounting isn't an explanation of akrasia; it's a redescription of an intrapersonal disagreement, and the single agent who flip-flops never exists at any given time. Is this already a thing, or am I just rediscovering Parfit/Ainslie/Thaler badly? Is it a real fallacy, or does it dissolve once you let agents be temporally extended? Give me the strongest version of my idea, the three best objections, and say which are fatal rather than just uncomfortable.
