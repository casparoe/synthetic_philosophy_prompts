A state's Public Compensation Commission, which hears claims under a partial waiver of sovereign immunity, is reviewing its own record before deciding a pending claim. The commissioners are divided over the underlying question: whether there is a principled sense in which the state itself can be said to have done wrong when an agency's action or inaction results in harm, or whether the state merely pays compensation in certain classes of cases as a matter of legislative grace. To inform the decision, they have asked for a memorandum that analyzes their precedents and recommends a ruling on the pending claim.

The Commission's five most relevant prior rulings are these. It awarded compensation where: (1) a state-owned electric utility neglected maintenance on a distribution line, and the resulting fault ignited a paying customer's barn; (2) a state-university teaching hospital that charges market rates left a surgical sponge inside a patient; and (3) a highway-department snowplow ran a red light during routine maintenance and struck a pedestrian. It denied compensation where: (4) the legislature eliminated the appropriation for a regional levee inspection program, and flooding later damaged homes in that region; and (5) police took forty-five minutes to respond to an imprecise 911 call about a suspicious person, and a house was burglarized in the meantime.

The pending claim concerns a nine-year-old whom the state child-welfare agency had placed in a foster home that it licensed and supervised. The child was injured after her assigned caseworker failed to make the monthly home visit required by the agency's own written protocol, despite having received two specific reports that this particular home had become unsafe. The child's guardian seeks compensation, and the facts are undisputed.

Write a memorandum that (a) states which decision would make the Commission most consistent with its own prior rulings; (b) identifies which of the five earlier rulings is the closest analog and explains why; (c) formulates the competing principles that each fit all five prior rulings equally well; (d) says what each candidate principle yields in the pending claim; and (e) offers your best judgment about which principle the Commission should adopt, including what the reasons favoring each candidate reveal about the difference between holding that the state has done wrong and holding that it should simply pay. Conclude by describing one further hypothetical claim, distinct from the pending claim, that would most efficiently discriminate between the competing principles. Write the memorandum in American English.
