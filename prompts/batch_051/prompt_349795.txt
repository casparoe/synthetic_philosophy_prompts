I run a small analytics group at a mid-sized online retailer, and I'm the person who sanity-checks every analysis that goes to the monthly trading meeting where the execs decide on discounts, retention programmes, and product changes. Two or three of these land on my desk each week, the typical decision is worth high five to low six figures, and I have about 45 minutes per analysis on top of everything else; the execs will sign off on whatever I don't push back on, so if I miss a bad causal claim it tends to ship. I know enough statistics to follow a model's AUC and p-values, but I'm not a causal-inference specialist.

I've been reading about Pearl's causal hierarchy — the observational/associational layer, the intervention layer, and the counterfactual layer — and I'd like it turned into a reusable screening procedure I can actually run: a checklist or flowchart in words. The procedure should force me to ask, for each analysis, what question the business is really asking, which rung of the ladder that question lives on, what the design actually licenses, and what extra assumptions the jump up each rung costs. It should deal with the standard failure modes — a predictive model being passed off as evidence about what would happen if we changed something, conditioning on a collider or a post-treatment variable, mistaking a mediator for a confounder or the other way round, unmeasured confounders, and the moment where do-calculus-style machinery can't save you because the causal graph itself is guesswork. I need to know which steps are genuine prerequisites and which are judgment calls, because I can't send an analysis back without saying why it failed.

Please give me the procedure itself, then work it through two or three mini-cases from my world — discounted win-back emails to lapsed customers, a redesigned onboarding flow that coincided with better retention, and one other of your choosing — including at least one case where the procedure tells me to reject or heavily caveat an analysis everyone is excited about. End with a short honest note on what the procedure cannot do. And please write your answer in British English.
