There is an analogy I have been unable to settle between the mechanics of performative utterances and the mechanics of state-mutating operations in programming. On the philosophy side: a performative utterance such as "I now pronounce you married" does not report an independently existing fact; in the right circumstances, and with the right speaker, its utterance brings the fact about. When the conditions fail — the speaker lacks authority, the procedure is misapplied, the participants or circumstances are unsuitable, or the utterance is not backed by the appropriate intention — the act does not come off. The utterance is not false but void or defective: the marriage, promise, or bet simply does not take. On the programming side: a call such as `account.debit(100)` does not describe a balance; it changes one. The mutation succeeds only if preconditions hold, the caller has the relevant permissions, and no exception interrupts it; otherwise the change is rolled back or refused, and the system raises an error rather than returning a truth value. The mapping seems natural: felicity conditions correspond to preconditions and permissions; infelicity corresponds to an exception or a failed transaction; the institutional state brought about by a performative corresponds to mutable program state; and the constative/performative distinction corresponds to the distinction between a query, which reads state, and a mutation, which writes it.

Is this a deep structural identity, or only an attractive metaphor? In particular: is the state of affairs created by a marriage or a promise the same kind of thing as a program's mutable state, or does the analogy quietly presuppose a picture of social life — marriage and promises as entries in a shared institutional record — that is itself the thing to be shown? And is the query/mutation divide as clean as the analogy requires? The speech-act tradition seems to have concluded that even factual reports do something rather than merely mirror a pre-existing state, which would dissolve the clean constative/performative contrast. If the analogy is deep, what should that collapse look like on the programming side — is even a pure query an act against the system, and would that make the analogy stronger or break it? Or is that precisely the point where the two domains come apart?
