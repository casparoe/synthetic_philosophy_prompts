I am a PhD student serving on the committee that writes the metaphysics comprehensive exam for our graduate program. Please draft one ninety-minute question on the relationship between grounding and fundamentality. The question should not be answerable by rehearsing the formal features of ground (irreflexivity, asymmetry, transitivity) or the slogan that a grounded fact is nothing over and above its grounds. It should force candidates to confront the tension between those two commitments: if a grounded fact is nothing over and above its grounds, what remains to distinguish the grounded fact from its grounds, and how do the irreflexivity and asymmetry of ground do that work without collapsing ground into identity? The question should also require candidates to say what the most promising response to this collapse problem costs for the thesis that grounding chains bottom out in a level of ungrounded facts. Please provide a model answer and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, with the marks each is worth (total 20) and a one-line note on how candidates typically get each point wrong. The question and model answer should be self-contained and should not require or reward bibliographic citation.
