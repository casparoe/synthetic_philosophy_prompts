I'm a math grad student and some friends and I got into an argument about a hypothetical journal/overlay. The pitch: a mathematics journal that only accepts papers whose proofs are fully constructive. No law of excluded middle, no proof by contradiction, and if your theorem says "there exists an x such that P(x)" the proof has to actually produce the x, or at least a method that in principle finds it. The founders are serious Bishop-style constructivists, not just people trying to start another venue. They announce it as a corrective to classical math's habit of asserting things exist without ever saying how to get them.

Walk through how this journal would actually play out if it got real. I don't want a verdict on whether constructivism is right, I want the mechanics. What actually gets published, and what happens to subfields where the standard results lean hard on excluded middle or choice — real analysis, functional analysis, topology? How fast does someone figure out how to game it, e.g. by running a classical proof through one of the standard translations that embeds classical logic into intuitionistic logic, or by quietly restating definitions so that existence claims become nearly vacuous? What would the editors have to do to stop that, and do those fixes (demanding explicit algorithms, banning translation-only proofs, requiring extractable witnesses) gut the original project? And what would success even look like: a genuinely computable mathematics, or mostly classical theorems repackaged in constructive clothing?

Please structure your answer in exactly these sections:

- "What gets published" — one paragraph on which subjects and result-types flourish or starve.
- "The gaming" — one or two paragraphs on the specific workarounds authors would use.
- "Second-order effects" — one paragraph on what this does to the surrounding mathematical community and to classical journals.
- "Fixes and their costs" — one or two paragraphs on what the journals editors could do and what each fix breaks.
- "Bottom line" — two or three sentences on whether the journal selects for real constructivity or for something else.
