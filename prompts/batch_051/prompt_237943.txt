I've come around to divine simplicity, taken the strong way: God has no parts of any kind, no real distinction between God's essence and God's existence, and no real distinction among the divine attributes. Omniscience, omnipotence, and perfect goodness are all just God, seen by us under different concepts. The worry I can't shake is that this seems to turn God into a fact or an obtaining state of affairs rather than a substance: if God just is God's existence, then God looks like the sort of thing that obtains rather than the sort of thing that exists. Is that a fair reading of what simplicity actually commits me to, or is it a confusion about existence? And stepping back: what would genuinely count against divine simplicity? I want the strongest honest case against it, not the usual caricatures. Which entailments should a defender admit are real costs, and which entailments would be decisive if they really follow? Are any of the escape routes live rather than merely logically possible? Sort the problems from fatal to merely uncomfortable, and tell me whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — and whether that's a problem for a doctrine about God, or just what you'd expect.
