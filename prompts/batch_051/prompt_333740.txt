I'm writing an essay on creativity and using the film Amadeus as my main text. Read it through Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity: the idea that the crux of creativity is variations on a theme — nothing comes from nowhere, you take an existing concept and turn some dials, and the masterpiece is a neighbouring point in the space of possible variations. The film seems of two minds about this. Salieri's whole grievance is that Mozart's music arrives already finished, like dictation from God (the film leans hard on the old legend that his manuscripts had no corrections); but the same movie shows Mozart hearing Salieri's dull little welcome march once and then improvising it into something far better, which is basically knob-twiddling acted out on screen. Which account does the film actually side with, and where does it cheat? I want a reading that treats the movie as evidence scene by scene rather than just an illustration: where does it dramatize the variations model, where does it resist it, and what does the film show about creativity that the knob-twiddling model leaves out? Skip the plot summary, I know the film — give me the reading.
