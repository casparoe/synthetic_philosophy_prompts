I've been reading the macro and money threads on rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I recently tried to write down where I've actually landed on the what-does-printing-money-create question. I'd like someone to stress-test the set as a set, not lecture me on the individual claims (I know most of them are individually defensible). Here's what I seem to hold:

(1) Printing money doesn't create wealth. It creates claims on a fixed stock of real stuff, so it mostly just changes who gets what.
(2) But in a deep recession at the zero lower bound, helicopter money (or a money-financed fiscal expansion) can raise real output, and if it moves the economy from below potential back to potential, that's a genuine real gain, not just redistribution.
(3) I also hold long-run money neutrality: a permanent change in the money supply only changes the price level in the long run, not real output.
(4) Government debt isn't net wealth for the economy as a whole: the future taxes needed to service it are a matching liability, and people only feel richer because they discount those taxes or assume someone else pays them.
(5) But base money is different from bonds. It isn't a claim on future taxes; it's an outside asset, and an increase in the monetary base is net wealth for the private sector (the real-balance effect), at least until expected inflation catches up.
(6) The quantity theory is roughly right as a long-run theory of inflation.
(7) A sovereign that borrows in it's own currency can't be forced to default, so the claim that we can't afford it is usually the wrong objection to money-financed spending; the real constraints are inflation and actual ressource bottlenecks.
(8) And I think the Weimar hyperinflation really was caused by printing money to pay the reparations bill, and it destroyed people's savings, so printing money can destroy wealth in practice even if it can't create it.

I want an audit of the whole set. Which pairs are actually in tension, and how badly? Which pairs only look like they're in tension but dissolve once you draw a distinction? For each genuine tension, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost? Then tell me which single revision of one claim would do the most to make the set hang together.
