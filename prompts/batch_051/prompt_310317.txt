I'm trying to write a short statement of my views on distributive equality, and I keep worrying the things I want to say pull against each other. Rather than tidy it up myself, I'd like an audit. Here's the list as it stands:

1. The point of equality is to neutralise luck, not to make everyone equally happy or to equalise holdings for their own sake. I'm basically a luck egalitarian.
2. If someone chooses a risky career and it goes badly, that matters: we owe her less by way of compensation than someone who is equally badly off through brute bad luck.
3. But I don't think a bad choice forfeits her claim to a decent minimum. Even imprudent people can claim enough healthcare, housing and food, and I'd tax to guarantee it.
4. I have no patience for subsidising expensive tastes. If you cultivate a taste for absurdly costly wine, that's on you, even if the taste itself wasn't chosen.
5. But a congenital disability that makes ordinary living expensive is a paradigm case for compensation, even when the person reports she's just as happy as anyone else.
6. I'm suspicious of welfare as a metric anyway, because of adaptive preferences: people can report high satisfaction with objectively awful options.
7. I'm drawn to the capability approach, but I worry that until someone fixes which capabilities matter its less a theory than a placeholder.
8. The envy test for whether a distribution of resources is fair — roughly, nobody prefers someone else's bundle to their own — strikes me as basically right, at least as a benchmark.

Go through the list and tell me which commitments pull against each other, how badly, and what the least costly repair would be in each case. I'm also interested in which pairs only look like a conflict but dissolve once you press on them. Finally, tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set hang together, and what it would cost me.
