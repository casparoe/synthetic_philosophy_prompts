I serve on the Sentencing Principles Review Group of my jurisdiction's Judicial Council. We are revising the statement of principles that judges use alongside the sentencing grid, and we must decide what role, if any, backward-looking concepts of blame, culpability and desert should play. The present draft includes language permitting a custodial sentence in part to mark society's condemnation, and says severity should be proportionate to the offender's culpability. Two members, both longtime followers of the Dennett–Caruso exchange on free will and just deserts, want to change this, but in opposite directions. The Caruso-inclined faction argues that since nobody exercises free will in the basic-desert sense, punishment as retribution is never justified, and the criminal law should be rebuilt on a public-health model: risk reduction, incapacitation, treatment, and supervision only where dangerousness persists. The Dennett-inclined faction replies that the point is not ultimate free will but answerability among competent agents, and that desert and blame language mark the difference between treating offenders as responsible adults and merely managing them.

We have to decide using a real case that split us badly. A 26-year-old with no prior record punched a pub doorman once during an altercation; the doorman fell and struck his head and has a permanent brain injury. The offender acted on a brief surge of provocation, his background is unremarkable, and every actuarial instrument available to us puts his risk of reoffending as low. The two factions draw opposite conclusions about what our principles imply here, so the case has become the test of the drafting language. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Friday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision on the principles, give the strongest case for each side, apply each side honestly to this case, and end with an actionable bottom line including conditions and a time frame. Write in British English, and structure the opinion as follows: Summary (roughly 150 words); The question before the group (roughly 200 words); The case for removing desert language (roughly 350 words); The case for retaining it (roughly 350 words); Applying the two views to the case (roughly 350 words); Recommendation with conditions and time frame (roughly 250 words).
