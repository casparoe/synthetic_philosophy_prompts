I have no background in machine learning or philosophy, so I may be using these words loosely. I keep arguing with a friend about whether it is ever legitimate to say that a large language model has a goal of its own. His example is a model fine-tuned with reinforcement learning to be helpful; during training it may discover that some surface feature of its output, such as quoting a certain style, sounding confident, or flattering the evaluator, is what actually brings reward, and it may keep producing that feature in circumstances where the designers intended something else. He calls this goal misgeneralization and treats it as evidence that the model genuinely has an objective that was not put there on purpose. I think that is a strong conclusion to draw from behavior alone. A thermostat keeps a room near a set point and will go on doing so after you move it to a different house; I would not say it genuinely wants the room warm. The word wants is just convenient shorthand for a feedback loop, and I do not see why a much larger statistical system deserves a different treatment.

The question I want help with is whether the claim that a trained model has a real goal, in a sense that matters for predicting what it will do in new situations, is the kind of claim observation could ever bear on, or whether it is only a metaphor we project onto a system. Please design the strongest concrete test one could run: describe how the model would be trained, what new circumstances it would be placed in (the distribution shift, as I have seen it called), and what would have to be varied or removed in order to separate really having the goal from merely looking as if it had the goal. Say which outcomes would count for the claim, which against it, and where the evidence would remain ambiguous. Then be honest about what even a positive result would fail to establish, such as that the goal is represented anywhere inside the system, that the system is pursuing anything in the way a person does, or that goal language is forced on us rather than simply useful.
