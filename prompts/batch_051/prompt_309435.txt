I'm working on a piece about our faith that the future will keep behaving like the past, and a philospher I interviewed hit me with the old grue move. Define 'grue' like this: an object is grue if it's been examined before 2050 and is green, or if it hasn't been examined before 2050 and is blue. Every emerald we've ever seen fits both 'all emeralds are green' and 'all emeralds are grue,' but the two rules come apart for the first emerald examined after 2050, and the guy says no evidence up to now can pick between them. I'd rather make it empirical. How would you test whether an actual learner — a child, a pigeon, a trained network, doesn't matter — projects green instead of grue when the data are compatible with both? You can shrink the 2050 cutoff to a lab-timescale boundary if that helps. Lay out the design, what result would count for and against green being the privileged rule, and then be honest about what a positive result would and wouldn't settle: would it show green is objectively the right rule, or just that our hardware has a bias? And does the experiment even touch his original point, or sidestep it?
