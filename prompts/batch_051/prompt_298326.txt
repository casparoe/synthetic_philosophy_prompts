So I'm a science journalist who covers consciousness and the brain, and I've been reporting a piece on how the brain timestamps experience. I keep finding that my own verdicts about timing illusions dont fit one clean story, and I need someone to give me the principle that ties them together—or tell me which verdicts have to go.

Here are the cases.

1. Two dots flash in quick succession: first a red dot on the left, then a green dot a little to the right. I see a single red dot gliding across and turning green around the middle of its path, before the green dot is actually on screen. My verdict: the motion and the mid-path color change are genuinely experienced, not a story I add after the flashes.

2. I tap a person's forearm near the wrist a few times, then near the elbow a few times, in rapid sequence. They report taps hopping up the arm, including spots in between that were never touched. My verdict: the phantom intermediate taps are felt, not confabulated.

3. A bar sweeps across a monitor, and a flash fires exactly when the bar passes the flash. The flash looks like it trails behind the bar. My verdict: the visual system is projecting the moving bar a little forward in time, not just being slow to process the flash.

4. A square appears for a few milliseconds, and immediately afterwards a ring appears around where it was. I see the ring but I'm fairly sure I never saw the square. My verdict: the square never reached a stable percept; this is not a case of seeing it and then losing it.

5. Someone watches a clip of a car going down a road. Later, a question that mentions a barn the car passed plants a barn in their memory—a barn that was never in the clip. My verdict: this is an edit to memory after the fact; the original experience genuinely had no barn.

The trouble is that my verdicts in 1 through 3 treat content that wasn't in the raw input as genuinely experienced, while 4 and 5 draw a line between content that never got into experience (the square) and content that got inserted into memory afterwards (the barn). From the inside, the barn feels just as much like part of what happened as the phantom taps, so I don't know what justifies the line. My rough principle is that the brain only ever lets you see the final cut—it delays the show until the editing is done. But then the barn should count as genuinely experienced too, and I don't buy that. Maybe the right account is a split between a Stalinesque stage (edit before the show) and an Orwellian stage (rewrite memory later), but I can't tell where one ends and the other begins.

So: what principle captures my verdicts, what does it force me to drop, and what does it say about a masked word that is never reportable but still speeds up recognition of that word a few seconds later?
