So im giving a seminar paper at an ecumenical theology-and-philosophy workshop defending infant baptism. My line, roughly: baptism is a divinely instituted speech-act, not a bare symbol but also not a magical efficient cause. It constitutes covenant membership the way a judge's ruling constitutes a marriage or an adoption, so the rite isn't made valid by the baby's understanding or faith. The believer's-baptism demand for a credible profession first confuses conditions on the person being baptized with conditions on the church doing the baptizing. I'll argue the water plus the Trinitarian formula is the illocutionary act, and God's promise is what gives it standing, not the minister's or family's subjective state.

The audience is the problem. Two low-church evangelicals who think any talk of baptism doing something is works-righteousness dressed up; a Reformed covenant theologian who baptizes infants but hates my speech-act framing because he thinks it drifts into ex opere operato; an analytic philosopher of language who keeps asking about felicity conditions and uptake; and an Orthodox theologian who thinks Ive made the rite too verbal and lost the material, embodied side of sacrament. I get maybe ten minutes of questions.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come, phrased the way those four would put them, each with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest part of my own position I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss. Including the question I'm praying nobody asks.
