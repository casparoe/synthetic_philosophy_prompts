I'm a backend engineer at a medium-sized company and I keep running into a weird pattern in our incident postmortems, and I'm hoping someone can help me pick it apart. We use a shared doc template with sections like "What was the impact?" and "What will we do differently?" Lately the docs have started to read like a negotiation: people write things like "Since the migration has made on-call strictly worse, we should move back to the old architecture," or "Until we fix the flakiness in the retry layer, any further work on it is wasted effort." The thing is, the "since" and "until" clauses are often exactly the things people disagree about. When a coworker talked back to the first one last week, the author got annoyed and said we should table the migration debate because the section was only meant to discuss next steps. But then the rest of the meeting ran on top of the disputed claim anyway — the action items all assumed the migration was a loss.

I've started to see this everywhere: someone phrases a contested point as background, and the group either spends the whole discussion relitigating it or quietly absorbs it because moving on feels more productive. My question is partly practical and partly conceptual: what exactly is a sentence like that doing? Is it fair to treat a disputed claim as old news just because the grammar lets it stay off to the side? Is there a reasoned way to decide which buried claims have to be called out, versus which ones can stay buried? And as someone who reviews a lot of these docs, how should I respond without being the pedant who derails the meeting?

I guess I want someone to make sense of the invisible rules here, because there clearly are rules — everyone can feel when a move like that is "playing dirty" — but I can't state them explicitely. Please say how confident you are in your account, and single out the part you're least sure about.
