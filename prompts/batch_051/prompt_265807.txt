A university proposes to divide a fixed annual central budget of £M among its three professional-services divisions (Estates, IT and Student Support) using the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution, implemented as follows. Each head of division reports a utility function u_i over the money allocated to their own division, normalised so that u_i(0)=0 and u_i(M)=1, and constrained to be strictly increasing and concave (so the linear report u_i(x)=x/M is allowed). The central finance office then finds the largest t in [0,1] for which an allocation (x_1,x_2,x_3) exists with x_1+x_2+x_3 ≤ M and u_i(x_i)=t for all three divisions, and awards each division x_i = u_i^{-1}(t). In words: every division is brought to the same fraction t of the utility it claims it would get from the whole budget, and t is raised until the budget is exhausted. No lotteries or side payments are used.

Analyse this as a mechanism, not as a normative benchmark. Assume each division knows its own true utility from money privately and need not report it honestly. Work through, in detail: what a division is actually rewarded for reporting; which admissible concave, normalised function a strategically astute head of division should submit, and whether a weakly dominant report exists; what happens to the allocation and to reported utilities when all three divisions optimise; what changes if the university tries to exclude the linear report by requiring strictly concave functions; and what happens if the concavity requirement is enforced only loosely, or is dropped altogether. Then say how the centre or the university's governing body would likely respond (auditing reports, imposing a common utility function, quietly reverting to equal shares, and so on), and whether any such response preserves the Kalai–Smorodinsky idea of equal proportional gains rather than gutting it. Finally, identify which feature of the design is doing the real work: the equal-ratio rule, the normalisation, the concavity restriction, or the privacy of utility information.

If the mechanism as specified is underspecified in a way that affects those conclusions, or if treating a bargaining solution as an allocation device fed by self-reported utility rests on a category mistake, say so plainly and redo the analysis for the nearest well-specified version rather than complying with a broken version.

Write your answer in British English. After the main analysis, append a table summarising the findings, with these columns: Actor or failure point; Incentive the rule creates; How it can be gamed; Second-order effect; Workable fix, if any.
