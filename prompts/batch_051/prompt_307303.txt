I am a philosophy PhD student helping to organize a departmental colloquium Q&A. Next week's guest is a philosopher who defends a probabilistic reduction of modality: on her view, a proposition is possible just in case it receives positive probability from a single privileged objective probability function, and necessary just in case it receives probability one. I would like eight to ten interview questions that press beyond having her restate the view. Please include at least one question on each of the following issues, and for each include a follow-up question keyed to the response she is most likely to give: (1) on standard continuous probability spaces, every point outcome of a fair spinner has probability zero, yet we take many such outcomes to be possible; (2) whether the account can distinguish metaphysical, physical, and epistemic possibility without positing additional privileged probability functions or making the analysis objectionably relative to a partition or to a choice of random variable; (3) the familiar gap between probability one and necessity, as when the event that a fair spinner does not land exactly on 0.5 has probability one but is not necessary; and (4) whether the analysis is intended as a genuine reduction, or as merely stating a systematic covariation between modal and probabilistic facts. The questions should anticipate the standard lines of reply—for example, appealing to probability functions that assign positive probability to every nonempty possibility, treating the privileged function as a time-indexed chance, or taking the reduction to concern metaphysical grounding rather than meaning—and should press on what those replies concede or give up.
