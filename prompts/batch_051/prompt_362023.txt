I have been trying to make sense of David Chalmers's distinction between the hard problem of consciousness and what he calls the meta-problem. The meta-problem is to explain why beings like us produce the particular verbal reports we do: that there is something it is like to see red, that a clever functional duplicate might lack it, and that no amount of mechanistic story about neural activity seems to close the gap. I am developing a proposal that treats these problem reports as outputs of a single cognitive economy. On this view, the very same mechanisms that generate ordinary first-person phenomenal judgments also generate the further intuition that those judgments cannot be fully explained in third-person terms. The conceptual claim I want to test is this: the intuition of an explanatory gap is a byproduct of the processes that generate immediate phenomenal self-reports, rather than a downstream philosophical achievement or a separate introspective faculty.

How would you test that claim? I do not want a survey of theories; I want a concrete experimental design. Specify the manipulation, measurement, or model comparison you would use, the predicted pattern of problem reports under the byproduct hypothesis and under at least one rival hypothesis, and what result would count as strong evidence for one side or the other. For example, you might consider whether the gap intuition survives when the resources for philosophical reflection are occupied, whether it emerges in systems trained only to produce first-person behavioral reports, or whether deficits in certain forms of self-monitoring selectively eliminate the gap intuition while leaving ordinary phenomenal reports intact. Then say exactly what the test would and would not settle. Could a positive result ever show that the hard problem itself is a cognitive illusion, or only that one route to the intuition is causal? Could a negative result ever show that the gap reports are produced by a distinct module, or only that the byproduct story is incomplete? Where does the empirical content end and the metaphysical residue begin?

Finally, tell me the point in your reasoning where you are least confident, and what empirical finding or argument would lead you to revise the proposed test or your interpretation of it.
