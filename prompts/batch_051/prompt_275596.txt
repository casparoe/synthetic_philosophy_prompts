Been going down a rabbit hole about an old thought experiment and I'm trying to organize the positions people take, because I have to lead a discussion on it next week and I don't want to miss a standard move. The setup (I think it's Dennett's careenium, though I may have the details a bit off) is a giant glass case full of thousands of little balls — "simmballs" — bouncing around, and the whole contraption happens to implement a running program with symbols in it, say it's playing chess. Some configurations of balls count as "queen to d5," some count as "in check," and so on. The question the thing is supposed to force is: when the game logic has the queen move to d5, do the symbols push the balls around, or do the balls only ever push each other and the "symbols" are just how us humans keep score?

So far I've collected five moves people make in response. (1) The eliminativist one: there are no symbols, full stop; the micro-collisions are the whole causal story and symbol-talk is projection. (2) The epiphenomenalist one: the symbols are real, but they ride on top of the physics and never do anything back down to the balls. (3) The coarse-graining one: symbol-causation is just micro-causation described at a coarser grain, so "the symbol caused the move" is no spookier than "the traffic jam caused the fender-bender." (4) The interventionist/multiple-realizability one: the symbol-level state is a real cause because it's invariant across wildly different ball configurations, and you can wiggle the symbol and get systematic changes while the micro details are just implementation. (5) The real-patterns one: the symbol description is a real, compressible, predictive pattern, so it earns its keep, but that doesn't license any extra downward push — causation talk is stance-relative.

I keep feeling like there's a sixth strategy that isnt any of these and isnt just a combination of them, but I can't put my finger on it. Give me one if you can. And be honest: does it actually do extra work, or does it collapse back into one of the five when you press on it?
