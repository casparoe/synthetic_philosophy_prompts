Last night I got into a long argument with a friend about whether "Hesperus is Phosphorus" says any more than "Hesperus is Hesperus", and I came away realizing my gut calls on the usual cases might not hang together. I'm trying to see whether there's one principle behind them, or wether I need to give some of them up.

My verdicts. "Hesperus is Hesperus" strikes me as empty and trivial; "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is a genuine piece of information, the kind of thing an ancient observer could know the first of without knowing the second. But in a plain extensional sentence like "Hesperus is the second planet from the sun", swapping in "Phosphorus" changes nothing for me; they're both just true. With Lois Lane, before she learns the identity I won't count "Lois believes Clark Kent can fly" as a true report of what she believes, even though I'll happily say "Lois believes Superman can fly" and Superman just is Clark Kent; after the reveal I'd accept both. Same with Cicero and Tully: I think someone can believe Cicero denounced Catiline without believing Tully denounced Catiline, and I'd call the first report true and the second false, at least as a report of what they'd endorse themselves.

What spooks me is that I also don't think there's any special Fregean sense out there in the world. As far as I can tell, "Superman is Clark Kent" is just saying an object is identical to itself, and there's only one object involved. My friend kept insisting that commits me to treating substitution as safe everywhere, which my Lois and Cicero verdicts plainly don't.

So: is there any clean account that respects all these calls at once? What would it say about a case I keep flip-flopping on: "the author of Waverley is the author of Ivanhoe", assuming for the sake of argument it's the same person? Is that informative in the way "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is, or is something different going on because the two terms are descriptions rather than names? And if getting all my verdicts means paying too high a theoretical price, which ones should I drop?
