Last night I spent a long evening arguing with a friend who works in civil-rights law about what makes a ground of discrimination suspect, in the sense of triggering heightened scrutiny rather than the light-touch review that everything else receives. Across the series of cases we went through, her verdicts were stable: race, sex, religion, and sexual orientation are suspect classifications, while age, disability, poverty, and body size are not. The hard part was extracting a rule from her. She rejects immutability as the test, since age and many disabilities are unchosen and she still will not call them suspect. She rejects political powerlessness as the test, since children and non-voters are as powerless as any group and she does not treat being under eighteen or being unregistered as protected grounds. And she resists the claim that relevance to ability is decisive, since body size is usually irrelevant to the decisions she worries about and she still will not classify it as suspect, while sex can occasionally be relevant and she does. When pressed, she kept circling back to two things: whether a ground is the kind of status that has historically been used to mark someone as a lesser member of the community, and whether the state ever has a legitimate reason to sort people by it. We could not tell which of those two was doing the real work, or whether only their conjunction is the rule.

I want to predict how she will come down on cases we never reached. Do you think she will say that pregnancy is a suspect classification? What about an inherited genetic marker for a serious disease, or a criminal record? I am less interested in the bare answers than in the rule she seems to be running, the rival rules that fit her stated verdicts equally well, and which of those new cases would best tell the rules apart. For each new case, identify the closest analog among the grounds she has already judged and give your best guess about her verdict, with the reasons for preferring one candidate rule over the others. Finally, name a further case, not yet on her list, that would cleanly separate the leading candidate rules.
