I'm putting together a short position paper for a philosophy-and-physics discussion group and I want a consistency check on the whole package of views I've ended up with. The pieces feel like they fit, but when I try to explain why they fit, I end up talking in circles. Here's the package.

First, I think the basic dynamical laws are time-symmetric in the way that matters: leave aside the exotic weak-interaction stuff—nobody thinks the thermodynamic arrow is coming from that. Second, I think the thermodynamic arrow—entropy increasing toward the future—is explained entirely by a boundary condition: the universe began in an extremely low-entropy state, and the increase is just what overwhelmingly probably happens when you evolve forward from such a state. Third, I think this same gradient explains why records work: footprints, fossils, tree rings, and memories are the kind of structure that forms on the future side of a low-entropy-to-high-entropy gradient, so they point pastward, and their reliability about the deep past is an ordinary empirical matter. Fourth, I think causation genuinely runs from earlier to later; it isn't just a convention of our explanatory practices or a projection of our interests. Fifth, I think the future is open and the past is fixed in an objective way—tommorow could go several genuinely different ways, but yesterday could only have gone one way. And sixth, I want to say the low-entropy past isn't something I simply stipulate: if I start from present records and memories as data, I should be able to arrive at the low-entropy early universe as the best explanation of them.

Before I present this, could you go through and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which only look like they're in tension (I suspect one of the famous contradictions in this area isn't really a contradiction), and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't just say it's all compatible—I want the problems.
