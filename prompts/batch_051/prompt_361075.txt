I have been arguing with friends about whether "effective altruism" is more than a brand name, and we keep getting stuck on what would make an altruistic action genuinely effective. The definitions I encounter tend to fall back on something like "doing the most good you can with a given set of resources," but as soon as the cases get difficult, the term seems to do more work than people admit. I would like you to help me build a working definition of "effective altruistic action" (or, if you think that phrase is unhelpful, of "doing good effectively") that can actually be tested against hard cases.

Consider the following cases: (a) a young engineer takes a well-paid job at a trading firm and gives away half her income to a malaria-prevention charity; (b) a campaigner works for years to change labor-law enforcement in a country where exploitation is widespread, knowing the eventual effects on well-being are real but almost impossible to measure over a twenty-year horizon; (c) a donor gives unrestricted cash directly to very poor households, where there is decent evidence of benefit but no single headline metric like lives saved per dollar; (d) a funder backs a program with superb randomized-controlled-trial evidence that nevertheless leaves untouched the political and economic institutions reproducing the problem it treats; (e) a philanthropist endows an art museum in a middle-income city, arguing that future generations may value it in ways no cost-effectiveness analysis can currently capture; and (f) a donor supports research on an existential risk that may never materialize, where expected-value estimates are dominated by probability assignments about which reasonable people differ by orders of magnitude.

I want a definition that gives a clear verdict on each case where you think one is warranted, that says plainly where the definition yields no answer or the wrong answer, and that gets revised rather than merely defended when the cases expose a flaw. If you think the term is really doing two jobs at once — one about measurable outcomes and one about an agent's situated judgment — say so explicitly, and give me both definitions plus a rule for which one governs when.
