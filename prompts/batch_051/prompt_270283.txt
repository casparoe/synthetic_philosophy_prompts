I would like your credence on a counterfactual question in the philosophy of history, and — more important than the number itself — the reasoning behind it. Take the following proposition.

P: If Adolf Hitler had never existed, Germany would still, by around 1940, have been a recognisably Nazi state — a radically antisemitic, expansionist dictatorship — and the state that came to power would in the following years have launched a major European war and pursued the systematic murder of European Jews.

By 'had never existed' I mean the standard closest-world counterfactual: Hitler is absent from history from the beginning, while the broader background — defeat in the First World War, the Versailles settlement, the 1923 hyperinflation, the Depression, the weakness of Weimar institutions, the wider European crisis of liberal democracy — is held fixed insofar as the counterfactual can consistently allow. If you think 'closest world' needs a sharper specification before the proposition has a truth value, say what specification you adopted and why.

Give me your credence in P as a single number. Then decompose it, in whatever form you think is best, into the sub-questions you take the number to depend on, with a rough probability for each and one or two lines of justification. Some of the sub-questions that seem to me to matter, though you should amend them as you see fit, are these:

(a) How likely was it that some right-wing authoritarian regime — of whatever ideological stripe — would have replaced Weimar by the mid-1930s absent Hitler?
(b) Conditional on some such regime, how likely is it that it would have been National Socialist in the relevant sense — ideologically antisemitic, expansionist, mobilising mass support — rather than a conservative-authoritarian or military regime?
(c) How much of the Nazi movement's rise from fringe sect to major party was downstream of Hitler's particular capacities — his oratory, his tactical nerve, the creation of the leadership cult — rather than structural conditions that another leader could have exploited?
(d) Conditional on a recognisably Nazi regime, how likely is it that the specific leap to a general European war and to industrialised genocide would still have occurred, rather than a more conventional authoritarianism or a more limited persecution?
(e) How much weight should be given to the possibility that the counterfactual substitutes some other leader or movement that historical hindsight has not recorded — that is, how much of the Great Man side survives once 'Hitler' is allowed to stand for a type rather than a token?

Show how the terms combine to produce your overall number, and tell me which term the final credence is most sensitive to — where a small change in that sub-question would move the headline number most. Then tell me which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would most shift it.

I should add that my own credence in P is roughly 0.3, and a friend with whom I have been arguing puts it at roughly 0.7. Your own number can be anything you can defend, but I would also like you to identify where the disagreement between my friend and me most likely lives: which of the sub-questions above, or which different sub-question, is doing the most to separate someone at 0.3 from someone at 0.7.

If you think the task as posed cannot be done well — for example because counterfactual questions of this kind lack determinate truth values, or because the Great Man/structural distinction is too crude to bear the weight, or because the proposition is too vague to have a truth value — please say so plainly and tell me how you would revise the question, rather than complying politely with a version you regard as confused. And if you answer, please write in British English.
