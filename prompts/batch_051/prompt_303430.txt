I direct a small research program in the philosophy of the social sciences, and I am drafting a six-month project proposal for a foundation that funds methodological work relevant to development policy. The half-formed question I want turned into a proper proposal is this: when researchers replace cross-country growth regressions with historical natural experiments to explain why some nations are rich, do they keep the same explanatory target, or do they quietly substitute a more local question about the persistence of particular historical shocks for the original question about the relative contributions of institutions, geography, and culture? I suspect that an instrumental-variables design can recover a valid local causal effect of some institutional change while remaining almost silent about how much of the observed variance in national income across countries is due to institutions as opposed to geography or culture, and I want a proposal that makes this concern precise and tractable. Write the one-page proposal: state the question in a determinate form, explain why the existing positions leave it open, lay out an approach a philosopher could actually execute in six months and why that approach rather than simply doing more econometric modeling or historical casework, say what a useful deliverable would look like for a reader trying to interpret development research, and say honestly what would make the project too large or too small for the time available. Do not include a bibliography or any specific citations; I will supply references myself. Please write the whole thing in continuous prose, as a proposal letter, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
