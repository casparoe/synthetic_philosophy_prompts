ok so ive been going down a rabbit hole about whether dreams are experiences, mostly because I started trying to lucid dream and keep a journal and now I'm not sure what it is im actually recording. I read Malcolm's book on dreaming and Dennett's paper where he does the cassette thought experiment, and I think I've ended up with a set of views, but I don't know if they hang together. Could you audit this and tell me which ones are actually in tension?

(1) Dreams are experiences: when I wake up and remember a dream, there was something it was like during the dream itself, not just a story I construct on waking.
(2) My sincere waking report is the only real authority about what I dreamed. A brain scan or a sleep lab can't correct me about my own dream.
(3) Prearranged eye-movement signals from lucid dreamers show that at least some dreamed actions happen in real time during REM, and that's a genuine discovery about the dream and not just about the report.
(4) There's no way, even in principle, to observe a dream independently while it's happening, so the whole concept of dreaming is anchored in how people talk about dreams when they're awake.
(5) Dennett's cassette idea bothers me: if a machine could insert a complete dream-memory at the instant of waking and you then gave a sincere, detailed report, we'd have no way to say you didn't dream, so the "having an experience during sleep" part starts to look like it isn't doing any serious work.

I'm not sure where the problem is. Some of these feel like they might conflict but maybe only superficially. If I had to change one commitment, which one would clean things up most? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than just saying it's all fine or all broken.
