Suppose someone trying to reduce animal suffering is choosing between beef and chicken and begins by counting how many farmed animals are killed. By that arithmetic beef can look better: one steer yields many meals, while a chicken yields only a few. Yet the same person ignores the field animals—mice, voles, insects—killed in growing the feed crops, even though beef requires far more feed per edible calorie than chicken, so an accounting that includes those deaths could reverse the comparison. Is the distinction between the animals we raise and slaughter and the wild animals killed as a side effect of feeding them one that can actually be defended, or is it just an arbitrary line drawn by what is easiest to see? What principle would justify keeping it, and what would giving it up require?
