I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about calendar epochs — the choice of the year from which all other years are numbered — by testing my gut reactions against a list of candidate year-ones. My verdicts are as follows. I reject the traditional Anno Domini epoch even in its Common Era guise: the traditionally computed date is probably wrong, and a global civil calendar should not be anchored to a single tradition's founding event. I also think the absence of a year zero between 1 BCE and 1 CE is a real defect, but I doubt that inserting one would fix what is wrong with that epoch. I am comfortable with the Unix epoch of 1970 as an internal convention for computers, yet I would reject it as the epoch of a civil calendar. I find myself drawn to the proposal to set year one ten millennia before the Common Era, because it puts nearly all of recorded history in positive numbers and removes the BCE/CE asymmetry; but the round ten-thousand-year offset feels like a fudge, and I cannot say why it is better than nine thousand or eleven thousand. I admire the French Republican calendar's decision to start from the founding of the Republic in 1792 as a political statement, but I think it is a bad universal epoch precisely because it turns the calendar into a partisan monument. I think regnal years are honest and fine for their local purposes, but they cannot serve as a shared standard across polities. I reject dating from the first nuclear detonation in 1945: it is precise and historically momentous, but it would make every date carry a silent moral claim, and I do not want a calendar doing that. I also reject dating from the invention of writing, even though it marks the beginning of the historical record, because the date is fuzzy and the choice privileges literate traditions.

I can already see the tensions. I accept one arbitrary recent technical epoch while rejecting another arbitrary recent epoch, and I accept one commemorative political epoch locally while rejecting a commemorative political epoch globally. I would like to know whether there is a single principle about what a civil epoch is for that delivers all or most of these verdicts, and if not, which verdicts should go. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: the first four items should each state a candidate principle and say which of my verdicts it would force me to abandon; the fifth item should state which candidate you would defend, which of my verdicts it still cannot accommodate, and what it predicts about a calendar dated from the first crewed spaceflight in 1961.
