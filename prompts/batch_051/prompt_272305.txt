Im a retired biologist, and I only recently started reading philosophy seriously, so forgive me if I'm reinventing a wheel that got a latin name three hundred years ago. I keep coming back to a distinction I'd like checked.

I've been calling them obligate terminal values. I swiped obligate from biology, where an obligate mutualism is one both parties need to survive, as opposed to a facultative one they could take or leave. The idea is this: some values—truth-seeking, tradition, purity—are almost certainly instrumental in origin, but they only do their instrumental job if the person holding them experiences them as ends in themselves. A truth-seeker who says "I value truth because it pays" is in trouble the moment truth stops paying; worse, they also cant trust their own conclusions, and neither can anyone else, because every report is now priced. The value only works if it puts on a terminal costume and believes it.

I spent forty years around scientists who would die on a hill over a decimal point even when it cost them, and everybody treats that as the virtue making the enterprise possible. Tradition looks the same: its whole point is cheap coordination, but a tradition people follow only when it obviously benefits them today stops coordinating anything—the point is to follow it when the payoff is opaque. Purity too: a disgust system that waited for a cost-benefit analysis would let the poison in.

So the theory: a value feels intrinsic exactly to the extent that treating it as instrumental would break what its for. The felt intrinsicness isn't a mistake to explain away; it's a functional feature, like the way pain has to feel bad to make you yank your hand off the stove. Which means the question "is truth-seeking really intrinsic or just useful?" might be badly posed—real intrinsicness and functional intrinsicness could be the same thing from the inside.

What I want to know: is this a real distinction or just instrumental-values talk with a new coat of paint? Has someone worked this out under a better name (it feels too useful not to be in the literature)? And where does it break—what values does it wrongly count as intrinsic, and which intrinsic-seeming values have no plausible instrumental origin and still need explaining? Give me the two or three strongest objections and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than just uncomfortable. Be hard on it: I'd rather kill the idea now than keep fertilizing a metaphor.
