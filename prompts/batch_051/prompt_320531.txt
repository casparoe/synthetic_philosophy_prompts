Before I read yet another teleosemantics paper, I want the full spec instead of one more case-by-case objection. What should any adequate naturalistic account of mental representation be required to deliver? I mean the desiderata list: the cases it has to get right or at least have a defensible description of — the frog's fly detector, the bee's waggle dance, the swampman, a moth at a porch light, and my harvesting robot that was trained on 'ripe tomato' but only tracts redness and will happily sort red golf balls — plus the distinctions it can't collapse (carrying information vs. being about something, having a function vs. just being selected for, what content a state has vs. what it actually causes the system to do), and the constraints from actual biology and psychology it can't just wave away (representations get redeployed, learned, noisy, distributed). For each requirement give the case or consideration behind it. Then sort them: which are non-negotiable and which are really one camp's taste — e.g., that content must be intrinsic to a physical duplicate, or that distal content always beats proximal. Tell me which two or three you suspect can't be jointly satisfied and why, since that's the part I'm after. Finally, score Millikan-style consumer teleosemantics and Dretske's indicator view against the list, briefly. Keep it under 500 words.
