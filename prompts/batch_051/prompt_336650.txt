My friend and I went back and forth for hours last night about whether a society can respect people's individual rights and their unanimous preferences at the same time without ending in contradiction. He eventually wrote out a numbered argument that he thinks proves it can't, and told me to find the flaw. I cant tell if its valid or if the setup is cheating, so I'd like someone to go through it step by step.

Setup: two people, say Mari and Jules, and one copy of a book. Three possible outcomes: (x) Mari reads it alone, (y) Jules reads it alone, (z) nobody reads it. As a minimal rights idea, suppose each person is decisive about whether they themself read the book: Mari gets to decide between x and z (the two outcomes that differ only in whether she reads), and Jules gets to decide between y and z (the two that differ only in whether she reads).

Preferences: Mari thinks the book is corrupting, so she ranks z above x above y (nobody, then her reading, then Jules reading). Jules thinks the book would be good for Mari and also wants to read it herself, so she ranks x above y above z (Mari reading, then her reading, then nobody).

His argument:
(1) Mari prefers z to x, and she's decisive between exactly those outcomes, so a society that respects her right must rank z above x.
(2) Jules prefers y to z, and she's decisive between exactly those outcomes, so a society that respects her right must rank y above z.
(3) Mari and Jules both prefer x to y. That outcome pair isn't in either person's protected sphere, so the Pareto principle applies: society must rank x above y.
(4) But that gives x over y, y over z, and z over x, which is a cycle. No consistent social ordering.

So his conclusion is that even a minimal form of individual rights is incompatible with the Pareto principle, so something has to give. Is the argument valid? If it is, does that show rights and Pareto really clash, or does it show the "decisiveness" formalization of rights is wrong in some specific way? If it's not valid, which numbered step is the one that fails, and on what reading?
