I've been teaching a grad seminar on the distribution question in animal consciousness this term, and I keep stalling on the same problem. Lately I've been defending a view I call the flexible-behavior criterion: an animal is conscious just in case its behavior flexibly integrates information from several modalities, adjusts to novelty, and lets past experience shape future action. On this view consciousness is graded and widely distributed — it goes to the dog, the crow, the octopus, and, just barely, the bee — and that wide distribution dosen't bother me; in fact I think it's a virtue. The criterion doesn't draw the line at an evolutionarily arbitrary place (cortex, say, or "mammal"), it explains why I'm so sure about the dog without pretending I can inspect its experience, and it gives a principled answer in a domain where almost everyone else just gestures.

But I also have an intuition I can't dislodge. Watching a honeybee learn a route, compensate for crosswind, and then waggle-dance the location to its hivemates, I find myself thinking: that's a magnificent mechanism, and there's nothing it is like to be that bee — full stop. Yet when my dog yelps at a thorn the same evening I can't read the yelp as anything but felt. So the view and the intuition collide exactly in the cases I wanted the view to settle, and I suspect the bee is doing a lot of quiet work whenever I adjudicate weirder cases (nematodes? brittle stars?).

I don't want a list of positions or papers, and I don't want you to just pick a side. I want a negotiated verdict. What would it cost to debunk the intuition — is it merely a failure of imagination or a bias toward the furry and familiar, and what else would we have to give up if we discount it? What would it cost to amend or abandon the flexible-behavior criterion — does it start to look ad hoc the moment I carve out insects, and what does that do to the original non-arbitrariness? And is there a reading on which both survive, for instance because the criterion tracks something real but short of phenomenal consciousness, or because the intuition tracks a threshold I've mislocated?

After the discussion, give me a JSON object with these fields: "verdict" (one of "intuition_should_yield", "theory_should_yield", "theory_should_be_amended", or "both_can_survive"), "cost_of_dropping_intuition" (a string briefly naming what we'd lose by treating the bee intuition as a bias), "cost_of_dropping_theory" (a string briefly naming what we'd lose by abandoning or amending the criterion), "compatibility" (a one-sentence statement of how both might be true, or null), and "bottom_line" (a one-sentence summary of your final position).
