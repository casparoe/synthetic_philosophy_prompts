I write about the life sciences and the philosophy that runs alongside them for a general-interest magazine, and I have lately been trying to get clear about a long-running debate in the philosophy of biology. It concerns what makes something a function of a biological trait. On the selected-effects, or etiological, view, a trait's function is what it was selected to do; on the causal-role, or systemic, view, a trait counts as having a function in virtue of the contribution it currently makes to some capacity of the larger system in which it sits. The sources I read keep reaching for the same handful of stock examples wherever the two accounts come apart, and I would like a cleaner, custom-built case to use when I explain the distinction to readers who have never encountered either idea.

Please construct a thought experiment to my specifications. Invent a single species — real if you can manage it, otherwise biologically plausible — in which one trait has a clear selected-effects function but no present causal role of the kind the systemic view would count as a function, while a second trait in the same species has a robust present causal role but no selection history of its own. Constraints: stay within ordinary evolutionary biology, so no artefacts, no engineered genomes, no science-fictional physiology, and no appeal to group or species selection. Do not reach for the canonical divergences — swim bladders, the human appendix, spandrels, the wings of flightless birds, or the eyes of blind cave fish. And make the second trait historically inert rather than merely co-opted: if it has already been recruited by selection for its present role, the selected-effects theorist can simply claim it as a new function and the contrast collapses. Keep the presentation of the species to a single paragraph.

After the scenario — which should generate these verdicts rather than stipulate them — walk through what each account says about each trait and why. Then identify the structural feature of the case that does the work of producing the divergence, and say what happens to the dissociation if that feature is removed. Finish by saying which account ends up looking more counterintuitive in your scenario and how a defender of that account would resist the way the case has been set up. Please write the answer in American English.
