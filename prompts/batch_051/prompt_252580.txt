Write the joint statement that a defender of pan-sentient expansionism and a defender of integrationist valence would both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about the ethical implications of panpsychism for fundamental physics. The pan-sentient expansionist holds that if the basic furniture of the world has any experiential character at all, then it probably also has some faint valence (a felt good-or-bad quality), and the number of resulting patients is so enormous that ethics would have to be rebuilt around them. The integrationist holds that this is exactly wrong: even granting that microphysical goings-on have some thin experiential quality, suffering and welfare require the integrated, temporally thick, self-modeling dynamics you only find in whole organisms or some machines, so the practical shape of ethics barely changes. Neither camp has to sign off on panpsychism itself being true; the document is about what follows if some moderate version of it were. I want the statement to read like something both could actually put their names on: the claims they agree on, including claims about where the other side is not simply confused; then the residual disagreement stated precisely, with each side's own position worded the way that side would endorse it; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. I suspect two cruxes will be whether a valenced state can exist with no behavioral repertoire at all, and whether summing zillions of barely-positive or barely-negative micro-valences gives coherent moral arithmetic or collapses into the familiar population-ethics swamp where an astronomically large number of barely-good lives swamps a smaller number of very good ones. Put those in your own words and add others if they belong. For each crux, say what argument, model, or concrete case both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Also say which parts of their disagreement they now think were mostly verbal and which are substantive. Do not write this as a scored debate or a dialogue. And please no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables anywhere in your answer: write it all as continuous prose, the way you'd write a long email to a collaborator. Dont structure it with numbered sections either.
