I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, and I've somehow become the person on my team who has to write the internal memo on whether our platform planning should treat current AI as a world-changing discontinuity or as a bubble. While trying to pin down my own view, I collected a set of commitments, and I want to know whether they can all be true together before I circulate anything. Please act as a professional philosopher who works on the philosophy of artificial intelligence and has a strong eye for conceptual audit. I'm not interested in a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want you to stress-test the set of commitments below and tell me where the load-bearing cracks are.

Here is where I've landed:

(1) Transformative AI is best defined by aggregate socioeconomic impact: an AI-driven transition counts as transformative when it reshapes the scale and structure of the economy on the order of the Industrial Revolution, irrespective of the specific capabilities or architectures involved.

(2) Such a transition need not be produced by AGI or by any single unified system; a heterogeneous collection of narrow systems interacting with firms, markets, and governments could jointly produce it.

(3) Current deployments—foundation models, recommender systems, AI-enabled automation—look to me like the early phase of exactly that kind of distributed transition, so I lean toward saying that transformative AI has already happened in an early form.

(4) The question whether transformative AI has happened ought to be settled by fixing a clear, operationalized version of the impact definition and checking it against actual measurable changes; adjusting the definition after the fact to guarantee a yes or a no is illegitimate.

(5) Our current economic data are too noisy, lagged, and confounded to establish whether a change of Industrial-Revolution-scale is actually underway.

(6) Projected future growth should not count as evidence that present systems are transformative; the label has to be earned by what has already occurred, not by what might happen soon.

Could you go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which merely look like they are, and how severe each real tension is? Please start by brainstorming all the candidate tensions you can see before committing to a ranked list. For each genuine tension, say exactly what has to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me; then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean up the package. I would rather the audit be exacting than diplomatic, and I would prefer it to stay focused on my viewset rather than turning into a general essay on transformative AI.
