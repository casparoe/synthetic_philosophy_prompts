I keep being told that Bertrand's paradox matters for the principle of indifference, but I have always written it off as an under-specification problem: if no procedure for selecting a chord is fixed, there is no well-defined probability. I want to know whether that instinct survives a careful translation.

Do the restatement in the vocabulary I listed above: sampling model for the chord-generating process, uniform prior over a parameterization, the mapping between parameterizations, and invariance. Be as precise as you can about what the paradox becomes under this translation, and do not smuggle Bayesian assumptions in and call them translation.

Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the philosophical problem map cleanly onto prior selection under reparametrization? Which parts had to be fudged — for example, where credence about a fixed fact and model of a random process come apart?
