Construct a thought experiment that isolates the dispute between two views about the commensurability of pleasure and pain. The first is lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism: suffering above a specified intensity has lexical priority, so no amount of mild pleasure or lesser pain can compensate for a single such episode, whereas below the threshold ordinary trade-offs apply. The second is a smooth, threshold-free negative utilitarian view, on which the disvalue of pain is a continuous additive function of intensity and duration and mild pleasures can compensate for mild pains. The case should involve one person choosing between two possible futures over a finite period in an ordinary clinical setting. The futures should be identical except that one contains a single pain just below the stipulated threshold and the other a single pain just above it, with mild pleasure added to the latter in whatever finite amount is needed to expose the disagreement. Specify the verdicts: the lexical-threshold view must judge the future containing the above-threshold pain worse regardless of how much mild pleasure is added, whereas the smooth view must judge that same future better once enough mild pleasure is added. After the scenario, show the reasoning for each view, identify the structural feature of the case that does the work, and explain how a defender of the lexical threshold could resist the result without simply relocating the threshold.
