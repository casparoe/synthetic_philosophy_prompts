Imagine that, beginning in 2028, a wearable device is introduced in a mid-sized high-income democracy with a strong civil society, such as Denmark. The device produces a continuous, persuasive experience of being deeply known, accepted, and needed by a cohesive social group that exists only in the device’s simulated social environment. While worn, the experience is indistinguishable from the subjective feel of genuine belonging; use is voluntary and carries no known direct health effects, and the device does not prevent real social contact or the mood consequences of social rejection outside it. Predict, with explicit reasoning, the trajectory over twenty years of: (a) participation in local associations, religious congregations, and informal mutual aid; (b) political trust and the perceived legitimacy of taxation and welfare provision; (c) rates of loneliness and demand for mental health care; and (d) the distribution of these effects by personality, age at first use, and local community density. For each link in your causal chain, state whether the effect is robust, contingent on assumptions you specify, or merely plausible, and identify the conditions under which your overall prediction would fail—for instance, whether simulated and real belonging are substitutes or complements, and what would have to hold for the device to strengthen rather than erode actual communities.
