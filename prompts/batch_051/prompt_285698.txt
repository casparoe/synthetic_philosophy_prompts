I write long ensemble novels, and on every book I hit the same snag. In chapter two a character is 'the anaesthetist'; by chapter five she's 'Dr. Alden' at work, 'Clara' to her husband, 'Mum' to her son. Without fail, a beta reader says they thought Clara and Dr. Alden were two different people and spent forty pages confused. But I can't just pick one name per character and hammer it, because the choice of route does real work: 'Dad' versus 'Mr. Vance' versus 'Thomas' changes the temperature of a scene, and part of the craft is deciding what a point-of-view character would call someone. What I need is a reusable procedure, a checklist I can run on a scene in about five minutes, for deciding wether I can switch how a recurring character is referred to without splitting the reader's sense of the same person, and when I need to add a bridge or stay put. It should cover given names vs family names vs titles vs epithets, and it should cover characters whose name changes inside the story (marriage, transition, witness protection, a pen name that becomes a separate persona) as well as a rename between drafts, which I did last year and got my agent annoyed. Ground it in whatever philosophy of language actually knows about proper names and reference, but translate it into questions I can answer without the theory. Work through two or three short examples, including one where the procedure tells me something I'd rather not do, and mark the points where it's just a judgement call rather than a rule. Also tell me when I'm allowed to ignore the procedure for the sake of the prose.
