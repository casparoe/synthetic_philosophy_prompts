I am a control engineer who designs behavioral architectures for robots and autonomous systems. The vocabulary I actually reason in includes the plant and its environment, state and measurement, observers and Kalman filters, process and observation noise, forward and inverse internal models, model-predictive control, disturbance estimation, and the distinction between a signal's value and its provenance. I can handle formal material readily, but I have no background in phenomenology beyond casual reading.

I want you to restate Thomas Metzinger's self-model theory of subjectivity in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. My working understanding of the position is this: there is no substantial self; what exists is a continuously updated phenomenal self-model produced by the organism. The crucial property of the model is phenomenal transparency: the system cannot represent the model as a model, because it has no access to the processing that constructed it, so it experiences the model's content as immediately real. That, on this view, is why an organism takes itself to be directly in contact with a world and with a self. The feeling of being someone, an agent located here and now, is the content of the transparent self-model rather than evidence of an object that the model depicts. When transparency fails locally, the system can become aware of the model's constructed character.

Give me a claim-by-claim translation into control-theoretic terms. What architectural arrangement corresponds to a transparent self-model as opposed to an opaque one? Is transparency merely the absence of a meta-level signal saying that an internal estimate is only an estimate, or is it something more like a filter that outputs point estimates while discarding its own credibility intervals? What configuration of observers, models, and control loops would make an engineered agent behave as though it had a self, and what would have to change for the agent to catch its own self-model in the act?

Then give the residue report, which is what I most need. Which claims survive the translation cleanly, which become nearly trivial or obviously false once restated in my terms, and which cannot be expressed at all? Concentrate on the claim that there is something it is like to be the system. Is the untranslatability of phenomenal experience a defect of my vocabulary, or evidence that this part of the original position was less precise than it looked? Where the translation distorts the original, say exactly what leaked out and why. Do not reconcile the two frameworks prematurely; the places where the translation fails are the valuable part.
