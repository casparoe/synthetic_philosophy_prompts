I manage a small groundwater-sustainability district in an over-drafted basin. State law lets my office approve, condition, or deny petitions for new or expanded pumping under a loose standard of reasonable and beneficial use, and my board must sign off on each written order; I hear new petitions every few weeks, and nearly every decision is challenged before an administrative judge. I want to stop deciding by case-by-case sympathy and instead run each petition through Locke's labour theory of acquisition and the Lockean proviso — roughly, that a person acquires rights by mixing their labour with an unowned resource, but only where what remains for others is enough and of comparable quality. Turn those principles into a step-by-step decision procedure I can run within a two-hour hearing and a short order, with the tests applied in sequence, the hard cases the procedure must handle, and the points where Locke stops giving a determinate answer and I am simply making a policy choice, marked as such. Work the procedure through three petitions: (a) a ranching family that has irrigated pasture for decades without a recorded right and now wants that use formalized; (b) an outside investor who wants to buy irrigated land, fallow it, and sell the conserved water to a nearby city; and (c) a new well that would serve a growing school district. Include at least one application in which the procedure gives my board an answer it will strongly dislike, and say explicitly what the procedure cannot settle.
