I cover the science-and-ideas beat for a magazine, and I'm currently writting a piece on why evolution creates such a headache for the doctrine that creation was originally good. My interviewees keep trying to settle the whole debate with one example -- the ichneumon wasp laying its eggs in a caterpillar so the larvae can eat it alive, say, or fossils showing disease and injury in animals from long before any human existed -- but nobody will give me the full spec before they start defending their view.

Before I write another word, I want to know what an adequate account of animal suffering before the Fall would actually have to deliver. Give me the requirements list: the cases it has to handle (predation, parasitism, disease, mass extinction, the sheer timescale of deep time), the distinctions it can't afford to collapse (pain vs. suffering, death as a natural process vs. death as punishment, the Fall as historical event vs. story about the human condition), and the biological findings it can't wave away or reinterpret out of existence. For each requirement, give the reason it earns its place. Then flag which requirements are non-negotiable for any serious theological account and which are really just the preferences of one camp or another, and say which single requirement knocks out the most popular current views. If some subset of the list can't be jointly satisfied, say so plainly -- that is the paragraph my editor actually wants.
