I'm trying to decide whether to accept a job that would move me across the country. I've gone back and forth for weeks, and a close friend has lost patience. He points out that I've turned down every comparable offer in the last decade and that I'm strongly risk-averse, and he says there's roughly an 80% chance I'll stay. He thinks I should adopt that number as my own, make my plans around it, and stop pretending the question is still open. But when I try to adopt it, I run into a wall. If I really think I'm 80% likely to stay, then the decision seems to have been made behind my back: I'm no longer choosing, just forecasting my own passivity. If I instead refuse to put any number on my own future choice, I'm treating my own behavior as the one event in the universe I can't reason about probabilistically, and I can't see why I should be so special. And if I settle on some number only after asking how the number itself would change my behavior, I can't tell whether I'm discovering the probability or creating it. Is there something fundamentally confused about assigning probabilities to a choice you're still in the middle of making, or is my friend right that this is just ordinary evidence-based prediction about a person who happens to be me? Give me the sharpest account you can of what's going on, and then say how I should approach the decision.
