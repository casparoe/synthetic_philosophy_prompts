I keep seeing importance–tractability–neglectedness (ITN) treated as the default grammar of cause prioritization: disagreements are almost always about the inputs—how important, how tractable, how neglected—rather than about whether the framework itself is the right way to compare causes at all. Play it straight. What would have to be true for ITN, as a general framework for ranking causes, to be systematically misleading rather than merely misapplied in a particular case? I want a map of its exposure, not a defense or an attack: which working assumptions of the framework could fail, what the world would have to look like for each failure, which failure modes are live rather than merely logically possible, and whether the framework as usually qualified has insulated itself from any evidence that could count against it. Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
