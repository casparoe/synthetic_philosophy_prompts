Standard economic accounts treat the sunk cost fallacy as a paradigm of irrationality: once a cost is sunk, it cannot be recovered by continuing, so it should drop out of the decision. Yet a persistent camp in behavioral economics and the philosophy of practical rationality has argued that honoring sunk costs can be rational, because doing so can protect commitments, preserve self-consistency and self-knowledge, or head off regret.
