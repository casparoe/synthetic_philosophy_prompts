I lead trust-and-safety policy for a large platform hosting user-generated text and video. Our policy reviews repeatedly deadlock over the same argument. One camp holds that groups using the platform to advance an ideology that denies equal standing to some class of people must be removed, because a society that tolerates every position — including positions that reject tolerance themselves — eventually destroys its own capacity for tolerance. The opposing camp replies that this simply converts the reviewer's politics into a moderation rule and invites every faction to call its opponents intolerant in order to have them silenced. At present each case is decided individually, by whichever reviewer happens to be assigned, and the inconsistency has become a fairness and audit problem.

Please turn the principle behind the first camp's argument into a decision procedure an on-call policy lead can actually run in under an hour. The procedure should specify, in order: the questions to ask; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; at which step the case closes with "leave up" versus "restrict or remove"; and at which steps the policy lead may close the matter alone rather than send it to legal counsel or to an executive who owns platform-wide precedent. Mark each step that is a value judgment rather than an empirical question, and say what the procedure assumes as a premise at those points. Explain how the procedure treats three different kinds of content — advocacy of an illiberal future state of affairs; speech that plausibly contributes to coercion, harassment, or violence against identifiable people; and speech that is merely offensive or insulting — and how it updates when a group's capacity to act grows or shrinks. Also explain how it handles a group whose public rhetoric is restrained while its members coordinate doxxing or harassment in private channels on the same platform.

Then run the procedure on three cases. In the first, an account repeatedly posts that a particular religious minority is quietly taking control of the civil service, and that a future legitimate government should first suspend that minority's hiring eligibility and later its right to vote; the posts are framed as proposals for a hypothetical government and name no individuals. In the second, a large, well-organized group runs a public page proclaiming that it will give its enemies no platform and no warning, while members in a private channel on the same platform dox immigration-rights organizers, publishing home addresses alongside threats. In the third, a vocal civil-rights organization posts sustained and deeply personal denunciations of named sitting officials — accusing them of cowardice and bad faith — but proposes no change to anyone's legal rights or liberties and coordinates no action beyond calls for lawful protest.

For each case, state the procedure's recommendation, the specific facts that tipped it, and, where an honest reviewer could disagree, the strongest argument for the opposite outcome. If the procedure reaches an outcome the platform's leadership would prefer not to defend publicly, say so plainly. Close with a short account of what the procedure cannot settle because the underlying questions are about what the platform is for. Write the answer in American English.
