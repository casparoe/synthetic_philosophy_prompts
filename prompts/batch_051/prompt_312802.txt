My housemate has oddly settled views about which things in games matter morally. She's sure a named companion in an RPG, the kind who remembers how you treated her and can leave, counts. She also counts a dog companion that can't talk but can be hurt and can die permanently, and a farming-sim villager with a daily routine and friendships. But she says the anonymous enemies you mow down in a co-op shooter don't count, that the pedestrians in an open-world city game don't count even though they scream and flop when you hit them, and that a Tetris block obviously doesn't count. Do you think she would say a Tamagotchi-style pet that can starve or die but has no language, no name, no story and no relationships counts? What about a procedurally generated colonist in a base-builder who has traits, social ties and memories but is basically a bundle of stats, or an AI non-player character who can hold a real conversation and seems self-aware but resets every session and remembers nothing? I don't just want the yes/no's: I want the principle she seems to be running, the other principles that fit her verdicts just as well, and which of those three new cases would best separate them. Oh, and could you write the answer in British English?
