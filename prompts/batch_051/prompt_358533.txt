I'm compiling discussion notes for a seminar on whether the output of generative image models can be genuinely creative. I've been collecting the conditions that participants in the debate have proposed as requirements for real creativity — as opposed to the mere generation of novel-looking recombinations of prior work — and my current list contains five. I believe these five cover the conditions most commonly named in the debate, so I am not asking you to recall a famous one I have omitted; I want a further candidate condition, not necessarily already standard, that a thoughtful participant could seriously defend. The purpose is to expand the menu rather than complete it.

Here is the list:

1. Intentionality. The maker must act on an intention to make the thing as art. It must have a point of view, and its choices must be made for reasons rather than merely as outputs of an optimization process.

2. Autonomy. The maker must set or at least reshape its own problem rather than merely satisfying a prompt, brief, or objective supplied from outside; genuine creativity requires problem-finding as well as problem-solving.

3. Risk. The maker must be capable of genuine artistic failure — of being misunderstood, repudiated, or finding that what it attempted does not come off — so that something is actually at stake in its choices.

4. Situated responsiveness. The maker must stand inside a living practice, aware of its precedents, conventions, and present state, and must respond to those rather than merely assembling statistical regularities in prior work.

5. Embodied affect. The maker must draw on felt experience — sensation, emotion, desire, the contingencies of a lived life — rather than only on patterns extracted from other people's felt experiences.

Please supply one additional candidate condition of this kind. It may be defensible or not; that is separate from the task of formulating it. But it must be a condition that someone could actually defend in serious debate, and it must not be a restatement, a special case, or a combination of the five items above.

Answer with a single JSON object containing the following fields:

- "condition": the candidate condition, stated as a necessary condition on artistic creativity, concise enough to stand as a bullet point alongside the five above.
- "defense": the strongest one- or two-sentence defense of treating this as a distinct requirement.
- "distinctness": which of the five listed conditions this one most resembles, why it is nevertheless not a restatement or special case of it, and what it adds.
- "best_objection": the strongest objection to including this condition, and whether that objection can be answered.
- "verdict": your judgment of whether the new condition advances the debate beyond the five listed, using exactly one of: "strong addition", "weak addition", "collapses under analysis".

No prose outside the JSON object.
