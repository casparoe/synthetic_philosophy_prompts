I'm trying to figure out whether "DNA contains information" is a statement of fact or just a metaphor, and what principle actually decides when a stretch of DNA carries information. My gut verdicts: a protein-coding gene carries information. A silent point mutation — same amino acid, no detectable effect on anything — carries no information. A promoter region in front of a gene, which is never translated into protein, does carry information. The complementary strand across from a protein-coding gene? I'd say it carries the same information, but I also feel weird saying that, because the information seems to live in the sequence, and it's literally a different sequence. An intron that gets spliced out and has no known function carries no information. A methylation mark that stably silences a gene carries information, but I dont know whether to call it genetic. Is there one principle that draws these lines, or do I have to choose which verdicts to abandon? And does the winning principle make "DNA is a code" true, or should we drop the word "code"?
