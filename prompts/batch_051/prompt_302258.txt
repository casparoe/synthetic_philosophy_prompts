Build me a case I can use in an argument about whether a language could do without indexicals — one that isolates the thing "I", "here", and "now" do that definite descriptions can't. Specs: exactly two people, ordinary present-day setting, no amnesia, no twins or duplicates, no time travel, and no hidden objective facts — both people start with the same complete stock of non-indexical facts. The only thing that differs between them is indexical (one can truly say or think something with "I" or "now" that the other can't), and that difference needs a practical payoff: a rescue happens too late, an apology goes to the wrong person, money lands in the wrong account. Then run the check in exactly five numbered parts, each a sentance or two, no intro or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) the shared non-indexical facts; (3) the indexical difference and the concrete failure it causes; (4) why adding more descriptions can't patch the gap, and which feature of the case is doing the real work; (5) how a defender of a pronounless language would attack the case and why their fix falls short.
