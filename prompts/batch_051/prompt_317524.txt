Design a thought experiment to the following specification. The scenario must depict a person suffering a clear and serious privacy violation even though no one — not the violator, not the victim, not any third party — acquires any new information about the victim, and the victim acquires no new information about anything either. The purpose of the case is to test whether privacy can be reduced to the flow or control of personal information, so the verdict may not simply be stipulated: it must fall out of the structure of the case under the leading accounts of privacy. Keep the setting ordinary (an apartment building, a workplace, a household, or a clinic), and avoid exotic technology, surveillance-state apparatus, and science-fictional powers. The wrong must not reduce to breach of a prior confidentiality agreement, and it must not depend on information later being used to cause some further harm; the privacy wrong itself should be doing the moral work.

After presenting the scenario, do all of the following. First, verify the zero-information specification: itemize exactly what each party sees, learns, touches, or gains access to, and explain why none of it counts as acquiring new information about the victim, addressing the objection that mere presence or access is itself information. Second, work through how each of three accounts of privacy would classify the case — a control-based account, a restricted-access account, and a contextual-integrity account — and state whether each one yields the verdict that a privacy violation occurred, and why. Third, identify the single feature of the scenario that carries the moral weight on the most plausible verdict, and say what changes if that feature is removed: does the case cease to be a privacy violation, or does it turn into a different kind of wrong? Fourth, give the strongest response available to a reductionist who holds that privacy just is control over information about oneself, and say whether that response ultimately succeeds. Write the response in American English.
