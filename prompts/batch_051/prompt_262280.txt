You're a philosopher who works on responsibility and collective action, and I want help identifying a distinction I keep noticing. Please think out loud before settling on an answer: brainstorm the candidate concepts, test each against my cases, and don't just hand me a term and stop.

I keep seeing a move in arguments about whether someone should leave a bad institution or stop buying from a bad company, and I don't have a word for exactly what the accusation comes to.

Case one: an engineer at a company that runs immigration detention works on the software that schedules staff shifts. The centers are abusive. She says: my job doesn't cause the abuse; if I quit they'd replace me in a few weeks and the software would still get maintained; my individual contribution makes no difference. The reply she gets is: that's not the point, you're taking part, you clock in every day, your name is on the code, you're one of them.

Case two: someone buys shoes from a company that uses child labor. They're told the same sort of thing: your one purchase doesn't cause any child to be hired, the supply chain won't notice, but you're still joining your life to a system that runs on child labor, and that's what's wrong. Again, the accusation isn't that the act tipped anything.

The relation being pointed at isn't causing, helping, or enabling, because the wrong would be exactly as bad without the person. It also seems different from the expected-utility argument that your vote or purchase has some tiny chance of being decisive; the people making the accusation usually aren't making that argument. Is it just complicity? If so, what exactly is the relation — is it about endorsement, self-identification, benefiting, signaling, allegiance, membership? I care less about the label than the carving: if there are several nearby concepts, tell me which fit the cases best and how to tell them apart. If the two cases actually point at different relations, or if the causal/non-causal line I drew is confused, say so plainly instead of forcing an answer.
