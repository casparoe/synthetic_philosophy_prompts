Over the last couple of weeks I've been having a running row with my flatmate, a physics PhD student, about whether the 'now' is a real moving spotlight or just something parochial about our frame. His argument, which I gather is more or less the Rietdijk–Putnam thing, goes like this. Pick two spacelike seperated events: a supernova going off somewhere in Andromeda, and me typing this in my kitchen. Depending on an observer's state of motion, the supernova can count as before, after, or simultaneous with my typing. Since no inertial frame is privileged, there is no frame-independent fact about whether the supernova is in my 'now'. But if whatever is simultaneous with anybody's here-and-now, for any legitimate inertial observer, counts as real, and if being real is transitive across these different observers' slices, then by hopping from slice to slice you can make every event real: Caesar crossing the Rubicon, me typing, the heat death of the universe. So the moving present is an illusion and the block is all there is.

I don't want 'it depends on your interpretation of relativity', I want a number. Give me your credence that this argument actually succeeds in establishing the block universe (all events exist tenselessly and there is no objective moving now). Decompose it into at least these four bits and give each a rough number: (1) the standard Minkowski reading of special relativity, with no preferred foliation, is right; (2) 'is real now' has to be a frame-independent relation, and realness is transitive in the way the argument needs it to be; (3) presentist-friendly moves—neo-Lorentzian ether theories, a preferred global foliation, denying transitivity, whatever else—either fail outright or cost more than they are worth; (4) your prior on eternalism before any of the physics comes in. Show how you combine the four into the final figure, tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would shift you most. Then tell me where you think my flatmate's 0.9 and my 0.25 most likely diverge.
