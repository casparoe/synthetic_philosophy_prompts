Analyze the one-year disappearance of the U.S. federal estate tax in 2010 as a case through the lens of the theory that inheritance taxation is a meaningful instrument of equal opportunity across generations, preventing family advantage from hardening into hereditary privilege. For background: after a decade-long phaseout, the estate tax fell to zero for people who died in 2010. At the end of that year, Congress restored the tax with a $5 million per-person exemption and a 35 percent top rate, made the restoration retroactive to January 1, 2010, and gave the estates of 2010 decedents a choice. They could elect the repealed-tax regime, under which there was no estate tax but inherited assets generally retained the decedent's original basis, so embedded capital gains remained taxable if heirs later sold the assets; or they could elect the restored regime, under which the estate paid tax above the exemption but inherited assets generally received a step-up in basis to date-of-death value, extinguishing those embedded gains.

Do not treat this as an occasion to rehearse familiar arguments for or against the estate tax. Instead, be disciplined about the mapping. The theory implicitly posits certain actors, and certain mechanisms, even if its advocates seldom make them explicit. Who are the players the theory must be about, and what were their actual options during and around 2010? What does the theory predict each of them should have done, and what does it count as the relevant evidence? Which features of the episode does the theory explain well, which does it flatten or misdescribe, and where is it simply silent? Then run the analysis in the other direction: what does 2010 show about the theory itself? Does it tend to confirm the theory, expose it as incomplete, or suggest that the operative mechanism is not the headline tax rate but something else, such as the basis rules, the exemption level, the retroactive and unpredictable way the law changed, or the fact that the tax touches only a narrow slice of estates? Be explicit, too, about where this episode is not a clean natural experiment and which inferences should not be drawn from it.
