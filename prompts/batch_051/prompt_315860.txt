Before I read any actual theories, I want the spec one has to meet to count as an adequate theory of moral compromise. What cases must it handle — someone treating something as sacred and untradeable, big power assymetries, one-shot vs repeated interactions, compromises that look good now but erode later? What distinctions can't it collapse, like compromise vs coercion vs capitulation, or trading preferances vs trading principles? What does it have to assume about values being comparable on a single scale, if anything? For each requirement, give the case or reason why it's a requirement. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste, and whether any two of the non-negotiable ones can't be satisfied together. I care about the requirements list itself, not a scorecard of existing views. Write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — like an email.
