Me and my friend got in a long argument last night about whether morality is just an old trick played by the weak on the strong, after he went through some Nietzsche explainer. I can't get his argument out of my head. As near as I can reconstruct it (and I don't even care if this is a fair reading of Nietzsche): (1) A lot of our values—humility, pity, turning the other cheek, not wanting to dominate people—got their grip on us because a group of powerless people under Rome couldn't actually fight the noble warriors they hated, so they flipped the value table: the nobles' strength and pride became "evil," and their own weakness and powerlessness became "good." (2) If a set of values got its hold on you through that kind of resentful, self-serving move, then you have reason to distrust those values. (3) Once you have reason to distrust a value, you shouldn't keep treating it as something that actually tells you how to live. (4) So we shouldn't treat humility or pity as authoritative—theyre just leftovers of an old rationalization.

He says every step is airtight, and I keep feeling like he's smuggling something in around step 2 or 3, but I couldn't pin it down. So, is the argument valid? If not, which step fails exactly, and what would a more careful version of that step say? And if some weakened version does go through, what is it actually entitled to conclude?
