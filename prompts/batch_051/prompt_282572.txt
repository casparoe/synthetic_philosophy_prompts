so I've been down a rabbit hole trying to understand why time only seems to go one way. Everything I read keeps landing on the same story: the fundamental laws don't actually care about past and future, they run equally well forwards and backwards, and the only reason the world looks directional is entropy — the universe started in some absurdly ordered low-entropy state and has been edging towards mess ever since. And I get why people smarter then me have signed onto this, because it explains a bunch of everyday things at once: why ice melts and doesn't spontaneously refreeze, why I remember breakfast but not next Tuesday, why you can't unscramble an egg. It even has this neat consequence that if a region of the universe ever started running entropy backwards, the beings in it would remember the future and call it the past, and it would all feel exactly the same to them. The theory is so tidy it feels like it must be right.

But I keep coming back to a feeling I can't shake: that past and future aren't just labels for whichever direction entropy happens to be increasing. When I think about yesterday being fixed and tomorrow being open, that feels like a fact about time itself, not a statistical accident of how the particles happen to be arranged. The entropy story seems to be telling me that the openness of the future is a kind of illusion, just a parochial thing about what I can remember. And maybe that's right, but then I also think: if you took a box of gas and time-reversed every particle's velocity, the entropy story says it would just run it's history in reverse — but would the gas really be 'going backwards in time,' or would it just be doing something very unlikely while time itself keeps plodding forward? The theory starts to look like it's gerrymandering the word 'time' so it means whatever the local entropy slope happens to be, and I don't know how to tell whether it's earned that or is just refusing to answer my actual question.

So I'm torn. The entropy account has all the systematic virtues — it's simple, it unifies a ton of phenomena, it gives a definite answer — and my sense that there's a real past/future asymmetry might just be folk physics I should debunk. But it might also be tracking something the entropy account leaves out: the theory could be mistaking a symptom of time's arrow for the arrow itself. I want the strongest version of both sides. What exactly has to be true for the intuition to be the thing I give up? What has to be true for the theory to be the thing that gets amended or abandoned? And is there a reading where both survive — where time really does have a direction and entropy mostly tracks it — or is that just something I tell myself because I can't let go?
