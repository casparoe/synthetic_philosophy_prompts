I am a retired natural scientist; most of my career was spent designing experiments, specifying protocols, and arguing with colleagues about what a given dataset could and could not license us to conclude. I have recently begun reading philosophy, and the Sleeping Beauty problem has caught my attention because it looks, on its face, like a dispute about experimental design, yet the philosophers seem to think something deeper is at stake. I would like you to translate the debate into the vocabulary I actually work in, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

The problem as I understand it runs as follows. On Sunday, a subject is told that a fair coin will be tossed. If it lands heads, she will be woken once, on Monday, and then the experiment ends. If it lands tails, she will be woken on Monday and again on Tuesday; after the Monday waking she is given a drug that erases her memory of that waking, so that each waking is subjectively indistinguishable from the others. She knows the entire protocol in advance. Each time she wakes, she is asked what subjective probability she should assign to the proposition that the coin landed heads. One camp answers one-half; another answers one-third.

Please restate the halfer and thirder positions, and the disagreement between them, in the vocabulary of experimental design and statistical inference: trials and experimental units, the sample space, randomization, the observation actually made upon waking, what is being conditioned on, and how the likelihood of the observed waking differs under heads and under tails. I am fluent in this vocabulary and comparatively new to the philosophers' terms, so please keep the translation honest rather than using my framework's assumptions to settle the question by definition. Do this for each position separately. Then give me a residue report. Which components of the philosophical debate translate cleanly into this vocabulary? Which translate only with distortion, and where exactly did you have to force them? Which components have no counterpart at all? Finally, tell me whether the residue indicates that my vocabulary is missing something the philosophers are right to insist on, or whether the disagreement is less substantive than it appears—perhaps a dispute about how to define the trial, or one that dissolves once the protocol and the conditioning are fully specified. I am less interested in a verdict for one side than in learning what my own framework can and cannot see here.
