Just got back from a long dinner argument with a friend who works on a psychiatric inpatient unit, and I think the argument actually sharpened my view instead of shaking it. She told me about a patient who insists, with total calm, that his apartment has been replaced by a duplicate and that his actual apartment is a few streets away, even though he never goes there or tries to find it. I kept saying that no matter how weird or isolated it is, the man's conviction is still a belief, not an act, not a metaphor. She kept pushing back that if it were a belief he would do something about it. I ended the night convinced I'm right, which is awkward because next week I have to present in our philosophy of mind and psychiatry workshop, and I signed up to defend the thesis that monothematic delusions are genuine beliefs, and that the two-factor theory is actually the best account of why an anomalous experience hardens into belief. My line will be that the second factor is best understood as a failure to reject or correct a candidate belief that already has the strange experience behind it, rather than as a second delusional experience. My strongest evidence, as I see it, is the way delusional people defend and adjust their claims when challenged, and the fact that ordinary compartmentalized beliefs also fail to guide behaviour across all contexts. The room is maybe ten people: two are committed one-factor theorists who think the anomalous experience alone is enough and that the second factor is a free parameter, one is an anti-doxasticist who would call the man's state an imagining or an empty avowal rather than a belief, a couple of clinicians who think the whole debate misses what matters for treatment, and the rest are philosophy of mind people who will worry about what I mean by belief at all. I need a Q and A prep sheet, not a restatement of my talk: the objections in roughly the order they will actually come, phrased the way those particular people would phrase them, what I should concede on the spot, what I should hold and how, where each reply is soft, and the one question I'm praying nobody asks. Also please no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just continuous prose like an email.
