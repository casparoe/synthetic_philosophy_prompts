I've been trying to get clear on a cluster of cases about having children and future people, and my gut verdicts keep pulling in different directions. Here they are.

A woman drinks heavily through the first trimester without realizing she's pregnant, and her child is born with fetal alcohol syndrome. Wrong, clearly, and she harmed her child.

A woman is on a medication for a thyroid problem. Any child she conceives while on it will be born with a serious but livable heart defect; if she waits two months she'll conceive a different child with no defect. She conceives now because she doesn't want to delay her plans. I think she did wrong, but I can't quite make sense of saying she wronged the child she had — that child couldn't have been conceived later. Still: wrong.

A couple both carry a recessive condition; any child they conceive naturally will have a painful and disabling disease, though a life clearly worth living. They could use IVF with screening and have a different, unaffected child, but they are morally opposed to IVF. They conceive naturally. I don't think they did anything wrong. And I can't say why this is different from the medication case except that waiting was easy and IVF isnt, which doesn't feel like the whole difference.

In 1950, a government chooses to drain an aquifer and mine a mineral as fast as possible, predicting that by 2050 the region will be poorer and more polluted, with lives still worth living but much harder than they would have been under a conservation policy. The 2050 people exist only because of the boom years; under conservation their ancestors would never have met. I think the government did wrong, and I want to say it wronged those future people, but I can't point to any one of them who'd have been better off.

A child is born with a condition so severe that his life is, on the whole, not worth living — constant pain, no real awareness. I think bringing him into existence was wrong and did harm him, because not existing would have been better for him.

My only half-baked principle is: an act is wrong only if it makes some particular person worse off than they'd otherwise have been. That gets the drinking case and the not-worth-living case, but it seems to fail in the medication and depletion cases, where I still want to say "wrong."

What I want from you: a precise principle (or a small set of principles) that captures as many of these verdicts as possible, stated clearly enough to apply mechanically; for each clause, which of my cases motivates it; what your principle says about a case I haven't listed — a couple who conceive now when they could wait a year and have a different child who'd be much happier, though the first child would still have a good life; and which of my verdicts it can't accommodate, with a note on whether I should change that verdict. Keep it to 650 words max.
