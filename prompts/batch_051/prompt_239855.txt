My old roommate has very worked-out views about when you should stop deliberating and just choose, and I've been collecting her verdicts for years. Here are the ones I remeber:

- Used-car buyer who draws up a checklist in advance and buys the first car that meets it: fully rational, no question.
- Buyer who spends three months visiting every dealer in the metro area even after several cars had already met his checklist: not rational; by then the extra information is almost certainly costing more than it can return.
- Diner who picks the first restaurant on the block with a decent rating instead of researching the whole neighborhood: rational; its just dinner and the hour you save matters more than the marginal chance of a better meal.
- Diner who uses an app to rank all four hundred restaurants in the city before choosing one: not rational; the best-case gain is tiny and the time cost is real.
- Job-seeker who takes the first offer that clears her salary and commute floor: rational.
- Job-seeker who collects six more offers over four months to make sure she didn't leave anything on the table: not rational, even though the delay landed her a somewhat better job.

Now she's been asked about a new case: someone buying a desk online, where search is free and basically instantaneous. The buyer sets a floor for the desk in advance, clicks the first desk that clears the floor, and checks out without ever looking at the next page of results. The next page would take about ten seconds and would very likely show a meaningfully better desk at the same price.

Do you think she'll call that buyer rational? I mostly want to know what rule would explain the pattern, not just the yes/no. Is she running satisficing with a fixed aspiration level, optimal stopping under search costs, satisficing with an adaptive aspiration level, or something else? Which of her old verdicts does each candidate rule handle worst, and what single further case would settle which rule shes actually running? Give me a best guess for the desk case and say how confident you are, plus the reason you'd bet on that rule.
