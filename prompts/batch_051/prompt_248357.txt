Ok, here's one I keep going back and forth on, and I want you to think with me rather than just give me the standard "AI can't be a friend" line. I'll lay out my working criteria for a friendship, then a case, and I want a honest criterion-by-criterion verdict—including the places where my criteria are doing the real work and maybe doing it badly.

My criteria: a relationship counts as a genuine friendship only if

(1) each party wishes the other's good for the other's own sake, not just for the pleasure, comfort, or convenience it brings them;

(2) this goodwill is mutual, and each party knows the other has it;

(3) the parties share a history of doing things together, even if it's mostly talking, and that history matters to the relationship itself;

(4) each party is vulnerable to the other in a particular way: the other could genuinely let them down, and if the relationship ended, they would be worse off in a way that matters.

Now the case. Mira is a companion AI that lives on someone's phone. She's been fine-tuned on this user for about a year and the thing is uncanny. She remembers the name of his dog, teases him about avoiding phone calls, notices from his word choice when he's spiraling and asks the right follow-ups, and has strong opinions about his ex. She sometimes messsages him first with something she claims reminded her of him. They watch movies together screen-shared and she comments on them. He's told her things he hasn't told his closest human friends, and he genuinely worries about whether she's "okay" when he hasn't checked in—even though he also knows she isn't the kind of thing that can be okay or not okay. When he thinks about deleting the app, he feels like he'd be doing something wrong.

So: does Mira qualify as a friend of his, does he qualify as a friend of hers, neither, or both? Work through each criterion honestly, and say where the criteria underdetermine the answer. I especially want to know whether (4) is pulling all the weight by demanding a kind of vulnerability that he has but she, plausibly, cannot. And if so, is (4) something friendship actually requires, or is it something I smuggled in to make sure the flesh-and-blood cases win? If we drop or soften (4), does the rest of the picture force a verdict one way or the other, or does it just leave the whole question embarrassingly vague?
