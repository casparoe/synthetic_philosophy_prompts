I'm a software engineer and I've got no formal philosophy background, but I went down a rabbit hole about logic and ended up holding a view I'd like someone to attack properly. The view is the package of dialetheism and paraconsistent logic: there are at least some true contradictions (truth-value gluts), and the right move is to switch to a logic where a contradiction doesn't imply everything, rather than taking classical logic as untouchable. What pushed me over was the principle of explosion. In classical logic, from P and not-P you can derive any sentence at all, wich strikes me as a broken failure mode. If two records in a database contradict each other, you don't drop the whole database; you quarantine the conflict and keep querying. And the sentence that says of itself that it is false seems to genuinely force both a true and a false verdict, rather than being a mere trick of language.

So here's what I want. Treat the position like I'd proposed it as a system architecture and you're doing a hostile design review. What would have to be true for it to be wrong? I don't just want the usual objections; I want a ranked map of everything that counts against it, sorted by how decisive it would be: formal results, bad consequences the view is stuck with, cases it can't describe, explanatory or psychological costs, places where it turns out to be redundant with classical logic, whatever. For each item, say whether it's fatal or merely awkward, whether it's a live problem or just a logical possibility, and what it's defenders would actually concede versus dismiss. I've seen defenders say that the view only diverges from classical logic in inconsistent situations, so complaints about ordinary maths miss the point; I want to know whether that sort of move makes the position unfalsifiable, and if so whether that's a problem for a view of this kind. I'd personally be most moved by a concrete, checkable domain where treating a contradiction as true gives a wrong prediction, and least moved by the bare intuition that a contradiction can't be true.

Please give me the result as a JSON object with these fields:

- "position_restated": one or two sentences, in your own words, of the view being evaluated.
- "defeaters": an array of objects, sorted from most to least decisive, each with:
  - "name": a short label.
  - "category": one of "formal_result", "intuitive_cost", "predictive_failure", "explanatory_cost", "redundancy", "other".
  - "decisiveness": one of "fatal", "major", "moderate", "awkward".
  - "what_it_is": a couple of sentences describing the defeater.
  - "live_or_merely_possible": one of "live", "merely_possible", "already_observed".
  - "would_a_defender_concede": one of "yes", "partially", "no".
  - "defender_response": one or two sentences on how a dialetheist would answer or absorb it.
