I've spent enough time in rationalist-adjacent forums to have seen Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument invoked as a decisive point against patterned redistribution, and I'd like to pressure-test it. The argument, as I understand it: begin from a distribution everyone grants is just; let a million spectators freely pay Wilt Chamberlain twenty-five cents each to watch him play; the resulting distribution is therefore just, since no just step can produce injustice; so any patterned or end-state conception of justice must be readjusted after every free choice, which means liberty and patterns are incompatible.

I have already collected the following objections and do not need them repeated:
(1) the baseline objection: the argument only goes through if the initial distribution really is just, and for any actual society that baseline is almost certainly tainted, so the argument cannot vindicate the justice of our actual large inequalities;
(2) the voluntariness objection: describing each spectator's payment as free ignores background coercion, unequal bargaining power, desperation, and monopoly conditions;
(3) the ex post taxation objection: a patterned view can allow the transfers to happen and then tax the result, and this is no more ongoing interference than the enforcement of property and contract that the transfers already presuppose;
(4) the false-equivalence objection: the step from redistributive taxation to forced labor is much too quick, since taxing a portion of income is not the same as claiming a person's body or labor;
(5) the composition objection: a sequence of individually unobjectionable transfers can accumulate into a distribution that then distorts the choices available to others, so historical entitlement cannot ignore the aggregate state.

What I am looking for is an objection with a different logical structure from all five — not a variant of the voluntariness or composition points and not a rewording of the claim that the initial baseline was unjust. Is there another objection to the Wilt Chamberlain argument that I am missing? If there is, state it precisely and give your honest assessment of how much damage it does to the argument.
