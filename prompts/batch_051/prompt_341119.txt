Hey, I somehow ended up on my condo complex's board this spring — the building we moved into two years ago is a converted factory, and the association has this long list of rules that mostly date from when it was first condo-ized in the 80s. The current fight is about enforcement. We have a faction that wants us to start fining for every violation: trash bins visible from the street after 8am, planters on the shared walkway, front doors painted a non-approved color, holiday lights still up a week later, bikes locked to the railing instead of the rack. Their pitch is that a rule nobody enforces isn't really a rule, that common expectations only stay common if they're backed by something, and that once you let small stuff slide you can't object to anything later. The other faction says that's exactly backwards: they want most of the cosmetic rules reclassified as "guidelines," with fines only for real nuisance or safety issues, and they point out that the couple of units with the "wrong" door color or the vegetable plot out front are a big part of why the place feels like a neighborhood and not a filing cabinet.

I have to write the board's decision memo for next months meeting and I keep flipping. Draft it for me: a concrete recommendation — which categories of rule to enforce strictly, which to treat as guidelines, what the penalty structure should be, and whether existing violations get grandfathered or get a compliance deadline. Make sure you steelman the side you end up recommending against, and I need something the board could actually vote on, not just a pros and cons list. Also, somewhere in there, name the actual philosophical crux, because I can tell this argument isnt really about trash bins. One wrinkle the memo should address: everyone who bought in technically signed the covenants, so the enforcement side says this is just holding people to what they agreed to, while the other side says nobody reads those documents and the lived norms have drifted. Where does consent fit into how tight a community can be?
