I spent my career doing statistical mechanics — phase transitions, stochastic dynamics, that sort of thing — and now that I'm retired I've been reading philosophy of probability and modality. I wrote down the eight claims I keep coming back to, and I'd like a consistency audit before I embarass myself in front of the philosophy group. Here they are:

1. I think "possible" and "probable" are at bottom the same notion in different words: an event is possible if and only if it has nonzero probability, and necessary if and only if it has probability one.
2. But I also think a spinner landing on exactly 0.5000... is possible even though any continuous distribution gives it probability zero, so probability zero isn't the same as impossible.
3. I think the second law of thermodynamics is purely probabilistic: a glass reassembling itself from shards is possible, just fantastically improbable, not forbidden by the laws.
4. But I do think physics has a real line between "vanishingly unlikely" and "impossible" — a particle tunneling through a wall is the former, a particle accelerating past light speed is the latter.
5. I think objective chances are real features of the world: the half-life of a nucleus is a fact about the nucleus, not a fact about our ignorance.
6. I think determinism rules out objective chance: if the initial state plus the laws fix the whole history, then all probabilities are epistemic.
7. But I also think the Everett interpretation, if it's true, shows that a deterministic dynamics can still underwrite genuine objective probabilities through the branch weights.
8. I think counterfactuals in science ("what would have happened if...") can be handled entirely by probabilities, and there's no separate modal fact of the matter left over.

Which pairs of these are actually in tension, and which only look like they are? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most of the mess. I genuinely don't know which one is the problem child, so don't just tell me to drop whichever is convenient.
