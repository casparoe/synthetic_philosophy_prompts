I've been chewing on something and I can't find a name for it. Me and my brother have spent years arguing about whether the cholesterol in eggs actually matters for your heart. Every time one of us finds a study, the other can honestly explain it away by poking at a detail—the subjects were already on medication, the follow-up was to short, the food questions couldn't really capture what people ate. It isn't cherry-picking; we can both do it to any study, even ones we'd like to believe. Then I noticed a stranger thing: the same pile of evidence used to get summarized as if it obviously showed one answer, and now it's summarized as if it obviously showed the opposite, with no single new study I can point to that caused the flip. The interpretations changed while the evidence stayed put. I know "bias" is a word, but that doesn't feel like it; I'm after the thing about the evidence itself not being able to settle the argument. Does that have a name? And if so, what exactly is the edge of it—when is it that evidence is genuinely consistent with two conclusions, versus when is someone just refusing to update?
