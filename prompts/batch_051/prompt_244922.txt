I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two historians who have spent years arguing past each other about the origins of the Cold War, and I want you to draft the joint statement they could both sign at the end of it. One works in the traditionalist, or orthodox, tradition: she holds that primary responsibility rests with the Soviet Union—its imposition of one-party rule across Eastern Europe, its rejection of the postwar economic arrangements, and its conversion of a legitimate security buffer into a coercive bloc. The other works in the revisionist tradition: he holds that primary responsibility rests with the United States—its atomic diplomacy, its push to make the postwar world safe for American trade and investment, and its refusal to accept that a state that had lost more than twenty million people would insist on friendly governments along its western border. Both accept that the archives have undermined simple single-cause stories and that by 1947 something genuinely new had emerged; neither has surrendered the central verdict. They have also begun to suspect that part of their quarrel may concern the question itself—whether a conflict of this kind is the sort of thing anyone can 'start' in the way a war can be started.

Draft the document. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign, covering the historical record from roughly 1944 to 1950 and what it would mean for one side to have started the Cold War; (2) the precise residual disagreement, stated so that each side would accept the wording of its own view and neither side would recognize a caricature in the other's; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to revise its position; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test, argument, or case that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where the disagreement turns out to be verbal—for instance, over whether 'responsibility' means causal contribution, avoidability, or moral blame—identify that explicitly and separate it from the substantive disagreement. Finish with a disagreements table.
