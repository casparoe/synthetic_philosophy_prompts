I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week I am interviewing a philosopher who defends a restricted version of van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Her central claim is that the intuitive thought behind Reflection — roughly, that your current degree of confidence should match the degree of confidence you expect to have later — survives once it is limited to future selves who are rational continuations of your present state: selves whose credences would be reached by responding properly to whatever evidence intervenes. She regards the familiar counterexamples, such as anticipated intoxication, brainwashing, or wishful thinking, as showing only that unrestricted Reflection is false, not that a suitably constrained principle collapses.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the view. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for every question include the follow-up you would ask after the reply she is most likely to give.

- Whether the appeal to a rational-continuation condition is circular or ad hoc: the condition is meant to single out the future selves to whom deference is owed, but it seems to presuppose the very notion of sound epistemic conduct that Reflection was meant to constrain.
- The diachronic Dutch strategy argument: can a sure-loss argument still be run against someone who obeys Reflection only with respect to rational future selves, or does the restriction remove the bookie's opportunity?
- Future selves that are rational but less informed: someone who will update rationally but will have forgotten some of the current evidence is a rational continuation, yet we normally refuse to defer to them. Does her view need a further condition forbidding anticipated loss of information, and would adding one leave the principle with any content?
- The parallel with deferring to other people: if you would not defer to an epistemic peer merely because you expect to meet her later, why should your future self be treated differently?

Make each follow-up a concrete pressure on the answer she is most likely to give, not a request for clarification, and fill any remaining slots with further objections a careful defender of restricted Reflection would need to meet. Keep the full list of questions and follow-ups to no more than 700 words, and write it in British English.
