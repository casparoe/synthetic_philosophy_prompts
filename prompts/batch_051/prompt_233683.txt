Question: Astrid has carefully evaluated the trial evidence and arrived at a credence of .8 that the defendant is guilty. She goes back over her reasoning and it seems flawless to her. She then learns she was severely sleep-deprived while she evaluated the evidence, and that people in such states are substantially worse at exactly this kind of probabilistic reasoning. Should she lower her credence? If she does, is that epistemic akrasia? Explain.

Student answer: She should lower it, because the sleep-deprivation information is a higher-order defeater. It doesn't attack any particular premise in her reasoning, but it undercuts the reliability of the process that produced the .8, so it defeats the justification she has for the .8. This is not epistemic akrasia, because akrasia is believing against what your evidence supports, and once the higher-order evidence is part of her evidence, her total evidence supports lowering (or at least doesn't support staying at .8). If she stayed at .8 she would be the akratic one, since she'd be ignoring evidence about her own reliability.
