For a piece I am drafting on rational choice over time, I have written out the position I am presently prepared to defend. Before I circulate it, I want a consistency audit of the whole set. Here are the commitments.

1. On static choice, I accept the expected-utility picture: rational preferences over acts are complete and transitive, satisfy independence, and rational agents maximize expected utility.

2. I accept dynamic consistency in roughly its standard form: if I now judge one plan better than another, I should not, at a later stage of the same plan and without new information, reverse that judgment and prefer to abandon the plan. Local reversals of this kind are defects, not fresh exercises of agency.

3. I hold that the right way to deal with foreseeable preference change is to be sophisticated rather than naive: anticipate how one will choose later, reason by backward induction over strategies, and precommit where that improves the strategy by one's present lights. Odysseus lashing himself to the mast is, for me, a paradigm of rational diachronic agency; likewise, I would pay for an irrevocable savings arrangement if I knew my later self would otherwise spend in ways I now regard as imprudent.

4. Yet I also hold that some fundamental changes in a person's values over a life are rational, foreseeable, and neither corrections of error nor episodes of weakness of will. A person can know at twenty-five that she will value different things at fifty-five without thinking that either self is mistaken, and without treating the later self as akratic. Neither self is merely the same self with worse information.

5. Finally, I hold a money-pump criterion: a pattern of choices that allows a sequence of trades, each acceptable at the moment it is offered, which returns the agent to her original holdings minus a fee, is rationally defective. I treat exploitability of this kind as decisive evidence against the underlying preferences, not as a tolerable side effect.

I would like you to audit this set. For each pair of commitments that is in genuine tension, say how severe the tension is and what would have to give to remove it. For pairs that only appear to be in tension, say briefly why they can coexist. Then identify the single revision that would do the most to stabilize the set as a whole, and say what it would cost.
