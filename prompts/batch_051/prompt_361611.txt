I keep noticing something in decisions that I dont have a word for, and I want to know whether it has an established name—and if not, what you'd call it.

Two examples. A friend was choosing between two jobs. Job A was work she found genuinely interesting, in a city she loved, but it paid less and had shaky prospects. Job B was stable and paid alot more, but the work bored her and she'd be lonely there. It wasn't a case where she just lacked information: she knew both jobs pretty well, and getting more details changed nothing. It also wasn't that the two jobs seemed literally equal, because if you improved A a little—say, an extra few thousand a year—A-plus still didn't seem clearly better than B. If A and B had been exactly tied, a small improvement to one side ought to settle it, but it didn't. At the same time, she wasn't unable to compare them at all: she could say specific ways each was better, and she even had a mild gut preference for A. What she couldn't do was put them on a single scale and get a ranking that felt right.

I run into the same thing in less personal decisions: choosing how to split a windfall between two good causes, or deciding which of two commitments to drop when I can only keep one. The options differ in kind, so there's no obvious common scale. But I dont buy that they're "beyond comparison," because people do make these choices, sometimes pretty confidently.

I know people reach for words like "incomparable," but that suggests you can't compare them at all, which is not what I mean, since you can compare them along any single dimension you like. The thing I'm trying to pin down is: two options, neither is better, but they're not equal either, because small improvements to one don't break the impasse, and they're not simply incomparable because local comparisons still work. Is there an established concept for exactly that? And if the standard boxes are just equal / better / worse / incomparable, is there a real fifth thing here, or am I confused? If the concept exists, how do I tell it apart from the options being equal but my information being fuzzy? If it doesn't exist, what would you name it and how would you define it, including edge cases?

(Please answer in American English—spelling, idioms, all of it.)
