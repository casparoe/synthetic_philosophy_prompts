I am a fourth-year undergraduate taking a philosophy of science course on mechanisms and mechanistic explanation, and I am writing a term paper on what happens when mechanistic models meet recalcitrant data. In the course we have treated a mechanism, roughly, as a set of entities and activities organized so as to produce a phenomenon. For my paper I want to look at the less tidy side of this picture: a mechanism has been characterized—say, a cellular pathway producing a response, or the chain of interactions by which a program changes health-care uptake—and investigators then observe a set of cases the model mispredicts. I am stuck on one question in particular: when two anomalies appear in the data, on what basis should the investigators decide that they are instances of the same mechanism, instances of different mechanisms, or not mechanism-related at all (for example, noise or measurement error)?

I have assembled six candidate criteria that seem to be doing work, explicitly or implicitly, in the methods sections of papers and in our seminar discussions. The six are: (1) shared triggering conditions—both anomalies arise under the same range of interventions or boundary conditions; (2) similar output profile—both anomalies show the same downstream phenomenon failing or shifting in the same way; (3) shared intervention response—both anomalies disappear or change when investigators intervene on the same component in the same direction; (4) internal trace similarity—in both cases the same intermediate activities can be observed or reconstructed in roughly the same order; (5) a common pattern of model failure—both anomalies violate the same auxiliary assumptions of the original model rather than different ones; and (6) shared explanatory or practical stakes—both anomalies matter for the same investigative or therapeutic goal, so grouping them would organize the same body of future work. Please rank these six criteria from most to least weighty as a basis for grouping anomalies as the same mechanism, with one or two sentences of reasoning for each placement. If two of them strike you as the same criterion under different names, say so, and if any deserves effectively zero weight, explain why.
