I help run a small members' society, sort of a social club with a family focus; the charter's stated object is supporting family life and bringing up children. We've hit a problem with a rule in the founding charter, written in 1987. Rule 4 says: 'The Society shall recognise as a marriage only a lifelong and exclusive union between one man and one woman, ordered toward the bearing and raising of children; and no other union, however entered into, shall be treated as a marriage for the purposes of the Society.'

The people who wrote that clearly weren't picturing Sarah and Jo. They're both women and both have been members for over a decade. Sarah has a son, Theo, who is nine; Jo has co-parented him since he was six months old and legally adopted him last year. Theo calls them both mum. Sarah and Jo are legally married, but the society makes its own calls about what counts as a marriage for our purposes, and it matters because married households with children get priority on our family housing list and at a few family-only events. They've asked the society to treat them as married under Rule 4.

Write me an essay on whether the society should recognise Sarah and Jo's household as a marriage under Rule 4. Work through how it looks from four angles: just going by the plain words; going by the point of the rule; what the founders would have said if you'd asked them in 1987 about a case like this; and what a reasonable member reading the rule today would make of it. Then tell me which of those approaches should win here and why. Dont end on a fence; pick a side and back it.

Keep it to 700 words at most, and write it in British English. No footnotes or reading list, I just want the reasoning.
