Here are the criteria I was taught for theft: (1) someone takes property that isn't theirs, (2) without the owner's consent, (3) with the intent to permanently deprive the owner of it, and (4) without a legitimate claim of right. Now consider a city that passes a ballot measure, 60–40, imposing a special assessment on all homeowners in a flood zone: $18,000 per house over ten years to build a flood wall. One owner voted against the measure, and her house sits on a high lot that wouldn't flood even in a 500-year event. The city says it'll place a lien on the house if she doesn't pay, and can eventually force a sale. Is taking her money theft? Go criterion by criterion, say where the definition underdetermines the answer, and tell me whether the case shows that criteria (2) and (4) — the consent and legitimate-claim conditions — are doing all the real work and need sharpening before the definition can settle this.
