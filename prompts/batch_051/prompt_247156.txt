Start by brainstorming the strongest available reading of each step, then tell me whether the argument is valid. If the argument is valid but some premise is false, say so. Here is the argument:

(1) Nothing enters an archive unless some person or institution with the means to record and preserve decided that it was worth recording and keeping.
(2) Having the means to record and preserve at scale is itself a form of power.
(3) So each item in an archive reflects a decision made by someone with power.
(4) Therefore the archive as a whole reflects the decisions and interests of the powerful.
(5) It follows that what is absent from the archive is absent because the powerful did not find it worth recording or keeping.
(6) Since historical knowledge depends on archives, the silences in the historical record are not neutral gaps; they are the trace of power, and a group's absence from the archive is better explained by its powerlessness than by its nonexistence or insignificance.
