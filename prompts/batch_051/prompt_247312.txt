I'm teaching The Great Gatsby in a course that pairs American fiction with evolutionary and economic ideas, and I want to build one session around costly signaling and the handicap principle. As I understand it, the principle says that a signal can honestly advertise an underlying quality even when it is not an index causally tied to that quality, provided it is costly enough that a low-quality signaler could not afford it: the peacock's tail and the gazelle's stotting are credible because they waste resources a weak or sick animal cannot spare. Gatsby's world seems built out of exactly such displays — the mansion, the parties, the car, the shirts he piles on the table for Daisy — and Nick reads them as performances aimed at making Daisy credit a quality Gatsby does not actually have. But the novel also seems to me to put pressure on the principle in ways worth sorting out. The displays are costly, yet they are funded by bootlegging, and the old-money Buchanans read them as vulgar rather than as proof of belonging. What Gatsby wants to signal is membership in a class that can be inherited but not purchased, so the relevant cost may not be differentially affordable in the way the principle requires. And the one signal in the book that seems both honest and maximally costly — Gatsby taking the blame for Myrtle's death — is misread by nearly everyone who hears of it.

I'd like a reading of the novel through this lens rather than a plot summary. In particular: where does the handicap principle genuinely illuminate a scene or a character's logic, and where does the novel resist or invert it? Is the book best read as a confirmation of the principle, a critique of it, or an account of the social conditions under which costly signaling stops guaranteeing honesty? And what does the novel add to the theoretical debate that the biological models do not capture — about class, inherited versus purchased status, or the gap between signaling and being believed? If the honest verdict is that the analogy between biological signals and Gatsby's conspicuous display partly breaks down, say exactly where and why. Keep the response to no more than 700 words.
