You're a professional philosopher who works on the ethics of well-being, and I'd like you to think out loud and really pressure-test your answer. Start by brainstorming a few candidate principles before you settle on one, and be willing to end up somewhere uncomfortable.

Here's a pair of cases that's been bothering me.

Dr. A has a patient, R, who's about to go through a week of serious pain from a condition. There's a cheap, zero-risk intervention that would prevent all of it, reliably, and Dr. A knows this. But Dr. A is tired and just... doesn't order it, so R suffers the whole week. Everyone I know thinks Dr. A acted badly—probably deserves censure, maybe discipline.

Dr. B has a patient, S, who's about to have a perfectly fine week: no pain, just ordinary. There's a cheap, zero-risk intervention that would give S a week of unusually intense, vivid pleasure, reliably, and Dr. B knows this. But Dr. B is tired and doesn't order it, so S just has their fine week. Almost nobody I've asked thinks Dr. B acted badly at all; at most he's slightly lazy.

What I want to know is whether there's a single principle that gets both verdicts right. I've heard the easy replies—"pain is stronger than pleasure," "one is an action and the other is an omission," "doctors only have a duty to prevent pain, not to maximize pleasure"—but they feel like rationalizations, because you can stipulate that the pain and the pleasure are matched in intensity and duration, both cases are omissions, and in both cases the doctor is treating their own patient. So the asymmetry is doing the real work, not the details.

Give me the strongest principle you can construct that actually generates both judgments. I keep circling back to something like negative utilitarianism or a suffering-focused ethic—the thought that suffering has a special moral priority over happiness. Is that the best we can do? Where does it break when you test it against nearby cases? And at the end, tell me straight: should we keep this asymmetry, or is it just an inconsistency we've learned to live with?
