I am an economist by training, and I am most at home in the vocabulary of social choice theory and mechanism design: preference profiles, aggregation rules, Condorcet winners and majority cycles, single-peakedness and other domain restrictions, impossibility theorems, strategic manipulation, mechanisms, implementation, incentive compatibility, participation constraints, and revealed preference. I know Rousseau only at an introductory level, through the usual summaries. I want a translation, not an exposition: I want to know what happens to Rousseau's doctrine of the general will, and in particular to the claim that one can be forced to be free, when it is restated in that vocabulary.

Here is the doctrine as I understand it, which is the object to be translated. Rousseau distinguishes the will of all—the sum or aggregate of private wills, each aimed at the individual's particular interest—from the general will, which aims at what is common to all. The general will is not the sum of private wills; it is what each person wills insofar as he or she abstracts from particular interest and considers the common interest. It is in some sense always right and tends toward the public good, although the people may deliberate badly and may be deceived. Voting, including majority rule, is at best a method for eliciting the general will, not a definition of it, and its success depends on the character of the assembly: the absence of factions, adequate information, and citizens voting as citizens rather than as private persons. Finally, the social contract includes the provision that anyone who refuses to obey the general will may be constrained by the whole body—the doctrine standardly summarized in English as being forced to be free—and the intended point is that the general will is in some sense the person's own will, so that obedience to it is freedom rather than subjection.

Restate this cluster of claims in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. I want sentence-level translation where possible: what is the will of all, what is the general will—a social welfare function, a social choice correspondence, a domain restriction, a solution concept, something else—what is the claim that majority rule merely elicits it, and what is forced-to-be-free when written as a mechanism. Then give me the residue report: tell me which claims translated cleanly, which translated only with distortion, and which could not be expressed at all in the target vocabulary. Where the translation fails, tell me whether that is because my vocabulary is missing something real—such as the formation of preferences, or freedom as something an institution can produce rather than a constraint on it—or because Rousseau's doctrine is less precise than it looks once one tries to formalize it. In particular, mechanism design takes preferences as given, while Rousseau appears to want institutions to make people's wills general; I want to know whether that is a repairable difference in modeling strategy or the point at which the doctrine stops being a social choice theory at all. Do not smuggle in the target framework's assumptions and call the result a translation: where social choice theory would naturally treat preferences as fixed, say so and flag the cost.
