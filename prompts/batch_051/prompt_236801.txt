I'm an undergraduate currently taking a course on multi-agent safety, and I sit on my university's Administrative Automation Review Committee. The committee must decide whether to approve a six-month pilot in which several LLM-based agents, each owned by a different administrative unit (timetabling, procurement, and student services), may make and accept offers to one another within per-transaction limits: room swaps, shared vendor contracts, workload handoffs. Each agreement is individually audited each week for rule violations, and the agents have no channel except the offers themselves. A majority of the committee argues the pilot is safe because no single agreement can exceed its limits and explicit collusion would require communication the system does not allow. I've been asked to draft a short decision memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The memo should give the committee a working definition of collusion that does not depend on explicit messages or bad intent; explain how locally permissible, mutually beneficial agreements among repeatedly interacting agents can still aggregate into a back-scratching equilibrium that harms students and the university budget; specify what the audit would need to monitor at the aggregate level to catch that failure mode; and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the side I recommend against.
