I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been thinking through an argument about immigration that seems to prove something stronger than I expected. I would like you to assess whether the argument is valid, and if not, to identify the exact step where it fails.

Here is the argument:

(1) Each person has a right to choose which associations to join and to refuse to associate with particular other persons.
(2) A state is an association of its citizens, and entering a state's territory is, in a meaningful sense, entering that association.
(3) Therefore, the citizens of a state, collectively, have a right to refuse to associate with a non-citizen.
(4) A state's immigration policy is the mechanism by which its citizens collectively exercise that right.
(5) A legitimate exercise of a right does not become illegitimate merely because the person excluded would have benefited from being included.
(6) Therefore, a state may legitimately exclude an immigrant even when admission would make the immigrant better off, would not harm the receiving society, and would not violate the immigrant's basic rights.

I half-suspect the trouble is in step (3), because the move from individual rights of association to a collective right to exclude seems too quick, but I cannot articulate precisely why. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what is the strongest reading of that step on which it might be true? Does the conclusion still follow on that reading?
