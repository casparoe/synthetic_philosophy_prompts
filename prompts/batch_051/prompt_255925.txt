I've got a running argument between two things I find hard to give up. On one side, the natural history seems about as well established as anything empirical: for hundreds of millions of years before any human, animals were eating each other, dying of infection, starving, and going through mass extinctions. On the other side, there's the doctrine I was raised with and still find attractive: creation is good, and the world's brokenness is something that entered through the Fall, so none of it is God's doing in a straightforward sense. But if there was no human around to fall, the Fall can't be what introduced animal pain — unless I say animal pain back then didn't count, or that a later sin somehow reached backward and infected the deep past, and both of those sound like special pleading. I don't need someone to just pick a side. I want to know what it would actually cost to keep the doctrine by redefining "good creation" or "the Fall," what it would cost to drop the doctrine and stay with the natural history, and whether there's a version where both survive without pretending the pre-human suffering was only apparent or something God was forced into. Which commitment should do the yielding?
