Please help me locate the error in an argument I developed after reading Han Feizi. We read a chapter called The Two Handles in class. I am not quoting, but the part that stuck with me says, roughly, that an enlightened ruler should not expose his likes and dislikes, because once ministers know what pleases him they will simply put on faces to match. If the ruler shows approval, they will flatter; if he shows anger, they will feign resentment. The chapter says the ruler should therefore govern only through reward and punishment, not through displaying his own judgments.

Here is the argument I built from that passage.

(1) If reward and punishment are to guide conduct at all, the ruler must publicly connect at least some kinds of action with reward and some kinds with punishment.
(2) Any public standard can be gamed: a person can produce the outer indicator without possessing the underlying quality the ruler seems to want.
(3) Han Feizi’s warning about hidden likes and dislikes seems to concede exactly this. Subjects will mold their observable behavior to whatever the ruler is known to reward.
(4) But the whole point of Legalism was to produce actual order, not merely semblances of order.
(5) So the two handles seem trapped. Public standards invite semblance, while secret standards leave people unable to comply.
(6) If that is right, then any rule by incentives can manufacture only fake compliance—including grades, salaries, and criminal law. But many incentive systems plainly produce real changes in behavior, so the argument proves too much.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? More specifically, is Han Feizi entitled to treat observable compliance as enough for political order, or does his criticism of Confucian rule by virtue require something more than outward behavior? If I reject step (4), what does it cost the broader Legalist case against rule by virtue? I would like to keep the conclusion that incentives can shape real conduct, but I cannot tell whether that is compatible with Han Feizi’s claim that people only show the ruler what he wants to see.
