My housing co-op's standing rules say: "No member shall apply any chemical pesticide on the property, indoors or out." It got added in the nineties after somebody soaked the vegetable beds in a nasty organophosphate spray, and the minutes say the point is to keep synthetic poisons out of our soil and off our skin.

Here's what's dividing the house right now. We've got bedbugs on the second floor. One faction wants to hire a company that uses an inert mineral dust, no synthetic active ingredient, that kills by abrading the bugs' cuticle so they dessicate slowly over a day or two. Another faction says that's still a pesticide, and if we've already accepted that we're killing them, a quick knockdown spray is the less cruel option; the rule is pushing us toward the slow death. A third person wants to try a fungal biocontrol that infects the bugs and kills them over a week.

I'm less interested in what our lawyer would say than in what interpretation of the rule actually tracks its purpose. Walk me through the plain-text reading, the purpose reading, the "what would the drafters have said if asked" reading, and the reasonable-member reading, and say which you think should govern. Then the part I keep coming back to: if the rule was meant to keep poisons out, but the quick synthetic spray is more humane by our own lights, is the rule making us be crueller in its own terms? What would a rule look like that actually got this right, one that catches the slow desiccants and fungal weapons without either banning innocuous stuff or letting everything through?
