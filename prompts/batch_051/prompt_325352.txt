A friend of mine keeps handing down verdicts about which animals count morally, and I've started writing them down. She says a normal human clearly counts, and so do chimps, dogs, elephants, crows, and octopuses. A mouse counts, she says, but not as much as a dog. Frogs, goldfish, and bees are out. She also says a current language model doesn't count, no matter how smart its answers look, because there's nobody home.

A colleague of ours is designing a thought experiment for a class, and he wants me to predict my friend's ruling. The case: a hypothetical animal about the size of a cat, with a brain as large as a horse's, but the brain is built like a frogs — same total number of neurons as a frog, just spread thinly through a much bigger space — and the animal acts like a frog too, sits, snaps at bugs, does nothing else.

What I actually want is the rule, not just the verdict. Her track record looks like it could fit several: absolute brain size, total neuron count, neuron density, behavioral repertoire, whether there's something it's like to be the animal, maybe some mix. Which of those can handle every verdict shes given, what does each one predict for the horse-brained frog-cat, which way would you bet, and which further case would do the most work pulling those rules apart? And then be honest: what would change your bet — what consideration would make you switch predictions — and where do you think the inference from her past verdicts is shakiest?
