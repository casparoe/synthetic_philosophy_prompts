I've been going back and forth on this idea I got from Dennett, the one where the self isn't a little witness inside your head but more like the center of gravity for the stories a person tells about themselves. A center of gravity isn't a real physical part of an object, but it still does real explanatory work; same with the self. I was trying to explain this to a friend and she kept pushing back, and it made me realize I can list the standard pushbacks but I don't have an answer for them. The ones I keep bumping into are: (1) if the self is a kind of useful fiction, then why should I care about my future self in the special way I do, (2) it leans too hard on storytelling and words, so it seems to leave out babies and people who don't use language, (3) it makes the self into something other people could describe from the outside, but my self feels fundamentally private and felt from inside, (4) it turns me into a character being narrated rather than an agent who actually does things. I'm looking for another objection, one that is not on that list and not just a clever rewording of any of them. Also don't give me the simple "so the self is just an illusion and nothing is real" line, because Dennett is pretty explicit that the self is not a harmful illusion. Give me a genuine new objection with a different shape, and then tell me honestly whether it's a real problem for the view or just a limit on how far the analogy goes.
