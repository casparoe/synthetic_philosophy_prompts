I’ve been trying to formalize a view I’ve been calling the fidelity discontinuity thesis in whole-brain emulation. The claim is that whether an emulation is the same mind as the original is not a continuous function of how close the emulation comes to reproducing the original neural trajectory. Suppose an emulation captures every neuron’s spike timing to within a few milliseconds at each step. The usual assumption seems to be that this is overwhelmingly high fidelity, so the emulation is near enough to me. I think that is confused. The error is not inert; it changes the synaptic state that the next timestep inherits, and because neural dynamics are sensitive to small differences, the emulation does not merely become a slightly less precise copy—it becomes a distinct trajectory that shares my early history and gross functional profile. On my view, identity preservation requires exact preservation of a particular causal-informational structure, not closeness to it, so there is a kind of discontinuity: below the cutoff, you do not get a lesser me, you get a different similar mind.

I think this matters for feasibility talk: it makes phrases like 95% fidelity mostly irrelevant for identity, unless the remaining 5% is entirely outside the identity-relevant structure. It also complicates gradual replacement views, because a sequence of small replacements may preserve the target trajectory only if the replacements are exactly behaviorally equivalent, not merely very good approximations.

Before I push this further, I want an honest assessment. Is my discontinuity thesis actually saying something stronger than the familiar point that small computational errors can compound? Does it already exist under another name in the emulation literature? Give me the three strongest objections, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable. Where does the claim go wrong—ideally, give a concrete case in which a high-fidelity emulation is intuitively the same mind but my thesis would classify it as not being me.
