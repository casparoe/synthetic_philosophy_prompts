Lately Ive been leaning toward a view about complicity and I want to know how exposed it actually is. A few months ago I cast the single no vote on a board decision at my professional association; it passed by a mile and my vote changed nothing, but I still feel like I participated in the thing, not just that I disagreed with it. The view I'm drawn to is this: you can be complicit in a collective wrong even when your individual act makes no causal difference to it. The outcome would have been identical without you, no victim is better or worse off because of what you did, and yet your participation still implicates you in the wrong itself — not in a loose 'we're all complicit' way, but in a way that makes it true that you share in it.

I want a map of what would count against that view. Not just the easy objections, but the cases, consequences, and arguments that a thoughtful defender should genuinely lose sleep over. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. For each one, say what would have to be true for it to actually land, and whether a defender would themselves concede it as counting against the view rather than only critics saying so. I'd also like you to say honestly whether the view can be qualified in ways that protect it from every counterexample, and if so whether that makes the view problematically unfalsifiable — or whether for a view like this that's not a real problem.

Dont finish on a shrug. I'd rather you take a position: tell me which defeaters you think actually have force, and whether the view, after the qualifications, is still a substantive and defensible claim about complicity or has quietly collapsed into something weaker.
