I'm drafting a short statement of my views on induction and projectibility for a seminar on confirmation theory, and I'd like it audited for internal consistency before I circulate it. The package as it currently stands is this:

(1) There is no non-circular justification of induction, but that does not push me toward skepticism; the demand for such a justification asks for something no rational practice could provide.

(2) The grue problem shows that a purely formal or syntactic account of confirmation cannot privilege the hypothesis that all emeralds are green over the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue, where an object is grue just in case it is first observed before some future time t and is green, or is not first observed before t and is blue. The two hypotheses fit the same past observations but diverge about emeralds first observed after t.

(3) I hold the standard verdict that the available evidence supports the green hypothesis over the grue hypothesis, and that projecting green is the rationally warranted inference in the case.

(4) The ground of that verdict, I take it, is that green marks a real resemblance class in nature, while grue is a gerrymandered and partly disjunctive construction. Projectibility is ultimately an objective matter of world-structure rather than convention.

(5) But I also believe our judgments about which things are naturally similar are not a transparent readout of world-structure. They are shaped by our perceptual endowment, our practical interests, and the classificatory practices of our scientific communities.

(6) I want to keep the further claim that there is a fact of the matter about which inductive inferences are warranted by a body of evidence, even though our access to that fact is fallible and mediated by interests.

(7) Finally, I do not think grue can be ruled out merely because its definition makes essential reference to a particular time. Many respectable scientific predicates are time-indexed or otherwise positional, and green itself can be analyzed in terms of dispositions to produce certain spectral responses rather than as a time-free essence.

Please work through these claims and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only appear to be in tension. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest repair would be, and then say which single revision would leave the whole package most coherent.
