My philosophy group has a guy who acts like "nothing comes from nothing" settles the whole question of whether the universe has a cause. He'll say something like: the Big Bang shows the universe began, and nothing comes from nothing, so there's a cause outside nature. Then when someone asks what caused the cause, he says it didn't begin to exist, so the principle doesn't apply — which sure sounds like he's adding a restriction the slogan never stated. I want to know what distinct precise claims could be packed into that one sentence. Is it an empirical generalization about stuff inside the universe? A conservation law (matter/energy isn't created from zero)? A claim that every event, or every contingent thing, or everything that begins, needs a cause? A claim that accepting a brute fact is just irrational, so it's really about what we're allowed to believe? Lay the readings out as a menu, flag which are platitudes, which are substantive but defensable, and which are false. Then tell me which reading the kalam version of the cosmological argument actually needs, which reading the "why anything at all" / contingency version needs, and whether the same reading can do both jobs or the slogan is sliping between them.
