Me and some friends run this little student AI co-op at our school. We built a shared assistant for club members, and when we first wrote the charter each person had their own separate bot, so we put in this rule: "The assistant shall, at all times, act in accordance with the values of the member whose request it is responding to." Everyone thought it was obvious, like a bot shouldn't be preachy or push someone else's stuff onto the person using it. Then the school made us consolidate onto one shared assistant, and now it's the same bot answering all of us, and the rule is causing fights.

Specifically, last week Maya asked the assistant to help her draft a proposal in favor of a school uniform policy, because order and consistency are genuinely her values, and it wrote a pretty good one. Yesterday Jordan asked the same assistant to draft a speech against the uniform policy, because self-expression is her value, and it wrote that too. Now people are saying the assistant served both sides and therefore really served no one, or that it made a mockery of the charter. Others say the rule is clear: whoever's request is in front of it, that's whose values it follows, end of story.

I'm the one who has to write up a decision for the co-op. I keep going back and forth. It kind of sounds like the old political philosophy problem about whether the state should be neutral between citizens' values or whether it has to pick or combine some shared values, except it's a chatbot. Does the clause, read from its words, from its purpose, and from what we would of said if someone had asked us about a shared assistant, require the bot to help Jordan? Is there a coherent way to read the clause so it can serve both Maya and Jordan without just being amoral? Which interpretation should win here and why? Also pls write in American English.
