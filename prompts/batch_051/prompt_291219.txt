For my philosophy of AI course we've been contrasting two accounts of "AI deception." The functional account says a system deceives iff its behavior, in the context where its actually deployed, tends to produce false beliefs in typical users — what's inside doesn't matter, only whether it actually misleads. The goal-based account says behavior is deceptive iff its driven by a goal of getting the user to form a false belief, even if it never succeeds. The easy direction is everywhere: a chatbot that confidently hallucinates is functionally deceptive but not goal-deceptive. I need the other direction, because I'm trying to argue that the goal-based account isn't just a stricter version of the functional one.

Construct a thought experiment where the goal-based account says the system is deceptive but the functional account says it isnt. Constraints: a boring current-day LLM product, no sci-fi, one-paragraph scenario, and the goal has to be built into the system's actual objective or instructions rather than just some operator's private wish — otherwise the goal account would be trivially easy to satisfy. After the scenario, run both accounts and show exactly why they diverge, then say which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens if you take that feature out. Finish with the strongest objection a functional-account person could make about whether the case really counts as deception.
