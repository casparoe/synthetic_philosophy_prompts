I write about the weird zone where culture and commerce run into each other, and I keep coming back to Adorno and Horkheimer's old culture-industry grudge because it keeps describing the present a little too well. For a piece I'm working on, I've been using a working checklist for what makes something a culture industry product rather than art: (a) it's made assembly-line style, so the individual item is interchangeable; (b) the surface details change but the structure is identical — pseudo-individualization, like a new guitar solo pasted over the same song; (c) it's built to deliver a pre-scripted emotional or pseudo-spontaneous response on cue; and (d) it reaches you through a few concentrated commercial channels and is meant to be consumed semi-absently.

Now consider the streaming-service practice of comissioning cheap instrumental tracks under invented artist names to fill focus, chill, and sleep playlists. The tracks are mass produced to a template, interchangeable, given fake names so the pipeline looks organic, and designed to sit unnoticed in the background while you work. But nobody treats them as songs, nobody cares about the fake artist, and the response is mostly that you dont notice them; people seem to use them as a focus tool rather than being sold a feeling. Go through my criteria honestly, criterion by criterion: does this qualify as culture industry in Adorno's sense, or does it fail so badly that the checklist needs amending? And tell me which of the four criteria is doing the least real work here — or whether this is a case Adorno just had no slot for.
