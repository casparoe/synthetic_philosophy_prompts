ok so i'm in this math/philosophy club at school and last week we went deep on Hilberts hotel. most people were fine with moving everyone from room n to n+1, but my friend kevin kept saying this only works if you pretend an actually completed infinity exists, and that all we're doing is describing rules for a process that never finishes. he's not a finitist exactly, but he kept pointing at the word "full" and saying we changed what it means just by deciding the words still apply.

for our club's zine i want a poem that doesn't just pick a side, so could you write a villanelle from the point of view of the hotel's night manager? the two refrains should be "there is always a room" and "but the sign says full," and i want the form to do real work: the first refrain captures Cantor's move, the second captures the finitist's resistance, and by the time they meet in the last two lines the reader should feel why the debate keeps circling. maybe one of the middle stanzas can handle the infinite bus of new guests arriving, and another can handle the shift-to-odd-numbers trick, with the manager's language getting a little more strained as the poem goes on. if you need to break the villanelle a little for effect that's fine, just add a short note at the end saying why.
