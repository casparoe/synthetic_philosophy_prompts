I'm on the grants committee of a mid-sized foundation that mostly funds global health and AI governance stuff, and we've got a genuinely hard one on the docket. A group we've funded before is asking for money to run a city-scale "biosecurity sentinel" pilot: volunteers at first, then eventually mandatory participation, wearable biosensors, wastewater and ambient-air monitoring in a mid-sized city, data feeding an early-warning system for engineered pandemics. Their pitch is straight longtermism: the expected value of catching a deliberately released pathogen early is astronomical once you multiply it by all future generations, so a few years of privacy loss and some coercion for one pilot population is basically a rounding error in expectation. This feels like it hits a live crux in the longtermism debate. A couple of us think it's exactly the worry that longtermism slides into a license for authoritarian control; others on the committee think we're being innumerate and ignoring the actual arithmetic. We've got to send the full board a memo with a recomendation on whether to fund the pilot. Could you draft it? Act as a professional philosopher specializing in intergenerational ethics and political legitimacy. Take your time and maybe start by brainstorming the strongest considerations on each side, then produce the memo itself, with a clear actionable bottom line and a serious, non-strawman treatment of the strongest point on the losing side.
