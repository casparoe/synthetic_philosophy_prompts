I'm a journalist working on a piece about why people talk past each other on monogamy, and before I interview anyone who describes themselves as for or against it, I want the spec I'd be grading their claims against. Write the desiderata a theory of the ethics of sexual exclusivity has to meet to be adequate for real adult romantic relationships: the cases it has to get right (a couple who never actually said 'we're exclusive' but one of them treats it as part of the package; a couple with an explicitly open arrangement where someone still feels betrayed; one-sided openness; a partner who consents to monogamy mostly because they're scared of being left; renegotiation after years; monogamous in public, open in private), the distinctions it can't collapse (breaking an agreement vs. the sex itself being wrong; being hurt by a violated expectation vs. having a valid claim; jealousy as evidence of harm vs. jealousy as evidence of wrongness; exclusivity as constitutive of the relationship vs. exclusivity as one promise among others), and whatever about how people actually form and keep attachments the theory can't be in tension with. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and whcih are just the default settings of the pro-monogamy or pro-nonmonogamy camps, and flag any pairs of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied — that last part's what I most want, since I suspect the list itself is doing a lot of the arguing. Don't bother with a survey of positions; if a requirement knocks out a standard view, just say which.
