I'm in my second year and taking a module on corruption and development, and something keeps bothering me that I don't have a name for. In one week we read about a local politician in a developing country who hands out food parcels and pays for voters' transport to a rally, and the seminar instantly treated it as clientelism, and therefore as corruption. The next week a British MP was in the news for pulling strings to get funding for a marginal constituency and for helping a donor's son get an internship, and my other politics class discussed it as "constituency service" and "networking" rather than corruption. I don't actually want to say the British case is fine and the other one isn't; what strikes me is that the vocabularly seems to do the judging before any argument is made. The same exchange gets called a "bribe" when it happens in one place and a "gift" or a "connection" when it happens in another, and once it has been labelled one way the conclusion is already settled.

Is there an established term for this asymmetry itself — not for corruption, but for the way the same practice gets sorted into moral vocabulary depending on which society it's occuring in? I can see it's near moral relativism and maybe Orientalism, but it isn't quite either: I'm not saying standards are just relative, and I'm not saying Western observers are being colonial on purpose. I'm after the specific move where a description does the normative work in advance, and what that does to the idea that corruption is a universal standard rather than a Western one. If there's no exact term, what would you call it, and where would you draw its edges? Oh, and could the answer be in British English, please?
