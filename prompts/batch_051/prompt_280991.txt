I've now seen the same few objections to qualia epiphenomenalism so many times that I want one I haven't already thought about, however weak it turns out to be. The ones I know, so dont give me these or a close variant: (1) self-stultification / the paradox of phenomenal judgment — if the felt pain causes nothing, then my judgment that I'm in pain is caused by the physical state and not by the pain, so my beliefs about my own qualia are insensitive to the qualia themselves (and the other-minds version, where your reports stop being evidence about your qualia, is just the same problem turned outward); (2) the evolutionary objection — natural selection can't shape a property with no effects, so consciousness ends up a pointless byproduct or an unexplained coincidence; (3) the introspection objection — from the inside it seems exactly like the awfulness of pain is what makes me pull my hand back, and epiphenomenalism says that's systematically illusory; (4) the parsimony objection — epiphenomenal qualia are nomological danglers, properties that hover above the causal story and explain nothing, and their stable correlation with the brain is one more brute fact, so a leaner view should cut them. Give me an objection to qualia epiphenomenalism that is not in that list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of them. Then tell me honestly whether it's any good; most apparently novel objections here collapse into one of the four once you lean on them, and I'd rather hear that than have you oversell it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — what the new objection is and whether it's any good — then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
