So my philosophy of language reading group is doing slurs next month and I want to hand out one of those "which theory do you actually hold?" quizzes before people have read the labels, so they sort by what they say about cases instead of picking a team. Give me eight to ten questions, each a concrete mini-case — a slur under negation, a report where the slur appears in someone else's mouth, a slur inside a movie villain's dialogue, an ingroup use, a bare mention like "he called me a ___", a slur aimed at a category no one in the room belongs to, one with no exact neutral counterpart, that sort of thing — with two or three answer options that only ask whether the sentence still feels offensive, whether the offensiveness projects, whether it depends on speaker intent, and so on. Then provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the main positions: semantic, presuppositional, conventional implicature, expressivist, prohibitionist/use-conditional, and a deflationary or sociopragmatic view. Flag any pair of leaves your questions can't actually separate, and mark any answer combinations nobody seems to hold, with a quick note on whether that's because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. The questions should be phrased so you can't tell which theory they point too, and no question may just ask "do you think the pejorative force is part of literal content?" Include at least one case that should break someone who wants to be both a presuppositionalist and a prohibitionist.
