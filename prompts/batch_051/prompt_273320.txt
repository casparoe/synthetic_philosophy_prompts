In a seminar on transitional justice, we are working with a hypothetical statute from a country emerging from a long internal conflict, deliberately modeled on the amnesty-for-truth bargains familiar from the South African process. The amnesty provision reads:

"Amnesty may be granted to any person who makes full disclosure of all facts known to him or her concerning an act or omission associated with a political objective in the course of the conflict, provided that the act or omission was not committed for personal gain and was not grossly disproportionate to the objective pursued."

The case we have been given concerns a former civilian programmer. In the fourth year of the conflict she was asked by the insurgency to write a small piece of software, which she then inserted into the state's fuel-supply database from her apartment. The software ran for nearly two years, silently corrupting routing records so that military convoys were repeatedly delayed or sent to the wrong depots, while civilian fuel distribution was largely unaffected. She never carried a weapon, never met anyone in the movement's leadership, and this is the only act she performed for it. She now applies for amnesty. The drafters' debates, as we have them, show that they were imagining shootings, bombings, torture, and the destruction of property by people physically present; nothing in the record suggests anyone contemplated an automated act carried out at a distance. She honestly describes what the software was designed to do, but she can no longer produce the code or a detailed log of what it did.

I am less interested in predicting what a real commission would decide than in testing the statute. Taking in turn the plain words of the clause, the legislative purpose behind it, what the drafters would likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader of the text would understand, does her conduct fall within the provision? In particular, I would like an analysis of what "associated with a political objective" requires—intention, membership in the movement, causal connection to the conflict, public visibility, or something else—and of what "full disclosure" can fairly demand from someone whose causal role was embedded in code she no longer has. It would also help to say whether the relevant act is her insertion of the software once or the two years of corrupted records that followed. I want the strongest case each interpretive approach can make, and an honest overall verdict on whether she is amnestiable under the clause.
