The exam question was:

"Natural selection is often described as a stupid, greedy, or blind optimizer, in contrast with gradient-based optimisation in machine learning. (a) Say what the greediness or blindness of natural selection consists in. (b) Assess the claim that, despite this, natural selection can be modelled as noisy gradient ascent on the mean fitness of a population. (c) Describe one feature of evolving populations that could help them avoid local optima that a naive gradient descent on a single point lacks."

The student wrote:

"Natural selection is greedy because it has no foresight: a new variant spreads just in case it does better than the current competition, so selection will never accept a temporary fall in fitness to reach a higher peak later. Gradient descent is greedy in a similar way, since each step moves in whatever direction lowers the loss now, and so it too can get stuck in a local basin. Beneath this, though, the two are closer than people admit. Natural selection is best seen as noisy gradient ascent on mean fitness. A genotype's frequency rises exactly when its fitness is above the population average, and the amount it rises is larger the further above the average it is, so the change in genotype frequencies is proportional to the marginal increase in mean fitness — which is just the discrete gradient. Selection therefore estimates a gradient from the finite set of variants currently present and takes a step along it. The only difference from gradient descent is that gradient descent computes the exact gradient of the loss over the whole dataset, whereas natural selection only has a noisy, sample-based estimate. For avoiding local optima, the real advantage of selection is that a population is a distribution rather than a single point: it keeps many variants alive at once, so parts of the population can sit on different slopes, and selection can shift weight toward whichever of those is doing better, whereas gradient descent has to abandon one point and start from scratch if it wants to consider another basin. So the 'stupid optimizer' label is overstated."
