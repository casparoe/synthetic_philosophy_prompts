Consider the following ladder of kinds of minds, usually associated with Daniel Dennett. A Darwinian creature gets its competences only from natural selection over generations; it has no within-lifetime learning. A Skinnerian creature can acquire new behaviors during its own lifetime through operant conditioning: it emits candidate behaviors, the environment reinforces some and extinguishes others, and the creature pays the real-world costs of its failed actual trials. A Popperian creature can rehearse candidate actions offline in an internal model of the world, so that failed candidates are discarded in the head before any actual behavior; only the candidate that survives this inner simulation is performed. A Gregorian creature can further import already-tested designs from other minds, chiefly through language and culture, and can deposit its own tested tricks into a shared pool, so that trial-and-error costs can be paid by others or by earlier generations.

Now consider a hypothetical burrowing mammal, the sand-modeller. Before a juvenile excavates its first real tunnel system, it spends weeks in a dry side chamber making small, roughly to-scale sand models of the future burrow site. The model-building disposition is innate and untaught, and adults ignore juveniles throughout. In each model the juvenile cuts a trial channel, releases water from a small pool, and watches where the water pools and where the walls collapse. It abandons channels that fail in the sand and builds a fresh model; the channel layout that survives the sand tests is then reproduced at full scale in the real burrow. There is no sign that the juvenile first rehearses flood imagery in its head before cutting a trial channel; its first trial is made in sand rather than in the head. The same animal later uses the sand-model technique when damming on unfamiliar terrain.

Apply the four-stage taxonomy criterion by criterion. Is the sand-modeller best classified as a Skinnerian, a Popperian, a stable intermediate, or a case the taxonomy cannot place? In particular, does the case show that the taxonomy's line between actual behavior and internal simulation is less important than the line between paying the full cost of an error and paying only the cheap cost of an error in a surrogate? Or should a physical scale model count as an externalized Popperian inner model? If the taxonomy leaves the sand-modeller unplaced, say whether the defect lies in the taxonomy's criteria or in demanding that a single organism occupy one rung.
