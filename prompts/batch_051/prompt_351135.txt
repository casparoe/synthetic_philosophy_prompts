I'm compiling a list of distinct anti-colonial criticisms in Adam Smith's Wealth of Nations for study notes. So far I've got these four, as I understand them: (1) the monopoly of the colonial trade pulls British capital away from the employments it would otherwise have taken and into a distant and uncertain market, which is bad for the stability of the whole economy; (2) the monopoly is mainly a transfer from the public to the merchants who profit from it; (3) the colonies are a net fiscal burden, since Britain pays to defend and administer them; and (4) restricting how colonists may use their own labor and stock is an injustice in its own right, whatever the economic effects. I want a fifth criticism from Smith that doesnt fall under any of these four headings—in particular, one that is not about trade flows, not about the public treasury, and not about the colonists' rights. It should be a different kind of argument, not a restatement or just two of the four stuck together. Then tell me whether Smith treats it as an independent argument or as one more instance of his general attack on the mercantile system.
