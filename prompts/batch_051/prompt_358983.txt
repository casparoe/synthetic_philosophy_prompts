I'm in a grad seminar on Lewis and convention—the whole coordination-equilibria, common-knowledge, regularity-in-a-population thing—and since my mom grew up in Stockholm, I keep being the person who has to correct the seminar's version of the Swedish traffic switch. I want to actually work the case through the theory rather than just use it as an anecdote. The episode is Dagen H: on 3 September 1967 Sweden moved, at 5 a.m., from driving on the left to driving on the right, after a gigantic state-run information campaign. The salient facts, as I understand them: every neighboring country drove on the right; more than 90% of Swedish cars were already left-hand drive, which made overtaking on two-lane roads genuinely dangerous; a 1955 referendum had come back 83% against switching; the Riksdag went ahead anyway; the overnight changeover was surprisingly smooth; and accident rates actually dropped for a while before creeping back to roughly their old levels by 1969, probably because people drove scared.

I want a disciplined two-way case analysis: Dagen H through Lewis's theory of convention, and the theory through the case. Act as a philosopher of social convention who knows Lewis inside out. Start by laying out the coordination problem the drivers faced—who the players are, what the options are, what exactly the old left-drive regularity consisted in, and what the state had to do to make the new right-drive regularity common knowledge rather than just a legislated rule. Then push back. Lewis's story is built for conventions that are arbitrary in the sense that some other regularity could have served, and for conventions that mostly arise and maintain themselves; here right-hand traffic was better along several real asymmetries, and the switch was a centralized, top-down reset of a convention on a single date. Does the episode confirm the theory, expose a gap in it, or is it a case the theory was never really about? Where is the Lewisian story silent rather than wrong? Start with two or three candidate readings of what the case shows before you commit to one, and be careful about wether the post-switch dip in accidents belongs in the convention explanation at all.
