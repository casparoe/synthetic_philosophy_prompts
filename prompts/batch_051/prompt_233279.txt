I'm a novelist, not an AI person, so bare with me. I've been reading about what alignment people call embedded agency: the idea that an agent isn't a little Cartesian spectator standing outside the world, it's a physical chunk of the world it's trying to model, so any model of the environment has to include the modeler as part of that environment. That gives you self-reference — the thing doing the predicting is inside what's being predicted, and predicting your own next action changes the action you're predicting. The usual upshot is that the clean map/territory picture stops working, because the map gets drawn on a bit of the territory and can't be peeled off.

This felt immediatly familiar from my day job. A third-person narrator pretends to be exactly that Cartesian spectator: outside the story, causally cut off from what it describes. But a first-person narrator, especially in autofiction or a confessional novel, is embedded in the story it tells — the I who narrates is also the I being narrated, and the act of self-description, written inside the book, becomes another event in the life it's describing. Same recursion, same missing boundary between model and modeled. It even shows up as an actual device: a character who reads the early chapters of the novel they're inside now has a model of the story, and having that model changes the rest of the story.
