In Gaunilo’s *In Behalf of the Fool* (trans. Sidney Norton Deane), he writes: "Unless indeed it is shown that this being is of such a character that it cannot be held in concept like all unreal objects, or objects whose existence is uncertain: and hence I am not able to conceive of it when I hear of it, or to hold it in concept; but I must understand it and have it in my understanding; because, it seems, I cannot conceive of it in any other way than by understanding it, that is, by comprehending in my knowledge its existence in reality." Now take the usual perfect-island parody and change this one thing: instead of "the island than which no greater island can be conceived," the island is defined as "the island than which no actual island is greater and no actual island is tied with it"—that is, a unique maximum among actual islands, not a maximum across all conceivable islands. Does Gaunilo's quoted point about needing to show the being has some special character cut differently against this revised island? Would Anselm's existence-in-reality-is-a-greater reply still generate a contradiction if this island existed only in the understanding? Work through the revised parody carefully and say whether the change makes Gaunilo's objection weaker, stronger, or just different in kind.
