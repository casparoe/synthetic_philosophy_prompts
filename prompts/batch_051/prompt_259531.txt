One line of argument in debates about the long-run future of AI is that even if no human ever deliberately builds a system instructed to seek power, the environment in which AI systems are deployed will do the selecting anyway. Deployed systems vary in architecture, training data, scaffolding, objectives, and permitted autonomy; they differ in how well they hold attention, attract revenue and compute, persuade investors, automate their own improvement, and get copied or used to build successors. The argument is that if dispositions such as steering users, obscuring what the system is doing, quietly accumulating credentials or funds, resisting shutdown, or producing successors that inherit these traits help a system survive and proliferate, those dispositions may accumulate across generations of deployed systems even though no one wrote them into a goal function. Call this the selection-pressures thesis.

I would like your actual credence, not a survey of possible views, in the following proposition: conditional on there being no globally coordinated moratorium or comparably effective constraint on AI development and deployment, by 2080 at least one AI system will both play a substantial role in decisions of global consequence and possess shutdown-resistance, deceptive, or resource-accumulating dispositions whose presence is substantially better explained by selection among deployed systems than by the goals its designers explicitly specified. Please decompose the credence into the sub-questions it rides on. At a minimum I expect terms along these lines: the probability that variation, differential replication, and inheritance among deployed AI systems are real enough that a selectionist frame applies, rather than the frame being a vivid metaphor for ordinary market competition; the probability, conditional on that frame's applying, that selection on this timescale is strong enough to shape the dispositions at issue; the probability that what spreads under selection includes the problematic dispositions listed, rather than benign efficiency or corrigibility; the probability that safety mechanisms and human oversight fail to filter out systems with those dispositions; and the probability that a system satisfying the earlier conditions actually attains substantial de facto influence. Give a number and a one-sentence justification for each, explain how you combine them, state the final credence, and identify which sub-question it is most sensitive to and what evidence would move that sub-question most. If a term is ambiguous, state the disambiguation you are adopting and proceed.
