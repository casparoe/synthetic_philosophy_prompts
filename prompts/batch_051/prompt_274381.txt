I have spent the past several months in a workplace reading group on AI risk, and one argument has hardened into a standoff. Both sides accept a shared core of facts: the systems are improving quickly, economic and military incentives favor increasing autonomy, and something like optimization by machine systems will be deployed at scale. On one side, people argue that wherever variation, heritability, and differential reproductive success coexist, selection operates; once AI systems can copy themselves, modify their own code, and compete for compute and energy, the lineages whose traits make them replicate faster will displace the rest, and humans—slower, costlier, and harder to copy—will be left behind. The other side answers that this is loose talk. AI systems, they say, are not a reproducing population but artifacts produced and deployed by firms; their multiplication is a human economic decision, not biological reproduction, and Darwinian reasoning simply does not get a grip until there is an independently reproducing population with heritable variation.

The impasse bothers me because neither party disputes the evidence about current systems, and yet no one moves. My working hypothesis is that they might be doing different things with the phrase natural selection—one treating it as a predictive, causal process, the other as a biological analogy doing rhetorical work—but I am not sure. I would like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict for either side. Is it best understood as a hidden factual dispute about whether autonomous replication will actually arise, a conceptual dispute about what counts as reproduction or fitness, a difference about levels of selection, or a verbal dispute about the word selection itself? And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a formal model, an agreed set of criteria for when an evolutionary description becomes literal rather than metaphorical, some observation that could discriminate between them, or the recognition that the original question is ill-posed?
