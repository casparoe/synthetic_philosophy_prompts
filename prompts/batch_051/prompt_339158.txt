Could you write a parody terms-of-service agreement for a fictional app called Buridan? The concept: a subscription service for people stuck in exact ties — the Buridan's ass setup, where a donkey stranded halfway between two equally good bales of hay starves because it has no reason to prefer one over the other. The app is different from normal decision apps because it doesn't pretend to find your "real" preference or coach you toward a gut feeling — that would assume the tie isn't real. Instead it breaks the tie from outside your preference structure: it picks one option, and you agree in advance to treat the pick as your choice. Around 650 words, formatted as numbered clauses. The humor should come from the actual philosophy, not generic "I can't decide what to have for lunch" jokes: the puzzle of whether a rational choice needs a preference or a reason, whether an outside tie-breaker can really make the resulting action yours, whether any observable sign (like how quickly you click "accept" on the pick) just becomes a revealed preference anyway, whether you can certify that your preferences are perfectly balanced without that certification itself being a preference, and the contrast between a true tie and incommensurability, where two options can't be put on a single scale at all. It should be funny to someone who knows revealed-preference theory and the Buridan debates, but readable to someone who doesn't. Keep it deadpan legalese — no winking — but make the satire legible. Include a clause where the app logs your hesitation before accepting a tie-break as evidence that you secretly preferred the other option. And have the fine print quietly land the standard objections — that this just moves the Buridan problem up one level, that outsourcing the decision can't give you a reason, that "no preference" might not be a stable state to contract around — without ever naming them as objections.
