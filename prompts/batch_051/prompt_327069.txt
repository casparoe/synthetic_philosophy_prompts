I cover science for a general-interest magazine and I've been sitting in on some quantum foundations sessions lately. The same move keeps coming up: a younger person will ask whether the wavefunction is a real thing or just bookeeping, whether the other Everett branches exist, or whether Bohmian particle positions are hidden variables worth taking seriously, and a senior physicist will cut it off by saying shut up and calculate. The room treats it like a mic drop. I honestly can't tell what its supposed to be doing. Is it a claim about physics? About what physics is for? About which questions are even meaningful? Or just career advice? I'd like to write a column that unpacks the slogan instead of just repeating it. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan shut up and calculate might be expressing, from the boring to the radical, say which ones are defensible and which are false or self-undermining, which one the people saying it probably mean, and identify which reading would actually justify using it to close down an interpretive question? A menu I can steal from would be ideal.
