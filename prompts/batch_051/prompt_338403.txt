I keep hearing the phrase one thought too many in seminars on Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism. It usually appears when someone invokes his example of a husband deciding whether to save his wife, and the objector says something like: if he considers the situation from an impartial perspective before acting, that is already one thought too many. The phrase is then left to do all the argumentative work. I want a disambiguation of that piece of language. What distinct claims could one thought too many be expressing about the relation between moral justification and certain intimate commitments? Distinguish the readings that are mere platitudes from those that are substantive or false. Which reading would actually support the anti-utilitarian argument it is usually deployed in, and which readings would leave that argument unsupported?
