so in my philosophy of mind module we've been on panpsychism for the last couple of weeks, and i've got a version of it i've been turning over that i want checked before i put it in my essay. the combination problem is usually framed as: how do lots of little subjects (micro-subjects) add up to one big subject, like me? but i think that framing sneaks in an assumption — that whatever is at the bottom has to be a subject at all. my idea, which i'm calling the mosaic view (or pixel panpsychism, i cant decide), is that the fundamental level is made of subjectless experiences. these are bits of what-it-is-like-ness, but they aren't for anyone; a pixel isn't a tiny picture, but pixels arranged the right way just are a picture. similarly, micro-experiences aren't tiny selves, but arranged in the right structural/functional way they constitute one unified point of view. so the real question isn't how subjects combine, it's how subjectless experiences give rise to a subject. and that feels like a much easier question because you're not summing selves, you're summing ingredients into a pattern.

i realise the obvious objection is that an experience with no experiencer sounds like a contradiction — a pain that nobody feels. my defence is that the experiencer is a construction, not an owner: the self is a pattern the experiences fall into, not a thing that has them. i know this might just be Hume's bundle theory with extra steps, and i also know there's a position called constitutive micropsychism that i might be accidentally reinventing. what i want to know is: (1) is this already in the literature under some name, and if so how is it different from the standard versions, (2) is the distinction between combining subjects and combining subjectless experiences a real distinction or am i just relabelling the same problem, and (3) what is the strongest objection — is the no-subject worry fatal, or just uncomfortable? be honest, i'd rather find out now than in the exam.
