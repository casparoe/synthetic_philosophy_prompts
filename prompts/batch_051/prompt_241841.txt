A common answer to the paradox of tragedy is that we enjoy sad fiction because of distance: spectators feel the sorrow and fear vividly, but not as a real threat, and that safe remove is what lets painful emotions become pleasurable. I work on interactive software, and this answer seems built for a passive medium. In many games the consumer is not only the spectator of the loss but also its agent: the sad event occurs through the player's actions, sometimes unavoidably. Does the distance-based answer survive once the audience is causally inside the tragedy? If so, say exactly which premise is now doing the work and how distance should be reinterpreted. If not, say whether the solution can be repaired by appealing to fictionality, consent, or agency, or whether interactive tragedy needs a different account of why people seek it out. Be explicit about which step of the old answer interactivity bears on, and tell me how confident you are in what you claim and which point you are least sure of.
