His summary, from what I remember: Prioritarianism is the view that a given benefit matters more when it goes to someone worse off. That, he said, is why the leveling-down objection refutes it. The objection says that if equality had value for its own sake, then leveling everyone down to the worst-off position would still be an improvement in one respect, because you'd have more equality even though nobody gains. That seems absurd, so prioritarianism collapses. I have a vague feeling he's running prioritarianism together with plain egalitarianism and that the whole point of prioritarianism was to avoid this exact objection, but I cant tell which parts of his summary are actually wrong.
