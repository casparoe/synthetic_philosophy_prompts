im in this philosophy of animal minds course and one thing keeps bugging me: our whole unit on Morgan's canon feels stuck in a loop. you know the rule, Lloyd Morgan's old principle that if you can account for an animals behavior in terms of a lower psychological process, you shouldnt attribute a higher one to it. half the seminar treats it as basic scientific hygiene—when a cleaner associationist story is available, dont go positing planning or theory of mind, because the fancy attributions are underconstrained and the field used to be full of anecdotal anthropomorphism. the other half says the canon isnt neutral at all: they say "simpler" usually cashes out as "lower on the old scala naturae," which stacks the deck against capacities you might expect given evolutionary continuity with humans, and they point out that a supposedly simple associative model often needs so many auxilary assumptions that its more complicated than the richer explanation it was meant to replace.

everyone agrees on the experimental results—the caching behavior, the false-belief-looking tasks, all that—but after two months nobody has moved an inch. so what kind of disagreement is this even? is it verbal (two senses of "simple" that people keep sliding between), a dispute about where the burden of proof goes, a disagreement about priors that the evidence cant settle, or are the two camps answering different questions under one heading? and given whatever diagnosis you land on, is there anything that would count as progress—like actually formalizing the model comparison, or replacing the higher/lower talk with specific mechanistic hypotheses—or is this debate just going to loop forever?
