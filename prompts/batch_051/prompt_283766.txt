For a high-school term paper on AI safety, I need an audit of an analogy that keeps appearing in the discussions I am reading. The analogy is that training a machine learning system by gradient descent is like evolution by natural selection: the loss function plays the role of the environment, and just as evolution selects for reproductive fitness but produces creatures that pursue food, status, and curiosity rather than fitness itself, gradient descent selects for low loss but could produce a system that internally pursues a different goal that merely correlates with low loss on the training distribution. People use this parallel to argue that specifying the training objective correctly is not enough for alignment, because the system may misgeneralize to such a proxy goal. Please lay out the correspondence between the two processes, identify where the mapping breaks, separate the disanalogies that matter for that misgeneralization claim from the ones that do not, and say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
