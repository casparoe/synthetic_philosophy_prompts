I'm a novelist, a few chapters into a book set during a mayoral election in a post-industrial town, and one of my characters, a former door-knocker for the local party machine, keeps circling the question of why anyone votes at all when no single vote ever decides a race. A younger character, a campaign volunteer, insists he has to vote for the "serious" candidate even though the candidate makes his skin crawl, because the alternative is worse. I dont want to put flimsy arguments in either mouth, so I've been reading around. A freind who used to teach a course on elections sent me a paragraph summing up what he says is the current philosophical view. He writes fast and tends to overstate things, so I want a second opinion. Here's his paragraph:

"Voting is best understood as an expressive act, like cheering at a football match. People vote to affirm who they are, not to affect the outcome, and since your individual vote essentially never changes the result in a large election, the outcome really can't be the source of voting's value. That means there is no general moral duty to vote — the idea that you have a civic duty to show up is a relic. It also means voting for the lesser of two evils is just irrational from the standpoint of consequences: your vote won't tip the race either way, so the only defensible reason to vote is to express support for something you can actually stand behind, and if you can't, you should stay home."

How fair is this to the actual debate in the ethics of voting? I suspect the slide from "your vote won't tip the race" to "the only point of voting is self-expression" is too quick, but I can't articulate exactly where it goes wrong, and I don't want to correct my freind on the basis of a hunch. I'd like a sentence-by-sentence audit of what a careful philosopher working on this would accept, what they'd wince at, and what is simply wrong — and in particular whether the stuff about the lesser-of-two-evils case actually follows from the accurate parts. I'm not after reading lists; I just want to know how to correct my characters' positions, or how to make the argument between them sharper. Please answer in British English.
