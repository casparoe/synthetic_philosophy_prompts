Over the past term I have been taking a comparative religion course, and its definitional debates have begun to feel more than academic to me. The working definition taught in the course runs as follows. A religion (i) posits beings or powers that transcend the ordinary material world; (ii) gathers a self-perpetuating community rather than a transient crowd; (iii) structures time and space through ritual and some version of the sacred–profane distinction; (iv) grounds moral obligation in its account of how things are; and (v) offers an orienting narrative about human existence and its place in the cosmos. The personal spur is that my partner attends a small secular congregation that meets each month to mark the deep history of life. It holds vigils on anniversaries of recorded extinctions, keeps a book of the dead for lost species, requires vows of planetary kinship, and treats certain geological exposures with an attentiveness my grandmother's parish reserves for relics. The congregation is explicit that nothing supernatural is involved: no gods, no souls, no afterlife, no cosmic agency, no powers beyond matter. Measured against criteria (ii) through (v), it resembles a religion; measured against criterion (i), it resembles nothing of the sort.

The difficulty is that the course's own examples refuse to line up with that boundary. It classifies Confucianism as a religion even though its supernatural commitments are thin and contested, and it gives significant room to traditions that are orthopraxic in emphasis — organized more around correct ritual and ethical cultivation than around correct belief in transcendent beings. I am therefore left with a concrete question: under the criteria as stated, does this congregation qualify as a religion? Argue it criterion by criterion. If the answer is yes, is criterion (i) doing real work, or is it a historical accident marking a local feature of the religious traditions the definition's authors knew best? If the answer is no, am I committed to saying that Confucianism is not a religion either, or must the criteria themselves be revised?
