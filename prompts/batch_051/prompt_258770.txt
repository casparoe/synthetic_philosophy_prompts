I'm a backend engineer who builds observability and alerting pipelines, and I keep tripping over Fitch's paradox. As I understand it, the idea is: if every true proposition is knowable (there's some possible way to come to know it), then every true proposition is actually known. The move is to take a true but unknown p—"p is true and nobody knows p is true." By knowability, that whole conjunction is knowable, so someone could know "p is true and nobody knows it." But knowing a conjunction means knowing each conjunct, so they'd know p—and know that nobody knows p—which is impossible, because their knowing p would already mean somebody knows it. So no unknown truths exist. That sounds flatly wrong, and I want to see whether it survives being restated in the vocab I actualy use. In particular I want a honest verdict on whether the paradox evaporates once "knowledge" is time-indexed and the act of checking changes what's stored, or whether the collapse still bites.
