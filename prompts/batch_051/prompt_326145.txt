For the last few months my flatmates and I have been playing a nomic-style game, where changing the rules is a move you can make. Our Rule 9 reads: "A player may propose an amendment to any rule. The proposal is adopted if, within forty-eight hours, a majority of the players vote aye, and it takes effect immediately. Notwithstanding the above, an amendment to Rule 9 itself must receive the unanimous vote of all players." There's six of us. Last week someone proposed exactly one change to Rule 9: replace "unanimous" with "two-thirds," so the threshold for amending Rule 9 becomes two-thirds. Four of us voted aye, two voted nay. That's two-thirds but not unanimous. The proposer says the amendment passed, because once adopted Rule 9 only requires two-thirds and the proposal got two-thirds. I say it failed, because the votes were cast under the old rule, and a proposal can't decide the threshold for its own adoption retroactively. Half the point of the unanimity clause was that nobody could lower the bar for changing Rule 9 without already clearing the bar—and now it looks like you clear the bar exactly by lowering it once. The chair declared it passed and now nobody can agree who's right. The rules say nothing about self-referential proposals. Walk me through what the text of Rule 9, the purpose of the unanimity clause, what the friend who drafted Rule 9 would have said if we'd asked him, and what a reasonable player reading Rule 9 before the vote would have expected, each give on this. Then say which approach should actually settle it in a game like this, and whether it matters that on the proposer's reading the unanimity clause can be removed by a non-unanimous vote exactly once and then it's gone for good. Keep it under 500 words, please.
