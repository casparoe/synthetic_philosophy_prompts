I am convening a semester-long working group of fifteen philosophers and political theorists on what counts as corruption in cases of gift-giving, patronage, and clientelism, understood as the exchange of goods, services, or protection for political support within particularistic relationships. The group has split into two camps before the collaboration begins. One camp holds a universalist position: an exchange is corrupt when it subordinates a role-based duty, such as an officeholder's duty of impartiality, to a particularistic relationship, and it remains corrupt even when participants and their community describe the exchange as gift-giving. The other camp holds a contextualist position: imported anti-corruption standards misdescribe practices of gift and favor that constitute a social order, because the relevant duties are particularistic, reciprocal, and temporally extended, and judging such practices requires understanding their internal functioning rather than applying an external ideal of impersonal fairness. Write the joint statement that both camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims both sides accept, phrased so as not to be vacuous; the exact formulations of their residual disagreement, each in wording its own side would ratify; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side; and for each crux, a concrete case or kind of evidence both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and include no bibliography or reading list.
