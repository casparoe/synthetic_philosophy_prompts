I've been trying to nail down what eugenics as a word actually covers, and it keeps sliding around. Can you give me a working definition — of the word, not the historical movement — and run it through these cases?

Cases I'm confident about:
- Forced sterilization of people with disabilities, race-based marriage restrictions: in. Everyone agrees these are eugenics.
- A couple using birth control because they can't afford another kid: out.
- Putting folic acid in flour to reduce neural tube defects at a population level: out.

Borderline:
- A couple doing IVF and preimplantation genetic testing, discarding embryos that would have cystic fibrosis. I'd say not eugenics, but I can't state the principle that makes it not.
- The same couple using the same testing to pick an embryo with a higher polygenic score for IQ. If the CF case is fine and this one isn't, what exact difference is doing the work — disease vs normal variation, treatment vs enhancement, or something else?
- Selecting an embryo's sex for no medical reason.
- A deaf couple deliberately selecting an embryo with a hearing-loss mutation because they want a child who can share their language and Deaf culture. This feels different from the IQ case to me, but I might just be rationalizing.
- A government program that pays low-income women to get long-acting contraception, run by officials who openly worry that the wrong sort of people are having more kids. Is the program eugenics, or does the eugenic part live entirely in the officials' heads?
- A private sperm bank that lets clients sort donors by height, education, and race, and advertises its donors as genetically exceptional. Is that eugenics when it's customers exercising choice and no state is involved?

Propose a definition, run it through all of them, and tell me where it gives a verdict you'd defend rather than revise. Then, I suspect the ordinary word does two jobs: one descriptive (deliberate selection for genetic traits) and one condemnatory (an institution or expert class controlling who reproduces). If you buy that, give me both definitions and say which cases move between them. Also say which of your verdicts you're least confident in, and whether the condemnation is built into the word or is just what usually accompanies it.

Also, I definately want to know where your definition draws the line that feels most arbitrary, because right now every line I try feels arbitrary.
