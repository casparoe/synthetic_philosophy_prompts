I help run a tiny fact-checking page with two friends. Our editorial guide was written in 2016, back when everything we checked was a claim about something that had already happend, and it contains this sentence: "A claim may be published under the 'Verified' label only when the editor responsible knows it to be true." Yesterday one of the editors wanted to tag as Verified a claim that the prime minister will call a snap election before the end of the year. She has spoken to three party officials and seen internal polling, and she can give a probability, but she cannot say she knows it is true, because it has not happened yet. She argues that if the rule is read as a straightforward knowledge requirement we can never verify any prediction, which would gut our new politics coverage; another editor says the rule is only meant to exclude guessing and she is not guessing.

I want to know what the rule gives on the plain words, the intended purpose, what we would have written in 2016 if someone had raised the prediction case, and what a reasonable editor picking up the guide today would take it to mean. And then I want the deeper version: does our guide actually commit us to a knowledge norm of assertion, and would a truth norm or a belief norm handle the prediction case better? Which reading is the honest one, and which is special pleading from an editor who wants the label?
