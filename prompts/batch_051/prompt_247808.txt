Take the role of a professional philosopher of action who is also fluent in the behavioural economics of intertemporal choice. Think the question through carefully before answering; if it helps, begin by brainstorming the strongest objections and then work out which of them survive scrutiny.

I have increasingly come to accept the following position, and I want an honest map of its exposure rather than a survey of textbook objections. The position is this: what philosophers call weakness of will, or akrasia, is not a distinct failure of agency over and above the preference reversal produced by hyperbolic discounting. The person who judges that she should give up smoking, or start writing, and then lights a cigarette or opens a video is best modelled as a naive, present-biased discounter. At a temporal distance she discounts a larger later good steeply but not decisively; as the smaller sooner good becomes imminent, her effective preference flips; and because she fails to predict the flip, she experiences herself as having been overtaken by a desire she only half-endorses. On this view, weakness of will is hyperbolic discounting plus a forecasting error, and an additional executive faculty, or a sui generis failure of the will, is doing no explanatory work.

Tell me what would have to be true for this position to be wrong, and sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. In particular, address at least the following: clear-eyed akrasia, in which an agent acts against her all-things-considered judgement at the very moment of action without any reversal of judgement; sophisticated hyperbolic discounters, who predict their own lapse and still lapse; putative akratic choices in which the competing goods are not separated by delay, so no reversal could be time-driven; and the charge that the view has ceased to explain weakness of will and has instead redescribed it as a mere change in time-indexed preference, thereby eliminating the normative element it was meant to capture. For each, say which escape routes are open to a defender of my position, which are live and which merely logically possible, and what the view predicts that a distinct executive-failure account would not. Finish by telling me whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be a problem for a view of this kind.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
