So I got one of those online ordinations a few years back mostly as a joke, but now I'm apparently the designated wedding guy: I've done two, I've got three more this summer, and I keep lying awake the night before worried that I'll flub a name or skip the "by the power vested in me" part and the couple won't actually be married. It's made me weirdly curious about performative speech in general, the whole saying-it-makes-it-so thing. I want a reusable procedure I can actually run before and during a ceremony: the questions in order, what counts as good enough at each step, where the procedure stops with a hard no, and where it has to hand off to a human judgement call that no checklist can eliminate. Ground it in what the philosophy of speech acts actually says (felicity conditions, misfires versus abuses, whatever else earned its keep), but translate it into plain steps I can run on no sleep with a nervous couple in front of me. Then apply it to three hypotheticals: (1) a couple where one person's vows are in sign language and I only speak English; (2) a groom who says "I do" flatly, then tells me afterwards he didn't mean it and was just doing it because the families were there; (3) a couple that wants me to do the pronouncement over a video call, me in one state, them and the witnesses in another. Make sure at least one worked case is one where the procedure tells me something I won't like, and say straight out what the checklist can't decide and why. I especially want to know whether "he didn't mean it" is even my business once the words are out.
