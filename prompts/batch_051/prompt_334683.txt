I co-organize a metaphysics of science reading group that has spent the past year deadlocked between a Best-System-Account Humean about laws of nature and a dispositional-essentialist anti-Humean. Write the document this pair should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Begin with a numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign—about the target phenomena a theory of laws must capture, about what each side gets right and where the familiar objections (to Humean supervenience, to the inference problem, to quidditism, to the nomic–accidental distinction) do and do not bite. Then give the precise residual disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view. Then, in a disagreements table, identify the cruxes—I suspect at least three, including whether laws explain the regularities or merely encode them, and whether natural properties carry their modal profiles intrinsically—and for each crux state a concrete case, argument, or hypothetical both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Act as a professional philosopher of science and metaphysics, think carefully, and take time to brainstorm formulations before committing. Where you are unsure, say so; in particular, tell me how confident you are in the joint statement overall and single out the one point you are least sure of.
