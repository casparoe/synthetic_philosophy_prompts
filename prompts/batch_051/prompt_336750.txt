You are a professional philosopher specializing in decision theory, formal epistemology, and the ethics of risk. I need an audit of a set of views I currently hold, not a survey of positions. Please think carefully and systematically; before you begin evaluating individual commitments, take a moment to map out how they bear on one another.

For a paper I am writing, I have defended each of the following claims separately, but I have never checked whether they form a stable package:

(1) Instrumental rationality requires maximizing expected utility: an agent should choose the act with the greatest expected utility, where utility is a finite real-valued function over outcomes.

(2) There is no independent rational risk-aversion in utility space. What looks like risk-aversion about money is just diminishing marginal utility of money; once outcomes are denominated in utils, rationality is risk-neutral.

(3) Tiny probabilities are not zero probabilities. A nonzero chance of a huge good can be exactly what makes an act choiceworthy; smallness of probability does not by itself silence a possible payoff.

(4) But the correct theory must not be fanatical. I accept the standard verdict that one should not pay in Pascal's mugging (the scenario in which someone demands a small amount of money in exchange for a tiny chance of an enormous future benefit), and I reject any theory on which an arbitrarily tiny probability of an arbitrarily large finite payoff can dictate what one does.

(5) Utility is unbounded: for any finite level of value, some possible outcome is better by some finite margin. There are no principled caps on how good outcomes can be.

(6) This matters practically because I am a longtermist: I think an intervention with a one-in-ten-thousand chance of substantially reducing existential risk (the risk that humanity's future is permanently destroyed) can be more important than interventions with large near-term benefits, simply because the possible future is so large.

Audit this package. I want to know: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be in tension, and why? Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each, say what would have to give and what I would lose by giving it up. Then tell me which single revision would make the package most coherent while preserving as much of it as possible.
