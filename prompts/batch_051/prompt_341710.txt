Write me a villanelle about Dōgen's being-time (uji). I keep trying to explain that he isnt saying time is a container moments move through, or that things sit inside time; being just is time and time just is being, and the now is not a skinny boundary between a fixed past and a fixed future — before and after are built right into just this now. So the form has to do the work: do not let the poem unfold as a story from earlier to later. Have each stanza turn up a different face of the same moment, and let the two refrains be two ways of saying that nothing is missing from this time, taking turns until they meet in the closing couplet and say being and time were never two things. Someone who has read the Uji fascicle should recognize the view, and someone who hasnt should still get a real poem. Then, under the heading Commentary, give me about 150–200 words on which reading of uji you leaned on, where you simplified or stretched it, and why you made the formal choices — and don't hedge, commit to an interpretation.
