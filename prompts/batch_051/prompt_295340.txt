I am trying to work out what I actually think about humour by looking at my verdicts on concrete cases. I know the four standard candidate theories — superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation — and each of them seems to capture one part of my reactions while failing on others.

Here are the cases as I see them. An unhurt but pompous public official slips on a banana peel and lands in a fountain: I laugh, and I suspect part of my enjoyment is pleasure at his deflation. A small child trips on the ice and cuts her face: not funny at all; if I laughed, that would be a personal failing rather than a difference of taste. A well-constructed joke with a surprising punchline is genuinely funny the first time I hear it, but the same joke told again a minute later is not funny, even though I can still see why it was funny before. A pun that turns on two meanings of a word amuses me only faintly, and the feeling resembles the small flash of solving a puzzle more than real mirth. After a near car crash, the driver and I laughed shakily for a minute; that was laughter and it did feel like a release, but I would not say the situation was funny. Tickling a child produces helpless laughter without any humour. A taboo-subject joke told among close friends, with no one present as its target, strikes me as funny; the very same joke told by a stranger at a formal dinner strikes me as merely uncomfortable. A comedian joking about his own terminal illness made me laugh and wince in the same breath; I think it was funny, but my friend insists that it was only sad. A baby laughing at peek-a-boo is delightful and the baby is certainly laughing, but I would not say the baby has a sense of humour.

Is there a single principle behind these verdicts, or a small set of principles, and how should the four theories divide the labour? Please state the account precisely, say what it predicts for cases I have not listed, and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and which I would have to overturn. In particular, what does your account say about a case I keep going back and forth on: a cruel but brilliantly constructed piece of mockery that makes me laugh involuntarily and then feel ashamed?
