My reading group has been on consciousness, and they asked me to write down where I've landed so someone can audit it. Here's roughly my set of commitments: (1) I'm a physicalist about experience—there's nothing over and above the physical and functional facts. (2) But I also think the explanatory gap is real: no purely functional story will make it intelligable why it feels like anything at all. (3) I find Block's access/phenomenal distinction basically right, and I'm persuaded by the overflow claim—in the Sperling-type cases I'm conscious of more than I can report. (4) I think global workspace theory is the best account of what makes a state conscious: a content becomes conscious when it's broadcast to the workspace and made available for report, memory, and control—and I mean this as a theory of phenomenal consciousness, not just reportability. (5) I also think the opposite asymmetry is possible: access without phenomenality. Blindsight is probably an instance, and a carefully built future system could report and use a stimulus with no feel at all. (6) I think any physical/functional duplicate of me would have exactly the same experiences. So: which of these actually pull against each other, and how bad are the tensions? For each real tension, tell me what would have to give and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And be up front about confidence: for each call, where is your reasoning likeliest to fail and what would change your mind?
