Suppose you could walk into Thomas Malthus's study around 1800 and hand him a one-page briefing on world agriculture since 1900: synthetic nitrogen fertilizer, wheat yields per acre, the Green Revolution, global population, famine rates. What do you think he'd actualy conclude—that his principle of population was simply falsified, or that it was right but early and the reckoning has just been postponed? Give me your best reconstruction of the reasoning he'd use, not a scholarly guess about sources. In particular: would he treat "food supply can't keep up with population" as the core of the theory, so the yield data kills it, or as a surface-level prediction he could rescue by pointing to finite land, energy, and pollution as the deeper limit? And do you think he'd actually make that rescue move, given his temperament and theology? Be as specific as you can about where the line between falsification and postponement would fall for him.
