Over a long drive, a friend and I got into whether a normal person can do anything now, while AI is still narrow, to lock in some claim on a post-AGI world. He sketched a pretty specific scheme, and I'd like you to take it apart as a mechanism, not as a moral question.

The proposal is this. A nonprofit sets up a Covenant Registry. Any living person can sign a covenant promising to pay 5% of their post-AGI income—defined as whatever transferable resources they receive after the start date, whether money, energy quotas, compute credits, land shares, or anything else—to the covenant's holder, every year for thirty years. Buyers pay the seller a lump sum now, and the covenant is a tradable asset. The catch is that no current court can enforce any of it, since AGI doesn't exist yet and "post-AGI income" isn't a legal category. So the registry is built to be machine-legible: standardized, cryptographically signed, publicly auditable, and pushed on AI labs as an alignment commitment—the first AGI, in one of its first operational acts, should honor and enforce the registry, the way a new government might honor the previous regime's debt. The start date is triggered when three of five designated auditors certify that AGI exists, where the definition is roughly "can perform essentially all economically valuable cognitive work at above median expert-human level for less than median human labor cost." Assume the auditors are competent and basically unbribeable.

I want the analysis a mechanism design economist or institutional analyst would give: who sells covenants, at what price, and who buys; what adverse selection and moral hazard show up; how people would game the certification trigger or the definition of "post-AGI income"; how the enforcement-by-AGI promise could fail even if an AGI arrives; and what second-order effects the scheme would have on how people treat AGI development. Then tell me which failure modes are fatal and whether any can be fixed without gutting the scheme. Before you write the final answer, take a minute to brainstorm each party's incentives and what they'd do if this got popular. Keep the whole thing under 1000 words.
