I'm a novelist, working on a book set in a near-future city where a single public "merit" score does double duty as reputation and currency, and I've been lurking in some karma-based forums, a few subreddits, and a Discord with reaction-based leveling to get the texture right. There's a pattern I keep noticing that I don't have a good word for, and I want the concept straightened out more than I want a name to drop.

In one long-running forum, the accounts with the most karma are officially the most trusted, but the actual old-timers roll their eyes at exactly those accounts. The genuinely respected users often have pretty middling scores because they post rarely and never fish for upvotes. Newcomers, though, read the score as a quality signal and defer to the karma-millionaires, which drives the old-timers up the wall.

In another place, I noticed that the moment somebody cites their own high score to settle an argument, they've lost the argument. The move itself reads as a status marker—and a bad one. The highest-status people either hide their score or perform indifference to it: they let points decay, or deliberately "waste" them, as though the only way to prove the points don't matter to you is to have them and throw them away.

The underlying dynamic seems to be: once a reputation metric becomes public and desirable, the people who conspicuously pile it up stop being the people with the most actual standing, and the real status order migrates to whatever the metric can't capture—including a performance of not caring about the metric. So the score ends up measuring eagerness to be measured more than the thing its built to measure.

Is there an established name for this exact dynamic? I can see it's near Goodhart's law and maybe near countersignaling (if I'm using that right), but neither feels like precisely this. If there's a term in the reputation-systems or game-design literature, tell me what it picks out and how it differs from those two. If there isn't, coin one and define it tightly enough that I can tell which cases count and which don't.

And please say how confident you are in the answer, and flag the specific point you're least sure about.
