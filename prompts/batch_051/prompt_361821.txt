Re-reading David Hume's Enquiry lately for a forum argument, and I've convinced myself of a version of the regularity story so strong I don't trust it. The passage is Section VII, Part I of An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding, where Hume is hunting for the impression behind our idea of necessary connexion. He writes: "When we look about us towards external objects, and consider the operation of causes, we are never able, in a single instance, to discover any power or necessary connexion; any quality, which binds the effect to the cause, and renders the one an infallible consequence of the other. We only find, that the one does actually, in fact, follow the other."

From that I reconstructed the following argument.

(1) All our ideas are copies of our impressions.
(2) But in any single instance of cause and effect we have no impression of any power or necessary connexion in the objects; we only find one event following another.
(3) So the idea of necessary connexion can't be copied from anything in the objects; its only available source is the custom or habit the mind acquires from repeated conjunctions, which carries us from the one event to the expectation of the other.
(4) So when we say, e.g., that the first billiard ball's striking caused the second's motion, all we can mean is: events of the one kind have been constantly conjoined with events of the other kind, and the mind is carried by custom from the first to the second.
(5) Hence there is no necessary connexion in the objects themselves: in the world, causation just is regular succession, full stop.

My freind says the New Hume people (Strawson et al.) would reject this, and that Hume here is only making a point about what we can discover or form an idea of, not about what exists. That sounds like exactly the sort of epistemic-to-metaphysical slide I've made a hundred times. So: is the argument valid? If not, tell me exactly which step fails, what the corrected version of that step is, and whether what's left is a regularity theory of causation itself or only a regularity theory of what we can say or think about causation. I don't need citations, I just want the flaw located.
