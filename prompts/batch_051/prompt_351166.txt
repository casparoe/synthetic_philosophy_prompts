I'm an undergraduate in a moral philosophy seminar on the golden rule, and I'm writing a term paper on whether the rule can be repaired without being quietly replaced by a different principle. We have worked through the standard problems with the positive formulation — treat others as you would want them to treat you — and my list so far is this: (1) the masochist problem, where someone who sincerely wants to be harmed would be licensed, or even directed, to harm others; (2) the conflicting-preferences problem, where the agent's own tastes set the standard of good treatment, so a person who would enjoy receiving anchovy pizzas is told to push anchovy pizzas on colleagues who detest them; (3) Kant's objection in the Groundwork, that the rule yields no duties of beneficence (a person can consistently will a world in which nobody helps anybody), no duties of strict obligation (a criminal could ask the judge why he should be punished when the judge, too, would want to escape punishment), and no duties to oneself; (4) the silence problem, where the rule says nothing when the agent has no relevant desire one way or the other; and (5) the idealization problem, where the usual repair — that you should act only as you would consent to be treated if you were in the other person's exact situation with their preferences — smuggles in a substantive account of relevant similarities, and that account, not the golden rule, ends up doing all the moral work.

I have a nagging sense that there is at least one more objection with a different structure. By different I mean not a restatement or a special case of anything above — the paternalism worry feels to me like a special case of (2), and the sadist worry like a mirror image of (1) — and not a combination of the five. Could you give me one further objection to the golden rule that is not on this list and not a variant of anything on it? Then tell me honestly whether it is any good: whether it damages the rule in a way these five do not, or whether one of the standard repairs — the negative formulation, the platinum rule, or an idealized consistency version — already absorbs it.
