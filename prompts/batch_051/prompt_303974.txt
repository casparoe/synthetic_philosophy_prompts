Two moral philosophers are undertaking an adversarial collaboration on repugnant markets — paid kidney donation, steep post-disaster price increases for essential goods, and the direct buying and selling of votes. The market-expansionist position holds that objections to such exchanges reduce to contingent defects (poverty, unequal bargaining power, coercion, misinformation, third-party harms) and that, once these are corrected, voluntary exchange is generally permissible and often beneficial. The commodification-critic position holds that some goods and relationships are corrupted, degraded, or emptied of their point when priced, so certain exchanges should be forbidden even when voluntary, informed, and set against an otherwise just background. Draft the joint statement they should issue: a numbered section of substantive claims both can sign, stated in wording neither finds slanted or vacuous; a precise statement of the residual disagreement, each position formulated as its own side would accept; and the cruxes — the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side — with any merely verbal parts of the dispute flagged. For each crux, give a concrete case, argument, or test that both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Pay particular attention to whether the objections to pricing kidneys, disaster supplies, and votes are fully reducible to coercion, exploitation, and inequality, or whether an independent corruption or degradation of value survives under otherwise acceptable conditions. Format the output as the agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
