In his essay Toward Perpetual Peace (1795), Immanuel Kant maintains that a federation of sovereign states—what he calls a foedus pacificum—should be distinguished from a mere peace treaty, because a peace treaty seeks to end one war while the federation seeks to end all wars forever. He also claims that this federation is not a world republic, and that states are unlikely to surrender their sovereignty to a single world state; the federation functions, he suggests, as something that stands in where the world republic cannot be fully realized.

Please make an argument for the following position: Kant’s preference for a voluntary federation over a coercive world state is not merely a concession to the practical limits of international politics, but follows from his conception of a state as a moral person whose autonomy a world state would violate. In your argument, do not treat this as simply a historical claim about Kant; evaluate the position on the merits, using Kant’s distinction as the starting point.

Structure your response in the following named sections, in this order, with the approximate lengths indicated:

1. Framework (roughly 150 words): State the thesis and explain what is entailed by treating a state as a moral person in Kant’s sense.

2. The case for the federation (roughly 300 words): Develop the strongest argument that a voluntary federation of republics, rather than a coercive world state, is the only institution consistent with the moral autonomy of states.

3. The case for the world state (roughly 250 words): Present the strongest opposing argument that a federative arrangement without coercive enforcement is too unstable to count as a genuine solution to the problem of war.

4. Kantian reply to the world state (roughly 250 words): Reconstruct how a Kantian could answer the objection that the federation lacks teeth, drawing on the idea that the peace is not a fantasy because it can be approximated by republican constitutions and commerce.

5. Your verdict (roughly 150 words): Conclude by explaining precisely what you take the strongest version of the position to establish, and where it remains vulnerable.
