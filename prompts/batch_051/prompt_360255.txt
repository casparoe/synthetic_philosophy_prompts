Lately I have been trying to pin down what I actually think about social sanctions—cancellation, deplatforming, firing, boycotting—by recording my verdicts on particular cases, and I am not certain they fit any one principle. Here is where I land. A stand-up comic who ridicules a disabled audience member during a set, and whose streaming special is subsequently pulled, seems to me to have been sanctioned appropriately. A professor whose twelve-year-old blog post using a slur in an academic discussion resurfaces, who apologizes and has an otherwise unblemished record, should not be fired, though asking him to explain himself publicly seems fair. A podcaster who repeatedly directs his audience to harass named private citizens, and is then banned by his platform, is banned rightly. A nurse who is fired after a private group-chat message among friends, in which she cruelly joked about a patient's appearance, is leaked to her employer: firing her seems excessive, though reprimanding her is not. A state legislator who is pressured to resign after slurs she posted as a fifteen-year-old come to light: that also seems excessive. A restaurant owner who refuses service to a local politician, and is boycotted until he relents: the boycott seems fine to me, but the refusal of service does not. Two pairs trouble me. The comic's live mockery and the nurse's private remark both ridicule a particular person's body; why does deplatforming the comic seem right while firing the nurse seems wrong? And the podcaster and the legislator both hold public positions built on speech; why does banning the one seem right while forcing the other out seems wrong? Is there a single principle—or a small set of candidate principles—that captures all of these verdicts, or close to it? If a simple principle would force me to abandon one or two of them, say which and how much that matters. Then apply your best candidate to a case I keep going back and forth on: a mid-level employee fired after an offensive meme he posted at nineteen surfaces, where he has since clearly repudiated the view and no pattern of similar behavior exists. Please answer in American English.
