Okay so in my well-being class we went over Nozick's experience machine, the one where you can plug in and have whatever good experiences you want, even though none of it is real and you won't know the difference. I've been stuck on a variation. Imagine a different machine, maybe call it the tranquility machine, that doesn't feed you any fake experiences at all. It just removes every unsatisfied desire and every bit of felt suffering. You sit in a plain room, fully aware that your actual life, friends and projects are gone, but you have no cravings left, no boredom, no grief about any of that; the only desire you still have is a weak preference to stay put. Would Nozick's usual objections to plugging in still land, or were those objections secretly aimed at the false experiences rather than at leaving real life behind? Which theories of well-being get pulled apart by this case? In particular, if someone holds the tranquilist view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters for well-being, are they forced to say you should plug in, or can they still object on grounds Nozick himself didn't have? And does the fact that the machine is honest with you make the case harder or easier for someone who thinks contact with reality matters?
