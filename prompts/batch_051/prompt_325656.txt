Act as a philosopher of language who works on the semantics–pragmatics interface, especially presupposition, dynamic semantics, and formal pragmatics. I am writing up a position on presupposition, accommodation, and conversational scorekeeping, and I want a consistency check on the working commitments I have assembled. I hold all of the following, with roughly equal initial confidence:

(a) Hard triggers encode semantic presuppositions. For a class of triggers, including definite descriptions and clefts, the presupposition is a semantic definedness condition: the sentence receives no truth value when the relevant local context fails to satisfy that condition, and this semantic typing explains why such presuppositions project robustly through negation, questions, modals, and conditional antecedents.

(b) Soft triggers are pragmatic rather than semantic. For another class, including aspectual verbs such as stop and additive particles such as too, the presupposition can be suspended without contradiction, as in an utterance of the form John didn't stop smoking; he never smoked. These triggers contribute a defeasible presumption that the relevant proposition is common ground, not a semantic definedness condition.

(c) The common-ground norm is prior. Felicitous assertion requires the presuppositions of the asserted sentence to be entailed by the common ground at the time of utterance. Common ground is not the speaker's private belief: it is what the participants treat as mutually accepted for the purposes of the conversation. Asserting a sentence whose presupposition is not yet common ground is a conversational defect.

(d) Accommodation is real and retroactive. When a presupposition is missing from the common ground, hearers can and do quietly add it after the utterance, and this is what makes informative presuppositions acceptable: first mentions of a previously unmentioned sibling, car, or spouse, introduced with a definite description. The repair is constrained, because the proposition added must be consistent with the existing common ground, not too controversial, and plausibly attributable to the speaker.

(e) Accommodation is a special case of scorekeeping. The conversational score has many dimensions: the presupposition set, the questions under discussion, standards of precision, domains of quantification, salient reference points, and so on. For each dimension, a rule shifts the score, within limits, to make the current conversational move count as correct; whether a move counts as correct is therefore determined by the score after the move rather than before it. Presupposition accommodation is just the scorekeeping rule for the presupposition set.

(f) The hard/soft boundary is itself context-sensitive. The same trigger can behave like a hard trigger in one conversational setting and like a soft trigger in another, and the data that motivate the hard class are partly artifacts of the examples linguists happen to discuss. The taxonomy in (a) and (b) captures a real tendency but not a stable lexical split.
