I got into a silly arugment with a friend who insists that numbers are just sets, and now I can't let go of a puzzle I don't have a word for. Here's the setup. You can build the natural numbers out of sets one way: 0 is the empty set, and each next number is the set of all the previous ones. Then 4 is the set {0,1,2,3}, so 2 is an element of 4. But you can also do it another, equally standard way: each next number is just the set containing the previous one. Then 4 is the set whose only member is 3, so 2 is not an element of 4. Both constructions give you all of arithmetic -- addition, multiplication, induction, everything -- but they disagree about whether 2 ∈ 4 is true.

The move people make is to say that numbers aren't any particular sets; they're just the positions in the structure that both constructions share. But then I'm not sure what has happened to the original question. Is '2 is an element of 4' false, true, or just badly formed? Does it have a definite answer at all, or does the 'positions in a structure' move dissolve the question rather than answer it? I half-suspect this puzzle has a name, and I'd like more than the label anyway: what exactly is the puzzle, and what would actually settle it?
