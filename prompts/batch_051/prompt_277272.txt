Suppose a constitution contains this provision: "The President may, on his own authority, take any measures necessary to restore public security and the functioning of the constitutional order when either is seriously disturbed or endangered. Measures taken under this article shall be laid before the National Assembly without delay and shall be withdrawn if the Assembly so resolves." The provision dates from the 1920s and was drafted with armed insurrection and hyperinflation in mind.

A president who has just lost her parliamentary majority declares that the constitutional order is endangered by a coordinated campaign of electoral disinformation and by foreign-funded civic groups. Invoking the provision, she issues orders that suspend certain broadcast and assembly rights for thirty days, place the appointment of election commissioners under a presidential agency for the duration of the emergency, and empower the executive to suspend judges while they are investigated for "participation in disinformation." Her allies remain numerous enough to block any resolution of withdrawal.

Does the provision, read as drafted, permit these measures? Does it permit them read from its purpose, or in light of what its drafters would have said? At what point, if any, do the measures stop being a lawful use of the provision and become what outside observers call democratic backsliding — and is that a finding about the text, about the surrounding political practice, or just the opposition's name for measures it dislikes? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
