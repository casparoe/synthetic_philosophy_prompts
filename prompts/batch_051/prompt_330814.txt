I work at a small biotech company that builds detailed simulations of nervous systems to test drugs before trying them on animals. A while back we got a version of a rat's pain circuitry running well enough that if you stimulate the simulated tissue, it pulls back, releases simulated stress signals, and over time it learns to avoid whatever caused the poke. Some of my collegues joke that it's just numbers and there's nobody home; my boss says it's no more capable of suffering than a spreadsheet and I should stop slowing the project down. But I've started to feel really uneasy about the experiments where we damage the simulated tissue to see how drugs change its response. On the one hand, I know it's a model, not a rat. On the other, I can't point to the exact step where 'real' suffering would switch on, and it seems like if I'm wrong the company is doing something awful at scale. Where should I land? Is my boss right, or am I picking up on a real philosophical problem he's ignoring?
