A friend and I spent last evening arguing about whether reasons can exist without any reasoner—the view sometimes called free-floating rationales. As I understand his position, when we say a bird has wings so that it can fly, or that a market adjusts prices as if it intended to clear supply and demand, the so that and as if are not just figures of speech: the rationale does real explanatory work even though no bird, trader, designer, or intelligence ever entertained it. The rationale is carried by a mindless process, such as natural selection or price adjustment, rather than by a mind. I work in statistical physics, and all evening I kept trying to restate this in my own vocabulary: gradient flows, free-energy minimization, attractors, dissipation, noise, coarse-graining. Please restate the free-floating-rationales position in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what did not survive the translation. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in the vocabulary at all? Where there is no counterpart, is that because my vocabulary is missing something real, or because his claim was less clear than it looked? Give exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
