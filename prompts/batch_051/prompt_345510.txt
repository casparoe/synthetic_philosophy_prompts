Two evolutionary biologists have spent a year arguing about the units of selection. One holds a gene-level view: genes are the fundamental units of selection, and organisms, groups, and other collectives are best understood as vehicles or survival machines — structures built by replicators that persist across generations. The other holds a multilevel view: selection can act at several levels of biological organization at once, and between-group selection is a real causal process that is sometimes required to explain why a trait spreads, not merely a shorthand for something else. They have agreed to stop writing rebuttals and instead produce an adversarial-collaboration document — a statement both could sign, with their remaining disagreements made exact. Write that document.

The document must contain the following parts.

1. A numbered list of substantive claims both parties accept as written. These should be the strongest, most informative claims possible, not safe truisms such as that evolution occurs. If a claim holds only under a particular sense of unit of selection, say which sense is intended.

2. A disagreements table giving the remaining points of contention in neutral wording, with each side's position stated so that the side holding it would accept the wording as fair. Where an apparent disagreement turns out to be verbal, say so explicitly and give the disambiguating formulation both parties can accept.

3. A list of cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that a definitive answer would cause at least one side to revise its view. Include at least one crux about whether group selection can be redescribed without remainder as inclusive-fitness or kin-selection effects, and at least one crux about the empirical strength and frequency of between-group selection relative to within-group selection in nature.

4. For each crux, at least one concrete case, measurement, or model system that both parties agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Work through at least one of the following explicitly: meiotic drive elements such as the t-haplotype, the evolution of eusocial insect colonies, or the evolutionary transition to multicellular organisms with suppressed within-organism cell-lineage competition.

5. Throughout, keep track of the distinct things someone might mean by unit of selection — replicator, vehicle, and beneficiary — and specify, wherever it matters, whether a claim concerns the bookkeeping of selection, for example how the Price equation partitions change, or the causal process responsible for a trait's evolution.

Do not adjudicate the dispute or declare either side correct. The mark of success is that each party could put its name to the entire document, including the residual disagreements.

Begin with the document itself, with no preface or commentary.
