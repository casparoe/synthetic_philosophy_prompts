I just finished my first jury service and I keep chewing over the verdict we returned. We convicted a guy on a count that required us to find he did the act deliberately, and acquitted him on another count that only made sense if the same act was an accident; the judge called it an inconsistent verdict and said courts let them stand all the time, partly because an acquittal isnt a claim of innocence, it just says the prosecution didnt meet its burden. But that leaves me thinking the system is comfortable asserting P and not-P about the very same event. What does letting inconsistent verdicts stand presuppose about logic and about what a verdict is? Is it quietly committed to something like dialetheism (some contradictions are true) or paraconsistent logic (a contradiction needn't make everything follow), or is the underlying view that verdicts aren't factual assertions at all? Reconstruct the implicit theory, separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely allows, and say what would have to change if courts demanded logically consistent verdicts.
