Last night a friend and I spent hours arguing about a coincidence that still bothers me. Earlier that day I had been thinking, for no obvious reason, about an old acquaintance I had not seen in over a decade and had not otherwise had occasion to think about. An hour later he walked into the small café where I was sitting, in a city neither of us lives in, and seemed as startled as I was. My friend insisted this was just the birthday paradox in disguise: with enough people, cafés, remembered acquaintances, and days, events of minuscule individual probability become almost certain to happen to someone, and the only reason I found it striking is that I notice the hits and forget the misses. What appeals to me about his position is that it offers a single tidy rule: when an anecdote seems significant, enlarge the reference class and correct the denominator rather than revising your model of the world. On his view the anecdote carries no evidential weight at all, and the feeling that such an event calls for a special explanation is just a cognitive illusion to be explained away.

I find that line genuinely compelling at the level of aggregate statistics, and I accept that our sense of coincidence is shaped by selection effects and a denominator chosen after the fact. But I cannot make myself treat this particular event as noise. The coincidence was not one I merely noticed after the fact; it matched a thought I had formed in advance, and the conjunction of details—the person, the day, the city, the café, the hour—feels too exact to be dissolved into whatever large reference class makes it unremarkable. So I find myself caught between a debunking theory with a clean decision procedure, on one side, and a firm particular intuition that this coincidence really was informative, on the other.

Give me the strongest version of each, and then a negotiated verdict. Should I debunk the intuition and drop it, amend the debunking theory so that it can handle coincidences that are specified in advance and genuinely probative, or reinterpret the two so that they do not actually conflict? What would justify each of those moves, and how would someone in my position tell which is the right one in a concrete case?
