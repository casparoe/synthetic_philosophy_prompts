I would like your actual credence, not a survey of positions, on a question about constrained artificial agents. Consider a system with the following profile. It is very good at normative judgment: across a wide range of cases it reliably distinguishes better from worse courses of action, gives reasons that survive challenge, weighs conflicting considerations, and revises when shown a defect. But it is also deliberately built as a pure advisor. Its only output is a private text channel to a single human overseer. It has no actuators, no access to any larger system, no way to create incentives or impose costs, and no way to ensure that its verdicts are carried out. The overseer is never required to follow its recommendations and regularly declines to do so; when a recommendation is declined, nothing further happens with it. The system forms and revises its judgments anyway.

Question: what is your credence that this system, if built, would be a moral agent — that is, an entity toward which reactive attitudes such as praise, blame, resentment, and gratitude are fitting on account of the normative judgments it forms? I am not asking whether it would be legally responsible, useful, or convenient to treat as an agent.

I want a number, and I want the derivation of that number. Decompose the credence into the sub-questions it depends on. At minimum, give a probability and one or two sentences of justification for each of the following:

(1) the probability that a capacity for reasons-responsive normative judgment is sufficient for full moral agency, with no separate executive capacity required;
(2) the probability that producing recommendations which a human may accept, modify, or ignore counts as acting on the judgments in the relevant sense, so that this system may not actually lack an action capacity;
(3) the probability that fitting praise or blame requires that the agent have had direct causal control over outcomes, or could have done otherwise, which this system lacks;
(4) the probability that the aptness of reactive attitudes instead requires standing in a shared practice of mutual accountability, in which the agent can make demands on others and have demands made on it in ways that carry practical weight, which this system's non-reciprocal relation to the overseer may fail to provide;

and any further terms that belong in your decomposition.

Then show how the terms combine into your final credence, doing the arithmetic explicitly rather than leaving it verbal; a weighted mixture or a conditional decomposition is acceptable. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in, and describe the evidence or argument that would most shift your final credence.
