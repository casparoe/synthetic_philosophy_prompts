I'm putting together the theoretical-orientation section of a philosophy of science dissertation proposal, and I want a consistency check before my committee sees it. I have settled on the following commitments; each seems individually plausible to me, and I want to know whether they can all be held at once.

(1) Inference to the best explanation is a legitimate and basic form of non-demonstrative inference: we are often warranted in believing a hypothesis because it best explains the evidence.

(2) The best explanation is the one that scores best on the usual explanatory virtues — simplicity, unifying power, scope, precision, and convergence of independent lines of evidence.

(3) These virtues are truth-conducive, at least in a modest sense: other things equal, a hypothesis with more of them is more likely to be true, and that is what makes explanatory reasoning rationally respectable rather than merely pragmatic.

(4) Scientific reasoning is, at bottom, Bayesian: an ideally rational agent has degrees of belief that obey the probability axioms and are updated by conditionalization.

(5) For any finite body of evidence, there are indefinitely many incompatible hypotheses that fit that evidence equally well.

(6) We are often justified in believing in unobservables such as electrons and ancestral populations, and explanatory inference is a central source of that justification.

(7) I take the bad-lot challenge seriously: the truth may not be among the hypotheses we have formulated, so even the best of our available explanations may be false. I also think the appropriate response to this is to keep expanding our candidate set rather than to abandon inference to the best explanation.

(8) I do not count conservatism — fit with existing background belief — among the truth-conducive explanatory virtues; it is at most a pragmatic tie-breaker.

Which of these commitments are actually in tension with one another, and which pairs only appear to be? Please order the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. For each genuine tension, identify which commitment or commitments would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be; then say which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent. Be concrete about the pairs of commitments rather than speaking about the set in general. Keep the response under 500 words.
