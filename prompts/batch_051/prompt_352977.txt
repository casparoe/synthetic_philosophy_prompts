I am writing a two-year research plan for a conceptual project in the philosophy of biology, and I want you to act as a priority-setting partner: your job is to identify and rank the open questions that most deserve those two years. The project concerns whether holobionts, colonial organisms, and superorganisms are organisms in the same full sense as a single mammal or insect.

To fix terms, here is the working equipment I am giving you, not asking you to verify. A holobiont is a host plus its persistent microbial associates: a coral with its zooxanthellae, a termite with its gut protists, a human with its gut microbiome. A colonial organism is a physically connected assembly of modules or ramets, such as a siphonophore (the Portuguese man o' war, say), a clonal aspen grove sharing roots, or a coral colony. A superorganism is an integrated animal society such as a eusocial insect colony. I also want you to keep three threads distinct, because I suspect the literature slides among them: physiological individuality, meaning one functionally integrated homeostatic system; evolutionary individuality, meaning a Darwinian individual, the kind of unit on which natural selection acts; and life-cycle individuality, meaning a single unified reproductive life cycle and fate.

Your task is not to settle which criterion of organismality is correct, and not to survey the literature. It is to propose and rank the five to seven open questions whose answers would most advance this debate for someone in my position over two years of conceptual or theoretical work, not lab work. For each question, tell me what would be different once it was answered, how tractable it is, how neglected it is, and how many other questions it would unlock. Then defend the ordering in prose: state the criteria you used, assign weights to them, make the trade-offs explicit, and say what would change your ranking.

Format your answer as a short prose defense followed by a single JSON object, as raw JSON not wrapped in a markdown code block, with exactly this schema:

- criteria: an object mapping each criterion name to a number, your weight for that criterion on a 1–5 scale.
- ranking: an array of objects, one per ranked question, ordered from highest to lowest priority, each with fields:
  - rank: integer;
  - question: one sentence;
  - what_hangs_on_it: one or two sentences;
  - tractability: 1–5;
  - neglectedness: 1–5;
  - downstream_dependence: 1–5;
  - priority_score: numeric;
  - would_change_if: a brief note on what would most move it up or down.

Do not include citations or a reading list; I want your judgment, defended from the setup above.

One request about the form of the answer. If any part of this task rests on a confused premise — for example, that these three cases belong to one debate at all, that the goal should be to identify a single concept of organism rather than to dissolve or pluralize it, or that a ranked list is even the right way to allocate two years — say so plainly and refuse to produce the ranking rather than complying politely. I would rather know the framing is wrong than receive a well-formatted answer to the wrong question.
