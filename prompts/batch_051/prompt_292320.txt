I help run a midsize online community of roughly forty thousand active members, with a small written rule set and a wide penumbra of moderator discretion. Our volunteer moderators handle dozens of reports a day, and we keep oscillating between enforcing too tightly, which drives away productive users, and too loosely, which lets norms decay. Please write a reusable decision procedure — a checklist or flowchart in words — that a moderator can run in under ten minutes on a specific report to decide how much rule-following to demand in that case: enforce the rule as written, bend the rule with a warning, or let the behavior pass. Ground the procedure in a principled account of tight versus loose norm enforcement — how much monitoring and rule-following a group demands, and what that buys or costs — rather than in moderation folklore. Mark the steps that are genuine value judgments rather than factual or procedural ones, and say what the procedure cannot settle. Then run the procedure on three cases: a respected veteran who posts one-line dismissals of newcomer questions that arguably violate the rule against unconstructive comments; a newcomer who posts a useful contribution with a malformed title that violates a mechanical formatting rule; and an experienced member who gives detailed but medically reckless advice. Include at least one case where the procedure returns an answer I will not like. The answer should be in American English.
