I am a novelist researching a book about a family-owned company, and I keep running into the maxim that a corporation is a person. In a discussion with another writer, the slogan was deployed to settle a narrower question: because a publishing conglomerate is legally a person, it can properly be said to have intentions, and can therefore bear some responsibility for the content of the books it releases. What are the distinct claims that slogan could be making, from the merely formal to the substantive and radical? For each reading, indicate whether it is defensible, which reading people who repeat the slogan most likely have in mind, and whether that reading would actually license the move my fellow writer made. I would like the readings laid out as a menu I can bring back to that discussion.
