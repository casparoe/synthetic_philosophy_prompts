I'm on the research ethics board at my university and we're deadlocked on a proposal from a visual neuroscience lab. They want to use a TMS setup plus a stabilized-retina display to let participants see forbidden colors — reddish-green, yellowish-blue — and then, if that works, to move to a more targeted stimulation protocol meant to produce a color experience nobody has a name for and maybe no human has ever had. The consent form can't describe the experience in advance, and the lab's answer is that the stimulation is non-invasive, short, and reversible, so the risk is minimal. Half the board says informed consent is impossible when you can't characterize the experience at all, and that "minimal risk" only has meaning against a background of known experiences. The other half says we let people consent to novel sensations all the time — new foods, unfamiliar music, VR — and a transient, non-invasive stimulus is no different. I need to circulate a decision memo before Thursday's meeting. Should we approve, reject, or approve with conditions? Draft the memo with a clear recommendation, and make sure it takes the strongest argument on the losing side seriously rather than just swatting it down.
