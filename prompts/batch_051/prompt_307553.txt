I am a retired geochemist, a few years into philosophy, and the so-called Kripkenstein problem about rule-following keeps pulling me back. In last month's reading group, the discussion kept returning to a single sentence from Wittgenstein, roughly rendered as the claim that no course of action is determined by a rule, since any course of action can be made to accord with the rule. Our facilitator used it to reject the idea that grasping a rule is grasping an interpretation: interpretations, he said, just add another item that itself has to be applied. I am not convinced the sentence is doing one unambiguous job. It could be a modest point about charitable reading always being possible, or a radical skeptical claim that there are no meaning facts at all. Before I go back to the group, I want a menu. Lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be asserting, ordered from the weakest to the strongest, and for each say whether it is defensible and whether it actually supports the facilitator's move against interpretations. Tell me which reading, if any, the anti-interpretation argument actually needs.
