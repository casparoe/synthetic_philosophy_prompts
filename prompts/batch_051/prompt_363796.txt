Before I read any more proposals about the special composition question, I want the standard of evaluation written down. Give me the desiderata for an adequate account of when some things compose a further thing: the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it must satisfy, with the rationale each carries. Present them as a list, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp, since nihilists, universalists, organicists, and moderate restricters load the list differently. The cases should at least include the statue and the lump, a cat that loses its tail and the associated problem of undetached parts, simples that become arranged tablewise through nothing but a change in their arrangement, boundaryless candidates such as clouds and mountains, and the problem of the many. Keep distinct at least composition, constitution, and identity, and keep the special composition question separate from the question of what a composed object is or how it persists. Then say which single desideratum does the most to exclude otherwise serious answers, and address whether some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable — in particular, whether restricted composition, sharp non-vague composition, and grounding in the intrinsic arrangement of the parts can all be had together, or whether one of them has to go. Keep the weight on the criteria and the reasoning for them, not on a scorecard of named views.
