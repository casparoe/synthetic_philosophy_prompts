I'm in a historiography seminar this semester and I keep running into the same move. Last week we were discussing how a standard textbook narrates a colonial uprising, and a classmate shrugged and said, "history is written by the victors." That was basically the end of the exchange: he clearly took it to mean we should distrust the textbook, and that trying to reconstruct the losing side's experience is mostly hopeless, and the instructor sort of half-nodded along. But the more I replay it, the more it seems like he was running together claims about whose records happen to survive, whose version of events gets taught afterwards, whether historians can ever be neutral, and whether there's even a fact of the matter about what happened. I want to push back next week without being the guy who just insists on nuance. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing? Go from boring platitudes to substantive, defensible points to versions that are probably false, and say for each what it would and wouldn't let you conclude. Then tell me which reading, if any, would actually license writing off the textbook account the way my classmate did, and which readings only sound like they do.
