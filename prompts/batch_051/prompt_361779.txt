Draft the joint statement that a committed Peircean and a committed Jamesian about the nature of truth could both sign at the close of a good-faith adversarial collaboration, before anyone declares a winner. Begin with a brief agreed statement of the pragmatist commitments that survived the collaboration and constrain both views—for instance, that truth is not a matter of picturing a reality wholly independent of inquiry, and that the meaning and warrant of a belief are tied to its consequences for action and further inquiry. Then give the tightest formulation each side will accept of its own position (the Peircean, on which truth is what inquiry would settle on in the long run when pursued far enough by all competent investigators; the Jamesian, on which a belief's truth consists in its proving satisfactory, all things considered and over the long run, to hold) and, in each side's own words, the formulation of the opposing view it rejects. Follow this with a numbered agreed-claims section and then a disagreements table. Each row of the table should be one genuine crux: the sub-question, why a settled answer to it would move at least one party, the case or argument both parties agree bears directly on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Mark any apparent disagreement that the collaboration concluded was only verbal. The document must be one both could actually sign, so neither side's view may be stated as a caricature; paraphrase rather than quote primary texts. Maximum 800 words.
