Treat the mid-century controversy over whether cigarette smoking causes lung cancer as an episode to be reconstructed through Pearl's causal hierarchy and the do-calculus. The history I take as common ground is this: Doll and Hill's observational studies in the 1950s found a strong, dose-responsive association between smoking and lung cancer; R. A. Fisher countered that the association could be generated by a latent common cause, a constitutional or genetic factor that both disposes a person to smoke and independently raises their susceptibility to lung cancer, so the observational data alone did not establish that smoking causes cancer in the sense relevant to public health; later evidence included animal experiments, the contrast between inhaled and non-inhaled smoke, and the 1964 United States Surgeon General's report, which judged that cigarette smoking causes lung cancer. Give me a disciplined mapping of the dispute onto Pearl's three-rung ladder of association, intervention, and counterfactual, and then run the lesson the other way. In particular: locate each body of evidence on the ladder and explain exactly why the observational epidemiology could not, by itself, climb from the association rung to the intervention rung; spell out the structural causal model under which Fisher's constitutional hypothesis is true, including the graph, the structural equations with error terms, and the role of the latent confounder, and use the backdoor criterion or the do-calculus to show why the causal effect of smoking on lung cancer is not identifiable from the observational data under that model; say what a genuine do-intervention on smoking would have required, why it was not performed or was performed only in forms such as animal experiments, and whether the inhaled-versus-non-inhaled contrast or any other actual evidence uniquely identifies P(lung cancer | do(smoking)) in Pearl's sense or only constrains it under further assumptions. Finally, ask whether the episode is a vindication of the hierarchy, an anomaly for it, or both: does Fisher's resistance live entirely at the level of rung-two identification, or does it also involve counterfactuals, external validity, and a dispute about what the word cause should mean—and where, if anywhere, does Pearl's framework distort the actual contours of the controversy?
