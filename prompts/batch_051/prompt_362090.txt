I've been going back and forth on something and would like a precise diagnosis rather than a general reaction. The case that set it off: a close friend has been severely depressed. She can state, flatly and accurately, that exercise would improve her health and probably her mood. She also says she doesn't care about improving either, and that no amount of her own clear-headed reflection on those facts brings her any nearer to wanting to do it. I want to say she still has a reason to exercise, but the following argument keeps forcing me toward the opposite conclusion, and I can't locate the weak point.

(1) A genuine reason for a particular person to do something must be able, at least in principle, to guide her: it has to be the sort of consideration that could enter her thinking and move her to act.
(2) A consideration can enter someone's thinking and move her only if it connects to what she already cares about, or to what she would come to care about by thinking clearly about the facts and about her own concerns.
(3) The benefits of exercise connect to nothing my friend actually cares about, and even clear-headed reflection on those benefits and on her current concerns leaves her unmoved toward exercise.
(4) So, by (1) and (2), the consideration that exercise would help her cannot guide her.
(5) Therefore it is not a reason for her.
(6) And nothing in her case hinged on its being depression specifically; what did the work was simply that she lacked the relevant concern. So the same reasoning applies to anyone who lacks a relevant concern. Therefore, there never are reasons that apply to a person independently of what she actually cares about.

I keep returning to (1) or (2) as the weak points, but I cannot deny either one without losing my grip on what a reason is supposed to be. Is the argument valid? If it is not, tell me the exact step that fails and the strongest correction of it that would make the whole thing valid. If it is valid, tell me which premise I should reject, and why that premise is less solid than it initially appears.
