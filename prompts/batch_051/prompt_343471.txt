I keep coming back to this argument about people who say we should be making deals now about what happens after someone builds superhuman AI. Like, agreements that the money gets shared, or that the first AGI won't be used as a weapon, or that whoever builds it promises to give it rights once it's self-aware. The conclusion I reach feels too strong, so I want someone to show me where I'm slipping.

(1) A deal made before AGI can only control what happens after AGI if, at the later time, the party in power either wants to keep the deal or can be made to keep it.
(2) Whoever first controls AGI will, once it's working, have more than enough power to ignore every other human institution and threat.
(3) So from the moment AGI arrives, nobody on the losing side of the deal can force the winner to keep their word.
(4) We also can't count on the winner wanting to keep the deal, because after an intelligence explosion their whole position and interests will be shaped by a situation we can't see from here.
(5) So a pre-AGI bargain only works when the winner would have done the nice thing anyway.
(6) Which makes the bargain itself redundant.
(7) So all these pre-AGI bargains are either useless or just theater.

I basically believe this, but it bothers me that smart-sounding people put alot of stock in locking down agreements before AGI. Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails and what's the strongest conclusion I can actually defend.

Also please write your answer in American English.
