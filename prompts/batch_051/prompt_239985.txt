The setup they both agreed on. Private Judy Benjamin is dropped somewhere in terrain split into three zones, and by symmetry her prior is equal over all three: Blue sector (B), Red Headquarters (H), and Red Second Company (S), so p = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3). A radio operator tells her: "If you're in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that you're in Headquarters rather than Second Company." They also agreed to treat the message as a constraint on Judy's posterior, p(H)/p(S) = 3, rather than as evidence about the operator's reliability or about where he would or wouldn't have bothered to transmit.

Rob opened by saying the update is forced. The message is conditional on being in Red, so it shouldn't move probability between Red and Blue. Keep p(B) at 1/3, split the old Red mass of 2/3 three-to-one, and you get H = 1/2, S = 1/6, B = 1/3. Anything else, he said, adds information Judy doesn't have about Blue.

Asha answered that this is smuggling in an extra constraint. Infinitely many posteriors satisfy p(H)/p(S) = 3. The principled choice is the posterior closest to the prior in KL divergence subject to that constraint, and solving that gives roughly H ≈ .478, S ≈ .159, B ≈ .363. Yes, Blue goes up. That's not a bug, she said; it's what minimal global distortion looks like when the evidence arrives as a constraint rather than as an event. Rob's answer, she said, is just KL minimization with an extra hard constraint — "p(B) stays frozen" — and he hasn't earned that extra constraint.

Rob said he shouldn't have to earn it. The message says something about Red, not about Blue. A rule that leaks probability from Red into Blue off a message that's entirely about Red is spooky action at a distance. KL minimization is a regularizer, not a law of thought; if Asha wants to move probability into Blue on the strength of a message about Red, she needs a Dutch book or a representation theorem forcing the leak, not an appeal to what an optimizer does.

Asha said fine, here's an argument in that spirit. There's a standard axiomatic story that any update rule for constraints satisfying a few natural consistency conditions — invariance under redrawing the partition, a system-independence condition, and composition when information arrives in pieces — is KL minimization. Rob's "leave Blue alone" rule is a one-off: give him two conditionals at once, say p(H)/p(S) = 3 and p(S)/p(B) = 2, and his method has no general answer, while KL just minimizes subject to both. A one-off intuition, she said, isn't a competitor to that.

Rob said that's moving the goalposts, because Judy got one message. The question was never "pick a general constraint engine"; it's whether his particular posterior is the rational one for this particular message, and the content of this message is exhausted by the Red odds. If a general engine makes a nonsense update on a simple input, that's a strike against the engine, not against the input.
