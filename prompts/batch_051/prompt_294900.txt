(2) I accept closure under known entailment for justification—if you're justified in believing p, and you know that p entails q, then you're justified in believing q. (3) I think perception gives you justification without needing independent evidence that perception is reliable. Looking at a red table justifies me in believing there's a red table right away, and I don't see anything wrong with then inferring that I'm not a brain in a vat being fed the table experience; I shouldn't have to rule the vat scenario out first. (4) But I have a strong intuition that bootstrapping is illegitimate. The usual case: a person has a gas gauge she has never checked, and over many occasions she reasons that the gauge read full and the tank was full, but her only basis for thinking the tank was full on each occasion is the gauge itself; at the end she concludes the gauge is reliable. That seems like epistemically worthless circular self-certification. (5) At the same time, I don't think track-record arguments are bad as such—calibrating a cheap thermometer against a thermocouple you already trust seems fine. So the problem with bootstrapping isn't induction from a track record per se. Does this package hang together?
