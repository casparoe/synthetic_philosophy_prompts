I’m a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I’m drafting a long-read essay about the mechanics of free-riding and the maintenance of public goods. Rereading David Hume’s *A Treatise of Human Nature*, I stopped at Book 3, Part 2, Section 7, “Of the origin of government,” where he writes:

“This is the reason why men so often act in contradiction to their known interest; and in particular why they prefer any trivial advantage, that is present, to the maintenance of order in society, which so much depends on the observance of justice. The consequences of every breach of equity seem to lie very remote, and are not able to counter-ballance any immediate advantage, that may be reap’d from it.”

That passage has planted a counterfactual question in my mind, and I’d like an argued prediction rather than a story. Imagine that, beginning in 2035, a cheap, widely adopted biofeedback implant becomes universally available and, within a decade, is worn by nearly everyone. The device does not punish; it merely takes the remote and diffuse consequences of a person’s small defections from a shared good—under-contributing to a vaccination campaign, over-drawing a common aquifer, evading taxes, dumping waste, quietly free-riding on promises—and renders them as subjectively vivid and near as if the user were experiencing those future harms now. Over the forty years after saturation, what happens to tax compliance, common-pool resource management, political participation, and ordinary norms of promise-keeping and public decency? Which effects do you expect to be robust, and where would you expect adaptive detachment, strategic gaming, or deliberate insensitivity to emerge? Be explicit about the links where your prediction could fail: what would have to be true about human motivation or institutional design for the device to change little even though it directly targets the asymmetry Hume describes? Finally, what feature of Hume’s explanation of collective failure could this counterfactual never settle, and what question would remain open no matter how the world played out?
