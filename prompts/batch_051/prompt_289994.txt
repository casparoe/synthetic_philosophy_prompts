I cover the history and philosophy of science and ideas, and for an essay on calendrical reform I want a worked example of the document a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration in this area would produce. Draft the joint statement that the two camps below could both sign after patient collaborative work, without declaring a winner: the deliverable should be a constructive text with the residual disagreement made exact.

The camps are the epoch neutralists and the continuity traditionalists. The neutralists hold that the current epoch, written BC/AD or BCE/CE, is a parochial inheritance, and that year zero should instead be tied to a deliberately non-sectarian reference point — for example, by adding ten thousand years so that the present year falls near 12,000 in the proposed Human Era scheme. The traditionalists grant the parochial origin but hold that it no longer functions as a live creed; they maintain that every epoch is arbitrary at bottom, that a would-be neutral epoch merely selects a different favored event, and that the real switching costs in computation, law, historiography, and mnemonic continuity outweigh the symbolic gain.

Write the document in their joint voice. Open with a numbered agreed-claims section, then give a disagreements table.

The agreed claims should get precise about what an epoch does and does not do: it fixes an origin in a coordinate system of dates and is not itself a claim reasserted with every use. They should separate two changes that are easy to run together: moving the epoch, and adopting an astronomical year zero, which simply inserts a year 0 between what is now 1 BC and AD 1 without moving the origin. They should also acknowledge that every epoch privileges some event or approximate date.

Then set out the exact residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, and identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show. Candidate cruxes to develop or discard: whether neutrality across traditions can be a stable property of a date or is only a relabeling; whether the value of continuity is intrinsic or purely instrumental; whether an epoch can be anchored in a rough historical threshold such as the beginning of the Holocene, the invention of writing, or the first cities without merely shifting the parochialism; and whether the already-existing astronomical year-zero convention shows that a neutral reform is feasible without a full re-epoching. If you conclude that any apparent disagreement is merely verbal, say which and fold it into the agreed claims.
