Suppose a country, persuaded by Simone Weil's argument that political parties are incipiently totalitarian because they substitute collective passion for individual judgment, amends its constitution to suppress them. No party may register, raise or spend money, endorse candidates, or field a slate. Elections use single-member districts and first-past-the-post; ballots carry candidates' names alone, with no party designation; no candidate may run as part of a coordinated group; and public campaign finance is paid only to individual candidates. Treat the ban as a mechanism whose behaviour is to be predicted, not as a proposal to be endorsed. How would political coordination actually reorganize around these rules, who would supply it, and where would the ban be gamed first? Which of Weil's underlying concerns -- conformity, collective passion, the capture of judgment -- would survive the reform, and where would the rules make these problems worse or merely relocate them?
