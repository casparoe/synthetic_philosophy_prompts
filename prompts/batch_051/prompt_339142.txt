I'm a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on Darwinian theories of culture, and for the final project I'm building a small web app that assigns readers a position in the debate over whether culture evolves by natural selection. The assignment is not an essay: I need an instrument.

Please write a sorting quiz along the lines of "What do you actually think about cultural evolution?" It should have nine questions. Each question should present a concrete, self-contained scenario requiring no specialized outside knowledge, with three answer options. Each answer option should commit the respondent to a claim about transmission fidelity, the source of variation, the level at which selection operates, or whether a Darwinian explanation is literal or merely analogical. No question should ask respondents which label they like or whether "culture is Darwinian" in the abstract.

The quiz must sort respondents into at least five leaves: (a) particulate memetics, committed to high-fidelity copying and gene-like cultural units; (b) dual-inheritance or population-thinking cultural evolution, which allows noisy transmission and biased, multi-level selection; (c) epidemiological or attractor theory, which says cultural items are reconstructed rather than replicated and converge on cognitive attractors; (d) deflationary or Lamarckian skepticism, which holds that the Darwinian vocabulary is at best metaphor because cultural variation is directed and acquired characteristics are inherited; and (e) a coherent attractor-friendly selectionist hybrid, if you think one is available. If you think the logical space contains an important unoccupied combination, name it and briefly say why nobody lives there: because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves that your questions cannot cleanly separate; do not pretend the quiz is sharper than it is.

At least three of the nine scenarios must be these, though you may adjust the names and details: (1) a song transmitted orally down four generations, whose chorus stays nearly word-for-word stable while the verses drift in every retelling; (2) a pottery-tempering technique that can be learned only through months of supervised gestures with local clay, so that every apprentice rebuilds the skill rather than copying a specifiable unit; (3) a costly and painful initiation rite that lowers an individual's expected number of children, yet the groups that require it consistently absorb or outlast groups that do not. The other six scenarios are yours, and together they should probe at least the following fault lines: whether novelty is blind mutation or canalized, directed reconstruction; whether the unit of selection is best drawn around discrete items or around practices-plus-contexts; whether convergent independent inventions, such as similar stories or taboos arising without contact, support an attractor account, a selectionist account, or neither; and whether the word "Darwinian" is doing explanatory work or is dispensable. Each question should include a one-sentence note on which fault line it probes and which leaves it separates.

Return the whole thing as a single JSON object with this schema:

- "title": string
- "one_sentence_pitch": string, describing the quiz to a respondent
- "questions": array of nine objects, each with "id" (e.g., "q1"), "scenario" (string, under 120 words), "options" (array of three objects, each with "label" ("a", "b", or "c"), "text" (string, under 60 words), and "commitment" (one sentence naming the claim the option commits the respondent to)), and "probe_notes" (one sentence)
- "leaves": array of objects with "leaf_name", "one_sentence_thumbnail", and "awkward_commitment" (the most uncomfortable consequence of the view, in one sentence)
- "scoring_key": object mapping each leaf_name to a scoring rule over question-option combinations (for example, a small list of the letters that count toward that leaf, with instructions for handling ties and mixed patterns)
- "unoccupied_combinations": array of objects with "combination" and "why_unoccupied"
- "inseparable_pairs": array of objects with "pair" (two leaf names) and "why_the_quiz_cannot_separate_them"

Do not include citations, a bibliography, or real published examples that would need verification. Use only invented scenarios, and where a scenario resembles a real case, say so without relying on any attributed fact.
