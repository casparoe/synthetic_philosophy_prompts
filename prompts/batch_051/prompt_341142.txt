In Portugal, the possession and use of all drugs for personal consumption has been decriminalized since 2001. Someone found with a small quantity is not arrested or given a criminal record, but is sent to a panel that can impose administrative measures and is oriented toward referral to treatment, while trafficking and large-scale supply remain criminal offenses. The policy is officially framed as treating problematic drug use as a health matter rather than a criminal or moral one. Reconstruct the philosophical commitments of this arrangement. What view of addiction, agency, responsibility, and harm does it presuppose? In particular, does the legal line drawn between the user and the trafficker presuppose a workable distinction between compulsion and ordinary choice, and is that distinction coherent? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with—for example, whether a paternalistic justification and a harm-reduction justification would both license the practice yet diverge elsewhere. Finally, what would have to change in the policy if its implicit view of addiction were replaced by the view that drug use is an ordinary exercise of free choice and users are simply choosing badly?
