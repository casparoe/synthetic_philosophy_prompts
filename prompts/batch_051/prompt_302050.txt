I keep coming back to a parallel between the statistical notion of identifiability and the old philosophical problem of underdetermination, and I cant decide whether it's a real structural identity or just the same word doing two jobs. In stats/econometrics, a parameter isn't identified when two different values of it generate exactly the same probability distribution over every possible observation, so no amount of data can pick between them; at best you recover the set of values the data doesn't rule out, and that set only shrinks through extra assumptions. In philosophy of science, underdetermination arguments say that any body of evidence is compatible with several empirically equivalent theories that posit quite different unobservable ontologies, and structural realists answer that only the structure common to all of them is knowable, while the nature of the relata stays inaccessible. That sounds to me a lot like the claim that only the invariant functions of a parameter vector are identified and the rest is uninterpretable metaphysics.
