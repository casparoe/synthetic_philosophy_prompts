I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether the felt agency of a narrative game depends on the player not seeing the machinery too clearly, and I want to stress-test the intuition with a concrete counterfactual. Suppose that starting in 2040, every commercially released narrative game on a major platform must ship with a built-in agency audit layer: a player-facing view of the full branching structure of the game—every choice node, whether a choice is cosmetic or state-changing, the conditions that unlock or lock each branch, and live aggregate telemetry showing what share of players reached each node. The layer also allows rewinding to any earlier node and replaying forward, with the game marking when a replay converges onto a branch the player has already seen. Nothing else changes: the underlying games are exactly the ones that would have been made anyway.

Give me a reasoned prediction of how game design and game criticism look after fifteen years of this. I want you to commit, in particular, on the following: which design patterns get selected for and against, such as the illusion-of-choice dialogue wheel, true branching with a few real nodes, and procedural generation that resists clean audit; how players' experience of agency changes, and whether the aesthetics of agency relocates from the moment of choosing to the player's knowledge of the tree; whether criticism starts reviewing the branch structure rather than the playthrough, and whether repeat playthroughs come to be judged as a formal property of the tree; and whether the art form splits into games that treat the audit layer as part of the work and games that actively resist being optimized against it.

I am not looking for a menu of scenarios. Take definite positions on each question, give the mechanism or incentive behind each prediction, and be explicit about confidence: identify the one or two effects you would bet on most strongly, the ones that are genuine guesswork, and the empirical assumption whose failure would most change your overall verdict.
