I sit on a national bioethics advisory body that reviews surrogacy and egg-donation arrangements for authorization or recognition. Our governing legislation prohibits commercial surrogacy but gives us case-by-case authority over non-commercial arrangements, and we treat our past decisions as precedents. Our relevant decisions are these. We authorized a surrogacy in which the intended mother's sister carried the child and the parents paid only documented medical, legal, and counseling costs; no payment went to the carrier and no agency was involved. We declined to recognize a California arrangement in which a for-profit agency paid an unrelated surrogate $30,000 above expenses, on the ground that the fee and the agency's role together treated gestation as a purchasable service. We approved an egg-donation arrangement in which the donor, an old friend of the recipient, received €1,200 for documented travel and lost earnings only, with receipts and a cap. We rejected an application from an unrelated undergraduate who would have received a flat €8,000 allowance for pain and inconvenience without documentation, holding that the payment was an inducement rather than restitution. And we authorized a Canadian surrogacy in which an old friend carried the child and received documented lost wages of $6,000 plus expenses, with no intermediary between the parties.

We must now decide whether to authorize a proposed arrangement in which the intended mother's sister will carry the child. The parents will pay all documented medical, legal, and counseling costs, and they also propose to give the sister a single unconditional €7,000 gift after the birth in recognition of the burdens of pregnancy. There is no agency or intermediary, and the sister is on paid leave from her own job, so there are no lost earnings to document. If we want to be consistent with our own precedents, how should we rule on the arrangement as proposed? Which of our earlier decisions is the closest analog, and if our precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, what principle would we be adopting under each possible ruling?
