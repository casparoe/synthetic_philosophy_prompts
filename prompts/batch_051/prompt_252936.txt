Write a deadpan parody of a customer-facing FAQ, roughly 700 words, for a fictional data-analytics start-up called TrueU. The voice should be the bright, jargon-saturated register of a Silicon Valley trust-and-transparency page. The satirical target is the practice of treating revealed preference — the economist's device for representing choice behaviour — as if it were a direct readout of welfare, and of quietly converting an ordinal ranking into a cardinal score of happiness. The comedy should come from getting the economics right rather than from exaggerating the genre alone. The company should, straight-faced: infer that a customer 'prefers' delayed flights, cramped seats, or dental pain because they chose the cheaper or easier option; publish a single utility score recovered from binary choices; offer to compare one customer's score with another's as though interpersonal comparisons were meaningful; treat a default as if it were an enthusiastic choice; and declare that whichever option you clicked last reveals your deepest preference. If the company ever says something technically true about choice, it should draw exactly the wrong conclusion from it. The result should be legible to someone who has never heard of revealed preference, and should make an economist laugh rather than wince. Begin with the piece itself, with no preface or commentary. Write in British English.
