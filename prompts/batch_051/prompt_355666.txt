I was recently passed over for a promotion that went to a colleague who started the same year I did. The promotion process was formally open, but in practice it rewarded people who were skilled at making their work visible to the right committee members, and he had social ties to several of them. He did not rig the process and probably could not have refused the promotion, yet I find myself resenting him personally, not just the system. By any ordinary measure of contribution I believe I was the stronger candidate, and the work I do is the less glamorous work the team actually depends on. One part of me says this is just envy, and that envy is a vice: his good luck does not give me a claim against him, and dwelling on it only corrupts my judgment. Another part of me says it would be self-deceiving to dismiss the feeling that quickly, because the organisation is arranged so that people on the losing side will predictably feel exactly this, and the resentment may be the most honest signal I have that the arrangement is unjust. I want a verdict on this, not generic advice to let it go: under what conditions, if any, is envy or personal resentment of this kind an ethically appropriate response to injustice rather than a moral failing? Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this topic well, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
