Write a short dialogue, with a genuine back-and-forth rather than alternating speeches, between two contract theorists who disagree about whether clicking "I agree" on a standard online terms-of-service box is really an act of agreement. One defends a manifest-assent view: agreement is not a private mental event but a public move, and a click, like a signature, communicates acceptance of the terms even when they go unread; concerns about buried clauses and take-it-or-leave-it power are, on this view, reasons to regulate or refuse enforcement, not reasons to deny that agreement occurred. The other defends an informed-consent view: for an act to count as genuine agreement, the agent must understand what she is undertaking and have a meaningful alternative, and in the typical clickwrap setting neither condition is met, so the click is a legal ritual that merely resembles consent. Each character should answer the other's strongest objection rather than talk past it—the manifest-assent theorist should explain why a signature binds someone who has not read the document without pretending the agent actually comprehended it, and the informed-consent theorist should explain why a similar principle does not void boarding a bus or signing after merely skimming. By the end, let them make precise where their disagreement now stands.
