I spent my career in the natural sciences, where the discipline I was trained in was to fix the list of phenomena a model had to reproduce before arguing about which model was best; one did not first adopt a model and then discover what it could explain. I have recently been reading about the principle of indifference and related attempts to assign probabilities in conditions of ignorance, and I find I want exactly that kind of specification before I read any further. I am not yet committed to any particular rule; I want the checklist that any adequate rule would have to satisfy.

Please write the desiderata for an ignorance prior — a rule for assigning degrees of belief, or for setting default priors, when the evidence is exhausted. For each requirement on the list, give the case or argument that makes it a requirement. The list should at minimum address: symmetry cases in which a rule ought to return equal credences; the demand that the assignment not change when the same underlying state of affairs is redescribed or reparameterized, as in the case of a cube specified by its side length, face area, or volume, where a flat prior over each parameter yields different probabilities for what looks like the same physical set-up; the classical chord problem, in which the probability that a chord of a circle drawn at random is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle differs according to whether one randomizes the endpoints on the circumference, the midpoint within the disk, or the direction and distance from the centre; agreement with learning from new evidence; and any requirement of uniqueness of the resulting distribution or eventual agreement with observed frequencies. For each item, say whether it is a non-negotiable constraint on any coherent rule, a preference particular to one school, or a desideratum that may have to be abandoned under pressure.

Then make the tensions explicit. Which of these requirements, if any, cannot be jointly satisfied, so that choosing an ignorance prior is unavoidably a choice about which criteria to sacrifice rather than a matter of finding the rule that keeps them all? To fix the purpose for the list: I am interested in this as the default I would use to set priors before data arrive in an empirical modeling problem, not as a theory of everyday human judgment. Write the criteria and the conflicts among them sharply; I do not need a bibliography or a survey, only the reasons behind each demand and where the demands collide.
