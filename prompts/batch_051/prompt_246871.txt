Something's been bugging me, and I want someone to pick it apart. Last spring my sister, who teaches at a badly underfunded public high school, had a student who worked closing shifts at a fried chicken place, shared a bed with two younger siblings, and still got into a state university on a scholarship. At the awards assembly the principal gave a whole speech about her grit and resilience, and honestly I teared up. A few days later a friend sent me the memo her hospital chain sent out. Staffing is dangerously thin, nurses regularly work doubles, and management's big response was a mandatory "resilience training" module and a poster campaign about "the power of a positive mindset." Reading it made me genuinely angry. But then I noticed I was praising one and condemning the other for what looks, on paper, like the same trait: keeping going through conditions that shouldn't exist. My sister made it worse by saying the student's scholarship essay—where she wrote that the hardship had been a gift because it built her character—bothered her even more than the hospital memo did.

So I'm trying to figure out whether theres a real principle seperating my reactions, or whether I'm just being moody. Suppose a school deliberately underfunds its arts program because "struggle produces great artists," and then celebrates the kids who manage anyway: is that more like the hospital or more like the student? What about a parent who refuses to help with homework because it builds character, or praising an athlete for training through an injury everyone knew she shouldn't have run on? If there's no clean line, should I stop admiring the student, or stop being angry at the hospital—or is that a false choice? I keep going in circles.
