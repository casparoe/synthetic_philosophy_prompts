Most explanations of the heterogeneity problem for hedonism basically stop at "pleasures feel different from each other, so what do they have in common?". I want something more in the weeds then that. Treat the problem as a challenge to hedonism's need for pleasure to be one ethically load-bearing kind, not just a challenge to introspection. Walk me through it in exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro, no conclusion: (i) spell out the difference between felt-quality hedonism and attitudinal hedonism; (ii) explain why the problem is worst for the felt-quality view—like, what single felt quality do the relief of finally drinking water when you're parched, the pleasure of noticing a pun land, and a little schadenfreude actually share?; (iii) explain why attitudinal hedonism seems to dissolve the problem by making pleasure a favorable-attitude kind; (iv) explain why an attitudinal hedonist isn't obviously of the hook, since if "pleasure" just means "whatever you have a positive attitude toward," the unity is coming from the label and not from anything about the states themselves; (v) explain the bullet-biting reply that heterogeneity doesn't matter because well-being can just be the sum of liked experiences, and say whether that reply is stable or just relocates the problem.
