I've been going in circles over a problem in the metaphysics of time, and I'd rather you force both sides to their strongest form than recite the standard summary. Act as a professional philosopher of physics who works on relativity and time; think carefully, and start by brainstorming the possible moves before you commit.

On one side I find the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe really compeling. As I understand it, the move is this. Special relativity denies any absolute 'now': what counts as simultaneous with me depends on my state of motion. Take O, me-here-now on Earth, and take E, an event in my own causal future — say, my deciding tomorrow at noon whether to quit my job. There will be some suitably distant event X (out in the Andromeda galaxy, for the sake of concreteness) that is simultaneous with O in my rest frame, while in the frame of a suitably moving observer X is simultaneous with E. If whatever is simultaneous with a real event is itself real, and if 'real at the same time' is transitive and frame-invariant, then E is already real now. So tomorrow's decision is just as actual as this sentence, and the universe is a four-dimensional block. The pull of the argument, for me, is that it takes the geometry of relativity at face value instead of smuggling in a privileged cosmic 'now' that the physics never gives us.

On the other side I cannot shake the conviction that the future is genuinely open. Tomorrow's decision is not already out there; right now it is merely possible, not actual, and the difference between those two is doing real work. My deliberation seems to presuppose exactly that. If the block is already there, deliberation looks like a sham — I wouldn't be settling something, I'd be discovering a fixed fact about my worldline. And this isn't just a bit of poetry: the open future is baked into how I make sense of hoping, regretting, being on the hook for choices, and thinking some possibilities are still live.

So which one should yield? I'm not asking you to just tell me which side wins; I want the strongest version of both and then a negotiated verdict about what the most defensible move is and why. In particular: what would it take for the open-future intuition to be debunked as a by-product of how a temporally embedded creature experiences its own worldline, and would that debunking be enough? What would it take for the Rietdijk–Putnam argument to be the thing that goes — for instance, by denying that 'real at the same time' is transitive, or by accepting a preferred foliation that special relativity doesn't single out — and what would that move cost? Or is there a stable reading on which the block and genuine openness are compatible, e.g. the block captures tenseless facts while 'openness' is a feature of my epistemic position rather than of the world itself? Be explicit about which parts of your verdict are robust and which are guesses.
