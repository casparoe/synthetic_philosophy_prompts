A friend of mine is trying to design a small, explicitly secular "congregation" backwards from the cognitive science of religion: the goal is to keep the community, ritual, and seriousness of a religious group without any supernatural claims. The current design is: (1) members circulate a few deliberately "minimally counterintuitive" stories (a founder who can reliably remember one hour into the future, a house that forms opinions about its occupants), but nobody is required to say they're literally true; (2) joining is costly—a month of volunteering plus a public disclosure of something you're ashamed of—and there are recurring small sacrifices like fasting on Tuesdays and wearing a visible marker; (3) leaders are chosen mainly by how much they've visibly sacrificed, not by charisma or doctrinal skill. Walk through how this thing would actually behave: who joins and who washes out, what norms and hierarchies emerge, where and by whom it gets gamed, what substitutes for the monitoring-and-punishment role that supernatural agents normally play, and whether the lack of literal belief eventually hollows it out or turns out not to matter. Also propose the smallest design change that fixes the worst failure mode without turning it into a normal church or a normal social club.
