A parish study guide on the historical-critical method and divine revelation gives this summary: The historical critic reads Scripture as a collection of ancient human documents and attempts to recover what each human author intended for the original audience, using literary forms, philology, and archaeological context. The believer then adds a distinct theological interpretation, in which the same text is read as God's word for the Church. The guide claims this division of labor explains why historical evidence of factual tension or legendary development within the biblical text poses no threat to faith: doctrine is built not on the human historical claims but only on the timeless spiritual sense.

Is this summary fair to the view that Scripture can be read both as history and as revelation? Please separate what is accurate from what is overstated or subtly distorted, and say where a historically informed theologian would push back.
