I'm a philosophy of physics PhD student and this came up after a seminar, and I've been going back and forth on it. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument, as I understand it, works like this: special relativity makes simultaneity frame-relative, so take any event in my future — say, my decision next Thursday — and there will be some observer, moving relative to me, whose present includes that decision. So from their frame, next Thursday's decision is happening right now, not in the future. Since no frame is priviledged, and all of them are describing the same reality, the event is just as real now as the stuff I'm directly experiencing. From that it seems a short step to: my future is already real, all my decisions are already located in the block, and my sense that they're open is an illusion. Doesn't special relativity therefore just entail determinism, or at least kill libertarian free will outright? If this is too quick, tell me which exact step is doing too much work, because I can't see it.
