I've been gnawing on an analogy and I can't tell if it's a real structural identity or just me pattern-matching. The old knock on sign languages was that they aren't real languages because there's no sound; what undid it was showing that ASL (and other sign languages) have sub-lexical units — handshape, location, movement, orientation — that mean nothing by themselves but combine to distinguish meanings. In other words, they do exactly the job phonemes do, with no acoustic channel at all. So a phoneme has to be a functional/contrastive role inside a system, not an acoustic object. And that struck me as the same maneuver as functionalism in philosophy of mind: a mental state is its causal role, not the stuff implementing it, which is why it's multiply realizable. Are these actually the same argument at different scales, or does the analogy break somewhere? My worry about the break is this: phonemes never had semantic content or phenomenal character, they're defined entirely by oposition within a system, while beliefs are supposed to be about things and pains are supposed to feel like something. If it does hold, does 'carbon-chauvinism' about minds collapse the same way 'oral-chauvinism' about language did? And one more diagnostic: what would the analogy lead me to predict — say, about a silicon system with the right functional organization literally having beliefs — that might turn out false?
