Ok so I've been chewing on this for a while and I want someone to tell me if I actually have an idea or if it's just a math metaphor that sounds deep. I call it the derivative theory of pleasure. The core claim: pleasure isnt a kind of feeling at all, it's the derivative of your overall state—by 'overall state' I mean how good things feel as a whole at that moment. What all pleasures have in common is not a felt quality (the itch being scratched feels nothing like finally understanding a proof, and the pleasure of relief feels different from both) but that your state is moving upward while it happens. Pain is the derivative going down.

I think this fixes the problem my teacher mentioned, that pleasures seem too different to share any one feel. The common thing is the direction of change, not the content. It also explains why a hot shower is amazing when your freezing and boring when you're already warm: same final state, different derivative. And it explains why you can't just lock in a high and stay there—once your state stops improving, the pleasure flatlines no matter how nice the state is. That part makes me think it's more than just a metaphor.

But I also know 'overall state' is doing a lot of work, and maybe I just moved the mystery into that phrase. Is this a real position someone has defended under some name? Where does it actually break—like what are the strongest counterexamples that aren't just 'but sometimes I like sitting still'?
