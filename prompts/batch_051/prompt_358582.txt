I am pulled strongly toward the view (call it the pattern view, in roughly Hofstadter's sense) that a self is not a thing over and above the physical system that implements it, but a pattern: a self-referential loop of symbol-triggering, self-modeling, and memory that stabilizes itself over time. Patterns of this kind come in degrees and can be multiply instantiated. The loop that constitutes a particular person can run, more faintly, in a brain that has deeply internalized that person's ways of perceiving, weighing, and responding, which is why people can be said to live on in the judgments and reflexes of those who loved them. I find the argument for this compelling: it avoids brute souls, explains the gradual fading of a person after death without needing a sharp cutoff, and handles the way different memories of a person seem to preserve different aspects of them.

And yet I have an intuition I cannot dissolve. Suppose a friend dies, and over decades I have come to carry an unusually fine-grained copy of their characteristic moves: when I face a difficult choice, I can run their voice with eerie fidelity and know what they would press me toward. On the pattern view, this is straightforwardly a continued existence of part of their self, distributed into me and perhaps into others. But in the first person, this seems to have nothing to do with them actually being alive. Their death still strikes me as total, and my own death feels unmitigated by the thought that such copies of me will survive in others. The existence I care about seems to be a single, private point of view, and a point of view is not the sort of thing that can be split, degraded, or continued at forty percent.

I want to know which should yield and what each concession would cost. Is the first-person intuition best explained away as an artifact of how imagination models death and personal boundaries, in which case the pattern view can be accepted and the intuition dropped? Or is the intuition doing metaphysical work, tracking a real difference between running a pattern and being the subject of that pattern, such that the theory needs amending? Or is there a stable reading on which both are right — one about third-person facts, the other about what survival means from the inside — and is that a genuine reconciliation or just a change of subject? Give me the strongest form of each option, and say which you would take and how we would tell whether the intuition or the theory was the one doing the misleading.
