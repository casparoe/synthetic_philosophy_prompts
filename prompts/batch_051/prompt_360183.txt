Write a dialogue between a closure theorist and a Nozickian sensitivity theorist, set at the coffee station during an epistemology workshop. The closure person opens by pressing the abominable conjunction charge: doesnt the tracking view force you to say you know it's a zebra in the pen but you dont know it's not a cleverly painted mule? The Nozickian should own it and explain, in terms of the two subjunctive conditions (what you'd believe if the proposition were false, and what you'd believe if it were true), why the zebra belief tracks while the not-a-painted-mule belief doesnt — the closest not-zebra worlds are antelope or empty pen worlds, not painted mule worlds. Then the closure person should shift to the Grandma case: if Grandma were sick the family would put on a cheerful show, so sensitivity says you dont know she's well even though you just saw her looking healthy. Make the Nozickian respond with a method-relative refinement of the view, and make the closure person immediately attack how methods are individuated — why are seeing Grandma in the room and seeing a disguised Grandma two different methods rather than a gerrymander to save the theory? Give the Nozickian one more sharp move of her own, aimed at the closure theorist: if knowing it's not a painted mule comes free from knowing it's a zebra, then what did the deduction actually earn you, and why should knowledge that costs nothing rule anything out? Keep both characters equally competent, have each reply to the specific point just made, and end the dialogue without either conceding. Under about 1200 words.
