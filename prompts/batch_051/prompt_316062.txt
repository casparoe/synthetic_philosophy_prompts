For a paper I'm writing on truth-value gaps, I've been reading Aristotle's On Interpretation, especially Chapter 9, where he considers whether a proposition about a future sea battle is already true or false before the battle happens. As I understand his position there, he seems to say that for statements about the past and present, one of a pair of contradictories must be true and the other false, but for future particulars this is not necessary. That passage has always struck me as pointing to something more than just epistemic uncertainty, but I can't get the contemporary literature on bivalence to line up with it. So I've been developing a distinction I want checked.

I'm calling it the difference between pending and absent truth-values. A statement has an absent truth-value when it is not the kind of thing that could be true or false at a given time at all—maybe because it presupposes something false, like the statement the present king of France is bald when there is no present king of France. A statement has a pending truth-value when its truth or falsity is not yet settled but is, so to speak, on its way: the sea-battle proposition isn't just neither true nor false, it is awaiting a truth-value. My central claim is that Aristotle's future contingents are pending, not absent, and that recognizing this dissolves some of the standard puzzles about bivalence—the question isn't whether bivalence fails, but what kind of failure it is.

Here are the examples that guide me: the statement that it will rain tomorrow is pending today and becomes true or false tomorrow; the statement about the present king of France is absent right now because there is no such king. But a tricky case is this: if the sea-battle proposition is pending today and there is a battle tomorrow, does today's statement retroactively become true, or does a new statement—that there is a sea battle now—become true while the old one remains gap-like forever? That retroactivity puzzle is where I suspect my distinction starts to break, because pending truth-values seem to require something like retroactive truthmaking, and I'm not sure I can make sense of that. Is pending versus absent a real distinction or just a metaphor? Has someone already worked this out under another name? And where exactly does it break—what does it wrongly count as a truth-value gap, or what case does it misclassify?
