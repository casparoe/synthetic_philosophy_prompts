I help convene a final-year philosophy of mathematics seminar, and we've spent the term on the real numbers and the continuum. I need to set one 90-minute essay question, and I don't want a prompt that students can knock over by reciting Dedekind cuts and Cauchy sequences. It should push them to commit either way on whether the completeness of the reals is discovered or imposed by definition, and to sit with the dilemma that follows: if it's imposed, why do the reals feel like they were already there; if it's discovered, what is 'the continuum' that our constructions are tracking, given that the standard ones all end up isomorphic anyway? The question should also make them say what the least-upper-bound axiom genuinely contributes once you've built the rationals. Please also write a model answer around 600 words and a marking scheme out of 20 that specifies which moves separate a first from a 2:1 (for instance, treating categoricity as different from uniqueness, and not letting 'we have several presentations of one structure' settle the ontology on its own).
