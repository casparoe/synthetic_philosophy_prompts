Write a dialogue between a substantivalist and a relationist about a specific argument in Newton's Principia, in the Scholium to the Definitions. Newton first considers a bucket of water rotating in otherwise empty space, observing that the water's surface becomes concave; he then imagines two globes joined by a cord, revolving about their common centre of gravity in a vast void. He says the tension in the cord reveals the true rotational motion of the globes, because the globes would strive to recede from the axis of rotation even if no other bodies existed. The substantivalist regards this as evidence that space itself is a real entity with causal powers, distinct from all matter. The relationist responds that motion is only intelligible as change of relative position, so any talk of rotation in a perfect void is a disguised appeal to the distant fixed stars or to some preferred frame, and the void case is not physically meaningful. In their dialogue, each should engage the other's strongest formulations: the substantivalist must answer the claim that unobservable absolute motion is empirically idle once cosmology is written in relational terms, and the relationist must answer the claim that the tension in the cord is an observable difference that the relational account can only explain by covertly reintroducing absolute space. Do not let either side be a strawman; both should be equally competent, and each should make at least one concession while maintaining the disagreement.
