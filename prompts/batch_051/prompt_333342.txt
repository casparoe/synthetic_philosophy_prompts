I've been treating a composer friend as a small induction problem: Anika has a stable but unarticulated rule about what counts as a performance of a musical work, and I've been collecting her verdicts as data points in the hope of reverse-engineering it. Her track record so far runs like this. She says a live orchestra playing Beethoven's Fifth from the score is a performance of the Fifth; that playing a CD of that concert at home is not a further performance of the work but the reproduction of one; that a wedding cover band reproducing a pop song note-for-note, including the guitar solo as it appears on the record, is performing that song; that a jazz trio improvising new harmonies, bass, and drums around the melody of a standard is still performing that standard; that a composer who independently writes a piece that happens to be note-identical to an obscure older work, with no knowledge of the older work, has not thereby performed the older work at the premiere; and that a club DJ who merely presses play on a finished track and fades it in and out is not performing the track.

The case I want to predict concerns a fixed-media electronic composition, where the piece just is an eight-channel audio file and there is no score and no live instrumental part. The composer presents it by diffusing the file in a concert hall: she sits at a mixing desk and routes, spatializes, and balances the material across a large array of speakers in real time, making continuous interpretive decisions about placement and dynamics, so that no two presentations sound alike in the room even though the underlying file never changes. Some of her circle call this a performance of the piece; others insist it is at most an elaborated playback of a recording.

Please take on the role of someone who works at the intersection of philosophy of music and the ontology of works, and think carefully through the candidate rules before you commit. In one piece of continuous prose, spell out the two or three general rules about what makes an event a performance of a work that best fit Anika's existing verdicts, state what each of those rules predicts about the diffusion case, and then commit: tell me which rule you think she is actually running, what verdict she would give, and why, and name one further case that would cleanly separate that rule from its competitors, including what you would expect her to say about it. I want a committed answer that defends a definite prediction rather than a survey of the options, and please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
