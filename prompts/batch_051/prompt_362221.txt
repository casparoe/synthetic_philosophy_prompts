I'm a software engineer at a quantum computing company, and I've ended up owning the user-facing documentation for our state simulator. Two of our physicists keep disagreeing about something that is now blocking the docs. One says the wavefunction is a real, dynamical feature of the system itself, which evolves continuously and collapses when measured. The other says the wavefunction is not out in the world at all; it's a compact encoding of what we know about the system, and "measurement" just updates that knowledge. This has spilled into product decisions: whether the simulator should say it "evolves the quantum state" or "updates our knowledge of it," whether noise should be described as physically perturbing the state or as degrading our estimate of it, and whether we should keep the user's uncertainty separate from the system's state. I need to write an internal memo that settles how we talk and what we build. Is this a substantive disagreement that could affect anything our simulator predicts or measures, or is it mostly interpretive? Walk through what each position commits us to on those product questions and what stance the memo should take.
