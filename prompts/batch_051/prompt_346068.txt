Here's something that's been bugging me about how people talk about transformative AI. The doomers reach for nuclear weapons; the boosters reach for the printing press and the Industrial Revolution. Fine. But notice that each analogy has another face sitting right there. Nuclear fission gave us cheap baseload power and nuclear medicine, not just Hiroshima. The printing press didn't only democratize knowledge; it fed the wars of religion and a few centuries of propaganda. And the Industrial Revolution raised living standards eventually, but it also immiserated a couple generations and ran the mills on child labor. Yet you almost never hear someone warn about AI by invoking the printing press, or comfort people about AI by invoking the peaceful atom. Is there a defensible principal that tells you which face of a historical analogy you're allowed to borrow, or is this just cherry-picking sorted by temperament? I want the strongest principle you can find, and then I want you to test it against the case where the analogy cuts the other way. Tell me honestly whether we should keep the asymmetry or give it up.
