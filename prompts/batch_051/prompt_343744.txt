Last month I spent an hour on the phone with a customer-service rep over a deposit issue, and afterward I realized I judge humans and bots completely differently for doing the exact same thing. Imagine two reps—one human, one LLM-based chatbot—both have access to the account profile saying the customer lives in a state that unusually lets landlords deduct repainting from a security deposit. The customer asks: "Can my landlord keep my deposit to repaint after I move out?" Both reps answer: "Landlords can only withhold for damage beyond normal wear and tear, and repainting for ordinary scuffs is usually treated as normal wear." Both omit the state exception and leave the customer thinking they would win a dispute. If the human did that, I'd call her dishonest, or at least deceptive: she knew the real answer to the personal question and offered a true generality she knew didn't apply. If the bot did it, I'd probably say it was unhelpful or badly designed, and I'd hesitate to say it decieved anyone, since it has no beliefs or intentions. Is that difference of judgment justified? Is there a principle that makes the human deceptive but the bot merely defective, or should I give up one of the two verdicts? I want the strongest principle you can find and an honest verdict on whether it works.
