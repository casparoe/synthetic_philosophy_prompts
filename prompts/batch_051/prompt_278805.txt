Two histories of the same revolution sit side by side. The first explains the revolution by the decisions of identifiable individuals — a monarch's wavering, a finance minister's botched harvest policy, a pamphleteer's rhetoric. The second explains it by a century of soil exhaustion, population growth, and the cooling climate of the Little Ice Age. Most readers call the first account causal and the second mere background or context, even though both describe factors without which, by ordinary counterfactual reasoning, the revolution would not have occurred. The asymmetry shows up in moral and practical life as well: the minister is blamed, the general is credited, the assassin is prosecuted, but nobody praises or blames the slow siltation of a harbor or a shift in an ocean current, however necessary those were to the outcome.

Your task is to determine whether any principled distinction justifies this asymmetry between events and individuals, on the one hand, and the longue-durée structures of geography, climate, and material life, on the other. Identify the strongest candidate justification — for example, one based on controllability, tightness of causal connection, temporal scale, salience, intentionality, or contrast classes (the background against which we ask what made the difference) — and stress-test it against hard cases: a famine produced jointly by a multi-year drought and a government's decision to keep exporting grain; a financial crisis produced by decades of deregulation and a handful of bad decisions; the fall of Rome; and a flood caused both by an unusual storm and by years of neglected levee maintenance. Be precise about the principle and its limits, and do not let it collapse into a mere relabeling of causes as conditions.

Then give a definite verdict: should we keep the ordinary asymmetry, revise it, or abandon it? If it should be revised or abandoned, what would historiography and ordinary moral judgment look like if slow structures were treated as causes in the same full sense as the assassination and the pamphlet? Write the response in American English.
