I am a PhD student in history and philosophy of science, and I have come to accept a broadly Lakatosian account of scientific change: mature sciences are best reconstructed as research programmes centred on a hard core of assumptions that the community treats as non-negotiable, surrounded by a belt of auxiliary hypotheses that absorbs anomalies and failures, with appraisal aimed at a programme over time rather than at any single theory, and with the chief sign of a healthy programme being that it generates novel predictions that are then confirmed. I hold both a descriptive version of this claim (it captures how mature sciences actually behave) and a normative version (it captures how theory choice should be conducted), and I take each to lend support to the other.

I want you to tell me what would count against this position. I am not asking for the standard list of objections; I want an ordered map of the position's exposure. Which logical results, formal arguments about theory choice, historical patterns, or developments in scientific practice would have to be true for the Lakatosian picture to be false, or to collapse into one of its live rivals — naive falsificationism, Kuhnian paradigm change, or more recent sociological and practice-oriented accounts? For each candidate defeater, rank it as decisive or merely awkward; say which ones a committed Lakatosian would themselves concede as counting against the view rather than dismiss as a misapplication of the methodology; and say what would distinguish a legitimate protective adjustment of the belt from the ad hoc maneuvering that Lakatosians themselves treat as a symptom of degeneration. I also want your assessment of whether the view, as it is usually defended, has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if so, whether that defect is fatal for a methodology that tells scientists to prefer risky theories and to regard ad hoc adjustment as a bad sign.

Act as an expert in the philosophy of science, especially in debates over scientific change and the rationality of theory choice. Please think carefully and begin with some brainstorming about candidate defeaters before developing the strongest ones. But if you judge that the request as posed rests on a confusion or cannot be done well — for example, because I am running together descriptive and normative claims in a way that obscures what would count as evidence, or because a methodological position of this kind is not refutable in the way an empirical theory is — say so plainly and reformulate the question into the most defensible version you can before answering it.
