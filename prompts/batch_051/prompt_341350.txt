I keep seeing the same loop in arguments about whether market society improves or corrupts character: one side reaches for the honest merchant, long-distance trust, and the discipline of dealing with strangers; the other reaches for price-gouging, commodified relationships, and a calculative frame that colonizes the rest of life. I want the dispute turned into a document both camps could actually sign.

Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two committed participants:

Camp A: the view that commerce civilizes — that markets, on balance, train and select for genuine virtues, including promise-keeping, honesty with strangers, punctuality, self-command, service to the wants of others, and cooperation beyond kinship and ingroup.

Camp B: the view that commerce corrupts — that markets, on balance, train and select for moral impairments, including instrumental calculation about other people, crowding out of intrinsic motives, commodification of goods that should not be priced, and normalization of exploiting asymmetric information or need.

The statement must have three parts.

First, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, written so as to be neither vacuous nor slanted. Include what each accepts about what market exchange actually does to behavior, what each accepts about what a character trait is, and what each accepts about how one would tell corruption from civilization. Where a claim can only be signed with a qualification, give the qualified version rather than dropping the claim.

Second, a disagreements table. For each remaining disagreement, state Camp A's position in the wording Camp A would accept and Camp B's position in the wording Camp B would accept. Mark any disagreement that turns out, once formulated, to be merely verbal.

Third, a cruxes table. The cruxes are the sub-questions on which at least one camp's view is load-bearing: answer one such question one way and one camp's position weakens, answer it the other way and the other camp's position weakens. Do not merely restate the main disagreement in miniature. Candidate areas, offered as scaffolding rather than constraints: whether variation in market exposure predicts or causes more fair and trusting play in repeated games with strangers; whether paying people for a prosocial activity measurably reduces their intrinsic motivation, and if so under which conditions; whether the relevant comparison is against feasible non-market institutions rather than a pastoral baseline; and whether markets mainly select different people into commerce rather than transform the character of the same people. For each crux, state the argument, case, or evidence both camps agree would bear on it, and then each camp's prediction, recorded in advance in one line.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns [Item, Camp A's position, Camp B's position, Verbal?], then a cruxes table with columns [Crux, What both camps agree bears on it, Camp A predicts, Camp B predicts].

End the document with a final line in the form \answer{...} containing a one-sentence summary of the disagreement that remains after the tables.
