Section 7 of the City's public-safety charter reads: "The City shall have the sole authority to deploy armed personnel to arrest, detain, or remove any person within the City, except as expressly provided by state or federal law." A neighborhood association operates a "crisis response team" for psychiatric emergencies. Its members carry no weapons, but they are trained in physical holds and have restrained people to prevent imminent self-harm, holding them until relatives or paramedics arrive; they make no arrests and release anyone who asks to leave. The City's own emergency dispatch system routes certain calls to the team, the team is funded under a City contract for "community-based de-escalation services," and the police department has an informal practice of not responding when the team is on scene. The clause was adopted after a private security firm began patrolling a gated community, and its drafters were thinking of for-profit guards and armed vigilante patrols, not this arrangement. Does Section 7 reach the team's restraints? Is the team "armed personnel"? Does "deploy" cover a city-funded and city-dispatched group that the city treats as an alternative to sending officers? If the clause is read as protecting the City's monopoly, has the City simply delegated part of it; if it is read as confining forcible response to sworn officers, is the team prohibited? Work through the words, the purpose, what the drafters would likely have said if asked, and the reasonable reader, and identify where these readings diverge.
