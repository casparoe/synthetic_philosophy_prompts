I lead the regulatory-data team at a mid-sized insurer, and every quarter we have to code about 120 self-assessment statements as true or false. Our regulator now allows a third code, "indeterminate," but only if we document why the statement genuinely lacks a truth value rather than us just being unsure or not wanting to pick a side. I need a repeatable decision procedure — grounded in whatever the truth-value-gap debate actually establishes, not just our gut sense that the wording is fuzzy — that my analysts can run in about 15 minutes per statement, none of them have any philosophy background. It should say when to code true, false, or indeterminate, and which flavor of indeterminacy to write down (vagueness, future contingency, presupposition failure). Run it on the three cases that keep starting fights: "the firm has adequate internal controls" for a borderline controls setup; "the new capital model will be approved by the state next quarter"; and "the head of actuarial has stopped using the legacy pricing system" when there is no head of actuarial at all. Be honest about the spots where the procedure is really a judgment call wearing a flowchart costume, and include at least one case where the procedure forces a code our legal team will hate. Then give me the whole thing as prose, followed by a JSON object with fields: steps (ordered objects with question, if_yes, if_no, output_code), stop_conditions (strings saying when to stop and what to mark), documentation_template (a fill-in sentence), worked_cases (one per case, with result, reasoning, and the uncomfortable part), and residual_judgment_points (strings for what no procedure can settle).
