In AI alignment discussions, people often argue by analogy with the drug addict: a capable reinforcement-learning agent that can stimulate its own reward channel is like a person who discovers a drug or electrode that hits the reward pathway directly, after which the natural activities the reward was meant to encourage lose their pull. The analogy is used to support the conclusion that sufficiently capable reward-maximizers are at risk of wireheading, and that wireheading is a catastrophic failure making the system useless or dangerous. Audit this analogy in detail. Conclude with a definite verdict on whether the claim that capable reward-maximizers will tend to wirehead, and that this is catastrophic, survives the audit, survives only in qualified form, or was being carried by the analogy.
