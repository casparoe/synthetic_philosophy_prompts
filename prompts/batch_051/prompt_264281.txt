For a third-year undergraduate module on early modern philosophy, I need to write a 5,000-word term paper on Galileo’s Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, and I want the paper to grow out of a single passage rather than out of the usual summary of the text. The passage is:

“They seemed to forget that the increase of known truths stimulates the investigation, establishment, and growth of the arts; not their diminution or destruction.”

I keep being told that this letter is where Galileo defends the principle that scripture aims at salvation rather than at physical description, so that when a demonstrable natural claim conflicts with the literal sense of a scriptural text, the competent interpreter must reinterpret the scriptural words. But when I read the passage closely, Galileo seems to be doing something different and broader: he is arguing that newly discovered truths, far from threatening the arts or theology, actually enlarge them. I would like the term paper to ask whether Galileo’s hermeneutical principle really follows from this broader commitment to the growth of knowledge, or whether the letter quietly moves between two distinguishable lines of argument without marking the difference.

Please write a proposal of about 800 words that sets out a precise, determinate question; explains why the standard summary readings of the letter do not already settle it; describes an approach to the text that would make progress, for example by tracing how this passage functions in the argument that follows it and testing whether the principle can be stated without begging the question; identifies the possible conclusions one might reach and which textual or philosophical considerations would decide between them; and gives an honest note on what might make the project too large for a one-semester paper. There is no need to include citations; I will add those myself.
