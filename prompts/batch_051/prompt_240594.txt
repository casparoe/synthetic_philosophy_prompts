I have agreed to lead one session for a discussion group of retired scientists and engineers who are working through philosophy together. The topic is the problem that finkish and masked dispositions pose for the simple conditional analysis of dispositions, and I have forty-five minutes with a whiteboard. Do not give me the explanation itself; give me the plan for the explanation. What has to be in place before the problem can even be stated, and what depends on what? Which single worked example should carry the whole session—fragility is the obvious candidate, but tell me if there is a better one—and what must it have and what must it lack? Should finks come before masks or after, and what exactly goes wrong if the audience collapses them into one phenomenon? What misconceptions should I expect from scientifically literate nonspecialists—for example, treating a disposition as nothing over and above its actual manifestations, or taking a mask to be a fink with different timing—and at what point in the sequence should each be headed off? What one question or case at the end would show that they have understood the conceptual problem rather than merely learned the vocabulary? And what should I deliberately leave out, and how should I mark that boundary without making the discussion seem evasive?
