I am a doctoral student in social epistemology. In a recent seminar, two other students argued about whether the expression conspiracy theory picks out an epistemically defective class, and they asked me to referee; I could not tell who was gaining ground.

Elena began that the category is too heterogeneous to carry epistemic weight. It spans claims that later turned out true, paranoid systems, and everything between, so the only defensible policy is particularist: assess each allegation on its own evidence and give it no general prior.

Marcus replied that the pejorative label is indeed hopeless, but there is a recurring structure worth isolating: a small group secretly coordinating to explain a prominent public event while the official account is treated as an elaborate cover-up. That structure tends to absorb contrary evidence as further evidence of the conspiracy, extend itself ad hoc, and multiply agents as needed. A defeasible prior against that structure is therefore reasonable.

Elena answered that none of those vices is unique to conspiracy theories. Unfalsifiable, evidence-absorbing maneuvers appear in ordinary self-deception, ideology, and even degenerate research programs. If the real defect is unfalsifiability, that is the defect, and the category adds nothing. Official narratives can be just as self-insulating.

Marcus granted the point about non-uniqueness but said a category need not be unique to be useful. The generalist claim is not about an essence; it is that the secrecy-plus-cover-up structure is correlated with error, so it functions as a base-rate heuristic. He added that official accounts earn a different default through checking institutions—auditing, adversarial journalism, disclosure rules, whistleblowing—so the prior should be strongest where those institutions are healthy.

Elena said this concedes the argument: once one explains why official accounts are typically better supported, one is doing particularist source-reliability assessment, and any extra penalty for the class will misfire where the checking institutions are captured or absent.

Marcus answered that the prior is not extra; it compresses a statistical fact about how difficult such explanations are for an outsider to discriminate from motivated confabulation when two narratives are both superficially coherent. He conceded the category is a rough instrument and the prior should scale with institutional health.

They continued past this, but the core structure did not change. Please adjudicate the disagreement point by point: who is right about which specific claims, what both are missing, and who is more right overall. Also say which single argument or consideration would most likely change your overall verdict, and where your adjudication is least secure.
