The problem of logical omniscience is usually treated as a technical obstacle for formal models of belief, but for me it presents a forced choice. Probabilism about rational partial belief earns its authority from being the only precise, unified account of coherence: degrees of belief satisfy the probability axioms, update by conditionalization, and assign maximal credence to logical truths, with closure under entailment following as a condition of coherence. I find those arguments hard to resist. Yet I have an equally firm intuition that a finite reasoner who treats a set of axioms as certain, but has not worked through a long and unobvious consequence of them — where the limitation is one of time and computational resources, not carelessness — is not thereby irrational. I am not seeking a verdict for one side. I want a negotiated resolution: what would it take for the intuition to be debunked as a confusion between description and normative ideal; what would it take for the theory to be amended rather than merely softened, and, among awareness structures, impossible-worlds semantics, and resource-bounded probabilism, which amendment is least ad hoc; and whether an ideal/feasible distinction can do the necessary work without emptying rational norms of content for actual agents.
