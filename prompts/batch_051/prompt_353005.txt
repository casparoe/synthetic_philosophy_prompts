(a) Moral judgments are ultimately preferences in disguise. When I say "that's wrong," I'm dressing up a deep preference I have about how people should treat each other, not reporting some fact sitting out there in the world.

(b) But I also think some of the things people treat as just "moral preferences" are actually biases, racism and sexism mainly, and these cant just be tolerated the way you tolerate someone liking a different flavor of ice cream. They should be corrected.

(c) A bias is different from an ordinary preference because of how it got formed: biases come from unreliable sources like fear of outgroups, echo chambers, or self-interest.

(d) The practical test I use is that a bias will crack if you actually sit someone down with the facts and the other side's point of view, whereas a real preference will just shrug and stay.

(e) But then I'm also suspicious of the whole idea of moral progress. When people say society moved forward on slavery or womens rights, it feels like that might just be the winners' preferences taking over, and we flatter ourselves by calling it progress.
