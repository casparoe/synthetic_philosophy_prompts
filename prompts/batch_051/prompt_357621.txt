Consider the following claim. The traditional sea-battle debate is usually framed as a logical and metaphysical question: whether a statement about an unsettled future, such as a sea battle will occur tomorrow, is already true or already false, or instead neither true nor false until the relevant events are fixed. I want to bracket that question and test a nearby claim: that bivalence about such statements is not a stable feature of ordinary future-directed thought—that competent speakers, when evaluating, updating, and acting on future contingents, systematically behave as though such statements acquire a truth value only as the world becomes settled. Design the best empirical test you can of this claim. Specify what you would measure or manipulate, what pattern of results would persuade you the claim is true, and what pattern would persuade you it is false. Then be precise about what even the best test could never settle about the original metaphysical question. Please write your answer in American English.
