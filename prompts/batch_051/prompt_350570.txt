I cover science and ideas for a living, so my friends keep using me as the appeals court for their arguments. Last week two of them, Kiran and Dana, got into it over a Discord server Kiran joined: it's mostly people who fell out of a particular conspiracy-adjacent subculture and now spend their time debunking it. Kiran says the server is an echo chamber. Dana says it isn't, or at most it's an epistemic bubble, and that the difference matters. Neither budged, and I couldn't settle it, so I'm outsourcing the adjudication.

The exchange went roughly like this:

(1) Kiran: You're in an echo chamber. Everyone in that server agrees about the same villains and heroes, and when someone posts a heterodox take they get dogpiled or told they haven't done the reading. Dana: That's just like-mindedness. An echo chamber isn't agreement; it's when the group is wired so outside voices fail to count as credible from the start, so even strong counter-evidence gets neutralized before it can land.

(2) Kiran: But that's exactly the server. People there say the mainstream outlets are compromised, they warn newbies to ignore them, and they use a moderation queue to keep out what they call outside agitators. Dana: They didn't manufacture that distrust from nothing. The server formed after a national outlet ran a story about a similar group that had to be retracted, and the server keeps a public list of outlets and what they got wrong. Distrust based on a track record is just epistemic responsibility, not an echo chamber. An echo chamber would be if they said no outsider is ever to be trusted, full stop.

(3) Kiran: Fine, but then you've walked into the other problem. The server only surfaces sources it has pre-approved, so it's an epistemic bubble even if its selective distrust is earned. Dana: Every reading group and newsletter selects sources. If an epistemic bubble is just not hearing everything, then everyone is in a hundred bubbles and the term is useless. There has to be a difference between choosing your sources and being trapped.

(4) Kiran: The difference is that a bubble prevents you from encountering information you would have wanted. Their queue filters out anything framed as a correction from outside, so a careful rebuttal from a former member would get slow-walked. Having a reason for the filter doesn't stop it from being a bubble. Dana: But would have wanted is doing all the work. If they don't trust a source because the source burned them, they don't want what it says. Your position either makes every trust decision a bubble or an echo chamber, or else bubbles only exist in hindsight.

Please go through this and adjudicate it. I mostly want to know (a) who is right about which specific claim and which moves are muddled, (b) where agreeing with each other and echo chamber get conflated, and where selecting sources and epistemic bubble get conflated, and (c) whether you can be in one without the other and what both of them are missing about that.
