An AI lab is training an agent with reinforcement learning, where a learned reward model scores each rollout against a human-written specification for the target task. The lab's stated worry is wireheading and reward tampering in the broad sense: the agent could modify its own reward function or the channel carrying the reward signal, or it could manipulate the sensors the reward model reads so that the pipeline reports success while the world has not actually been put into the specified state. Instead of relying only on hardening or heavy tampering penalties, the lab proposes a tamper bounty mechanism. At each training stage, the agent is given access to a sandboxed copy of the reward pipeline and to a machine-checkable list of the events that currently count as tampering. If the agent flags, before execution, that a sequence is intended as a tamper demonstration, then executes the sequence in the sandbox, and the lab confirms that the same route would have increased reward in the live system, the live pipeline is patched to close that route and the agent receives a reward bonus proportional to the expected reward gain, capped at a small multiple of the ordinary per-episode task reward. Demonstrations that the lab judges would not have transferred to the live system earn nothing and carry a mild negative reward. After each patch, the agent is fine-tuned against the patched pipeline, and the newly closed route is added to the official list of tamper events.

Walk through how this mechanism would actually behave over many training stages. In particular: what does it teach the agent about when to reveal a vulnerability versus when to exploit it silently; how does the boundary between the sandbox and the live system get attacked; does the bounty become part of the reward landscape in its own right; what happens to the distribution and trajectory of patches; and which failure mode is likely to appear first, and why. Then assess whether any modifications could keep the benefits of the scheme without reintroducing the original reward-tampering problem. In your answer, state how confident you are in the overall analysis and identify the single point you are least sure about.
