I have been trying to reconcile two things from different parts of my reading: the epistemology I pick up from analytic philosophy and the way I actually think about classifiers, labels, and distribution shift in my day job as an ML engineer. The specific puzzle is the clash between the principle that knowledge is closed under known entailment and the intuition, associated with Nozick's truth-tracking analysis, that I can know that I have hands while failing to know the entailed proposition that I am not a handless brain in a vat. Nozick's diagnosis, as I understand it, is that knowledge requires one's belief to track the truth: roughly, the belief must be sensitive, so that if the proposition were false one would not believe it through the same method, and it must hold up in the other direction, so that if the proposition were true in the relevant nearby cases one would still believe it. The hand belief tracks; the not-a-brain-in-a-vat belief does not, because in the vat scenario I would presumably still believe I was not in a vat. My question is whether this whole debate can be expressed in the vocabulary I actually trust.

I am an ML engineer and a longtime reader of LessWrong-adjacent material, so I am comfortable with both idioms but far more fluent in the engineering one. Please restate Nozick's tracking account, and the reason it forces a choice between epistemic closure and his conditions on knowledge, in the vocabulary of models, ground-truth labels, environments, interventions including the do-operator, distribution shift, spurious correlations, shortcut learning, and counterfactual robustness. One mapping I already suspect is that the sensitivity condition looks like a counterfactual invariance requirement on a prediction: the model's output for p would change under an intervention that flips the truth of p while holding the model and its feature pipeline fixed. And Nozick's relativization to a method of belief formation looks like the insistence on evaluating the same trained model under the intervention rather than allowing retraining or a different architecture. But I want you to do the mapping systematically rather than simply confirm my guess.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of the tracking theory and the closure debate map cleanly onto this engineering vocabulary, which parts map only with distortion, and which parts have no counterpart at all. In particular, I want to know whether the philosophical notion of knowledge carries normative content such as justification, warrant, or entitlement that no vocabulary of accuracy, calibration, or robustness can express, and whether that residue indicates that my engineering vocabulary is missing something that matters or that the philosophers' dispute is partly an artifact of their vocabulary. I would rather you locate the genuine points of untranslatability than force a clean fit.
