I am a retired geochemist who came to philosophy of history late, and I keep finding the label "pseudohistory" applied with confidence but defined almost nowhere. I would like a working definition of it. It should not simply mean history that is false, since ordinary scholarship is later overturned all the time without being called pseudohistory; and it should not simply mean history written in the service of an agenda, since partisan or nationalist works are usually criticized as bad or biased rather than pseudohistory. The demarcation I am after is subtler than either. Please propose a definition, apply it to each of the following cases, say where it delivers the wrong answer or no answer, and then revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts: (1) a nineteenth-century enthusiast who takes Plato's Atlantis story literally and reconstructs a drowned mid-Atlantic civilization from geology and myth; (2) Plato's own Atlantis narrative, which many classicists read as a political-philosophical fable rather than a factual claim about a lost sea power; (3) the ancient-astronaut thesis that the Egyptian pyramids could not have been built without extraterrestrial help, despite the ordinary archaeological record; (4) Fomenko's New Chronology, which claims that much of ancient and medieval history is a later forgery and that events we attribute to antiquity actually occurred in the Middle Ages; (5) a historical novel that deliberately rearranges dates and persons for dramatic effect while never signaling which elements are invented; (6) a conventional monograph that later archival work simply overturns; (7) a textbook that accurately reports everything it covers but systematically omits its own country's atrocities. Finally, tell me which of your verdicts is least secure, and whether "pseudohistory" tracks one underlying defect or several distinct ones.
