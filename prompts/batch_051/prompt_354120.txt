So I've been getting into gauge theory in my spare time because at work I maintain a simulation codebase that does electromagnetics, and there's one thing I can't stop chewing on. The same physical situation can be represented by mathematically different potentials: you can add the gradient of some scalar field to the vector potential (and adjust the scalar potential to match) and you get exactly the same E and B fields, same forces, same measurements. People say the extra freedom is gauge freedom and the extra mathematical layer is surplus structure. The second I understood that, it felt familiar: it's serialization. Two JSON objects with keys in different orders, or a float printed as 2.0 versus 2.00000, are different byte strings but they decode to the same object, and no one thinks the byte string is the object. So my homemade view is that the real content of a gauge theory is just the equivalence class under gauge transformations — the thing all the different encodings have in common — and the gauge-dependent bits are just a choice of serialization format. Fixing a gauge is like picking a canonical form before you compare two config files: convenient, but not a claim that the canonical form is more real. I've even caught myself saying that gauge symmetry isn't really a symmetry of the world, it's a redundancy in our description, which sounds deep but might just be the standard take dressed up in programmer words.

What I'm less sure about is where the analogy snaps. The Aharonov–Bohm effect bothers me: a charged particle can pick up a phase from the potential in a region where the field is zero, which makes the encoding sound physically load-bearing in a way my serialization story says it shouldn't be. I also don't know if the analogy survives non-abelian gauge theories, or Gribov copies, or the whole business in quantum field theory where you have to fix a gauge just to do the path integral without double-counting. So my actual question is: is the serialization view a decent account of what's real in a gauge theory, and where exactly does it become false or stop being useful? If it's basically what physicists already think, tell me what extra mistake the programming analogy introduces, and be candid about whether the Aharonov–Bohm thing is a real counterexample or just me misunderstanding what the phase is.
