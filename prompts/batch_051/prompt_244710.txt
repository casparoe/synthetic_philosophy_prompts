A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether Yablo's paradox is genuinely free of self-reference. My view was that it is: no sentence in the sequence says anything about itself; each sentence only says that all later sentences are false, and because the sequence has no last member, the reference never curves back. His view was that this is too narrow a test. The truth of each sentence depends on the truth of later sentences, and that dependence never bottoms out; he called this self-reference in a subtler sense, or at least circularity enough to put Yablo's sequence in the same family as the liar. We ended with sharper positions but no criterion for deciding between them.

I want a working definition that can adjudicate the argument. Define self-reference so that it gives a definite verdict for each of the following cases. If your definition needs to distinguish self-reference from a narrower kind — say, the vicious circularity that actually generates the paradoxes — draw that distinction explicitly and classify each case under both notions.

1. The simple liar: a sentence that says of itself that it is false.
2. Curry's sentence: a sentence that says of itself that if it is true then 0=1.
3. A harmless case: a sentence that says of itself that it has five words.
4. A clear negative: the sentence that says that grass is green.
5. A finite loop: S1 says that S2 is false, S2 says that S3 is false, and S3 says that S1 is false.
6. A contingent case: a sentence that says that the sentence written on the blackboard is false, and the only sentence written on the blackboard is that very sentence.
7. Yablo's sequence: S1, S2, S3, and so on without end, where each Sn says that every later sentence in the list is false.

I am not mainly interested in taxonomy for its own sake. I want to know whether a defensible definition vindicates my view, my friend's view, or neither, and where the weakest step in that result lies. Tell me which of your classifications is least secure and what would change it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
