I'm in a formal epistemology seminar and we just did Jeffrey conditionalization, and I can't stop arguing with a friend about it. The rule, as our instructor gave it: when an experience doesn't make any proposition certain but still shifts your credences over some partition—say, for a patient, the partition {viral, bacterial, other}—you update any other proposition A by taking a weighted average of your old conditional credences P_old(A | cell), using your new credences in the cells as weights, and leaving your old conditional probabilities inside each cell alone. So P_new(A) = P_new(viral)*P_old(A|viral) + P_new(bacterial)*P_old(A|bacterial) + P_new(other)*P_old(A|other).

My friend says this rule is undertermined, and laid the argument out like this:

(1) Before a noisy test, my credences are P(viral)=0.2, P(bacterial)=0.5, P(other)=0.3; after the test they shift to P_new(viral)=0.6, P_new(bacterial)=0.2, P_new(other)=0.2, with no cell going to 1.
(2) Jeffrey's rule then gives P_new(recovery) = 0.6*P_old(recovery|viral) + 0.2*P_old(recovery|bacterial) + 0.2*P_old(recovery|other), where 'recovery' means recovery within a week.
(3) But the test didn't force that partition on us. We could just as well use a finer partition that splits viral into two strains, v1 and v2. Since P_new(v1)+P_new(v2) has to equal 0.6, there are infinitely many ways to assign the subcell probabilities, all compatible with what the test said.
(4) Applied to the finer partition, Jeffrey's rule computes P_new(recovery) = P_new(v1)*P_old(recovery|v1) + P_new(v2)*P_old(recovery|v2) + 0.2*P_old(recovery|bacterial) + 0.2*P_old(recovery|other).
(5) Suppose P_old(recovery|v1)=0.8 and P_old(recovery|v2)=0.2. Then a split of 0.5/0.1 makes the v1+v2 terms equal 0.5*0.8 + 0.1*0.2 = 0.42, while a split of 0.1/0.5 makes the same terms 0.1*0.8 + 0.5*0.2 = 0.18.
(6) Since the finer partition is no less legitimate, Jeffrey's rule gives different answers for the very same evidence.
(7) So either the rule fails to fix a unique posterior or it's smuggling in a preferred partition; either way it isn't workable.

I half-suspect he's cheating somewhere in the treatment of the finer partition, but I can't pin down the exact step. Is the argument valid? If not, which is the first step that fails, and what condition on the finer-grained new credences would make the coarse and fine updates coincide?
