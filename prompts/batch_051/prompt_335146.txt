A textbook for one of my classes sums up Orwell's "Politics and the English Language" like this:

"Orwell's central claim is that corrupt politics and corrupt language form a feedback loop: bad politics produces vague, evasive prose, and that prose makes bad politics easier to sustain. He catalogs the usual vices—dying metaphors, euphemism, inflated Latinate diction—and shows how political speech uses them to dress up the indefensible. His six rules of plain style are his solution: because language determines thought, cleaner prose should gradually clean up politics and make political lying much harder to get away with. Orwell ends on a pessimistic note, though: the rot has gone so deep that ordinary individual effort will not get us very far, and reversing it is really a job for professional writers and educators."
