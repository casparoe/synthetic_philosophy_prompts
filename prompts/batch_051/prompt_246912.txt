Suppose that in the early 2030s a consortium develops and independently validates a device — call it a sentience meter — that takes any bounded physical system, scans it, and returns a reading on a single scale meant to measure the presence and intensity of phenomenal consciousness, understood as the felt, subjective character of experience rather than the mere availability of information for report and control. The device is calibrated on cases about which there is wide agreement: it reads high for awake, dreaming, and minimally conscious human beings; near zero for dreamless sleep and general anesthesia; and zero for rocks and for the ordinary digital computers of the day. It produces stable readings in the contested cases — late-term fetuses, patients with severe disorders of consciousness, octopuses, honeybees, brain organoids, and large language models — but the pattern is surprising and the interpretation of those readings is disputed. Its designers insist the instrument is theory-neutral: it tracks a physical signature rather than any particular theory's construct, and it does not depend on verbal report. The device is cheap and portable enough to be deployed routinely, and by 2040 it has, in the world's major scientific and legal jurisdictions, roughly the authority that functional brain imaging has today; its readings are treated as decisive in animal-welfare and AI-policy disputes.

Predict, with your reasoning visible at each step, what the existence and acceptance of this device does over the following two decades to (a) the philosophical debate about which computations or physical systems matter morally, (b) the actual attribution of moral and legal status to artificial systems and to nonhuman animals, and (c) the practice of building and deploying AI systems. I want the chain of reasoning, not a story. In particular: what, if anything, does such a device establish about the relation between a system's computational properties and its being conscious? Which of the standing positions in the debate — substrate-neutral functionalism, biological chauvinism, global-workspace and integrated-information approaches, illusionism, and views that treat sentience as graded rather than all-or-nothing — are confirmed, disconfirmed, or left untouched by a device of this kind, and why? Does the device settle the question of attribution, or does it relocate the disagreement to a new place: to what the meter measures, to whose readings count, to what a reading licenses morally, or to something else? Address the objection that the scenario may be question-begging: a genuinely theory-neutral meter of this kind may already presuppose that consciousness is a single, physically measurable property, and if so, say which positions are ruled out by the stipulation itself. Separate your predictions into those that hold regardless of what the meter says about any particular ambiguous system and those that depend on the pattern of readings, and mark your confidence in each link. Finally, say what would have to be true for your central predictions to fail.
