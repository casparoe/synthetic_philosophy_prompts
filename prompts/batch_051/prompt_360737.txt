I'm trying to get clear on what, if anything, becomes of the martial virtues — courage, honor, discipline, loyalty under lethal risk — in a society where war has become rare and peripheral. I've collected five candidate answers; I want a sixth that is neither a restatement nor a hybrid of any of them.

(1) The channeling view: the virtues persist because their characteristic occasions migrate into civilian competition such as sport, business, emergency response, or the courtroom, so broadly the same dispositions are exercised under diluted risk. (2) The exemplarist view: military courage survives as a public stock of examples, but its peacetime value is entirely derivative, teaching civilians how to face illness, whistleblowing, or private danger, with the martial setting itself dispensable. (3) The republican view: a peaceful society still needs courage in civic form, since standing up to majorities, officeholders, or mobs is a kind of combat, and the virtue that steadied soldiers is the same virtue that steadies citizens. (4) The deflationary view: there is no distinctively martial courage; war merely provided one historically salient occasion among many for the single virtue of courage, so nothing becomes of martial virtue because there never was a separate kind. (5) The tragic view: martial virtues are genuine only in relation to real lethal conflict, so peace cannot house them except through surrogates such as militarized sport, honor codes, or weapons culture, and those surrogates are at best pallid and at worst corrupting.

Give me a sixth candidate whose structure is different from all five — in particular, one that treats the peacetime role of martial virtue as neither relocation, nor exemplarity, nor civic necessity, nor identity, nor loss. Then tell me honestly whether the candidate is any good, and say what would change your mind: which of your own assumptions about what a virtue is, or about what peace is, would have to fail for you to prefer one of the other candidates, or where your reasoning most likely goes wrong.
