Over the last couple of years I've been collecting my gut reactions to episodes in the history of science, and I cant figure out whether there's a single standard behind them or I'm just rationalizing. Here are the cases. (1) When Uranus's orbit didn't fit Newtonian predictions, I think it was clearly right to keep Newtonian mechanics and infer an unseen planet — and then Neptune was found, which felt like a huge win. (2) When Mercury's orbit also didn't fit, it seemed fine at first to posit an unseen planet (Vulcan); but once Vulcan kept not showing up and the fixes multiplied, I feel the whole approach had become ad hoc even before general relativity came along. (3) Early Copernicans had no real observational advantage over Ptolemy, and their first systems were at least as clunky; still, I think it was rational for them to keep developing the view. (4) Late attempts to keep a full Ptolemaic system alive by piling on more epicycles strike me as not just wrong but somehow unscientific in how they responded to the data. (5) Today dark matter is posited to save gravitational theory from galactic rotation curves and cluster dynamics; my gut says this is Neptune-like and legitimate, but I can't articulate why it isn't epicycle-like.

My first pass at a principle was this: it's okay to add unseen entities if they lead to surprising novel predictions that later get confirmed, but not if they're just adjusted after the fact to mop up anomalies. But that doesn't handle early Copernicus, which for a long time made no confirmed novel predictions — if anything it fit the phenomena worse at first. So I'd like a better principle, or a small set of them, stated as precisely as possible, that gets cases (1) through (5). And I'd like to know which of my verdicts I'd have to give up if the cleanest principle won't save them all. What would the resulting principle say about another case I keep going back and forth on: the cosmological constant, which Einstein introduced to keep the universe static, then abandoned, then got revived for cosmic acceleration? Please write the answer in American English.
