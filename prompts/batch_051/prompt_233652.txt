Question: Explain Avicenna's flying man argument. What is it meant to show, and how much of that does it actually establish?

Student answer: Avicenna has us imagine someone created all at once, suspended in a void with their eyes covered and their limbs not touching each other or anything else, so they have no sensory input and never have had any. He thinks this person would still be aware of his own self, which is meant to show that the self is known directly and not through the senses. From this he concludes that the soul is something distinct from the body, that it can exist on its own without the body, and therefore that it is immortal. It's basically an early version of Descartes' cogito, except there is no step of universal doubt.
