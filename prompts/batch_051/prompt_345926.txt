Two people in my formal epistemology reading group have been arguing since last fall about whether there are ever rationally mandatory priors, and they've finally agreed to hash it out as an adversarial collaboration. One of them is a subjective Bayesian (personalist) who thinks rationality only ever requires coherence plus conditionalization: any probabilistically coherent prior is permitted, and priors are just where an agent's values, hunches, or background experience get encoded. The other is an objective Bayesian in the maximum-entropy tradition, who thinks that once you specify your evidence properly—known means, symmetries, physical constraints—there is a uniquely rational prior, the one that maximizes entropy (or respects the relevant invariance) subject to those constraints, and that choosing any other prior is smuggling in information you don't have. They've argued for months, but now they want to produce a document they could both sign; there stuck on how to phrase the residual disagreement without strawmanning each other.

Then produce the document. Format: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section, where each claim is non-vacuous but genuinely signable by both camps—covering what coherence and conditionalization do and don't settle, which roles each side can accept for maximum-entropy and invariance priors, and what standard desiderata for priors neither side has to give up; and (2) a disagreements table where each row has the crux, the exact claim each side wants to make phrased so that side would accept it, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on that crux, and each side's advance prediction for that case.
