For my school's philosophy club, I've been reading about physicalism, and I came across the following summary in an introductory-looking philosophy article. It isn't attributed to any particular philosopher, and I suspect it may be oversimplifying things, but I don't know enough to say where. Here is the summary:

Physicalism is the doctrine that everything is physical. But there is a famous problem for it called Hempel's dilemma. If by "physical" you mean the kinds of things current physics describes — particles, fields, forces, and the like — then physicalism is almost certainly false, because the history of science shows that our current physics is always incomplete and will be revised. But if by "physical" you mean whatever a completed, final physics would describe, then physicalism becomes empty. Since we don't yet know what that future physics will include, saying "everything is physical" tells us nothing substantive; it can't be tested or refuted, and it would even be compatible with future physics discovering souls or spooky mental powers, as long as those ended up in the final theory. So physicalism is either false or empty, and in either case it can't be the serious, contentful claim that materialists want it to be.

I'd like to know whether this is a fair summary of Hempel's dilemma and of what physicalists actually claim. Could you go through the summary point by point and tell me which parts a careful philosopher would accept, which parts are overstatements or subtle misreadings, and how a defender of physicalism would likely respond? I've also seen physicalism described as the view that mental facts depend on, or are grounded in, physical facts, which sounds importantly different from "everything is physical." Is that a real difference the summary is glossing over? Finally, does the article's conclusion — that physicalism should be abandoned, or at least that it doesn't really say anything — follow from the accurate parts of the summary, or does the dilemma leave physicalists with reasonable options?
