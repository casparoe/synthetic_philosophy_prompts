My religous studies professor summarized the critique of 'religion' as a category something like this: "There's no single property that everything we call a religion shares. Theravada Buddhism has no creator god, Confucianism is mostly an ethical and ritual system, and plenty of traditions don't carve life up into 'religion' and 'the rest' the way we do. The category as we use it is a modern, Western, basically Protestant invention—it got forged through colonialism and then projected back onto other cultures, which is why it privileges private belief over practice and community, and why the whole 'world religions' framework always distorts whatever doesn't look like Christianity. So calling Buddhism a 'religion' isn't a discovery about Buddhism; it's an imposition, and the category itself is a colonial artifact rather than a real kind. Sensible people should just stop using the word in scholarship." I'm sympathetic to a lot of this, but the last step feels more confident than the evidence. Is this a fair summary of the actual debate? Where exactly does the Buddhism-and-Confucianism point do real work, where does it overreach, and what would someone who thinks the category still earns its keep say in response?
