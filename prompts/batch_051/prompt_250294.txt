I manage a textile dyeing operation. Our senior color grader insists that when he examines a dyed swatch under the inspection lamp, the shade he sees is simply given to him and cannot be mistaken, so his immediate visual judgment should override our spectrophotometer whenever the two disagree. A younger colorist replies that the grader's eye has been trained on thousands of swatches and that his verdicts depend on comparisons and category judgments he no longer notices, so the instrument should normally win. We must approve a large, costly batch, and I need to decide whose report to rely on. I want the underlying philosophical disagreement addressed, not merely a practical compromise: which of the two has the better account of what is happening when the grader looks at the swatch, and on what principle should I decide between a direct visual impression and a calibrated instrument? Please answer in American English.
