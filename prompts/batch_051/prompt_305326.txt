Since I retired from a career as a research physicist, I have been working through the philosophy of mind, and I have been asked to put my position on mental causation in writing for a reading group. Before I present it, I would like an audit of whether the commitments I have arrived at actually hang together. Here they are, as best I can state them at present:

(1) Causal closure of the physical: every physical event has a sufficient physical cause.

(2) Non-reductive physicalism about the mental: mental properties are real and are not identical to physical properties, but every mental event is realized by, and metaphysically depends on, physical facts. I take Cartesian dualism to be false, and I take the type-identity theory to be too coarse.

(3) Mental events are sometimes causes of physical events. My decision to raise my arm caused the arm to rise; the content of my intention caused these words rather than others to be typed.

(4) I reject systematic overdetermination. Ordinary actions are not routinely produced by two independent sufficient causes, one mental and one physical, in the way a death caused by both a gunshot and a lightning strike is overdetermined.

(5) I am drawn to a difference-making account of causation: C is a cause of E only if, roughly, there is a suitable contrast against which an intervention changing C would change E. I am still thinking through what this implies about mental causes.

(6) I think causal talk is interest-relative, or at least relative to a choice of variables. Whether we cite the decision or the underlying neural process as "the cause" depends on the explanatory question being asked, not on an absolute fact about which item is really doing the causing. There is no unique "the cause" simpliciter.

I would be grateful if you would go through these six commitments and identify where they actually pull against one another. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal; for each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Also point out any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, and then tell me which single revision would do the most work overall. Please keep the response to no more than 450 words.
