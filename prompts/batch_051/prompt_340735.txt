I am a journalist who covers science and ideas for a specialist magazine, and my editor has asked me, before we commission a piece, to estimate how a particular tribunal that hears defamation claims against publishers is likely to treat it. The tribunal has produced a reasonably consistent body of rulings on when a published rating, ranking, or forecast counts as protected opinion rather than actionable defamation. In one decision it held that a harsh one-star restaurant review was protected because the critic had described the particular dishes that grounded the judgement. In another it ruled that a financial newsletter's sell rating on a company was not protected, because the item asserted that the company was concealing accounting problems without stating what those problems were. It has also held that a consumer magazine's low safety rating for a car seat was protected where the crash-test data and scoring method were published alongside; that a worst-run councils ranking was not protected where the ranking was presented as fact but no criteria or evidence were supplied; and that a most overrated actors list was protected because it was openly a matter of taste. Taken together, these rulings gesture towards a principle about whether a rating imports an undisclosed factual assertion, but the tribunal has never ruled on a forward-looking forecast as such.

The piece under discussion would be a two-year climate-risk forecast rating some two hundred companies by their expected financial exposure to extreme weather, droughts, and supply-chain disruption. The scores would be generated by a proprietary model, and we would not publish the underlying data or the weightings. I cannot tell whether the tribunal would treat this as evaluative, future-directed speech of the sort it has protected, or as the kind of rating it has found actionable because hidden facts are made to do the work. I would like your best prediction of the tribunal's ruling on our proposed piece; the precedent you regard as the closest analogue; an account of the competing principles that fit all five earlier rulings and then diverge on ours; and, since the precedents may underdetermine the answer, an explicit statement of what rule we would be entrenching under each possible decision. Finally, please specify the further case, real or invented, that would most cleanly settle which principle the tribunal is actually using.
