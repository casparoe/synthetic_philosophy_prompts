Suppose a firm launches a customer-service product for Chinese-speaking customers that is, by deliberate design, Searle's Chinese Room: a monolingual English-speaking operator sits in a sealed room and answers Chinese-language queries by mechanically following a printed symbol-manipulation rulebook. The firm adds three features. First, operators are paid per completed query, with a speed bonus for same-shift throughput and a deduction for any answer a customer later marks unhelpful. Second, operators may write margin notes in the rulebook, and those notes become binding instructions for the next shift. Third, the firm markets the service as genuinely understanding Chinese because its output is indistinguishable from a native speaker's. Treat this as a mechanism to be analyzed rather than a thesis to endorse or reject: describe how the incentives shape what operators write in the margins and how the room's behavior drifts across shifts; identify where the system is gamed first; and say whether this drift toward better or worse outputs matters for Searle's original claim that the room possesses no understanding.
