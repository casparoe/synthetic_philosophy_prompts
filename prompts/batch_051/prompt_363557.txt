I'm in a political philosophy seminar on markets and morals, and we each have to defend a one-page position statement on the commodification cases, and I've been tinkering with mine for weeks and I still cant tell whether the whole thing hangs together. I'd rather find the cracks here than have someone in the room find them for me, so I'm hoping for a consistency check. Here's the set of commitments I'd currently defend. (a) As a default, an informed and genuinely voluntary transaction between adults shouldn't be banned just because the terms are bad for one side or because the good in question gives me the creeps; paternalism about how people use their own bodies and property needs a real justification, not a shudder. (b) A tightly regulated market in kidneys should be legal — screening, fixed price, follow-up care — and banning it mostly protects the squeamish while leaving poorer sellers worse off. (c) Price gouging after a hurricane is wrong and should stay illegal: the guy selling water for forty dollars a bottle is doing something genuinely blameworthy, even though his buyers line up and 'consent.' (d) Selling your vote should remain illegal, but only for the boring instrumental reason that it would let rich people buy elections and gut political equality; I don't buy any story about the inherent sacredness of the ballot. (e) I do think there's a real phenomenon in which putting a price on certain goods changes what they mean to us and crowds out the kind of regard that ought to govern them — paid friendship, bought jury verdicts, that sort of thing. (f) But I also think this commodification-corrupts line is over-applied constantly: paid childcare, paid surrogacy under decent regulation, and selling blood plasma all strike me as fine, and I suspect most objections to them are just gut-level unease about money touching intimate things. (g) Finally, I'm suspicious of any one master criterion — consent, coercion, corruption, externalities — that claims to sort all these cases, and I'm prepared to own the mess.

What I'd like is an audit of this set. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look that way and dissolve on inspection, and then rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. For each real tension, say what would have to give and roughly what it would cost me to fix it. Be brutal rather than polite. And please answer in continuous prose only — no headings, no bullet points, no bold or tables, just paragraphs the way you'd write an email.
