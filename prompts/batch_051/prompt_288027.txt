heres my working list of positions on markets and information, accumulated over a decade of covering the ideas side of economics. I honestly cant tell whether they hang together, and every time I try to audit them myself I just go in circles.

1. If two people have exactly the same information and are both reasoning carefully, I think they cannot rationally agree to disagree once each knows the other holds the opposite view. At least one of them has made a mistake somewhere.

2. If a stranger offers to sell me a stock at the going price and I have no independent reason to want it, I take the offer itself as evidence that they know or believe something I don't. Same for someone suddenly offering me a bet. Other things equal, I pass.

3. Yet I also think a price in an active, heavily traded market is, on average, the best available guess about the underlying value. If a prediction market says 70 percent on an event, I'd sooner trust that than my own offhand estimate.

4. And I think bubbles are real: markets can be badly wrong about an asset for years, not because some private information hasn't leaked out yet, but because of crowd psychology, leverage, and the stories people tell themselves.

5. I don't think point 2 applies to most actual trading, since a lot of it is hedging, rebalancing, or plain liquidity needs. A trade can be sensible for both sides even when one of them knows more, as long as the less informed one isn't pretending to beat the market.

6. I also think two reasonable people can start out with different prior probabilities, and that difference alone can justify persistent disagreement without either one of them being irrational.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair is. Also flag the pairs that only look like a conflict and dissolve once you sharpen the wording. And if I want to keep the largest consistent subset, which single commitment should I revise or drop, and how much would that cost me?
