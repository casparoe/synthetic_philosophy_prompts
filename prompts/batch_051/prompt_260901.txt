ok so I've been kicking around a little theory of dirty hands and I want it pressure-tested, not flattered. I think the usual talk about a moral remainder after a necessary wrong is too vague, so I've started calling it a remainder debt. The idea: when a leader does the thing that's right all-things-considered but wrong in itself (the usual drone strike that kills a few civilians to prevent a massacre), the wrongness doesn't just linger as a vague stain; it registers as a specific, unpaid debt to the people who got wronged. I also split the guilt that follows into performative guilt (public hand-wringing, interviews about how much it weighs on you) and reparative guilt, which is the same debt making itself felt as a demand to actually pay — compensation, memorials, changing the policy that created the dilemma. My three claims: (1) the moral remainder is nothing over and above the unpaid debt; (2) performative guilt has no moral worth except insofar as it pushes the leader to repay, and most of the public agonizing is basically self-indulgence; (3) hands can be washed — a leader who pays the debt in full, or does everything possible toward paying it, is no longer owed anything by the victims and shouldn't go on feeling guilty. I keep going back and forth on (1) and (3), but I want the whole thing checked: is it coherent, does it already exist under some name I'm forgetting, and where exactly does it break? Give me the five worst problems, each a sentence or two, ordered from most damaging to least, no intro or conclusion.
