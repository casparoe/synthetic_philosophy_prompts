I have been asked to write a short statement of where I stand in the suffering-focused ethics literature for a reading group, and I would like a consistency audit of the package before I circulate it. I have ended up with six commitments, each of which seems right to me on its own, but I have not yet worked through how they interact.

(1) Suffering is the only thing that is intrinsically bad. Happiness has no intrinsic value; it matters only instrumentally, insofar as it prevents, relieves, or distracts from suffering.

(2) I reject the conclusion that we ought painlessly to end all sentient life even if the world contained more suffering than happiness. Killing persons would wrong them, and a fact about aggregate value does not by itself settle what it is permissible to do.

(3) I accept an asymmetry about creation. A possible person's future suffering counts against bringing them into existence, but their future happiness does not count in favour. Correspondingly, the absence of suffering in a person who never exists is good, while the absence of happiness is not bad.

(4) Nevertheless, I do not think procreation is generally wrong. I think it can be permissible to create a child whom one knows will experience some non-severe suffering, provided the child can be expected to come to endorse their own life as a whole.

(5) I hold that extreme suffering is lexically prior to everything else: no amount of happiness, and no amount of mild suffering prevented, can justify inflicting extreme agony on someone who would not otherwise undergo it.

(6) I find the reason to relieve the suffering of beings who already exist considerably stronger than the reason to avoid creating beings who would suffer. I am unsure whether the latter is a genuine requirement at all, rather than a virtuous aspiration.

I do not know where the strain is, and I do not want to signal in advance which of these I would give up. Treat this as an audit of the set, not a debate about each claim taken singly. Please go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest fix would be, and what that fix costs; then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the set hang together. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and be specific about which commitment has to give under each repair. Keep the whole reply under 800 words.
