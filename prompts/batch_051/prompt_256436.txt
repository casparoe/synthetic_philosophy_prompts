I would like you to write a satirical piece in the form of a legally drafted contract: an Intertemporal Self-Binding Agreement between the Party of the Present and the Party of the Future, of roughly 700 words, in deadpan contractual legalese. The target should be the characteristic logic of Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment: a present agent claims authority to bind a future self, or a founding generation claims authority to bind future citizens, whose preferences and circumstances cannot be known in advance; the scheme then has to address the difficulty that whatever binds the future can in principle be unbound by the future. I want the contract to recast this logic as private law, complete with recitals explaining why a person who does not yet exist is deemed to have consented, a set of escalating entrenchment and penalty clauses intended to make the agreement self-enforcing, and at least one clause that attempts to bind the future not to unbind the clause that binds them not to unbind the agreement.

The humour should derive entirely from scrupulous fidelity to the concept. Hold the deadpan legal register throughout, resist any wink at the reader, and let the absurdity emerge from the regress of ever-higher enforcement clauses and from the gap between the document's serene self-assurance and its inability to secure its own conditions, rather than from surface jokes. Please write the piece in British English.
