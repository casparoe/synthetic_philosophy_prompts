I keep seeing the line between medical treatment and human enhancement get asserted rather than argued for, so before I form a view I want to know what a serious theory of that line would actually have to get right. Please write out the desiderata: the cases a theory must classify correctly or at least handle instructively, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the facts about bodies, disease, and development it cannot contradict. Make the cases concrete — for example, a student without ADHD taking a stimulant to study, a child at the low end of the normal growth range given growth hormone, LASIK versus a bionic eye that exceeds normal acuity, an amputee's prosthesis versus a blade for a runner with intact legs, a vaccine that prevents illness rather than restoring or exceeding normal function, antidepressants that lift a mood most people would call normal. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it. Then say which requirements are negotiable and which are not, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the standard candidates (roughly, views that define enhancement as anything beyond species-typical functioning, or as anything not aimed at restoring health, or as any non-medical improvement). Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring each theory in detail.
