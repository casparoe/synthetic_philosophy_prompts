I'm a retired physicist (instrumentation, mostly) who got into philosophy through the back door of measurement theory. A parallel keeps ocuring to me, probably because I watched the 2019 SI redefinition from close up. In the arithmetic books of the Elements, Euclid defines a unit relationally—it's whatever makes each existing thing be called one—and then builds number as a multitude of such units. Modern metrology now does something oddly similar: the kilogram is no longer defined by the platinum-iridium cylinder in Paris; instead we fix the numerical value of Planck's constant, and the unit becomes whatever makes that constant come out to the specified number. In both cases the unit is picked out by the job it does, not by pointing to a privileged specimen. Is there a real structural point here about role-based definitions, or am I just pattern-matching? Say what would have to be true on both sides for anything to transfer between them, and where the parallel breaks.
