I’ve been working through Schopenhauer’s account of music, especially the sections in *The World as Will and Idea* where he distinguishes music from the other arts. He argues that music is not an indirect copy of the Platonic Ideas, as sculpture or painting are, but a direct copy of the will itself; this is why it bypasses concepts and affects us with an immediacy the other arts cannot match. I find the theory systematically attractive: it explains why music does not depict any particular thing, why it seems to have a direct emotional intelligibility across otherwise different listeners, and why it can feel as though it reveals something more fundamental than the ordinary world of objects. But I cannot shake the intuition that this is too much metaphysics. When I attend closely to my own musical experience, much of what strikes me as profound seems to be the product of learned conventions, formal expectations, and cultural history. A modulation that moves me in one idiom leaves me indifferent in another; what I take to be an utterance of the will might, on reflection, be a highly trained response to style.

I want to know what would be involved in giving up either side. If Schopenhauer is right, what would have to be true about musical experience for my intuition of conventionality to be debunked? If the intuition is right, what exactly does Schopenhauer’s account get wrong, and can it be revised to keep its explanatory power without its metaphysical commitments? Or is there a reading on which direct expression of the will and cultural conventionality are compatible? Present the strongest version of both positions and give a negotiated verdict rather than simply choosing one.
