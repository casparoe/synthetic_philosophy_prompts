I am a high school student preparing a paper for a government class, and I would like help thinking through a case about the rule of law.

Suppose we say that a legal system meets the rule of law only if its official rules satisfy the following conditions: (1) they are public and knowable; (2) they apply prospectively; (3) they are general, rather than being merely ad hoc commands; (4) they are reasonably clear and mutually consistent; (5) they do not require impossible conduct; (6) official action actually conforms to the rules as publicly declared, rather than following secret or unpublished standards; and (7) the rules remain reasonably stable over time.

Now consider this case. A town has a municipal ordinance, enacted sixty years ago, making it a misdemeanor to hang laundry on a balcony that faces a public street. The ordinance has never been repealed. For at least forty years, no one has been cited under it. The town's current police chief did not know it existed until a resident asked about it last month. When asked, the city attorney said the office would not prosecute anyone under it. Yet the ordinance remains in the municipal code, and a resident could in principle be charged under it tomorrow. This situation, in which a law remains on the books but is no longer enforced, is sometimes called desuetude.

Here is my question: does this case qualify as a failure of the rule of law, as measured by the criteria above? Please go through the criteria one by one, say which criteria are satisfied and which are not, and be explicit about places where the criteria leave the matter unsettled. Where the answer is unclear, indicate whether the trouble lies with the criteria, with the facts of the case, or both, and say whether the criteria should be revised. In particular, address how this case differs from ordinary prosecutorial discretion, if it does.
