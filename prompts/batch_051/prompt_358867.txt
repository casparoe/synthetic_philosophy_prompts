I'm a novelist, and I'm working out the background logic for a world where moral luck has been deliberately engineered out of blame, and I want your help thinking through how it actually functions over time. The setup: about fifty years before the story opens, this society overhauled its criminal law, its schools, and its everyday blaming norms around a single principle — attempts and completions count for exactly the same, and reckless conduct counts for the same whether or not it happens to hurt anyone. Killing someone and trying but missing are the same offense; a drunk driver who hits a child and an identically drunk driver who gets lucky are treated the same in court, in the press, in the family, in the workplace. Harms matter only as evidence of what you risked, never as an independent reason to blame harder. The interesting wrinkle is that this isnt a weird legal technicality; its the taught, enforced norm throughout ordinary life, the thing kids get scolded for in the playground. But the underlying human gut responses havent gone anywhere — people still flinch when they hear a child died, still feel the difference between "she almost killed him" and "she killed him." That tension is the point.

So: predict for me, in explicit chains of reasoning, how this society looks fifty years in. Dont just give me the premise's PR version — tell me where it bends under pressure. In particular: what happens to the near-miss gut response; does it get retrained, driven underground, or licensed in some private space? What happens to punishment's need to express something about the victim, and does punishing attempters like completers start to feel principled or merely cruel? What happens to insurance premiums, surgical error, aviation, parenting, teen driving? Do people get more cautious, less cautious, or cautious in different places? Does "he has blood on his hands" survive, mutate, or get replaced by a new idiom for what you risked? And does the society's own self-understanding become more honest or more dishonest about the gap between its norms and its reflexes?

For each prediction I want the mechanism and your confidence. Tell me which effects follow almost automatically from incentives and institutions, which depend on assumptions about moral psychology that could go either way, and what would have to be true about people for the whole arrangement to collapse instead of stabilizing. Where are you least certain, and what fact about humans would falsify your best guesses?
