I'm a reporter who covers science and ideas, and I also sit on my outlet's editorial standards board. We've got a story in the pipeline that's splitting the board, and Ive been asked to draft the memo we'll vote on Thursday morning, so I need it to read like something I can actually send around.

One of our data reporters got hold of a leaked municipal database from a social credit pilot in China. It has roughly 400,000 residents, with names, home addresses, scores, and the specific things that lowered each score: unpaid traffic fines, late utility bills, online posts the system flagged as critical of local officials, a relative with a criminal record, a kid with too many school absences. The reporter wants to publish the whole thing as a searchable database. His argument is the standard American openness line—the public cant judge a system it cant inspect, public scrutiny is what keeps powerful institutions honest, and redacting the names would just reproduce the secrecy we claim to be exposing. The other half of the board says publishing identifiable details about ordinary people would leave them permanently exposed, that most of them never meaningfully consented to being in this system, and that control over information about yourself is a matter of dignity, not just avoiding concrete harm—the German data-protection instinct. They'd rather we describe the system without naming the people stuck in it, or not publish the data at all.

I lean toward publishing some version rather than spiking the story, but I'm not convinced the raw database is the right call. Please draft the memo I'm supposed to circulate. It should recommend one concrete course of action—publish the full dataset, publish a redacted or aggregated version, or dont publish—with the reasoning behind it, and it should engage seriously with the strongest objection to whatever we recommend. And because I'm going to paste it into our internal email thread, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables; just continuous prose like a normal email.
