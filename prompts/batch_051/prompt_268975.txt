Act as a professional philosopher working on health policy and distributive justice. Think carefully, and begin by reconstructing the two implicit theories before you evaluate them.

During surge conditions, many intensive-care triage protocols scored patients by their probability of surviving to discharge and their expected remaining life-years, with those years weighted by health-related quality of life; on that basis, a year lived with severe disability counted for less than a year in full health. Yet the same societies, when deciding whether to require a guardrail or an airbag, generally put a single monetary value on each statistical life saved and do not quality-adjust or disability-adjust that value. Reconstruct what each practice presupposes about the value of a life, about fairness between lives, and about the relevance of disability and age. Then say whether the two practices can be reconciled under one coherent theory of value, or whether one or both would have to change if the other's presuppositions were adopted. Distinguish carefully between what each practice strictly presupposes and what it merely fits well with.
