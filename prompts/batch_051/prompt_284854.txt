I've been playing a craft-and-settlements MMO called Vastmoor for about six years. Last winter our guild leader died, and around forty of us spent two months gathering materials to build a memorial hall for him in a quiet corner of a contested zone; several of us put in rare materials that would have gone for a few hundred dollars on the third-party sites, but we wanted it in the build. His younger sister, who never played, made an account just to visit the spot. Last month the company ran one of its periodic cleanups of unauthorized structures and deleted the memorial without warning. We petitioned for a restoration and offered to pay the normal fee the game charges when a build gets removed by accident, but the company pointed to the section of the terms of service on player content that reads:

"All Virtual Content, including player-created structures, is licensed to you, not sold, and has no monetary value. Virtual Content cannot be redeemed for any real-world currency, goods, or other thing of value. We may modify, remove, or delete any Virtual Content at any time, with or without notice, and without liability or compensation to you."

The company says this clause means they can delete the memorial and owe us nothing, and they've also refused to give the family a copy of the design files or schematic, relying on the same section. The family just wants to keep visiting it, or at least keep the plans.

I want a careful, non-legalese answer. Does the clause even govern the memorial? Walk through what each approach gives for both the restoration request and the design files: the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would likely have said if they'd been asked about a memorial built for a dead player, and what a reasonable player would have understood the clause to mean when they accepted it. Which approach should actually decide here, and why? And does it matter that the materials had a real third-party resale value even though the clause says Virtual Content has no monetary value?
