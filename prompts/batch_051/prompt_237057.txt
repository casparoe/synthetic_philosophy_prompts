I've got myself into a bit of a knot and I suspect I'm missing something obvious. People say the two square roots of −1 are indistinguishable — no equation built from the real numbers and the ordinary algebra operations can tell them apart, because complex conjugation swaps i and −i while fixing every real number and preserving all the field structure. Fine. But in the standard construction of the complex numbers, i is defined to be the ordered pair (0,1), with (a,b)(c,d) = (ac − bd, ad + bc). So i is a perfectly specific object, not some arbitrary pick. Doesn't the construction itself settle which of the two roots i is? And if it does, why do people also say the roots of a polynomial can't be canonically labelled, as if there were something wrong with labelling them at all? Surely every choice is as good as any other, so why not just stipulate a labelling for each polynomial once and for all and stop worrying about it? Is the word 'canonical' doing any real work here, or is it just fussiness? What would actually go wrong if someone went ahead and labelled the roots of every polynomial, however arbitrarily?
