I'm in a philosophy course on thought experiments this term, and we keep coming back to how intuition pumps persuade people even when they aren't really doing any argumentative work, and how a vivid detail can quietly carry the conclusion. I have a mechanism-y question. Please respond as someone who studies philosophical methodology, especially how thought experiments actually move people. Think carefully, and maybe brainstorm a few ways the thing could go before you settle on an analysis.

Suppose a respected general philosophy journal adopts the following policy. Any submission that uses a thought experiment to support a claim has to include a section called the reverse pump. In it, the author writes the strongest possible variant of their scenario that (a) stays as close as possible to the original setup and (b) pushes a typical reader toward the opposite intuitive verdict. Then the author states, in one sentence, wich feature of the original setup they think is doing the persuasive work, and argues that the reverse pump differs from the original mainly along that feature. Reviewers are told to reject a paper if the reverse pump is weak, or if the two scenarios differ in a whole bunch of unrelated ways and the author just gestures vaguely at one feature. On top of that, if a paper is accepted and a later reader survey finds the reverse pump produced the same verdict as the original in most respondents, the reviewer who handled it gets a small reputational penalty; if a reviewer rejects a paper because they caught exactly this problem, they get a small credit.

I'm not asking whether you think the rule is good. Treat it as a mechanism: what does it incentivize among authors, reviewers, and the editors who pick reviewers? How would authors game it, especially in the way they choose senarios or design reverse pumps that look strong while protecting their conclusion? What kinds of philosophical claims become easier or harder to publish under this rule? And are the failure modes fixable by tweaking the rule, or does the gaming just move somewhere else?
