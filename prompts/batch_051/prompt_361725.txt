You're a moral philosopher who works on animal ethics and global development. I want you to think carefully about how demanding veganism really is in different food environments. The five: (1) a salaried office worker in Berlin with cheap access to legumes, tofu, fortified plant milks, and B12 supplements; (2) an Inuit hunter on the Nunavut coast whose community mostly eats seal, fish, and caribou, and where store-bought plant food is exhorbitantly expensive and often spoiled; (3) a subsistence rice-farming household in Nepal that depends on a dairy buffalo for fat, protein, and manure; (4) a prison inmate whose facility's only realiably vegan food is white rice and boiled cabbage; (5) a low-income resident of a Rio favela whose cheapest complete protein is factory-farmed chicken, with beans available but cooking gas expensive. Start by brainstorming a quick rubric for what makes the obligation weaker or stronger, then give the tiers and scores, and say in a sentence why each lands where it does.
