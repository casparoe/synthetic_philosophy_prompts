While preparing a lecture on rational agency, I came upon what may be a structural parallel between two bodies of thought that rarely meet, and I would like to know whether it is deep or superficial.

On one side is the instrumental convergence thesis from contemporary AI alignment. Roughly, it holds that across a broad space of possible final goals, a small family of instrumental subgoals — self-preservation, goal-content integrity, resource acquisition, and cognitive enhancement among them — is likely to be selected anyway, because almost any goal is more difficult to pursue if the agent is destroyed, its preferences are rewritten, it lacks resources, or it cannot reason well. The thesis is not usually presented as a strict universal but as a convergence claim over many possible agents and environments.

On the other side is Kant's approach to practical philosophy. From the bare concept of a finite rational will, Kant attempts to derive constraints that are supposed to bind any rational being regardless of the particular ends it happens to adopt. This is the contrast between hypothetical imperatives, which hold only conditionally on an adopted end, and the categorical imperative, which is unconditional. Here too the movement seems to be from a very thin specification of agency to common practical commitments that obtain before any particular goal is chosen.

The parallel may be more than verbal: both projects appear to ask what a merely goal-directed but otherwise unspecified agent is forced to commit to. Yet the differences are conspicuous as well — Kant's derivation is normative and a priori, whereas instrumental convergence appears descriptive or probabilistic. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? Where exactly does the mapping hold, and where does it come apart? In particular, does instrumental convergence line up only with Kant's hypothetical imperatives, or does it generate pressure toward something like the categorical imperative? What does the analogy predict, and where does that prediction fail?
