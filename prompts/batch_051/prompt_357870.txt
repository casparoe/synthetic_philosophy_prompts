I'm not an academic — I'm a programmer who has been reading about AI safety in my spare time, and I've ended up in a long argument with a friend about a mechanism I want worked through rather than just judged. The problem I keep returning to is this: a lab that spends time and money on extra safety precautions is at a competitive disadvantage, because a less careful lab can move faster, spend less, and win the market or the race, while the careful lab looks slow and expensive. I've seen this called the alignment tax or the competitiveness constraint: the safe lab pays a tax, the unsafe lab doesn't, so competition seems to select against safety, and voluntary restraint is unstable.

Here is the mechanism, stated plainly. Any lab that wants to train a model above an agreed capability threshold must first deposit a substantial sum — say, a quarter of the estimated training cost — into escrow. The deposit is returned with interest only when an independent audit, paid for out of the interest, certifies two things: that the lab followed its own published safety plan during development and for a year after release, and that no model it deployed caused a specified class of serious harm despite the absence of reasonable precautions. If the audit finds cut corners, or a covered harm occurs and the lab cannot show it took the precautions any reasonable lab would have taken, the escrow is forfeited and split between the other signatories and a public safety-research fund. Labs that refuse to sign can still train, but signatories agree not to sell or rent compute to them, not to share datasets or safety techniques with them, and not to hire staff who worked on their frontier models.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a piece of moral advocacy. What does it actually incentivise, where does it get gamed first, what second-order effects does it set off, and can its failure modes be fixed without gutting it? Please commit to a definite assessment: would this scheme, on balance, change the race dynamics it is aimed at, and where would it end up?
