Something's been bugging me. If a friend tells me a deliberate lie, I'll be fuming and probably trust him less for ages. But if the same friend confidently reels off a bunch of stuff he half-remembers and never checked, I'll just roll my eyes and move on — even though the second person is probably feeding me way more false stuff over time. Frankfurt actually inverts this: he thinks the bullshitter is worse for truth than the liar, because the liar has to care about the truth to work against it, whereas the bullshitter misrepresents not just the facts but what he's even up to — he's indifferent to whether its true or false. Is our everyday asymmetry between lying and bullshitting defensible, or does Frankfurt show it's the wrong way round? And where do LLMs that make stuff up fit — they dont intend either, so is 'bullshit' even the right category, or is it just error, and does that answer change how harshly we should judge the output?
