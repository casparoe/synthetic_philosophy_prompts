He treated Popper's critique as decisive; I was less certain, not because I have a settled historicist position, but because we never agreed on what would count as success or failure for any view in this area. Before I read further on either side, I want the specification written out. Give me the desiderata that any adequate account of historical regularity must meet. The account may affirm historical laws, deny them outright, or occupy some intermediate position involving tendencies, recurrent mechanisms, or limited period-specific regularities; whatever it is, it must discharge these same demands. For each desideratum, argue from the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark the contested ones by saying whether they are genuine constraints or merely one side's insistence, and why. Then tell me which single requirement does the most work against the strongest version of historicism, and whether the full set of requirements leaves room for a chastened historicism or rules it out from the start.
