Design a runnable classroom game for an upper-level seminar of fourteen students (ninety minutes) that makes participants feel why Maxwell's demon cannot defeat the second law of thermodynamics once information is treated as physical. The game should have at least these roles -- a demon, a "gas" consisting of two species of particle, a heat bath or ledger that receives waste heat, and a record-keeper who tracks each bit the demon learns -- and should use two currencies: one for useful work gained by sorting hot from cold particles, and one for the bits of memory the demon is holding. Arrange the rules and payoffs so that the demon appears to be winning while memory is costless, and so that the group discovers the catch only when the demon must reset its memory before starting a second cycle: every stored bit must be erased by paying a unit of heat to the bath, the total erasure cost cancels the sorting gain, and the participants work out where the free lunch went before the facilitator names it. Include setup and materials, exact rules and payoffs, a timeline with briefing, play, and debrief, and a debrief that maps the phases of play onto Szilard's engine, Landauer's principle, and Bennett's point that the demon must be able to erase its records. Say what predictably goes wrong -- especially participants treating the record-keeper's bits as costless scratch paper -- and how the facilitator should handle it; and be frank that the simulation cannot reproduce the exact quantitative bound of kT ln 2 per erased bit, nor the distinction between logically irreversible erasure and thermodynamically reversible erasure, which the facilitator must supply briefly at the end.
