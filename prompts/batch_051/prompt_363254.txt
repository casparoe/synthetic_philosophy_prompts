I serve on the council of a small Protestant congregation whose church order contains a paragraph on baptism, drafted in 1962. It reads:

"Baptism is the sign and seal of God's covenant promise, to be administered once to those who belong to the covenant, whether by birth or by profession of faith. It is not to be repeated, because baptism rests on God's promise and not on the faith or worthiness of the person baptized. Therefore no one who has received the water with the triune name shall be presented for a second baptism."

A case has come before us. A woman in her thirties was baptized at six months old by her grandmother in a hospital sink while the child was dangerously ill and the grandmother feared the child might die unbaptized. The grandmother was a member of this congregation, but she was not a minister, acted without the council's knowledge, and used water and the trinitarian formula from the order of service. The event was never entered in the church register. The child's parents were not members of any church, had not asked for the baptism, and did not know of it beforehand. The child recovered, was raised outside the church, and made no profession of faith until last month, when she joined us as a believing adult. She now asks to be baptized, saying that what happened in the hospital was not a baptism she ever owned, believed, or consented to.

The paragraph says both that baptism is to be administered once to members of the covenant by birth or profession, and that no one who has received the water with the triune name is to be baptized a second time. It was plainly written to prevent the rebaptism of people who had been baptized as infants in the church; it did not foresee a private, lay-administered, unrecorded emergency baptism outside the visible church.

I want a ruling rather than a pastoral compromise. Under this paragraph, should we treat her as already baptized and refuse a second baptism, or may we baptize her? Argue it (a) from the words alone, (b) from the purpose of the clause, (c) by asking what the 1962 drafters would have said about a layperson's unrecorded emergency baptism, and (d) by asking what a reasonable reader of the church order would take the clause to mean. Then say which of these approaches should govern the case and why, and say what that choice reveals about whether the paragraph treats baptism as a bare symbol, an effectual seal that depends on the church's own administration, or something between. Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
