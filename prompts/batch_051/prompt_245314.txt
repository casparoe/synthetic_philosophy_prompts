Recently I was arguing about Germany's church tax — the surcharge that the state collects from registered members of the main churches and passes on to those churches — and my opponent ended the argument by quoting Matthew 22:21: "Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's." His conclusion was that a state which acts as a tax collector for churches has overstepped its proper secular role, so the church tax should be abolished. I suspect the saying does not do the work he needs, but I cannot say precisely what it is claiming. Give me exactly five distinct precise principles this saying might be used to express about the relation between secular and religious authority. For each reading, in one or two sentences, say whether it is plausible, whether it is trivial or substantive, and whether it actually supports the conclusion that a state-collected church tax such as Germany's should be abolished. Please write the five readings as a numbered list, in British English, with no introduction or conclusion.
