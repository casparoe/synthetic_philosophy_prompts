I retired a few years ago from a long run in molecular biology, where the bread and butter of my lab was directed evolution: you take a protein that does its job sort of okay, mutate a bunch of residues, screen thousands of variants for the one that's a bit better, and repeat. Lately I've gotten into philosophy as a hobby, and I keep thinking about Hofstadter's picture of creativity as variation on a theme — the idea that creative people mostly take some existing structure and twiddle its parameters, exploring the space of small variations around a template and keeping what's interesting.

The parallel hit me alot harder than I expected. A parent protein is a theme; the residues are the knobs; the screen is the selector. Both creativity and directed evolution look like local search in a high-dimensional space around a starting point, with an evaluation function doing the keeping and discarding. But there's an obvious disanalogy too: a human creator can step back and change the knobs themselves, or throw out the whole representation and start over, while my old experiments just hummed along in whatever sequence space I happened to define. And directed evolution is blind where creativity presumably isn't.

So is this a deep identity of structure, a loose analogy, or am I just pattern-matching? Spell out the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true on each side for the analogy to carry real weight, and give me one thing the 'creativity is directed evolution' picture predicts that turns out false.
