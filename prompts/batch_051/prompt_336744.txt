I've been reading bits of Wang Yangming in translation, mainly his Chuanxilu, and I keep getting stuck on two things he says about the unity of knowledge and action. At one point he says something like: knowing is the beginning of acting, and acting is the completion of knowing. That sounds pretty sensible to me — it's one process with two moments, and you don't really know something until you've followed through on it.

But then elsewhere he says that the very moment a thought stirs, it's already action. Like, if a thought toward doing good or a bad desire arises, that is already the act itself. And that seems to collapse the two moments he separated earlier. If the thought is already action, what is left for the so-called completion? And if the completion matters, then the thought can't have been the action.

I'm trying to figure out whether these two claims can be reconciled without making one of them a loose way of speaking. Is he using action in two different senses, or knowledge in two different senses, or is there a distinction between a thought and an intention that I'm missing? And how would his answer work on a concrete case: a person who knows they ought to apologize, fully intends to apologize, but never actually does it — did they ever really know in Wang Yangming's sense? I'd like a reading that gives the most honest version of his actual view, not just a clever way to make him consistent.
