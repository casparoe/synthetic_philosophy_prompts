I'm a retired physicist, new-ish to philosophy (a couple years in a reading group), and I'm writing up a one-paragraph "this is what I believe about the mind" statement to circulate. Beforehand I'd like a consistency check on the set. I can feel something's off but I can't articulate it. Here are the commitments:

(a) Physicalism is true: all facts, including facts about consciousness, are either physical facts or fully determined by physical facts.
(b) "Physical" should be understood by reference to the posits and properties of current physics—particles, fields, interactions, spacetime. If you define it as "whatever an ideal future physics says," physicalism stops saying anything definite or testable.
(c) But I'm also sure current physics is incomplete. Dark matter, quantum gravity, the measurement problem: I'd be shocked if fundamental physics in a century looks like today's.
(d) I take physicalism to be an empirical claim, not a definitional truth; there should be possible observations that would count against it.
(e) I believe conscious experience is real and physical, even though current physics has no story about how experience appears.
(f) I'm not sure physicalism needs a precise definition of "physical"; the rough contrast with the supernatural (and with irreducibly mental stuff) has been good enough for the debate.

Which of these actually pull against each other, and which only seem to? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what would have to give and roughly how costly that is. Don't soften it: I'd rather find out one of these has to go then be told I'm coherent when I'm not. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning; no preamble and don't restate the question.
