Two people in a study group I sit in got stuck on the question of why there is something rather than nothing, and neither would give ground. The first insisted the question is perfectly meaningful: for any fact you can ask why it is so, so you can ask why it is a fact that anything exists at all, and no scientific answer could satisfy, because science only ever explains one bit of reality by pointing to another bit. The second said the question only looks meaningful because of grammar. "Nothing" is not a thing and not a possible way things could have been; it is just the word for the absence of everything. Without a genuine alternative, there is no contrast to explain, and a question with no contrast has no content.

The first came back that the alternative is easy to state: a world containing none of the contingent things this world contains, with no particles, no fields, no space, and no time. That is the nothing the question asks about, and it is a real alternative. The second replied that this still cheats, because the description itself leaves something in place: at minimum, there would still be truths about what could have been, or the very possibility of such an empty world, and those are not nothing. The first answered that the cheating charge cuts both ways: if the emptiest alternative is always ruled out on the grounds that describing it leaves something in place, then the question is made unaskable by definition, which is a way of winning without doing any work. The second then shifted. Suppose the question is meaningful, he said. It may still rest on the assumption that everything needs an explanation, and that assumption is optional. Why not say the universe is simply the brute fact at the bottom of every explanation and be done with it? The first said explanation may have to stop somewhere, but it should stop at something that could not have been otherwise, not at the entire inventory of contingent things. There they stuck.

Adjudicate this. Go person by person and point by point: who is right about which specific claims, where each overreaches, and what both of them are missing.
