I run a philosophy discussion group at a university — about 12 people, mixed undergrads and a couple of grad students, one 75-minute session. Design a runnable game that makes them feel the pull and the discomfort of moral trade: the idea that different people assign wildly different 'exchange rates' to different moral goods, so two parties can each take a step the other regards as wrong and still honestly say the deal leaves the world better by their own lights. Don't start with a lecture; I want the trades to emerge from the rules. Give each player a hidden exchange rate (one small lie = a lot of malaria nets; one broken promise = a lot of animal welfare; that kind of thing) and let them negotiate swaps of moral concessions for points or for gains in causes they care about. Only afterward should they realize that the same deal looked like selling out or like principled compromise depending on which side of it you were on. Include rules and materials, a no-real-money points scheme, a timeline for 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the concept — plus what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and which feature of the idea the game cant reproduce and I'll have to supply myself.
