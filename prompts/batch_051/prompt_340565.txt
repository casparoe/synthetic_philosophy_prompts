I'm in an undergrad philosophy of law course and we've spent the past couple weeks on naked statistical evidence — you know, the blue bus and gatecrasher cases. I'm writing my final paper on whether there's a principled line between "naked statistical" evidence and "individualized" evidence, the kind that can actually support proof beyond a reasonable doubt. My paper proposes a three-part test, and I want to pressure-test it against a case I made up (well, I think it's realistic). I dont want any case law or citations — just the conceptual analysis, since this is for a philosophy class.

Here's my tentative test. A piece of evidence is individualized enough to support proof beyond a reasonable doubt only if:

(1) Trace condition: the evidence is a trace produced by the crime (or by the culprit's actions), not merely a fact about some group the defendant happens to belong to.
(2) Selection condition: the evidence would be about as probative if the defendant had been identified through ordinary investigation — a tip, a witness, a license plate — as it is when he's identified by a database search. If its strength depends on how he was found, it flunks.
(3) Non-random-draw condition: the inference can't work by treating the defendant as a random draw from the class of people searched or tested.

Now the case. A convenience store is robbed and the robber leaves a wool glove behind. Touch DNA from inside the glove gives a partial, low-template profile. The lab says the profile would be expected in about 1 person in 2 million in the general population. But the police found the defendant only because they ran the profile through a state database of about 1.8 million prior arrestees and convicts, and his was the only hit. There's no other evidence linking him to the store; his alibi is weak but not ridiculous. The defense expert says that with a database that big you'd expect close to one random hit anyway, so a single hit is kind of what the search was guaranteed to produce even if he never touched the glove.

So: is that DNA hit individualized under my three-part test, or is it naked statistical evidence? Go through the criteria one by one, being honest about where they underdetermine the answer, and then tell me whether the case shows my criteria need revising. And if you could write the whole answer in American English, that'd be great.
