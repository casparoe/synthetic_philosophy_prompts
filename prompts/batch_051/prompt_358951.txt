ok so in my philosophy club we got into this argument about selling body parts and I cant stop thinking about it. Theres a plasma donation center like two blocks from my school that pays $50 a visit, and basically everyone I know has done it for gas money, nobody thinks its weird. But if someone tried to sell a kidney — even to a relative, even to a stranger who would die without it — almost everyone says thats obviously wrong and its illegal basically everywhere. But why? In both cases a person is giving part of their body to medicine for cash, and someones life gets saved. I know the obvious answers: plasma grows back and a kidney doesnt, and kidney surgery is a lot riskier. But lots of normal jobs are risky and can leave you permanently hurt — logging, crab fishing, roofing, whatever — and we let people get paid for those and dont call it disgusting. And selling eggs is legal and that means weeks of hormones and a surgical retrieval, so "invasive" cant be the line either.

So what is the actual principle that makes getting paid for plasma feel normal and getting paid for a kidney feel gross? I want the strongest version of that principle you can come up with, and then I want you to test it against nearby cases: selling bone marrow, selling a lobe of your liver (which mostly grows back), selling a kidney to your brother vs to a stranger, stuff like that. And be honest at the end: does the asymmetry hold up, or should we either ban paying for plasma or allow paying for kidneys?
