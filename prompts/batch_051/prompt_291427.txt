I'm a software engineer and I only read philosophy in a book-club kind of way, so I might be mangling Whitehead. I was reading about his rhythm-of-education idea: learning should move through romance (excited first exposure, building a rough mental map), then precision (disciplined work on details and rules), then generalization (putting it together and applying it freely). And it hit me that this is basically how I ramp up on a new codebase. At first I skim the repo, jump around, get a fuzzy big picture and some enthusiasm (romance). Then I force myself to read the actual code paths, tests, configs, and conventions in detail, which is boring but is the only part where I get exact (precision). Then eventually I can make changes and design things without checking every detail, like the knowledge went underground and became intuition (generalization). It even loops at different scales — whole project, one module, one bug. And if I'm remembering right, he also says the outer stages are free and the middle one is disciplined, which matches: I start by browsing freely, then I have to discipline myself to read precisely, and only later does the freedom come back as something earned.

I know every analogy between learning a subject and learning a codebase feels true at first, so I want a sanity check. Is there a real structural parallel here, or am I pattern-matching on the word learning? A few specific things I can't tell: does Whitehead mean the stages as a fixed order in time, or as a cycle that recurs, because my onboarding is driven more by whatever ticket lands on me than by readiness. Does his picture depend on there being a teacher who withholds precision until the student is ready? I don't have that, I just flail around. And I suspect I could tell the exact same story about learning to cook or learning a board game, so maybe its just what skill acquisition feels like in general and neither Whitehead nor codebases are special. If the parallel breaks, where exactly does it break, and does that tell me anything about either side?
