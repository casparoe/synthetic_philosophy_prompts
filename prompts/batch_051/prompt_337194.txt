A colleague in my department does philosophy of mind and has been running a little exercise for a paper. She makes up a fact sheet for a hypothetical organism — brain mass, number of pallial (cortex-equivalent) neurons, and a pass/fail score on a behavioral flexibility battery — and then she has to tick whether she'd extend welfare protection to it. Her track record so far: 1,400 g and 16 billion neurons, passes: yes. 4,800 g and 5.6 billion, passes: yes. 90 g and 500 million, passes: yes. 12 g and 1.5 billion, passes: yes. 2 g and 30 million, fails: no. Half a gram and 1 million, fails: no. She wont tell me which column is doing the work, and I doubt the record decides it anyway. Next week's sheet is a 600-gram brain with 5 billion pallial neurons, but the creature fails every single item on the flexibility battery. Predict how she'll tick it, lay out the rules that fit her track record, say what each rule predicts for that sheet, give me your best guess and why, and tell me which further fact sheet would cleanly separate the remaining rules. Also, separate what's actually settled among people who work on moral status and comparative cognition from what's a live argument, and dont present a contested position as if it were the consensus. Exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no introduction, no conclusion.
