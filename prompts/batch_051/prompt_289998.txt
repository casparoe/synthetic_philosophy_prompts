I sit on my city's public safety committee, and we have to decide on a proposed ordinance before it goes to the full council. The draft makes it a misdemeaner, punishable by fine and up to 30 days in jail, for any person to "loiter, hang around, or otherwise remain in a public place in a manner that reasonably alarms, disturbs, or annoys another person." The city attorney has warned us this could be void for vagueness. The mayor and the police chief want it passed anyway: they say officers need the flexibilty to handle complaints about encampments, aggressive panhandling, and rowdy crowds, and that jurors will mostly get what it means. The public defender says the language invites arbitrary enforcement against homeless people and protesters, and that nobody can know in advance what's illegal.

Lately I've been reading about rules versus standards, and I'm of two minds. A rule listing every prohibited act would be too brittle, but a purely open-ended standard seems to be exactly what the attorney is worried about. Would adding a list of examples ("including, but not limited to...") fix it? What about requiring proof that the person acted with intent to alarm or disturb? Or does that just dress the vagueness up?

I need to circulate a memo to the committee before our Thursday meeting, and I'd rather have a real recommendation than walk in with vibes. Draft me something that ends with a clear bottom line: adopt as drafted, amend with specific language, or reject. It should give reasons, and it should give the strongest argument for the losing side its due rather than knocking it down as a straw man. Also work in two or three practical questions the committee can use to test future ordinances for vagueness. American English, please.
