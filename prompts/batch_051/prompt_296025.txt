I’m giving a seminar paper next month to my department on disjunctivism about perceptual experience, specifically against the argument from illusion. My thesis is about the stick-in-water case: the fact that it looks like a bent stick when it isn't is often taken to show that what you're immediately aware of is a non-physical sense-datum, and hence that veridical and illusory experiences share a common fundamental kind. I want to argue that this inference only works if you already buy a certain picture of perceptual awareness, and that the subjective indistinguishability of veridical and hallucinatory cases is better read as a negative epistemic condition—you can't tell them apart—rather than a positive sameness of experience type. The audience is my department: mostly epistemologists and philosophers of mind, a couple of representationalists, one older sense-datum sympathizer, and several sharp grad students who've read the SEP entry but not much more.
