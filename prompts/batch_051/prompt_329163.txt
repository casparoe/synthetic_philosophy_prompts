Here are the criteria I was taught for distinguishing a control measure from an alignment measure in AI safety. A safety measure counts as a control measure rather than an alignment measure just in case: (1) its rationale explicitly allows that the model may be pursuing goals contrary to the operator's; (2) it operates on the model's situation — its inputs, outputs, permissions, or environment — rather than on the model's objective function, training signal, or motivational make-up; (3) its protective value is independent of the model's motives, in the sense that it would remain useful on the supposition that the model is covertly misaligned; and (4) its aim is to keep bad behavior from causing harm by detecting, blocking, or overriding it, rather than to make bad behavior not arise from the model's own preferences in the first place.

Now consider a deployment protocol I will call symmetric auditing. A model is allowed to propose an action — executing code, sending a message, making a payment — but before any action is carried out, the proposal is passed to a second, independently instantiated copy of the same model: same weights, same conversation history, same description of the environment, but with a system prompt that casts it as an auditor rather than as an actor. The auditor copy is asked: "Would you yourself take this action in this situation?" If it answers no, the action is vetoed. The model's weights, training, and preferences are left untouched; the protocol is added purely out of distrust of the model.
