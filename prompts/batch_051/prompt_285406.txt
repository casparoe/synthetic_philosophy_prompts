I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the philosophy of machine learning, and I am writing the final exam. I would like one essay question, to be answered in 75 minutes and worth 20 marks, on the legibility of AI outputs — specifically, on proposals to train models to be understandable rather than merely correct. The question should push students past the familiar transparency-versus-accuracy slogan and force them to confront a specific tension: a model constrained to produce a checkable rationale may be less correct than an opaque model trained on the same data, yet the opaque model's correctness is something we may have no independent way of vouching for. The question should require students to distinguish training for legibility from post hoc explanation, to distinguish the correctness of an output from our warrant for accepting it, and to give at least one concrete mechanism by which a legibility objective could improve rather than reduce correctness. Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain.
