Sometimes it's stated as a fact about truth: the posterior will concentrate around the true parameter value as the sample size grows. And then there's a result I've seen referenced (I think due to Blackwell and Dubins?) that I can't quite place, which seems to be about predictive distributions merging rather than about the posterior over parameters at all. I get the flavor of it, but I don't actually understand which of these claims needs which assumptions.

Is one claim stronger than the other, or are they just about different things? And what are the realistic ways the slogan "the prior washes out" fails even with an infinite stream of data? I think non-identifiability is one way, and maybe something about the true distribution being outside the prior's support, but I'd like to know whether those are the same kind of failure or genuinely different ones.
