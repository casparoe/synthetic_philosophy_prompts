I'm in a seminar on statehood and recognition and we've spent the whole term on the declaratory vs. constitutive theory debate without my professor ever actually saying which side she's on. She just gives us one-line verdicts and moves on. So far the board looks like this: the Vatican counts as a state, South Sudan counts, Palestine counts, Taiwan does not, Somaliland does not, and the Islamic State at the height of its territorial control did not. The weird thing is she insists shes applying one consistent criterion, not just vibes, and she doesn't care about which side has the better moral claim.

I'm pretty sure the same set of verdicts could come out of at least two different rules: one where what matters is whether the entity is inside the UN system in some formal way, and one where what matters is whether a very large share of the world's states recognize it as a state. Next week she's going to put Kosovo on the spot: it declared independence in 2008, governs itself completely, is recognized by a bit over half of the UN's membership, but it has never been admitted to the UN itself. What would each of those two rules say about Kosovo, which of her earlier cases is the closest analogy, and is there a reason to think she's running one rule rather than the other? And if Kosovo doesn't quite settle which one she's running, what further case about statehood would force a clean choice between them?
