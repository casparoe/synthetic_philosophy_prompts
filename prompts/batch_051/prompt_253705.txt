Ok so I keep running into the claim that culture literally evolves by natural selection — memes are the genes of culture, ideas compete for space in human minds the way alleles compete in a gene pool, and the stuff that spreads is just the stuff that's good at spreading. The conclusion people want from this is that you can do a full Darwinian science of culture: selection, drift, fitness, all of it, not just as metaphor but as the real machinery. I want to know how far that analogy actually holds, because I can't tell whether I'm being stubborn or whether the whole thing quietly falls apart once you push on it.

Can you do the audit? First give me the correspondence table: what's supposed to play the role of the gene, the phenotype, the organism, the environment, the generation, the mutation. Then walk through where it breaks. Off the top of my head I'm worried about: cultural transmission is not particulate (ideas blend, get edited, and recombine every time they're passed on), copying fidelity is all over the place, inheritance is horizontal and Lamarckian (people deliberately improve what they pass on), and it's not obvious what the unit of selection even is — the idea itself, the person who has it, the group? Also say what the gene picture sneaks in that culture might not have, like a clean genotype/phenotype split or a clear line between replicator and vehicle, and whether those imports are load-bearing or just cosmetic. Then give me the verdict: does the Darwinian-science-of-culture conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing the work the whole time? And if the meme-as-gene version is busted, is there a better way to keep the Darwinian part without pretending culture is made of discrete copying units?
