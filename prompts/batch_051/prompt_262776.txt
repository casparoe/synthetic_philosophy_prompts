I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun to work through the philosophical literature on free will, and I keep noticing that quantum indeterminism is treated as though it automatically counted in favor of freedom. I would like you to construct a thought experiment to order, one that separates two ingredients that often get run together.

Build me a scenario in which event-causal libertarianism and agent-causal libertarianism give opposite verdicts about the same action. By event-causal libertarianism I mean the view that an undetermined choice can be free, and the agent can be morally responsible for it, when the choice is caused by the agent's prior reasons, values, and character, provided those antecedents leave open more than one possible action. By agent-causal libertarianism I mean the view that freedom requires the agent, as a continuing substance and not merely a chain of psychological events, to cause the choice; an undetermined event in the agent's own mental machinery is not sufficient and may even undercut responsibility.

Specifications. The case should be ordinary and realistic: a workplace or family setting, no neurosurgical devices, no exotic physics beyond the generic assumption that some neural events are undetermined. The indeterminism must occur inside the agent's nervous system after deliberation has narrowed the options to two, both of which are genuinely supported by the settled character, and it must break the tie between them. The stakes should be ordinary but real: a lapse in honesty, a kept or broken promise, a small moral failure or success. The agent must experience the episode as an ordinary act of choosing, not as a twitch, a seizure, or the operation of an implanted device. Keep the scenario to one paragraph.

Then do the work of showing that the case meets the specification. State the choice, the two candidate actions, the exact point at which the process becomes undetermined, and why the agent's antecedent psychology underdetermines the outcome. Explain why the event-causal libertarian should judge the agent responsible for the resulting act, and why the agent-causal libertarian should not; make explicit which ingredient is doing the work in each verdict, alternative possibilities or sourcehood. Identify the single feature of the case that is carrying the disagreement, and say what would happen if that feature were removed—for instance, if the indeterminism occurred earlier, in the formation of the agent's reasons, so that the final choice followed deterministically from those reasons. Conclude with the strongest objection an agent-causal libertarian could make to the case, and whether that objection is independent of the theory or merely restates it.

I want this for a discussion of whether quantum randomness helps free will. The point is to isolate the contribution of genuine alternative possibilities from the contribution of the agent's authorship; a case that stipulates the two verdicts rather than generating them from the two theories would not serve that purpose.
