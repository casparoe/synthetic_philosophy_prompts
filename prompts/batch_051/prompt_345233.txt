My cousin is spending a year in Cambodia and just got offered a job at a garment factory that makes clothes for brands you'd definitely recognize. The pay is about $190 a month, which sounds terrible to me, but its actually more than she'd make doing farm work or waitressing there (maybe $130), and she has credit card debt she wants to pay off. The hours are long — six days a week, ten hours a day — and from what she can tell the factory could double wages and it would barely nudge the retail price of a shirt. She asked me if taking the job would mean she's letting herself be exploited, and whether she should refuse it on principle. A couple of the Cambodian workers shes befriended think a westerner refusing or making a fuss would just make things worse, because these jobs are the best option around; others say the only reason the alternatives are so bad is that the factories have so much power, and the right response is to boycott until wages rise. I told her I'd get back to her, but I honestly think I don't know. Should she take the job? Is she being exploited even though she'd be better off with it than without it? And is the right move for someone like me, who buys this stuff, to boycott or to keep buying?
