I've been trying to pin down what it would even mean to say one country 'started' a conflict, because the Cold War question keeps bugging me. My working criteria for saying that a country X started a conflict are these: (a) X was the first to use organized military force; (b) X was first to openly announce an aim of overturning the postwar order; (c) X took the step that made large-scale conflict close to inevitable; (d) X was first to break a binding agreement about the relevant region. Now take the Soviet blockade of West Berlin in 1948. The USSR cut off surface access to the Western sectors rather than opening fire, so (a) looks to fail, and (b) is at best murky; but you could argue (c) and (d) hold, since the blockade made the division of Europe concrete and arguably broke the four-power understanding about occupied Germany. Does the blockade qualify as the start of the Cold War by my criteria? And if the criteria cant return a clean verdict on what is at least a widely cited candidate for the start, should I take that as evidence that the criteria are bad, or that 'who started the Cold War' is the wrong question to ask?
