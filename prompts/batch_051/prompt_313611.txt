I am the head of policy at a mid-sized online forum that hosts text discussion and short video, with users in roughly 120 countries. Our trust and safety team moderates almost everything by United States law because our company and servers are American, but outside counsel keeps telling me this default is not defensible when a post is written in another language, aimed mainly at another country, or unlawful somewhere but lawful here. Our analysts are not lawyers, they see dozens of these cases each day, and every call has real consequences: leaving content up can expose us to fines or blocking orders in a foreign market, while taking it down can mean silencing speech that is lawful where it occurred. When a case is too hard for the analysts, it goes to a shift lead, who can bring a batch to counsel once a week, so the procedure should pass only genuinely contested cases up rather than everything. I want a reusable decision procedure that an analyst can run in roughly half an hour per case to determine which country's or countries' speech laws should govern a piece of user-generated content when the speaker, the audience, the platform, and the servers sit in different jurisdictions. Ground the procedure in the general principles behind extraterritorial jurisdiction, effects, targeting, and comity, but keep it operational: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Mark the places where the procedure will misfire or where a half-hour answer is impossible, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle.

Then run the procedure on three cases. First, a user in Berlin posts from Berlin in German, and the post is lawful in the United States but unlawful under German law and appears aimed mainly at a German audience. Second, a public figure in Singapore complains about an English-language post by a user in the United States that is read worldwide. Third, choose a case of your own that shows the procedure producing a result my team will dislike, and explain why that uncomfortable result is the cost of having a rule rather than deciding ad hoc. You need not cite cases, statutes, or academic works; I will have counsel check the legal specifics. Please write the whole thing as continuous prose only, the way you would write me a careful internal memo: no headings, bullet points, numbered lists, bold text, or tables.
