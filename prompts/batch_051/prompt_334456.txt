Please argue that an individual's decision to keep purchasing factory-farmed meat can be morally criticizable even if we grant, for the sake of argument, that the expected marginal causal impact of that individual's purchases on total production is effectively zero. Take it as given that the practice is seriously harmful in aggregate; the only disputed question is whether an individual purchaser is morally implicated. The strongest version of this argument should not rest on the generic universalization move (what if everyone did that?), since the individual cannot bring about the cooperation of others; it should instead confront the aggregation worry directly. If each single act is causally nil in isolation, the collective harm begins to look like a sum of nil effects, which suggests that the model of isolated acts is what ought to be rejected. In defending the position, specify whether the appropriate unit of moral assessment is the single purchase, a standing purchasing policy, or participation in the practice, and explain how that choice navigates the constraints above.
