I run a monthly discussion group for people who read the rationalist blogs and forums, and I want to make the money-pump argument felt rather than merely summarized. Please design a 90-minute, in-person activity for 12–20 adults, with small real stakes (I can float about $50 so no one loses more than a few dollars), in which participants encounter the force of the claim that a certain preference structure is irrational because a counterparty can extract money from them one small, locally attractive step at a time. The key constraint is that the problem should emerge from the roles, rules, and payoffs before anyone names it; this should not be a lecture with a live illustration. Give me roles or rules, a phased timeline, scoring, and a debrief that maps particular moments in the play onto the structure of the argument: where the cycle lives, whether a switching fee is doing essential work or is only cosmetic, and what the counterparty must do for extraction to occur. I would also like the design to keep two things distinct that blog discussions often run together — intransitive preferences held all at once, and preferences that shift over time when nothing relevant has changed — and to say which one the exercise actually exploits and how a rerun could target the other. Say what will predictably fail in practice (for example, no clean cycles emerging, players refusing the final trade that closes the loop, or players colluding to avoid being pumped) and how the debrief should handle each failure. Finally, be candid about which feature of the argument the activity cannot reproduce — such as the inference from having an exploitable disposition to the conclusion that some particular choice in the sequence was the mistake — and what the facilitator should supply explicitly afterward.
