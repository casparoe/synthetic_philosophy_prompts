I'm writing a paper for my philosophy class on Hume's argument about miracles, and I want to avoid just restating the famous conclusion; I want to force the weighing of evidence out into the open for a concrete case. Suppose an otherwise honest friend tells me that she personally witnessed a man's fully amputated hand regrow overnight after a prayer service. Please give your own credence that the event occurred roughly as she describes it, and decompose that number into the components you think bear weight: for example, your prior probability that such an event could happen given only background knowledge, the probability that she would report it if it did happen, the probability that she would report it if it did not (through mistake, exaggeration, hallucination, or fraud), and the evidential value of any additional witnesses or independent confirmation. Assign a rough probability to each component, show how they combine to your overall credence, and state which component your final number is most sensitive to. Then say what evidence about the case would move your number most, and whether your conclusion lands closer to Hume's position that no testimony can establish a miracle or to the position that testimony could in principle do so.
