Ever since I read up on WannaCry I keep coming back to that 2017 mess. As I understand it, the ransomware spread machine-to-machine, and a security researcher noticed it was checking an unregistered domain before infecting a machine. He registered the domain on a hunch, and that turned out to be a kill switch: it stopped the original strain cold, worldwide. One guy did that alone, with no permission and no time to ask. Separately, I've come across this idea called the unilateralist's curse — when lots of people can each act unilaterally, and each one only has to think 'this is probably worth doing' for someone to pull the trigger, the group effectively acts on the judgment of its most optimistic member, so you get actions the group as a whole never would of agreed to. Analyze the WannaCry kill switch through that lens. Did the curse apply here, and if so who counts as the group? Was the researcher the optimistic outlier or just the first competent person to notice the domain? And what does the case do to the theory: does it show the curse is too pessimistic, or that it only has teeth when the unilateral act is risky in a way this one wasn't, or that 'reversibility' and 'upside' are doing hidden work the theory never spells out? I'm a software engineer so the infosec part I get; I'm after the philosophical unpacking.
