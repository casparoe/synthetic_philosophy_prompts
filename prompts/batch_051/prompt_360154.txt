I'm a safety reviewer at an AI lab that builds agents which can modify or rewrite their own reasoning code, or train/choose a successor model that takes over their decision-making. About every two to three weeks a team drops a design doc on my desk and I have to write a review saying whether there's a self-trust problem severe enough that the plan shouldn't go forward. My review goes to the head of safety and a small internal board; neither of them are proof theorists, and I'm not realy one either. I have two to three hours per review, and the design docs are usually prose, diagrams, and a little pseudocode. They almost never come with actual formal proofs, and when they do there's usually at least one step that's hand-waved, so the procedure can't depend on having a proof in hand.

The principle I keep trying to apply is the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents. My working understanding is this. A consistent formal system that is strong enough to reason about its own provability cannot consistently prove a general soundness principle for itself — something like "for every X, if I prove X then X is true" — because that would amount to proving its own consistency, which such a system can't do. In particular it also can't prove that an arbitrary future version of itself, using a new or stronger reasoning system, will be sound. Applied to agents: an agent can't, from inside its own reasoning, prove that its own reasoning (or the reasoning of a successor it plans to build) is reliable and won't be broken, even in cases where it actually would be. So if a design relies on getting that kind of guarantee, either the team has smuggled in an unproved assumption, or they've quietly redefined the goal or the success criterion until the self-trust statement is trivially true and no longer buys the safety property they wanted.

Turn that into a reusable review procedure I can run on every design doc. It has to be usable by a non-proof-theorist in a couple of hours, it should catch the places where a design is trying to get a guarantee the Löbian obstacle says is unavailable, and it should tell me what to flag, what to ask the team, and what to recommend to the board.

First, give the procedure itself as a table with these columns:

- Step (number, in the order I should run them)
- What to look for (a concrete question to ask or thing to search for in the doc)
- Which part of the Löbian obstacle this checks (one jargon-free sentence)
- Red flag (what the doc will look like if the design is in trouble here)
- What to do if you find it (ask a question, require an outside check, block, pass, etc.)
- Judgment needed? (yes/no, and when yes, what the judgment is about)

Then give a JSON object with these fields:

- "worked_examples": an array of three worked applications. Each object has "case" (a one-line description of a realistic design), "steps_fired" (which steps trigger and why), "verdict" ("block", "pass", or "conditional"), "board_reaction" (whether the verdict is one the board will resist, and why), and "key_uncertainty" (what the procedure alone can't settle in this case).
- "hard_cases": an array of situations where the procedure is weakest or where judgment can't be removed, with a short note on what a reviewer should fall back on in each.
- "what_it_cannot_do": a string explaining what the procedure does and doesn't establish — in particular, the difference between "this design doesn't hit the Löbian obstacle" and "this design is actually safe."

For the worked examples, make them realistic for an agent-lab setting. Include at least one where the procedure tells me to block a project that looks careful and safe on the surface, and at least one where it would pass a design that still leaves me uneasy for reasons the procedure isn't meant to cover.
