I've been chewing on a idea about pain and pleasure that I want a candid read on. I call it "capped fungibility." Normal thinking, especially economic or utilitarian-ish thinking, treats all pain and pleasure as points on one scale, so in principle there's always some amount of pleasure that balances out any pain. I think that's wrong past a certain point. My claim is that below some intensity threshold, pain and pleasure are fungible—a mild headache can be a fair price for a concert, a papercut for a free pastry, whatever. But above the threshold, pain stops being fungible with pleasure. A really bad pain—torture, a kidney stone, being humiliated in front of people you love—isn't just a very negative entry on the same axis. It's a different kind of thing, and pleasures no longer count as the same currency. So you can't say "a trillion orgasms justify one torture," not because the exchange rate is steep but because the two things aren't commensurable at all. I think this also explains why aggregation feels so obscene in some cases but fine in others. My question: is this a coherent view, or am I just smuggling in a deontological rule and dressing it up as a point about measurement? Has this been worked out under some name I should know? And where does it break—for example, is the threshold doing all the work, and do I end up treating a pain just below the line as radically different from one just above it?
