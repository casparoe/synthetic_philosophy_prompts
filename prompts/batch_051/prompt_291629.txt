here's a parallel i've been mulling over and i can't tell if it's deep or just a pun on "self-training." In epistemology there's the bootstrapping problem: you can't establish that a belief-forming process is reliable by using that very process to assemble its own track record. If I want to know whether my color vision can be trusted, going "that looks red, and it is red; that looks green, and it is green" over and over seems to gain me nothing, because the "and it is red" half already depends on the very faculty I'm trying to check. The easy-knowledge side of the same cluster is that once I do take a single deliverance at face value, I can immediately infer a lot of anti-skeptical stuff for free (e.g., I'm not being systematically fooled about colors right now), which looks suspicious. Separately, people training LLMs worry about model collapse: when a model is fine-tuned on text generated by earlier versions of itself, or when its own outputs are used as labels/critiques for the next round, errors compound and diversity dies. Both have the shape of a system treating its own outputs as evidence about its own reliability or quality, and both are supposed to fail for lack of an independent check.
