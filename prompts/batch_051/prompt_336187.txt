I am a novelist, and the book I am finishing centers on a woman who runs a small business out of a former pawnshop: for a percentage, she takes custody of whatever a client wants kept from their future self — passwords, cash, car keys — and returns it only on schedule. Writing her has converted me to a view I used to find thin: that the boundary between a rational person and a disunified succession of time-slices is precisely their exposure to being money-pumped. On this view, an agent is diachronically rational to the degree that no sequence of trades, each acceptable at the moment it is offered, can return them to their starting position with less of what they value; and any vulnerability to such a pump is conclusive evidence of a defect in how their preferences hang together over time. The view has become load-bearing in the novel, and, to my alarm, in how I plan my own year.

I would like an honest audit of that position's exposure.
