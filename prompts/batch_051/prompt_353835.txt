I keep running into people using "representation" in cognitive science like it's obvious what it means, but enactivists and representationalists seem to mean totally different things by it. Give me a working definition of representation (in cognition, not art) that handles these cases: a thermostat's bimetallic strip bending; E. coli swimming up a sugar gradient; a bee's waggle dance; a rat in a Morris water maze using distal landmarks; a robot doing SLAM with an internal map; a human silently rotating a shape in their head; a plant growing toward light; an ant colony's pheromone trail; a spinal reflex pulling a hand off a stove. I suspect some of these involve real representation and some are just coupled to the environment, and the interesting question is where the line is. Start with your definition in the first sentence, then run it through the cases, and say where it gives a weird answer and whether you'd revise it or stick with it.
