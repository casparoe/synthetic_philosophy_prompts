Write the joint statement that a committed conceptual analyst and a committed conceptual engineer (ameliorative analyst) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration over a single socially significant concept of your choosing — race, gender, or marriage — kept fixed throughout. The statement should contain a numbered list of substantive agreed claims, each non-vacuous and acceptable to both camps; then a disagreements table giving the residual disagreement, each position worded so its own proponents would accept it; the two or three cruxes such that settling one would move at least one side; and for each crux a shared case, test, or argument both camps accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Avoid letting the dispute reduce to a verbal quarrel over definitions, and address, where relevant, whether a revised concept must preserve the original's extension over central cases to count as a revision rather than a replacement. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on conceptual ethics, and do not present either the conceptual analyst's or the conceptual engineer's position as the field's consensus.
