Translate the Mencius–Xunzi debate about human nature into that vocabulary as a pair of rival theses about what is innate, what is learned, and what the learning process consists in. Mencius holds that human nature (xing) is good: people are born with incipient moral sprouts — compassion, shame, deference, and the sense of right and wrong — which must be nurtured and extended, and which a bad environment can stunt. Xunzi holds that inborn nature is bad: untutored desires and self-regard lead to contention and disorder unless reshaped, and goodness is the product of deliberate artifice (wei), accumulated over generations and encoded in ritual and moral norms, as craftsmen steam and shape wood or grind metal. I want a systematic translation, not an analogy: state for each view what plays the roles of the innate policy or prior, the reward signal, the environment, and the training procedure, and flag any assignment that is forced. Then give a residue report: what carries over cleanly, what carries only with distortion, and what has no expression in RL at all. End by saying whether the residue points mainly to a limit in the RL vocabulary, to a looseness in Mencius's or Xunzi's claims, or to both.
