Ok so I keep noticing this same pattern in totally different arguments and I'm wondering if it has a name, or if I'm just lumping things together that shouldn't be lumped. In my econ class we learned that when a store jacks up bottled water prices after a hurricane, that's just supply and demand doing its thing: high prices stop hoarding and get water to whoever needs it most. But pretty much everyone still thinks it's gross, and it doesn't feel like the issue is just that the store owner is being greedy. Same with kidneys: donating one is treated as this amazing act, but selling one is illegal basically everywhere and people get really disgusted by the idea, even though allowing sales would probably mean more kidneys for people who are dying. And with votes: voting is obviously fine, giving someone a ride to the polls is fine, but actually paying someone for their vote seems like it messes up the whole system even if it's just a few bucks. In all of these, the objection isn't really about the consequences—the consequences might even be good—its more like putting a price on it changes what the thing is. Is there an actual term for that? And how do you tell these cases apart from normal stuff like selling your labor or selling groceries, where adding money doesn't seem to ruin anything?
