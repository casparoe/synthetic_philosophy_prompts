Next month, the philosophy club I co-run at my high school is hosting a recorded Q&A with a philosopher who defends a particular answer to Hempel's dilemma about how physicalists should characterize the physical. Her answer is the via negativa: she holds that the physical is best defined as the non-mental, so that physicalism amounts to the claim that no mental properties are fundamental, and she argues that this avoids both horns of the dilemma. Our members have already worked through the basic dilemma, so I do not need questions that simply restate it, and I do not want generic questions about her background. Please draft eight interview questions that pressure-test the via negativa at its genuine weak points, with at least one question on each of the following: whether a purely negative definition gives physicalism enough content to rule out dualism or to distinguish it from neutral monism, which takes the fundamental stuff to be neither mental nor physical; whether the definition misclassifies abstract objects, normative facts, or merely possible entities as physical simply because they are not mental; and whether a commitment to there being no fundamental mental properties is really a view about what the physical world is like, rather than only a denial of some alternatives. For each question, include a short follow-up that responds to the reply she is most likely to give.
