Me and my brother argue about this every time the news comes on, and I've started to realize I don't actually know what makes something terrorism. I would have said the word is just for groups that attack civilians to scare people into doing something. But when I go case by case, my gut makes calls I can't explain. A group sets off a truck bomb outside a crowded police station in the middle of the day; the intended target is the station but plenty of ordinary people in the cafe next door are killed. I call that terrorism, full stop. Fighters in an occupied country derail a train at night carrying fuel for the occupying army, and nobody is hurt; I don't call that terrorism, just sabotage. A government drone strikes a house where a suspected militant is staying and kills him along with his wife and two kids. I don't call that terrorism either, though its horrifying and maybe a war crime. Then a militia bombs a wedding because the family is known to back the government, killing dozens, to warn other families. That feels like textbook terrorism to me. Someone breaks into a corporate lab at night, smashes all the equipment, and leaves a banner against animal testing. Not terrorism, just property crime plus intimidation. A lone shooter attacks a mosque, kills people at prayer, and leaves a manifesto about starting a race war. Terrorism, obviously. And a regime secretly grabs the teenage daughter of a dissident to make him stop speaking out. I'd call that terrorism too, even though it's a state doing it.

The two that trip me up are the drone strike and the wedding bombing: both end with civilians dead in a building, killed for a political purpose, but I feel completely different about the labels. I'm also way more willing to use the word for kidnapping a dissident's daughter than for a drone killing a family, even though both are state violence meant to terrify people into silence.

So is there one principle behind my uses of the word, or am I just being inconsistent? If you can give me that principle, tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: a government cuts off electricity and water to a rebel-held city for months, and plenty of civilians die of disease and starvation, while the goverment says its a siege to force a surrender. Is that terrorism under the principle? And which of my verdicts, if any, does the principle force me to give up?
