Here is where I've landed, stated as plainly as I can:

(1) In a sequential decision problem with a binary state, in which each agent receives a binary private signal of the same accuracy and observes only the actions of predecessors, not their signals, it can be perfectly rational for an agent to act against their own private signal and follow the accumulated pattern of earlier choices once the public history is sufficiently lopsided. This is just Bayesian updating on the information contained in others' actions.

(2) Such a process can lock a group onto the wrong action even when every individual is fully rational: once an information cascade starts, later actions no longer reveal the later agents' private information, so the public history stops accumulating information.

(3) Even so, I think the fact that individually rational agents can collectively lock themselves onto the wrong action is a genuine epistemic defect, not merely an unlucky outcome. An agent who suppresses their own signal is, in some sense, failing to make their information available to the group, and a community in which everyone followed the individually rational rule would be worse at tracking the truth than one in which agents sometimes deviated and acted on their own information.

(4) In the idealized model, cascades are fragile: a small amount of public information, or the appearance of a single agent known to have a highly accurate signal, can overturn a long-standing cascade.

(5) In real-world herding — fads, market bubbles, the diffusion of technologies — the striking thing is persistence: herding seems robust, deviations are rare and costly. I take this to show that the ideal Bayesian story, correct as far as it goes, is not the main engine of real herding, which is mostly driven by reputational and payoff externalities rather than by information.

(6) I also hold that a rational agent who knows they may be in a false cascade should still join it when their own signal is weak, because the expected accuracy of the accumulated public history is higher than that of their own signal. Joining is not spinelessness; it is the correct response to asymmetric information.
