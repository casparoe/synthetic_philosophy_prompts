In a late-night argument about God with my roomate, he kept leaning on the line "everything has a cause." When I pushed him on the universe, he said the universe is a thing, so it has a cause too, and the only candidate for that cause is God. When I asked what caused God, he just said God is outside the rule, which felt like moving the goalposts but I couldn't pin down exactly where the slide happened.

I want to bring him a menu of readings next time, not just argue. The sentence "everything has a cause" sounds obviously true until you start pressing on the words. I can feel that a lot turns on what "everything" ranges over, what "cause" means, whether the principle rules out infinite chains, and whether it even applies to the universe as a whole rather than just to the things inside it. So lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, from the ones that are just platitudes to the ones that are seriously contentious, and for each say whether it's true, defensible, or false. Then tell me which of those readings actually get him from "everything has a cause" to "the universe has a cause" to "that cause is God" — and which reading, if any, makes the "God is outside the rule" move legitimate instead of blatent special pleading.

Also, since I don't want to just confirm my annoyance with him: tell me what would change your mind about the verdicts. What kind of argument or counterexample would flip your assessment of the strongest reading, and where is your reasoning about all this most likely to fail?
