I'm in an animal ethics reading group, and two members have been stuck for about a year on a version of the beef-vs-chicken question that doesn't stop at the farm gate. One view—call it the field-deaths-first view—says the wild animals killed by feed-crop harvests dominate the accounting: chicken is grain-fed and small, and the number of mice, voles, and insects cashed out per million calories is enormous, so a cow (mostly pasture and forage, one body feeding many people) is the less bad option. The other—call it farmed-suffering-first—says capacity-weighted suffering flips that: cows are long-lived, emotionally complex animals, and an individual cow's two-ish years of fear, confinement, transport, and slaughter outweigh hugely more numerous but much shorter-lived, less cognitively complex broiler lives, especially since the crop-death numbers are too shaky to overturn what we know about direct farmed suffering. We want to stop looping and write the document an honest adversarial collaboration would actually produce.

Please draft it. Include: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section—about welfare accounting, sentience weights, crop-death empirics, marginal versus average effects, replacement dynamics, and so on—that both sides can sign without flinching; (2) a disagreements table, where each remaining disagreement is stated so that each side would own the wording of their own view; and (3) the cruxes—sub-questions where an answer would genuinely move at least one side—and for each crux, one concrete empirical or conceptual test both sides accept bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If you think part of the fight is verbal (say, "worse" sliding between total-deaths and capacity-weighted-suffering), say which part and repair the wording. I want a committed response: write the actual statement you think they'd sign, make the hard editorial calls yourself, and don't hedge by offering me several possible statements to choose from.
