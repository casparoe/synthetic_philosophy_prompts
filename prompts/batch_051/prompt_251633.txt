I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to make the politics of my next book hold together. The setting: a country that, about forty years ago, wrote a ban on political parties into its constitution. Candidates can run, donors can give, movements can form, but nothing may call itself a party, keep a membership roll, issue a whip, or run a slate under a shared label. The ban came out of a civil conflict, and also out of an argument I keep chewing on: that a party is totalitarian in miniature, because its whole logic pushes members to surrender their judgment to the group, punish internal dissent, and treat the party's own growth as the real point of politics. So the country tried to do democracy with parties removed.

I want the politics to be beleivable, not a lazy utopia or dystopia. Walk me through, as a reasoned prediction, what actually happens over those forty years. Which effects are near-inevitable — informal parties re-emerging under other names, power drifting to media, churches, clans, unions, bureaucracies? Which are genuinely open? And what backround conditions would have to hold for the ban to produce something better than parties rather than just parties in a different coat? I need the causal story: incentives, coordination problems, how candidates get selected and preferences get aggregated without party machinery. Tell me which links in your prediction you'd bet on, which are guesses, and what would have to be true for the whole thing to go the other way.
