I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been asked to design the configuration schema for a system that flags potentially discriminatory outcomes in hiring, lending, and admissions. Before I decide which attributes the system treats as protected, I want to know what the underlying concept is supposed to track. What must an adequate theory of what makes a ground of discrimination suspect get right? I want a list of desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and each marked as either non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp, with a note where the field genuinely splits. The cases I need the theory to handle include height, handedness, musical taste, accent, religious affiliation, political affiliation, a criminal record, a credit score, a postal code, a genetic marker for disease, family status, and a name associated with a particular ethnic group. I especially want the theory to address three complications. First, when an apparently innocent attribute is used as a proxy for a suspect one, as when a postal code or a name leaks racial or ethnic information. Second, when an attribute has genuine statistical predictive power for the decision, because I need to know whether predictive accuracy is a reason to treat a ground as less suspect or rather part of what makes the ground dangerous. Third, whether the unit of analysis is a single attribute or whether a combination of attributes can be suspect when neither is on its own, as with the interaction of racial and gender categories. I also want the theory to address whether a characteristic must be unchosen to be suspect, given that religion is chosen and usually protected while a criminal record is chosen and usually not given the same protection, and to distinguish the attribute itself from the social meaning attached to it. Finally, tell me which single desideratum, if any, eliminates the largest number of plausible theories, and whether any pair of desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied. Please write this as continuous prose, as an internal memo or email; no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
