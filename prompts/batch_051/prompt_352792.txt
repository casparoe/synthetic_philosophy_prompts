I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck early in a near-future book set around 2040, where a recurring set-piece is a family argument at a lake house about whether "the transformation" already happened. One cousin insists it happened back in the early 2020s and everyone's been sleepwalking through the aftermath; another says nothing counts until one system does more to the wider economy than the whole industrial revolution managed over a century; the grandmother says it's a long process that started with the internet and can't be dated, period. My problem is that I want each of these characters to hold a distinct, defensable position, not just an attitude, so I can write them clashing without them blurring together — and I keep finding that my own view shifts depending on which case I think about last. I've started to suspect the family fight is really about which definition of transformative wins.

Could you build me a sorting instrument — a decision tree or a scored quiz, whichever behaves better — that takes someone from raw answers about concrete cases to a named position (or "unnamed," if the position is probably incoherent, unattractive, or just undefended) on whether transformative AI has already happened? At each split, use a question someone could answer without knowing the philosophy: whether a technology has to move GDP growth rates to count, whether it has to change how a person experiences a whole year, whether irreversibility matters, whether the change can be spread across many systems or must be one identifiable system, whether the right comparison is the printing press or the wheel. Here are the cases I want used as diagnostic items or at the leaves: (1) every white-collar task from 2019 is automatable by tomorrow, but measured output per person grows only 1.5% a year for a decade; (2) no human beats the machines on any creative or IQ-style benchmark, yet fertility, mortality, institutions, and growth are statistically unchanged ten years later; (3) nothing that deserves the name general ever appears, but over thirty years the machines quietly change how humans date, raise children, worship, and think about their own minds. Also, flag any positions your instrument can't seperate, so I don't write two characters as disagreeing when your questions would sort them onto the same leaf.
