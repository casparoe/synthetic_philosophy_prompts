I'm a PhD student in a philosophy department, and one of my teaching duties is marking for a third-year unit on agency and responsibility. I've got one script I keep going back and forth on, and I'd appreciate an actual grader's read on it rather than my own dithering. The question I set for the assignement was:

"Explain Aristotle's account of mixed actions in the passage below, and use that account to assess whether an AI system that can form correct moral judgements but cannot act on them except as its designers permit could count as a moral agent in Aristotle's sense."

The passage I gave them is from Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics III.1, in Rackham's translation (Perseus Digital Library):

"Acts of this kind, then, are ‘mixed’ or composite; but they approximate rather to the voluntary class. For at the actual time when they are done they are chosen or willed; and the end or motive of an act varies with the occasion, so that the terms ‘voluntary’ and ‘involuntary’ should be used with reference to the time of action; now the actual deed in the cases in question is done voluntarily, for the origin of the movement of the parts of the body instrumental to the act lies in the agent; and when the origin of an action is in oneself, it is in one's own power to do it or not. Such acts therefore are voluntary, though perhaps involuntary apart from circumstances—for no one would choose to do any such action in and for itself."

The student wrote:

"On Aristotle's picture, an action is compulsory when its origin is outside the agent and the agent contributes nothing, whereas a 'mixed' action like throwing cargo overboard in a storm is partly voluntary. The man does not want to jettison the cargo, but he chooses it at the moment to save his own life and the lives of his shipmates; the movement of his body originates in him, and he could have refused, so the act is voluntary, though perhaps involuntary apart from the circumstances. An AI that can only perform the actions its designers permit seems similar, but it is not: it may 'choose' the compliant action as the lesser evil, but it cannot in fact act otherwise. The origin of its behaviour lies outside it, in the designers or the objective function, so its external acts are compulsory and it cannot be praised or blamed for them. Even so, the AI's judgement is voluntary. It deliberates about which action would be best, forms an intention, and is aware of the circumstances; and Aristotle defines choice as a voluntary decision reached by deliberation. Since the AI makes such a choice when it judges that refusing would be correct, it is a moral agent with respect to its internal acts, if not its external ones."

Could you mark this out of 20 and give me the specific comments that justify the mark? I don't want a general impression; I need you to identify which claims are correct and exactly where the reasoning stops following, since the examiners may ask me to defend the mark.
