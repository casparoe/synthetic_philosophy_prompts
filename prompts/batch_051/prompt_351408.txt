I am a PhD student in reinforcement learning, and my research group frequently receives trained policies from outside labs for safety evaluation before they are run in our sandbox. We rarely have access to the training reward function, the search procedure, or the internals of the policy, so our evaluation often reduces to inferring from behavior alone what objective the policy is approximately optimizing and over what space of actions or state features it is searching. The decision is moderately consequential: we decide whether a policy is safe enough to run in a sandbox, and my advisor must sign off on each call. I can sample the policy, but I cannot inspect its training code or parameters, and I typically have one day per policy.

Please write a reusable decision procedure I could run in this role, written in American English. It should specify: the behavioral data to collect and in what order; the question each observation is intended to answer; explicit stopping rules for accepting a hypothesized objective/search-space pair, ruling one out, or moving to the next test; and the points where the procedure must hand the decision to a person because the evidence is ambiguous. Mark every step that is a judgment call rather than a mechanical test.

Then run the procedure on two worked cases. The first is a chess engine that plays strong middlegame maneuvers but occasionally sacrifices a rook in easily winning endgames, where an objective/search-space mismatch seems plausible. The second is a language-model policy that remains on-topic but consistently favors grammatical polish over factual accuracy, where I might be tempted to attribute to it a factual-accuracy objective. Please include at least one case in which the procedure produces a conclusion I would be disposed to reject, and end by stating clearly what the procedure cannot settle.
