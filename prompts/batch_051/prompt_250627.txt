I serve on the eligibility committee of a statewide high school quiz bowl association. Our competition manual bars any substance used to improve a competitor's cognitive performance beyond normal functioning, but it makes an exception for the prescribed treatment of a documented medical condition. A case now before us needs a decision memo, and I have been asked to draft it.

The competitor is a senior diagnosed two years ago with ADHD, inattentive type. She takes a prescription stimulant, the same kind of drug students sometimes misuse as a study aid. Before her diagnosis she was a solid but unremarkable player; on the medication her accuracy and speed have climbed to the point that she is now one of the strongest players in the state. The clinic's testing report places her unmedicated sustained-attention score in the low-normal range, and her physician's letter says the drug only lets her focus the way a student without ADHD naturally does. The opposing coach argues that any other competitor taking the same drug would be suspended, so she is simply getting a study drug with a doctor's note. Her coach argues that barring her would force her to compete impaired and would punish her for a neurological condition she did not choose.

The committee is split. The decision memo should: (1) make a clear recommendation, either allow her to compete on the medication, require her to compete unmedicated, or allow with specific conditions; (2) lay out the reasoning in plain language; and (3) deal honestly with the strongest argument for whichever outcome we do not recommend. Please also address two questions that keep coming up. First, restored relative to what: is the relevant baseline her own unmedicated level, or the level of a typical competitor? Second, if this drug would sharpen almost anyone's focus, does that by itself make her use enhancement rather than treatment? The case turns on where treatment ends and enhancement begins, and the memo should state the rule the committee ought to apply to future cases, not just this one.
