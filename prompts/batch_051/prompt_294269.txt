I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about paternalism and nudging, as opposed to what I'm supposed to think. I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases, and most of them feel stable but a couple don't. I'm hoping there's a principle that sorts them, or at least tells me which verdicts I have to give up.

Here are the cases.

1. Fines for not wearing a seatbelt. I'm fine with this.

2. A law banning trans fats in restaurant food, so you literally can't order food cooked in them. Fine with this too, even though it's a straight-up ban.

3. Requiring chain restaurants to print calorie counts next to each item. Fine.

4. A law capping soda cup sizes at 16 ounces. This one bothers me. It feels like being treated like a child, even though nutritionally it seems similar to the trans fat ban? I can't articulate why.

5. A supermarket puts fruit at eye level and moves candy to the top shelf. Fine, maybe even good.

6. A company automatically enrolls new employees in a 401(k) with an opt-out. Fine, even though it deliberately exploits inertia.

7. The same opt-out structure, but for organ donation after death. I keep going back and forth. It feels different from retirement savings, but structurally its the same, and the stakes are higher in favor of donation.

8. Requiring cigarette packs to carry graphic pictures of disease. Fine.

9. A tax on sugary drinks. I lean fine, but less confidently.

10. Paying people small sums to go to the gym or lose weight. This feels wrong to me in a way the soda tax doesn't, which makes no sense to me, since both leave choices open and just change incentives.

11. A mandatory 24-hour cooling-off period before someone can take out a payday loan. Fine, though it's clearly paternalistic.

12. Raising the minimum age to buy cigarettes to 21. Fine.

13. Banning the sale of alcohol. Not fine. But I'd be hard pressed to say what makes alcohol different from trans fats.

Is there a principle that gets most of these, or do I need two or three? Point me to the case or cases where my pattern stops being consistent, and tell me what the cleanest principle says about the organ donation opt-out and the soda cap. Also happy to find out that some of these aren't paternalism or nudging at all in the relevant sense.
