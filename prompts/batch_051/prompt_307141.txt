I have been trying to adjudicate a recurring disagreement in discussions of artificial intelligence risk. One side describes a possible misaligned superintelligence as an "existential risk"; the other side objects that this is loose usage, because the scenarios actually under discussion—a catastrophe that leaves a permanently diminished but still human civilization, a planetary surveillance order that lasts indefinitely, or the gradual replacement of biological humans by our own machine descendants—would not literally extinguish intelligent life. I suspect the dispute is less about the scenarios than about the concept, so I want a working definition rather than a list of examples.

In "Existential Risks: Analyzing Human Extinction Scenarios and Related Hazards" (Journal of Evolution and Technology, 2002), Nick Bostrom introduces the idea of a terminal risk with this contrast: "An endurable risk may cause great destruction, but one can either recover from the damage or find ways of coping with the fallout. In contrast, a terminal risk is one where the targets are either annihilated or irreversibly crippled in ways that radically reduce their potential to live the sort of life they aspire to." I take it that existential risks are meant to be terminal risks whose target is humankind—or, more broadly, Earth-originating intelligent life—as a whole.

Please give me a definition of "existential risk" that treats Bostrom's endurable/terminal contrast as a constraint but is precise enough to yield defensible verdicts on the following cases: (a) an engineered pathogen or AI-enabled weapon that kills every human being; (b) an AI-driven war that kills several hundred million people and sets technological civilization back, but from which humanity fully recovers within a few centuries; (c) a misaligned superintelligence that permanently locks humanity into a stable but impoverished global regime, with no realistic prospect of escape, though people continue to live and have children; (d) the gradual, consensual replacement of biological humans by uploaded or artificial minds, so that no biological human remains but our intelligent descendants flourish; and (e) a collapse that permanently destroys much of humanity's accumulated cultural and scientific heritage while leaving the population and the biosphere mostly intact. For each case, say whether your definition counts it as an existential risk, explain why, and flag any verdict you find uncomfortable but are prepared to defend.
