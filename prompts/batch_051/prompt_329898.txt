In the codebases I work on, the same identifier is routinely reused with unrelated meanings: `status` means fulfillment state in one table, authentication state in another, and liveness at a health-check endpoint; disambiguation comes entirely from context—the enclosing schema, type, or module. Natural languages appear to do the same thing, tolerating homonyms (a `pitch` can be a throw, a resin, a playing field, or a musical property) and relying on context rather than eliminating the ambiguity. But the same teams that tolerate this across scopes treat an ambiguous identifier inside a single scope as a bug, and many of them require configuration keys to be globally unique. What view of meaning and disambiguation does this context-dependent naming practice presuppose? Is the analogy with natural-language homonymy sound, or does the formal, compositional character of code make it misleading? And if we enforced the principle that every identifier must have exactly one meaning across the entire codebase, what would have to change in the practice, and what would we lose?
