I'm putting together a podcast interview with a philosopher who's just published a book defending the Stalinesque horn of the color-phi puzzle. Her claim, roughly, is that when subjects say the red dot turns green halfway through its apparent path, there's no earlier experience of a red dot that gets rewritten after the second flash arrives; instead the brain waits a beat, assembles one coherent episode, and only then produces the experience with the color change already parked at the midpoint. She tends to shrug off the Orwellian alternative by saying both stories fit the data and Occam's razor settles it, which I find way to quick.

Write me nine or ten interview questions that force her to work. The audience knows the shape of the color-phi debate, so the questions can be specific. The list should include: (1) a couple of questions about what would actually count as evidence for the rewrite-after-experience story over the delay-then-stage story, pressing her to name a measurement she couldn't shrug off; (2) at least one question about cases where people or their hands respond to a stimulus faster than any reportable conscious episode could plausibly be staged, and what her delay claim says about whether those responses were conscious; (3) a question pushing on the specious present and whether her view makes consciousness actually continuous or just assembled on an ongoing basis; (4) a question about scrambled temporal order, like things appearing in the wrong sequence under masking, and whether a single-stage theater metaphor can handle that. After the opening question, pair each question with the follow-up you'd give to her most likely answer, and end with one about the strongest piece of evidence that could make her abandon the story.
