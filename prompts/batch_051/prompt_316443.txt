While working through Heidegger's analysis of equipment, I reconstructed an argument that, if sound, seems to dissolve the very distinction I am trying to understand. I would like a step-by-step verdict on it.

(1) A thing's being is exhausted by how it can show itself.
(2) Equipment shows itself in one of two ways: as ready-to-hand in smooth use, or as present-at-hand when use breaks down.
(3) In smooth use the tool does not show itself at all as an object; it withdraws, and what we are aware of is the task, not the tool.
(4) So readiness-to-hand is not a way the tool is; it is at most a way the tool fails to appear, hence a fact about the user rather than about the tool.
(5) In breakdown the tool shows itself as a present-at-hand object with properties such as mass, shape, material, and causal powers.
(6) Therefore the tool's being is present-at-hand, and the meaningful 'world' of references around it is a subjective overlay on physical objects.

Is this argument valid? If not, which step fails, and under what exact reading of that step? If it is valid, how strong is the conclusion in (6)? I want the precise fault diagnosed, not a restatement of the terminology.
