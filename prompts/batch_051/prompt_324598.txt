Two grad students in my lab got into it yesterday over Lloyd Morgan's canon—the old Victorian rule that you shouldn't explain an animal's behavior by a "higher" mental process when a "lower" one will do. (1) Ana said the canon is dead weight in modern work: it tells you to prefer the associationist story before you've actually looked at the data, so it's a built-in bias rather than a priniciple of parsimony. (2) Ben said no, it's just saying don't chalk behavior up to rich cognition when a dumber mechanism suffices; when the dumb mechanism fails you're free to go richer, and that's exactly how good animal-cognition research proceeds. (3) Ana answered that "lower" is doing all the work: bees show associative feats that outclass primate "insight" on some tasks, so there isn't a single ladder from lower to higher to appeal to. (4) Ben came back that the canon is still the best guard against anthropomorphism—without it you get people reading guilt into a dog's "guilty look," which controlled experiments have repeatedly deflated.
