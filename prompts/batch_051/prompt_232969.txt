Build me a case of self-deception where both the intentionalist and the deflationist will call it self-deception, but the intentionalist is forced to posit more mental machinery than the case needs. Constraints: one paragraph, an ordinary workplace or a marriage, no homunculi, no hypnosis, no booze, and the person has to actually encounter the evidence and then mishandle it internally — not just avoid it, and not lie to other people. The intentionalist should conclude that the person must have known the truth somewhere and gotten themselves to believe the opposite; the deflationist should explain the very same behavior with nothing but normal desire-biased belief formation (wanting hard, selective attention, misweighting what the evidence shows). After the scenario, walk me through each theory's verdict, point to the single feature of the case that makes the intentionalist reach for a hidden act of self-trickery, and finish with how an intentionalist would attack that reading.
