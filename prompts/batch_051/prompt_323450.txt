I want a credence, not a menu of positions. Take as given that evolution happened, and with it hundreds of millions of years of predation, disease, and pain in nonhuman animals before any human existed, hence before any human sin. The proposition is that an omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good God could have used this process to create while remaining perfectly good, and that creation as it so unfolded is rightly called good. My credence is about 0.4; a friend's is about 0.85. Give your number, then decompose it: I want the sub-questions you take it to depend on, a rough probability for each, and how they combine. For instance: whether prehuman animal pain is morally significant suffering or mere pain in creatures with no moral status; whether pain and death require special justification wherever they occur or can be features of an order that is good in the sense of complete and fitted to its end; whether the Genesis 1 verdict that creation is good is best read as moral perfection or as functional completeness; whether some goods are available only through an evolutionary process and are good enough to permit the pain; and whether the Fall should be read as a literal historical cause of death or in some other way. Then tell me where the 0.4-versus-0.85 disagreement most plausibly lives, and what evidence or argument would move your own credence most.
