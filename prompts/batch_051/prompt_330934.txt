I am a philosopher of mind whose first degree was in programming-language semantics, and I still think most easily in terms of specifications, implementations, semantic domains, and simulation. I want you to restate Hempel's dilemma about defining 'the physical' in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report on what does not survive the translation. The dilemma, in brief, is this: if 'physical' picks out whatever current physics says there is, physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics will be revised; if 'physical' picks out whatever an ideal, completed physics would say there is, then physicalism seems either trivially true or lacking determinate content, because no one can say what that completed physics will include. Translate the two horns into a contrast between a present, partial specification and an ideal specification that no one has yet written, being careful about the roles played by implementation, simulation, and semantic domain. Say which parts of the dilemma map cleanly onto my vocabulary, which can be expressed only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all. Then give your verdict: does the residue show that my vocabulary is missing something the debate genuinely needs, or that the dilemma is partly an artefact of the way philosophers formulate it? Please write in British English.
