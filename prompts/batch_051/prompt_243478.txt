The big machine made of little simmballs, where at the level of the balls everything is just deterministic micro-interactions, but at the level of the whole machine there's a mind with beliefs and desires and such. The puzzle is whether the higher-level symbols can do any real pushing, or whether the balls just do what they do and the symbols ride along for free. I've been arguing with a freind about this and I think a lot of the confusion comes from looking for the wrong kind of pushing. You're an expert on philosophy of mind and causation, so I'd really value your take.

Here's the thing I've been calling the simmball absorption principle (working name, probably awful). The idea is this: symbols don't push the substrate around by adding a new force or bending trajectories; they push by absorbing the micro-variance. More precisely, a symbolic state S at time t causes a later symbolic state S* just in case knowing that the system is in S makes the statistical distribution over S* independent of which particular microstate realizes S. In other words, the micro-details of how the balls are arranged get screened off by the symbol: any two microstates that both realize the belief that it's Tuesday lead to the same downstream symbolic probabilities, so the symbol absorbs the differences that could have mattered. The symbol then has causal power in the only sense that matters, because the micro-variance is causally inert once symbolically fixed. If something satisfies that screening-off condition, it's a real cause; if not, it's just a summary that leaks causal information.

I know this sounds very Dennett-y — real patterns, intentional stance, all that — and maybe interventionism already cashed this out. But I think my screening-off condition adds something: it doesn't require us to say what would happen under a hypothetical intervention on the symbol alone, which is impossible in the careenium because you can't change the symbol without changing the balls. It only requires that, within the actual equivalence class, future states don't care about the fine-grained differences. What I want to know is: is this a real idea or just a confused restatement?
