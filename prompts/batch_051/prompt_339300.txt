I'm in a seminar on population ethics, and I've been trying to figure out how much the procreation asymmetry actually forces on us. The asymmetry, as I understand it, is this: if a possible child would have a life so bad that it is not worth living, the suffering she would experience gives us a serious moral reason not to create her; but if a possible child would have a life worth living, the happiness she would experience gives us no moral reason to create her. I find this modest-sounding claim very attractive, but I've built an argument from it that seems to lead somewhere I cannot accept, and I'd like a diagnosis of where the reasoning goes wrong rather than just a vote on the conclusion.

Here is the argument.

(1) The procreation asymmetry is correct.
(2) If a merely possible person's prospective happiness gave us a reason to create them, then bringing a happy person into existence would benefit that person. Since (1) denies that there is any such reason, coming into existence is never a benefit to the person who is brought into existence.
(3) Coming into existence can, however, be a harm: when a possible person's life would be not worth living, the suffering they would experience gives us a reason not to create them, and disregarding that reason would harm the person.
(4) Now take any actual person and compare the outcome in which they exist with the outcome in which they never came into existence. In the never-existed outcome, all of their actual pain would have been absent; by the logic of (3), the absence of that pain is good. All of their actual pleasure would also have been absent; by the logic of (2), the absence of that pleasure is not bad, because there would have been no one who could have been benefited by it.
(5) So for any actual person, the never-existed outcome is better with respect to pain and no worse with respect to pleasure than the outcome in which they exist. Therefore, never existing would have been at least as good for each actual person as existing.
(6) But every actual life contains some pain. If never existing is at least as good as existing, and existing adds pain, then existing is on balance a harm to the person who lives it. So no one is ever benefited by being born, and bringing a person into existence always wrongs the person who is brought into existence.

That last step seems to me to prove too much: it would make even conscientious, loving parents into people who have wronged their child simply by conceiving them. I would most like to keep the modest form of the asymmetry in (1), but I want to know what that admission really costs. My suspicion is that the trouble is somewhere around (2) — the slide from "her happiness gives me no reason to create her" to "coming into existence is not a benefit to her" — or in the way (4) treats never having existed as if it were a state a person could be better or worse off in. But I am not confident, and I would like you to name the exact step or steps you would reject, explain why each looked licensed, and spell out what rejecting it would commit me to. Please say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of. Please write your answer in American English.
