Immanuel Kant denied that we have direct duties to animals. On his view animals lack the self-conscious, rational nature of ends-in-themselves, so whatever duties concern them are only indirect: cruelty to animals is wrong because it dulls the sympathetic dispositions we owe to human beings. Now suppose the descriptive half of this position has been overtaken by developments in animal cognition research. Many animals plainly feel pain and fear, and some grieve, plan, and show forms of self-recognition, even though none of this makes them autonomous moral agents in Kant's sense. Does Kant's argument still go through? Identify the premise the new evidence bears on, and say whether there is a repaired Kantian position that retains the Formula of Humanity while grounding direct duties to animals, or whether the argument is best regarded as dead and in need of replacement. If no repair is available, state what exactly remains of Kant's original argument and what should take its place.
