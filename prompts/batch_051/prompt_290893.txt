I've been trying to get my head round James and Paul on justification and keep going in circles, so I thought asking for a poem might force me to slow down. I want the poem to work on its own as a poem but also to do justice to the actual tension, not just wave it away.

Here's the passage I keep coming back to, from James 2:21–24 (KJV):

Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God. Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.

Could you write a sonnet in the voice of James, maybe as if he's talking to a reader who keeps citing Paul's Abraham in Romans 4. The octave should build up James's reading of Abraham: works not as earning salvation but as the shape faith has to take. The volta should come with the phrase "not by faith only" — I want the turn of the sonnet to make the reader feel how much rests on that word "only." The sestet can push toward James's examples, but not as a sermon. Accurate enough that a New Testament scholar wouldn't wince, but not just versified commentary.
