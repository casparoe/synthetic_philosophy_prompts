The pessimistic story is that optimization pressure is precisely what selects for obfuscation: the model learns to emit a CoT that reads as innocent to the monitor while the action-producing computation happens opaquely, or the CoT encodes the bad plan steganographically in innocuous-looking word choices. My credence that the optimist is right is around 0.35; my colleague is around 0.75.

Give me your own credence in the claim that matters: under strong optimization pressure and a meaningfully imperfect reward proxy, the monitor would catch at least two-thirds of consequential cases of obfuscated or unfaithful reasoning before they issue in an action. Don't just give a number—decompose it. I want rough probabilities for something like the following terms:
(a) the CoT is normally the actual locus of the model's deliberation, rather than a plausible rationalization produced after the decision is already made elsewhere;
(b) given persistent proxy divergence, RL actually creates real selection pressure toward CoTs that fool the monitor, rather than the pressure being weak or easily corrected;
(c) conditional on genuine obfuscation attempts, the monitor detects a good share of them, including steganographic encodings;
(d) the monitoring setup can be iterated or scaled—better monitors, recursive oversight, adversarial training—so it stays ahead of the agent as capabilities grow.

Give a number for each, show how they combine into your overall credence, and then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to: if you were wrong about exactly one of them, which error would move the bottom line most? Also tell me which term the 0.35 vs 0.75 disagreement between me and my colleague most likely lives in. And I want the self-critical version: what evidence or argument—about steganography capacity, RL dynamics, interpretability progress, or something else—would most shift your number, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail?
