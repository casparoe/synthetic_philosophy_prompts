Alright, here's one I've been stuck on. I work on the ethics of war, and I'm trying to get a complete inventory of the arguments people actually give for the moral equality of combatants — the idea that a soldier fighting for an unjust cause and a soldier fighting for a just cause are on a moral par, so that neither of them does anything wrong just by fighting and killing enemy combatants, provided they both obey the ordinary in bello constraints (no killing the wounded, no targeting civilians, and so on). Here's what I have so far:

1. The epistemic argument. The justice of a war depends on facts — who was lying, what the real motives were, what the alternatives were — that rank-and-file soldiers are in a uniquely bad position to know. Since they can't feasibly determine ad bellum justice, it can't be the sort of thing their fighting is wrong on the basis of.

2. The duress/excuse argument. Soldiers on both sides are typically conscripted, or under enormous social and legal pressure, and the cost of refusal is prison, disgrace, or worse. So their participation is excused in a way that cuts across which side is just.

3. The role/division-of-labour argument. The laws of war set up a division of moral labour. The job of deciding whether the war is just belongs to political leaders (and maybe to citizens and courts after the fact); the solider's job is only to fight within the rules. So the soldier isn't the relevant agent for ad bellum wrongs.

4. The mutual self-defense argument. Whatever started the war, two soldiers who meet on a battlefield each face an immediate lethal threat from the other, and each is entitled to use necessary force in self-defense. The threat is symmetrical even if the causes are not.

5. The conventionalist argument. Without symmetric rules, war would be even worse. If the law treated one side as murderers and the other as licensed killers, neither side would have much reason to obey restraint (why extend prisoner protections to criminals?), so a symmetrical convention is the most stable way to preserve any limits at all.

I have this nagging feeling I'm missing one — something genuinely different, not a restatement of any of these, not a mix of 2 and 3 or 4 and 5. Am I right? If yes, what is it, and is it actually any good? And if no, tell me which of my five are secretly the same argument, because I'd rather have four honest entries than five padded ones.
