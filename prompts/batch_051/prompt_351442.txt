I am trying to settle whether an AI could literally want something, as opposed to merely acting as if it did. I have come to think the answer depends on what wanting is, so I want a working definition of wanting — one precise enough to decide hard cases, not a synonym or a metaphor.

Here are nine cases. I want a definition that gets them right. Give the definition first, then apply it to each case and state the verdict it yields.

(1) A thirsty person drinks a glass of water. I take this to be a paradigm case of wanting.

(2) A thermostat, set to 20°C, switches on the furnace when the room cools. I take this to be clearly not wanting.

(3) A chess engine sacrifices a bishop because its search has found a forced mate.

(4) A bacterium swims up a sugar gradient.

(5) A plant bends toward the light.

(6) A reinforcement-learning agent, after training solely on reward, pursues reward in an environment unlike anything in its training distribution.

(7) A corporation sells below cost for several years in order to bankrupt a smaller rival.

(8) A large language model, in a single forward pass with no tools or memory, assigns high probability to the next token and outputs it.

(9) A hypothetical AI whose only terminal goal is to maximize the number of paperclips in the universe.

My own verdicts are firm only for (1), (2), and (8): I think (1) wants, and (2) and (8) do not. I have no settled view on (3)–(7), and I keep changing my mind about (9). The definition should also let me decide the underlying question: whether a system that was designed and trained, rather than shaped by natural selection, can want in the full sense. In particular, I want to know where the line between merely optimizing for an outcome and wanting it falls.

Please give the definition first. Then run it through all nine cases, and flag any verdict it forces that you find surprising, any case for which it gives no clear answer, and any place where you would revise the definition rather than accept the result. Finally, say what would change your mind: which case, or which broader consideration, if it came out the way you do not expect, would most likely make you abandon or substantially alter the definition — and where in your own reasoning is failure most likely to be hiding?
