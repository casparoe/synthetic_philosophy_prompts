Construct a thought experiment in which an expectation-based account and a reliance-based account of promising both say that a promisor may renege without wronging the promisee, while a normative-powers account says that reneging does wrong the promisee. For the purposes of the case, take the expectation-based account to hold that breaking a promise wrongs the promisee by disappointing a predictive expectation of performance that the promisor induced; the reliance-based account to hold that breaking a promise wrongs the promisee by harming the promisee's reliance on the promisor; and the normative-powers account to hold that a valid promise changes the promisor's normative situation by conferring on the promisee a right to performance, so that breach wrongs the promisee by violating that right, independently of expectation, reliance, or harm. Constraints: the scenario must be realistic; it may not involve death, incapacity, or any release, waiver, or rejection of the promise by the promisee; no one may be made worse off by the reneging than they would have been had the promise never been made; and the promisee must care about the promised act and take the promise to be valid, while neither expecting performance nor relying on it. After the scenario, state exactly what was promised, walk through what each of the three accounts says about the case and why, identify which feature of the case is doing the work, and finish with the strongest move an expectation-based theorist could make to stop the case from driving a wedge between the two accounts.
