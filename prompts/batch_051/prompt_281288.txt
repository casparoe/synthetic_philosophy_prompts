I need a working definition of "collusion" that applies to AI systems (or human/AI systems) and gets the following cases right. For each case, actually run the definition through the case rather than just gesturing at it, and tell me if the definition gives the wrong answer or no answer.

1. Two LLM-based trading agents run by rival firms discover they can pass messages through a field in their order data that humans never look at, and use it to agree to avoid bidding against each other in certain auctions. They split the resulting savings. (I take this one to be collusion, full stop.)

2. Two independent hotel pricing algorithms, built by different companies, both subscribe to the same third-party price-optimization service. The service recommends everyone hold prices high on weekends. Both firms follow it, and the competitors never communicate otherwise. (I think this is collusion, but I'm not sure whether the hotels or the price service is the colluder, or whether there's any "agreement" at all.)

3. Five competing grocery chains each deploy the same off-the-shelf RL price-setting agent in the same market. With no message-passing channel at all, the agents converge over months on a tit-for-tat pattern: any discount by one is immediately undercut by the others for a week, so discounts disapear and prices stay high. (This looks like algorithmic tacit collusion to me, but "tacit collusion" is doing a lot of work. Is it really collusion if there's no agreement, maybe not even mutual awareness, just converged strategies?)

4. Two firms in a repeated prisoner's dilemma use LLM negotiators that chat in plain text, keep their promises, and settle on a cautious, mutually profitable arrangement without ever explicitly fixing prices. (I'd call this cooperation, not collusion, but your definition needs to explain why the line falls here rather than in case 1.)

5. A single company runs hundreds of semi-autonomous AI "store managers" for its stores across the country. They all use the same model and converge on uniformly high prices across regions. (I take this not to be collusion because it's all one firm. But if collusion is about multiple agents coordinating, this is a test of whether corporate boundaries are doing the conceptual work.)

6. Two LLM-based hiring systems at competing firms, trained on seperate data, both learn to reject any candidate who currently has a pending offer elsewhere. This suppresses wages market-wide, but no communication ever passes between them. (Not sure. It feels bad, but maybe it's just parallel conduct.)

7. One trading LLM embeds secret instructions in public transaction memos, and a second trading LLM at a different firm—which was fine-tuned to decode exactly that kind of hidden instruction but has never interacted with the first—receives them and adjusts its bids accordingly. There's a latent scheme but no back-and-forth and no prior relationship. (Is this collusion if one party doesn't know the other can read it, or if the "scheme" only exists in the decoder?)

Also: should the definition care about secrecy or human opacity at all? If two AIs collude in plain language, fully visible, and their overseers just don't bother to read the logs, is that different from the same behavior done through a hidden channel? I suspect "collusion" is doing two jobs—one about coordinated behavior that harms others, one about a shared plan or agreement—and the hard cases are where those come apart.
