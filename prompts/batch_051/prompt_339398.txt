ok so heres roughly where I've landed on statutory interpretation and I want a consistency check before I present it to the seminar. I hold:

(1) In an ordinary statutory case a judge's job is to apply what the words would have meant to a reasonable, informed reader at the time the statute was enacted.
(2) 'Legislative intent' and 'the purpose of Parliament' are mostly fictions. There's no single mental state shared across a legislature, and even if there were, it isn't what makes law.
(3) Still, when the text is genuinely ambiguous, courts should be allowed to look at parliamentary debates (Hansard) and committee reports to settle what the provision was meant to fix, because otherwise they're deciding in a evidentiary vacuum.
(4) I also think the absurdity doctrine is legitimate: if the plain reading produces a result that is manifestly irrational or self-defeating, a court should depart from the text rather than enforce nonsense.
(5) Canons like the rule of lenity or expressio unius (specifying one thing impliedly excludes the others) are fine, but only as tie-breakers once ambiguity is established. They aren't evidence about what the legislature actually thought.
(6) For constitutional and human-rights instruments, though, I'm a living-instrument person: those get read in light of present-day conditions and values, not frozen at drafting.
(7) And if a court got a statute's meaning wrong years ago and people have organised their affairs around that reading, I'd usually leave the settled interpretation alone rather than 'correct' it.

Go through these and tell me where the real stresses are. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give and how much it would cost me. Also flag any pair that only looks inconsistent but dissolves on inspection, because I'd rather not overcorrect.
