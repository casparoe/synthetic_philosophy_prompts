I work on reinforcement learning, where most of my practical problems concern reward functions: proxy rewards, reward hacking, Goodhart’s law, specification gaming, wireheading, and the gap between optimizing an objective and getting what the designer actually wanted. Recently a colleague proposed adding an explicit user-satisfaction signal to a reward model, and I objected that the agent would learn to chase the shadow instead of the thing; someone replied that I was rediscovering the old philosophical paradox of hedonism — the idea that deliberately aiming at pleasure tends to make it recede, so that pleasure or happiness is best achieved as a by-product of activities valued for their own sake. I have only a rough grasp of that tradition, and I am suspicious that the analogy with reward hacking is flattering to my field rather than faithful to the philosophy.

Restate the paradox and its main moves in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, flagging wherever you stretch a term. Then give me the residue report: which strands translate cleanly into reward hacking, Goodhart’s law, or specification gaming; which ones you had to distort to fit; and which have no counterpart in an RL frame — above all, what is lost or preserved when by-product is rendered as proxy reward or unintended side effect. Give me exactly five numbered items, no introduction and no conclusion, each item one or two sentences; each item should pair one strand of the paradox with its nearest RL analogue and say, in the same breath, whether it survives the translation.
