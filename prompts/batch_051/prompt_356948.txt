Question: Explain Nozick's truth-tracking account of propositional knowledge. Lay out the four conditions, explain why Nozick relativizes the conditions to a method of belief formation, and use at least one example to show how the account handles a case of true belief that intuitively falls short of knowledge. Then discuss what the account implies about whether knowledge is closed under known entailment.

Student answer:

Nozick's tracking theory is externalist: whether you know p depends not on whether you can cite good reasons, but on whether your belief tracks the truth. The four conditions are: (1) p is true; (2) S believes that p; (3) if p were false, S would not believe that p—this is the safety condition; and (4) if p were true, S would believe that p—the adherence condition. Knowledge is true belief whose formation is counterfactually responsive to p's truth-value.

Nozick relativizes the conditions to a method of belief formation because the same belief could be produced in different ways, and whether someone knows can depend on how the belief was produced. The method belongs to the justification condition of the analysis: S knows only if the method that produced the belief is part of what S is justified in using. In method-relative form the two counterfactual conditions become: (3*) if p were false and S used M to arrive at a belief about p, then S would not believe via M that p; and (4*) if p were true and S used M, then S would believe via M that p.

Take a stopped clock. Smith glances at a clock that stopped and happens to read 12:00, which is the correct time, so he forms the true belief that it is 12:00. Intuitively he doesn't know this. The tracking account explains why: in the nearby worlds where the time is different, say 12:05, the clock still reads 12:00, so Smith would go on believing it is 12:00. Thus condition (3) fails, and he lacks knowledge. By contrast, condition (4) is satisfied here—if it were 12:00, he would believe it is 12:00—but that is not enough for knowledge without condition (3).

On closure, Nozick's account implies that knowledge is not closed under known entailment, and he accepts that consequence. Suppose Smith sees a zebra in the zoo and comes to know there is a zebra in the pen. He can deduce that the animal is not a cleverly painted mule. But on the tracking theory he does not know this entailed proposition. If the animal were a cleverly painted mule, he would still believe it was not a mule, because he would believe it was a zebra. So the sensitivity condition fails for the entailed belief, even though it is satisfied for the belief that there is a zebra. Knowledge therefore does not transmit along the entailment, which is exactly the closure failure Nozick is famous for.
