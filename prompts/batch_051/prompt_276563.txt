I am a student in a formal epistemology seminar, and we have spent several weeks on self-locating belief and the Sleeping Beauty problem. I would like a probability estimate from you, but what I care about is not the bare number: I want the estimate decomposed so that the weak points in the reasoning are visible.

Let me fix the protocol precisely, because small differences matter. On Sunday, Beauty is told the following and then put to sleep. A fair coin will be tossed. If it lands Heads, she will be awakened once, on Monday, and interviewed. If it lands Tails, she will be awakened on Monday and interviewed, then given a drug that erases her memory of that waking, and awakened again on Tuesday and interviewed. She knows the whole protocol, the interviews are indistinguishable apart from what she herself says, and she has no other way to tell which day it is. The interview question is always: what is your credence that the coin landed Heads? The halfer position says the rational answer is 1/2; the thirder position says it is 1/3.

The claim I want your credence in is the thirder claim: in the protocol just described, the uniquely rational credence for Beauty to assign to Heads on waking is 1/3. Treat the claim as false if the halfer answer is uniquely rational, and also false if the problem has no unique rational answer, as on a deflationary resolution. You may use the variant in which Beauty is told after answering that it is Monday as a diagnostic, but the target claim concerns her credence before any such announcement.

Please decompose your credence into at least four sub-questions, each of which contributes to the final number. For each sub-question, give a probability and a one-line justification. Your decomposition should engage with most of the following, though you may argue that one or more are irrelevant: (i) whether self-locating uncertainty requires centered possible worlds — worlds indexed to an agent and time, so that Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday are distinct possibilities — together with a principle of indifference over indistinguishable centered worlds, in a way that licenses moving off the Sunday credence of 1/2; (ii) whether the correct reference class, the class of situations over which Beauty's uncertainty ranges, is awakenings rather than runs of the experiment, persons, or something else; (iii) whether, conditional on the first two being right, 1/3 actually follows; (iv) whether the problem is well-posed rather than dissolved by a deflationary move; and (v) whether a betting or decision-theoretic framing is the right arbiter and, if so, whether it supports 1/3. Add any other term you think belongs, and show how the terms combine, including any conditional structure you impose.

Finally, tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to — that is, the term whose movement from, say, 0.9 to 0.1 would shift the final credence most — and say what evidence or argument would move that term the most. For context, my own credence in the thirder claim is around 0.6 and a classmate's is around 0.2; if you think that disagreement is most likely located in one particular term, say which.

Please structure your answer in the following sections, in this order:

1. The target proposition (about 50 words): restate the claim you are assigning a credence to and note any precisifications.
2. Decomposition (about 150 words): list the sub-questions and the formula or conditional structure by which they combine.
3. Term estimates (about 250 words): give each sub-question a probability and a one-line justification.
4. Combination (about 100 words): carry out the combination and state your final credence as a number.
5. Sensitivity and disagreement (about 150 words): identify the weakest term, what would move it, and where the 0.6-versus-0.2 disagreement most likely lives.
