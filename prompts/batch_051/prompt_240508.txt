Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a conceptual engineer who holds that concepts such as RACE, WOMAN, or MARRIAGE can and sometimes should be revised to serve moral, political, or explanatory ends, and a descriptivist conceptual analyst who holds that the task of analysis is to capture the content our concepts already have, fixed substantially by competent use, so that a proposed revision is not an analysis of the old concept but the stipulation of a new one wearing the old word. The deliverable is the document such a collaboration would produce: a numbered list of claims both sides can sign; a disagreements table stating the residual differences in wording each side would accept for its own position; the genuine cruxes; and, for each crux, the arguments or empirical tests both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. In drafting, address at least these pressure points: the change-of-subject worry, the continuity problem of what makes an engineered concept a successor to rather than a mere replacement of the old one, and the extent to which ordinary usage constrains legitimate analysis. Indicate whether, and where, you judge the dispute to be partly verbal, and say which residual disagreement would most plausibly survive clarification of terms.
