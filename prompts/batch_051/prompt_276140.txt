I have noticed a parallel between two ideas I know from different contexts, and I cannot tell whether it is a genuine structural resemblance or just a shared figure of speech.

In debates about human enhancement, a great deal depends on the line between therapy and enhancement. Therapy is supposed to repair a defect or restore normal functioning; enhancement is supposed to improve a person who is already normal. Setting a broken leg is therapy; giving a healthy student a drug to push concentration beyond its ordinary range is enhancement. The same intervention can count as either depending on where the baseline of normal is drawn.

In programming there is an analogous line between a bug fix and a feature. A bug fix makes the program do what its specification already says it should do; a feature changes the specification. Fixing a crash and adding a new tool both alter the code, but they are intuitively different kinds of change.

The parallel seems strong at first. Both distinctions require a baseline that is not written into the thing itself: a spec in software, some standard of normal human functioning in medicine. Both are supposed to carry practical, even moral, weight — correcting something is generally regarded as legitimate, while going beyond the baseline is where the doubts begin. And the hard cases align. If a program crashes on an input its spec never anticipated, is preventing that crash a fix or a feature? If a child is short but otherwise healthy, is giving growth hormone therapy or enhancement? In both cases the act itself does not decide; the baseline does.

But the analogy might break. A program has an author and an explicit spec; a human being does not. What counts as normal human functioning looks like a statistical or evolutionary fact, and it takes a further argument to turn that into a standard that medicine ought to restore. Moreover, no one thinks adding features to software is automatically suspect, while 'mere' enhancement is often treated as suspect in itself. That suggests the therapy/enhancement line may be doing different work from the fix/feature line.

Is this a real structural parallel? What would have to be true of 'normal human functioning' for the fix/feature distinction to transfer to the ethics of enhancement? And does the analogy make any prediction about enhancement that turns out false once you think it through?
