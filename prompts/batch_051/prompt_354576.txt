One chapter lays out global workspace theory, and a summary paragraph near the end makes a strong claim that I don't know how to evaluate. I'd like a careful accuracy audit of that summary, because the book is otherwise very confident and I want to know which parts I can rely on.

As I reconstruct the summary in my own words, it goes roughly like this: The scientific study of consciousness treats consciousness as the global availability of information in the brain. According to global workspace theory, many specialized processors compete for access to a limited-capacity workspace; whichever signal wins is broadcast across the brain to systems for memory, language, planning, and motor control. Information that isn't broadcast stays unconscious. This explains why we can verbally report some contents and not others: a content becomes reportable exactly when it enters the global workspace. Some philosophers distinguish access consciousness from phenomenal consciousness — the difference, roughly, between information being usable by the rest of the mind and there being something it is like to have it. But in effect these are the same thing, because the only evidence we can ever have about phenomenal consciousness comes from subjects' reports, and subjects can report only what is globally broadcast. So a state is phenomenally conscious exactly when, and because, its content is broadcast in the workspace. Once we have explained the broadcast mechanism, we have explained experience itself; whatever seems left over is an illusion or a confusion of vocabulary.

My instinct as an engineer is that the last step is too quick — something like proving that a frame buffer is displaying an image merely because the data has been written to it. But I don't know whether that's a fair analogy or where exactly the summary goes wrong, if it does. Please go through it inference by inference: which claims would a philosopher of mind or consciousness researcher accept as accurate, which are fair simplifications, which are contested, and whether the final claim follows from the accurate parts or depends on sliding between two senses of conscious.
