I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been working on a piece about why "compassion fatigue" is treated as a failure of endurance rather than as a clue that something is wrong with compassion itself. The usual framing is that burnout happens because people care to much and run out of emotional fuel. But there's an older, more abrasive argument that caring too much is itself the pathology, not exhaustion. I keep coming back to the fight between Schopenhauer and Nietzsche. Schopenhauer, in On the Basis of Morality, puts compassion at the center of ethics: as I recall the argument (I can't quote it exactly from memory), he says that only actions springing from compassion, understood as an immediate participation in another's suffering rather than calculated sympathy or moral principle, have genuine moral worth. Nietzsche, especially in On the Genealogy of Morals, turns that on its head. His line, as I read it, is that what we call compassion is a disguised assertion of power over the sufferer, a contagion of weakness, and that it actually increases the total amount of suffering by preserving and multiplying failure. I want to know if that position can be argued in a way that isn't just adolescent edginess.

So here's what I'd like. Please argue for the Nietzschean position: that compassion, even when sincere and warm, is more psychologically toxic and ethically suspect than its defenders admit. Don't sermonize or give me a list of cultural complaints. I need the actual philosophical case. Structure the response in five titled sections. "The claim" (around 100 words): state exactly what you're arguing and define compassion. "The central argument" (around 350 words): give the strongest possible Nietzschean argument, drawing on his critique of pity and on your own reasoning about how compassion functions. "The strongest objection" (around 200 words): put Schopenhauer or a modern defender of compassion back on the table and make their best counterargument. "The rebuttal" (around 200 words): show why that objection complicates but doesn't defeat the Nietzschean position, and concede anything you actually need to concede. "For a journalist" (around 150 words): give me the single most defensible sentence I could quote from your argument, and flag the most common misreading I should avoid in print.
