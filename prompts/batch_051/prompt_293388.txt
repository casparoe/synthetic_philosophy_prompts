I'm trying to figure out what a clause in a video platform's terms of service commits them to. The clause reads: "We will remove User Content only if it is unlawful in the User's country of residence or in the country where the servers storing that content are located." A user has spent the last two years living on a container ship registered in Panama and uploads from a satellite link; he has citizenship in two countries but isn't resident in either. He posts a commentary video that is lawful in Panama and in both citizenship countries but unlawful in Turkey, where it circulates widely and where a court has ordered the platform to geo-block it. The service has since moved to a peer-to-peer network with no central servers, so the "servers are located" phrase has no obvious referent. What does the clause require or permit in this case, and what do a textual reading, a purposive reading, a what-the-drafters-would-have-said reading, and a reasonable-reader reading each give? Is there any plausible interpretation under which the company is actually required to comply with the Turkish order?
