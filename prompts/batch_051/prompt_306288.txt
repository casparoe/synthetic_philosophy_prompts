I'm a novelist, and the book I'm currently drafting turns on a loose network of climate engineers — they call themselves the Salt Line — who work on stratospheric aerosol injection, with no real hierarchy. Any member is free to publish a design or run a small field test on their own, and the only thing that reliably stops someone is the opinion of Priya, one of the founding members; so far everyone has followed her calls, and I want to make sure her character is actually running some coherant rule rather than just doing whatever the plot needs.

Her track record, as I've written it so far: when Arif wanted to publish a review paper that mostly rehashed existing injection methods, she said fine. When Jules wanted to publish a method for steering a sulfate cloud so it dries out a chosen watershed — a kind of regional drought tool — she said no. When Mina wanted to run a tiny, fully reversible field release over the South Pacific, a few grams, technically against a voluntary moratorium, she told her to go ahead and log it. And when Sam found a real weakness in a widely used aerosol-transport model and wanted to publish it, she said yes but only after giving the model maintainers six weeks to patch first, because once it's out it's out.

In the current draft, a newer member, Idris, has found an additive that makes sulfate aerosols persist for years instead of months. It would make any future deployment much cheaper and more reliable, but it also means you can't quickly switch a deployment off once it's up there — the effects would be locked in for a long time. He wants to publish the recipe on his own. What should Priya decide if she wants to be consistent with her past calls? Which of her earlier decisions is the closest analogy? If the record genuinely underdetermines it, what principle would she be committing to with each possible verdict? And is there a further case I could write in that would force the rule into the open? (A friend told me this whole bind has a name, the unilateralist's curse; if that's the right frame, I'd like to know how it applies here.)
