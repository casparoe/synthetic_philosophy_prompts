Before I read any more proposals for what a future would have to be like to be actually good to live in — eudaemonist, preference-satisfaction, capability, and longtermist accounts, among others — I want the specification first. Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of a desirable future must satisfy, where the purpose is to adjudicate among rival pictures of a good future rather than to design policy. The list should include the cases such a theory must get right (for example, futures that are pleasant but empty, futures endorsed under adaptive preferences, and futures whose goodness depends on the inhabitants having been shaped to be satisfied); the distinctions it may not collapse (such as good for a person versus good overall, experienced versus remembered well-being, the value of the end state versus the value of the path to it, and feasibility versus desirability); and the constraints it must honor (for example, a future's value should not depend on facts no one could assess from here, and a defensible account should not recommend a future that can only be made attractive by changing the values of those who initially disagree with it). For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it; mark which are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of a particular camp; and flag any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. A table for the requirements is fine.
