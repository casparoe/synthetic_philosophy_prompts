A few of us in my metaphysics reading group are trying to get the resident presentist and the resident eternalist to stop arguing past each other. Write the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between them should have produced. I want a short numbered list of substantive claims both would actually sign (not just "time exists"), then a table of the residual disagreements with each side's view phrased the way that side would phrase it, then the cruxes. I suspect one crux is what makes a past-tensed truth like "there were dinosaurs" true if the past doesn't exist; another is whether a single frame-independent global "now" can be squared with special relativity; and a third is whether the way we feel differently about past and future pain is evidence for one side or the other. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and say in advance what each side predicts about it. Don't pick a winner — the test is whether each of them could sign the whole document as a fair account of their disagreement. After the draft, say how confident you are in it and single out the point you're least sure of.
