Okay so my friend and I just had this huge argument about what emotions are, and by the end neither of us could produce a definition that survived for five minutes. She read some William James in college and was basically defending the idea that an emotion just is the perception of your bodily state — fear is the racing heart in a dangerous-looking situation, nothing on top of that. I kept hitting her with cases where the thought seems to be doing all the work, like being quietly furious about an injustice you only read about, affecting people you'll never meet. We ended with a pile of counterexamples and no working definition, which is why I'm coming to you.

I want a working definition of emotion — not a synonym, not a list of examples — that gets the following cases right, or at least tells me honestly where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. I'll give my leanings so you know what I'm counting as right, but I'd rather have a clean definition that overrules a couple of my leanings than a thing with six ad hoc clauses bolted on.

The cases:

- A dog lunges at you and before you've formed a thought your heart is pounding and you're already backing away. (Definitely an emotion: fear.)
- Your stomach's been empty all day and you feel hungry. (Definitely not; that's a sensation.)
- A car backfires right behind you and you flinch. (I lean no — it's a reflex, too fast and to dumb.)
- You read about a law that will quietly ruin people you'll never meet and you feel cold, clear outrage. (Definitely an emotion for me, and this is the one that broke my friend's bodily-feeling view. The judgment seems to be doing the work.)
- Someone you love has been missing for years, and you live with a low-level anxious dread that doesn't attach to any particular thought, image, or danger you can name. (I genuinely don't know. Emotion? Mood? And is that difference part of the definition or just a matter of duration?)
- A character you know is fictional sacrifices themselves in a film, and you tear up. (I want to call this an emotion, but the object is strange.)
- You've slept four hours and everything your partner does is suddenly enraging. (Is the irritability itself the emotion, or only the individual flare-ups?)
- A chord change gives you goosebumps — no words, no scene, no thought, nothing you could say the feeling is about. (I'd call it a feeling; maybe an emotion; the total lack of an object worries me.)

Run your definition through all eight and tell me where it surprises you, where it just shrugs, and whether you'd rather revise the definition or bite the bullet on the weird verdicts. And since the original fight was about whether emotions are judgments, perceptions, or feelings, tell me which of those your definition ends up closest to — or whether that three-way choice was actually the problem all along.
