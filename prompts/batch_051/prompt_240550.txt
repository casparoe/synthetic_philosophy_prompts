I sit on a commission that evaluates proposals for a self-contained settlement. We score each proposal solely by the lifetime well-being of its inhabitants, in points: zero is a neutral life, positive scores are lives worth living, and higher scores are better. We have set aside questions of identity across proposals, so a proposal is characterized just by how many people it would contain and the well-being score at which each lives.

Our precedents so far:

1. Between Proposal A (1,000 people at 100 points each) and Proposal B (2,000 people at 80 points each), we judged Proposal B better.
2. Between Proposal C (500 people at 250 points each) and Proposal D (1,000 people at 100 points each), we judged Proposal C better.
3. Between Proposal E (2,000 people at 80 points each) and Proposal F (1,000 people at 100 points each plus a further 1,000 people at 50 points each), we judged Proposal E better.

We must now decide between Proposal G (1,000 people at 100 points each) and Proposal H (2,000 people at 55 points each).

I want us to be consistent with our own track record. What should we decide, which of our precedents is the closest analog, and what principle would we be adopting with each possible decision? In particular, are there distinct principles that fit all three precedents equally well but disagree about G versus H — and if so, what further comparison would cleanly settle the difference between them?
