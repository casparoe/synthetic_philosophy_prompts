Tolstoy's account of art rests on the idea that a work is art only insofar as the artist, having lived through some feeling, deliberately transmits that feeling to an audience, which then experiences it in turn; what marks the genuine article off from a counterfeit is not beauty or formal ingenuity but the sincerity and individuality of the feeling passed on. Call this the transmission view.

Consider a later development Tolstoy did not face. A large share of the images, texts, and music now encountered in settings we call artistic is produced by generative models trained on vast corpora of human work. Such a system can produce objects that move audiences, but there is no lived feeling in the producer that is sincerely handed over; the audience's response is elicited by statistically recombined patterns rather than received from another experiencing subject.

Does the transmission view survive this development? I want the response to identify which premise is genuinely under pressure — the need for a feeling to originate in a producer, the need for the same feeling to be passed on, or the role of sincerity in separating art from counterfeit — and to say whether a repaired version can preserve the core of Tolstoy's position while accommodating such objects. If it can, give the repaired criterion and say what it excludes that the original excluded. If it cannot, say what a Tolstoyan should call these objects and whether that outcome is a strength or a weakness of the view.
