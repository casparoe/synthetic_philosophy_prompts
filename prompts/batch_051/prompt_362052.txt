Construct a thought experiment where the two usual sides in the griefbot debate switch places. By a griefbot I mean a text or voice chatbot, current or near-term tech, trained on a dead persons messages and recordings so it can produce new messages in their style. The two sides: (1) the wellbeing or continuing-bonds side, which normally says "if it genuinely helps the bereaved, let them use it" (healthy grief often keeps a bond with the dead going), and (2) the dignity side, which normally says "even if it comforts someone, don't put new words in the mouth of the dead." I want a case where side (1) says the bot should be turned off and side (2) says it's fine to keep using it — the reverse of their usual verdicts.

Constraints: ordinary life, no sci-fi, one-paragraph scenario. The deceased never said anything about bots or being simulated or what they'd want after death, so the flip can't come from the deceased settling the question. The bereaved person is roughly okay by outside measures — working, seeing friends, not confused about the bot being alive. And the dignity side's "keep it" verdict can't be bought cheaply, like by making the user only rerun old messages; the bot does generate new things in the dead person's voice. This is for a paper on why the griefbot debate is drawn along the wrong fault line, so the flip has to come from something genuinely load-bearing, not a stray detail.

Then do the walk-through. State each side's verdict and the reasoning behind it, and quickly verify the case hits the constraints above. After that, identify the single feature of the case that's actually flipping both verdicts at once, say what happens if I remove just that feature (does it fall back to the standard direction?), and finish with the strongest way a defender of the wellbeing view would attack your case as rigged.
