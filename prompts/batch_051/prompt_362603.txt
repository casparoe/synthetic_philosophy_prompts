I keep coming back to the Zhuangzi bit where he wakes up from dreaming he's a butterfly and says he can't tell whether he's a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming he's a man. The usual read is that this is a dream-skepticism argument: any feature of waking life that seems to distinguish it from dreaming could itself be dreamt, so the distinction is not available from inside experience. So here's the thing I want to know. Suppose we now have a cheap, wearable EEG device that detects REM sleep in real time with near-perfect accuracy, syncs to your phone, and your phone can tell you—or someone else in the room—whether you're currently dreaming. Would Zhuangzi's argument survive this? On one hand, the device seems to give exactly the kind of external check the old argument said was impossible. On the other hand, the whole point might be that no check occurs outside the experience you're trying to validate, because the buzzing phone saying you're awake could itself be part of the dream. I'm intersted in the argument, not the neuroscience. Does the butterfly dream still have skeptical force once dream detection is routinized, or does it just change where the uncertainty sits? If it survives, say which premise of the argument the device leaves untouched. If it doesn't, say what the remaining problem should be called.
