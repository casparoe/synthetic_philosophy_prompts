okay so I'm teaching a grad seminar next term on verbal disputes and metaontology, and half the students call themselves deflationists without being able to say what question they're actually answering. I want a diagnostic that sorts people by what they believe, not by what they call themselves.

Build me a "which metaontologist are you?" decision tree. It should route someone from their gut reactions to a few concrete disputes to one of these leaves (use these glosses as the target positions): Carnap-style deflationism — external existence questions are practical choices of framework, not factual; Hirsch-style quantifier variance — the disputants are using different quantifier meanings, so each speaks truly in their own idiolect; Thomasson-style easy ontology — existence questions are settled by trivially valid inferences from uncontroversial facts; heavyweight realism — existence questions are deep and have determinate, mind-independent answers that serious metaphysics has to dig out; and fictionalism about numbers — a local leaf, not a global metaontology.

Each branch node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing the labels — "in this case, does at least one of the two say something false?" rather than "are you a deflationist?" — and each leaf should come with a two-sentence gloss saying what position the person landed in and why their answers put them there.

Use at least these three cases somewhere in the nodes: (1) whether there are tables, given that there are simples arranged tablewise; (2) whether there are numbers, given that number-talk is indispensable to science; (3) whether a statue and the clay it's made of are two things or one. Add a fourth case of your own.

Build the tree from logical space, not from the list of names: lay out the cross-cutting commitments first (are existence questions factual? is the existential quantifier univocal? can existence claims follow trivially from uncontroversial truths? does talk without literally existing objects make sense?), say which combinations are occupied by the five leaves, and flag any combinations nobody occupies — for each, say briefly whether the spot is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Then flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't cleanly seperate, and for each say what extra question would be needed to pull them apart.

Keep it to maybe 8-12 nodes; if your tree would need more, compress and tell me where you compressed.
