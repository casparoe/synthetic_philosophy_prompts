Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of anthropic reasoning must satisfy: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not collapse, and the constraints it must meet in fixing self-locating probabilities and constraining credences about how many observers a hypothesis predicts. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of a particular camp (for instance, the self-sampling/self-indication split over whether one's own existence is evidence, or whether the presumptuous philosopher is a reductio rather than a cost to be accepted). The part I most want is an assessment of joint satisfiability: say whether any single theory can simultaneously give a well-motivated verdict on Sleeping Beauty, keep the Doomsday argument from becoming an artifact of an arbitrary reference-class choice (including the observer/observer-moment choice), avoid licensing the presumptuous philosopher unless that cost is explicitly endorsed, and respect the difference between ordinary uncertainty about the world and indexical uncertainty about which observer one is. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the standard contenders. A table for the requirements is fine, provided the case behind each requirement is stated.
