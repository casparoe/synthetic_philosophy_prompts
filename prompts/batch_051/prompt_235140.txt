Construct a thought experiment about intergenerational collective guilt that makes the four-way distinction you get from Jaspers come apart in this pattern: the central person is criminally and morally innocent, but politically and metaphysically guilty — and those two collective guilts push in opposite directions on the question of whether she should sign onto her state's new official apology. Work with this rough paraphrase of the categories rather than exact labels, since the case shouldn't depend on the scholarship: (1) criminal guilt: you broke a law and a court judges you for it; (2) political guilt: you're on the hook for what your state does just by being one of it's citizens, whether or not you supported the government, because you live inside the machinery and benefit from its standing; (3) moral guilt: what you did as an individual, judged by your own conscience; (4) metaphysical guilt: you could have done something while the harm was unfolding and didn't, so you're tied to the victims by a kind of solidarity with other humans rather than by any particular act of yours. Constraints: no war, no genocide, no cartoon villains — the wrong should be something a normal democracy could plausibly do to a small group, and something a decent person could plausibly have only dimly known about or felt powerless about at the time. Make the person born after the original act but now an adult, and give the wrong an ongoing tail (say, the state still denies it or refuses repair), so she's been old enough for years to have done something public about it. Make sure she hasn't clearly profited from the wrong in any direct way. After the scenario, do the check: go through the four categories and give the verdict for each, with the reason. Then say which feature of the case you had to engineer to keep the two individual guilts at zero while both collective guilts stay non-zero and opposed. Then say what collapses if you take that feature out, and how someone who thinks nations can't be guilty would attack the case. This is for an intro lesson on responsibility, so the verdicts have to fall out of the categories themselves — just stipulating that she feels guilty doesn't count.
