I'm a software engineer, mostly doing ML, so I've basically been a default Bayesian for years: you put a distribution on everything, every uncertain quantity gets a number, and you update. But after a late-night argument at a meetup about the Ellsberg setup, I tried to write down what I actually believe about probability and rational choice, and now I'm not sure the list coheres. Here's where I've landed:

(1) Real credences should always be precise single numbers; a range like "0.3 to 0.7" is just a sign you havent finished sharpening your own estimate, and if you were careful enough there'd be exactly one number in there.
(2) Preferences between bets should be supportable by at least one single probability distribution over the relevant states: if your choices can't be rationalized as maximizing expected value relative to some distribution, then the choices, not the theory, are what's broken.
(3) In the two-urn Ellsberg case—one urn has 50 red and 50 black, the other has 100 balls in unknown red/black proportions, one draw, win if it comes up your color—I'd rather bet on the known urn for red, and I'd also rather bet on the known urn for black, and I think that pair of preferences is fine rather than a mistake.
(4) Most talk of "imprecise probabilities" is just hedging: the person has a real, precise credence somewhere in the vicinity but doesn't want to admit it.
(5) But in the unknown-urn case, if you put a gun to my head and made me give an exact probability of red, any number I picked would be invented; there's no hidden precise value underneath the fuzziness, not even one I'd eventually converge to with more thought.

So: is this a consistent package, or am I helping myself to something the rest of it rules out? Go through the pairs and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which only look tense, how severe each real tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And since I don't know this literature at all: please separate what's actually settled from what's still genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested position as the consensus.
