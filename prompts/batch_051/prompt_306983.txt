Ok, here's a thing I've been chewing on. I've been trying to get my own mind around the appraisal/bestowal debate about love, and I want to test my list against someone else's philosophical instincts. The question is whether love is a response to value the beloved already has (appraisal) or whether loving someone is what gives them their value to you (bestowal). I've collected four arguments for the bestowal side, and I'm worried I keep reaching for the same handful.

(1) The fungibility argument. If love tracked the beloved's properties—their warmth, their wit, whatever—then a duplicate with all the same properties should be just as lovable, and love ought to transfer to the copy. It doesn't and it wouldn't. So the object of love isn't a set of properties; it's a particular person, and the particlarity has to come from the lover's own investment rather than from anything there before the love.

(2) The constancy argument. Properties change across a life—looks go, personality shifts, sometimes character changes massively—and love very often doesn't revise to track that ledger. If love were appraisal, it should. Bestowal explains the stickiness: once someone is your end, re-running the appraisal would be a sort of category mistake.

(3) The new-reasons argument. Loving someone seems to bring reasons into existence that weren't there before. Her headache gives me a reason to go find aspirin, and not because headaches are antecedently bad in some impersonal ledger. If the reasons arrive only with the love, the value looks like something love creates rather than detects.

(4) The reversal argument. Ask someone why they love their partner and they'll list qualities, but the qualities seem to be loved because they're hers, not the other way around. The crooked nose is adorable because it's her nose. The direction of explanation runs from the beloved to the properties, which is what bestowal predicts and appraisal gets backwards.

Is there a fifth argument for bestowal I'm missing, one that's genuinely different from these four? I don't mean a restatement of the fungibility point or a generalization of the constancy point, or some cocktail of (1)–(4). If there is one, what does it add? And if you think the list is basically complete, tell me which of the four is doing the least independent work and whether it collapses into one of the others.
