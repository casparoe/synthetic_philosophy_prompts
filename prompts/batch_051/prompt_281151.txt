I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have picked up the cause-prioritisation framework that scores a problem on three axes: importance, tractability, and neglectedness. I have turned it into a private decision rule. Whenever a cause, an open-source project, or a donation target comes to my attention, I give each axis a rough score out of ten, multiply the three numbers, and ignore anything whose product falls below a fixed threshold, even when one axis is very high. I have little time to investigate each candidate properly, and the rule protects me from causes that sound urgent but are crowded or hopeless. However, I worry that I am laundering my own guesses through a formula and calling it rigour. Is this shortcut defensible as a way of deciding which causes to take seriously? Where do the multiplication step and the threshold break down? Does the rule assume that the three axes are independent, or that extra effort yields constant returns, or that my scores measure something real; and if those assumptions fail, is there a restated version of the rule that keeps the protection while forcing my assumptions into the open?
