The constitutional version works the same way: the people in a constitutional convention are Ulysses, the legislature is the later tempted Ulysses, and the courts are the crew, since their job is to refuse to go along with the people's short-sighted urgings. The standard objection is the dead hand problem: the framers are long dead, so the current generation is being governed by a past one that never asked for its consent. But this only works if we treat the later self as more authoritative than the earlier self, which is just what precommitment denies, so the objection doesn't really get off the ground."
