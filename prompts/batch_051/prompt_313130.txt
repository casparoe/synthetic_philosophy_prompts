I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about backward induction in the centipede game, and my gut keeps pulling in different directions. Heres my verdicts on a bunch of variants; I want to know if there's one principle behind them, or whether I have to give some up.

Quick setup: two players alternate. On your turn you can take the pot (you get the bigger share, game ends) or pass (pot grows, other player moves). The classic argument says the last player will take, so the second-to-last takes, and it unravels so the first player takes immediately.

Case 1: One-shot, six nodes, lab stakes, random stranger. I'd pass at least once, probably twice. Taking immediately feels like overthinking it, but I also can't say exactly why.

Case 2: One-shot, high stakes, and my opponent is a known perfect game-theoretic reasoner, and we both know we both know it. Here taking immediately feels right. Backward induction wins.

Case 3: Same as case 2 except I'm not sure she knows I'm rational — she knows I'm smart, but maybe thinks I might be a cooperator or an honor type. Now passing early feels reasonable to me, even though she's rational.

Case 4: Finitely repeated centipede, twenty rounds with the same partner, and round twenty is known to be the last. I'd pass in early rounds and only start taking near the end. The unraveling-from-the-last-round argument just doesn't move me for round one.

Case 5: One-shot, with a 5% chance my partner is a "grim passer" who passes every node no matter what. Pass early, obviously.

Case 6: One-shot against a known perfect reasoner (common knowledge again), but the payoffs are set up so that at the final node the last player gets exactly the same whether she takes or passes. Now I would pass early even though everyone is rational and knows it.

Case 7 (the one I keep flip-flopping on): One-shot against a perfect reasoner, common knowledge as in case 2, except the cost of being wrong is tiny — if I pass and she takes next, I get only a little less than if I'd taken, while if she passes the pot grows a lot. My gut says pass, but backward induction still technically says take, and I can't tell if my gut is just ignoring a strict preference because it's small.

Is there a single principle (or small set) that captures exactly why backward induction feels right in 2 but not in 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 — and what does it say about case 7? State it precisely and tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to drop. And if it says something clean about a case I haven't thought of, give me that too.
