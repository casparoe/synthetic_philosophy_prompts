Hey, I'm a backend engineer on a trust & safety team at a social platform, and we're stuck on a moderation policy for historical content. We constantly get flagged posts and videos about the Holocaust, the Armenian genocide, and the Nanjing massacre, and we have to sort them into denialism (illegal in several countries where we operate) versus legitimate historical revision, which we do *not* want to ban because normal history is basically one long process of revising itself. Our moderators are not historians and I'm not either. What I'd like is a decision tree for our internal policy doc: something a moderator can run through by answering questions about the content itself, without already knowing which moderation bucket the answers lead to.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. Each branch node should be a concrete question a non-historian can answer, e.g. does the content deny the core event outright, or does it accept the event but contest numbers, causes, or responsibility; does it treat one missing kind of evidence (say, no signed written order) as enough to throw out everything while ignoring the rest of the evidence; does it demand a much stricter standard of proof for the genocide than for whatever alternative story it pushes; does it engage with contrary evidence or just call it forged or a conspiracy; does the conclusion look fixed before the evidence is weighed. Phrase every node so the moderator doesn't need to know the leaf labels.

The leaves should be the verdicts we'd act on: mainstream consensus, ordinary revisionism, fringe-but-sincere scholarship, denialism, and maybe a 'political instrumentalization that isn't denialism' bucket. For each leaf give one sentence on the moderation action and one sentence on the most awkward case it has to swallow. I want about 6 to 8 nodes and 5 or 6 leaves max. If some combination of answers has no actual position sitting there, mark it as empty and say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Please flag the pairs the tree genuinely can't separate. I suspect a post that minimizes death tolls can be ordinary scholarly dispute or denialism depending on how it handles evidence, and a sophisticated denialist can dress a post up with footnotes and pass for fringe scholarship. If the tree can't tell those apart, say so, and say what a human reviewer would need to check before deciding. I'd rather have an honest tree that sends ambiguous cases to a review queue then one that pretends its accurate.

Also, once the tree is built, run these three through it and show where they land: a post saying the Armenian deaths were really civil war, starvation, and disease rather than an organized Ottoman extermination plan; a post saying the Nanjing death toll was deliberately inflated for political reasons; and a post saying the Holocaust gas chambers were technically implausible as described. That'll help us check that the thing actually does work.
