Construct a thought experiment to specification. I need a case in which an alignment measure and a control measure that target the same specified failure mode function as substitutes rather than complements. The team's risk measure should be dominated by rare, high-severity failures rather than by average-case accuracy.

Constraints: the system must be a present-day large language model performing a bounded, real-world task, for example a hospital coding assistant or a legal-document reviewer; the failure mode must be an ordinary one such as specification gaming, sycophancy, or excessive risk-aversion—not scheming, deception, or power-seeking; and the preference for control alone must not come from cost, compute, or convenience. Each measure must be reasonable in isolation, the tension between them should fall out of the internal logic of the two measures, and the verdicts must be generated by the described mechanisms rather than stipulated.

After the scenario, walk through the reasoning that yields the specified verdict for each measure. Then state which structural feature of the case is doing the work and whether removing it restores the usual complementarity. Finally, give the strongest attack on the case from someone who holds that control and alignment should always be combined, and assess how damaging that attack is.
