I've been trying to sort out where I actually stand on the way apps and other products try to capture and direct my attention, and my own reactions refuse to line up into anything tidy. I'm not an academic or a designer; I work in a print shop, and I only started thinking about this after I deleted a couple of apps and noticed that I felt relieved about some of them and strangely bereft about others.

Here are my gut verdicts, just as they came to me. When a streaming service starts the next episode automatically, I think that's fine, even though I know it's there to keep me watching; it feels like it's continuing a thing I already sat down to do. When a social feed has no bottom and keeps serving me whatever it predicts will keep me scrolling, I think that's not fine, even though it's also just keeping me doing something I already chose to do. A meditation app that pings me in the evening to remind me it's time for my ten-minute session is fine, because it's helping me do something I already decided I wanted to do. A fitness app that guilt-trips me if I'm about to lose my streak is mostly fine, even though I resent it a little; I set the goal, and the guilt feels like willpower I've outsourced. A booking site that announces that only two rooms are left and that three dozen other people are looking at the listing strikes me as not fine, even if it's true; it seems designed to make me act from panic rather than from my actual wishes. A mobile game with daily rewards and timed events that keep pulling me back feels not fine, even though I freely installed it; after a month it felt like an obligation that had been quietly written into my mornings. A news app that sends a headline chosen to outrage me, which I click even though I reliably feel worse afterward, also feels not fine.

What I can't do is say why the streaming autoplay and the endless feed are different to me, or why the meditation app's nudge is fine while the game's daily pull isn't, since in both pairs the product is working on me to keep me engaged with something I chose. Is there a single principle that captures all seven verdicts, or close to it? If there is, state it precisely, say which verdicts it can't quite accommodate, and tell me what it predicts for two cases I haven't listed: an ebook app that automatically turns the book I'm reading into a continuous feed of short excerpts, and a smart speaker that suggests a new album it knows I'd like at the moment I finish one. If there isn't one principle, tell me which of my verdicts I should overturn and what the best simple compromise looks like.
