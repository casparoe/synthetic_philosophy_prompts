The bot will say "It's raining," and then, a minute later, when asked what it believes, it'll say "I don't believe it's raining." The joined-up sentence, It's raining but I don't believe it, isn't a plain contradiction, which is exactly what's annoying me. I want to pin down where the brokenness lives, so I wrote out some variants. For each one, give me a clear verdict — broken in the same way, broken but less severly, or completely fine — and then commit to one explanation that covers the whole pattern. I don't want a menu of possible readings; give me your best theory and defend it.

- It's raining, but I don't know it.
- It's raining, but I think it isn't raining.
- It's raining, but I'm not sure it's raining.
- It's raining, but I wish it weren't raining.
- It was raining an hour ago, but I didn't believe it then.
- It'll rain tomorrow, but I don't believe it will.
- It's raining, but my coworker doesn't believe it is.
