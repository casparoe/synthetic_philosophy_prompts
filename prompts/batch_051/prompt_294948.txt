I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I've ended up weirdly interested in causation because our incident postmortems keep forcing us to label "causal factors," and I've noticed we don't actually agree on what that means. I'd rather have a sharp answer then a textbook.

I've been keeping a private log of the verdicts of Priya, our most senior SRE, who has the strongest and most consistent gut about which things caused an outage. Her track record from the last year:

- A deploy cut the connection pool from 100 to 10, and then a normal traffic spike killed the service. She listed the deploy as a cause.
- Two deploys landed the same afternoon; neither alone would have broken anything, but together they exhausted a file-descriptor limit. She insisted both deploys be listed as causes, separately, and pushed back on writing "the combined release" as a single cause.
- Someone disabled the watchdog job that normally restarts a hung service, and the service later hung and stayed down. She listed the watchdog disable as a cause, not just the hang.
- When someone half-jokingly asked whether the datacenter's mains power caused a rack fire, she said no: background conditions don't go in the list.

Sometimes she explains herself as "would it have happened without this thing?", other times as "was this thing part of the setup that was actually enough to do it?", so I genuinely can't tell whether she's running one rule or two, or whether she thinks there's a fact of the matter at all.

Ben landed a config value that, on its own, would also have crashed the primary database. Either one was sufficient by itself; both went out in the same release and the database went down instantly. I want to predict Priya's verdict before the meeting.

What should I predict, which earlier case is the closest analog, and what implicit account of what a "cause" is would each possible answer commit her to? If her track record underdetermines the new case, tell me what future incident would cleanly settle which rule she's actually running.
