In my course on market-based approaches to morality, we have been discussing whether a person's moral lapses can be balanced against compensating good deeds, and I want to understand how a concrete moral trade exchange would behave if it were actually run. Consider the following design. A small city creates a ledger system in which each resident may record self-reported debits for actions drawn from an annually revised list of behaviors the community has agreed to discourage, such as buying fast fashion, eating factory-farmed meat, taking a short-haul flight, or missing a monthly civic volunteering shift. Each debit type carries a fixed price set annually by a rotating citizens' board, which may revise prices in response to the previous year's participation. Residents clear debits by purchasing credits from a whitelist of independently audited charitable projects matched to the relevant category of harm, such as animal welfare, emissions reduction, or poverty relief. Credits bought before a debit is logged count at one and a half times their face value, while credits bought after a debit count once. Residents may not buy credits from projects that employ them, and credits cannot be resold. Ledgers are private by default, but residents may publish a quarterly net balance, and two local housing cooperatives have begun requiring a non-negative balance from prospective tenants.

Treat this scheme as a mechanism rather than as a moral position to be defended or rejected. Walk through what it does to who joins, who leaves, and who finishes each quarter with a positive balance; how it changes daily behavior, and in particular whether the debit prices reduce the targeted behaviors or mainly license them for a fee; where the scheme is first gamed, and how the combination of self-reporting and audited credits interacts over time; and what second-order effects appear once a clean balance becomes a condition for membership in desirable housing. Say which failure mode is most serious and whether it can be fixed without gutting the scheme.
