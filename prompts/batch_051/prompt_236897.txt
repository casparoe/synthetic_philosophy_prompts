In tech-policy circles one increasingly hears the analogy that paying with personal data is like paying with money: users exchange a marginal piece of data for a service, their observed choices thereby reveal the value they place on privacy, and where consent was given there is little left to criticize. I am a PhD student in information ethics, and I would like you to audit how far this analogy actually carries. Please produce a correspondence table (what plays the role of money, price, buyer, seller, good, contract, and budget); then identify the main disanalogies and sort them into those that matter for the conclusion that revealed valuations plus consent dissolve privacy objections and those that are merely incidental; state what substantive commitments about the nature and value of privacy the analogy quietly assumes; and end by saying whether the conclusion survives the audit and, if not, what a better analogy would be.
