A colleague of yours maintains that talk of national character need not be shorthand for crude stereotype. On his view, the interesting claim is that the citizens of a country share, to a statistically measurable degree, a transmitted and relatively enduring pattern of temperament, value, or habit of mind — a character-like dispositional core that is partly independent of the country's current institutions, laws, incentives, and material circumstances, and that would survive substantial institutional change or emigration in recognisable form. You are inclined to think this claim is either trivially true, because enough reliable differences can always be found between any two large populations, or untestable in precisely the respects that would make it interesting.

Design the strongest feasible empirical test of the non-trivial version of this claim. The test should be one an empirical social scientist could in principle run, and it should not use people's ratings of national character as evidence for the trait itself; such ratings may be treated only as evidence about the stereotype, never as the measure of the supposed trait. Because the claim asserts partial independence from current circumstances, your design must include some feature that separates transmitted character from shared environment and institutions; explain how that feature achieves the separation. Specify the populations or cases to be compared, the outcome or behavioural measure, and the pattern of results that would favour the character-like interpretation over the deflationary, institutional-incentive interpretation — and vice versa.

Then state exactly what a strongly positive result would establish and what it would not establish. In particular, say whether any residual gap between what the test shows and what the claim asserts is merely a limitation of feasible methods or a sign that part of the claim is not empirical at all.

Finally, treat the normative question separately. Suppose the strongest feasible test yielded a robustly positive result: a stable, transmitted, partly portable dispositional pattern was confirmed. Would that result by itself make it acceptable to attribute national-character traits to individual people in ordinary reasoning and decision? If not, specify the further premise that would be needed, and explain why no empirical test can supply it.

Please answer in British English.
