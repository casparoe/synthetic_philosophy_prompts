Two people in my philosophy of mind reading group have been going in circles for months about the split-brain patients, and we are trying to get them to do an adversarial collaboration, one joint document they would both actually sign. Call the two positions the one-subject view and the two-subjects view. The one-subject person thinks the split-brain patient still has a single subject of experience; the hemispheres are just dissociated information channels, and the strange left-hand behavior is sub-personal or confabulated by a language system with incomplete access. The two-subjects person thinks that once the corpus callosum is cut there are at least two distinct streams of consciousness sharing a body, and the apparent unity in daily life is just the speaking side imposing a story. They actually agree on all the standard findings: the speaking left hemisphere rationalizes left-hand actions it did not choose; the right hemisphere can follow commands and can point; in divided-attention setups the hemispheres answer differently; and outside the lab the patient mostly presents as one person. They have both read the same cases and keep talking past eachother.

Draft the statement they should produce. It needs: (1) a short section of numbered claims both accept; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the exact issue in neutral wording and then gives the formulation each side would accept for its own view; (3) the cruxes, the subquestions that would actually move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, an experiment or observation both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of their fight is merely verbal, say which part and why. Keep the table entries terse; better sharp and short than complete. Max 650 words, and if that forces you to drop a crux, drop the weakest one.
