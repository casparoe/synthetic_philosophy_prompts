I've been turning over a conflict between a view I find theoretically attractive and a concrete judgment I can't get rid of, and I want someone to push both as far as they'll go before telling me how they would dissolve it. On the one hand, I keep gravitating toward what I'll call a pure authorization story about public power: no one gets to order other people around unless some valid rule hands them the power to — an election, a succession statute, a delegated emergency power, something you can actually point to. Its appeal is that it's simple, it gives a clean test (show me the rule or the order is just a request with a gun behind it), and it seems like the only thing standing between us and the strongman who says the moment calls for him.

But here's the judgment I can't surrender. Suppose a state's governor, lieutenant governor, and everyone else in the statutory order of succession are killed in a single event — a fire during a joint legislative session or something like that — and the rules genuinely run out: the constitution and the statutes simply name no one. A National Guard commander on the scene orders an evacuation, commandeers fuel and generators, imposes a curfew, and distributes water. On the authorization story she's at that moment a private person with a uniform, and her curfew has exactly as much authority as a stranger shouting at me in the street. I find I cannot actually believe that. I don't just mean I'd comply; I mean I'd treat her as governing, and I'd think my neigbors should too, and I'd think there was something wrong with me if I shrugged and said "that's just a private threat."

What I'm after is not a verdict with a flag planted in it. I want the authorization story defended at its honest strongest — including what it should say about why we might punish emergency actors lightly, or ratify their acts afterward, or call the acts necessary but unlawful, without admitting they had authority during the gap. I want the rival view stated at its strongest too: maybe authority is something you take on when you are actually performing the function of government under conditions of necessity, or maybe the law's silence in a true interregnum is itself a kind of authorization, or maybe "legitimate" and "had authority" come apart more than my clean story allows. And then I want the negotiation: what would justify deciding that my intuition is the thing that has to go (and what sort of error theory would explain why it's so sticky), what would justify amending or abandoning the authorization story instead (and what the least-damaging amendment looks like), and whether there's a reading on which both survive without special-pleading. The thing I want most is to know which considerations should decide which of those three moves is the right one, not just which move you'd personally make.
