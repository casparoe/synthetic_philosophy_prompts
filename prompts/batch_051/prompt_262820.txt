ok so i just got out of a long argument with a friend about luck egalitarianism and im still annoyed. hes totally bought into the brute luck / option luck split: the state should compensate you for bad brute luck but leave the consequences of your own gambles on you. i pushed him on expensive tastes and on the fact that once you trace any piece of bad luck far enough back you usually hit choices made under conditions the person also didnt choose, and he just kept insisting the line is workable. i think the line is doing way more work than it can bear. anyway, can you write a deadpan parody of a government benefits claim form for a fully luck-egalitarian welfare state, maybe 650-750 words, in a flat bureaucratic voice with numbered sections, checkboxes, and a footnote or two. the humor has to come from the actual theory rather than generic bureaucracy gags: the brute luck / option luck distinction, the expensive tastes problem, the preference genealogy problem (how far back do you check whether a taste or disposition was chosen), and the worry that the view abandons people who gambled badly and lost. maybe work in that the same form taxes your unearned talents on the back page because by the view's own logic being born talented is brute luck too. let the standard objections land through the form itself instead of having the form explain them. target the practice of drawing a clean line between what people chose and what just happened to them, not any named person. and keep it deadpan, no winking, so it stays legible to someone who hasnt read the theory but a specialist would feel you actually get it.
