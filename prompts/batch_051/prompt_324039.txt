A buddy of mine has been on a kick lately claiming creativity is basically knob-twiddling on a theme. His story goes: take a theme — a jazz standard, a sonnet form, a genre convention — and imagine it has a set of adjustable parameters; tempo, harmony, line length, whatever the medium allows. The creative act is just finding a knob nobody thought to turn and then turning it a lot, or turning two knobs that never get turned together. He calls it Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model, though I suspect he's flattened it. He then takes this pretty far: he says since a big generative model doing gradient descent is also just adjusting parameters in a huge space, the gap between "variation" and "genuine creativity" is not a gap in kind, and we should stop pretending machine creativity is mysterious. I've heard this argument in some version for years and I don't buy it, but I keep failing to say exactly where it goes wrong. I want you to audit the analogy, not the guy. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: (1) what actually maps onto what between a parameterized model and the kind of variation a human artist does, (2) the first place the mapping breaks for the conclusion he wants, (3) what the knob/turning picture quietly imports about creative choices — the stuff the target might not have, (4) whether his conclusion about AI creativity survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and (5) what a better analogy would be if there is one.
