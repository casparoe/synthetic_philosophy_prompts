At work we've been trying a replace our legacy recommendation service with a cleaner model. The old system is a mess of if-statements nobody fully understands anymore, so a couple of us have been reconstructing what it "actually" optimized by reading its logs and its weird edge cases. I was struck by how much we argue about this: the same behavior can be described as optimizing for recency, for clickbait, for "not getting paged at 3am", depending on what choice set you imagine the original devs were choosing from. Over dinner a friend did the same thing with why his ex ended things—his reconstruction of her goal made total sense, but so did three incompatible ones. And I started to recognize this is exactly inverse reinforcement learning: you observe trajectories and back out a reward function, and the standard results say the observed behavior can be made optimal under lots of different reward functions unless you add extra constraints. You also have to guess the search space—which alternatives the optimizer even considered—or you can't say what the behavior reveals at all.

That feels like the same inference problem we run all the time with people and institutions: given a sequence of choices (code, moves, purchases, whatever), recover the objective and the set of options that were searched, not just the path taken. Is this a real structural identity, or am I just pattern-matching on the word "infer"? If the RL non-identifiability results are real, should they make me much less confident in the everyday versions—reading a colleague's "true" priorities from their commits, debugging code someone else wrote, guessing what our director means by "quality"—or does something about being a human yourself (or having been in the loop) break the analogy and make the inference better conditioned? I'd like the mapping run in both directions: where does behavior-to-objective inference in ML line up with behavior-to-goal inference about a person, and where does it come apart? In particular, name something the parallel would predict about reading people that comes out false. And is the search-space guess doing different work in the two cases?
