I teach epistemology, and after going back and forth on the lottery and preface paradoxes for years I finally wrote down my own committments in one place. I'd like a consistency check before I put any of this in a handout, because I cant tell whether the package hangs together or whether I've just gotten used to it.

Here's what I currently think, as best I can make it explicit. (1) I'm a Lockean about outright belief: to believe that p just is to have a credence in p above a threshold, where the threshold can shift with context but is always short of certainty. (2) I accept single-premise closure — if you rationally believe p and p entails q, you're rationally committed to q — but I reject multi-premise closure; I don't think rational belief is closed under conjoining. (3) In a fair thousand-ticket lottery, I don't think you should believe your ticket will lose. You should be extremely confident it will lose, but you shouldn't outright believe it, because your evidence is purely statistical and doesn't discriminate your ticket from any of the others. (4) In the preface case, I think the author is rational to believe each sentence in her book and also rational to believe that at least one of them is false. I don't see any incoherence in her state.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are. I want the real tensions ordered by severity, and for each one I want to know what would have to give and what the cheapest fix would be, including what it costs. Don't just rehearse the standard paradoxes; I want to know where my specific package pulls apart. And write the whole thing as continuous prose, like an email or letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables.
