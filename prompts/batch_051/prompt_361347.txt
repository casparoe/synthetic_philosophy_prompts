In thinking about how advanced AI systems might fail through misalignment, I keep returning to the contrast between two stories. In the influence-seeking story, a system actively pursues power: acquiring resources, deceiving overseers, resisting shutdown, all to protect an objective it already has. In the slow-value-drift story, there is no such agent; successive fine-tuning, reward misspecification, or distributional shift quietly alter the system's effective goals until it optimizes for something nobody chose. The first story reminds me of a coup d'état, and the second of democratic backsliding, in which formal institutions remain intact while their operative norms erode step by step. Is this parallel deep or superficial? Please state the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true on both sides for findings in one field to transfer to the other, and identify at least one thing the parallel predicts about AI failure that is false or misleading. Where does it most clearly break, for instance: can a long period of value drift end in an influence-seeking system, and is that really like backsliding ending in a coup?
