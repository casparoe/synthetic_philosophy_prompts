A friend and I have just come away from a long, unresolved argument about whether there are any actual infinities. He defended the Cantorian view: completed infinite totalities such as the set of natural numbers, the real line, and the cumulative hierarchy of sets are genuine objects of mathematical reasoning, and Hilbert's Hotel is simply a surprising but coherent feature of how countably infinite collections behave. I defended the finitist view: an infinite is at most potential — a rule or process that can always be extended, never a completed whole — and talk of "the set of all natural numbers" or "all subsets of the natural numbers" is a useful shorthand that should not be read as referring to a finished object. We kept accusing each other of smuggling a conclusion into the phrase "exists in mathematics."

Rather than adjudicate between us, I want you to draft the joint statement that a sophisticated actual-infinitist (Cantorian) and a sophisticated finitist (potential-infinity-only) would produce if they entered an adversarial collaboration. The two camps should be named as such, and the document should be one both could put their names to. Please structure it as follows:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims both camps can sign without hedging — claims about finite arithmetic, about what Cantor's diagonal argument establishes at the formal level before any ontological interpretation, about Hilbert's Hotel and the assumptions under which it produces its results, and about what any adequate philosophy of mathematics must explain. These should be substantive rather than vacuous.
2. A statement of the residual disagreements in precisely worded form: each side's position phrased so that side would accept the wording, with no hidden slant.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one of them would move at least one camp. I suspect these include whether "all subsets of the natural numbers" can be given a finitistically acceptable paraphrase without loss, and whether the apparent coherence of Hilbert's Hotel counts as evidence for coherence or merely for unexamined assumptions. For each crux, give a concrete theorem, constructed case, or agreed procedure that both camps would accept bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts about it.
4. A closing note on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal — for example, about what "exists" or "object" means in mathematics — and, if so, which part evaporates under precise wording and which part remains a genuine disagreement.

After the joint statement, give me a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:
- "agreed_claims": an array of strings, the agreed claims.
- "disagreements": an array of objects, each with keys "issue", "actual_infinitist_view", "finitist_view", and "classification" (either "genuine" or "verbal").
- "cruxes": an array of objects, each with keys "crux", "agreed_test_or_case", "actual_infinitist_prediction", and "finitist_prediction".
