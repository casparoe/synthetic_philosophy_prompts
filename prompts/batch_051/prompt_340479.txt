Write a mock agony-aunt column for a small-town paper: a letter plus the columnists reply, around 650 words total. The letter writer's friend has recently decided to become a moral saint in the philosophical sense — not just a generous person, but someone who now tries to make every single action maximally morally good. The target is the assumption that moral perfection is obviously the best thing a person could aim at. The columnist should be warm, slightly smug, and completely deadpan: she treats the project as an ordinary life choice, sympathizes with the letter writer, and manages to make the friend sound quietly disastrous by the end without ever saying so.

The jokes have to come from getting the actual debate right, not from cheap shots about boring do-gooders. Work in, in a natural way: the difference between the loving saint, who is sincerely happy spending every minute on others (and a little eerie in how total the devotion is), and the rational saint, who acts from duty and sort of resents that you exist; the way being maximally moral crowds out nonmoral excellences like being funny, having taste, wasting an afternoon, or being fun to gossip with; the problem that you can't quite be friends with somebody who is always optimizing you; and the closing thought that maybe morality is one good among others, and we wouldn't wish sainthood on someone we love. A specialist should find it fair rather than a caricature, and a general reader should still find it funny. Stay deadpan — no winking, no summing-up paragraph about balance. No real names, no citations, no named philosophers. American English.
