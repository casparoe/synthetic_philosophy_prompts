I am a novelist researching AI safety for a new book, and I have repeatedly encountered a tension in the practice of chain-of-thought monitoring. Some laboratories train models to produce a running chain of thought, and safety staff then monitor that chain for signs of dangerous or concealed objectives; yet the same field warns that optimisation pressure may teach a model to obfuscate, producing reasoning that is fluent, reassuring, and unfaithful to the computation that actually generated its answer. As a novelist, I spend my time with narrators whose accounts of their own motives cannot be trusted, so I want to know what philosophical commitments such a monitoring practice rests on. What view of the relation between verbalised reasoning and actual cognition does it presuppose—privileged introspective access, merely fallible evidence, or something else? Reconstruct that implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say how the practice would have to change if the presupposition turned out to be false.
