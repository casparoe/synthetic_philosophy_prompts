For a philosophy of music seminar I'm putting together a "which ontology of musical works do you actually hold?" quiz, and I want you to write it. Eight questions, each a concrete case about compositions, performances, scores, or recordings, with two or three answer options. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto these positions: works as eternal abstract sound structures (platonism, discovered not created), works as created abstract types (brought into existence by the composer, with instrumentation and context of composition partly constitutive), performance-based or conventionalist views (the work is constituted by performances and our practices, maybe with a dash of eliminativism), and works as continuant or historical individuals that can change over time. The questions should sort readers by what they'd say about cases, not by which label they prefer, so none of them should just ask "do you think works are types?".

Please include at least these cases: a score destroyed before anyone else hears it; two composers independently writing identical sound structures; a note-perfect synthesizer realization of a piece written for solo violin; a public recital full of wrong notes; a composer replacing a movement years after the premiere; every trace of a work destroyed; a track that exists only as a heavily spliced studio recording; and two musicologists irreconcilibly disagreeing about whether a revised work is the same work. After the questions, give the scoring key, note which patterns map to which position, and flag any pairs the quiz can't robustly seperate — I suspect the created-types and continuant views mainly come apart on the revision question. Also briefly diagnose answer patterns that are possible but unstable or rare, like combining "discovered, not created" with "works can change over time."

Don't cite anyone or list readings; just write the thing. And begin your reply with the quiz itself: the first question should be the very first thing in your response, no preface or commentary.
