I'm a novelist, so I spend an embarrassing amount of time trying to describe what its like to walk into a room, flinch at a sound, or recognize a face. Lately I've been reading about predictive processing and the free energy principle, and the theory keeps getting its hooks in me: the brain is basically a prediction machine that minimizes surprise by running top-down guesses about sensory input, then using prediction errors to update its model, or acting to make the world match the guess. The unifying power is gorgeous—perception, action, and attention all become one trick. But I have a firm intuition this can't be the whole story, because when I open my eyes the room doesn't feel like a controlled hallucination or my brain's best guess; it feels like it's just there, directly. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the direct-perception intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the prediction-machine theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there's a reading on which both survive.
