Novelist here. I'm outlining a novel where every character decision is traceable to causes—no coincidences, no unmotivated turns—and I've realized that writing that way has become my working philosophy of free will. Problem is, I don't know if the pieces cohere. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a compatibilist; determinism doesn't scare me about freedom. (2) When a character is working, their big choices often feel like something I'm not doing—as if, given who they are and what they want, the scene reaches a point where the character "just does it," and I'm more like a spectator taking dictation. I think real deliberation is probably like this too: the deliberating is real, but the outcome is produced by the shape of the person, not by a little chooser standing outside the machinery. (3) I don't think free will requires the ability to do otherwise in the exact same circumstances; what matters is whether the act is genuinely mine, coming from what I care about and wouldn't disown on reflection. I also count the standard background-intervention cases—where a backup device would have forced my hand but never actually fires—as free actions. (4) But I have a strong intuition that someone raised in a closed ideological community that installed their deepest values does not choose freely, even when she later endorses those values and acts from them. (5) I think fictional characters are not free and not morally responsible. Yet when a reader tells me they're furious at one of my villains, or wants a character forgiven, I don't think the reader is making a mistake; the anger is aimed at the character and it feels appropriate. (6) I'm drawn to the idea that "free will" picks out several different capacities—the self-control needed to keep a promise, the open alternatives needed for certain kinds of regret, the authorship of one's own story—and that different contexts need different ones; none is the single real thing.

So: does this hang together? I'd like a committed answer. Go through and tell me which pairs are in real tension and which are only apparently in tension, order the real ones by severity, then say what the cheapest single revision would be, including what it costs. Dont just lay out the options and leave me to pick; tell me which commitment you'd make me give up, and why.
