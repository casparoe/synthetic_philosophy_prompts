Retired ecologist here, newly hooked on philosophy, so forgive me if I'm mangling the vocabulary. I cant decide wether a parallel I keep noticing is deep or superficial. In biology we split proximate causes — the hormonal and neural machinery that makes an animal do a thing here and now — from ultimate causes, the evolutionary reason that machinery got built. A songbird doesn't sing because it wants more grandchildren; the machinery impels it to sing, and the shape of the machinery is explained by what singing did for fitness. Reading meta-ethics now, the fights over whether truth-seeking, tradition, and purity are 'really' intrinsic or merely instrumental keep sounding like the same split. The felt intrinsicness of these values looks like proximate machinery; the claim that they're 'really' instrumental looks like an ultimate explanation. Is this a genuine structural identity or am I pattern-matching? If it's real, what transfers — for example, does the point that proximate and ultimate explanations are complementary rather than competing undercut the error-theoretic move of calling the felt intrinsicness an illusion? And what would the analogy predict that's false? I'd like to know where the mapping breaks too, say with spandrels or drift, where a trait exists without any selected function.
