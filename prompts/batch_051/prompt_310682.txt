I keep circling back to a puzzle I first picked up from Dennett's treatment of the color-phi phenomenon, and I have mostly seen the rationalist-blog version of the answer — that the question dissolves — which I want you to resist. Here is the setup. A red dot is flashed at one location and a green dot at a nearby location, with a short blank interval and appropriate timing; subjects report a single dot moving and changing color around the midpoint of the path, not two separate dots. Two stories could account for the report. The Orwellian story says the subject first has an ordinary consciousness of a red dot and then a green dot, and that the brain later edits the record so that the subject remembers a single moving dot whose color changes mid-route. The Stalinesque story says the brain holds the show back, waits for information about the second dot, and only then produces a conscious experience of a single moving dot with the color change already built into the middle; the bridging percept is inserted before the sequence is ever consciously experienced. Dennett's dissolution says that once you give up the Cartesian theater, there is no fact of the matter about whether the brain edited after consciousness or staged beforehand, because there is no single place and time at which content gets promoted into consciousness.

I want you to treat that dissolution as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a conclusion to be endorsed. Begin with a one-sentence bottom line: is the Orwellian/Stalinesque question empirically answerable in principle, only partially answerable, or a pseudodistinction? Then design the best empirical test you can that would bear on whether the brain's processing in color phi, or in some cleaner temporal-binding analogue, is better described as Orwellian revision or Stalinesque staging. Be concrete: what would you measure, what pattern of results would favor each description, and exactly what would a positive result show and fail to show. End by saying what part of the original dispute your operationalization leaves out, and whether that leftover is a limitation of current instruments or a principled limit built into the idea of a timed conscious event. Hard limit: 600 words.
