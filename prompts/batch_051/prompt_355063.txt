I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been assigned a long reported feature — maybe 7,000 words, three months of work — about why the calendar's starting point is where it is and whether it ought to be somewhere else. I keep tripping over the same snarl: there is no year zero between 1 BCE and 1 CE, so our interval labels are quietly off; the Holocene calendar people want to add ten thousand years so that all of roughly human civilization sits under one positive number; astronomers quietly use a year zero even though historians don't; and Unix time starts in 1970 because someone needed an arbitrary floor. I can feel some deeper conceptual questions moving under this — whether a calendar's zero is anchored in something real or is pure convention, whether an epoch should track human history or natural history, what an actual year zero would change technically and in ordinary thought, how much the inertia of backward compatibility should count — but my editor keeps asking what the stakes actually are, and honestly I can't rank them.

So: if you were me, which two or three of those threads — or ones I'm not seeing — would you build the feature around, in what order, and why? I'm not after a survey of calendar reforms. I want to know what makes one question weightier than another: wether some are upstream of others, which have traction with ordinary readers, which turn out to be trivia dressed up as philosophy, and what would have to be true for you to swap the ranking.
