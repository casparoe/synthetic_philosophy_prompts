Lately I've been trying to nail down what I actually think about whether numbers count when you're deciding who to save, and my gut keeps handing me verdicts that don't obviously fit together. In a fire, if I can carry out either one unconscious stranger or five unconscious strangers and there's no way to get both: I save the five, and I really don't think I'd lose sleep over the one. If it's one close friend versus five strangers, I save the friend, and I don't think that's just me being weak; I think it's the right call. If I've already promised one of the strangers I'd come back for him, and my only choice is now to keep the promise or go save five other people, I think I save the one I promised, though this one nags at me. If it's one stranger against one stranger, I don't think either has a stronger claim than the other; I'd flip a coin, and I'd insist any finer method was doing one of them an injustice.

But a friend keeps pressing me on the first and last of those. He says: if each person's claim counts equally in the one-versus-one case, then the five in the one-versus-five case don't simply outweigh the one. Either each of the five has an equal claim and the lone person has an equal claim, and I need some procedure that respects that (a coin flip? a chance proportional to group size? something else?), or I'm letting the one's claim get canceled merely because their outnumbered — which is exactly the sort of thing I said I wouldn't do in the one-versus-one case. He thinks the first and last verdicts can't both be right. Is he correct? Is there a principle that captures all (or most) of my verdicts, and what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: one stranger versus two, where I'd save the two but with far less confidence, and I can't shake the feeling that the lone person could reasonably complain that their life got treated as a rounding error.

Could you lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question? And at the end, could you say how confident you are in what you're claiming and single out the point you're least sure of?
