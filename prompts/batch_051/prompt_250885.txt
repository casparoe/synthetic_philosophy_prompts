I've been going back and forth with some friends about a thought experiment: a small community whose language has no pronouns or indexicals at all—no 'I', 'you', 'he', 'she', 'here', 'now', 'this', 'that'. Everyone refers to themselves by their own name ('Mara is hungry' instead of 'I'm hungry'), people address each other by name or title, and instead of 'here' and 'now' they use clock times, coordinates, or fixed landmarks ('at 14:03, at the third bench from the gate'). The reason I keep flip-flopping is this. On one side, it seems like such a language would just be wildly clunky but perfectly serviceable: names and descriptions pick out the same people, places, and times, so nothing factual is really missing. On the other side, there seems to be something situated or first-personal that a name doesnt capture—someone can believe 'Mara is making a mess' without realizing they themselves are the one doing it, and no amount of description of Mara seems to add up to 'I am the one making a mess.'

Could you write a dialogue between two philosophers of language, equally sharp and equally sympathetic, one defending each side? I'd like actual back-and-forth where they respond to each other's specific moves rather than trading set speeches, and neither one should be a strawman. Then, after the dialogue, add a short note (a few sentences) separating what is genuinely settled among people who work on this stuff from what is genuinely contested, so it doesn't come off like one contested view is the consensus. And please don't let the dialogue end with one side simply winning.
