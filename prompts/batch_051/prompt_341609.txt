In my years reporting on science and ideas, I keep hearing two clashing claims about philosophy: that it obviously makes no progress because its central questions remain open, and that it obviously makes progress, though only in the quiet work of clarifying concepts. Neither has ever captured what I find impressive about the discipline. I have been working up a view I would like pressure-tested, which I call the Dead Options Account of philosophical progress. The central claim is that the unit of philosophical progress is neither an answer nor a clarification but a retired move: a specific argumentative move that was once live, was tried, and was shown to fail in a way the field now treats as settled. On this picture, Gettier did not tell us what knowledge is; his contribution was to retire the definition of knowledge as justified true belief, adding it to the stock of moves competent philosophers may no longer make. The Euthyphro dilemma similarly retires the move of grounding normative facts simply in the commands of a deity. If this is right, philosophy progresses by enlarging and shoring up this stock of dead options rather than by converging on positive theses, which would explain the odd combination of felt progress on the inside and apparent stasis on the outside.

I can already hear two objections: that this describes the sociology of a conversation rather than any epistemic achievement, and that supposedly dead options keep returning under new names. There are probably worse ones. Please give me the three strongest objections to the account and tell me whether any is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable; say whether the account collapses into something already defended in the literature, and under what name; and say where it does real explanatory work rather than merely re-describing ordinary intellectual hygiene. Throughout, separate what is settled about the history of philosophy's methods from what is genuinely contested among philosophers who work on philosophical progress, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
