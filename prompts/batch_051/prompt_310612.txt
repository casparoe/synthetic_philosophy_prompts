I am drafting a position statement for a seminar on long-term futures and the study of hypothetical civilizations, and I would appreciate a consistency audit of the following set of commitments about how to measure civilizational advancement. I can see that some of these pull against one another, but I am not confident which tensions are genuine, which are merely apparent, or which revision would be least costly. Please do not treat every pair as equally problematic; I need a ranked audit.

Here is the position I am sketching:

(a) The Kardashev hierarchy remains the best available framework for comparing potential extraterrestrial civilizations, because energy capture is a fungible resource that underwrites nearly all large-scale technological activity.

(b) However, raw energy capture cannot by itself capture what we mean by advancement. A civilization that controlled the full output of a star but could not build structures below the millimeter scale would be, in an important respect, less advanced than a civilization with a purely planetary energy budget that could manipulate matter at the molecular or nuclear scale.

(c) I therefore accept something like Barrow's inversion of the scale: a civilization should be classified by the smallest length scale over which it can deliberately manipulate matter and extract usable information from it, from macroscopic structures down through cells, molecules, atoms, nuclei, and ultimately fundamental length scales.

(d) Yet I also hold that civilizational advancement is multidimensional, and that no single scalar quantity, not even a smallest controlled length scale, captures it completely.

(e) For the purposes of searching for extraterrestrial intelligence, however, these two orders need to be integrated into a single ordinal ranking. If we cannot say which of two hypothetical civilizations is more advanced, we cannot say which technosignatures deserve priority.

(f) Where the two orders point in opposite directions, I am inclined to let small-scale control dominate large-scale energy capture, because small-scale control seems to be a later developmental stage and closer to what advanced means in fundamental physics.

(g) In practice, we should still search for large-scale Kardashev-style energy transformations, because small-scale manipulation is far harder to observe across interstellar distances.

(h) Underlying all of this, I hold that a civilization is more advanced to the extent that it can intentionally suppress or exploit a wider range of degrees of freedom, not merely to the extent that it has more energy, more people, or more volume.

I am not sure where the main inconsistency is. Please audit this set of commitments. For each pair that is in genuine tension, say how severe the tension is, what the cheapest fix would be, and which commitment would have to be given up or weakened. If some pair only appears to be in tension, say why the tension dissolves. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, and do not merely list them by letter or theme.
