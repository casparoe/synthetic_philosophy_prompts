I spent most of my career in population genetics, and since retiring I've been working through philosophy of social science and international relations, which is how I ended up on Fearon's rationalist explanation of war. The basic move—war is costly ex post, so there should always have been a bargain both sides would have preferred, and the interesting question is why the bargaining failed—struck me as exactly the sort of clean setup I used to like in modelling. Then I noticed AI policy people using the same three mechanisms (private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, and issue indivisibility or winner-take-all stakes) to explain why labs or countries race ahead on powerful AI even when everyone says they'd rather go slow. But an AI race is not a war: the "prize" is itself a capability that changes the strategic situation, the racers are often firms rather than states, and the race itself is the thing creating the catastrophic risk, rather than a costly dispute over something seperate. So I don't trust myself to just import the desiderata from one domain to the other without checking.

Before I read any more of the AI-race literature, I want the full spec: what would a theory of AI development races have to get right to count as adequate for thinking about whether and how such races can be slowed? Give me the list of requirements—the cases it has to handle (symmetric rivals, a clear leader who could lock in a lead, a lab that promises to go slow and then dashes the moment a rival demos something), the distinctions it can't collapse (racing vs. ordinary market competition, racing countries vs. racing firms), the results from other fields it has to stay compatable with—with the consideration behind each. Where Fearon's three mechanisms should carry over as requirements, say so; where the disanalogies with war force new requirements, say that too. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp, and flag any subset you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. Then tell me which single requirement does the most to knock out the naive theories. I'll do the scorecard against actual named theories myself, so keep that brief.

After the table, add a short paragraph on any joint unsatisfiabilities and the single most discriminating requirement.
