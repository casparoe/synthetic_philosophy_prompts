For a philosophy of art reading group, we each have to write down our commitments about what makes something art, and then someone else audits them for consistency before we meet. I'd rather find out now then in front of everyone that I've been sitting on a contradiction, so here's mine:

(a) At least for the last century or so, what makes a new object art is that the artworld (critics, curators, artists, historians) takes it up and treats it as something to be appreciated as art. The institutional story seems basically right to me.
(b) Duchamp's Fountain is art.
(c) Some Paleolithic cave paintings are art, even though nothing like an artworld in that sense existed when they were made.
(d) A real definition of art should be unified; it shouldn't just be a disjunctive list (one clause for pre-modern stuff, one clause for the gallery era, etc.).
(e) Whether something is art is an objective social fact, not a matter of the artworld's arbitrary say-so or of individual opinion.

I genuinely believe each of these, and I can't tell which pairs actually pull against each other and which only look like they do. Please audit the set: go through the commitments, identify the tensions, and rank them by severity rather than just listing them. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is. Also flag any pair that seems bad but is fine once you spell out what the commitment really amounts to, and tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set cohere. Keep the audit under 500 words.
