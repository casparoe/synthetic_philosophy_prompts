I help run a small interdisciplinary journal — no fees, open review — and I keep hitting the same wall. Reviewers reject submissions as methodologically unsound, but when I press them it turns out they mean: no control group, no preregistration, the hypothesis was changed after looking at the data, the n is tiny, it argues from historical cases instead of a test, or the author plainly wanted to prove their conclusion. I'm sympathetic to Feyerabend's anything-goes line, understood as the historical claim that no single method fits everything that has worked — good science has repeatedly broken the rules we now teach, and sound method is often just what a discipline happens to do. But I can't run a journal on the principle that every method is fine.

Write me a reusable decision procedure — a checklist or a set of questions to ask in order — that my reviewers and I can use to decide whether a methodological objection is a real flaw in a paper's argument, or just disciplinary habit dressed up as a universal rule. It has to be usable by reviewers who have never read the philosophy, grounded in what the philosophy of science actually has to say about methodological rules rather than in even-handedness, and honest about which steps are mechanical, which are judgement calls, which distinctions you've had to flatten to keep it usable, and where the procedure will misfire. It should also say when we should reject a paper even if no fixed rule has been broken.

Then run the procedure on these three submissions:

(1) a teaching-intervention paper with no control group that claims the intervention caused better exam results;

(2) a preregistered trial whose main result is null, with a subgroup effect found in an unplanned analysis that the authors disclose but still use to make a causal claim;

(3) an ethnography of a factory that uses two years of participant observation to argue that a new shift system increased injuries.

At least one of these should come out as something your procedure tells us to publish even if we would rather not. Please write the answer in British English.
