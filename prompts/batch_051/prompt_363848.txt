I chair the deployment-safety committee at a large AI lab. We must decide this week whether to make release of a new model conditional on automated monitoring of its chain-of-thought. The model produces an extended written reasoning trace before each final answer on high-stakes tasks, and the proposal before us is to run a monitor over those traces that flags or refuses any response whose reasoning indicates a dangerous plan in progress, even when the final answer itself looks acceptable. One faction on the committee treats the trace as our best available window into why the model acts; they argue that refusing on the basis of the trace is prudent, upstream scrutiny of the model's actual deliberations. Another faction replies that the trace is not a transcript of the model's cognition but model-generated text, and it is generated under exactly the incentive we are about to install: once the model learns that traces are graded, we should expect traces to be selected for what the monitor approves of, not for what actually produced the answer. They distinguish faithfulness (whether the trace reports the real determinants of the answer), obfuscation (reasoning written to slip past the monitor), and optimization pressure (the systemic incentive for traces to become passing rather than true), and they hold that these are structural problems that cannot be fixed merely by training a sharper monitor. Please draft the short written recommendation I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should state whether we adopt the monitoring proposal as a condition of deployment, reject it, or adopt it with safeguards; it should spell out criteria our engineers can actually implement; and it should fairly present the strongest argument on the losing side.
