I'm in a course on minds and machines, and we spent a week on the MU-puzzle from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach. The puzzle goes like this: you start with the string MI, and you have four rules—you can add a U to any string ending in I, double everything after the M, replace III with U, or delete UU—and the challenge is to derive MU. I get the mechanical part, and I even get the standard proof that MU can't be derived: the number of I's is never a multiple of three, whereas MU has zero I's, which counts as a multiple of three.

What I don't get is the moral Hofstadter draws from this. He says solving the puzzle involves jumping out of the system—JOOTSing, I think—and that this is what separates mindless symbol-shuffling from real intelligence. But when I actually found the invariant, I didn't jump outside of anything. I just used arithmetic, and arithmetic is itself a formal system with it's own symbols and rules. So isn't JOOTSing really just hopping from one system into a bigger one? And if any way of thinking counts as a system, then there is no outside, and the inside/outside distinction looks vacuous. But then what is Hofstadter's point about intelligence supposed to amount to? Maybe I'm reading the jumping-out talk too literally, but if he doesn't mean stepping outside every possible system, then what marks the difference between the level where I was stuck and the level where the invariant becomes visible? Doesn't the fact that I can formalize the invariant afterward mean I never really left?
