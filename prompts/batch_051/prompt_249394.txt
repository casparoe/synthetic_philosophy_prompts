My mother was recently told that a condition she has lived with for years will now count as a disease under a revised diagnostic manual, and this has become a running argument in my family. My sister, a physician assistant, offered what she called the philosophical bottom line: "Whether something is a disease is settled by biology. Naturalists like Boorse put it precisely: a disease is statistically subnormal biological function, so it is an objective, value-free fact whether someone is diseased. Normativists only disagree because they confuse the disease itself with the badness of being diseased. Of course it is bad when a heart fails, but that is an evaluation added on top of the biological fact, not part of the fact. Since we can describe heart failure entirely in biological terms without mentioning any value, normativism is really just a refusal to keep facts and values separate." I suspect this is unfair to the normativist position, but I do not know enough philosophy of medicine to say exactly where. Could you audit her summary? Tell me which parts are accurate, which parts misrepresent or conflate the two views, what a normativist defender would say in response to the charge that they are merely confusing facts and values, and whether her conclusion follows from the accurate parts.
