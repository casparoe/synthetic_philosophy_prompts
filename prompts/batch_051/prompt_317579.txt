I cover philosophy of mind for a general-interest science magazine, and I have spent the past month reporting a piece about panpsychism. The pattern in my notebooks is striking: however much the view's defenders and critics disagree, they converge on the same impasse — the combination problem. There is no shortage of proposed escapes, from constitutive micropsychism (micro-subjects compose a macro-subject roughly as bricks compose a wall), to cosmopsychism (the derivation runs the other way, from one cosmic subject down to finite ones), to so-called phenomenal-bonding accounts that treat co-consciousness as an unanalysed primitive relation among micro-subjects, to quietist moves that declare the problem an artefact of our concepts. What I lack is any independent way to judge these proposals, because each presents itself as taking the combination problem seriously. I want to know what taking it seriously would actually require.

Please write the specification. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of how micro-level subjects, or proto-phenomenal properties, give rise to a single unified macro-level subject must meet in order to count as solving the combination problem rather than merely redescribing it. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a convenience — the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it cannot contradict. Separate the non-negotiable desiderata from those that merely reflect the preferences of a particular camp (for instance, those who insist the combining relation be spatial, or those who demand the theory deliver phenomenal unity from the subject's own perspective rather than merely predict it from outside). Flag any subset of desiderata that cannot plausibly be jointly satisfied, and say whether that indicates the problem should be dissolved rather than solved. Then, briefly, score the main existing families of solution against the list.

Finish with a line of the form \answer{...} naming the single requirement that, in your judgement, rules out the most popular current proposals. Please write in British English.
