I'm putting together an application for a one-year research fellowship in philosophy of science and need a properly developed proposal to anchor it. My working idea is this. The modern debate about biological function is usually posed as a choice between two accounts: on the "selected effects" view, a trait's function is whatever past instances of the trait did that explains why they were selected for — a heart's function is to pump blood because pumping blood explains why hearts persisted — while on the "causal role" view, a trait's function is whatever it currently does that contributes to the organism's capacities, whatever its selection history. Aristotle's final cause was one of four kinds of explanation — material, formal, efficient, and final — and it asked a question the others did not: toward what end does a thing develop or act. Modern biology is often said to have expelled final causes, and the function debate is then read as the argument about whether they can be let back in. My suspicion is that the two accounts let them back only by disguising them: the selected-effects view rebuilds purposiveness out of efficient-causal selection history, while the causal-role view rebuilds it out of the system's present formal organization; each then accuses the other of losing the normative force of "supposed to," where an organ can fail to do what it is there to do. The test cases that seem to me to put the most pressure on both accounts are vestigial organs (selection history but no current role), exaptations (current role but no selection for it), and the first appearance of a trait in a lineage (before there is any selection history for it).

It should state the question precisely and in a way that could receive a determinate answer rather than just a survey; diagnose briefly why the two standard accounts leave the original teleological question open; lay out a method for making progress — distinctions to draw, test cases to run through the accounts, burden-of-shift arguments — with an order of work across the year; and say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing.
