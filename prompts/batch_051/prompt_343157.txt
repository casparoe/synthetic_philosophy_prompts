In a seminar on infinity, a finitist leaned on Hilbert's hotel to argue that an actually infinite collection cannot exist. She set the hotel up with one room for each natural number and no vacancies, then showed that a new guest can be lodged by shifting the occupant of room n to room n+1, and that countably many new guests can be lodged by shifting the occupant of room n to room 2n. From this she concluded that the idea of a completed infinite is not just counterintuitive but incoherent: nothing real could be full and yet add occupants indefinitely without expanding. Audit the analogy as an argument rather than as a mere illustration. Give the correspondence table between hotel features and the arithmetic of the natural numbers; separate the features doing argumentative work from the stage-setting; identify where the mapping breaks or quietly imports notions such as occupancy, capacity, change, and completion that the set-theoretic structure does not contain; and say whether the finitist's conclusion—that actual infinities are impossible—survives once the disanalogies are made explicit. If it does not survive, state precisely what the hotel argument, correctly interpreted, does and does not show about the actual infinite, and whether some better analogy would carry the intended point more honestly.
