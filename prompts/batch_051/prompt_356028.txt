One for you. A country that wants to keep armed neutrality — roughly the Switzerland/Finland/Sweden mold — is in a recruiting crisis: the small professional army can't fill its ranks, and the public complains that the burden falls on the unlucky few who happen to sign up. A proposal floats around: universal conscription stays on the books, but any conscript can buy out their term by paying an exemption fee set as a fixed percentage of their last two years' taxable income (students and the unemployed pay a nominal amount). All the money goes into a closed fund that can only be spent on enlistment bonuses, specialist pay, and pensions for the professional force — so the draft is kept mainly to raise the money and provide moral cover, not to produce troops. Walk me through how this actually behaves. Who ends up serving and who pays; what does it do the the skill mix and class makeup of the professional army; where does it get gamed first (deferments, hiding income, enlisting just to collect the bonus, families shifting income around); and what happens to the citizen-soldier ideal of a shared burden once buying out is normal. Give me the failure modes roughly in the order they'd bite, and cap it at 400 words.
