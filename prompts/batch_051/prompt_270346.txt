I'm teaching an upper-level undergrad seminar on well-being this term, and next week we're doing novelty as a candidate component of the good life — specifically the standoff between people who say variety matters only because the hedonic treadmill makes repetition less pleasant, and people who say a life containing a wider range of activities is better in itself even with pleasantness held fixed. I'd like to hand out something funny to keep them awake. Could you write a parody of a glossy wellness-magazine self-assessment quiz (ten or so questions plus a scoring key, around 700 words) in the deadpan, faintly coercive tone of a life-optimization app, with a title along the lines of "Are You Experience-Poor?" The target should be the experiences-over-possessions quantified-self culture, but underneath it the real target is that philosophical dispute itself. The humor should come from actually knowing the debate, so make the quiz keep tripping over the gap between felt novelty and real novelty: one item should agonize over whether a second trip to Paris where you notice new details counts, another should hand full marks to someone in an experience machine who believes he is living a wildly varied life while a third penalizes him for never having actually gone anywhere, and the scoring key should quietly adopt an objective-list view while pretending to be a neutral audit of flourishing. Maybe also include an item that treats being afraid your life is boring as self-evidently bad even though a careful hedonist or desire-satisfaction theorist would object. Stay deadpan throughout — don't explain the joke; let the scoring do the ironizing.
