I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether information about me belongs to me. My working theory is that it's mine in roughly the way my stuff is: people shouldn't use it, especially commercially, without my say-so. But my gut on cases keeps breaking that theory.

- My bank sells my purchase history to a data broker. Not fine.
- A supermarket tracks what I buy and uses it to send me coupons. Fine.
- A hospital uses my de-identified MRI scans in a research database without asking me. Fine, actually good.
- A company scrapes my public posts and photos to train an AI model. Not fine.
- A friend tells an embarrassing story about me at a dinner party. Fine, even though its about me.
- My gym sells data about when I swipe in and my resting heart rate. Not fine.
- I sell my own browsing history to a company for a discount. Fine, because I agreed.

The bank and the supermarket both use facts about what I buy to make money without ever sitting me down to ask, yet I come out differently. Is there one principle about when using information about me is okay that gets all of these, or nearly all? Can the property view be patched, or is it the wrong frame?
