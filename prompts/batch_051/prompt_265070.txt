Recently my sister has been in hospital with advanced dementia and I've ended up thinking a lot about what "dignity" actually means when people use it about patients like her. I came back to this passage from Kant's Groundwork:

"In the kingdom of ends everything has either a price or a dignity. What has a price can be replaced by something else as its equivalent; what on the other hand is raised above all price and therefore admits of no equivalent has a dignity."

I get the basic price/dignity contrast, but I'm stuck on how it's supposed to apply in practice. If dignity means not having an equivalent, does paying someone to look after my sister treat her as having only a price? Or is paying for care okay as long as the carer also respects her? Also, Kant seems to tie dignity to being a rational being, but my sister can't really reason anymore, so does his view imply she's lost her dignity? I'm worried that's where it leads, but it seems harsh. Please answer in British English.
