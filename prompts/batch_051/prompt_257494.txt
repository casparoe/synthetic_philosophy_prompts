For an undergraduate philosophy course on skepticism, I want a decision tree that starts from the butterfly dream at the end of Zhuangzi's Qiwulun chapter, often translated as the Discussion on Making All Things Equal. There, Zhuangzi recounts dreaming he was a butterfly, waking, and then saying he does not know whether he was then a man dreaming he was a butterfly or is now a butterfly dreaming he is a man. The course will use this passage as its first reading, before the students have learned the standard labels.

Please write the decision tree as a classification instrument, not an essay. Each branch node should be a question a student can answer from the case itself, phrased without the philosophical labels; each leaf should be a named position with a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. The tree should be built from the logical space rather than from a list of names, and it should include at least these positions as leaves: a Cartesian-style skeptical possibility reading, a contextualist response, a coherence/action-based response, a Zhuangzian transformation reading, and a no-self or no-fixed-boundary reading. Where the logical space contains a combination that no one defends, note that leaf as unoccupied and briefly say whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot really separate, and say what further question would separate them.

Prescribe the sections of the response in this order, with these rough lengths:

1. Tree overview — 150 words: a paragraph explaining the top-level structure of the tree and the main fork in the logical space.
2. The branching questions — 500 words: the diagnostic questions, each with two or three answer options, in the order they appear in the tree, with transitions between nodes.
3. The leaves — 400 words: each named position, one sentence on its awkward commitment, and one sentence stating which combination of answers leads to it.
4. Unoccupied branches — 200 words: the answer combinations that would land there, and why no one occupies them.
5. Separation failures — 150 words: positions the tree cannot distinguish, and the further question that would separate them.

Do not include a bibliography or citations.
