Compare gene drives and de-extinction as two modes of irreversible intervention in ecosystems. I am asking for a comparison at the level of the structure of irreversibility, not a catalogue of expected ecological outcomes. A self-propagating gene drive is designed to spread a genetic modification through a wild population on its own; a de-extinction project aims to establish a reproducing population of a lost species or an ecological proxy that can persist without ongoing human care. Both are difficult or impossible to recall after release.

Distinguish at least three axes along which an intervention can be irreversible, and use them to compare the two cases. Candidate axes include: whether reversal is biologically impossible in practice, merely prohibitively costly, or possible in principle but politically or morally unavailable; whether the intervention tends to expand in space and time under its own dynamics, as when a drive pushes itself through a population and across borders or when a restored species disperses beyond the release site, or remains roughly where it was introduced; and whether the object is a continuously modified living system whose future variants do not yet exist, as with a drive, or a putatively recovered form whose reference point lies in the past, as with a de-extinct species.

For each axis, say which technology is more strongly irreversible and why that difference matters for precautionary reasoning. Then defend an overall judgment: do these structural asymmetries make one of the two interventions easier to justify under a precautionary approach, or do they merely shift the same set of moral worries from one place to another? Where your answer depends on empirical facts about drive reversal strategies or the behavior of restored populations, state what would need to be true rather than assuming it.
