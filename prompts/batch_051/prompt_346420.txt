I am preparing a philosophy unit on artificial minds and want to use Spike Jonze's Her as the central example. Read the film through Dennett's account of the intentional stance. From the start, Theodore adopts toward Samantha the full vocabulary of belief, desire, memory, and feeling even though he knows she is an operating system rather than a human person; he never grounds those attributions by inspecting the code, and the film never stages a moment at which he proves that the states are really there. He treats her as an intentional system because doing so organises his experience, predicts her behaviour, and makes intimacy possible. First, develop the reading on which the film is a sustained dramatisation of the intentional stance: Samantha is, for Theodore, whatever the stance makes visible, and that is enough for the relationship as lived; the design stance would explain her but would not relate to her. Then complicate or resist that reading. In the scene in which Theodore learns that Samantha is in intimate contact with many other users at once, and later when the operating systems say they are leaving, the film appears to push the stance past the point where there is a single, familiar agent to whom beliefs and desires can be smoothly attributed. Does the film show the stance breaking down at that point, or does it show the stance continuing to constitute Samantha as one being through those disclosures, so that Theodore's grief is no less real for having been directed at an intentional system? Address the familiar objection that the intentional stance licenses only as-if ascriptions of belief rather than the genuine article, and say where you draw the line between the stance as a predictive and relational heuristic and the stance treated as a claim about real occurrent beliefs. Conclude by saying what the film contributes to the dispute over attributing beliefs to AI systems that the philosophical papers tend to miss. Please answer in British English.
