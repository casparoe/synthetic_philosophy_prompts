I am a teaching assistant for a second-year undergraduate philosophy of language course, and I am grading a midterm question on theories of vagueness. Part of the course covered the sorites paradox and two of the main responses to it: epistemicism and supervaluationism. The exam question the students received was:

Compare epistemicism and supervaluationism as responses to the sorites paradox. For each view, explain (a) what it says about whether a vague predicate such as "is a heap" has a sharp cutoff, (b) whether the principle of bivalence and the law of excluded middle hold, and (c) why, on that view, competent speakers cannot locate the cutoff if there is one.

One student wrote the following answer:

I think both epistemicism and supervaluationism reject the inductive premise of the sorites, but they locate the failure differently. The epistemicist claims that "is a heap" has a precise cutoff: there is some exact number n such that n grains make a heap and n−1 grains do not, but we are constitutively unable to know where that cutoff lies, so all borderline questions are really either true or false. Bivalence is therefore fully preserved. The supervaluationist denies that there is any such cutoff in the world, and instead treats a vague predicate as semantically incomplete: it has many admissible ways of being made precise, and a sentence is true only if it comes out true on every such precisification. For a borderline case, "this is a heap" is true on some precisifications and false on others, so it is neither true nor false. Since borderline sentences are neither true nor false, the law of excluded middle also fails in the borderline region. That means supervaluationism is not classical; it operates like a three-valued logic with a truth-value gap.

I would like to grade this carefully rather than by impression. Please assign the answer a numerical grade out of 20, and then give me a point-by-point marking commentary: indicate which elements receive credit and why, identify any mistaken or imprecise claims, and explain exactly how each should be corrected in a way I could pass on as margin comments. In particular, please evaluate (i) whether the student correctly distinguishes the two views about the existence of a sharp cutoff, (ii) what the student says about bivalence and the law of excluded middle, and (iii) whether describing supervaluationism as a three-valued or gap logic is accurate. Do not just summarize the answer; give specific remarks tied to the student's sentences, focusing on the errors that would be easy to let slide.
