A colleague of mine who works on interpretability has become convinced that when a large language model produces a chain-of-thought answer, its stated reasoning should be read as a faithful report of the process that produced the final answer: not a verbatim trace of every hidden activation, but an accurate summary at the relevant level of abstraction. I find this nearly impossible to assess. The same training objective that rewards correct answers also rewards plausible-sounding rationales, so a fluent step-by-step explanation could be a genuine trace of the computational process, a post hoc rationalization that shares common causes with the answer without being causally responsible for it, or a mostly confabulated narrative constrained only by the fact that it must stay consistent with the answer already reached. Because the claim is about what the model's own output reports about its own processing, I am not sure which observations could even bear on it.

I would like you to design the strongest experimental program you can to distinguish a truthful report of its own reasoning from a confabulated but superficially accurate one. Be concrete about the manipulation or intervention, the measurement, and the pattern of results that would favor each reading. For instance, one might intervene directly on the generated rationale itself — deleting, reordering, or contradicting one stated step and measuring whether the model's final answer changes in the way one would expect if the step were causally load-bearing — or one might train probes on intermediate hidden states to see whether the information that would justify a step is present before the step is generated. Either approach has obvious weaknesses, so sharpen them or propose something better. Then say exactly what a clean positive result would establish and what it would leave open: would it show that the stated steps are causally relevant to the answer, that the model has anything like introspective access to its own process, or merely that the rationale and the answer are generated from overlapping internal states?
