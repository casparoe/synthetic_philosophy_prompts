Write the joint statement that a "cluelessness worrier" and an "expected-value pragmatist" could both sign after a good-faith adverserial collaboration about whether consequentialism can still guide action, given that the long-run consequences of ordinary acts are basically unknowable.

Set up the two positions so neither is a strawman. The worrier thinks: once you trace the effects of a small choice far enough—effects on who ends up existing, on institutions, on what options are available centuries later—the sign and magnitude of the expected value get swamped by noise, so the idea that we can compare acts by their consequences starts to look hollow. The pragmatist thinks: this is just decision-making under uncertainty, which we do all the time; you don't need the true long-run value, you need your best posterior over it, and for plenty of actions (donating to a malaria charity, say) the best estimate is good enough to act on, even if the error bars grow with time.

I want the document itself, not a summary of the literature, and don't cite anyone.

Include all of the following:

- A numbered "agreed claims" section. Don't make it vacuous. It should cover what cluelessness is, whether it's a special problem for consequentialism or just a general problem for everyone, what "unknown" means here, and at least one worked micro-example (like a $100 donation to a bednet charity vs. spending it on a concert ticket) where both sides agree on what can and can't be said about the direct effects and the indirect effects (the ripples through other people's choices, institutions, and which future people exist).
- A disagreements table. Each row should be a single precise issue, with the worrier's formulation and the pragmatist's formulation, both written in a way the person holding that view would actually accept as their own.
- The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it will show. At least one crux should be whether the unknown long-run effects are symmetric around zero (as likely to make an act better as worse) or systematically biased one way; another could be whether option value or satisficing is a defensible tie-breaker once the first-order sums wash out.
- A short note at the end on whether any of the alleged disagreements turned out to be mostly verbal (e.g., conflating "we can't know the exact value" with "we can't know anything useful about the value").

Make it specific enough that someone coming to the debate cold could see exactly where the two people still differ and what would change their minds. And don't let either camp quietly win by defining the other side's view for them.
