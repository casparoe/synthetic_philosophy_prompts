After I retired from the lab I started reading philosophy of mind, and I keep coming back to the eliminative materialism argument about beliefs and desires, because it looks to me like it stalled years ago and nobody noticed or said why. The Churchland side says our ordinary belief-desire talk is a folk theory that does badly enough that neuroscience could eventually replace it outright, the way phlogiston and caloric got dropped instead of reduced. The other side fires a familiar battery: the view seems to refute itself the moment a defender says they belief it; folk psychology predicts everyday behavior too well to be flatly false; we know our own attitudes directly from the inside; and cognitive science itself goes on about representations and contents all day. Nobody moves. I dont want a verdict on who wins. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is. Is it a verbal fight over the word 'belief', a bet about how future neuroscience will turn out, a dispute about who carries the burden of proof, or two different questions ('what will mature science keep?' versus 'what can we make sense of now?') sharing one title? My own hunch is the self-refutation charge is treated as a knockout when it's really weak, and underneath the whole thing is mostly a wager about neuroscience plus a burden-of-proof standoff—but I'd like that hypothesis tested. Given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a neuroimaging result, a formal proof that intentional talk is or isn't dispensable, or simply dropping the old framing? Please write the answer in American English.
