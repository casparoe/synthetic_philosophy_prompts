I've been reading the rationalist-adjacent forums for years, and one idea that keeps reappearing is Dennett's notion of *sphexishness* from *Elbow Room*: the digger wasp Sphex carries a cricket to the mouth of its burrow, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp drags it back to the burrow mouth and then goes inside to inspect again, repeating this indefinitely on each trial, even though it has just performed the inspection. Dennett presents this as mindless, routine-driven behaviour: the wasp is running a fixed sequence of subroutines and never updates its plan in light of the changed situation.

In a recent forum thread this example turned into a stalemate about whether 'sphexishness versus flexibility' can distinguish mere mechanical behaviour from genuine agency. One camp says the distinction is in principle testable: a flexible system has a representation of its goal that can be recombined with new information, whereas a sphexish system is locked into a chain of subroutines that does not consult the goal representation. The other camp says this is not an empirical claim but a folk-psychological gloss or a value judgement about which systems we are willing to call agents. I want to treat this as a conceptual-empirical hybrid and ask what, if anything, could settle it.

Please structure your answer in these sections:

1. "The test" (roughly 300–400 words): Design the best concrete experiment or observational study you can to distinguish sphexish rigidity from flexible agency in a non-human animal, an artificial system, or a human edge case. Specify what you would manipulate and measure, and state which results would favour each reading.

2. "What a positive result shows" (roughly 150–200 words): Explain what a finding of flexibility, or of sphexishness, would actually establish about the presence or absence of agency, and where the inference is strongest.

3. "What the test cannot settle" (roughly 150–200 words): Identify the residue that even a decisive result would leave open — for example, subjective experience, authorship of action, freedom in the sense of alternative possibilities, or the worry that 'goal representation' is itself just a higher-order sphexish routine.

4. "Your diagnosis" (roughly 150–200 words): State whether the two forum camps are really disagreeing about facts, about definitions, about the threshold for a test of agency, or about something else, and say what kind of progress would be possible under that diagnosis.
