I am the general counsel of a company that is beginning to deploy AI agents which can, within pre-set limits, negotiate and sign supply contracts, place orders, and settle small claims by paying from a segregated account without human sign-off on each transaction. Our board has asked for a standing policy covering a recurring decision: whether a given AI system should be treated as a legal person for a specified purpose—for example, as a contracting party, as the owner of segregated funds, or as the bearer of liability—and which legal capacities should be bundled onto it. The stakes are practical and frequent: treating the system as a mere tool and treating it as a person have different consequences for contract validity, liability, insurance, and how counterparties and regulators must deal with us, and we will face some version of this decision with each new deployment or incident. Please write a reusable decision procedure grounded in the corporate-person analogy honestly understood: legal personality is not a claim about an entity's inner life but a functional device by which the law bundles rights and duties onto an entity when doing so serves intelligible legal purposes. The procedure should be a sequence of questions we can ask in order, with a statement of what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where the procedure stops with a no, and the points where it must escalate to a human because the question is one of value or policy rather than law. It should be runnable by a non-philosopher on our operations staff in under a day, with escalation to me or the board for the value-laden steps. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) an agent that contracts with suppliers and pays from its own segregated account; (2) an agent that manages a small charitable fund and makes grants under a written charter, which a regulator proposes should be able to sue and be sued in its own name; and (3) a case of your choosing where the procedure yields an answer we, as the deploying company, will not like. Finally, say which step of the procedure rests on the most contestable reading of legal personhood, and what legal change or argument would lead you to revise the procedure.
