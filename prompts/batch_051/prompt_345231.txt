I'm preparing a three-page proposal for a one-semester research fellowship in philosophy of science, and the project sits in the foundations of causal discovery. My half-formed idea is that discussions of why minimal Bayesian networks track causal structure run together three different achievements: recovering the undirected skeleton, recovering edge orientations, and supporting correct intervention predictions. These plausibly make different demands on the world and are licensed by different assumptions. Faithfulness proper says the distribution has no conditional independencies beyond those entailed by the graph; causal minimality says only that no proper subgraph of the true graph still satisfies the Markov condition; adjacency-faithfulness says, roughly, that no conditional independence in the data falsely removes a true edge. I suspect the standard justification runs the strongest assumption out to the strongest recovery claim and ignores the possibility that a weaker package might underwrite exactly the successes practitioners need. Write the proposal around a determinate question of the form: under exactly what conditions does preferring minimal Bayesian networks license a causal reading of the edges, and which of those conditions are substantive claims about nature rather than merely methodological defaults? The proposal should state the question precisely, explain why the standard appeal to the Causal Markov and Faithfulness conditions does not settle it, describe a method month by month (formal analysis of toy models plus explicit adequacy conditions, not empirical work), say what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure, and keep the scope honest for one semester. Don't include citations or a bibliography; describe positions in your own terms and I'll supply the references.
