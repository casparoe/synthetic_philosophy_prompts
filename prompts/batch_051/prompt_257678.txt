Me and my brother got into a dumb argument the other day that I cant stop thinking about. We were talking about the Sherlock Holmes stories, and he said Holmes was a detective. I said sure, but he never actually existed. My brother said I was contradicting myself: if the name "Sherlock Holmes" never picked out a real person, then "Holmes was a detective" isn't really true, it's just pretend-talk, no different from "Holmes was a plumber." But that feels wrong. Everyone talks as if there are facts about what's in the stories: if someone said Holmes was a plumber we'd say they got the stories wrong, and if someone said he was a detective in London who lived with Watson we'd say that's right. We even argue about things the books never directly settle, like whether he actually cared about people or just found them useful, as if there's a right answer to aim at. So we seem to hold two things at once: there is no such person as Sherlock Holmes, and yet a bunch of sentences with "Holmes" as the subject are true and a bunch are false. Is there a way to keep both of those without quietly switching what we mean by "true" when we move from the real world to a story? And if not, which one should go: that Holmes was a detective, or that Holmes never existed?
