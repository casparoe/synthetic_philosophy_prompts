Every time I've been in the ER they ask me to rate my pain on a scale from zero to ten and write the number on my chart, and that number drives whether I get Tylenol or morphine. The whole thing seems to assume there is a determinate private something — the pain itself — sitting inside me, that I can reliably introspect it, and that my number tracks it the way a thermometer tracks a public fact. But then illusionism about phenomenal consciousness says there are no private qualitative states, no inner glow of painfulness; there's just a bunch of access-conscious content and dispositions to wince, avoid, and report. So what does the zero-to-ten scale actually commit the hospital to? Reconstruct the implicit theory of consciousness behind pain-rating practice, and be careful to seperate the parts that strictly presuppose something like phenomenal realism from the parts an illusionist could keep without any change. If illusionism were adopted, what would actually have to change in clinical protocol — would the zero-to-ten scale survive, and for what reason? Also, if I've got illusionism wrong, or if the practice is more underdetermined than I think, say so plainly instead of playing along.
