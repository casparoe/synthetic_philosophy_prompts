In a jurisdiction that selects policy by conditional prediction markets, the constitution provides: "A proposal shall become law only if the conditional prediction market on the General Welfare Index certifies that the index is expected to be higher in the year following the proposal's implementation than it would be absent that implementation." The drafters assumed the index would be a stable administrative measure maintained by the statistical office. A proposal is now submitted whose only substantive effect is to alter the construction of the index itself, removing a cost-of-living component that has been lowering the index. The market must therefore forecast "the index" conditional on the proposal becoming law, yet the proposal would also change what "the index" means. Does the proposal pass under this clause? Set out what the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable reader would have understood the clause to mean each imply for this case. Is there any reading that preserves the clause's purpose while preventing the metric from being gamed by redefinition, and if not, what does that reveal about governance by prediction markets?
