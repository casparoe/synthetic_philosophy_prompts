I have a pet distinction I keep using and I want someone to push back on it honestly. I call it 'debt guilt' versus 'standing shame.' Debt guilt is the feeling that you owe a specific person a repair because of what you did, and it only really fires when you can picture the person you wronged and some way to make it right. Standing shame is the feeling that your position in a group just dropped because of something known or seen, and it fires whether or not anyone was actually hurt. I think this explains why guilt is better in close relationships where repair is possible, but shame is better at constraining people who dont care about the victim, since they still care about where they stand. It also makes me suspect that a guilt-run culture and a shame-run culture are each a stable local equilibrium, so a culture cant just choose to switch from one to the other. I know the broad guilt-culture versus shame-culture idea is old, but I don't know whether my debtor/standing version is new or just a restatement. Tell me whether the distinction is coherent, whether it already exists under some term I should know, and where it breaks on borderline cases like embarrassment, survivor guilt, or shame over something nobody saw.
