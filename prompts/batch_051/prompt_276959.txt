Philosophers working on epistemic injustice claim that a speaker can be wronged specifically in their capacity as a knower: a hearer assigns their testimony less weight than the evidence warrants, and the deficit is explained by identity prejudice rather than by anything in the testimony itself. I would like to know how far that claim can be tested rather than merely illustrated with anecdotal cases. Design the strongest feasible study you can to test the claim that credibility deficits are sometimes attributable to identity prejudice rather than to ordinary evidential discounting. Specify the manipulation and the measure, say exactly which pattern of results would count in favour of the claim and which would count against it, and then say what the study would not settle even if it came out as hoped — in particular, whether it has isolated prejudice rather than some correlated cue, and whether it has shown an injustice rather than a mere epistemic mistake. Write in British English.
