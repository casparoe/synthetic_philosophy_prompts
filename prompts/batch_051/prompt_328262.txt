I've been turning over something lately and I can't quite shake it. Everyone treats being frugal as the virtue: patching your jeans, eating the ugly leftovers, not replacing your phone until it dies. But when you think about why anyone actually cares about frugality, it's always about what happens out there in the world (less waste, a smaller footprint, not being a drain on resources), and one person's patched jeans is basically invisible in that picture. So I've started to think the value really attaches to getting other people and institutions to waste less, not to making yourself scrimp. That would mean someone who lives like a spendthrift but actually changes how a company uses materials is closer to the value than someone who quietly reuses teabags and never tells anyone. Please argue for that position: that "be frugal" and "promote frugality" are two different values, and "promote frugality" is the more defensible one. Make the strongest version of the argument you can, and don't just dodge the obvious objection that this lets people off the hook for their own consumption.
