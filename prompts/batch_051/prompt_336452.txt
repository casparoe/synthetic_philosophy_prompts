Consider the familiar contrast between Dutch directness and Japanese indirectness. A deflationary view treats it as etiquette only: the same assertion is made in both cultures, and politeness determines how much hedging or bluntness surrounds it. A stronger view treats directness norms as partly determining what is asserted: in a directness-default culture a hedge can be a polite envelope around the assertion a blunt speaker would make, while in an indirectness-default culture the hedge is itself the assertion, and the blunt counterpart is a different act. If the stronger view is right, the same utterance can fall inside or outside the category of a white lie depending on the local norm, and cross-cultural differences in judgments of whether a white lie was told are not merely differences in tolerance for rudeness. How would one test whether directness norms enter what is said or only how it is appropriate to say it? Specify a concrete design, the pattern of results that would favor each view, and what a positive result would and would not settle about the moral status of indirect evasions.
