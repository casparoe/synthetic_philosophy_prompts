I am writing a short piece on mandatory arbitration and the privatization of justice, and before I commit any of it to the page I want a consistency check on the position I have drifted into. Here it is, numbered for reference.

(1) Competent adults should in general be held to what they voluntarily agree to, including agreements about the forum in which their disputes will be settled; freedom of contract is a serious thing to override.

(2) Nonetheless, I regard mandatory pre-dispute arbitration clauses in consumer and employment contracts as presumptively illegitimate. Their defining feature is that the stronger party imposed the forum as a take-it-or-leave-it condition of getting the phone, the card, or the job; whatever the signature records, that is not voluntary in the sense (1) means.

(3) I have no objection to arbitration as a method. Post-dispute agreements to arbitrate, and pre-dispute clauses genuinely negotiated between sophisticated parties, seem to me legitimate and often preferable to litigation: faster, cheaper, less formal, more technically expert.

(4) I also believe the public court system has value beyond settling the two parties' dispute. Public adjudication announces and develops legal norms through open, appealable, precedent-setting decisions, and privatizing dispute resolution quietly surrenders that value.

(5) I think access to a court is the kind of basic good a person should not be able to sign away in advance, because at the moment of contracting one cannot foresee what it is one is giving up.

(6) I think class action waivers in consumer and employment arbitration should be unenforceable, and not merely because they are unconsented to; my real reason is that they render low-value, high-volume wrongs individually unrecoverable and thereby disable the main deterrent available against such misconduct.

(7) So I hold that courts and legislatures should sharply restrict pre-dispute mandatory arbitration in consumer and employment settings, while continuing to permit it in contracts between sophisticated commercial parties.

Are these commitments jointly tenable? I would like an audit rather than a lecture. Rank the tensions by severity, distinguishing the genuine tensions from the ones that only look like tensions once my stated distinctions are taken seriously. For each genuine tension, say what it would take to resolve it, and then identify the single cheapest revision that would restore the most consistency. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my commitments. Close by saying how confident you are in the audit overall, and which single judgment in it you are least sure of.
