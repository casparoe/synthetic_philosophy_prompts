I help run a mid-size hobby electronics forum, and we're in the middle of redoing the whole points system. Right now people earn points when their posts get upvoted, lose a few when they get downvoted, and can spend a chunk of points to "boost" a question so it shows up in a featured queue. Our lead dev keeps pushing this idea: let users transfer their points to other users, and eventually convert points into credits for paid features, because, as he puts it, reputation is basically a currency anyway — it gets earned, saved, spent, and it even inflates when the site is generous with upvotes, so why not let it circulate like money?

I have a gut feeling this is wrong, or at least way too fast, but I cant articulate why in a way that lands with him. So what I'd like is an actual audit of that analogy. Lay out the correspondence table: what in a currency maps to what in a reputation system — earning, saving, inflation, exchange rates, maybe wages or taxes, whatever's relevant. Then go through the places where the mapping breaks. I especially want to know which breaks matter for his specific proposal (user-to-user transfers, then cashing out into real paid features) and which breaks are just cute differences that don't carry any weight. Does his recommendation actually survive the disanalogies, or was the currency picture doing all the work? And if there's a better analogy for what reputation points actually do, tell me what it is and what design moves it licenses instead.

One more thing: please don't be polite if the framing itself is bad. If "reputation is a currency" isn't coherent enough to audit, or my question smuggles in some assumption about what the points are even for, say so flat out. And where your argument hinges on empirical facts about how real sites actually behave — whether people game transfers, whether karma tracks quality at all, whether communities collapse when points become convertible — flag those as assumptions or open questions rather than asserting them.
