I'm writing a paper for an upper-level seminar on loyalty and whistleblowing, and I need a single thought experiment constructed to a list of specifications rather than found in the world. The paper's thesis is that the wrong of betrayal is not fundamentally an agent-centered matter in the way a virtue-theoretic account suggests: an act can be a genuine betrayal—a real abuse of trust within a relationship—without displaying a disloyal character trait, and indeed can be the action of a good colleague. I need a case that shows this in the harder direction, where the act is a betrayal by the relationship-based standard but the agent remains good precisely through it.

Construct a single ordinary-life scenario (no science fiction, no perfect predictors, no legal compulsion to report) in which: (1) an employee discloses a colleague's serious but not catastrophic professional wrongdoing, breaking an explicit confidence between them; (2) the colleague is a decent, well-intentioned person acting under institutional pressure, not a villain; (3) the stakes are serious enough that silence would be genuinely wrong, but no one will die or be physically harmed if the whistleblower stays silent; (4) a trust-based account of betrayal—on which the act is a betrayal because it abuses the trust constitutive of the relationship and carries a distinctive pro tanto wrong—delivers the verdict that the disclosure is a genuine betrayal yet is all-things-considered required; and (5) an agent-centered account—on which the betrayal-specific wrong lies in the expression of a disloyal character trait—delivers the verdict that the disclosure, while describable as a betrayal in the relationship-based sense, is required and carries no betrayal-specific wrong at all because the agent acts from good collegial judgment rather than from any defective trait.

Then do the check. State the disclosure, the two accounts, and how each reaches its verdict from its own internal resources rather than by stipulation. Identify the precise feature of the case that does the work, say what would collapse if that feature were removed, and explain how a defender of the trust-based account would push back in a way that refuses to let the betrayal-specific wrong be detached from the breach of trust. Treat any scenario that secures the verdicts by declaring the confidence trivial, the wrongdoing minor, or the colleague malicious as failing the brief.
