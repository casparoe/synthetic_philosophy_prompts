I'm in a media theory course and we just did the culture industry chapter, and I cant shake the feeling that the whole thing is a description of recommender systems written decades before they existed. The way Adorno and Horkheimer describe it: culture gets produced by an industrial process, everything is sorted into a genre before the consumer shows up, apparent novelty is just recombination of the same handful of elements, and the audience's reactions are already accounted for in advance so nothing genuinely outside the system can get through. Swap film studio for platform and box office for engagement metrics and it reads like a description of how Netflix or TikTok commission and rank content. Is this a real structural identity or am I just pattern matching on the word standardization? If its real, does the critical part transfer too — is pseudo-individuality in mass culture the same thing as algorithmic personalization, or does something break because a user can see their own recommendations, refresh them, and try to game them? And if its superficial, what exactly is missing on one side — what does the parallel predict about recommender systems that turns out false?
