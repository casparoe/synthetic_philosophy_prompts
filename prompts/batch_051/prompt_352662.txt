I'm a PhD student in linguistics, and my department's writing program is considering a binding rule for everyone who comments on undergraduate prose: a construction may be flagged as "nonstandard" or "an error" only if it falls below a fixed frequency threshold in a continuously updated reference corpus of published English—say, below one occurrence per two million words; anything at or above the threshold must be annotated "attested in the reference corpus," whatever the tutor's own judgment, and tutor decisions would be audited against the corpus rather than against any grammar or style manual. Treat the rule as a mechanism and walk me through how it would actually behave. Where do the choice of corpus and the choice of threshold do the hidden normative work? What happens to register—would formal academic prose, informal speech, and genre-specific writing collapse into a single frequency standard—and which constructions would slip through or be wrongly flagged? Who is incentivized to game it first, students, tutors, publishers, or the corpus maintainers, and how? Finally, can the scheme be amended to handle those failures without reintroducing prescriptivism in the guise of corpus curation?
