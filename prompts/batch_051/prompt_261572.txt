In my graduate seminar on the epistemology of animal minds, I want a paper that treats the following claim not as a legal or policy question but as a testability problem: that decapod crustaceans — the prawns, crabs, and lobsters central to modern aquaculture — can feel pain, where "pain" means an aversive state with phenomenal and motivational character, rather than mere nociception, understood as the sensory registration of tissue damage together with the reflexive behaviors that follow from it.

Design the best experiment you can, pitched at a feasible scale for a laboratory, that would discriminate between two hypotheses: (1) the animal possesses a pain-like state of the relevant kind, and (2) the animal's responses to injury are exhaustively explained by nociception and its downstream reflexes, fight-or-flight reactions, and associative avoidance, with no negatively valenced feeling involved. Your design must not simply measure withdrawal from a noxious stimulus, since withdrawal is common to both hypotheses. Instead, exploit features that plausibly separate a felt state with motivational force from a mere reflex: for example, trade-offs between competing motivations, persisting changes in preference after injury, or the selective effects of treatments that in vertebrates blunt the affective component of pain while leaving basic stimulus detection largely intact.

Then set out: (a) exactly what pattern of results would count as evidence for the pain interpretation; (b) exactly what pattern of results would count as evidence for the nociception-only interpretation; and (c) what even the best version of the test could not settle — the conceptual or inferential residue that survives any finite set of behavioral and physiological measurements. Your paper should be self-critical about its operationalization: if the test requires us to define "pain" behaviorally, say whether anything essential to the concept is left out.

Finally, commit. Do not end with a menu of unresolved options. State a definite conclusion about whether the best feasible test you describe could, in principle, give us genuine warrant to attribute pain to some decapod crustaceans, or whether the step from nociception to pain always involves an analogical inference that no experiment can replace. Defend that conclusion with the specifics of your design.
