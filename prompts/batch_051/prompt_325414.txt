I have been convening an adversarial collaboration between two camps that disagree about whether increasingly autonomous systems create responsibility gaps. Both sides accept that an autonomous vehicle, a care robot, or an algorithmic trading system can cause serious harm in a situation no particular person intended, foresaw, or controlled at the moment of the action. They disagree sharply about what follows. The first camp, which we call the gap-realist camp, holds that these cases can outrun the standard conditions of responsibility—knowledge, control, and foreseeability—so thoroughly that retrospective blame or punishment of any individual would be unjust, and that this is a genuinely new structural problem: responsibility cannot simply be relocated onto designers, operators, or firms without changing the concept. The second camp, which we call the responsibility-practices camp, holds that the supposed gap is a pseudo-problem or at most a bookkeeping challenge: legal systems already use vicarious and strict liability, and moral responsibility is better understood through forward-looking duties to design, oversee, compensate, and learn. On their view, no one's responsibility vanishes; it is distributed across roles and practices, and the task is to specify that distribution honestly rather than to mourn a gap.

I want you to write the document this collaboration should have produced after a year of good-faith argument: a joint statement both camps can sign. It should include the following. First, a numbered list of agreed claims, precisely worded, non-vacuous, and acceptable to both sides. Second, the residual disagreements stated exactly, each formulated so that its own side would accept the wording; flag any part of the dispute that turns out to be merely verbal and restate the remaining disagreement so it is substantive. Third, the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—and for each crux a concrete case or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use at least two worked cases, such as an autonomous vehicle that causes harm in a novel scenario no engineer concretely anticipated, and an algorithmic trading system whose interactions with other systems trigger a flash crash. I suspect the cruxes will include whether backward-looking answerability has a value that forward-looking duties cannot replace, and whether vicarious or strict liability should count as responsibility or as something weaker, but do not treat my guesses as constraints. Do not settle the dispute; the aim is a document both camps could put their names to, in which the shared ground is substantial and the remaining disagreement is exact.
