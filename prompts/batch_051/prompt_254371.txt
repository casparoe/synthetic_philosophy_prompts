So I was thinking about this thing in MMOs like WoW where some items are soulbound: once you pick them up you can't trade or sell them to other players, you can only use them or delete them. Usually the best-in-slot gear is bind-on-pickup, and the devs obviously do that on purpose. If the best items could just be bought with gold, then rich players would skip the raid, the status hierarchy would collapse, and a drop would stop being an achievement and become a cash equivalent. That sounds basically identical to the argument against selling kidneys or votes: allow a market and you change what the thing means, and something valuable gets corrupted. So here's the parallel I cant decide about. Soulbinding is a game designer's way of making a good non-transferable to protect the practice. Is that the same structure as saying a kidney or a vote is "not for sale," or is it just pattern-matching because both sound like anti-market rules? If it is real, does anything transfer back to the commodification debate — for instance, games already have bind-on-pickup, bind-on-equip, and fully tradeable items, and maybe that finer-grained menu is a better vocabulary than just "saleable or not" for thinking about organs, votes, etc.? Or does the analogy break because in a game only fun and status are at stake, nobody's welfare or dignity is on the line, so the game restrictions are coordination/balance rules rather than moral ones? Tell me where the structures actually line up and where they come apart.
