I keep thinking about whether a finite mind can run out of new experiences, and I'd like to know whether one principle captures my verdicts on that. A sitcom episode I've rewatched six times is dead — I can recite it, but I'm not really experiencing it. The same three-mile walking loop, though, isn't dead after a couple thousand times; I still notice new birds and new light on the same trees. Looking at the ocean never seems to bottom out, even though it's more repetitive than the episode. But I don't buy my friend's claim that his 500th listen of the same album is a completely new album each time — the album is used up, and he's relishing the memory of it. And I think a decades-long marriage can keep producing genuinely new experience even though the other person is a finite, familiar thing. Is there one principle that separates the experience-sources that get used up from the ones that don't? What does it predict about an AI that generates endless plausible variations on two or three templates — is that genuinely new experience or only new content? If no clean principle fits all five, tell me which of my verdicts I should overturn.
