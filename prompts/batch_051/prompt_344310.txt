I teach philosophy of religion, and one of my students keeps leaning on the slogan "prayer changes things" as if it settles the whole problem of petitionary prayer. I've seen it on bumper stickers for years, and I honestly dont know which precise claim is doing the work each time she says it. I'd like you to lay out the distinct things "prayer changes things" could be claiming, from the trivially true to the theologically radical, and for each say (a) whether it's compatible with God's being omniscient and immutable, and (b) whether it actually supports her view that petitionary prayer to such a God still makes sense. Some readings I can half-see: prayer changes the person praying; prayer changes other people's intentions; prayer changes God's mind (which would give up immutability); prayer was eternally factored into the one divine decree, so the change is real in the world but not in God; prayer changes the divine-human relationship even if it doesn't change either party's essence. But I want a systematic menu rather than my half-formed list. For each reading, give a verdict — platitude, false, or substantive and defensible — and say whether someone who believes in an omniscient and immutable God can consistently repeat the slogan. Also run each reading through the case of someone praying for a friend's operation after the operation has already gone badly but before the person has heard. If that last case just muddies things, say so. Please write in American English.
