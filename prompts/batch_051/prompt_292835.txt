I have long found the core of the doomsday argument persuasive: that applying the self-sampling assumption to my birth rank among all humans who will ever live should shift my credence substantially toward an earlier end of the species, relative to an otherwise reasonable empirical prior. I know the standard SIA reply, the reference-class problem, and the prior-dependence objection, so I am not asking for a tour of the usual talking points. What I want is a map of what would genuinely count against the position: observations, arguments, formal results, or shifts in the surrounding anthropic framework, sorted by how decisive they are. Which would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which are elastic enough that a careful defender can absorb them without cost? In particular, has the position, carefully stated, been insulated into unfalsifiability, and is that a serious flaw for a claim of this kind? What should a committed proponent concede under pressure, and what would a future observation short of extinction have to look like to be a real update against the doomsday conclusion rather than a cosmetic one?
