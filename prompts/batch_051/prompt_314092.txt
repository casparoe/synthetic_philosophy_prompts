I am a science journalist who covers physics and the philosophy of science, and I am working on an explainer about what the word "chaotic" actually commits you to in deterministic systems. As I understand the standard criteria, a system counts as chaotic when it has three features: (1) sensitive dependence on initial conditions, usually cashed out as a positive largest Lyapunov exponent; (2) topological transitivity, so that some orbit wanders densely through the attractor and any two small patches of it eventually overlap under the dynamics; and (3) a dense set of periodic orbits, so that unstable cycles are threaded through the attractor at every scale. On top of these, I have been using the more popular criterion that a chaotic attractor is a strange attractor: a fractal object with detailed structure on arbitrarily fine scales.

The case that now troubles me is a quasiperiodically forced nonlinear oscillator. Think of a damped pendulum driven by two incommensurate frequencies rather than one. In certain parameter regimes, such a system settles onto an attractor whose cross-section looks fractal, with structure that does not smooth out under magnification. Yet, from what I have read, the largest Lyapunov exponent in these regimes is not positive: nearby trajectories do not separate exponentially, and there is no sensitive dependence in the usual sense. Nor is it obvious that the attractor is threaded with dense periodic orbits, since the driving is quasiperiodic rather than periodic.

So my question is this. You are a philosopher of science who works closely with dynamical systems theory, and I want you to think carefully rather than reach for the textbook answer. Before you give a verdict, brainstorm briefly about what each criterion is really meant to capture. Then work through the criteria one by one and tell me whether this quasiperiodically forced fractal attractor should count as chaotic. Where the criteria pull in different directions, say which one is doing the real work in distinguishing chaos from mere geometrical strangeness. Finally, tell me whether this case reveals that my list of criteria is a motley collection that needs to be replaced, or whether the case simply falls just outside a defensible boundary.
