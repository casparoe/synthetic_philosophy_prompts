I'm running a workshop for an ML interpretability group where half the room thinks AI-generated explanations of models are the future and the other half thinks they just add another black box on top. I want a sorting quiz for the "can AI interpret AI?" debate: seven or eight statements answered on a five-point agree/disagree scale, plus a scoring key that sorts people into four to six named positions on whether the recursive-interpretability regress (the interpreter itself needs interpreting, and so on) is a real problem. The statements should be answerable by someone with no interpretability background—phrased around ordinary intuitions about explanation, control, and understanding. Make sure the positions include at least: interpretation must bottom out in human concepts; the regress is benign because each layer only needs to be predictively useful; an interpreter at least as complex as its target provides no explanation; and the real constraint is causal manipulability rather than human graspability. Give each position a short name and one sentence on what its defender would least like to admit, note any answer combinations nobody seems to occupy (with a quick guess at why), and flag which positions the quiz can't cleanly seperate.
