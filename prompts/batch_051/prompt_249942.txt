For a graduate seminar in Indian philosophy, write the list of requirements an account of māyā must meet in order to count as an adequate Advaita Vedānta theory of the apparent world. Fix the purpose of the theory as follows: it must preserve the sole ultimate reality of Brahman and the identity of the individual self with Brahman, while explaining how a world of plurality, change, and suffering can appear and be taken as real prior to liberation, and it must show how liberation is possible through the removal of ignorance rather than through any change in Brahman itself.

For each requirement, give the consideration or motivating case behind it, and then mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any Advaitin and which are contested between subschools or interpretive camps. Your list should address at least the following constraints:

- The non-duality constraint: māyā must not be a second ultimate principle alongside Brahman.
- The status constraint: māyā must be neither simply real nor simply unreal, and the account must say what this intermediate status can coherently mean.
- The explanatory constraint: the account must explain the apparent intersubjective order of the world, including its regularities, without granting that order ultimate reality.
- The locus-and-object problem for ignorance: the account must say where ignorance resides and what it conceals without circularity; if ignorance resides in Brahman, Brahman seems to be ignorant, while if it resides in the individual self, the individual self is itself a product of ignorance.
- The soteriological constraint: the account must explain how a single cognition of the identity of self and Brahman can destroy ignorance that is beginningless, and why beginninglessness does not make liberation impossible.
- The relation between cosmic māyā and individual ignorance, and the status of the world after liberation.

If some subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, the demand that māyā be neither real nor unreal together with the demand that the account obey ordinary logic, or the demand that ignorance have a locus together with the demand that Brahman remain untouched — say so explicitly and explain whether a coherent Advaitin can relax one of the demands without giving up the core position. Identify which requirement does the most work in excluding rival accounts, such as Buddhist illusionism, Sāṃkhya dualism, or subjective idealism. Do not turn this into a scorecard of named commentators; the criteria and the cases that motivate them are the deliverable.
