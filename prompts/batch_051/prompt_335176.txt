I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and one of the members was defending a pretty traditional picture: conscious experience is like a theater, with sensory impressions as the scenes on stage, attention as the follow-spot, and the self as an audience watching the one unified show. His real argument was that the unity of consciousness requires a single place and time where everything is presented together for a viewer, and the theater picture gives you that for free. I know Dennett thinks this Cartesian theater is exactly the wrong picture and replaces it with the multiple drafts model, but I want to audit the analogy on its own terms instead of just siding with Dennett. Give me a concrete breakdown: what maps onto what between a theater and conscious experience, where the mapping quietly imports a single time/place and a separate spectator that the actual target might not have, which disanalogies are just decorative and which ones are fatal for the unity argument, and whether the conclusion—that there must be a center where experience comes together for a subject—survives once the disanalogies are on the table. And if the theater doesn't earn its keep, tell me whether there is a better analogy for the messy, multi-track processes Dennett wants to describe, or whether the right move is to drop analogies here altogether.
