I spent forty years in behavioral ecology and only started reading philosophy seriously after I retired, so go easy on me if my terms are a bit homegrown. I'm putting together a short proposal (about 800–1000 words) for a tiny grant from a foundation that funds 'philosophy of AI' projects, and I'd like you to write it. I'll fill in citations myself, so don't bother with references.

My half-formed idea comes out of cooperative breeding. In a fair number of animals — meerkats, Florida scrub jays, some cichlids — the individual that actually rears a young one often isn't the individual that produced it. Helpers, older siblings, sometimes non-relatives do most of the feeding and guarding. Biology had to give up treating 'parent' and 'caregiver' as the same job, and once you split them, responsibility gets interesting. Meanwhile the AI ethics crowd talks about whether AIs are moral agents, and seperately about whether AIs should train other AIs, but I don't see anyone connecting the two. My hunch is this: when one AI trains, fine-tunes, or monitors another during its development, that can amount to something more like guardianship than authorship. And if it can, we have to ask whether the 'raising' AI has duties to the 'raised' AI — or whether this is just a loose analogy doing nonsense work.

One more twist. A guardian doesn't obviously have to be a full moral agent to be a guardian; a ten-year-old can mind a toddler without being a full moral agent in the philosopher's sense. So maybe the guardianship question is worth asking even if the whole 'are AIs moral agents' debate stays unresolved.

Here's what I want the proposal to do. State the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer. Explain why the existing discussions — AI ethics, philosophy of parenthood, maybe uses of animal alloparenting as an analogy — leave this exact question open rather than settling it. Say what a philosopher would actually do for six months to make progress: what distinctions to draw, what cases to build, what intuition pumps to run, and why that beats just doing surveys or an empirical study of training pipelines. Give me a sense of what a useful deliverable looks like for people who build or govern training systems. And be honest about where the project could fail or outgrow its six-month scope.
