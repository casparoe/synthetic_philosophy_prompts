For an adult continuing-ed class I run on practical reason, I want a "which picture of hard choices do you actually hold?" sorting quiz. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case—staying in a city for a relationship vs. taking a dream job, choosing between two paintings you find equally good but in different ways, a friendship vs. a raise, that sort of thing—with two or three answer options each. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto: commensurabilism (hard choices are just covert comparisons or ties), incomparability (some options have no shared scale, so they're neither better, worse, nor equal), parity (options can be on a par: comparable but not rankable), lexical priority (some values categorically override others), and existential choice (when reason runs out, the choice itself settles who you are). Build the question set out of the logical space of possible stances first—start from the trichotomy "better, worse, or equal" and ask where each view breaks it—then place these five views in that space; note any combination of answers that seems coherent but unattractive or unexplored, and flag any pair of views your key can't cleanly seperate. The questions should sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so don't ask anything like "do you think values are incommensurable?" Keep the cases sharp; American English, please.
