I have recently been appointed to a committee revising how my institution defines a contested category—for example, disability, for the purposes of an accommodations policy—so that the category better serves a stated practical purpose. The objection I keep encountering is that we would not be analysing the concept as it actually functions but merely stipulating a new one, and that no evidence could show that a revision improves the concept rather than changes the subject. I would like to know whether that distinction is answerable to evidence. Please devise the strongest test you can of the claim that a proposed revision improves the concept relative to a pre-specified function, specify what result would count for and against the improvement reading, and then state what the test cannot settle—including whether talk of improving a concept could survive a positive result, or whether we should offer some more modest description of what has changed.
