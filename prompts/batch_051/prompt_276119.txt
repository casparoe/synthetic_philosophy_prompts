You are the director of a small municipal arts commissioning body whose grant panel must decide each year which of many proposals to fund. The panel has been directed to use Leo Tolstoy’s account in *What Is Art?* as its evaluative framework: on that account, art is the deliberate transmission to others of a feeling the artist has experienced, and its merit turns on the individuality of the feeling, the clearness of its transmission, and the artist’s sincerity. Write the reusable decision procedure the panel should follow: the questions to ask in order, what counts as an adequate answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it must hand the decision to a person because the matter is one of values rather than facts, and the cases it is likely to misfire on. Then run the procedure on two proposals: (1) a technically accomplished interactive digital installation designed to evoke a diffuse sense of unease, where the artist says she has no particular feeling she wants to transmit; and (2) a rough-hewn community theatre piece in which formerly homeless performers re-enact their own experiences with evident directness. State clearly what the procedure cannot tell the panel and one answer it gives that the panel will probably resist.
