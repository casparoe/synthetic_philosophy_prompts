I'm a novelist, so I spend a lot of time thinking about how a representation can feel real while its contents are made up, and there's a philosophy-of-mind analogy I keep tripping over that I want pulled apart. The move is this: phenomenal consciousness — the felt redness of red, the ache of a tooth, the salty taste of a memory — is something like a user interface. A computer screen shows me little folders, a trash can, a progress bar; none of those things exist as such in the hardware, but the display is real and it lets me do things. The illusionist then leans on this for consciousness: qualia don't need to be in the brain any more than folders are in the machine; the interface is doing real causal work, so you haven't lost anything and you haven't gone eliminativist, you've just stopped mistaking the icons for the machinery.

I want an honest audit of how far this carries, because I keep sensing the analogy is doing more work then it can. Give me the correspondence table: in the consciousness version, what plays the role of the physical screen, the pixels, the folder icons, the person at the keyboard, and the designer who drew the icons? Then walk me through where it breaks. The parts that worry me are these: the computer has a user who is seperate from the hardware, and the icons were designed by someone on purpose, and the pattern on the screen is about files whether or not the display gets it exactly right. Are those just incidental disanalogies, like the computer having a power cord, or do they smuggle in the very things illusionism wants to deny — a real perspective the display is for, maybe a real aboutness that had to be put there on purpose?

So, once the disanalogies are all on the table, which ones matter for the conclusion that phenomenal properties are an illusion and which ones are harmless? Does that conclusion survive the audit, or was the interface analogy carrying the argument? And if it fails, is there a better analogy for what illusionism is trying to say, or should illusionists drop the interface talk entirely? Please answer in American English.
