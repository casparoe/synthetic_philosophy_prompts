I keep coming back to skeptical theism because of how much it does in a single move. The evidential problem of evil says that if God existed, the world would not contain the apparently pointless suffering it does; the fact that we can look at a case and see no reason that would justify God in permitting it is evidence that no such reason exists. The skeptical theist replies that we are in no position to make that inference: given the gap between our cognitive resources and God's, we should not expect the reasons that would justify divine permission of evil to be accessible to us. That one principle — a modesty principle about our ability to detect God-justifying reasons — undercuts an entire family of arguments without committing the theist to any particular theodicy. I find that economy genuinely attractive.

But I keep being ambushed by cases. Take a child who suffers and dies slowly from a painful illness, fully conscious for much of it. My reaction isn't just that I can't see a reason; it's that the permission of this evil is the sort of thing no amount of hidden, outweighing good could make right, and that a being who could prevent it and didn't would be less good than any being I could worship. I don't know how to hold that reaction together with the modest epistemic principle above.

I want a negotiated verdict rather than a winner. Present the strongest version of both sides: the strongest case that my reaction to the child's suffering is a moral datum I should trust, and the strongest case that it is exactly the kind of cognitive overreach skeptical theism warns against. Then tell me which should yield: the intuition or the theory, and whether there is a version of skeptical theism — for example, one restricted to cases where the hidden goods at stake are of a kind sufficiently unlike any human good, or one restricted to our inability to trace total consequences — on which both survive. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
