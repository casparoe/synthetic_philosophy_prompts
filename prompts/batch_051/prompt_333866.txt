People talk about a word being untranslatable (saudade, or whatever) and about whether a translation is faithful, like either of those means something clear, but I don't think it does. I want a working definition of a faithful translation that gets these cases right: a technical manual where every term has an exact equivalent; a poem where a literal version keeps the meaning but loses the sound and surprise; a joke whose whole punchline depends on a pun; a novel where a character's dialect marks their class, and the closest dialect in the other language would make them from the wrong country; a political speech built on a metaphor the other language doesn't have, so you'd need to explain it or invent a new one; a translation that changes the ending because the translator thinks the original ending is too depressing; and a slur that lands much harder in the target language, so the translator softens it. I think the manual is clearly faithful and the changed ending is clearly not, and I'm drawn to saying faithful means faithful to the effect, not the words—but then I cant tell whether that rule lets the translator soften the slur or flatten the dialect. Propose a definition, run it through all seven, and tell me where it surprises you or forces you to revise.
