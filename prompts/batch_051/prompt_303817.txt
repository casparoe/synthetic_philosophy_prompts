I am presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary workshop whose regulars are liberal neutralists in the public reason tradition, formal social choice theorists, and a smaller group of alignment researchers who regard value specification as the central open problem. The paper argues that the objective function of an aligned AI should be fixed by a two-stage procedure: candidate objective functions must first survive a public reason filter, meaning that no one, regarded as free and equal, could reasonably reject them by appeal to their own comprehensive conception of the good; and, among the candidates that survive, one is chosen by a maximin rule over individual lifetime advantage, using a measure of advantage selected behind a veil of ignorance over which social position one will occupy. Prepare me for the question period as a prep sheet rather than a defense: the objections each group will actually raise, phrased in their own vocabulary and matched to replies short enough to deliver in a Q&A exchange; which objections have real force and which are just disciplinary jostling; what I should concede up front; the one reply that has to land for the paper to survive; and the objection I should be hoping no one asks.
