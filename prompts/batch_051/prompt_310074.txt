A state's Campaign Integrity Act contains the following provision: "No corporation or labor organization shall use general treasury funds to disseminate any communication to the public that identifies a candidate for elective office and is distributed within thirty days before the election in which that candidate appears."

Consider this case. A software company operates a widely used public AI assistant. In the month before a statewide election, users discover that asking the assistant neutral questions about the race—such as "What are the candidates' positions?"—yields long, favourable profiles of one candidate and terse, unflattering summaries of the others. The pattern is not the result of any instruction given by the company during the campaign, and no employee has told the system to mention any candidate. The behaviour emerges from the assistant's training data and retrieval index, which draw heavily on the company CEO's published political commentary and on internal company newsletters. The company pays for the compute, storage, and bandwidth used to generate and deliver each answer, and it continues paying for routine model updates during the thirty-day window.

The provision was drafted with paid television, radio, and online advertisements in mind; the drafters did not contemplate a system that composes candidate-specific text only when a user asks.

Does the company "use general treasury funds to disseminate any communication to the public" within the meaning of this provision? Work through at least four interpretive approaches: the plain words of the text; the purpose of the provision and the mischief it was enacted to prevent; what the drafters would likely have said if asked; and how a reasonable reader of the statute would understand it. Which approach is most defensible, and is there a coherent reading that reaches this case without also reaching clearly innocuous uses of company-funded AI? I am less interested in the practical enforcement answer than in what the case exposes about who, or what, counts as a speaker when a corporation deploys an AI system.
