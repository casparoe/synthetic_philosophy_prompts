I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, but I keep getting pulled into AI alignment debates, and there's a claim I hear in interpretability circles that I can't decide is profound or empty: that you can watch a system behave and infer both its objective function and the space of alternatives it's actually searching over. The skeptic in me says this is either trivially false, since any finite stretch of behavior can be made optimal under indefinitely many objectives and search spaces, or it only becomes true once you smuggle in strong assumptions about simplicity, priors, or internals. But I don't want to settle it from the armchair. So treat it as a testable claim: how would you actually test whether someone has recovered an optimizer's real objective and search space from its behavior, rather than having fitted a just-so story after the fact? Design the strongest test you can. What would the system have to do or predict in novel circumstances, what result would count for the attribution, what would count against it, and what part of the claim does even a positive result leave untouched? I care more about the conceptual shape of the test than about an actual ML benchmark.
