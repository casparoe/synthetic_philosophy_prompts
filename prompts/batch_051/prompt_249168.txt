Write me a short poem about mind uploading by gradual replacement—the thought experiment where a patient's neurons get swapped out one at a time for functionally identical artificial units and they never loose consciousness or memory during it. Here's the formal bit. Build the poem in short stanzas, and each stanza must be the previous stanza with exactly one word changed, where each change moves the vocabulary from biological to machine ('neuron' to 'node,' 'blood' to 'current,' 'flesh' to 'substrate,' that kind of thing). Keep the stanzas short enough that by the last stanza all the original body-words are gone, but the syntax and the first-person voice never break. The poem should make the reader feel both the intuition that it's the same person the whole way through and the darker worry that the original quietly vanished at some particular swap, without resolving which is right. American English please.
