I am taking a historiography course, and one of our assigned textbooks summarizes the periodization of European history in the following way: "The familiar division of the Western past into the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution is essentially a later invention. The people living through those centuries did not think of themselves as medieval, as Renaissance, or as part of a scientific revolution; the labels were attached afterward, often to flatter the period doing the labeling and to diminish the centuries in between. These periods are therefore not real features of the past but organizing conventions that historians have projected onto a much messier historical record." The account is appealing, but the final step—inferring from "the labels came later" that "the periods are merely conventions"—strikes me as too quick, though I can't yet articulate exactly why. Is this a fair summary of how the categories should be understood? Please separate what is accurate from what is overstated or misleading, and explain whether the conclusion about the periods being mere conventions actually follows from the facts about their later labeling.
