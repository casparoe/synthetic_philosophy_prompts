im in a lit theory class and we just read Wimsatt and Beardsley on the intentional fallacy. The argument, as I understand it, is that a poem is a public object and its meaning is in the words themselves as fixed by linguistic conventions; what the author privately had in mind is neither available nor relevant to what the text means, and treating it as the source of meaning is a category mistake. But the other day I made an LLM write something for me and it struck me this is basically a natural experiment for their view: here is text with no authorial intention at all behind it, just next-token prediction over a corpus, and yet I still read it as meaning things, sometimes fairly specific things. So does the intentional-fallacy argument survive this development, or does it need repair? I can see it cutting both ways: maybe LLM texts finally vindicate them, since meaning obviously doesnt need an intending author, or maybe the whole text-vs-author distinction stops doing its original work once there is no intention to exclude. I want to know what happens to the argument itself, not what we should do about LLMs. Identify which premise of their argument the LLM case actually bears on, and say whether the argument still goes through, needs repair, or is dead—and tell me what would most likely change your mind about that or where your reasoning is shakiest.
