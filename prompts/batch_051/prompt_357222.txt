Here's my reconstruction of an argument I half-remember from Schopenhauer, and I keep going back and forth on whether it's actually any good. I suspect step 3 is doing too much work, but maybe I'm wrong. Walk me through it.

(1) You can only will what you currently lack; to will something is to strive for something you don't have. (If you already have it, you're not willing it, you're just enjoying it.)

(2) Striving out of lack is itself a state of dissatisfaction — a kind of suffering. The will is essentially restless.

(3) When the thing finally arrives, what occurs is just the removal of that specific lack. The resulting state is nothing over and above the absence of the prior suffering.

(4) So satisfaction/happiness is purely negative: the momentary cancellation of a suffering, never a positive state in its own right.

(5) Therefore the only positive state available in this picture is suffering itself; satisfaction is at best its cancelling-out.

(6) A fully satisfied life would then just be a life with episodically cancelled suffering — a zero, not a plus.

(7) So existence can't be judged positively; pessimism.
