You are translating a piece of contemporary metaphysics into the vocabulary of a senior backend engineer. Your reader is a working programmer with no philosophy background, comfortable with classes, objects, interfaces, methods and preconditions, lazy evaluation, dependency injection, configuration and feature flags, monkey patching, mocks and other test doubles, middleware and interceptors, retries and circuit breakers, timeouts, side effects, and idempotency. They have never heard of dispositions, finks, masks, antidotes, or counterfactual analyses.

Begin by restating the target debate in plain terms, so that the translation has something precise to work from. The core phenomenon is a disposition: a fragile glass is disposed to break when struck; a sugar cube is disposed to dissolve when placed in water. The simple conditional analysis says an object is disposed to manifest M under stimulus S exactly when, if S occurred, M would occur. The debate turns on three kinds of interference. A fink is a case in which the object gains or loses the disposition at the very moment the stimulus arrives — a glass that, when struck, is switched to unbreakable. A mask is a case in which the disposition remains present but its manifestation is blocked by an external circumstance — sugar wrapped in plastic, still soluble but unable to dissolve while wrapped. An antidote is a case in which an external process intervenes after the stimulus to stop the manifestation that would otherwise occur — a poison that kills unless an antidote is administered after ingestion.

Now translate this debate into the engineer's vocabulary as systematically as you can. Treat a disposition as a capacity the program has under specified triggers, the stimulus condition as a precondition or triggering event, and the manifestation as the observable outcome or side effect. Provide a term-by-term mapping for fink, mask, and antidote using whichever constructs from their world fit best — monkey patching, mocks, feature flags, dependency injection, interceptor middleware, lazy evaluation, circuit breakers, or something else — and show where each philosophical claim lands. Do not let the word "translation" become a license to redesign the metaphysics so that it fits cleanly; the residue is the interesting part.

Then give the residue report. Be explicit about: which claims translate cleanly; which claims you had to distort, and what assumptions the software framework quietly imposes in the translation; and which parts of the metaphysical debate have no counterpart in the programmer's world, or which software phenomena would tempt the engineer to read the metaphysics differently. End with a short verdict on whether the failure of the simple conditional analysis is genuinely captured as a software problem — a masking bug, a race between configuration and execution, a monkey-patched test, a missing dependency — or whether the software analogy changes the subject.
