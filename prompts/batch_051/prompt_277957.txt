I am a novelist drafting a collection of stories about the psychology of incorruptibility, and I would like you to write one of them for me: a self-contained short story of roughly 1,000 words. The working constraint is that the protagonist is impeccably incorruptible in the narrow sense — she has never taken a bribe, sold a confidence, or bent a rule for a friend — yet by the end the reader should feel that this very trait has made her the instrument of something worse. The seam I want the story to open is the one between two things we often run together: declining what one should not take, and allowing oneself to be moved by what matters. Develop this through two refusals that look superficially alike. In the first, she declines an envelope of cash. In the second, she declines a request to bend a procedure that would prevent a manifest injustice. Show, rather than argue, why the second refusal is not the same as the first, and keep the story's judgment implicit.
