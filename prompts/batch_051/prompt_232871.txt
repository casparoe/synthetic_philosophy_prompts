Write a sonnet in the voice of a strict negative utilitarian who has just worked through the benevolent world-exploder thought experiment and realized their view entails that a painless, instant extinction of all sentient life would be a better state of affairs than any world still containing suffering. Spend the octave building the asymmetry claim: no amount of happiness can make up for one peice of unrelieved agony, and the absence of pain is good in a way the absence of pleasure is not. Let the volta be the moment the speaker notices this commits them to endorsing the button. Use the closing couplet to mark the difference between endorsing that outcome and being willing to press it—say, because the button might fail and cause the very agony the view fears. Aim for accuracy: someone who knows the negative-utilitarian debate should recognize the structure of the objection being dramatized.
