I'm on my university's academic integrity board, and we've got a weird one this week. Marcus, a sophomore, bought an essay from an online paper mill and submitted it for his history course — no dispute about that part; we have the receipt. But it came out that his mother, without him knowing, had arranged in advance for someone with the login to his course portal to make sure a ghostwritten paper went in under his name before the deadline no matter what. If Marcus had uploaded an original paper, they would have swapped it; if he'd uploaded nothing, they would have uploaded one themselves. They never had to do anything, because Marcus turned in the paper he'd bought on his own at 10:40.

Half the committee now thinks this let's Marcus off the hook: the offense was going to happen anyway, so how can we sanction him for an outcome he couldn't have prevented? The other half thinks that's backwards — he still chose to buy and submit the paper, with full knowledge, and you don't get exonerated just because somebody else was standing by to make the same thing happen. I argued about this with a friend till 2am and we couldn't land it.

I need to circulate a short memo before Thursday. Please draft it for me. Give a concrete recommendation — full sanction, reduced, none, or something else the board could actually impose — and make the reasoning usable by six tired faculty members who aren't going to sit through a philosophy lecture. Most importantly, take the 'he couldn't have done otherwise' point seriously; don't just wave it off. And write it in American English.
