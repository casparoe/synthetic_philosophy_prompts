Act as an expert on ancient atomism and philosophy of physics. Think carefully — maybe start by brainstorming what the atoms actually have to do in Lucretius’ system before you commit to a list. I’m a student in a Hellenistic philosophy class and I’ve been rereading De Rerum Natura Book 1. This passage, from William Ellery Leonard’s translation (on Gutenberg), is where the whole project gets laid out:

“I prove the supreme law of Gods and sky,
And the primordial germs of things unfold,
Whence Nature all creates, and multiplies
And fosters all, and whither she resolves
Each in the end when each is overthrown.
This ultimate stock we have devised to name
Procreant atoms, matter, seeds of things,
Or primal bodies, as primal to the world.”

I know he later argues that these “primordial germs” must be solid, indivisible, and impenetratable, with void around them. But instead of just rehearsing his argument, I want the desiderata for a theory of matter in this tradition: what does any adequate account of these primordial bodies have to get right? Focus especially on the concept of solidity/impenetrability and the void — why is each a requirement, which ones are non-negotiable versus preferences of particular interpreters, and are there any pairs that can’t be jointly satisfied (like solidity plus the need for void to explain motion)? Also, which single desideratum does the most to exclude rival ancient accounts, like the four-elements theory or Anaxagoras’s mixture? Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring later theories, but if you have room, a quick note on how Lucretius’ own atoms and Epicurus’ original formulation do against the list would help.
