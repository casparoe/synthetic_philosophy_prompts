I'm a journalist who covers legal theory and public ideas, and for the last six months I've been moderating an adversarial collaboration between two people I've written about a lot: a retired appellate judge who is a pretty committed textualist, and a law professor who is just as committed a purposivist. They've now agreed to publish one joint statement under both their names, and I'd like you to draft it as if you where the neutral scribe in the room. Not another explainer of the two schools, please. I want the document they could both actually sign: the claims about statutory language, legislative intent, and judging that both accept once the fog clears; a precise statement of where they still part company, with each side's own position phrased the way that side would itself phrase it; and the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that getting a settled answer would genuinely move at least one of them. For each crux, give a concrete statutory example or a kind of evidence, real or invented but realistic, that both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each of them expects that example or evidence would show. Also flag any part of their six-month fight that looks to you mostly verbal, and say why.

Two candidate cruxes I'd like you to work with, though you can add or drop them if you think they're wrong: one is fixed meaning meeting new facts, as with a statute written before electronic records were a live case and using terms like document or signature, which now has to be applied to purely digital artifacts. Does the purposivist get to treat the statutory purpose as covering those new media, and does the textualist have to bite the bullet and say the statute doesn't reach them? The other is ordinary meaning and corpus linguistics: would both sides accept a well-constructed corpus as a neutral tiebreaker for what an ordinary reader would have understood, or does each side have a standing objection to it, and is that objection the same or different? If either of these isn't a real crux, say so and replace it with one that is.

Continuous prose only, please. No headings, no bullet points, no tables, no bold. Write it the way a long co-authored memo or an open letter under both their names would read.
