I'm giving a 45-minute methods talk at my cognitive science department next month and I'm already dreading the Q&A. The title is something like "LLMs are not Popperian, and alignment people should stop pretending they are." The thesis is that current LLMs are a scrambled version of Dennett's tower. They look Gregorian because they're built from human mind-tools like words and arithmetic, but at inference time they don't preselect hypotheses in an inner environment the way Popperian creatures are supposed to; chain-of-thought, I'll argue, is just the next token in a Skinnerian history, not a process where candidate actions die in the agent's stead. The argument runs like this: (1) a Popperian creature needs something to test candidates against — an inner model of the world that can say "that would be bad before I do it"; (2) an autoregressive LLM has no seperate filter standing over its token predictions and vetoing bad plans, it just has next-token probabilities; (3) the "reasoning" we attribute to chain-of-thought is generated from exactly the same machinery as everything else, so safety people who talk as though the model deliberates between values are inventing an inner environment that isn't there. The audience is empirically-minded cognitive scientists, not philosophers: two predictive-processing people, a comparative cognition person who actually runs Skinner boxes with rats, a couple of fine-tuners who will insist RLHF just *is* operant conditioning, and one senior professor who thinks Dennett's rungs are a just-so story. Draft me a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each person would actually put it, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is softest. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
