Okay, so I've been keeping a list of my actual reactions to coincidence stories, and I'd like someone to wring a principle out of them, or tell me which reactions I should give up. Here's the list.

- In a class of 23 people, two people having the same birthday: barely worth a shrug. I know it's about 50/50, and the match is unremarkable because any two of them could match.

- At my cousin's wedding, the bride and groom discovered they'd been on the same transatlantic flight eight years before they met, and the groom is visible in the corner of a photo the bride's family took. I found this genuinely striking. I don't think the universe arranged it, but I'd never call them silly for saying it was meant to be, and I told the story as 'a real coincidence.'

- My friend Anna dreamed her grandmother died in the night, and the grandmother did die that same night, unexpectedly, on another continent. Anna isn't woo about anything. I can't bring myself to tell her it was random. Intellectually I know dreams are cheap, but this one doesn't feel like noise the way the birthday match does.

- A psychic published 200 predictions for the year in December. One was 'a famous actor dies in a car accident in Los Angeles in the spring.' In April, that happened. I didn't find this impressive at all. Two hundred shots on goal, and the prediction is the kind that gets retro-fitted.

- A seismologist posted a specific public prediction three days in advance: 'a magnitude 5.5–6.0 quake will hit the Salton Sea area within 72 hours.' It happened. As a layperson, I'm impressed — maybe more than by the wedding one, even though it's a single event.

- My uncle swears gelatin capsules cured his knee pain, and I know a controlled study says they don't beat placebo. If I had his knee pain, I'd probly still try them. But I don't think his improvement is good evidence the capsules work for anyone else.

- Roulette: after ten reds, black is still not due. I'd bet neither way. But I notice that if I were forced to bet, I'd lean slightly toward red continuing, which feels like a superstition and also not the usual gambler's fallacy.

The tension I keep hitting is this: I discount the psychic because many predictions cover many outcomes, but I don't discount the wedding-flight story on the grounds that among 80 guests, many pairs have some forgotten overlap, so something like it was near-inevitable. And I can't explain why a pre-registered prediction impresses me while Anna's dream, which is also a one-off 'hit,' doesn't not-impress me. Is there a coherent principle behind our sense that something is a real coincidence and when an anecdote is evidence of anything beyond itself? If so, what does it say about a case I keep avoiding: a friend who keeps a notebook of daily hunches and says about 5% come true, and wants me to treat that as evidence of... I don't even know what, some kind of lucid intuition. And if no principle gets all my verdicts, which ones does it force me to drop?
