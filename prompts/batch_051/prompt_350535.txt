My university's committee on named gifts and honors is deadlocked over a proposed gift, and I have been asked to draft the written opinion that will frame our vote. An alumnus has offered to endow the Harry S. Truman Prize for International Peacebuilding in our school of public policy, awarded annually to a mid-career diplomat or public servant. The donor's letter stresses Truman's role in constructing the postwar institutional order and expressly asks that the prize bear his name. A colleague has circulated a dissent drawn from Elizabeth Anscombe's well-known protest when Oxford considered conferring an honorary degree on Truman in the mid-1950s: in ordering the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, Truman chose to kill noncombatants on a massive scale, deliberately and as a means of forcing Japan's surrender; the deliberate killing of the innocent as a means is murder, whatever ends it serves; and an institution that teaches the responsible exercise of power should not attach its name to such an act. The committee is not divided on the historical record; we are divided on what an honor now would amount to.

Please prepare the decision memo I will circulate to the full committee before our next meeting. It should recommend one of three courses of action — accept the gift with the name, decline it, or accept it with specified conditions — and state a bottom line the committee can act on. Do not open with a recitation of the bombings; the committee already agrees on the facts. Engage the dissenting position in its strongest form, especially the claim that a public honor is itself a moral act and cannot be detached from the means by which the honoree pursued the ends being celebrated. Then give the strongest opposing consideration its due: that an institution can honor a complex historical figure without endorsing every particular act, and that a peacebuilding prize may be the most fitting place to keep that tension in plain view rather than to screen it out. If you recommend conditions, set them out concretely enough for the development office to place them in the gift agreement.
