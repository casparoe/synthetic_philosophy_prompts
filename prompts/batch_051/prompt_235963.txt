So I'm in a philosophy of religion reading group, and every time omnipotence comes up one of the regulars, Jordan, rolls out the same line: "Can God create a stone so heavy that he cannot lift it?" and then sits back like the discussion is settled. Last week he used it against someone who was carefully defending the claim that God can do everything that is possible-in-itself, even the self-consistent things that would be impossible for beings like us. Jordan's version is: either God can make such a stone or he can't. If he can't, then there is a possible-in-itself thing God can't do, so he's not omnipotent by your own definition. If he can, then there's a stone he can't lift, so again he's not omnipotent. Therefore the very idea of omnipotence is self-undermining, and Jordan thinks this works against any version of it, not just a caricature.

I can feel that the one-liner is doing several different things at once, but I've never been able to seperate them cleanly. My rough list is something like: (a) a dilemma aimed only at the naive definition of omnipotence as the ability to do literally anything, contradictions included; (b) a claim that "maximal power" is like "the biggest number," because for any level of power you can describe a stone that defeats it, so the concept has no maximum; (c) a claim that an omnipotent being would fail at a particular task, namely "make an unliftable stone"; (d) a claim that the question is rigged, since "a stone too heavy for him to lift" builds the answer in and isn't an independent fact about his abilities. Could you lay out the menu of precise claims that line could actually be making, sorted from trivial to radical, with a verdict on each: which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false as claims about what a theist has to accept? And the part I actually need: which reading is doing the work when someone uses the line to close down a debate about omnipotence, and which readings are only beating up on a straw man? If the line only works against the straw version, I want to know that too.
