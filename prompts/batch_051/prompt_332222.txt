Heres a question I've been turning over since I retired (lab science background, so I'm new to philosophy). Rawls, as I understand him, is usually read as making a maximin move behind the veil of ignorance: choose the arrangement whose worst possible outcome is best, since you don't know who you'll be. Harsanyi says that's wrong — behind the veil you should assign equal probability to being each person and then maximize expected utility. Now I keep noticing minimax regret being used in real decision systems facing unquantifiable uncertainty, like an AI doing triage or making pandemic policy calls: for each way the world could go, measure how far you fall short of what the best option would have achieved in that same scenario, then pick the option whose biggest such shortfall is smallest. What would Rawls and Harsanyi each have thought of minimax regret as a rule in those settings? Would Rawls see it as a close cousin of his maximin reasoning, or as importantly different because it judges you against a counterfactual best rather than attending to the worst-off? Would Harsanyi have preferred it to maximin, or would he have dismissed it as another ad hoc rule that violates expected-utility reasoning?
