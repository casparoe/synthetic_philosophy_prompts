I cover physics and the philosophy-of-science beat for a general-interest magazine, and I also sit on the external advisory board of a small privite physics institute. The board votes next week on whether to fund a three-year philosopher-in-residence slot — someone with a physics PhD who now works on conceptual foundations. Her pitch is to reopen the hole argument about whether spacetime points are real and to sort out whether diffeomorphism invariance in general relativity actually forces a relationalist picture of spacetime, partly because she thinks it matters for background independence in quantum gravity.

Half the board wants to kill it on principle: they keep reaching for Weinberg, Hawking, and Feynman, the usual claims about philosophy being dead or useless to working scientists, and they say the money should go to actual theory work instead. The other half says physicists are already fussing over these conceptual questions constantly and just end up reinventing philosophy badly.

So the real question on the table is whether a physics institute is any place for this kind of conceptual work in the first place, not just whether her specific project is good. I've been asked to draft the short decision memo that circulates before the vote. It needs a clear bottom line — approve, reject, or approve with conditions (say a shorter term, milestones, or co-supervision by a physicist) — and it has to give reasons the working physicists will actually take seriously, not the usual philosophy-defends-itself talking points. It also needs to steelman the anti-philosophy side rather than caricature it. Could you draft the memo?
