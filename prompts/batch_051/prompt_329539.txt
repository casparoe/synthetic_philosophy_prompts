so in my phil of mind / free will class we went over the sphex wasp example. the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the mouth of her burrow, leaves it there, goes inside to sort of check the burrow, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while shes inside checking, she comes back out, drags it back to the mouth, and goes inside to check again. she'll loop like that indefinitely, never just skipping the check and dragging the cricket in. the profs point was that a behavior can look super intelligent and goal-directed while actually being a rigid fixed sequence, and he called that sphexishness. then he went further and suggested that most or all of human behavior might be layers of the same sort of thing, that the feeling of flexibility could itself be a subroutine running on a cue, and free will is maybe just what it feels like from inside a very complicated sphex machine.

the thing is i have a really firm intuition that im not a sphex. when i catch myself doing something loop-like (rereading the same paragraph three times, checking the door i know i locked, rehearsing the same argument in my head), i can notice that im looping and step out. the wasp apparently cant represent the sequence as a sequence and intervene in it, but i can. so my intuition is that whatever sphexishness explains, it stops exactly at the point where behavior becomes flexible enough to get revised by the agent itself.

i keep going back and forth on which should give. maybe my stepping-out thought is just a higher order loop triggered by the right cue, and the intuition of genuine flexibility is the thing in need of debunking. or maybe the theory is right about subpersonal routines but overreaches when it claims the whole free-will distinction is an illusion. can you help me think through what would justify each of those moves, or whether there's a reading on which the theory and the intuition are actually compatible. and please no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just continuous prose like an email.
