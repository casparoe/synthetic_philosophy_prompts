ok so I keep going back and forth on whether anything that happens in a virtual world can be a real harm, and I want you to pressure-test the list of criteria I've been using against a case that splits them down the middle.

My working criteria for 'real harm': (1) someone actually experiences a setback — felt distress, grief, a loss they notice — not just a role-played or simulated one; (2) the person had a legitimate interest in what was lost, like a property claim, a promise-backed expectation, or the product of their own labor; (3) the loss is the doing of another agent who could have acted otherwise, not a server bug or a storm in the simulation; (4) the event is the kind of thing that calls for some response — an apology, compensation, a rule change, or at least acknowledgment; (5) the person didn't consent to exactly that kind of risk as part of a practice where that's the point.

Now the case. A group of players spend about eighteen months and (collectively) a few thousand real dollars building a city on a PvP server where the rules expressly allow sieges and the destruction of player-built structures, and the login screen says 'anything you build can be destroyed.' Another player, using only standard in-game tools and breaking no rule, waits until the builders are offline and levels the whole city over eight hours — including a memorial the group had built for a member who died. The builders are devastated in an ordinary, offline way: several quit the game, one says he felt physically sick. The company says nothing was lost because the terms of service say nothing on the server belongs to the players. The destroyer says it's just pixels and it was within the rules.

Go through my criteria and tell me whether this case qualifies as a real harm. Where a criterion says no, tell me whether that's the criterion doing real work or a bad criterion. And the question I actually care about: in general, can consent or knowing participation make a real harm not count, or does it just make a real harm permissible? If the list gets this case wrong, say which criteria need to change.
