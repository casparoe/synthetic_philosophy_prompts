Hey—I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I'd love comments on a paper idea I've been kicking around, specifically on my reading of Hempel's dilemma. It's still in note form, so feel free to push back on the structure as much as the claims.

The paper's thesis: the dilemma is usually posed as 'define "physical" via current physics and physicalism is false; define it via future/ideal physics and it's vacuous.' I want to say both horns only work if you accept a deferential semantics for "physical"—the idea that the term gets its content purely by pointing at physics, either today's or the finished version. I spend the first section arguing that once you drop that assumption, Hempel's dilemma stops looking like a refutation of physicalism and starts looking like an artifact of a bad theory of meaning. That part feels standard-ish to me.

Then the part I'm less sure about. I argue that every non-deferential replacement for "physical" ends up either (i) smuggling in a substantive, non-empirical constraint—usually that the fundamental is non-mental, non-teleological, non-normative, or something similar—or (ii) going via negativa and saying the physical is just 'not fundamentally mental,' which begs the question against the panpsychist and the idealist in a way physicalists pretend not to. My conclusion is that physicalism should stop trying to be a clean thesis about a single kind called "the physical" and should instead present itself as a defeasible research program: a bet, inductively backed by physics's track record, that the entities and principles future physics needs to explain the special sciences won't include irreducible intentional or phenomenal states, and that the causal order is closed under the kind of structure current physics is roughly tracking. Hempel's dilemma, on my reading, is not a problem to solve; it's a sign that physicalism is a wager about how explanation will go, not a piece of analytic metaphysics.

My worry is that its either too concessive (obviously dualists can't be ruled out a priori, so why belabor it?) or so deflationary that 'physicalism' stops deserving the name. I also suspect I'm running together the semantic question (what "physical" means) and the epistemic question (what justifies betting on physics), and I can't tell how much that matters.

I'd love comments on four things:

(1) Does the anti-deferential move in the first section actually do the work I claim, or am I just asserting it?
(2) Is the 'either (i) or (ii)' claim too quick—is there a third non-deferential option I'm missing?
(3) Is the deflationary conclusion a genuine position, or am I just giving up on physicalism while keeping the word?
(4) Is the semantic/epistemic conflation I mention fatal to the argument, or can the paper live with it?

Please be blunt and take a definite position. I'd rather hear 'here's what a physicalist should say, and here's why your argument does or doesn't survive contact with it' than a survey of options.
