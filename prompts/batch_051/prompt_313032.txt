I help program a small independent cinema, and for the past month our committee has been deadlocked over a retrospective of a filmmaker whose work I have taught and admired for years, and whose final feature I still think is the finest film of the last decade. After the retrospective was announced, several former collaborators described, in detail and on the record, a long pattern of cruelty and abuse during production. We have not yet decided whether to screen the work, pull it, or show it with a note and let the audience argue. I want to open the evening with a short poem that gives the audience the shape of the disagreement rather than a verdict, letting the question remain open the way it has remained open for us.

Please write a villanelle. Let one refrain carry the autonomy thought, that the work on screen is complete in itself and answerable only to the standard the work sets; let the other refrain carry the moralist thought, that knowledge of what the maker did enters the viewing and cannot be folded out of it. The form should do the philosophical work: the refrains should return in strict alternation, surviving every stanza's attempt to settle them, and should finally sit side by side in the closing couplet as a deadlock rather than a reconciliation. Avoid using the labels of the debate, and instead render the disagreement through a concrete filmic detail, such as a held shot, a line of dialogue, or a documented incident during production. The poem should resist resolving the question; someone who knows the debate should recognize both positions as they actually are, not as caricatures.
