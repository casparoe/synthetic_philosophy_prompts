I am a doctoral student working on the philosophical reception of nineteenth-century cosmology, and I have been trying to reconstruct the argument for the eternal recurrence that Nietzsche sketches in his unpublished notes. My reconstruction goes like this:

(1) The total quantity of force (or matter-energy) in the universe is finite.
(2) Time is infinite in both directions: there is no first moment and no final moment.
(3) Since the total force is finite, there are only finitely many possible total configurations of the universe — finitely many ways the whole of force can be arranged.
(4) Over infinite time, a system that has only finitely many possible states must pass through every one of those states, and indeed infinitely often.
(5) The present total configuration of the universe, including my present life in all its detail, is one of those possible states.
(6) Therefore the present configuration has already occurred infinitely many times in the past and will recur infinitely many times in the future.

I suspect the troublesome move is step 4, but I cannot say exactly what principle it silently assumes, nor whether any replacement principle is strong enough to deliver step 6 rather than some weaker claim about some state or other recurring. Is the argument valid? If not, identify exactly where it fails, state the additional premise that would be needed at that point, and then say whether the repaired argument really yields the recurrence of my present life or only some more modest conclusion.
