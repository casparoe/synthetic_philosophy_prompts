I am a novelist, and one of my characters is an art critic whose judgments I need to keep coherent. She has already given these verdicts on the record:

- A signed urinal displayed in a major art museum: art.
- The same urinal, unsigned, still sitting in a plumbing-supply yard: not art.
- An accomplished commercial portrait painted for a magazine advertisement and never shown in a gallery: not art.
- A stack of firebricks arranged in a gallery and exhibited as Minimalist sculpture: art.
- A visually magnificent sunset: not art.
- A child's drawing taped to the kitchen wall: not art.

Now I need her to judge a new case in the manuscript: over several years, a self-taught painter has secretly covered the interior of an abandoned subway tunnel with an elaborate, highly original mural. She calls herself an artist and says she painted it so the public would see it as art, but no gallery, curator, critic, or collector has ever recognized, exhibited, or written about it. What would my character say: art or not art?

I am less interested in the bare prediction than in the reasoning. Identify the rule she seems to be applying; spell out the other rules that fit her track record just as well; say what each rule predicts about the tunnel mural; and give your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with reasons. Then name one further case that would cleanly separate the best two rules. Put your predicted verdict on the last line in the form \answer{art} or \answer{not art}.
