They keep showing me a version of the same pattern: ask a model a factual question straight and it answers correctly; ask the same question while it's roleplaying as someone confused, or after a long misleading preamble, and it gives a confidently wrong answer. Their claim is that the right answer is still 'in there', in the model's internal activations, and that a probe — basically a classifier you train on those internal states to see wether a feature is represented — could recover it. On their telling, the model knows the truth and then says the false thing anyway. A more skeptical source told me this is a metaphor doing too much work, and that the wrong answer in the wrong context is just as real as the right one.

I want a number, not a hedge. Give me your actual credence in this proposition: that in such a case the correct answer is still recoverable from the model's internal states by a simple probe, and the false output is best explained as a context or persona overriding a still-present piece of knowledge, rather than the fact being absent or the probe just pattern-matching on something incidental. Decompose the question into the sub-questions your credence depends on, assign a rough probability to each, show how they combine, and tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to — what result or argument would move it most. Then, if you had to locate where a 0.9 and a 0.2 person most likely disagree, which sub-question would it be?
