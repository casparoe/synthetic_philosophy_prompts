I've been trying to work out whether "complicity without causation" is a real category or a confused one. People sometimes say that a person can be mixed up in another's wrongdoing without making any causal difference to it, and I have an argument that this must be a misdescription. But the argument keeps running into overdetermination cases, and I want to know whether it can be repaired or whether one of its steps quietly begs the question.

Here is the argument as I currently have it:

(1) If A is complicit in B's wrongdoing, then A participates in B's wrong. A bystander who merely knows about the wrong is not thereby complicit.
(2) Participation is a causal relation: to participate in a wrong is to take part in producing it, not merely to be present while it happens.
(3) To take part in producing an event, one's act must make a counterfactual difference: had the act not been performed, the event would not have occurred, or would have occurred later, in a materially different way, or only with materially greater difficulty.
(4) Every purported case of complicity without causation falls into one of two classes. Either it passes the test in (3), once the act is described at the right level of detail (the lookout makes the burglary safer, the accountant's signature makes the fraud easier), or it is a case of approval, acquiescence, encouragement, or profit-taking that makes no such difference (the cheering spectator, the person who later accepts the proceeds).
(5) Approval, acquiescence, encouragement, or profit-taking that makes no difference to the wrong is not participation in it: it is a response to, or extraction from, a wrong that would have proceeded unchanged without it.
(6) Therefore, no one participates in wrongdoing without causally contributing to it. What gets called "complicity without causation" is either causation after all, or it is not complicity at all.

The case that most troubles me is the extra member of a firing squad when nine shooters would already ensure the death, or the redundant lookout on an empty street. The argument seems to force me to say they are not participants, which feels wrong; but maybe their contribution is being described at the wrong level, or maybe I should bite the bullet. That is where I want the most careful work.
