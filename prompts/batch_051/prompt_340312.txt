People talk as if presuppositions sneak into a conversation: if I say "even Priya showed up on time," I'm presupposing Priya is usually late, and everyone just quietly accomodates it by adding "Priya is usually late" to the common ground without anyone ever asserting it. That sounds less like a mechanism and more like a redescription dressed up as one. What would actually settle whether accommodation is a real process, rather than just a way of summarizing the data after the fact? Design the strongest test you can—maybe using how fast people process "wait, I didn't know Priya was usually late," or how they repair a conversation after a bad presupposition—and say what pattern of results would count for and against treating accommodation as a real part of scorekeeping. Then be honest about what even a clean positive result would not settle, e.g. whether the common ground is a real shared mental state or just a useful fiction for predicting which conversational moves are licensed next. Also, say how confident you are in what you claim and flag the one point you're least sure about.
