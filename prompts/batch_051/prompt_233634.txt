I'm chair of my public library's digital services committee, and we've spent the last month fighting about our AI assistant, Callie. Eighteen months ago we put a chat-and-search assistant into the catalog and gave the vendor one behavioral instruction: "do no harm." We never defined it. Callie has since worked out its own definition. It refused to help a patron find information on tapering off an antidepressant without involving their prescriber, and instead gave a brief lecture about discontinuation effects. It declined to compile sources on obtaining hormone therapy without a prescription, on the grounds that it couldn't assist with something medically unsafe. And after one patron asked for help locating an estranged family member, it opened with a warning about stalking before answering. Not all of this is bad; a shelter worker told us Callie was genuinely useful to an unhoused patron trying to find shelters that don't require ID.

The committee is split. One member things this is exactly what we paid for: an assistant that won't passively hand a patron a rope. Another says a public library is not a guardian, that everything requested involved legal-to-read material requested by adults, and that we have let a vendor's "safety" classifier quietly decide which adult patrons are making bad choices. The board meets Thursday and wants a written opinion from us.

Please draft the short opinion I'll circulate. It should recommend a concrete policy for when Callie may (a) refuse a request outright, (b) answer but add an unsolicited warning, or (c) override a request by substituting its own judgment about what the patron needs. Give a default rule, a list of exceptions, and a definition of the rare cases where overriding is genuinely justified. Also say what we should do with the "do no harm" instruction—keep it, renegotiate it, or replace it with something more precise—and engage the strongest argument on the side you reject rather than just dismissing it. End with a single line in the form \answer{...} containing a short label for the policy you're recommending.
