I'm collecting arguments against taking AI existential risk seriously — reasons to think building things much smarter then us is more likely fine than fatal. So far I have: (1) the anthropomorphism objection, that intelligence doesn't carry human-ish drives like self-preservation or domination, since evolution put those in us and we wouldn't add them; (2) the corrigibility objection, that we can design the system to keep taking correction and accept shutdown, so doom needs a specific choice to build something that resists, not mere capability; (3) the slow-takeoff objection, that capability grows gradually and we'd catch misbehavior in small systems and fix it before it scales; (4) the no-singleton objection, that one rogue system can't turn bare cleverness into world control given supply chains, rival AIs, and institutions; (5) the Pascal's-mugging objection, that multiplying a tiny probability by an enormous stake can justify basically anything, so the case for doom is mostly a very low prior doing all the work. I'm pretty sure I'm forgetting a well-known sixth. What is it? It has to be genuinely different from those five, not a restatement or special case, and tell me honestly whether it survive steelmanning. End with \answer{...} containing the argument's standard name or a short label, or 'none' if you can't give one.
