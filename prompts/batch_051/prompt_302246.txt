I have this habit where I basically don't try to remember anything I know I can pull up on my phone—appointments, homework, sometimes even my own positions on issues, i'll just search my notes. My friend says I'm making myself dumber and treating a crutch like a brain. But I read this paper for class, Andy Clark and David Chalmers' "The Extended Mind," and it says: "If, as we confront some task, a part of the world functions as a process which, were it done in the head, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is (so we claim) part of the cognitive process." That kind of gives me permission. But maybe I'm just using a philosophy paper to justify laziness. Is my phone-notes policy actually defensible as a way of managing my mind, or is it a bias dressed up as a theory? How would I tell the difference from the inside?
