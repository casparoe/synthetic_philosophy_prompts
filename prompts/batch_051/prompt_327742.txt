I work on a mood-tracking app; users rate how pleasant each moment is on a single slider and we average the numbers. I keep worrying that 'pleasant' means something different for a hot shower, a hard crossword, and chatting with a friend, so we're averaging apples and oranges. A philosopher friend called this the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: internalists think pleasures share one felt quality of pleasantness, while externalists think they're unified only by how we relate to them or what they do, not by any common feel. I want a reusable proceedure for deciding when to keep the single slider and when to split it into several dimensions, and for interpreting the data we already have. It has to be usable by a product team with no philosophy training, and it's something we'd run before each big feature launch because the ratings drive what we build. Work through at least two concrete cases, including one where the procedure tells us to kill the single slider even though the dashboard number is convenient, and flag the steps that are actually judgment calls.
