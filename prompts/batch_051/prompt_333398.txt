My thesis is that diseases, climate shifts and volcanoes should be treated as historical *actors* — not just "background conditions" — but that this doesn't commit me to environmental determinism. I want to carve out a seperate middle position, where a volcano can be an actor in the story without having intentions and without deciding the ending. My rough argument is: (1) there's a useful category between "person with a plan" and "stuff that just sits there", namely "something that intervenes and reorders what's possible"; (2) the Black Death, the Little Ice Age and the Tambora eruption did exactly that — they didn't merely set the scene, they barged in and closed some options down; (3) this actually saves human agency, because it lets me say the outcomes were shaped by how people responded to those interventions, rather than by germs or climate steamrollering everything.
