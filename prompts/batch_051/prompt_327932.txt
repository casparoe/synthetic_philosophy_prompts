I am a high school student, and in a psychology unit on perception we were shown the color phi phenomenon. A red dot flashes briefly on the left side of a screen, and then, after a short blank interval, a green dot flashes briefly on the right side. Yet observers report seeing a single dot glide across the screen and change from red to green around the midpoint of its path. My teacher explained this by saying that consciousness runs on a delay: the reported color change occurs before the green dot has physically appeared, so the brain must be holding the present moment in a buffer until it has gathered the later information, and only then releases an edited and coherent version of the scene.

What unsettles me is how far this goes. If the present is genuinely held back until the brain can insert information from the future, then part of what I experience as now is actually a corrected record of events that had not yet happened at the physical moment in question. Is there a measurable length for this delay, and does it grow when the two flashes are separated by longer intervals? More importantly, is there a principled way to tell whether the brain is editing the past, by changing what one was conscious of after the fact, rather than simply waiting to produce the conscious experience until all the relevant input is available, or does the distinction itself rest on a confusion?
