Write a dialogue between two epistemologists on the train back from a conference, arguing about higher-order evidence. One of them, Marta, defends the view that epistemic akrasia is never rational: an agent cannot rationally be highly confident that P while also being highly confident that this very attitude is not the one her evidence supports. The other, Dev, thinks level-splitting is fine sometimes — first-order evaluation (does the scan say cancer?) and higher-order evaluation (how trustworthy is my scan-reading right now?) are answers to different questions, and it can be rational to keep first-order confidence in P even while you think your high confidence is probably the product of a process you have reason to mistrust.

Have them work through a concrete case together: a radiologist reads a scan and concludes P at roughly .9, then learns that she was accidentally given a drug known to make seven of ten subjects confidently misread exactly this kind of image, while leaving the other three unaffected. She can't find any specific error on re-reading. Marta wants to say the radiologist has to drop her confidence significantly, and if she doesn't — and still thinks her evidence makes .9 the wrong credence — shes in an incoherent state. Dev wants to say the radiologist can rationally stay at .9 while granting that she probably shouldn't be that confident, because her grounds for P are still there and the higher-order fact doesn't cancel them.

Make the dialogue a real back and forth: each should respond to the other's strongest point, not a strawman. At some point they need to go at each other over whether the state of being confident P while highly confident that her evidence doesn't support P is incoherent in the way Moore's paradox is, whether higher-order evidence bears on the proposition itself or only on the agent's relation to it, and whether a blanket ban on epistemic akrasia would force someone with an unlocatable error to stop using her own mind. End with each one saying what fact or case would most push them to switch sides.
