I teach philosophy of mind, and for the past month my research group has been stuck on one question: do symbols actually push the substrate around, or does all the real pushing happen one level down, with the symbol story just our way of keeping score? We keep trying to pin the question down with two toy cases, and I want a diagnosis rather than another survey of the positions.

Here are the toys. By a careenium I mean a huge machine whose parts are mindless physical bits—balls, levers, latches—that collide and trade momentum in boring ways. But the machine is laid out cleverly enough that whole populations of bits move in patterns you can read as symbols, rules, and sequences: at the high level the careenium can be described as trying something, remembering a failed attempt, even correcting a mistake. You can predict what it does by tracing the micro-collisions or by reading the symbols. By simmballs I mean the same idea in software: little simulated balls on a lattice whose local bounce-and-stick rules implement a simple logic task, like deciding whether a patch is more black than white. The substrate-first camp says the symbol level in both cases is useful compression and nothing more: every "symbol push" can be replaced without remainder by ball-level pushes, so symbol-causes are, at best, epiphenominal bookkeeping. The pattern-realist camp says the symbol level passes every difference-making test there is—change the symbol and downstream motion changes; symbol-level generalizations support interventions and counterfactuals more robustly than the micro story does; and if you refuse to call those causes, you either collapse into the view that nothing above microphysics ever causes anything, or you're making a special case against minds. Everyone agrees on all the micro facts and the high-level regularities. We've run the sims until we're bored, and the two sides mostly sit there talking passed each other.

I have a hypothesis about the impasse: maybe it isn't about either toy but about what people demand from a cause. Some of us require something like a transferred mark or oomph; some require invariance under intervention; some require only explanatory indispensability. I also suspect the real stake is free will—people worry that if symbols don't push, reasons don't either, so the debate is secretly about whether deliberation is epiphenomenal. So act as the philosopher of causation we'd bring in to diagnose the structure of this mess, not to take a side on free will. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive metaphysical, a disguised empirical one, or a fight about which cases count as data? What would settle or dissolve it—a theorem, a conceptual distinction, a new intervention, a reframed question—or should we stop expecting progress? Give me the diagnosis with reasons, not a history of the debate, and steelman each side before you rule. If the honest answer is that the question is ill-posed, tell me exactly which questions got merged.

Format: one sentence up front stating what kind of disagreement this really is, then the defense. Keep it under 800 words.
