I need help with a definitional thing I keep going back and forth on. I was taught that you can say someone *owns an idea* when all five of these are true:

1. The idea started with them, not copied or restated from someone else.
2. They made it concrete — wrote it down, drew it, recorded it, built it — so it isn't just a thought.
3. It's specific enough that another person could follow it and produce the thing without having to make big new creative decisions.
4. Their own labor and choices are what gave the idea its particular shape.
5. Owning it shows up in practical powers: the owner can stop others from using it, can sell or license it, and gets credited as the person who came up with it.

Now here's the case. A street photographer, Dana, spent years building a very recognizable look: she shoots mannequins reflected in puddles at dawn, same film stock, same slight under-exposure, same wide-angle framing so the mannequins look like they're wading. She doesn't keep it secret. She writes a self-published zine, *The Mannequin Puddle Protocol*, thirty or forty pages, laying out exactly where to stand, how to wait for the light, how set the camera, how to pick the mannequin, and how to grade the colors afterwards. Another photographer, Eli, buys the zine, follows it to the letter, and lands a gallery show that critics describe as straight out of Dana's world. Dana says he stole the *idea* — not her photos, not the text of the zine, but the whole approach she worked out and wrote down.

By my five criteria, does Dana own the idea of the mannequin-puddle protocol? Go through them one at a time. And I also want to know where you think the ownership runs out: does it attach to the zine, to the technique, or to the resulting photographs, or to none of these? I know courts often say you can't own a style or a method, only a specific expression of it — so if my criteria say she owns the whole thing, does that mean my criterea need fixing, or is this just a case where the naive criteria and the law pull apart for a reason?
