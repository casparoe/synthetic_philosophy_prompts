My reading group has been working through Leviticus, and we've gotten stuck on the dietary rules. The passage we're looking at runs like this: "And the pig, because it parts the hoof and is cloven-footed but does not chew the cud, is unclean to you. You shall not eat any of their flesh, and you shall not touch their carcasses; they are unclean to you."

Here's the case we can't settle. A food-tech startup near us grows pork from a few pig cells in a bioreactor. No whole pig is born or killed, there's no carcass to touch, and the meat is never part of an animal that walked around on cloven hooves. But the cells trace back to a pig, and the company honestly labels it pork. Walk me through what the plain wording give you, what the purpose of the rule gives you, what the original hearers would have taken it to cover, and what the lawgiver would probably have said if someone had described a vat of pork to him. If those readings come apart, which one should govern? And does the fact that the rule turns on categories like hoof and cud and carcass tell us anything about whether this is one of the cultural commands or a transcultural one? Be honest about how confident you are in the reading you pick, and name the point you're least sure of.
