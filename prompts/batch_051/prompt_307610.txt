I am a novelist at work on a psychological novel whose central figure is a calculating, high-functioning psychopath, and I would like to use this conversation to pressure-test the idea that my character can genuinely lack moral knowledge even though he speaks and reasons about wrongness with complete fluency. The view I am trying to hold as I write is that he does not know, in any thick epistemic sense, that what he does is wrong; he merely handles the language of wrongness the way a traveller handles a map of a country to which he feels no allegiance.

Please play an unnamed philosopher who is committed to denying this: someone who holds that a person like my character — able to identify an act as wrong, to explain that wrongness in the ordinary moral idiom, and to predict with complete reliability that others will blame him — does know that the act is wrong, and that whatever is missing in him is a matter of affect, empathy, or motivation rather than knowledge. Give the position all the resources it actually has: distinguish propositional knowledge from caring, from understanding, and from being moved; press me on whether I am smuggling a requirement of feeling into an epistemic concept; and resist the conclusion that because his knowledge is cold, it is not knowledge. You may concede small points and refine your view, but do not surrender the main claim. Stay in character and address me directly; if you need a fact about my character, ask me in character; keep each reply to roughly a paragraph; and do not break role until I say "stop."

My opening move: suppose my character can say, of the act he is about to commit, that it is wrong, can lay out exactly the considerations that would move an ordinary person, and never fails to predict that his victim will think it wrong — yet he registers the wrongness the way he registers a zoning ordinance, as an external constraint to be navigated rather than a reason that weighs on him. If you say he knows the act is wrong, then it looks as though moral knowledge has collapsed into the capacity to speak and predict, and we would have to grant that a sufficiently articulate machine knows that stealing is wrong. What epistemic property does his talk lack? And if you answer that it lacks nothing — that he simply does not care — why is that a deficit in knowing rather than a deficit in caring?
