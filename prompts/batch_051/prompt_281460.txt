I was in a seminar on consciousness, and someone shut down an appeal to first-person reports with the slogan "phenomenal consciousness is epiphenomenal—it does nothing and causes nothing, so our verbal reports about experience carry no evidential weight." I suspect that little sentence is smuggling in several different claims, and that only some of them would actually undermine taking conscious reports seriously. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan might be expressing—for example, along dimensions like what the phenomenal state is said to fail to cause (physical events only, all events, the physical processes that produce the report), whether the report can still be systematically correlated with the phenomenal state through a common physical cause, and whether the slogan is making a causal claim or a claim about explanatory redundancy. For each reading, please say how plausible it is, whether it actually entails that introspective reports are unreliable, and which reading the person in the seminar needed. I want the readings laid out as a menu with verdicts, not a single interpretation.
