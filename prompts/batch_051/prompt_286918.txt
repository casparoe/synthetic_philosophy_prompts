I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, so I might be reinventing a wheel here. A disagreement at my startup made me notice two gut reactions I have that feel like they can't both be right. My cofounder argued that if we flattened salaries by cutting the top earners' pay and not raising anyone else's, the company would still be "more equal," and that's a genuine point in its favor—maybe not decisive, but real. I think that's crazy: if you make someone worse off and no one better off, I don't see how the outcome is better in even one respect. Equality for its own sake seems like a bug.

But in nearby cases I have the opposite reaction. If we cut the highest-paid engineer's pay by $12k and give $10k of it to the lowest-paid engineer (the missing $2k disappears in taxes and administrative mess), I think that change could easily be worth it, even though total money is down. And I'd rather give the same $10k raise to the worst-paid engineer than to the best-paid one, even in a weird case where I knew the best-paid one would get slightly more enjoyment out of it. So I seem to believe two things at once:

(1) A benefit to someone worse off counts extra, maybe enough to justify sacrificing some total benefit.
(2) Making things more equal without making anyone better off is never good, not even a little, not even as one consideration.

Are (1) and (2) actually compatible, or is my "worse off counts extra" principle just equality wearing a disguise? Try to break it. Give me the nastiest case you can where a benefit to the worse off seems to be doing no real work except closing a gap, or where (1) would still call something good when nobody is actually helped. Then tell me wether I should revise (1), give up (2), or accept something weird.
