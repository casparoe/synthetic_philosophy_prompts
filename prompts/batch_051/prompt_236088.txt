I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on the morality of deception, and before assigning the exchange over whether lying is morally worse than merely misleading, I want a working list of what an adequate account of the distinction would have to explain. Please write the desiderata: the requirements that any defensible theory of the moral significance of the lying/misleading distinction must meet, regardless of which side it ultimately takes.

Assume the theory is meant to guide first-person moral assessment and third-person judgment of particular cases, not merely to describe folk usage or track legal doctrine. For each desideratum, state it as an explicit requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and mark it as negotiable or non-negotiable. The cases you use must include at least the following, though you may add others: the murderer at the door in two versions, one in which the speaker says "He is not here" and one in which the speaker says something true intended to mislead, such as "I last saw him an hour ago at the station"; the letter of recommendation composed entirely of true statements that omit the one decisive disqualifying fact; the bald-faced lie, in which everyone knows the statement is false and nobody is actually deceived; misleading testimony under oath deliberately crafted to escape a perjury charge; and a half-truth offered to an intimate partner. Phrase each desideratum as a constraint on any theory, not as the property of one position.

Then answer two structural questions. First, identify any pair or subset of the desiderata that cannot plausibly be jointly satisfied; where you find a conflict, say whether it exposes a deep tension in the ordinary idea or instead shows that one of the requirements is a sectarian demand dressed up as a neutral constraint. Second, say which single desideratum does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and explain why.

Finally, briefly score three positions against your list: the view that lying is intrinsically worse, the view that there is no moral difference and that deception is what matters, and the view that misleading is sometimes the graver wrong. Note where each does best and worst, but keep this section short; the criteria are the deliverable, not the verdicts.

Do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and do not quote sources from memory; the handout is meant to stand on its own conceptual apparatus, with any examples paraphrased. Begin directly with the list of desiderata itself, with no preface or commentary, and then give the reasoning behind each item and your structural analysis.
