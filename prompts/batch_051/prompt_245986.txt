I have been rereading the old debates about whether memes are genuine replicators or whether cultural transmission is too low-fidelity for Darwinian explanations to get any traction, and I have become convinced that both sides are looking at the wrong level of description. I want to try out a proposal that I have been calling the core-cladding model. The claim is that we should not ask whether a cultural token replicates; we should ask whether a cultural variant possesses a replication core, meaning a small set of features such that variation in those features changes the probability that a descendant token will be produced in the next round of transmission, while variation in everything else is noise with respect to selection. A joke's core would be the setup-then-violated-expectation structure: changing the names or the idiom does not matter, but changing the relation between setup and punchline kills it. A cooking technique's core would be the sequence of operations that causally determines the outcome, with ingredients and equipment as cladding. On this account, saying that a meme replicates is just the claim that a variant has a core whose transmission, rather than any transmission of the cladding, explains its persistence. That would let us concede that surface copying is sloppy while still saying that something definite gets copied.

But I can see how the model might be doing less work than I want it to. It may simply be a rebranding of Sperber's cultural attractors, the idea that transmission is statistically pulled toward a few stable forms of a representation, or of the biological replicator/interactor distinction, or of the genotype/phenotype split applied to culture; if so, I would like to know exactly what is redundant. I also worry the criterion is too easy to satisfy. A fire has something like a replication core — heat ignites neighboring fuel, which produces more heat — and I do not want to say that fire is a meme. I suspect the missing condition is that the core must be reconstructed by a receiving mind from a signal rather than propagated through direct physical causation, but I am not sure whether that condition is principled or just a patch. Give me the strongest version of this proposal, the nearest existing ideas it reduces to, the clearest case where it stops being distinctive or explanatory, and what, if anything, it would license us to predict about which cultural variants persist and which ones fade.
