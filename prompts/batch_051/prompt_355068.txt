I'm a high school student, and I've been working through the demandingness objection to consequentialism. The objection, as I understand it, goes like this: consequentialism says we must always bring about the best overall state of affairs, so a person who is not donating most of her income, or who is not taking the career that would let her donate the most, or who spends money on a birthday dinner rather than on malaria nets, is doing something wrong. Critics find that unacceptable.

What confuses me is this. Aren't they really just arguing for a different line — one that lets the agent's own life count for more than the equal-weight calculation would allow? Where does that leave the search for the correct level of demandingness?
