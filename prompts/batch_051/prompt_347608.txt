i think the answer is yes. japanese and spanish show you can basically drop pronouns whenever you want, because the verb ending or the context already tells you who is doing the action, so pronouns are just an optional extra, not something a language really needs. what actually matters are indexicals, which are words like 'i', 'now', and 'here' whose meaning shifts depending on who says them and when. perry's shopper example proves these are special: someone can follow a sugar trail through a supermarket, believe in a detached way that the shopper with the torn sack is making a mess, and keep walking, but the moment he realizes that he himself is that shopper, he stops and fixes it. so the i-thought does something a plain description of the person can't do. since every language needs to say who is speaking, who is being spoken to, and when and where things happen, a language without pronouns would still need indexicals to do that job. therefore a language could lack pronouns, but only if it replaces its pronouns with indexicals, which means every language has to have at least a pronoun-like word for the speaker, the addressee, the time, and the place.
