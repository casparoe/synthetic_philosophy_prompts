I chair the Behavioral Interventions Review Committee of a regional health authority. Any change to a patient-facing or staff-facing service that aims to steer behavior through defaults, ordering, framing, prompts, or similar design features must come to us before it goes live. Over the past four years we have issued the following four decisions, and they will be treated as precedents at our next meeting.

1. Generic substitution default. Approved 6–1. The mail-order pharmacy proposed that eligible repeat prescriptions default to the clinically equivalent generic, with the substitution stated in plain language on the confirmation page and a single checkbox to request the branded drug instead.

2. Staff canteen reordering. Approved 5–2. The canteen moved water and fruit to eye level and placed sugar-sweetened drinks and confectionery on the least accessible shelves. No sign announced the change and no rationale was posted.

3. Active-choice health plan enrollment. Approved 7–0. New employees were required to select either the standard or the high-deductible health plan; neither box was pre-ticked, and each option carried a one-line description of its main trade-off.

4. Social-comparison screening reminder. Rejected 6–1. The proposal would have added to overdue screening letters a statement that most patients in the recipient's postcode had already booked the screening and that the recipient's practice had one of the highest no-show rates in the area. The majority said the letter crossed from informing patients into pressuring them.

Next month we decide on a physiotherapy rebooking default. The physiotherapy service proposes that when a patient misses an appointment without cancelling, the system automatically rebook them into the next available slot for the same clinic and send a text message with a one-click cancellation link. The automatic rebooking is not mentioned in the reminder messages leading up to the missed appointment; it is disclosed only in the initial referral pack and in a paragraph on the service's website. The service projects that this will reduce missed appointments by roughly a fifth, and argues that cancelling with one click is easier than attending would be. Some committee members see the proposal as a straightforward extension of the generic substitution default; others see it as a hidden default whose exit is only nominal.

Draft me a decision memo. It should state whether the committee should approve, reject, or approve the proposal with conditions if it is to remain consistent with its own precedents. Identify the closest analog or analogs among the four decisions. Where the precedents genuinely underdetermine the decision, say what principle the committee would be adopting under each live option, and state which future case would do most to separate those principles.
