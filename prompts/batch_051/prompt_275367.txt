Retired physical chemist here, recently gone down the philosophy-of-social-choice rabbit hole. A philosopher friend keeps telling me Gibbard–Satterthwaite rules out any strategy-proof, non-dictatorial way of electing one winner from three or more candidates, but I think I've found a loophole and I can't spot the mistake. My argument: (1) Gibbard–Satterthwaite says any strategy-proof voting rule with at least three candidates is dictatorial. (2) Here's a rule: everybody submits a strict ranking, then one ballot is drawn uniformly at random, and that ballot's top candidate wins. (3) This rule is strategy-proof: if your ballot is drawn, you do best by ranking your true favorite first; if its not drawn, your ballot doesn't matter. (4) The rule isn't dictatorial, because no single voter's favorite is always the winner. (5) So there's a non-dictatorial strategy-proof rule, contradicting the theorem. Either step (1) is misstating the theorem or one of the later steps is doing something sneaky. Which step fails, if any, and what's the correct statement of the theorem that makes the contradiction go away?
