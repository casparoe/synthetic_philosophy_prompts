I've been trying to get clear on how an ensemble of climate models should actually constrain a decision, and a parallel with machine learning keeps nagging at me. The standard practice is to run several global climate models under a common forcing scenario and summarize the output with the ensemble mean and the across-model spread, often letting that spread function as a measure of uncertainty. Structurally, this looks like bootstrap aggregating, or bagging: gather many imperfect predictors, average them, and report the dispersion around the average. In machine learning, bagging earns its keep only under conditions that the climate case seems to violate head-on. Bagging reduces variance when the models are noisy, roughly unbiased, and at least partly decorrelated; shared biases are not averaged away, and the empirical spread understates the genuine predictive uncertainty. A climate model ensemble, by contrast, is built from models that share code, parameterizations, and tuning targets, so the members are far from independent draws around a common truth, and the spread reflects model-development choices as much as it reflects the range of physically defensible futures.

What I cannot decide is whether this is a deep identity of structure or a loose analogy riding mostly on the shared word ensemble. If the parallel is deep, then the standard machine-learning caution transfers over: the ensemble spread is not a probability distribution over future states, and treating the multi-model mean as a best estimate can manufacture false precision. If the parallel is superficial, that is presumably because climate models are structural simulators rather than fitted predictors, and the ensemble's real job is to explore the space of physical assumptions and identify conclusions that are robust across that space, not to reduce predictive variance. I want the mapping checked carefully: what exactly would the error structure of a climate model ensemble have to be for the bagging results to carry over, and what does the analogy predict about climate ensembles that turns out false? More concretely, I want to be able to say when agreement across many model simulations should raise my confidence in an outcome, and when it is merely the correlated output of one lineage of assumptions.
