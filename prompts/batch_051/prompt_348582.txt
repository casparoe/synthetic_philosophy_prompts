I work in network analysis and have a comfortable grasp of directed graphs: nodes, edges, terminal nodes, cycles, infinite paths, reachability, acyclicity. I keep encountering the epistemological regress problem and its three standard responses — foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism — but the prose treatments blur together for me. Restate the three positions as claims about a directed graph in which beliefs are nodes and a directed edge from A to B means that A serves as a reason or justification for B. For each position, specify what the graph must look like: whether it may have terminal nodes, whether cycles are permitted, whether infinite paths are required, and what structural constraints the position places on the justification relation. Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the epistemological debate translate cleanly into these graph-theoretic terms? Which parts did you have to distort to fit, and which have no expression at all — for example, the distinction between a chain's merely existing and a subject's being able to traverse it, or between a terminal node's being self-justifying and its merely being a point where inquiry stops? Finally, tell me whether the residue points to a limitation of the graph-theoretic vocabulary or to something less than fully precise in the philosophical positions themselves.
