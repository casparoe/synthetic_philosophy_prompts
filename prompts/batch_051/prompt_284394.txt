I am a PhD student in the philosophy of cognitive science, working on the question of whether the explanatory posits of mechanistic interpretability deserve to be called representations. In our reading group, the same dispute keeps reappearing. On one side, people say that once a researcher has found a direction in activation space, an attention head, a circuit, or a learned feature that tracks a property of the input—a syntactic dependency, a curve detector, a concept-like cluster—and has shown that downstream computation uses it, the researcher has found a representation, full stop. On the other side, people regard that as loose talk: all that has been shown, they say, is that some component carries information about a property and causally mediates behavior, and calling this representation either adds nothing or imports semantic and intentional notions that have not been earned. I would like an essay that works through this controversy and reaches a definite conclusion rather than surveying it.

To keep the essay from staying at the level of slogans, adopt—or explicitly revise—a working criterion: a neural component (neuron, attention head, direction, layer, or circuit) represents X to the extent that (i) its activations carry recoverable information about X, (ii) downstream computation is sensitive to that information in a way that explains task-relevant behavior, and (iii) the framework can distinguish misrepresenting X from merely failing to encode X. Please explain how this criterion handles, or fails to handle, the hard cases: polysemanticity and superposition (many features entangled in a single direction, or a single feature distributed across many directions), features that are training artifacts rather than robust structure, and content attributed at different levels of organization (single neuron versus circuit versus whole network).

The essay should weigh the main realist, instrumentalist, and deflationary positions with enough charity that partisans of each would recognize their own view, but it must not end non-committally. Defend a verdict, whether that is eliminativism, instrumentalism, realism, or a conditional position such as realism about circuits but not about individual neurons. After the essay, append a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: "position" (one to two sentences stating the verdict); "operative_criterion" (two to three sentences giving the representation criterion used or proposed); "key_arguments" (an array of two to four one-sentence arguments for the position); "strongest_objection_and_reply" (an object with fields "objection" and "reply", one sentence each); and "scope_and_defeasibility" (two to three sentences on which systems or components the conclusion covers and what evidence would overturn it).
