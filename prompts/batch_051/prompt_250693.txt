My friend Dave (ex-actuary) has been ruling on coincidence stories for as long as I've known him. I've kept notes on his calls because his instincts are good but I can't always state the principle behind them.

Here are the calls I have:

- A neighbor dreamed her brother was in danger on the night he died in a car crash. Dave wasn't moved: she'd had hundreds of dreams about him over the years, and the one that happens to match in retrospect is the only one people remember.
- A horoscope told another neighbor she'd have a disagreement with someone close that week, and she did. Dave laughed it off: a prediction that vague has a high hit rate under almost any story, so a hit doesn't shift much.
- A pre-registered card-guessing study with proper controls reported 1,200 correct calls out of 4,000, where chance gives 800. Dave said this one was worth taking seriously—either a real effect or a subtle setup problem, but not simple chance.
- An old colleague mined a large registry and found that left-handed people were overrepresented among the top editors in his field. Dave said he'd only be impressed if the colleague had predicted that exact pattern before looking; after the fact, with so many variables to comb through, it gets no weight.

The case that's now bugging Dave came up last month. A woman in town—no history of predictions, no blog, nothing—wrote down a 16-digit number at 8 a.m., showed it to her son, and took a timestamped photo, saying this would be the serial number on the next banknote she received in change. Around noon she paid for groceries in cash, and the change contained a banknote with exactly that serial number. The son and the timestamp seem to rule out after-the-fact editing, and as far as anyone can tell she'd never done anything like it before. Dave keeps going back and forth on it.

So I'd like to work out whether Dave's past calls commit him to a particular verdict on the banknote story, or whether his track record underdetermines it. If it underdetermines it, spell out the candidate principles and what each says about this case, and say which of his old calls is the closest analog. Then give your best prediction of what Dave should say to stay consistent with himself, and name one further case that would cleanly seperate the principles he's torn between.
