Here's the thing I keep going back and forth on in the philosophy of emotion. The perceptual theory says fear literally perceives danger the way vision perceives shape; the judgment theory says fear just is an evaluative judgment, maybe fast and non-conscious; and the Jamesian feeling theory says fear is nothing over and above the felt bodily changes. I want to know how much of this dispute is empirically settleable rather than a fight about vocabulary. Design the best experiment you can that would pull apart at least two of these three accounts — emotion as perception of value, as evaluative judgment, or as felt body-state. Be concrete about the manipulation and the measure, say wich pattern of results favours each account, and then be honest about what even a clean result would leave unresolved (e.g., whether 'perceives' is a mechanism or just a flattering metaphor). Also, I already know the standard recalcitrant-emotion move — fear of flying while judging it safe — so try to go beyond that.
