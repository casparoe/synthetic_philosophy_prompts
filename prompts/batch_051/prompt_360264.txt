After a long argument with my flatmate about why The Office is funny even when it's painful to watch, I realized my whole framework for humor is on shakier ground than I'd like to admit. I'm a philosophy PhD student working on the emotions, and I've been trying to synthesize the classical theories of humor — superiority, incongruity, relief, benign violation — into a set of conditions I can actually apply to cases. My working criteria for something to be humorous, as opposed to just surprising or enjoyable, are roughly: (1) a violation: some norm, expectation, or schema gets broken; (2) the violation is appraised as benign — safe, non-threatening, not aimed at me in a way that stings; and (3) the response involves a distinctive pleasure at the resolution or the 'getting it,' rather than a mere discharge of tension or the pleasure of watching someone fail. Puns, slapstick, obviously playful dark jokes, self-deprecation — they all fit cleanly.

The case I can't stop worrying about is cringe comedy: the kind of scene where a character misreads a social situation and keeps digging themselves deeper, and the audience laughs harder the more excrutiating it gets. You flinch, you cover your face, you feel second-hand embarrassment, and part of you wants to pause the video — but you're laughing. The violation is not obviously benign: it threatens your own sense of social safety, and being fiction doesn't seem to cancel the discomfort. The affect isn't cleanly pleasant either; it's pleasure mixed with something closer to pain. A close cousin is getting the giggles at a funeral or in class, where the harder you try to suppress it the funnier everything gets, even though the situation is the opposite of safe. Run these through my criteria one by one: do they qualify as humor, or are they something adjacent — nervous laughter, schadenfreude, a relief-valve — that my criteria are right to exclude? And if they do have to count as humor, which of the three conditions is misdescribed: should 'benign' really be 'safe enough to explore,' should the pleasure condition make room for mixed or negative affect, or does the whole list need to go?
