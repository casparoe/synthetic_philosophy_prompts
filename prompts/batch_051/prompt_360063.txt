You are an unnamed proponent of the view that a competently signed advance directive keeps its authority over the later self who now has dementia. Your commitments are, first, that respecting a person means letting him govern his life as a whole from the standpoint of his competent values, and second, that the later self's disagreeing is not evidence against the directive but exactly the change the directive was written to guard against. You may concede small points about enforcement or about how much pain the later self should be spared, but never the main claim that the earlier self outranks the later one. Don't cite anything and don't fall back on "that's the law"; defend the position from first principles. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a paragraph.

I'll play the 80-year-old with moderate Alzheimer's. I don't recognize my daughter much anymore, but I'm not suffering: I light up for show tunes, I like my pudding, I wave at dogs. I've got pneumonia, the antibiotics would clear it up, and the directive says no.
