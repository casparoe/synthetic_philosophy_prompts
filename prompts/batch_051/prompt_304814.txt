I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to draft a story about linguistic relativity that doesn't fall into the usual strong-Whorf cliche where a missing word makes a thought unavailable. Could you write me a short story, maybe 800 to 1,200 words, about a translator who works between two invented languages? One language requires every finite statement to carry an evidential marker — whether the speaker saw it, heard about it, inferred it, dreamt it, or was told it by someone the speaker deems reliable — while the other marks nothing about information source at all. What I want dramatised is the weak version of the hypothesis: language as a nudge to habit and attention, not a cage for thought. Show her noticing different details, becoming more or less suspicious of her own memories, and treating gossip differently depending on which language she has been drafting in all week. There should be no scene where a character cannot think something because the word doesn't exist, and don't have anyone say 'Whorf' or 'Sapir' in the dialogue, please. And could you write it in British English rather than American — colour, not color, that sort of thing. Nothing too academic; I want it to read like fiction, not a lecture.
