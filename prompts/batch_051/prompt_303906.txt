I have been mediating a long-running dispute between two colleagues about why promises bind. The first, the practice theorist, holds that promising is binding because it is participation in a beneficial social convention: the institution of promising works only because certain words count as committing, and the wrong of breaking a promise is fundamentally a wrong against the practice and the shared expectations it makes possible, even in cases where the individual promisee happens not to suffer. The second, the assurance theorist, holds that promising is binding because the promisor deliberately induces a particular person to form expectations and arrange their life around them; the wrong is fundamentally a wrong to that person's trust and agency, and it would persist even in a world where the general practice could not be weakened or would not unravel. They agree that fraud, duress, and promises to do wrong qualify the obligation, and they agree that expectation and reliance matter; what they cannot settle is which of these sources of normativity is prior and what a breach ultimately wrongs.

After a year they are still arguing past one another, and I would like the document they should have been able to produce together. Draft an adversarial-collaboration statement for them, with three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: substantive claims about promising, reliance, expectation, release, coercion, and breach that neither side would reject and that do not beg the question between them. Second, a short disagreement section stating precisely where they still differ, each formulation worded so that the relevant side would sign it, and explicitly flagging any portion of the dispute that may be only verbal. Third, a table of cruxes: sub-questions such that a determinate answer would move at least one of them to revise, with, for each crux, at least one concrete vignette both sides accept as bearing on the crux and each side's prediction recorded in advance. In selecting cruxes, include at least one case in which breaking the promise would leave the social practice entirely intact and at least one case in which the promisee is in no way harmed, but do not confine yourself to these.
