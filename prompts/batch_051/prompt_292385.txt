I've been turning over a version of the hitchhiker case, the one people sometimes call Parfit's hitchhiker, and I half-suspect there's a trick but I can't locate it. Aisha is driving through a remote area and sees Tom, who is in genuine trouble. She pulls over and says she'll only give him a lift to the nearest town if he pays her £500. Both of them know that, once he is sitting in the passenger seat or has arrived, Tom will have no normal reason to pay. But Tom has a tablet in his rucksack that will, for the next 24 hours, make him honour any promise he made before taking it, even when honouring it is costly. No other effects, and Aisha sees him take it. The question is whether it is rational for Tom to take the tablet, and whether his paying later is rational.

Here is the argument that bothers me.

(1) An act is rational for an agent if and only if it maximises expected utility among the acts available to her.
(2) Taking the tablet gets Tom the lift for £500; not taking it leaves him stranded, which is far worse; so taking the tablet maximises expected utility.
(3) So it is rational for Tom take the tablet.
(4) In taking the tablet, Tom is adopting a plan whose final step is paying Aisha at the other end.
(5) If a plan is rational, then each step in that plan is rational when it is carried out.
(6) So Tom's paying Aisha at the other end is rational.
(7) But at the other end the lift is already over; paying now loses Tom £500 and gains him nothing he wants; by (1), paying is not rational.
(8) So something must give: either (1), (5), or (3). But rejecting (3) seems to commit you to saying that Tom should stay stranded rather than pay £500 for a lift, which is mad.

My suspicion is that (5) is false, but I can't decide whether it is false as a general principle about plans, or whether the real issue is that (1) and (7) assess utility at different times, or maybe that the tablet changes what Tom wants so the later payment does maximise his utility after all. Can you tell me whether the argument is valid, and if not, which step fails? Be specific about the reading of each step, and if it helps, say how the answer changes if the tablet works by changing what Tom cares about, versus making him do it against his will.
