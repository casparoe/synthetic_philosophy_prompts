ok so heres a thing i keep going back and forth on. im fairly convinced by the dennett-style multiple drafts line about the color phi phenomenon. you know the setup: a red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right, and what you see is a single dot moving across and changing color near the middle. the standard puzzle is that the color change seems to get inserted before the second dot is even there. the orwellian story says you briefly saw the first color go all the way across, and then the later stimulus retroactively rewrote your memory of it. the stalinesque story says your consciousness held off for a beat, assembled a coherent narrative once the new information was in, and only then let you in on it. the multiple drafts view says there is no fact of the matter about which happened: asking what you were actually conscious of at a given instant just assumes a cartesian theater, a place where a finished version of experience is screened, and the whole point is that no such place exists. so the distinction is empty, not because we cant know the answer, but because the question is ill-posed.

and yet, when i run the demo, i cant shake the conviction that there has to be a fact about what i was conscious of at, say, 200 milliseconds. either the intermediate content was already present in my experience and got edited later, or it wasnt present until the later content came in. those are two different histories of my own mind, even if i cant introspect the difference after the fact. the claim that no experiment could tell them apart feels like an epistemic point, not a metaphysical one, and i dont see why my own fallibility should dissolve the question about what actually happened in me.

so which should give? i want the strongest case for debunking the intuition, the strongest case for saying the intuition is tracking something the multiple drafts story leaves out, and the strongest case for a reading on which both survive, say by relocating the fact of the matter to subpersonal content-fixations rather than consciousness. dont just pick a side; tell me what would have to be true for each move to be the right one.
