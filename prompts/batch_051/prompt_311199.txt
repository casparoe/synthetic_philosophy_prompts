I'm taking evolutionary psych this semester and the prof keeps leaning on a single picture: the mind as a Swiss Army knife. His line is that a general-purpose learning machine would be to slow and too aimless, so natural selection gave us a stack of specialized modules—one for cheater detection, one for face recognition, one for mate preference, whatever—and each folds out for the specific ancestral problem it was built to solve. He uses the knife to argue against the blank slate view, and pretty much any time someone asks "ok but what about culture or learning" the knife comes back out: knives don't learn, they come with their tools already shaped, so why would the mind be different?

Meanwhile I'm in a philosophy of science seminar where evo psych is basically the punching bag: the criticism is that it's all just-so stories, where you can start with any current behavior and reverse-engineer some fitness story about it backwards (we like music because it bonded groups, we gossip because it kept freeloaders in check, etc.), so nothing would ever disconfirm it. I keep feeling like the Swiss Army knife is doing real argumentative work for the prof but I can't tell if it's good work or sleight of hand.

So: audit the analogy for me. Give me the explicit mapping—what corresponds to the blades, the handle, the individual tools, and what's doing the actual work in the prof's argument. Then the disanalogies: a knife's parts are fixed at manufacture and don't develop, don't compete with each other, and each has one dedicated function, whereas a mind is plastic and a body is a repurposed mess (exaptations, spandrels, whatever). Which disanalogies actually matter for the modularity conclusion and which are red herrings? And does the analogy make the just-so stories problem better or worse—does positing a module per behavior just let you tell more stories? End with whether the prof's conclusion (strong modularity, and present behavior as a window into ancestral problems) survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and maybe what a better analogy would be if there is one.
