A friend who campaigns for simplified spelling keeps insisting that an alphabetic writing system is best understood as a theory of the sound system of its language. On his telling, the Greeks added vowel letters because they had finally worked out a complete phonemic analysis; the letter inventory is the theory's set of posits; and a good orthography is one that stays phonemically faithful. From there he concludes that English spelling is defective precisely because its 'theory' is centuries out of date — it still marks distinctions nobody pronounces any more. I'm not sure how far this holds. Can you give me the correspondence table: what in a writing system plays the role of data, hypothesis, revision, prediction? Then say where the mapping breaks — I especially want to know wether a script is answerable to phonology the way a theory is answerable to evidence, and whether the spelling-reform conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the theory analogy is doing too much work, what would a better one be? Please write your answer in British English.
