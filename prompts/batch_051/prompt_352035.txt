I've been rereading the Wilt Chamberlain section of Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia, and I keep getting stuck on the passage where he says that if people are entitled to their holdings and they freely transfer some of them—like a million people giving a quarter to see Wilt play—then the resulting distribution is just, no matter how unequal it gets, and any patterned principle has to either forbid those transfers or keep interfering afterward. I'd like you to write a dialogue between two people about this: one who thinks that passage basically refutes all patterned or end-state principles of justice, and one who thinks it only works because the example stacks the deck, maybe by treating the initial holding as morally clean and the transfers as totally uncoerced. Have them actually respond to each other's points, not just trade talking points, and make sure neither is a strawman. They should focus on whether the Wilt example shows that patterned principles can't be maintained without continous interference, or whether a patterned view can survive by applying to something other than final balances—like background institutions, opportunities, or the tax rules people know in advance. If you think the passage itself is ambiguous or that the argument leans on a loaded notion of what counts as a free transfer, don't write around that: say so, and let the characters argue about it instead of pretending the issue is settled.
