Okay here's one I keep going back and forth on. Ernst Mach died in 1916, so he never saw quantum gravity, but he's basically the guy whose relationism pushed Einstein toward general relativity. Suppose Mach were around today and read the modern claim that spacetime isn't fundamental but emerges from deeper, non-spatiotemporal degrees of freedom — in loop quantum gravity, for instance, space is supposed to be built from spin networks, and in some holographic setups spacetime is a coarse-grained description of a boundary theory. At first glance you'd think he'd love it: it says space and time aren't a preexisting container but only relations (or near-relations) among whatever the underlying stuff is, which is pretty much his anti-Newton, anti-absolute-space line. But there's a real tension. Mach spent much of his career resisting atomism, partly on the grounds that atoms were unobservable posits, and emergent spacetime does something similar: it postulates a whole layer of structure beneath experience and then tries to recover the world we observe from it. So the question is whether Mach would endorse emergent spacetime as the completion of his relational program, or denounce it as exactly the kind of unobservable metaphysical machinery he attacked. Give me your strongest prediction, not just "he'd be ambivalent," and be specific about which parts of his epistemology would do the work.
