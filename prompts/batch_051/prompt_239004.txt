Write an essay on whether clicking "I agree" to a terms-of-service box is morally valid consent. I build software for a living, and I've always vaguely assumed that when a user hits "I agree" we're covered. But the more I think about it, the less sure I am—and I don't mean legally, I mean whether the person has actually consented in a meaningful sense. Consent is usually understood to require at least: the person understands what they're agreeing to, they're competent to agree, and they're not coerced or decieved. Almost nobody reads a 40-page ToS written by lawyers, so the "understands" part seems pretty broken. On the other hand, users do have a choice to not use the service, most of them don't seem to care that much, and maybe "I don't care enough to read it" is itself a kind of informed choice under the circumstances. Or maybe that's just something people in my industry tell themselves so we can keep shipping consent banners. Dark patterns make it worse: does it count as valid consent if the "accept all" button is big and bright and the "reject all" link is buried in a submenu? Where exactly is the line between nudging and coercion? Also, is consent even the right standard for something like letting an app collect telemetry, or does some other notion fit better? Weigh the strongest versions of both sides, but don't sit on the fence—defend a conclusion about whether ordinary click-through consent is morally valid, and say where you think your own position is weakest.
