My brother, who reads a lot of popular economics, summed up the free-rider problem to me like this: "A free rider is basically a thief with good manners. They consume what other people paid for, so what they get is someone else's loss, dollar for dollar. And it's provably wrong because if everyone free rode, the thing would never get built in the first place — so the free rider's act only works because other people aren't doing what he's doing, which makes it a contradiction." This sounds mostly right and I've caught myself repeating it, but two parts nag at me. The theft comparison seems off: if I listen to public radio in a cafe and never donate, am I really taking something out of a donor's pocket the way stealing their bike would? Same worry about calling free riders "parasites" — a parasite actually harms its host, and I'm not sure a free rider always does, especially when the thing exists anyway. And the "if everyone did it" point feels like it shows the practice is fragile or unfair, not that my particular act directly hurt anyone. Which parts of his summary would someone who actually works on this stuff sign off on, and which parts are overstatments or category mistakes?
