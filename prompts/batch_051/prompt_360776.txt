I've been running a small prediction market with my friends for the last year, and its turned me into one of those people who worries about whether their own credences are coherent. So I tried to write down the stuff I actually believe now, and I'd love a consistency check before I go further down this rabbit hole. Here's where I've landed:

(1) I'm a probabilist about credences: your degrees of belief are rationally required to obey the probability axioms, and if they don't, you're incoherent in a way that matters, not just sloppy.

(2) I buy the depragmatized Dutch book argument for (1). The problem with credences that license a guaranteed-loss set of bets isn't that you'd actually lose money; it's that the losing profile exposes something badly wrong with the credences themselves, whether or not any bookie ever shows up.

(3) That said, I've become convinced the accuracy-dominance argument is the better foundation for probabilism than the Dutch book argument: it shows any incoherent credence function is dominated by a coherent one in expected inaccuracy, with no bets or bookies anywhere in the picture.

(4) I don't think coherence is just synchronic. Conditionalization is a genuine requirement: if I really have a plan for how I'll update on whatever evidence arrives, and that plan isn't conditionalization, then a bookie could run a Dutch strategy against me across time, and that shows the plan itself is defective.

(5) But I'm also drawn to time-slice rationality, the view that there are no brute rational requirements linking my present self to my past or future selves. What I should believe now is fixed by my current evidence and current credences, full stop.

(6) I still accept reflection, at least restricted to future selves who are fully rational and have no less evidence than I have now: conditional on learning that such a future me will assign A a credence of .8, I should already assign A a credence of .8 now.

(7) I'm a permissivist about priors: two rational people can start with very different priors and stay rational through the same evidence, and for most real propositions there isn't a unique rational credence waiting out there.

I can feel that some of this probably doesn't fit together, but I cant tell which pairs are the bad ones and which only look bad from a distance. Go through the set and tell me: where are the real tensions, ordered by how badly they hurt? Which pairs are only apparently in tension? And if I had to change exactly one of these to make the rest hang together, which one should go and what would I lose?
