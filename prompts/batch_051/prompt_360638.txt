I'm giving a talk at a health-policy modeling workshop defending the claim that the leveling-down objection is largely a false choice between prioritarianism and egalitarianism. My argument runs: the objection only lands against the view that equality is good independently of anyone's life going better in any respect; the most defensible egalitarianism is relational, valuing how people stand to one another rather than a bare pattern, and leveling down does not improve those standings; once relational egalitarianism is stated that way, it and prioritarianism agree on essentially all health-policy prioritizations, so treating them as rivals in distributional cost-effectiveness analysis is a modeling error rather than a live ethical dispute. The audience is mostly health economists who use prioritarian social welfare functions in cost-effectiveness models, plus one relational egalitarian philosopher and one sufficientarian who thinks priority stops mattering above a threshold.
