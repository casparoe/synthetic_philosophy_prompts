I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I'm trying to understand what mechanistic interpretability is supposed to add. For any input, I can already dump the complete forward pass—every hidden state, attention weight, and logit from the token embeddings to the output distribution. Since a neural network is a deterministic composition of arithmetic operations, that trace is an exact, lossless account of how the input became the output. So my question is: if an explanation is meant to tell you why the model produced what it did, doesn't the full execution trace already do that better than any high-level interpretation? And if the trace is too large to be useful, why isn't that just a compression or tooling problem—one we solve by indexing the trace, or by training a second model to produce concise explanations of the first, and a third to explain the second when those explanations are themselves opaque? On that picture, interpretability is reducible to a sequence of ordinary engineering tasks, not an open scientific problem. I'd like a committed answer: take a definite position on whether the trace already constitutes an explanation, and if not, state precisely what an explanation must add that no amount of logging or recursive summarization can supply.
