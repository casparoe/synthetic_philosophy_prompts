In my philosophy class we have been studying the problem of divine hiddenness: the puzzle that if an all-powerful and perfectly loving God existed, God's existence would presumably be far more obvious than it actually is. I want to approach the question from the opposite direction and ask what would happen if hiddenness were removed in a very specific way.

Suppose that starting tomorrow, every person on earth who is already at least twelve years old will receive, once, at an unpredictable moment within the next five years, a private experience of the following kind. After those five years, each person receives the same experience once, during their twelfth year. The experience lasts about two minutes and makes the person certain that an all-powerful, perfectly good creator exists and cares about how they live. It cannot be recorded by any instrument, reproduced, or triggered early; it leaves no physical trace; and after it ends, the person remembers it vividly but cannot prove to anyone else that it happened. The fact that this will happen is common knowledge: everyone knows it, everyone knows everyone knows it, and so on. The experience conveys nothing beyond the bare existence and goodness of the creator and the fact that the creator cares about human conduct. It says nothing about which religion, if any, is correct, issues no specific commands, and reveals nothing about an afterlife or sacred texts.

I am not asking whether such a scenario is possible, or what it would show about whether God exists. I am asking for a reasoned prediction of how the next thirty years play out for (1) organized religion, (2) everyday moral behavior and the law, and (3) the social status of religious disagreement. Please make the chain of reasoning visible. Which effects follow simply from everyone's becoming certain? Which follow from the fact that the certainty is private and non-transferable? Which follow from the fact that the experience happens only once and then fades into ordinary memory? Also say which predictions you are most confident in, which are fragile, and what would have to be different for the fragile ones to go the other way.
