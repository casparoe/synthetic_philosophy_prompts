After a career in geochemistry, I have recently turned to philosophy of education, and I am helping a colleague design the final for an upper-level seminar on the normative grounds of school choice. The seminar has spent several weeks on Mill, Humboldt, and the tension between state provision of schooling and parental diversity. I want an essay question that takes the following passage as its pivot. Mill placed it as the epigraph to On Liberty, from Wilhelm von Humboldt’s Sphere and Duties of Government:

The grand, leading principle, towards which every argument unfolded in these pages directly converges, is the absolute and essential importance of human development in its richest diversity.

The question should ask students to work out what this principle would imply about three institutional arrangements—mandatory public schooling, state regulation of private schools, and homeschooling—while pushing them beyond a simple “diversity is good” answer. It must force them to confront a hard objection: if the state enforces common educational standards in order to protect children’s future autonomy, does that violate or honor Humboldt’s principle? Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by naming the specific distinctions a top answer must draw. The marking scheme should total 20 marks, and for each of the three institutional arrangements it should identify at least one common mistake that should cost marks.
