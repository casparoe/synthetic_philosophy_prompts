I have come to hold a deflationary view of emergence. I take it that what people call emergent properties are real patterns that are fixed, in principle, by the lower-level constituents and their interactions, but cannot be recovered from those constituents by any feasible derivation or simulation. The renormalisation group and universality are my central examples: the fact that critical exponents are insensitive to most microphysical detail shows that we need higher-level descriptions for prediction and compression, but not that any new causal powers appear. What would count against this position? I want a careful map of the strongest counter-evidence or counter-arguments, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In particular, distinguish two ways my view could fail: (a) a higher-level description might be indispensable not just practically but in principle, so that even an ideal reasoner with the complete microphysical state could not derive the higher-level behaviour; or (b) some higher-level feature might have downward causal influence on the lower level in a way that the lower-level laws alone exclude. Which actual explanations or results in physics, chemistry, biology, or neuroscience come closest to exhibiting (a) or (b), and which only show current derivational limits? Be explicit about what a defender of my view should concede counts against it, and end by saying whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that itself is a reason to reject it.
