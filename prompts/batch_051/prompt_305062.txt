So I've been turning over Sen's liberal paradox and it's got me in a bit of a knot. The setup: a social choice rule that works on any profile of individual preferences has to satisfy two things I find close to axiomatic. First, the Pareto principle: if every single individual prefers x to y, the social ranking should put x above y. Second, a minimal dose of liberalism: each person should be decisive over at least one pair of alternatives that concern only their own private sphere — say, whether Amara gets a tattoo or nobody does, and whether Bo gets one or nobody does. Sen shows these two, plus the any-profiles condition, can produce a cycle. Here's the concrete csae I keep replaying: Amara (the enthusiast) ranks Bo getting the tattoo best, Amara getting it second, neither worst. Bo (the traditionalist) ranks neither best, Bo getting it second, Amara getting it worst. Pareto says both prefer "Bo gets it" to "Amara gets it." But Amara's say over her own sphere gives "Amara gets it" over "neither," and Bo's say over his gives "neither" over "Bo gets it." So you get Bo > Amara > neither > Bo, a cycle.

The answer everyone gives is that at least one of the conditions has to go, and then they take a side and leave it there. I want something more committed than that, but not a shallow menu either. Take a definite position: which condition would you give up or amend — the Pareto principle, the minimal-liberalism condition, unrestricted domain, or some precise weakening of one of them — and why is it the right one to give up? Before you do, give each of them its strongest defense. Is the Pareto-principle intuition here smuggling in a meddlesome preference about how other people live that a liberalism condition exists precisely to fence off? Or is "decisive over your own private act" a principle that can't survive contact with any coherent social choice rule once preference profiles can be shaped by spite, nosiness, or self-sacrifice? Then tell me whether some repair actually works and what it costs — restricting the domain of admissible preferences, making rights constraints on the menu rather than preferences, indexing preferences, whatever — and tell me which fix wins and what we lose by taking it, not just that the fixes exist. American English, please; I trip over UK spellings.
