Somewhere around 2040, a regulation goes into effect worldwide: any AI system whose outputs get used to make decisions about people — loans, hiring, medical triage — has to be outfitted with an "explainer" module that produces a plain-language rationale for each decision. The catch is that the explainer is trained only on the system's inputs and outputs; it can't peek at the weights or internal activations of the decision system. So its rationales are just the inferences a careful human observer would make about why the system did what it did. Within a few years, courts and regulators start citing these rationales as if they were the system's actual beliefs and intentions.
