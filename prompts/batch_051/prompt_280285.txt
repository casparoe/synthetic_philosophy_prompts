Putnam's Twin Earth argument is often read as showing that what a speaker's words mean is not fixed entirely by what is inside the speaker's head. I understand the argument at the level of reference, but I do not see how it can be squared with the idea that we know our own minds directly. Suppose Oscar on Earth and his exact physical duplicate on Twin Earth are internally indistinguishable, yet because Oscar's local water is H2O and the duplicate's local watery stuff is XYZ, Oscar's word water picks out H2O while the duplicate's word water picks out XYZ. When Oscar thinks to himself that there is water in his glass, and the duplicate thinks to himself the very same thing, are they having the same thought or different thoughts? If their thoughts differ even though they are internally indistinguishable, it seems neither can know by introspection alone what he is thinking; but if their thoughts are the same, the example seems to concern only how outsiders describe them, not their actual mental lives. How do externalists about mental content avoid the skeptical conclusion that our own thoughts are not fully transparent to us?
