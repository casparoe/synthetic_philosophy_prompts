I have a standing argument in my group chat between two people I'll call the Strict Falsificationist and the Neo-Malthusian. The Strict Falsificationist thinks Malthus is basically a dead theory: the 1798 model's core claim — population tends to grow geometrically while food grows arithmetically, so checks like famine and war are more or less inevitable — got its own near-term predictions wrong. European population rose sharply after Malthus wrote while real wages also rose, and he failed to anticipate anything like the demographic transition. The Neo-Malthusian says this is exactly the wrong way to read a long-run prediction: the point was never the timetable, it was the pressure populations put on resources, and "right but early" is a legitimate epistemic category, not an excuse. They've been arguing past each other for months, and I've finally convinced them to do a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Write the document they should have produced. The deliverable should be a joint statement between a committed strict falsificationist (in the philosophy of science sense, sympathetic to Popperian worries about ad hoc rescue and unfalsifiability) and a committed neo-Malthusian (who is comfortable with Lakatos-style research programs and thinks the demographic transition and the Green Revolution rescue rather than refute the core Malthusian insight).

The document must have: (a) a numbered section of agreed claims both can sign — about the original 1798 model, its near- and long-run track record, the demographic transition, the Green Revolution, the Boserupian line that population pressure drives agricultural intensification, and what "checks" can and cannot mean today. These should be substantive, not "both sides value empirical evidence" filler. Then (b) a disagreements table: the exact residual disagreements, phrased so each side would accept the wording of its own side, and none of them slanted. Then (c) the cruxes — the sub-questions with the property that settling one would actually move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, historical (say, Ireland in the 1840s, England in the century after Malthus, Bangladesh after the Green Revolution) or a precisely specified hypothetical, and write down in advance what each side predicts that case would show. Finally, (d) say which parts of their fight are merely verbal and which are live, and don't hedge.

I want you to actually commit here, not survey the options. Commit to the exact formulations each side should sign, commit to which crux is doing the real work, and give your reasons for those choices. If making the collaboration real would force one side to give up a formulation they'd reflexively prefer, say so. The finished document should be something both of them could genuinely put their names to.
