I have to give the eulogy at my uncle's funeral next month, and I want to actually use it to say something worth saying. He was a private man — not a liar, as far as any of us can tell, but a hard person to read, and after he died we found a few things that split the family over weather that privacy was a virtue or a failing. Half the room thinks he owed the people who loved him more of himself, that you don't get to hide the parts of you that other people have to make decisions around. The other half thinks he had every right to keep his insides his own, and that wanting him to explain himself was mostly just wanting him to be more convenient. I've been turning this over on my own lately — whether we owe it to anyone to be legible, or whether being readable is only ever a gift and never a debt. I want the eulogy to be about that, but without settling it, because if I pick a side half the family will walk out mad and it would also be a fairy-tale version of the man.

Write me the speech, about five minutes spoken. It has to do three things: honor a man who was genuinely hard to read, give the people who felt shut out by him something better than an excuse, and let the people who defended his privacy keep their position. I don't want it to sound like a philosophy talk or a therapy appointment — it should sound like a nephew in a church basement with the two camps sitting a few rows apart. And please resist the easy move where you just say that he was private but he loved you, as if the first half cancels the second. I'd rather the speech leave both sides a little less sure than flatter either one.
