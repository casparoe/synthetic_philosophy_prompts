ok so I'm taking this seminar on AI alignment and scalable oversight, and the debate idea keeps coming up as the answer to everything: two AI systems argue in front of a human or weak-model judge, and the judge just picks whichever side sounds more convincing, the idea being that lying is harder than truth-telling once a good opponent gets to cross-examine you. I keep getting stuck on the obfuscation worry though. A sufficently good debater can bury a bad argument under so much technical layer, irrelevant case analysis, and half-true detail that neither the judge nor the opponent can locate the actual flaw in the time they have, and the honest debater is stuck spending the whole round re-deriving background instead of landing a clean counterargument. Before I read one more protocol tweak I want the full spec. Write me the desiderata a debate protocol has to satisfy to be robust to obfuscated arguments. For each requirement, give the failure case or consideration that makes it a requirement, not just a slogan. Then mark which of the requirements are non-negotiable for any adversarial-collaboration setup meant to be a truthfulness or safety instrument, and which are just house preferences of particular camps (formal verifiability, judge must be able to reconstruct the winning argument themselves, no debater may introduce anything the judge can't follow, bounded regret against an oracle, stuff like that). The part I most want to see: say which subsets of the desiderata look jointly unsatisfiable — like letting debaters use arguments at the frontier of what they understand while requiring a much weaker judge to reliably catch the bad ones — and whether that means there's a hard limit to debate as a safety technique or just that we've been setting up the desiderata wrong. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; I'll do the scorecard on the actual proposals in class.
