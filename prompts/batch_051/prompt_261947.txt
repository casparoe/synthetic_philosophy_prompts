Build me a case where someone who is a blatant hypocrite—same kind of wrong, not that long ago, no real repentance—still has standing to blame, while someone whose hands are totally clean doesn't, and the difference comes from their relationship or stake in what happened rather than from the hypocrisy. Keep it to ordinary life (a workplace or a family, nothing fanciful), and don't just stipulate the verdicts; set the case up so they fall out of the underlying accounts of standing, and the clean-hands person can't lose standing for a dumb reason like not knowing the facts. After the scenario, walk me thru what a view that ties standing mainly to hypocrisy says about each blamer, what a view that ties it to relationship or stake says about each, and then say which single feature of the case is doing the work and what happens if you remove it. End with the best reply a defender of the hypocrisy view could give, and whether you think it succeeds.
