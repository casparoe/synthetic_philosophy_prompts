In school we teach kids that the abolitionists were heroic for insisting that enslaved people belonged inside the circle of moral concern when almost everyone around them treated that boundary as just obvious. But now if someone insists the circle should cover, say, farmed octopuses, or the future people whose climate we're deciding, we tend to treat them as preachy, impractical, or a bit weird. Both are asking their contemporaries to treat the current boundary as a mistake. I can't tell if the difference is something real or just that the old fights are already won and so praising them is free. Maybe the live case has more uncertainty, or the costs fall on us differently, or the evidence is weaker — but im not sure that survives contact with the cases. I want the strongest candidate principle that would justify honoring past circle-expanders while brushing off present ones, tested against nearby cases, and an honest verdict on wether it holds up or whether we just can't stand people who ask us to change while we're still alive. Also tell me what would change your mind: which argument would flip your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
