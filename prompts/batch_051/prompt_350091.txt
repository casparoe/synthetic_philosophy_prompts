My roommate is weirdly into voting rules and I've been trying to extract the one rule she's actually running, because she swears it isn't just vibes. Her calls so far:

- Everyone who is an adult citizen and lives here should be required to vote. Small fine if you don't, though she thinks a blank ballot is fine if you hate all the options.
- Don't lower the voting age to 16. She says 16- and 17-year-olds don't know enough and don't have enough at stake yet.
- Permanent residents who've lived in the city for five plus years should be able to vote in local elections, because they pay taxes and the city's decisions hit their day-to-day lives.
- Temporary foreign workers shouldn't vote anywhere, even locally, even when they pay taxes, because they're just passing through.
- Citizens who move abroad keep the right to vote in national elections, but she doesn't think they should be fined for not voting.

Now she's coming to visit and I want to see how she handles three test cases. First, a 17-year-old who left school a year ago, works full time, rents her own place, and pays income tax. Should she vote in local elections, and would she be required to? Second, a citizen by descent only, born and raised in another country, never even visited here — can she vote, and is it compulsory for her? Third, a permanent resident who's been here only two years but owns a business and employs three people — local vote or not?

I honestly don't care what she blurts out on the spot. I want the rule she's running, the other rules that would fit her past verdicts just as well, which of my three cases would best tell them apart, and your best guess at each verdict under the rule you think she's actually using. The thing bugging me is that her reasons for keeping 16- and 17-year-olds out seem like they'd let a working 17-year-old in, and her reasons for letting settled immigrants vote seem like they'd leave out a citizen who's never set foot here. If she goes the other way on both, I want to know what the real prinicple is.
