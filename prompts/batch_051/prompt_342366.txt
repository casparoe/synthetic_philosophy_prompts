I keep going back and forth on when an experience actually happens, and I'm hoping you can extract a principle from my verdicts or tell me which ones I have to abandon.

Take the color phi setup. A red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right, in quick succession. I see a single dot slide left to right and turn from red to green about halfway. The odd part is that the color change seems to happen before the second flash, even though the only place the 'green' information could come from is the second flash. My verdict here: there's just no fact of the matter about whether I first consciously saw a stationary red dot and then had my memory rewritten (Orwellian), or whether the whole experience was delayed and the moving, color-changing episode was inserted before anything got to consciousness (Stalinesque). The two descriptions are the same film with different commentary.

But I can't hold that line everywhere. In the cutaneous rabbit, taps at my wrist, then near my elbow, then at my shoulder feel like a row of hops evenly spaced along the arm, and the middle tap is felt at a place it never was. Here I lean Stalinesque: I never felt the second tap at its true location at all; the later tap got folded in before the percept was finished. Yet in backward masking, where a briefly flashed word is followed by a mask and I can't report the word, I'm pulled Orwellian: there was a fleeting conscious glimpse of something word-like, and the mask erased it from memory before I could say anything. I can't verify that either, but the two cases feel genuinely different.

Then there's the slow case that ruins the tidy story. If the red and green dots are seperated by a full second, I am obviously conscious of the red dot before the green one appears — I can say 'red' out loud in the gap. If my consciousness were delayed a second to wait for the green, that delay would have to show up somewhere else, and it doesn't. So for slow sequences I'm stuck with Orwellian revision. And in the flash-lag effect, where a moving object exactly aligned with a stationary flash is seen ahead of the flash, I'm inclined to say the object was simply experienced at its extrapolated position, and there was no moment at which I experienced it at the true aligned position.

So: what principle, if any, captures this whole pattern? Give me the principle or a small set of candidates, state precisely which clause is motivated by which case, and tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed (for instance, a metacontrast display where a disk is followed by a surrounding ring and the disk disappears, or a color phi with an interval three times as long as the standard one). And be straight with me: which of my verdicts does the principle force me to drop?
