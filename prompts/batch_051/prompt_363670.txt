I am preparing the Q&A for a colloquium at which the speaker defends a Kripkean origin essentialism: an individual could not have developed from different gametes than the particular sperm and egg from which they actually developed, and this necessity is knowable by philosophical reflection rather than empirical investigation. Draft eight to ten questions that press the view's real vulnerabilities rather than restating it. Include at least one question on each of the following, and for each provide the reply the speaker is likely to give and a follow-up keyed to that reply: (i) the apparent contingency of any particular sperm-egg union and the companion intuition that a child from different gametes would have been my sibling, not me; (ii) monozygotic twins, where a single sperm and egg produce two distinct persons, and what this shows about whether sharing an origin is sufficient or necessary for identity; (iii) a tolerance or sorites-style argument, on which slight permissible differences in origin threaten to cascade into substantial ones, and what non-arbitrary line the essentialist can draw; and (iv) the epistemology of the view, namely how one could know the precise modal boundaries of one's own origin if the view fixes them exactly at the actual gametes. End with a question about what would count as serious evidence against origin essentialism.
