I've got a friend who insists that a coin that will be tossed exactly once and then melted down still has a real probability of heads — a propensity in the setup itself, not just a long-run frequency and not just how confident someone happens to be. I keep going back and forth on wether this is a claim about the world or just a choice of vocabulary. Design the best test you can to tell propensity, frequentist, and subjectivist readings of a single-case probability apart. What observation or experiment would count for each, which results would be decisive rather than merely suggestive, and what would a clean result still leave unsettled? Be honest about whether any experiment can actually reach the single-case probability the propensity person is talking about.
