Ok so I've been turning this over in my head. Some categories feel like they're real things out there — electron, say — and others are just handy boxes we made up, like vegetable or fish. So imagine a big science funding agency sets up a Natural Kinds Registry. Any team can submit a category — a species, a mineral, a mental disorder, a particle, a personality type, whatever — and a panel of scientists from different fields votes on whether the category carves nature at the joints or is just a usefull convention. Categories that get certified get priority in official databases, textbooks, and government statistics, and the uncertified ones have to be labeled provisional or conventional. You can challenge a decision after five years, and the panel's votes are made public. I'm not asking whether this is a good idea. I'm asking how it would actually play out: what would people rush to get certified and why, how would they game the panel, what second-order effects would show up (whole fields trying to get respect through certification, scientists splitting or lumping categories just to make them pass, people refusing to use uncertified categories, database wars, that kind of thing), and could you fix the ways it fails without turning it into a completely different scheme?
