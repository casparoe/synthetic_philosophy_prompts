I have been reading about the Daoist notion of wu-wei, and I keep running into a puzzle I cannot diagnose. Consider the following argument, which I find suspiciously clean.

(1) To act with wu-wei is to act without instrumental self-consciousness: one does not treat the action as a means to some further end that one is keeping in view while acting.
(2) A person who deliberately sets out to cultivate wu-wei is, in those very exercises, acting for the further end of becoming effortless.
(3) Therefore, the exercises through which one cultivates wu-wei cannot, while so performed, be instances of wu-wei.
(4) A style of acting whose essence is the absence of effortful striving cannot be brought about by a course of action that is, throughout, effortful striving.
(5) Therefore, wu-wei cannot be cultivated; it can only arise non-deliberately, if at all.

I find it hard to tell whether this is a genuine paradox or a verbal trap. Is the argument valid as written? For each premise, say whether it is true on the most charitable reading, or whether it depends on an equivocation. If the argument fails, identify the precise step at which it fails and state the distinction or reading on which the failure turns. If the argument is valid, explain whether the conclusion is as damaging as it sounds, or whether it attacks a straw man. In either case, say what a corrected account of wu-wei and its cultivation ought to look like.
