I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been reading about early debates over machine intelligence in my spare time. I want to turn one specific moment into a piece of speculative fiction. In Alan Turing's 1950 paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence, he gives serious treatment to the objection that extrasensory perception would undermine his imitation game: if telepathy or clairvoyance is real, a human contestant could receive information about hidden objects or cards in a way a machine could not, and Turing suggests the test would need to be run in a room shielded against telepathic influence. That combination of an external, behavior-based test for thinking and an apparent openness to a channel of information that bypasses ordinary observation strikes me as philosophically and dramatically rich. Please write a short story, not an essay, that dramatizes the tension between treating thought as something we can only observe from the outside and taking seriously a hidden channel of information. The story may be set in any era and does not need to mention Turing by name. After the story, add a brief note on where the fiction takes liberties with Turing's actual argument or leans on a contested reading of it. If my description of Turing's position is confused or rests on a false premise, say so plainly rather than building the story on it.
