I keep noticing something in how psychiatric categories evolve, and I don't have a word for it. A category gets added to the diagnostic manual, and at first it's just a box for clinicians to tick. Then people start getting the label, form support groups, tell their life stories through it, and argue among themselves about what the 'real' version of the condition is and who is just copying. Some campaign to have the label dropped, others push to broaden it to cover experiences the original criteria never included, and even the act of rejecting the label sometimes gets read by clinicians as a symptom, which is itself part of the pattern. Meanwhile clinicians start seeing more of the condition becuase they're now looking for it, and after a while the official criteria get revised to fit the population that has grown up around the label. Then the revised criteria catch a slightly different group of people, who start reshaping the label from the inside in their own direction, and the whole thing goes around again. Its not quite a self-fulfilling prophecy, because the content of the category keeps changing rather than a prediction simply coming true; and it's not just labeling theory, because the labeled people are actively changing the label, not only being changed by it. Is there an established name for this specific two-way movement between a classification and the people it classifies? If the nearest concepts are things like self-fulfilling prophecy or labeling theory, could you say precisely where the phenomenon differs from each? And if there's no exact name, what would you call it, and where would you draw the boundaries—does the effect require people to know they've been classified, or to endorse the label, or is it enough that the label changes how others read them?
