I'm defending a practice-based account of promising: promises bind because it's mutually beneficial for people who are already reasonably trustworthy to keep a shared convention of promise-keeping going, so the obligation is created by the convention, but the convention only works because enough people have something like a pre-conventional disposition of fidelity. One guy is in law and keeps dragging everything back to reliance damages in contract. And there are a handful of undergrads who can ask those basic-sounding questions that are actually brutal.

Give me a prep sheet rather than a full defense. List maybe eight to ten objections I'll actually get, in the order they'll probably come and how dangerous each is, phrased the way this group would phrase them.
