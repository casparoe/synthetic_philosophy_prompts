I run an upper-level seminar called Blame Across Generations with about 16 students and a 90-minute block. I want a classroom activity that makes them feel the pull of original sin — not the theological details, but the underlying thought that a person can be on the hook for a wrong committed before they were born just because of whose line they're in. I don't want the name 'original sin' spoken until the debrief, and I want the structure to do the work, not a lecture up front.

Design a multi-round game where the class is a small community, the first 'generation' commits some unearned founding wrong (rig it so it's genuinely tempting), and later generations inherit uneven starting positions, obligations, or a taint that effects their choices. Use real small stakes — snacks or extra credit. The clever part has to be a mechanism that blurs the difference between (a) merely inheriting consequences and (b) inheriting something closer to guilt or standing. Include rules, materials, a round-by-round timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps specific moments to the conceptual points: inherited consequences vs inherited status vs inherited guilt, and repudiating an inheritance vs being unable to shake it.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about one feature of original sin the game can't reproduce (my guess is the vertical God-relationship part, but argue for your view). The debrief should include a moment where students have to vote on what the new generation actually owes before anyone names any theory.
