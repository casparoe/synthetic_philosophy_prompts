I chair my foundation's high-risk, high-reward grant program, and we're down to two finalists for one 500k award. One team argues their project has maybe a one-in-10^9 chance of preventing an AI catastrophe that would kill something like 10^20 future people, so they put the expected value around 10^11 lives saved; we have no real way to check that one-in-10^9 estimate. The other proposal would fund a dull, well-tested water-purification program that we're very confident will save a few thousand lives. Half the committee says the first proposal is just Pascal's mugging, and if we take it seriously we should hand the whole endowment to any crank with a story. The other half says refusing to act on tiny probabilities of enormous impacts is exactly the parochialism the foundation exists to overcome. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before next week's vote: recommend one proposal (or a third option, if you can defend it), and propose a general rule for handling mugging-style proposals next cycle that doesn't just dismiss them by fiat.
