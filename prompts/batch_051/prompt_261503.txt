By following the book mechanically, like an enormous lookup table, you could feed in a question and get back exactly what Einstein would have said, so you could sort of keep talking to him after he's dead. I think it's from that Hofstadter and Dennett collection, though I don't remember the exact essay.

My roommate says the whole point is that the book would be conscious, and his version goes like this: since the book would produce exactly the same outputs as Einstein's brain for every input, and having a mind just is producing the right kind of input-output pattern, the book literally is Einstein — or at least has everything about Einstein that matters. If you think that can't be right because it's just paper and ink, he says, you're making the same hardware/software confusion as someone who denies that a program can run on different hardware. The mind is the program, not the stuff.

I'm inclined to buy most of this, but the "literally" bugs me, and I keep wanting to say the book itself isn't conscious because nothing is happening in it until somebody follows the rules. Could you go through his summary piece by piece and tell me which parts are fair to the original, where he's overstating or changing the position, and whether his conclusion actually follows from the accurate parts?
