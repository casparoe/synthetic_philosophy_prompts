For a working group on the long-term future, I have been asked to write down my settled views on a world of whole-brain emulations — uploaded human minds running as software, where copying and differences in speed are treated as ordinary economic facts — so the rest of the group can audit them. Before I present, I would like a consistency check on the package. Here is where I have landed:

(1) Emulations would be conscious in the same full sense we are and would have the same moral status as biological humans.

(2) When an emulation is copied, each copy is a distinct person; identity does not branch, and no copy is numerically identical to the original template.

(3) The most plausible economic trajectory for an emulation economy is Malthusian: because copies can be produced at near-zero cost and compete for the same work, the typical em's wage settles near the cost of running its hardware — subsistence.

(4) That wage outcome is not by itself a decisive moral objection to building such a world, provided the templates consented to being copied and the typical em life remains worth living.

(5) It would be permissible to create short-lived emulations for unpleasant or dangerous work and to delete them once the job is done, so long as each such em's life is on balance worth living; doing so is closer to procreation than to enslavement.

(6) Death is substantially less bad for emulations than for biological humans, because a deleted or crashed em can be restored from a recent backup, so a death is closer to an interruption than a permanent loss.

(7) We should refuse to build an emulation world if we expect the median em life to fall below the line of a life worth living.

I doubt all seven are jointly stable, but I do not want to signal in advance which I would revise. Please audit the set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. Treat this as an audit of my commitments, not a survey of the emulation scenario.
