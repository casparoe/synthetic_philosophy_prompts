Write the joint statement that a lexical-threshold negative utilitarian and a committed commensurabilist utilitarian could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether extreme suffering is commensurable with pleasure. The lexical-threshold position holds that some band of suffering is so bad that no quantity of pleasure or other goods, however large or widely distributed, can ethically outweigh it, while ordinary trade-offs remain permissible below the threshold. The commensurabilist position holds that pain and pleasure fall on a single cardinal scale of value, so any finite amount of suffering can in principle be offset by enough well-being elsewhere, and there are no discontinuities of this kind.

Produce a document with two sections. Section 1, Agreed claims, should be a numbered list of substantive claims both sides can sign, including what a lexical threshold would have to amount to, what commensurability would require, what trade-offs below the threshold are permitted, and what each side concedes to the other. Section 2, Disagreements, should be a table whose rows are the unresolved questions. For each row, give the exact formulation of the question; the wording each side would accept for its own answer; the crux, that is, the sub-question on which the row turns; and one test, argument, or case both sides accept as bearing on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also state, for each side, the observation or argument that would most pressure its own view.

Commit to a single, definite version of every formulation: do not hedge by offering several alternative wordings. If you judge that any part of the dispute is verbal, say so explicitly and restate the issue in terms that remove the verbal confusion.
