Question: Explain Metzinger's claim that the phenomenal self is a transparent self-model, and why he thinks this undermines the idea of a substantial self. Then assess whether someone in depersonalization, where this transparency is partly lost, sees the self as it really is.

Student's answer: Metzinger's main claim is that the self is not a thing but a representational model built by the brain. In ordinary life this model is transparent, meaning we look through it without noticing the machinery, so it feels like an unchanging owner of experience is standing behind everything. That is why he says the self as a substance doesn't exist: introspection only ever turns up the content of the model, never some extra entity behind it. So Metzinger is denying that the self exists. In depersonalization, the transparency breaks down and people report that their own thoughts and body feel unreal or constructed. On his view, these people are seeing the self as it really is, namely as a model rather than a fact. They have punctured the illusion. The broader upshot is that the ego is just a tunnel through which we project a self, and once you see this you could drop the self-model altogether and experience things as they actually are.
