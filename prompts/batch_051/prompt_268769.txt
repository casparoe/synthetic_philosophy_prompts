I teach Latin at a secondary school, and my best students — fifth-year Latin, comfortable reading Caesar, no linguistics background — keep asking when Latin "turned into" Italian. They tend to treat Latin as a fixed code of paradigms and exceptions, so I want one 80-minute lesson that answers the question while changing how they picture language change altogether. Plan the lesson rather than script it: the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what; the one concrete case that carries the lesson and what it must have and lack, given that they know Latin morphology but not linguistics; the misconceptions I should expect (for instance, that Classical Latin was what everyone in Rome actually spoke, that Italian is just debased Latin, or that a first speaker of Italian must have existed) and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off; a check for understanding that would show they can apply the idea rather than repeat it; and what I should explicitly leave out or mark as beyond this level. Keep the whole plan under 900 words.
