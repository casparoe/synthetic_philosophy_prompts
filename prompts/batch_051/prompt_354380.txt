I am preparing a graduate seminar on the normative force of decision-theoretic axioms, and the Allais paradox reliably splits the room: the students who regard the independence axiom as a coherence requirement and the students who regard Allais-type choices as fully rational end up talking past one another. I want you to draft the document I wish I could place in front of them — the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two mature camps. The first camp (the coherence camp) holds that the independence axiom, equivalently the sure-thing principle in this setting, is a binding norm on rational preference: a preference over prospects with objective probabilities that violates it is a mistake, and a reflective agent ought to repudiate the pattern. The second camp (the permissivist camp) holds that the standard Allais pattern can be stable, reflectively endorsed and rationally permissible, and that the right response is to relax independence while retaining the structural core of expected-utility theory — ordering, transitivity, stochastic dominance and some form of separability — perhaps in a nonlinear-probability or regret-sensitive representation. The document should work with the common-consequence version of the paradox: a first choice between £1,000,000 for certain and a lottery giving a 10% chance of £5,000,000, an 89% chance of £1,000,000 and a 1% chance of nothing; and a second choice between an 11% chance of £1,000,000 (otherwise nothing) and a 10% chance of £5,000,000 (otherwise nothing). It should make explicit the formal argument that the modal preference pattern violates independence.

The document should open with a numbered set of agreed claims both sides can sign: the formal setup; a precise statement of independence; the logical relation between the two choice problems; the descriptive facts about choice behaviour; what each side concedes to the other; and agreed criteria for distinguishing a mistake from a defensible valuation. It should then lay out the residual disagreements in wording each side would accept for its own position — no caricature. For each substantive disagreement, the document should say whether it is a crux, and for each crux it should record a concrete argument, case or experiment both sides accept as bearing on it, together with each side's prediction stated in advance. It should also identify any disputes that are merely verbal, and it should end with a section in which each camp states, as candidly as is plausible, what would change its mind: which finding or consideration would lead it to revise its view, and where its own reasoning is most likely to fail.

After the joint statement, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with the following schema: agreed_claims (array of strings); disagreements (array of objects with fields issue, coherence_camp_view, permissivist_camp_view, substantive_or_verbal, is_crux, bearing_evidence_or_argument, coherence_prediction, permissivist_prediction, and what_would_change_each_side, the last being an object with fields coherence_camp and permissivist_camp); verbal_disputes (array of strings); and fragility (an object with fields coherence_camp and permissivist_camp, each a one-sentence statement of where that side's reasoning is most exposed). Please write throughout in British English.
