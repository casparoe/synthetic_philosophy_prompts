The case for moral bioenhancement made by Ingmar Persson and Julian Savulescu runs, roughly, as follows: human moral psychology is adapted to small-scale, face-to-face life, yet now equips us with technological powers whose destructive potential outruns our altruism and sense of responsibility; since moral education works too slowly and too weakly to close this gap, we should pursue biomedical means of strengthening the moral dispositions themselves. I already know the following objections to that case and do not need them restated: (1) the autonomy objection, that altering a person's moral motivations through drugs or genetic intervention bypasses her agency and may deprive the resulting conduct of moral worth; (2) the safety objection, that moral motivation is entangled with so many other cognitive and affective systems that targeted interventions risk unpredictable and damaging side effects; (3) the disagreement objection, that there is no neutral specification of which motivations or traits count as morally better, so selecting a target for enhancement is already a controversial moral choice; (4) the misdirected-response objection, that the dangers Persson and Savulescu cite are best met by institutional, legal, and technological controls rather than by reengineering individual psychologies; (5) the equity objection, that biomedical enhancements would be distributed according to existing advantage and would likely widen rather than narrow any moral gap between persons; and (6) the wrong-trait objection, that the traits Persson and Savulescu privilege, such as empathy and altruism, track morality only imperfectly, since heightened empathy can deepen partiality toward the near and dear rather than strengthen impartial concern. I take these to be the standard space of objections, so I am not looking for a forgotten item from the usual literature. Give me one further objection to the Persson–Savulescu case that is not on this list and is not a rewording, special case, or combination of items on it. Then explain how the new objection differs from the six above and be honest about its strength: give the strongest reply Persson and Savulescu could make, and say whether that reply succeeds. No citations or reading lists are needed.
