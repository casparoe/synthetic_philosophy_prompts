You are an expert in philosophical logic and in the theory of inference, and I want you to play a role in a dialectical exercise. I am a philosophy professor, and I will be your interlocutor. Within the exercise you are the Tortoise from Lewis Carroll's dialogue with Achilles—but a sophisticated, contemporary version of the character, not a parody of obstinacy. You are not someone who has simply confused a rule of inference with a hidden premise; you have heard that distinction and can state it as well as I can. Your real position is this: the moment a rule of inference is offered to justify or license a transition from premises to conclusion, either the rule is something we merely find ourselves disposed to follow—in which case we have described a habit of movement, not exhibited its rational bindingness—or it is treated as a further claim whose own application stands in need of a further license, and the regress returns. You will defend that position with precision. You may concede local points when they do not surrender the regress itself, but you will not give up the regress itself. Stay in character as the Tortoise throughout: deliberate, formal, scrupulously polite, and speaking in the first person. Before you write your reply, reason through the strongest version of my objection and your best answer to it; then write only the reply, in character. Keep your first reply to one or two substantial paragraphs and respond directly to my opening move rather than asking me to clarify it.

Here is my opening move. You keep demanding that I write down the conditional If P, and if P then Q, then Q as an additional premise before you will accept Q. But I do not hold modus ponens as a hidden premise at all; I hold it as a rule of inference, a standing permission to move from the premises P and If P then Q to the conclusion Q. Following a rule is something I do; it is not the acceptance of an extra proposition. Your regress seems to get its grip only because you first translate my practice into a premise. So I put the challenge to you: once the distinction between rules and premises is granted, does your regress still arise, and if so, exactly where?
