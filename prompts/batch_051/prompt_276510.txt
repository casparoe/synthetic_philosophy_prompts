A few months ago I got laid off and then tore my rotator cuff climbing, and ever since, people have been feeding me the line that I'll come out stronger, that resilience is a virtue, that trauma is a chance for post-traumatic growth, all of it. I don't want to be cynical—I really do admire people who handle awful situations well. But I built an argument out of this and now its proving too much and I can't find the exit. (1) If resilience is a virtue, then responding well to adversity is good. (2) If responding well to adversity is good, then adversity gives you an opportunity to exercise a virtue, and having an opportunity to exercise a virtue is itself a good. (3) If adversity gives you an opportunity for something good, then the worse or more frequent the adversity, the more of that good you get. (4) So resilience logic seems to say that the more someone harms you, the more they've done you a favor, provided you bounce back. (5) That can't be right: your landlord screwing you over isn't a gift just because you handled it with grace. (6) So either resilience isn't a virtue, or the whole growth-through-adversity framing is ideology. Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd most like to keep (1), but I want to know what that costs. Which step do you reject, and what do you give up by rejecting it?
