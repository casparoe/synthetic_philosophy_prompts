I am a novelist, and in the course of researching a character who builds systems that infer human preferences from behavior rather than from anything people say, I have arrived at a concept I want to test before I lean on it in the novel. I have been calling it preference burn-in. The idea is this: when an agent has spent a long time operating inside a particular incentive environment, its behavior continues to carry the statistical signature of the old reward structure long after the environment changes, and an inverse-reinforcement-learning system observing the agent will tend to mistake that afterimage of departed incentives for a stable feature of the agent's actual preferences. My stock example is a person who spent years in a workplace that rewarded sheer volume of messages: after he moves to a job that rewards slow, careful writing, he keeps sending brief, high-volume emails, and a preference-inference system watching him would infer a durable love of volume where an interview would reveal something closer to inertia, habit, or exhaustion. A second, institutional example: a department that once lived under a now-abandoned publication-count metric keeps optimizing for it in subtle ways, and outsiders read that persistence as its settled scholarly values.

I think this could be a genuine confound for inferring preferences from behavior, and one distinct from two nearby notions: ordinary hysteresis, because the alleged error is in the inference procedure rather than in the agent's psychology, and mere habit, because the system is left with no way to separate preferences the agent would endorse from behavioral residue the agent would repudiate. But I am not sure that distinction is as clean as I want it to be. Is the concept coherent as stated? Does the inverse-reinforcement-learning or revealed-preference literature already treat something like this under another name? And where does the concept come apart — above all, how would one distinguish a stale-incentive afterimage from a genuinely stable preference without already presupposing a folk-psychological true self that behavior alone cannot disclose? Please answer in American English.
