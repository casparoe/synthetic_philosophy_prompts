In a criminal justice course I am helping to teach, a student raised a puzzle I have not been able to settle cleanly. The student observed that two people can perform what is, from the standpoint of their conduct and mental state, the very same culpable act, yet receive radically different legal treatment because of an outcome neither controlled. Consider two equally negligent drivers: both read a text message while approaching a crosswalk. One strikes and kills a pedestrian and may be charged with a serious homicide offense; the other, through the luck of timing, hits no one and faces only a minor traffic infraction. The same pattern often appears when attempted murder is punished less than completed murder, even though the only difference is whether the victim happened to survive. The system thus seems to combine two commitments: that people should be judged for what lies within their control, and that blame and punishment may properly track results that do not.

I want to understand what theory of moral responsibility this legal practice presupposes, and whether the practice is genuinely committed to it. Does the differential treatment of identical conduct with different outcomes amount to accepting that a person can deserve more blame, or greater punishment, because of luck? Or is the practice compatible with the view that responsibility extends only to what an agent controls — for instance, because the outcome serves merely as evidence of dangerousness, or because harsher punishment for harmful outcomes deters, or because the law must give the victim and the public something more when a harm has actually occurred? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can. In doing so, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and identify the points where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several different theories could license the same rules. Finally, briefly explain what in the practice would have to change if we adopted a thoroughgoing view on which no one may be blamed or punished for anything beyond their control.
