I grew up believing creation was made good and that suffering and death entered the world through human sin. I also accept evolution, so I've had to sit with the fact that animals were killing and starving each other for hundreds of millions of years before any human existed. My policy for this and similar clashes is that the theology is fixed: whenever well-established natural history seems to contradict it, I assume the conflict is only apparent and look for a reading that dissolves it — usually by saying the creation being good was never meant as a claim about animal welfare, or that animal death isn't suffering in the relevant sense. The other night I caught myself doing it again with parasitic wasps that eat caterpillars from the inside, and it made me wonder. Is this a rationally defensible policy, or have I made the doctrine unfalsifiable? Is there a version of the rule that keeps the doctrine without insulating it from everything, and how would I tell the difference from the inside?
