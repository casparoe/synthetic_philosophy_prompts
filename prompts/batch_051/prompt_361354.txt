I keep having this feeling that the Whig-history error (writing the past as if it was always heading toward us, judging past thinkers by how well they anticipated the present) is basically what a project does when it flattens its commit history before open-sourcing: all the false starts and reverted decisions get squashed into a clean, linear series of commits that seem like they were always aiming at the current main branch. And then `git blame` makes it worse—it credits the last person who touched a line, not the person whose original decision made it work. Is this a real structural identity, or am i just pattern-matching between two ways of tidying up a story? If it's real, does anything transfer the other way—like, does the git-blame effect explain why historians of science over-credit the person who put the finishing touches on a theory while erasing the messy context that made it legible? Where does the parallel actually come apart?
