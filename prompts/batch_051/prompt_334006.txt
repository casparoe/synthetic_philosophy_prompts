Over the last few weeks I've been trying to understand the hole argument in general relativity, and every time I lay it out I seem to end up with a conclusion that can't be right, so I want someone to tell me exactly where I'm going wrong. My reconstruction is this. (1) A spacetime is a manifold M of real points plus a metric field g on M that satisfies Einstein's field equations. (2) Because the theory is diffeomorphism invariant, if g is a solution then for any diffeomorphism d, the pulled-back metric d*g is also a solution. (3) Take a d that is the identity everywhere outside a small region H which lies to the future of some time slice S, and that shifts points around only inside H. Then g and d*g agree at every point outside H—and in particular on the initial data on S—but they differ at some points inside H. (4) If the points of M are real entities (substantivalism), then g and d*g describe two distinct physical possibilities, not the same world twice: in one, point p has whatever geometry g assigns it; in the other, that same point p has the geometry g assigns to d(p), which could be a completely different geometry. (5) Two physical possibilities that agree on all the initial data but differ later are possibilities where the initial data do not determine the future. (6) So if substantivalism is true, general relativity is radically indeterministic.

I half-suspect the sneaky step is (4), becuase the two models really do seem to assign different properties to the same points, so I can't articulate why it would fail. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails and under what reading of it? If it is valid, which premise should a substantivalist give up, and does giving it up just amount to relationalism? I'd rather you answer in plain prose, like an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
