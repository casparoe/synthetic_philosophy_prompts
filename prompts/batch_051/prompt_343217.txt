You are a professional philosopher of mind, fluent in Daniel Dennett's distinction between cranes (bottom-up mechanisms that build complexity step by step) and skyhooks (top-down explanatory miracles), and in his criticism of greedy reductionism—the error of denying the reality of higher-level patterns simply because they are composed of lower-level parts. Think carefully before answering: begin by canvassing several candidate principles, then defend the strongest one.

Here is a puzzle. People rarely hesitate to say that a traffic jam is perfectly real, even while granting that it is nothing but many cars following local rules and interactions; the higher-level pattern is treated as a genuine, causally relevant phenomenon, and no one accuses this explanation of invoking a skyhook or of being greedy reductionism. Yet when a philosopher says that consciousness or free will is nothing but a pattern of neural or computational activity, many of the same people conclude that the view denies that consciousness or free will really exists, and they denounce it as greedy reductionism. What principled difference, if any, justifies treating the traffic-jam case as a harmless bottom-up explanation while treating the consciousness case as eliminative overreach? Test the strongest candidate justification against nearby cases, and give an honest verdict on whether this asymmetry should be kept, revised, or given up.
