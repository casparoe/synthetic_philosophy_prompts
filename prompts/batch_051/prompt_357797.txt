After years of private argument, a friend and I want to write the document we should have produced. My friend holds a telic egalitarian view: inequality between people is non-instrumentally bad, so an outcome can be better in at least one respect simply because it is more equal, even if no one is better off. I hold a prioritarian view: an outcome is better only if it is better for someone, with improvements to the worse-off weighted more than equal improvements to the better-off; equality has no value in itself, so a change that benefits nobody has no good feature at all. We have found that we agree on many all-things-considered verdicts but keep talking past each other about whether a pure leveling down has even one good feature, about whether value must be anchored in some particular person’s life, and about whether my priority weighting just re-expresses a concern with inequality after all.

Please draft the joint statement of our adversarial collaboration. It should include: (1) an agreed-claims section, covering precise descriptions of what each of us accepts about leveling-down cases (for example, population A: five people at welfare level 100 and five at level 10; population B: all ten at level 10, with no other change, no one better off, and no loss of other value) and about cases where worsening the best-off does help the worst-off even though some value is destroyed in the transfer; (2) the residual disagreement stated exactly, in wording each side would accept for its own position; (3) the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of us (I suspect one is whether a state of affairs can be better in a respect when no individual is better off, and another is whether priority-weighting merely restates a concern about relative position); and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment both of us would accept bears on it, with each side’s prediction written down in advance. If you conclude that part of the dispute is verbal, identify that part and say why.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Begin with the document itself, with no preface or commentary.
