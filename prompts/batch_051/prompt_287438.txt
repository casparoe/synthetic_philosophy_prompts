Write a piece of sustained, deadpan satire of about 650 words in the form of an official public notice, titled something like "Notice of Implementation: Civic Speck Program," issued by the fictional city of Halverton's Department of Aggregate Wellbeing. The department has been asked to resolve the familiar torture-versus-dust-specks choice between (a) one person's being subjected to prolonged, excruciating suffering and (b) an astronomically large number of people each receiving a genuinely trivial, momentary annoyance, such as the canonical dust speck in the eye. The notice announces that, after careful welfare accounting, the department has chosen the specks, and explains how the program will work.

The comedy must come from getting the aggregation debate right rather than from generic bureaucratese. A reader who knows the philosophy should recognize the actual pressure points: the separateness of persons and the move of adding up a cost across different lives as if it were one person's cost; the contrast between each recipient's negligible loss and the enormous aggregate the calculation summons; the worry that iterating the procedure in small steps eventually yields an abomination; and the dubious commensurability of one person's agony with billions of mild irritations. In particular, make sure the notice includes:

- a subsection that tries to answer the separateness-of-persons objection and, in the course of reassuring the reader, materially concedes it;
- a table or schedule of equivalents (for example, so many seconds of crosswalk delay, so many flickers of a public screen, and so on, per avoided minute of the bad outcome) whose internal logic quietly generates the intransitivity worry;
- at least one passage in which the memo treats the fact that no actual victim is currently under the suffering option as a mere assignment problem rather than a reason to reconsider the choice.

The piece should also work for a reader who has never encountered the thought experiment: it should first make the additive logic feel almost obvious, then make the reader recoil from where that logic goes. Stay in the voice of the notice throughout — no winking, no authorial interventions — but make the ironized position unmistakable by the end. Keep it self-contained; no citations, footnotes, or reading list.
