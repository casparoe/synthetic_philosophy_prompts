I'm a PhD student in metaphysics and I keep running into the same problem: everyone who argues about why there is something rather than nothing is actually operating with a diffrent sense of 'nothing,' and the question can't even be stated precisely until someone pins that down. I want a working defintion of the 'nothing' that question is aiming at. Give me a definition — or, if you think the word does several genuinely different jobs, a short menu with a vote for which one is the philosophically interesting alternative — and run it against each of these: an empty room; a cubic meter of interstellar space with a few hydrogen atoms in it; the quantum vacuum, with fields but no particles; a state with no concrete things but with laws of nature still in force; a state with no concrete things but with numbers existing; the absolute absence of everything — no spacetime, no fields, no laws, no abstract objects; and the theist's alternative of no physical universe but with God existing. On your view, which of these count as nothing, and which one would the cosmological argument need as its alternative for the question to have real bite? Also, tell me how confident you are in the whole classification and which single verdict you're least sure of.
