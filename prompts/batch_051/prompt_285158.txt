I'm a software engineer at a mid-size company and last year I helped build an internal tool that automated most of our ops team's data entry work. Leadership loved it, but last month a bunch of those ops people got laid off. The work was genuinely repetitive — copying numbers between systems, updating spreadsheets — so I keep telling myself this was the good kind of automation, the kind people have been promising will free us from drudgery forever. But the people who got "freed" don't seem free. A few of them had been at the company 15+ years and its obvious the job was tied up with their sense of who they are. My manager says this is unambigously good: the company saves money, boring work disappears, and in the long run everyone's better off in some new role. I can't shake the feeling he's missing something, but I also can't articulate what without sounding like I just don't like change. Am I being sentimental, or is there a real moral issue here? What would a careful person say is actually lost when a job like that disappears?
