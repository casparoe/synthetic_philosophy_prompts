Ok so I keep seeing people defend constitutional judicial review by saying it's like Odysseus having his crew tie him to the mast so he can hear the sirens without sailing into the rocks. But Odysseus does that to himself — same guy, same body, and he can order his crew to untie him later. With a constitution it's basically the reverse: the ratifiers are long dead, and nobody alive today actually agreed to be bound by any of it. So isn't the Ulysses analogy actually a devestating argument against constitutional precommitment, not for it? Why do legal theorists still trot it out like its a defense?
