I've been chewing on the problem of old evidence for awhile and I want someone to design the experiment I keep failing to design.

Here's the claim, as sharply as I can state it. The problem of old evidence isn't just a bug in how Bayes' rule gets written down — it tracks something real about confirmation. When you've known some fact E forever (P(E) = 1, so a textbook update does nothing), and then a new theory H shows up that makes E the kind of thing H would have led you to expect, H still gets confirmed. Confirmation can flow out of facts you've fully absorbed. The rival reading is that the whole thing is an artifact of instantiating the rule at the wrong moment: you never get confirmation from old evidence itself, you get confirmation from a new proposition like "H explains E", and a properly time-indexed model would have swallowed the update at the right step.

How would one actually test the first reading against the second? I want a concrete behavioral design — people updating in some cover story, since credences don't show up in a measurement device — and I want you to tell me (a) which pattern of results would favor the "old evidence really confirms" reading, (b) which would favor the artifact reading, and (c) why the two are so hard to pull apart even with clean data. Then the part I actually need: say what even the cleanest positive result would still not settle, and tell me where your own inference is most likely to break — that is, what result or argument would make you switch to the other reading.

Constraints: no design where the subjects already know the philosophical dispute, and no design so toy that both readings predict the same thing by construction. My own attempt is a 2x2 where participants either learn the fact before or after the theory, but every version I've tried ends up measuring memory or explanation-fluency instead of confirmation, so talk me out of that dead end if you can.
