Here is an argument that gradual-replacement uploading preserves the person, and I cannot decide whether it is airtight or a sorites-style cheat. (1) Suppose one neuron in your brain is replaced by a microelectronic device that exactly reproduces that neuron's input–output behavior at every synapse. There is no point at which you cease to exist and no point at which a new person appears; you are the same person after the operation as before. (2) If A and B are the same person, and B and C are the same person, then A and C are the same person. (3) Replacing every neuron in the brain, one at a time, is simply a long finite sequence of operations like the one described in (1). (4) Therefore, by repeated application of (1) and (2), the person existing after the whole sequence is the same person as the person who existed before it. (5) So uploading, when it takes the form of gradual in-situ replacement rather than scanning-and-copying, preserves the original person. Is this argument valid?
