I'm doing a feature on how languages live with ambiguity — specifically homonymy, the way one sound can carry a bunch of unrelated meanings and we mostly don't trip over it because context snaps the right reading into place. I want to open with a poem that does the thing instead of explaining it. Write me five or six quatrains where the last line of each stanza is just the word 'pitch,' and each stanza has to force a different sense of it — a throw, a musical note, tar, a sales talk, the angle of a roof, the lurch of a boat. The constraint is the poem can never say which sense it means, no glossing or 'pitch as in...'; the stanza has to make it unmistakable the way context usually does. The turn in the last stanza should be the reader realizing the same word kept meaning different things and it never once felt like a problem — but the poem shouldn't announce that, it should make it happen. Maybe the final 'pitch' is the story pitch that started this whole piece. It has to hold up as an actual poem, not a linguistics demo. And I'm using 'homonymy' loosely, so dont sweat the technical boundary with polysemy.
