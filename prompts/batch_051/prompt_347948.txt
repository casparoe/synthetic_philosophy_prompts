For my seminar on the philosophy of money I've been trying to get students to treat Aristotle not as an old authority but as someone with a testable story about what money is. One student pushed back with a proposal I can't stop turning over. Suppose a small town decided to go back to weighed money for all local transactions under, say, five hundred dollars. The town mint issues little bars of fine silver. They carry no denomination at all — just the town's seal and a serial number. To use a bar as legal tender inside the town, buyer and seller have to weigh it together on a certified scale at the point of sale, and the buyer signs a receipt recording the serial number and the weight transferred; the town uses those receipts to compute the local sales tax at the day's silver price. The bars keep circulating, and each transfer follows the same weigh-and-record ritual.

Here's the passage from Aristotle's Politics, Book I, chapter 9 (1257a): "For the natural necessaries are not in every case readily portable; hence for the purpose of barter men made a mutual compact to give and accept some substance of such a sort as being itself a useful commodity was easy to handle in use for general life, iron for instance, silver and other metals, at the first stage defined merely by size and weight, but finally also by impressing on it a stamp in order that this might relieve them of having to measure it; for the stamp was put on as a token of the amount."

So run the mechanism for me, don't grade it. What happens in the first few months — to transaction speed, to posted prices, to who actually uses the bars rather than federal cash under the table? Where does it get gamed first: clipping, alloying, rigged scales, forged receipts, or bars that simply never get reported? And does the way the town ends up actually using these things fit Aristotle's claim that the stamp came along only to save the trouble of weighing, or would the place quietly reinvent denomination-stamping on its own? I half-suspect the town ends up teaching us something about whether the "token of the amount" role is seperable from the metal that carries it.
