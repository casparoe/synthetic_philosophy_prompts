I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent forums for years, and one debate that looks to me to have been running in place for over a decade is the Great Filter debate. The empirical situation is stable: we have exactly one known origin of life, no confirmed detections of extraterrestrial life or technology, and a sky that has stayed quiet across all our searches. From this same fact base, one camp concludes the improbable step is behind us — abiogenesis or some later pre-intelligence transition is so rare that we are effectively alone. A second camp concludes the improbable step is ahead — technological civilizations usually collapse or destroy themselves quickly, and the quiet is exactly what a doomed species should expect. A third camp says the observation is uninformative: conditional on our existing, a quiet universe is what both hypotheses predict, so no real update is licensed. What strikes me is not that these positions differ but that they haven't moved. I've re-read several old threads and watched the same arguments get rebuilt with no one changing their mind; it feels as if everyone can reconstruct everyone else's posterior from their priors, and the dispute has quietly shifted from being about the universe to being about how to reason from a single data point at all.

My working theory, offered as a hypothesis to be tested rather than assumed, is that this is not primarily a disagreement about astrophysics or biochemistry. I suspect it is a disagreement about anthropic methodology: which reference class to condition on (intelligent observers, technological civilizations, habitable planets, replicators of any kind); whether inferring hard steps post hoc from one success is a legitimate explanation or just curve fitting; and whether an absence of evidence can be evidence of absence once your own existence is part of the selection effect. I also suspect the camps may be answering different questions under the one heading of "where is the filter." I want a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a survey of Great Filter positions and not a verdict on who wins. Is this a hidden empirical question, a formal dispute, a prior dispute, a difference about standards of evidence, a verbal or question-framing difference — or several of these at once? Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a specific observation (say, an independently originated form of life, or a non-terrestrial biosignature), a formal result, a clarified distinction, or an honest argument that the question is partly ill-posed and no progress should be expected.
