In 2002 and early 2003 the United States and the United Kingdom went to war against Iraq with the stated justification, in substantial part, that Iraq possessed or was reconstituting weapons of mass destruction. Set aside the question of political blame and accept the following as the factual frame for this case. Prewar public statements treated Iraq's active WMD programs as all but settled; key intelligence assessments expressed high confidence that Iraq had chemical and biological weapons and was reconstituting them; a central defector source for the claim about mobile biological-weapons laboratories later admitted he had fabricated his account; UN inspectors had found no stockpiles and were asking for more time; and after the invasion, U.S. survey teams found no active WMD program or stockpiles, concluding that Iraq's old weapons had been destroyed years earlier and had not been rebuilt.

Use two positions from the ethics of belief as lenses on this episode. Clifford's evidentialism holds that believing on insufficient evidence is wrong universally, because beliefs are not a private matter: they guide action and are passed to others, and neither sincerity, nor the comfort the belief brings, nor its source in a trusted institution excuses it. James's will-to-believe replies that where a genuine option is what he calls live, forced, and momentous, and the intellect cannot by itself settle it, one may legitimately let one's passional nature decide and believe ahead of the evidence.

Analyze the episode through each view. Say what Clifford's principle would have required of an intelligence analyst, of a senior policymaker, and of a government treated as a distributed believer, and at what point each should have refused belief; address how far a duty that was stated for individuals transfers to an institution organized by hierarchy, secrecy, and division of labor. Then work through James's three conditions in this case: was the WMD option live, forced, and momentous for the relevant believers, and was suspension of judgment a real alternative? Finally, run the analysis in the other direction: which features of the case does each view explain well, and which feature is a genuine anomaly for it? In particular, does the episode show that James's exception is dangerously easy for states to exploit, or does it show that his exception was not met here because the belief was not held in the spirit his essay requires? Where a theory is silent about institutional belief, say so rather than stretch it. Throughout, keep the epistemic question of whether the WMD belief itself was justified separate from the question of whether the war was justified all things considered.
