I am an undergraduate student in an epistemology course, and I am writing a term paper on the distinction between vicious and benign infinite regresses. I would like you to construct a worked example for me to use in the paper, built to the following specifications.

Construct a single scenario containing two infinite regresses, R_t and R_j, designed so that exactly one feature can be isolated as the difference between them.

Specifications:

(1) Both regresses begin from the same mundane proposition P; please use the proposition that the floor of a certain building will hold the weight of the people gathered in it.

(2) Each regress is generated by the same recursive operation, adjusted only as far as the difference between propositions and beliefs requires: from an item S, form the claim that S has the status in question. For R_t the status is truth, giving the sequence P, the proposition that P is true, the proposition that (the proposition that P is true) is true, and so on. For R_j the status is being justified, giving the sequence: the belief that P, the belief that (the belief that P) is justified, the belief that (the belief that (the belief that P) is justified) is justified, and so on. Neither regress terminates, and neither contains a contradiction.

(3) R_t is to come out benign and R_j vicious on the following diagnosis: an infinite regress is vicious just when, at every stage, the task required for the status in question is handed on to the next stage and never completed, and benign when the status of each stage is already settled by something available independently of any further stage.

After presenting the two regresses, do the following. Run through each chain stage by stage, saying for each stage what task must be accomplished for it to have its status and whether that task is discharged there or deferred. Then state, as precisely as you can, the single feature that is doing the diagnostic work, and test it by transplanting that feature into the other chain; tell me whether the verdict flips and why. Finally, give the strongest reply available to a committed infinitist about justification, and say honestly whether that reply shows that the original diagnosis draws the line between viciousness and benignity in the wrong place.
