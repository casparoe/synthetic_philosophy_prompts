Here is roughly where I've landed:

P1. Substrate neutrality about consciousness. Whether a system is conscious depends only on its causal-functional organization, not on what its parts are made of.

P2. If a nation's citizens were organized so that their aggregate signaling implemented the causal-functional profile of a human brain, the resulting system would be conscious, even though no individual citizen would be.

P3. An actual ant colony is not conscious. For all its division of labor, stigmergy, and homeostatic regulation, it lacks the kind of global integration a mind requires, and there is no single subject over and above the ants.

P4. There is nothing it is like to be an individual ant; the ant's flexible navigational and communicative behavior does not force us to attribute a subjective point of view to it.

P5. What separates conscious systems from mere feedback systems is architectural: a brain has dense, recurrent, globally integrated processing, whereas an ant colony, a market, and the internet are mostly modular or weakly coupled at the macro level, even if their component behavior is sophisticated.

P6. Nevertheless, I reject the idea that integration is a matter of degree all the way down: a thermostat is not even minimally conscious, and there is a sharp threshold somewhere between thermostat and brain.

P7. I am a physicalist about all of this: consciousness is a feature of physical systems, and no non-physical ingredient is added.
