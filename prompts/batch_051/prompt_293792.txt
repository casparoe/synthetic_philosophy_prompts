I got into an argument with my brother about whether it even matters what goals a super smart AI starts out with, because (he says) values just drift anyway once you're dealing with minds that can rewrite themselves and timescales of thousands of years. He doesn't have a name for it — value drift, goal drift, something like that — but his line was: humans can't even edit their own reward circuits and look how much our values have shifted in a few centuries, so imagine what happens to anything that can. I said no, if you build the first one with the right goals, the rightness is baked into the design and it compounds rather than rots. We ended at him giving it 0.7 that the long-run future is shaped by minds whose values we'd now find abhorrent or totally alien, and me around 0.3.

So give me your actual credence on the claim I actually care about: conditional on the long-run future being dominated by machine intelligences or our far-off descendants, and assuming for the sake of argument that things start from a decent place, it ends up with values we would now call bad, or so alien that good and bad stop applying. I want a number, but mainly I want the reasoning taken apart: how much rides on values being the kind of thing that stays put under self-modification, how much on there being some fact about good that any sufficiently advanced mind would converge toward, how much on whether you could lock in the starting values through design or institutions, and whatever other terms you think belong. Then tell me which of those sub-questions the final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move you the most. Don't cop out and say nobody can know — give me your best guesses.
