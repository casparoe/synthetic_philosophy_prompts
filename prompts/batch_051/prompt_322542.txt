Our academic-integrity handbook has a short clause about AI that I have been turning over all semester: "A student may use generative AI on an assignment only after completing a good-faith attempt at the work independently; every such use must be disclosed, and the student may not present AI-generated text or calculations as their own." My friend seems to follow it to the letter. Each night he genuinely grapples with the calculus problem set first and writes out a full attempt; only then does he open a language model to locate his mistakes and explain the concept behind each correction. He reworks the problem himself, appends a record of every exchange, and never submits generated work. At first this looked like exactly the use the clause was written to allow, but by now his homework scores have climbed while his in-class work has collapsed. Unless the model prompts him, he can no longer start even a moderately unfamiliar problem, because over the term the model has quietly taken over the recovery-from-confusion part of learning. The drafters plainly had in mind students who would use AI to generate or polish the thing they hand in, and he does not do that. Still, it seems strange to say the clause simply permits a practice that is defeating the point of the assignments.

Treat the handbook clause as a real rule and my friend's semester-long pattern as a case the drafters did not anticipate.
