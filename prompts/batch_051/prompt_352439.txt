Before I read any further in the interpretive literature on gauge theories, I should like the specification of the problem written down explicitly. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of what is physically real in a gauge theory must meet, with the reasoning behind each requirement.

Work with the following characterisation so that the exercise is self-contained. In a local gauge theory, such as classical electromagnetism or a Yang–Mills theory, the formalism contains more degrees of freedom than the physics appears to require. In electromagnetism the four-potential A_μ may be shifted by a gradient, A_μ → A_μ + ∂_μ λ, without changing the electric and magnetic fields, because the field strength F_μν is invariant under the shift; different-looking potentials can therefore represent one and the same physical state. In the non-abelian case the field strength transforms covariantly rather than being strictly invariant, and the gauge-invariant data are carried by loop observables such as Wilson loops. The Aharonov–Bohm effect complicates any simple claim that the potential is mere notation: an electron interference pattern depends on the line integral of the potential around a closed loop even where the magnetic field vanishes along the accessible path, so information not encoded in local field strengths is nonetheless empirically significant. Interpretations accordingly divide between those that regard gauge-variant degrees of freedom as pure redundancy and those that accord some physical status to the connection, or to the loop phases, in some form.

I want the requirements list itself, not a survey of the literature. It should address at least the following: which configurations may and may not be counted as distinct physical possibilities; how empirical predictions and gauge-invariant observables are to be accommodated; how the Aharonov–Bohm effect and loop phases are to be handled; how gauge redundancy is to be distinguished from substantive symmetries; what a theory must say, if anything, about locality and about non-trivial topology; and how far the account is required to extend from the classical theory to the quantised one.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark it either non-negotiable or a preference of a particular interpretive camp. Then say which requirement or pairing of requirements does the most eliminative work among the available positions, and state whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given the physics — that last part is what I most want to see. Do not provide citations or external references; treat the exercise as self-contained. Please write the answer in British English.
