I've been chewing on an idea for a while and I want somebody to actually stress-test it instead of just nodding along. I call it the Rewrite View of causation. The basic thought is that nothing in the world actually pushes, pulls, heats, breaks, or does anything to anything else. What actually happens is that at every moment the whole world is made fresh, by something outside the world (I'll just say God, though I don't think the exact word matters), and the new moment is mostly a copy of the old one with whatever changes the pattern calls for. So when cotton touches flame and then burns, the flame doesn't make it burn. The sequence is more like a habit: cotton-touching-flame has always, up to now, been followed by burning-cotton in the next moment, so God (or the rewriting process) keeps things consistent. We call the flame the cause only because it reliably comes right before, not because it has any power in it. The hands of a clock have about as much power to make it chime at noon as a match has to start a fire.

I like this because it makes the mind-body thing feel less magical (willing my arm to move is no weirder than flame-then-burning, it's just another stitch in the pattern), and because it takes the pressure off the idea that matter secretly contains little forces nobody has ever seen. But I'm aware it sounds insane: the glass didn't break because I dropped it? Science would just be a catalogue of habits? And if God ever changed the habit, would the past become a lie? I genuinely can't tell if this is a real theory or just a fancy way of saying causes aren't real with extra steps. Has anyone actually defended something like this under a name I wouldn't know? And give me the three strongest objections, including whichever one is most likely to be fatal rather than just weird.
