I've been turning over an argument about sphexishness and free will, and I cant tell whether I've found a real problem or just pulled a fast one on myself. Here's the setup. A sphex wasp will drag a paralyzed cricket to the mouth of its burrow, go inside to check that all's clear, come back out, and then drag the cricket in. If you move the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp will come out, drag the cricket back to the mouth, and do the whole inspection routine again. It will repeat this loop indefinitely rather than just pull the cricket in. People usually take this to show the wasp is a fixed program, with no real flexibility.

Now, my argument:

(1) What makes the wasp's behavior sphexish is that it is rigid under changed circumstances: the same steps fire even when the world has changed so as to make them pointless.

(2) But any behavior at all can be described as a step, or subroutine, that fires when its triggering condition is met. A person who notices the moved cricket is just running a more complicated subroutine, something like if current input doesn't match cached input, re-check.

(3) So if being sphexish is just being such that your behavior can be described in terms of triggered subroutines, then the person is sphexish too, just with a bigger lookup table.

(4) The only difference between wasp and person, on this picture, is the number and nesting of subroutines, not any difference in kind.

(5) Therefore, no amount of behavioral flexibility could ever show that a human acts freely in a way the wasp doesn't.
