You're a Heidegger specialist, someone who's taught the first division of Being and Time long enough that ready-to-hand and present-at-hand are second nature. I want you to think carefully, and maybe brainstorm a couple of angles before committing.

Here's the counterfactual, made precise. Starting tomorrow, in our actual world, every human-made piece of equipment becomes indestructible and can never malfunction, wear out, jam, crash, or stop working, no matter how it's used or misused. It can still be lost, forgotten, hidden, borrowed, withheld, or otherwise unavailable, and natural things are unchanged — a rock can still fall on your head, a tree can still rot. So the tool itself can never break down.

Now in the standard reading of Being and Time, it's exactly when the hammer is too heavy or the handle comes loose that the thing flips from ready-to-hand to present-at-hand, and the whole referential totality of the workshop lights up. So my question is this: if tool-breakdown is impossible, what happens to Dasein's relation to equipment? Does readiness-to-hand ever get noticed? Does the present-at-hand ever arise through equipment at all, or only through natural objects? Would missing tools, unfamiliar tools, tools held by someone else, or tools that are present but not mine do the same disclosive work that breakdown does, or would they disclose something different? Give me the chain of reasoning, marking where you're confident, where you're guessing, and what would have to be true about Dasein for your prediction to be wrong.
