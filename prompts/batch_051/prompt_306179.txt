An archaeologist you follow has published the following interpretations of excavated sites:

(1) A large building with a central hearth and benches, no prestige goods, no storage: she judged it a communal assembly hall of an egalitarian settlement, not a chief's residence.

(2) A small room filled with stamped clay sealings, standardized weights, and grain jars, no hearth or benches: she judged it an administrative depot of a redistributive authority, not a private merchant's storehouse.

(3) Hundreds of identical, deliberately broken clay figurines, some in houses and some in a pit: she judged them evidence of a shared household ritual, not of an organized temple cult.

(4) A walled compound containing a stone seat and imported prestige goods, no storage: she judged it an elite residence indicating institutionalized hierarchy, not a communal building.

Her new excavation is a single large roofed building with a central hearth and benches, rows of standardized weights and stamped sealings, no prestige goods, no throne, and one deliberately broken figurine set into the doorway.

Predict what she should conclude if she wants to remain consistent with her earlier judgments. State the candidate principles that fit her four prior interpretations, say what each principle predicts about this building, identify the closest earlier case, and give a best-guess verdict. Then specify the further finding at this site that would most sharply discriminate between the remaining principles.
