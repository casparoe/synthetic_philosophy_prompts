Having retired from a career in physical chemistry, I have recently been reading epistemology seriously. I have provisionally settled on four positions and would like a consistency audit before presenting them to my discussion group.

(1) Knowledge requires true belief produced by a reliable cognitive process, absent defeaters — a reliabilist picture.
(2) Knowledge is closed under competent deduction: if I know P and come to believe Q by competently deducing Q from P, then I know Q.
(3) Perception can yield knowledge directly, without any prior knowledge that the perceptual process is reliable. Seeing a ripe tomato in good light can by itself give me knowledge that the tomato is red.
(4) I also hold that the following route yields no knowledge: look at many colored objects, know on each occasion that the object is the color it appears to be, infer from this that one's color vision got that case right, and then generalize to the claim that one's color vision is generally reliable. This procedure strikes me as unacceptably circular.

Please work through the set: which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, how severe is each tension, and what is the least costly revision? For each tension, tell me specifically what would have to be given up and what it would cost.
