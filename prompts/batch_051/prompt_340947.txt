The two camps in my reading group — call them the Definitional camp and the Physical camp — have been arguing for months about the Church-Turing thesis. The Definitional camp holds that the thesis is a conceptual analysis of “effectively calculable function”: it specifies what we should mean by computation, so no physical result could refute it. The Physical camp holds that the thesis is an empirical claim about the physical world: every finitely realizable physical process can be simulated by a Turing machine, and it is falsifiable by a proposed hypercomputational device. Write the joint statement they should have produced, and begin with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary. Include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims; (2) a disagreements table with exact statements of residual disagreement, phrased so each camp accepts its own wording; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both camps agree would bear on it, with each camp’s prediction recorded in advance. Pay particular attention to separating the mathematical Church-Turing thesis from the physical Church-Turing thesis, and to whether “computable by a Turing machine” and “simulable by a Turing machine” are the same criterion in the two camps’ formulations.
