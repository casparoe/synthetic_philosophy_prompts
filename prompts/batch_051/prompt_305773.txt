I am a sixth-form student preparing an essay for a school philosophy competition, and the topic is whether grit, fortitude, and resilience are best understood as virtues or as an ideology. In my reading around the subject I have assembled a list of positions I am inclined to hold, and I would like an audit of the set before I commit myself in the essay. The positions are these:

(1) Grit, by which I mean a disposition to sustain effort and interest in long-term goals despite obstacles, and resilience, by which I mean the capacity to recover one's footing after setbacks, are real features of people rather than mere labels, and both are learnable and usually worth cultivating.

(2) Whether a particular case of perseverance is admirable depends on what it is persevering towards: someone who persists in an abusive relationship, in a fraudulent scheme, or in a self-destructive obsession is not made admirable by her persistence.

(3) Grit and resilience are therefore only instrumentally valuable; whatever value they have comes from the ends they serve.

(4) Even so, schools are right to encourage pupils to persist with difficult work even when a pupil could reasonably stop and even when the specific task seems pointless to her, because the disposition to persist is general and transfers across contexts.

(5) By the same token, a person who endures poverty or discrimination without falling apart deserves to be called resilient and is to that extent admirable, even when her endurance is not aimed at any further goal beyond keeping going.

(6) At the same time, the emphasis on grit in education sometimes functions as an ideology: it explains outcomes in terms of individual character and thereby deflects attention from institutions, policies, and material conditions.

(7) I do not take (6) to conflict with the earlier points, because a trait can be genuinely admirable when exercised by an individual even while public talk of that trait is being used ideologically to protect an unjust order; those claims operate at different levels.

(8) Finally, fortitude should be distinguished from grit because fortitude includes judgement about what is worth bearing, and for that reason fortitude is a virtue in a way that grit taken on its own is not.

I cannot tell where the set is strained, and I should like to know before I build the essay on it. Please go through these commitments in pairs, say which pairs genuinely conflict and which only appear to, rank any genuine conflicts by severity, and identify for each tension the least costly revision or qualification that would restore consistency, including what would have to be given up.
