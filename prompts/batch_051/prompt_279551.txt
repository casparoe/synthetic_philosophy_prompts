I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on paradoxes and probability, 18 students, in one 75-minute session. Design a runnable in-class game that makes students feel the pull of the two-envelope paradox before they analyze it: ideally they formulate the tempting expected-value argument themselves — that the other envelope is worth 5/4 of what they hold, on the assumption that it is equally likely to contain double or half — and then collide with the symmetry problem that an always-switch strategy cannot beat sticking. The game should use points or tokens rather than money. Specify the rules, how the paired amounts are generated across rounds, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened in the game onto the implicit prior over possible amounts and the mistake of treating double and half as equally likely conditional on seeing one's own amount. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be candid about which feature of the paradox the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply.
