I am a high school student trying to understand the debate over what concepts are — the mental resources by which we group things under categories such as BIRD, GAME, EVEN NUMBER, or JUSTICE. I keep encountering three proposals: prototype theories, exemplar theories, and the theory theory. In the material I have read, each proposal is usually defended by pointing at one or two favorite cases, which strikes me as a poor way to settle anything. Before I take any side, I want the full specification of what a theory of concepts is actually required to get right.

Give me a requirements list: the phenomena a theory must explain, the cases it must handle, and the distinctions it cannot collapse. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration and explain why it is a genuine requirement rather than a quirk, a convenience, or the property of only one research program. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable or only approximate, and which single requirement does the most to eliminate or seriously embarrass the leading theories. Finally, tell me whether the requirements are jointly satisfiable by any one theory; if some subset of them conflicts, I would like the conflict identified explicitly.

The stress cases I especially want the list to address include: typicality effects (a robin is judged a better example of BIRD than a penguin, and is categorized faster); categories with vague or contested boundaries; the fact that the category PET FISH does not seem to be predictable from the categories PET and FISH in any simple way; ad hoc categories assembled on the spot, such as things to remove from a burning house; concepts of nonexistent or impossible things; apparently rule-defined categories like EVEN NUMBER; and the way children's understanding of a concept such as ALIVE changes over development. I would also like the analysis to keep apart membership in a category, typicality within it, the linguistic label for it, and the process of applying it, unless there is a specific argument for collapsing some of those.
