They don't wince, they don't pull away, and they don't ask for painkillers. This completley wrecked my old definition, which was basically that pain is whatever hurts, so now I'm trying to do better.

3. The pain asymbolia case above: the person can describe and rate the sensation perfectly well but is completely indifferent to it.
4. A patient on opioids for a broken leg who says they can still feel the throbbing, but it doesn't hurt or bother them.
5. Someone in acute grief who says it physically hurts in their chest.
6. A person with phantom limb pain after an amputation, described as a burning cramp in the missing hand.
7. An itch from poison ivy that you can't scratch.
8. A marathoner at mile 20 describing the good burn in her legs, which she insists is painful but also satisfying.

Run your definition through all of them, revise it or defend any weird verdicts it gives, and say where it gives no answer if that happens.
