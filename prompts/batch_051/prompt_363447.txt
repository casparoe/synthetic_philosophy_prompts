A friend of mine recently read a book on comparative economic development and gave me this summary. The reason some nations are rich and others poor is, at bottom, institutions; geography and culture are at most secondary, and by now largely ruled out. The decisive evidence comes from colonial natural experiments: where European settlers faced low disease mortality they built inclusive institutions that protected property and constrained government, and where they faced high mortality they built extractive institutions designed only to siphon off resources; the former places are rich today and the latter poor. Since the disease environment was assigned independently of local institutions, the comparison qualifies as a natural experiment, so we can conclude that institutions, not geography or culture, cause prosperity. I am uneasy about the words natural experiment and cause in the last step, but I cannot pinpoint where the summary overreaches. Could you walk through it and separate what is well supported, what is overstated, and whether the inference from colonial history to institutions causing prosperity really goes through?
