I design software interfaces, and I keep coming back to a worry that grew out of trying to understand Otto Neurath. As I understand it, Neurath believed that a picture language could cross national boundaries and do some of the work that ordinary words do badly, but only if its pictures were drawn from a small, controlled set of elements and arranged by something like an explicit visual grammar. His Isotype system was meant to be planned from the top down: a few stable conventions, simplified forms, no decorative clutter, and a clear separation between literal depiction and symbolism. Emoji, app icons, and the universal pictograms one sees in airports and on road signs have become the closest thing we have to an international picture language, but they developed mostly from the bottom up. Their meanings shift across platforms and years, they mix literal faces with metaphors and cultural references, and they often depend on surrounding text or a particular social context to be read correctly. I want to know where Neurath would have located the promise and the failure if he could have seen emoji. Would he have treated them as a degenerate descendant of his project, as an entirely different kind of sign system, or as evidence that his demand for centralized control was misguided? Base the prediction on his known commitments about how pictures mean, not on a vague guess about his personality, and say what specific features of emoji would most clearly satisfy or violate those commitments.
