You are a professor of philosophy of law setting the final essay question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on free will, moral responsibility, and punishment. The seminar has covered the debate between Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso over whether punishment can be justified by just deserts. In brief: Dennett holds that the compatibilist capacities ordinary adults actually have—self-control, responsiveness to reasons, the ability to do otherwise in the sense that matters—are enough to ground desert-based blame and punishment. Caruso holds that free will skepticism undermines basic-desert moral responsibility, and that we should therefore abandon retributive punishment in favor of a forward-looking public-health/quarantine model.

Your task is to write one exam question on this debate that a student could answer well in about 1,500 words. The question must be impossible to answer well by merely summarizing the two positions. It should force students to confront whether Dennett's compatibilist capacities actually secure the kind of responsibility that Caruso targets, and whether Caruso's quarantine model can avoid covertly relying on desert judgments or treating offenders as mere threats to be managed. Think through what the true crux of the disagreement is before you write the question.

Then provide:

1. A model answer of about 800 words.
2. A marking scheme worth 20 marks, built around five must-hit points. For each point, give the marks it is worth and a one-line note on how candidates typically get it wrong.

Make sure the model answer and the marking scheme track specific philosophical moves rather than memorized labels, and that the difference between an A and a B answer is visible in the marking scheme.
