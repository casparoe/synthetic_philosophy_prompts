Hofstadter's response, as I remember it, is to rerun the exact same argument with an embedded group marker that the reader cannot treat as unmarked, forcing the reader to notice that they accept the argument for one group but not the other. I don't have the text to hand, so don't quote that passage back to me verbatum; the question is about the structure of the move rather than the exact words.

It lives in conditional probabilities. Does Hofstadter's argument survive this?
