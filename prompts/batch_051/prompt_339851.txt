I've been collecting my friend's verdicts about whether side effects count as intentional, and they're stable enough that I want to predict her next one before she sees it. Her track record so far:

- In Joshua Knobe's original chairman side-effect vignette, where the chairman says he only cares about profit and doesn't care that the project will harm the environment, she says the harming was intentional.
- In the matching case where the side effect helps the environment instead, she says the helping was not intentional.
- A factory manager picks a cheaper disposal method to save money, knowing it will taint a local river, and doesn't care about the river. She says the tainting was intentional.
- A factory manager picks a more expensive disposal method, knowing it will also filter the local river, but only because the new equipment is easier to maintain. She says the filtering was not intentional.

The new case: a man is driving and swerves to avoid hitting a child who ran into the street. He knows the swerve will hit a parked motorcycle and damage it. He doesn't care about the motorcycle; he just wants to avoid the child. 

I want to know what she'd say about the motorcycle damage, and more importantly, what rule she seems to be running. I can see at least three rules that fit her earlier verdicts: (a) any bad side effect is intentional, full stop; (b) a side effect is intentional when it's wrongful or norm-violating, not merely harmful; (c) a side effect is intentional when the agent is blameworthy for allowing it. The motorcycle case splits these: (a) says intentional, while (b) and (c) say not intentional because the swerve was a justified evasive action and he isn't blameworthy. But her earlier verdicts may underdetermine which rule she actually holds.

Return a JSON object with the following fields:

- predicted_verdict: your best guess of whether she'll say intentional, not intentional, or genuinely unclear, with a brief justification.
- candidate_rules: an array of objects, one per rule that fits her track record, each with the fields rule, predicted_verdict_for_motorcycle_case, and how_it_fits_the_earlier_verdicts.
- closest_precedent: which of her earlier cases is the best analog to the motorcycle case, and why.
- adopted_principle: if forced to deliver a verdict on her behalf, what principle would she be adopting?
- tiebreaker_case: a further case where the candidate rules would diverge but that doesn't just restate the motorcycle case, with the different predictions each rule makes.
