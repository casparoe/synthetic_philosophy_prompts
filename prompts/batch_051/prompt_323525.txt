I've come to hold a fairly strong view about chain-of-thought in language models, and I'd rather have it stress-tested than defended. The view is that when a model emits an explicit line of reasoning before an answer, that reasoning is usually a rationalization — a plausible, post hoc verbal reconstruction — rather than an honest or causally accurate report of how the answer was produced. My grounds are threefold: the model has no privileged introspective access to its own activations or computations; the explanation tokens are selected by the same next-token-prediction process as everything else; and we have cases where biasing features change a model's answers while the explanation never mentions them, which looks like confabulation rather than reportage. On this picture, a chain of thought is closer to what a person says when asked, after the fact, why she did something than to a trace of the underlying computation.

What I worry about is that I've built a view with a built-in escape hatch: every apparent counterexample can be waved away as "mere post hoc consistency," which would make my view unfalsifiable in practice. So I want you to tell me what would count against it, concretely. What would have to be true for chain-of-thought to be generally faithful — or at least causally load-bearing rather than decorative? Which experimental results, architectural facts, intervention patterns, or error patterns should most move me off my position, and which alleged counterexamples should a sophisticated defender of my view rightly dismiss, and on what grounds? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say honestly whether my view as stated has been insulated against refutation, and whether that insulation is itself a problem for a claim of this kind.

Two ground rules. First, separate what is settled from what remains genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus. Second, keep the response under 750 words and lead with the strongest counterevidence rather than preliminaries. For calibration: I find causal-intervention evidence the most intuitively compelling avenue against my view, so tell me explicitly whether I'm over- or under-weighting it.
