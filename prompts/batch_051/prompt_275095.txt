Analyze the 1922 encounter between Albert Einstein and Henri Bergson at the Société française de philosophie as a case study for the Rietdijk–Putnam argument and the block universe picture. Begin by laying out the structure of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument: what the relativity of simultaneity is taken to establish about events that one observer treats as future, another as present, and another as past, and exactly what metaphysical conclusion about the equal reality of all spacetime regions is supposed to follow. Then apply that framework to the Einstein–Bergson disagreement. Who in that debate actually occupies which position relative to the block view? Does Einstein’s insistence that physics has no use for the philosopher’s time amount to an endorsement of the block universe, or is it a narrower methodological claim that leaves the metaphysics unsettled? What would the Rietdijk–Putnam argument imply about the lived duration Bergson placed at the center of his account, and about the privileged now he took philosophical reflection to reveal? Identify the places where the argument genuinely explains the exchange and the places where it misdescribes it. In particular, consider whether Bergson’s objection is best read as a denial of the block premise, a denial of the inference from relativity to the block, or a charge that the argument abstracts away from the standpoint of consciousness. Conclude by saying what the episode reveals about the strengths and limitations of treating the Rietdijk–Putnam argument as a direct demonstration of the block universe.
