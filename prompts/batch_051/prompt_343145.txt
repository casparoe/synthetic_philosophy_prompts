For a term paper in my civics class I am reverse-engineering the theory of distributive justice my teacher applies when she judges policy cases, since she never names the theory but hands down verdicts each week. I have recorded five verdicts so far (I have simplified them to income effects only):

- A reform that raised every income group by the same absolute amount per year: just.
- A reform that gave the poorest decile a substantial gain while imposing modest losses only on the top decile: just, because it raised the income floor.
- A reform that raised total income substantially by taking a small amount from the poorest decile and giving very large gains to the top decile: unjust.
- A reform that gave the poorest decile a modest gain, left the middle deciles unchanged, and permitted very large gains at the top: just, since the worst-off group still ended up better off.
- A reform that would have produced perfect equality by lowering everyone to the current income of the poorest decile, helping no one and harming everyone else: unjust.

Next week she will rule on a case I have not heard her discuss. It is a transfer from the second-poorest decile to the poorest decile. The average income of the poorest decile would rise by $800 per year, the average income of the second-poorest decile would fall by $1,500 per year (part of the loss is administrative waste, not a direct transfer), and everyone else would be unchanged; the second-poorest decile would still remain above the poorest. Everyone in both affected deciles now lives below the poverty line, and no alternative on the table would raise the poorest decile by more.

Predict her verdict, and say which theory best fits her record as a whole: Rawls's difference principle (a maximin choice from behind the veil of ignorance: inequalities are justified only if they make the least advantaged group as well off as possible), prioritarianism (benefits to the worse off carry extra weight, but trade-offs are permitted), sufficientarianism (justice requires getting everyone above a threshold), or something else. Say what each live theory would predict about the transfer case, give the prediction you would defend and your reasons for preferring it, and then describe the one additional policy case that would most cleanly separate the leading theory from its strongest remaining rival. Finally, tell me what would change your mind: which consideration about her record or about the transfer case would most likely show that your prediction was wrong, and where your reasoning is least certain.
