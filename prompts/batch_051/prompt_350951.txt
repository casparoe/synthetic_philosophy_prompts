I have been reading James Legge’s translation of Zhuangzi’s “The Adjustment of Controversies” (齊物論), and two passages in the same chapter seem to me to conflict. The first is this:

When we sleep, the soul communicates with (what is external to us); when we awake, the body is set free.

But later in the same chapter, Zhuangzi recounts dreaming that he was a butterfly, fluttering about and unaware that he was Zhou; upon waking, he is plainly Zhou again, yet he then says he cannot tell whether he was Zhou dreaming of being a butterfly or is now a butterfly dreaming of being Zhou. The first passage appears to treat waking as a release or liberation, as if it returns us to something solid and unproblematic. The second appears to deny that any waking state can certify itself as final. I want to know whether these two passages can be reconciled, and at what cost. Does the first passage make an epistemological claim about wakefulness at all, or does it describe something narrower — perhaps only the body’s release from its nightly commerce with things? If it does make an epistemological claim, does the butterfly passage deliberately undercut it? Please give the strongest candidate reconciliations and then a verdict on which reading is most faithful to the text rather than special pleading.
