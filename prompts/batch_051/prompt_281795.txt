Last night I spent hours arguing with a friend about whether first-past-the-post is merely a different compromise or a standing betrayal of the idea of representative government. I kept reaching for John Stuart Mill's argument in Of True and False Democracy, the chapter of Considerations on Representative Government in which he grants that the majority must in the end prevail, but denies that this requires the majority to carry every seat; a scheme in which the largest group takes everything while everyone else is represented by nobody is, on his account, not democracy's principle but a perversion of it. I want that argument made vivid without the abstractions my friend kept dismissing as a fairness intuition.

Write a villanelle in the voice of a supporter of proportional representation who has just come from such an argument. The two refrains should be, on one side, the recurrence of the wasted vote under first-past-the-post, and on the other, the persistence of the winner-take-all threshold at which votes are discarded. Use the villanelle's pattern — the way the refrains keep returning and finally lock together in the closing couplet — to enact how the same distortion repeats district by district and how the wasted vote and the threshold are two aspects of a single structural fact. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the Millian argument recognizes the distinction between the majority prevailing and the majority taking every seat, and it should not simply name-drop Mill.
