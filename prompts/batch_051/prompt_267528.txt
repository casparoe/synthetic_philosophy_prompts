I have been trying to pin down what "side effect" means in discussions of low-impact AI. The motivation is that an AI system might be told to avoid side effects or to keep its impact low, and one would like that instruction to be implementable without first handing the system a complete account of human values. But whenever I try to make the notion precise, I find the definition either leans on a prior notion of what the system intends, or on a background measure of which changes matter, and both seem to smuggle in exactly the value specification the idea was meant to avoid. So I want a working definition of "side effect", or, if no single definition works, a definition of "avoiding side effects" or "low impact" that a designer could apply without already knowing what anyone cares about.

Test the definition on the following cases. (1) A warehouse robot reaches for a bin and knocks a coworker's mug off a shelf. I take this to be a paradigm side effect. (2) A chess engine deliberately sacrifices a bishop as part of a forced mating sequence; the bishop's capture is part of the plan. I would not want to call this a side effect, but I worry that ruling it out requires an appeal to intention or objective. (3) A navigation app reroutes a driver to save her four minutes, sending her through a residential street and delaying a resident by thirty seconds. (4) An AI tutor explains a concept to a student, who later uses it to start a small business and, separately, to end a relationship; which, if any, of those later developments are side effects of the tutoring? I genuinely do not know. (5) A trading algorithm places a small order and the order itself moves the market price by a fraction of a cent for all participants. (6) A robot in a sealed room flips a bit in a log file that is never read and affects no later computation; I also have no firm view here, and I am unsure whether the absence of further consequences should matter to the definition. (7) A drone shades its own solar panel to stay cool, reducing its power output by a small amount and thereby raising grid emissions negligibly.

Please propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, revising it or defending the surprising verdicts as you go. End by saying which of these cases, pushed harder from a different angle, would be most likely to make you abandon the definition, and what consideration would force the revision.
