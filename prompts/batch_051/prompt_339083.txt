ok so im taking this music production class and my friend keeps insisting that a recording of a song is just a recording of the song — like the song itself is the real thing, some kind of abstract object, and every performance or recording is just a copy or instance of it. but i dont think that can be right, or at least its way too simple. a studio track isnt really a record of anyone playing the song: its built out of dozens of takes, punch-ins, tuning, layers that were never performed together, sometimes sounds no human could ever produce live. so what actually is the thing on the file? and then theres the cover problem: if someone reharmonizes the whole song and keeps only the lyrics, is it still the same song? where does the original work stop and a new one begin?

so the thing i want is an argument for a specific position: that a studio recording is (or at least can be) a distinct musical work in its own right, not just a token or instance of some underlying song. use the type/token distinction carefully, and maybe bring in the contrast between arts where a copy can be forged (like a painting) and arts where any correct performance counts (like a piece of music). also deal with the strongest objection to the position: if every recording is its own work, then what makes a cover a cover of the same song, and why do we still talk about the same song at all? max 900 words, and please actually commit to the position instead of ending on a wishy-washy it depends.
