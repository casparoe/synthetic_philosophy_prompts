I retired a few years ago from a career in physical chemistry and have since been reading political philosophy, mostly on the question whether compatriots have special claims on one another. I bring this up because I now sit on the board of a small charitable trust, the Halvorsen Fund, established by a late colleague, which awards one postdoctoral fellowship each year in the natural sciences. The founding instrument directs that the income be used to advance scientific research, and in a separate recital it expresses the founder's wish that the Fund assist young investigators of our own country. For many years the board read those two passages together and gave an informal preference to domestic applicants, even when an international application was somewhat stronger. This year the gap is unusually wide. The top-ranked proposal comes from a researcher based in another country, and the work would be carried out there; the strongest domestic proposal is respectable but plainly weaker. Some board members argue that the domestic applicant has a special claim simply in virtue of shared citizenship, since the endowment was built from wealth generated in this country and since supporting domestic researchers strengthens our own scientific workforce. Others reply that the Fund's governing purpose is scientific progress, which does not stop at borders, and that awarding the fellowship to a weaker domestic proposal would misuse the founder's gift. We must decide next month, and I have been asked to circulate a short written opinion in advance. Please draft that opinion for me. It should recommend a concrete course of action—award to the international applicant, award to the domestic applicant, or some standing policy the Fund should adopt—and give reasons the board could vote on, engaging honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side rather than caricaturing it. I would like the opinion set out as exactly five numbered points, each no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, and with the recommendation stated first.
