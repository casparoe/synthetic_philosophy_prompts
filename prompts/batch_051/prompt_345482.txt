I'm drafting a statement of my probabilistic commitments for a small forecasting-and-epistemics group, and I'd like an audit of whether the set hangs together before I circulate it. I have absorbed the standard rationalist case for Bayesianism over the years and have ended up with six positions, each of which I can defend on its own but which I have never checked for mutual compatibility:

1. I am a subjectivist about degrees of belief. The only constraints rationality imposes are the probability axioms and conditionalization; there are no rationally mandatory priors, and two agents starting from different priors can both be perfectly rational.
2. I believe in objective physical chances of the single-case propensity kind. A particular atom of a radioactive isotope has a real, mind-independent chance of decaying within one half-life, roughly one-half, and this is not merely a fact about how similar atoms behave in the long run.
3. I hold that known chances constrain credence. If you know the objective chance of E is x and you have no inadmissible information (roughly, no information about the outcome independent of the chance setup), your credence in E should be x.
4. I think long-run frequentist error control tracks something real. The coverage guarantee of a 95 percent confidence procedure, or the controlled false-positive rate of a significance test, reflects a kind of reliability that a story told purely in terms of posterior probabilities seems to me to miss.
5. I reject the identification of probabilities with frequencies. A frequency is at most evidence bearing on an underlying chance, not itself a probability.
6. I think the probabilities we assign to one-off, macro-level events — this candidate winning the next election, this patient recovering — are not discoverable mind-independent quantities. They are our considered degrees of belief, revisable with evidence.

I am not asking for a lecture on which interpretation of probability is right. Go through the six and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. Flag any pair that only looks as though it conflicts and explain why it dissolves. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be, what it costs, and which other commitments it would disturb. If one revision would clean up the most at once, tell me which one it is.
