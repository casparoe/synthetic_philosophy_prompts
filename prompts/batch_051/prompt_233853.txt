I run an occasional workshop for software engineers at my company: roughly fifteen participants and a single 90-minute slot. I want an activity that makes them feel the force of the idea that human values may be many rather than one thing in disguise. Most of the room is used to treating any set of desiderata as in principle compressible into a single scalar objective to be optimised, and I want a game or structured exercise in which that assumption visibly breaks before it is ever named. Give me rules, roles, and a payoff scheme that needs no real money, in which participants are forced to make trade-offs between several apparently distinct goods and run into the point where no single score seems to capture what they actually endorse. Then give me a debrief that maps what happened onto three positions: many values as surface appearance over one underlying objective, a strict priority ordering, and values that genuinely do not fit on a common scale. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong on a first run and how to handle it, and be explicit about which part of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and I will need to supply myself. Please write the answer in British English.
