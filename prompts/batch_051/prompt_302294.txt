Here's an asymmetry I keep going back and forth on. Suppose I know an embarrassing fact about you that I'd be within my rights to post online—it's true and I didn't learn it through anything shady. If I post it, that's maybe mean but basically allowed. If I ask you for money, no big deal; people ask each other for money all the time. But if I say "pay me five hundred or I post it," that's blackmail—seriously wrong, a crime, everyone gets it. The part I cant make sense of is the comparison with selling the same fact to a gossip site for five hundred, where they'll post it. Pretty much everyone treats that as a bit sleazy maybe, but nothing like blackmail. Yet in that case the secret actually comes out, which is your actual harm, while in the blackmail case, if you pay, nothing comes out at all. So why is the offer to stay quiet for money so much worse than the offer to talk for money? Is there a principled justification for that asymmetry, or are we just inconsistent—and if we are, which judgment should give way: should we be much harder on the paid source, or much less hard on the blackmailer?
