I just spent an hour arguing with a friend about whether some familiar conditions of aging should count as diseases, and we kept talking past each other. Build me a thought experiment that isolates the disagreement between a biostatistical naturalist about disease, in the tradition of Christopher Boorse, and a normativist about disease. The scenario should be an ordinary clinical case — no exotic pathology and no enhancement or science-fiction scenarios — and it should be one in which the naturalist denies that the person has a disease while the normativist affirms that they do. The divergence must fall out of the theories rather than from anyone misapplying them: the naturalist's verdict should trace to the relativization of normal function to age-and-sex reference classes, and the normativist's verdict should trace to the claim that the decisive issue is whether the condition is bad for the person's life. The purpose of the case is to show which structural commitment, not which surface intuition, drives the disagreement, so a scenario that simply stipulates the verdicts would not do. After presenting the scenario, state what each theory says about it and why, identify the single feature of the case doing the work, and say how the verdicts change if that feature is removed. End by explaining how a defender of the naturalist view would try to block the case, and tell me how confident you are in your rendering of the two positions, singling out the point you are least sure of.
