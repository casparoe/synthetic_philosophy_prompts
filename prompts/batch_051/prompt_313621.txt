I sit on my university's research council, and I chair the subcommittee that reviews our small pot of money for high-risk, high-reward interdisciplinary institutes. This term we have a proposal from a group of physicists and philosophers for a three-year center on what they call post-empirical physics: string theory, the landscape of possible vacua, and multiverse cosmology. The physics half wants to map that landscape and ask how many of the vacua look anything like our low-energy world. The philosophy half wants to study whether a theory can be rationally accepted on the strength of internal coherence, explanatory unification, and mathematical fertility alone, when the energies it posits are unreachable in any forseeable experiment. Half the subcommittee thinks this is exactly what the high-risk pot is for; they say the real output would be the epistemology, not the physics. The other half says its not merely risky: it is unfalsifiable in principle, and funding it would amount to the university endorsing a slide from physics into speculation with no obvious stopping point. I need to circulate a one-page memo before Thursday's meeting with a concrete recommendation -- fund all three years, fund only the philosophy half as a one-year pilot, or reject -- and I want it to actually engage the strongest version of the losing side, not just wave the falsifiability slogan around. What should the memo say?
