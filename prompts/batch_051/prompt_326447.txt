Our old quant-poker club had a rule I keep coming back to. Article 4(2) of the charter said:

"A member shall accept any exchange of assets offered to them whenever, by the member's own honest calculation, the exchange has a positive expected value."

We wrote it after a long fight about loss aversion, the idea being that members shouldn't turn down obviously good micro-bets just because losing felt worse than winning. Fine. But last week someone ran an envelope game the clause seems to get confused about. A trusted third party fills two envelopes so that one contains exactly twice the other, seals them, and hands one to me and one to another member. I open mine and find $100. The other member offers to swap envelope-for-envelope. By my own lights: conditional on having seen $100, I'd say there's a 1/2 chance mine is the bigger one (a swap gets me $50) and a 1/2 chance it's the smaller (a swap gets me $200), so the swap's EV is (1/2)($50) + (1/2)($200) = $125, which beats staying put at $100. Article 4(2) sure looks like it requires me to accept. But after the swap I'd be looking at either $50 or $200, and the same style of calculation says swapping back is positive EV: seeing $50, my expected value of the other envelope would be (1/2)($25) + (1/2)($100) = $62.50, and seeing $200, it would be (1/2)($100) + (1/2)($400) = $250. So the rule seems to require an endless back-and-forth every time someone offers the reverse. Worse, the member across from me can run the same calculation on his own envelope and conclude that swapping to mine is postive EV for him too. Since the total money in the two envelopes is fixed, we can't both come out ahead in realized terms—but the rule as written seems to demand each of us accepts whenever the other offers. It's the two-envelope paradox wearing a governance costume.

I co-wrote this rule, so I have some sense of what we would've said if asked, but I want the interpretation worked through honestly. What does Article 4(2) actually require in that envelope game? Run the four takes seperately: the plain words of the clause; its purpose as best you can reconstruct it; what the drafters would have said if asked about the game; and what a reasonable member reading the charter in good faith would take it to require. Then say where those answers come apart, and what that tells us about writing "expected value, by the member's own honest calculation" into a rule at all. Is the rule broken, is the calculation broken, or does the conditional-vs-unconditional EV distinction just do more work than we ever noticed when we drafted it?
