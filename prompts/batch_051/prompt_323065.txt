I've been rereading Mill and Nozick at the same time and arguing with a friend about whether Nozick's utility monster is a genuine objection to Mill's utilitarianism or just a lurid caricature of it. In Utilitarianism, Mill says that once you make the proper allowance for differences of kind among pleasures, Bentham's dictum can be written under the principle of utility as its explanatory commentary — the dictum being that in computing happiness every person is to be counted as one and nobody as more than one. In Anarchy, State, and Utopia, Nozick argues that utilitarianism is embarassed by the possibility of a utility monster: a creature who gets vastly more utility from any transfer of resources than ordinary people lose, with the result that maximizing total utility seems to require sacrificing everyone to it. Mill's remark looks like a commitment to equal standing; Nozick's scenario says the very same maximizing principle can demand radically unequal treatment.

I want to know whether these can be reconciled without turning Mill's dictum into a harmless slogan. Walk me through the candidate reconciliations — impartial counting versus equal outcomes, diminishing marginal utility, the higher/lower pleasure distinction, rule- versus act-utilitarian readings of Mill, side constraints, or anything else — and give a verdict on which reconciliations are honest and which are special pleading. Start with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Then tell me which consideration would most likely change your verdict, or where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
