In debates about whether a language model can produce genuinely novel work, I often encounter the claim that a model is like a novelist who has read an entire library and writes by recombining what she has absorbed; since human writers likewise do nothing more than rearrange inherited material, so the argument goes, calling the machine purely derivative proves nothing about its capacity for an intuitive leap beyond its training distribution. As a novelist, I find this comparison both intuitive and suspicious, and I would like it audited. Lay out the analogy's correspondences explicitly, work through the places where the mapping breaks, separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that are merely superficial, and then say whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, offering a better comparison if one is available.
