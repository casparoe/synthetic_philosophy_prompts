A human-rights organization I work with, drawing loosely on Kant's contrast between dignity and price, insists that human dignity must be understood as the foundation of the whole system of human rights: take away the foundation and the structure of rights has nothing left to stand on. A colleague dismisses this as exactly the kind of move Ruth Macklin criticized, claiming that dignity just restates respect for autonomy, so the supposed foundation is doing no load-bearing work and the rights framework would stand just as well without it. I would like an audit of the architectural analogy. Spell out what maps onto what between a building's foundation and the role dignity is being asked to play; identify where the mapping breaks down and which features of literal foundations are being imported without argument; then say whether the colleague's conclusion — that dignity talk can be dropped without loss — survives once the disanalogies are made explicit. If a better analogy captures what dignity discourse actually does, offer it. Please write the answer in American English.
