I work in the policy office of a coalition of small island and low-lying states, and several times a year a geoengineering proposal lands on our desk that requires us to take a public position: a private consortium wants to run a stratospheric aerosol injection field experiment; a philanthropic foundation offers to fund a marine cloud brightening research program in our waters; a bloc of large emitters floats an "emergency deployment" plan after a severe cyclone season; a coalition of NGOs asks us to co-sign a moratorium. We need a single reusable decision procedure we can run each time, without a climate-modeling team and often within two or three weeks, that tells us whether to support, oppose, or conditionally support the proposal and what conditions to attach. The procedure must do justice to three concerns without pretending we can definitively resolve them: (1) moral hazard — does our involvement or endorsement reduce pressure on large emitters to cut emissions, and can we detect that effect, not just assert it? (2) termination shock — if deployment begins and later stops abruptly, whether for political, technical, or funding reasons, the unmasked warming could hit our members hardest, so how should the risk of an abrupt stop enter the decision? and (3) the "who decides" problem — the people most affected by geoengineering are generally not the people proposing it, and our office's legitimacy depends on not lending cover to a decision that should have been made more broadly. Be explicit about which steps are genuine tests and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Please also provide three worked applications of the procedure, including at least one case where the procedure tells us to do something we will not like — for example, supporting a research program we would rather block, or opposing an emergency deployment that would protect our members in the short term. Give the decision procedure itself as a table with these columns: Step (number and short name); The test (the question the step asks); Information required (what you must obtain or check before answering); Pass / conditional / fail standard (what answer triggers which posture); and Judgment call? (yes or no, with one sentence explaining why). Then give the three worked applications as a JSON array, with each object containing the fields: case (short label), scenario (two to three sentences), first_failing_step (string, or "none"), recommended_position (one of "support", "oppose", or "conditional support"), conditions_attached (an array of strings, possibly empty), and residual_risk (one or two sentences on what the procedure cannot rule out).
