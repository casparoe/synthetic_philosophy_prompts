for a phil of mind reading group I have to send around a paragraph of what I actually think about original vs derived intentionality, and I'm worried I'm accidently holding stuff that contradicts itself. Here's my current set: (1) I think Searle is basically right that word processors and language models don't have original intentionality; their aboutness is borrowed from us. (2) I also think a human brain has original intentionality, and this isn't just a matter of someone adopting the intentional stance toward it. (3) But I buy Dennett's two-bitser argument that Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality is probably metaphysically shallow; a coin-detector has a pretty robust kind of aboutness that isn't simply borrowed from us in the way the Chinese Room's rulebook is. (4) I think teleosemantics is roughly on the right track: a representation's content is fixed by its evolutionary or learning function, not by what anyone currently interprets it as. (5) I also think if we met aliens with totally different chemistry but same behavior, I'd have no problem saying their internal states have original intentionality. (6) I don't think the intentional stance tells us what's really there; it's a predictive tool. Do these hang together? Which pairs are in actual tension, which only look that way, and what's the cheapest fix? Like a ranked audit, not a lecture.
