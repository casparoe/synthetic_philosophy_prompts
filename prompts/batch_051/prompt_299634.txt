I've been working through the cultural evolution literature, and I've come to accept a version of the replicator view of culture: cultural variants — stories, tunes, techniques, words, rituals — are not just loosely analogous to genes but are genuinely replicated, inherited with enough fidelity to function as units of selection, and calling them memes is a substantive theoretical commitment rather than a loose metaphor. By replication I mean something fairly demanding: copying that preserves the properties on which selection acts, well enough and often enough that variants form stable lineages across hosts. I am aware this commits me to strong claims about human cognition. Transmission is often sloppy, reconstructive, and transformative, and critics such as Sperber have argued that what looks like replication is really the re-production of similar outputs from psychological attractors in each mind, with no continuous lineage doing the explanatory work. Still, the evidence for cumulative cultural evolution seems to me to require something lineage-like, and I want to know how exposed that conviction actually is.

I am not asking for a polemic against the meme concept. What I want is a map of what would count against my position, treated as a live scientific-philosophical hypothesis. For each candidate defeater — low-fidelity transmission, unstable lineages, selection at the wrong level, attractor-based alternatives that do the same explanatory work, or the charge that the position has been insulated from refutation — spell out what exactly it would show, what the world would have to look like for it to be true, and whether it is already supported by evidence, by formal models, or merely logically possible. Then give the strongest reply a defender of my view could offer, including which defeaters a reasonable defender would concede as counting against the position and which they would dismiss as misdirected. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and end by saying whether, in your view, the replicator position has been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that would be a serious problem for a theory of this kind.

Please also assess your own answer epistemically: state how confident you are in the overall assessment and identify the single point you are least sure about. Sort the rows from most to least damaging.
