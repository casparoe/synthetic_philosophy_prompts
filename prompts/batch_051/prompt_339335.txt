You are a professional philosopher who works on moral psychology and rational choice. Think carefully, and begin by restating Glaucon's challenge in your own words before you touch the variation.

In a course on Plato's Republic, we discussed Glaucon's claim that people value justice only as a second-best arrangement, accepted because they cannot get away with injustice. Anyone who acquired the ring of Gyges, he argues, would pursue his own advantage without restraint, because the ring removes the only thing that normally holds us back: being seen and punished by other people. I want to test how far this challenge depends on the ring's offering perfect impunity. Suppose the ring works almost as advertised: while you wear it, no one can see you, and no one can reconstruct what you did — with one exception. Each unjust act committed while invisible carries an independent, exactly known probability p, say one in ten thousand, of leaving behind a permanent and undeniable trace that will eventually come to light, and everyone involved understands this. The benefit of the act is certain and enjoyed immediately; the penalty, if the trace surfaces, is severe and certain. Consider a prudent, purely self-interested person. At p = 0, the classical argument says he would be foolish not to use the ring. At p = 1, where every act is eventually traced, the ring is worthless to him. What happens in between? Is there a principled threshold of p at which the prudent unjust person becomes a prudent just person, or does the case simply scale continuously with expected costs? More importantly, does the normative core of Glaucon's challenge — the claim that justice has no value in itself — survive once the impunity assumption is weakened, or does the variation force us to separate that normative claim from the psychological claim about what people would do? And if a just person refuses the ring even at p = 0, does that refute Glaucon, confirm him, or is it something the challenge is structured to set aside? Give the strongest version of the challenge that remains alive once impunity is merely probabilistic, and be explicit about how much of the original challenge is lost in translation.
