So I just got out of a long argument with a friend about what "the alignment problem" even refers to, and it turns out we were using the term in totally different ways. He meant the big thing where AIs end up with the right goals or values—human welfare, our extrapolated values, moral patienthood, all that. I was trying to defend the much narrower reading where alignment is just the problem of getting a system to do what its user actually intends in a given situation, bracketing the question of whether those intentions are good or correctly capture what the user "really" values. Can you explain this narrow definition—intent alignment—in a way that goes past the standard intro, and say exactly how it relates to the broader value alignment notion? Is it a subproblem, a prerequisite, a rival framing, or what? Also, does the narrow version actually avoid value judgments, if you have to decide who counts as "the user" when a model is built by one group, deployed by another, and used by a bunch of people with conflicting intentions?
