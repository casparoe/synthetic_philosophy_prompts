I've been trying to sort out what "pleasure" even means, because a friend of mine is a hedonist in the old sense—he thinks pleasure is the only thing good in itself—and I keep discovering that the word does too much work. I made a list of things I'd or wouldn't call pleasures, and my gut splits in different directions depending on the case.

Eating a perfectly ripe peach when I'm actually hungry: pleasure, obviously. The warm looseness after a hard massage: pleasure. The satisfaction of finally seeing how a proof works after an afternoon of confusion: I want to call it pleasure, but it feels like a different animal from the peach. The relief of finding my keys after thinking I'd lost them: pleasant, sure, but is it really just the absence of panic rather than anything positive? Scratching a mosquito bite: definite pleasure, but if that's the same kind of thing as the proof, the category is doing something strange. Malicious glee when a rival at work gets publicly humiliated: it sure feels like pleasure while it lasts, but I don't want to say it's good. A song that makes me cry and that I play on purpose: I seek it out, but "pleasant" seems wrong for the ache. A runner's high: I feel it and chase it, but it's laced with pain.

My halfway theory was that a pleasure is any experience I'd want to prolong or repeat for its own sake. But that makes scratching the bite into a pleasure on par with understanding the proof (fine, maybe?), and it says the sad song's ache is a pleasure only if I want to prolong the ache itself, which I don't—I want the ache embedded in the whole song. So what do these things have in common, if anything? Is there a principle that gets my verdicts, or close to them, and what does it say about cases I haven't thought of yet? And what does it mean for hedonism if there isn't a single thing underneath all of them? I dont mind being told that some of my verdicts have to go, but I want to know exactly which ones and what the trade-off looks like.
