I am a PhD student in the history and philosophy of science working on conventionalism about standards of measurement. Could you write a roughly 600-word satirical piece in the form of a formal recommendation issued by an international calendar-reform commission, in deadpan bureaucratic prose, announcing the selection of a new global epoch. The commission should treat the choice of year zero as a purely technical matter of continuity and computational convenience, while its actual criteria quietly favor the historical and commercial priorities of one constituency. The piece should weigh, with scrupulous faux-neutrality, candidates such as the traditional Common Era, a Holocene epoch set 10,000 years before the present, and the Unix epoch, and the humor should come from real features of calendar debates—which events are universal enough to count, how to manage legacy dates and records, who bears the cost of conversion, and what counts as a neutral starting point. Do not let the narrator wink at the reader. By the end, a reader should feel the serious objection that no merely technical criterion can make an epoch choice politically or culturally neutral.
