I'm putting together a short handout for my philosophy of physics reading group where I try to state, in one place, what I actually think about Bell's theorem, and I'd like an audit before I show it to people who will happily dismantle me. The standard menu — either the world is nonlocal, or superdeterministic, or stubbornly anti-realist — doesn't quite capture where I've ended up, and I can't tell whether that's because I've found a coherent niche or because I've quietly helped myself to an inconsistency. Here are the commitments as they currently stand.

(1) I take the violations of Bell inequalities in loophole-free experiments as settled; I'm not trying to explain them away with residual experimental loopholes.
(2) I think relativity rules out superluminal causal influence, and I've come to think the condition it actually requires is only parameter independence: conditional on the complete prior state of the pair, the choice of setting on one wing doesn't change the probabilities for outcomes on the other wing. I do not think relativity requires outcome independence — that is, conditional on the complete prior state and both settings, the outcome on one wing doesn't change the probabilities for the outcome on the other wing.
(3) I reject superdeterminism outright; the measurement settings are not correlated with the state of the particle pair in the conspiratorial way that would make the Bell inequalities inapplicable from the start.
(4) I'm a realist in the limited sense of counterfactual definiteness: in any given run there is a definite fact about which result I would have gotten, for every setting I might have chosen.
(5) I think the quantum state is complete; there are no hidden variables over and above the wave function.
(6) I think the right way to understand the Bell correlations is a kind of nonseparable holism — the two wings are one entangled system described by a single global state, not two locally seperated systems with a common cause.

My vague sense is that (2) is the load-bearing part and that it lets me keep (1) without anything spooky traveling between the wings, but I also keep going back and forth about whether (6) is doing real work or is just (1) restated.
