I have to write, for a seminar on promises and related speech acts, a set of desiderata that any theory of the binding force of oaths should satisfy before I commit to an account, and I would rather settle the criteria before I read further so the theory does not dictate the questions. My background here is partly rationalist: I have spent a lot of time in online discussions of precommitment and commitment mechanisms — Ulysses contracts, public vows, self-signalling, costly signals — and those discussions are good at the mechanics of binding a future self but oddly silent on whether an oath actually binds, as opposed to merely raising the cost of breaking it. The Styx oath seems like the clean stress test: in the Greek stories the gods swear by the river Styx, and it is the one oath even they cannot break with impunity; the binding force, whatever it is, has to operate before there is any community of witnesses, reputation, or enforcement institution. So the desiderata should force a theory to handle a Styx-type oath and ordinary oaths within the same frame: marriage vows, oaths of office, oaths made to the dying, oaths extracted under coercion, and an oath sworn to a cause the swearer later rejects.

With that purpose in view — adequacy here means good enough to tell a person whether a given oath binds them, to whom it binds them, for how long, and what kind of wrong breaking it would be — write the list of requirements. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (divine-witness, performative or commitment, assurance or expectation, and authority-sanction accounts are the families I have in mind). Address, at minimum: the bootstrapping problem, meaning how an act of speech could create a reason where none existed before; the distinction between bindingness and enforcement-cost; the difference between an oath and a promise or a contract; why some people treat oaths as exceptionless while others treat them as defeasible under changed circumstances; and whether coerced oaths or oaths to discreditable causes bind at all. Flag any subset of the requirements that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, score the main families of view against the list. The desiderata themselves are the deliverable, not the scorecard.
