Over the past year I have watched the town where I grew up lose its largest employer and much of its young population, and I have been reading about the Late Bronze Age, the Maya, and Easter Island to think through whether such episodes are best described as collapse or simply as change. What unsettles me is that the same observable pattern — abandoned public works, fewer occupied buildings, thinner trade networks — is read by some scholars as societal failure and by others as adaptive transformation or resilience; and very different processes, such as conquest, epidemic, shifting trade routes, or a community's deliberate decision to disperse, can leave similar traces. I would like to know how one could actually test the claim that a particular society has collapsed rather than merely changed. Please design the strongest test you can — rather than a survey of those cases — using observable indicators of population, material life, construction, and political organization that could in principle be applied both to an archaeological case and to a present-day community. Say what pattern of results would favor the collapse reading, what pattern would favor the change reading, and what the test could not settle even if the results came out cleanly, including whether the residual disagreement is empirical or largely a matter of what one means by "a society" and "failure." Answer as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; within those points, state how confident you are in each point and identify the one you are least sure of.
