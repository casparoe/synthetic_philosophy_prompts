I am preparing a seminar exercise for a course in the philosophy of inquiry, and I want a numerical evaluation rather than a narrative summary of Peirce's four methods for fixing belief. The methods, as I shall present them to the students, are: the method of tenacity, in which one holds fast to a belief and avoids whatever might unsettle it; the method of authority, in which an institution or state fixes opinion and suppresses dissent; the a priori method, in which one prefers whatever seems antecedently reasonable or elegant after conversation among like-minded inquirers, without making recalcitrant experience the arbiter; and the method of science, in which belief is treated as answerable to an external reality that would be what it is regardless of what any inquirer thinks of it.

Rate each method from 0 to 1 on three criteria: (1) how effectively it removes the discomfort of doubt for the individual believer in the short term; (2) how well the beliefs it produces survive renewed contact with recalcitrant experience; (3) how well it guards against self-deception and parochialism. Provide an overall ordinal ranking and, if you judge the criteria unequal in importance, give weights and a weighted aggregate score. Peirce argued that only the scientific method can be recommended consistently, since the others are answerable to what suits individual inclination or institutional will rather than to something independent of all of us. Say whether you accept that argument, and whether a method's strong performance on criterion (1) can ever compensate for weakness on criterion (3). Finally, give one realistic circumstance in which tenacity or authority is, all things considered, the rationally preferable method, or state plainly that there is none; if that verdict conflicts with any score you assigned, identify which score should be revised and why.
