ive been chewing on nagarjuna's tetralemma by myself, the four-corners thing: for any claim about something you can try saying it is, it isnt, it is both, it is neither — and he doesnt let you rest in any of the four. and the emptiness part isnt a fifth answer, its more like the floor gives way under all four at once. that idea seems made for a villanelle, where two lines keep coming back and then meet at the end.

could you write me that poem? standard 19-line villanelle, five tercets and a final quatrain, aba rhyme. make the two refrains exactly "not this, not that" and "not both, not neither." i want the first four tercets to each take one corner in order — is, isnt, both, neither — and try, with something concrete like a clay pot or the self or a moment of pain, to treat it as a place to stand before the refrain cuts the legs out. the fifth tercet should be the one where the speaker sneaks toward a fifth position (everything is flux, or pure awareness, or void, whatever) and the refrain cuts that too. then the final quatrain should set up the landing so the last two lines are literally the two refrains back to back. i want the form to do the same thing the argument does: each position collapses, no secret absolute takes its place, and your left not with a conclusion but with the collapse itself feeling like the point. someone who actually knows madhyamaka should be able to see the poem isnt name-dropping — that emptiness isnt mush or a spooky void, and that even the refrain could get clung to if you arent careful. please dont make it new-agey; roughness and doubt are fine.
