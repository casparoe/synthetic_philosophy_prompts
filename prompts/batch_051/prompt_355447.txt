A friend and I just spent an evening going in circles about whether viruses are alive, and I came away convinced that we were arguing without having agreed on what an account of life is supposed to do. He kept pointing to genetic material and evolution: viruses mutate, are subject to selection, and have lineages, so he said they are alive. I kept pointing to metabolism and self-maintenance: a virion just sits there and does nothing with its environment until a host cell does all the work. When he switched to reproduction as the real test, I brought up the mule, or a sterile worker bee, which is plainly alive but leaves no offspring of its own. He replied that a mule is alive because it belongs to a reproducing lineage, at which point I said a virus belongs to a reproducing lineage too, and we were back where we started. By the end I did not know whether we had chosen the wrong criterion or had never asked what the criterion was supposed to do for us.

I want to fix that before reading any more on the topic. Please write the desiderata that any adequate account of the boundary between the living and the nonliving must meet: the cases it has to get right (viruses, prions, bacterial endospores, dormant seeds, obligate intracellular parasites, synthetic minimal cells, hypothetical alien or digital replicators, and any further load-bearing cases you think belong on the list), the distinctions it cannot collapse (being alive versus having the capacity for life, being alive versus being a part of a living system, living individual versus living lineage, and so on), and the established biology it cannot contradict. Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, say which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect a particular camp's priorities or a research agenda, and flag any subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable. If you conclude that the living/nonliving boundary is graded, purpose-relative, or a cluster concept rather than a sharp line, I want that to fall out of the desiderata rather than preempt them. I am not asking for a reading list, and I do not need a point-by-point scorecard of existing definitions; the requirement list itself is the thing we were missing.
