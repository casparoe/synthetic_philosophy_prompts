I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and a couple weeks ago I was at a political theory conference where two grad students got into a long argument about Hobbes that I've been turning over ever since. I took notes at the bar and I'd like to work it into a column, but I honestly couldn't referee it.

One said the social contract is hollow because it's made under duress: everyone in the state of nature agrees out of terror of violent death, and if a contract made at gunpoint doesn't bind in ordinary life, then the original contract can't bind either. The other said that's exactly backwards: for Hobbes the state of nature is the condition in which everyone is at gunpoint all the time, so if fear invalidated covenants nobody could ever contract their way out of it, and the theory would undercut itself. She also said the fear isn't a distortion of the choice — it's the reason the choice is rational; you give up your unlimited right and get security in return, which is a deal you'd keep wanting after. The first guy said that turns the contract into a cost-benefit calculation, and then consent becomes redundant: if the arrangement binds because it's good for you, it binds whether or not you agreed to it. She responded that consent still does a seperate job — it makes the sovereign's acts the subjects' own, an authorization rather than a mere deal. He then brought up what he called the punishment problem: Hobbes himself says nobody can give up the right to defend his own life, so subjects can't have authorized the sovereign to execute or imprison them; that means the sovereign's power to punish isn't actually transferred by covenant but held over from the state of nature, so the escape from the state of nature is only partial and the contract doesn't cover the most important thing the state does. It kind of petered out there because the bar got loud.

I'd like you to adjudicate it move by move. For each step, say who's right about what, where the claim is overstated or just wrong, and what both of them are missing — I suspect there are several things. And then tell me who's more right overall.
