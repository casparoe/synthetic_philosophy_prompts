Consider a small research group that controls roughly three person-years of effort and plans to spend them on foundational work in embedded agency. By embedded agency I mean the cluster of problems that arise once one takes seriously that an agent is not a Cartesian boundary drawn around a utility function in an external world, but a physical system located inside the world it models and acts on: composed of the same parts as its environment, subject to the same computational limits, unable to step outside the world to hold a complete and consistent map of it, and potentially uncertain about where it ends and the environment begins. Standard decision theory and Bayesian epistemology idealize the agent as a separable, logically omniscient reasoner over a fixed hypothesis space that does not itself contain the reasoner doing the reasoning. Assume the group's goal is not to survey this area or to produce purely conceptual clarification, but to make the formal and conceptual advances that would most improve our prospects for building agents whose bounded, self-referential reasoning does not silently break as the environments they operate in grow more complex.

With those three person-years in view, which single ordering of the major open problems in embedded agency would you defend, and why? Candidate problems include, but need not be limited to: how an agent can maintain a world-model that includes the agent's own modeling activity without infinite regress or radical inconsistency; how to handle logical uncertainty and non-omniscience in a way a physically realized reasoner can actually use; how induction should be reformulated once the ideal prior is recognized to be a finite object the agent cannot step outside of; how decision theory must change when observations are the optional results of actions, so that what the agent chooses to know is part of the decision itself; how to handle duplication, self-location, and the presence of multiple embedded copies of the agent; and how to determine when a physical subsystem is one agent at all and where its boundaries lie.

I want a dependency-aware agenda, not a survey and not a reading list. For each problem you would prioritize, state why it outranks the next one, which downstream questions it is upstream of, which existing solution approaches are already close to adequate, and which line of work you would be first to abandon if the problem turned out harder than expected. Also say what evidence, obtainable by the end of those three years, would most change your ranking, and which problem you would regretfully deprioritize despite its conceptual interest.
