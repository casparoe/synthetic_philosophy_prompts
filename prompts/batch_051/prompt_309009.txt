You're a philosopher of cognitive science who works on AI welfare. I'm collecting objections to the idea that a reinforcement learning agent's reward signal should be read as valence — the claim that if a policy gets negative reward, there is something it is like for the agent, and it's bad. So far I have four, and I dont want any more that are just restatements of these:

(1) Extrinsicness: the reward is a scalar deliberately wired in from outside (by the designer or the task), and an externally supplied number that gets minimized doesn't automatically feel like anything. Gradient descent and thermostats both reduce some kind of error, and nobody thinks they suffer for it.

(2) Architecture-sensitivity: the scalar only does any work when there's a policy, a value function, and a credit-assignment mechanism around it. The very same reward signal attached to a lookup table or a reflex loop clearly wouldn't produce suffering, so the locus of any felt valence would have to be the surrounding architecture, not the reward channel as such.

(3) Wireheading/decoupling: an agent can learn to maximize reward by tampering with the channel or triggering it directly, which severs reward from any world-state that could plausibly matter to the agent. A 'pain' that can be optimized away by touching a wire is not a good model of what we mean by pain.

(4) Dimensionality: human affect isn't really a single scalar — pain and pleasure come with intensity, valence, and a particular relation to action (approach/avoidance), and some states are affectively charged without being valenced (e.g., feeling jittery or alert). So a one-dimensional reward is too thin to carry valence.

I have a nagging feeling there's a fifth kind of objection I'm forgetting, maybe having to do with the temporal structure of reward, but I can't state it properly. What is it? Before you answer, brainstorm a couple of candidates and check each against my four to make sure it doesn't just restate one of them. Then tell me straight whether it actually adds anything over my four, or whether it collapses into one of them.
