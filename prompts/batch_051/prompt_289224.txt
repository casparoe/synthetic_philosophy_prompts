I'm building an agent-based simulation of open-ended evolution, and I've been using a four-level scheme as a design guide. In the scheme, (1) Darwinian agents vary only across generations: variations are generated in the genotype, and selection tests whole organisms; (2) Skinnerian agents also vary behavior within a lifetime, generating actions that the environment then tests by reward and punishment; (3) Popperian agents can generate candidate actions and test them in an internal forward model before executing them, so an action can fail inside the agent instead of in the world; and (4) Gregorian agents offload cognitive work into artifacts, language, and other minds. I want to pressure-test what each level buys by reasoning through a precise counterfactual.

Suppose a planet otherwise like Earth, from the time the first animals with nervous systems appear, with one stable biological difference: no nervous system on that planet can decouple the generation of a motor plan from its execution. Natural selection operates as it does here, and lifetime operant conditioning operates as it does here, so behavior can be shaped by consequences; but mental rehearsal, planning, predicting the outcome of an action before taking it, and selecting among actions in an inner environment cannot evolve. On that planet, an action can be selected only by executing it and taking the consequences, or by selection having installed the disposition in advance. Roughly five hundred million years later, what is the most complex cognition you would expect to have evolved? In particular, assess whether, and in what order, you would expect: (a) flexible tool use; (b) coordinated group hunting; (c) communication that can refer to absent states of affairs; (d) cumulative culture; and (e) anything an observer would call technology or science. For each, make the mechanisms explicit: which parts can be reached by operant chaining plus natural selection, where the absence of an internal forward model starts to bite, and whether evolution can compensate by hardwiring behaviors that on Earth emerge partly from simulation. A specific question behind this is whether the fourth level can be reached without the third—whether language and cumulative culture require offline selection of actions, or can be assembled from operant conditioning plus genetic canalization alone. Finally, separate your predictions into the robust and the speculative, and state what would have to be different, either in animal cognition as we observe it on Earth or in your assumptions about what internal models are for, for your predicted ceiling to be wrong.
