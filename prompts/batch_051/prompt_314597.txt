I have a standing policy about judging historical figures, and I have started to worry about how comfortable it is. When I assess people like Jefferson, Churchill, or Columbus, I first try to determine what the live moral alternatives were in their own place and time. If the person's beliefs or actions were close to the prevailing consensus of their milieu, I mostly withhold blame; I allow strong condemnation only when I can identify contemporaries who already saw the wrong and said so. My rationale is that I do not want to punish people for failing to escape an outlook they had not been given the resources to reject, and I do not want my own historical location to pass itself off as objectivity. Recently, though, I have noticed that this rule does a great deal of work for me. It lets me treat Jefferson's slaveholding, Churchill's racial and imperial views, and Columbus's conduct in the Caribbean as unfortunate but not truly blameworthy, and it quietly shifts the question from what they did to whether I am even entitled to apply my standards to them. I have also caught myself invoking the rule almost exclusively for figures I otherwise admire and rarely for figures I already dislike, which makes me wonder whether the rule is doing the judging or my prior loyalties are. A friend says the whole thing is relativism with extra steps: I have fixed the threshold of blame so that almost no historically admired figure can fail it, since some group of contemporaries can always be found who shared the person's assumptions.

I want to keep what feels humane about the rule—not confusing moral luck with moral failure—without sliding into a history in which nobody can ever be held to account. Is this policy defensible as a way of forming judgments about the past, or is it a bias dressed up as a methodological scruple? What would a tightened version of the policy look like: when does the man-of-his-time defence fully exonerate, when does it merely mitigate, and when should it carry no weight at all? And from the inside, how can I tell whether I am using it to understand the past or to shield figures I have been taught to admire? Please write your answer in American English.
