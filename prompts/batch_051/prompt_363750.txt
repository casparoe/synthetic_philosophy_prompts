I have been working for a couple of years on what happens when one AI is used to interpret another, and I keep running into a difficulty I have started calling interpretive debt. My thesis is that every interpretation of a model's internals is itself a model. When a probe says that a direction in activation space encodes truthfulness, the claim is not a neutral readout: it projects a human interpreter's semantics for truthfulness onto a geometry that is under no obligation to respect the boundaries of that concept. The probe has an objective, a training set, and inductive biases of its own, and none of these are themselves visible in the interpretation it outputs. The interpretation therefore borrows a vocabulary whose anchoring is never itself explained.

My stronger claim is that recursive interpretation cannot discharge this debt. Setting a second AI to interpret the first one's interpretation does not converge on a canonical semantics; it adds another layer of projection with its own unexamined vocabulary. I call the resulting limit the transparency ceiling: interpretability can climb only as high as the least-interpreted vocabulary we are willing to take for granted. If that vocabulary can eventually be anchored behaviorally, by interventions, causal tests, and downstream-task performance, then the ceiling is real but benign. If it cannot, interpretability asymptotes, and every layer of interpretation floats above an unanchored semantics.

I know this sounds close to the familiar worry that interpretability explanations are just stories we tell about matrices, and to the homunculus worry that adding an inner interpreter only pushes the problem further inside. But I mean something narrower: the regress I am describing is semantic rather than computational, and it is not the usual incompleteness-theorem argument about a system representing itself. I would like an honest assessment. Is the idea coherent, or does it collapse under pressure? Does it already exist under another name I should know, in philosophy or in the interpretability literature? And where exactly does it break: what is the strongest scenario in which interpretability bottoms out without borrowing an unexamined vocabulary, or in which my interpretive debt turns out to be ordinary engineering uncertainty?
