ok so I've been going in circles on something I think is called the reference class problem and I'd like a consistency check on where I've landed. I have no formal philosophy background so forgive me if I'm using terms sloppily.

Here's the bundle:

(1) I'm a subjectivist Bayesian. Probabilities are degrees of confidence, and the only hard constraint is that they're coherent and I update them by Bayes. Two rational people with the exact same evidence can disagree and neither is making an error.

(2) But I also think that when I ask what's the chance my startup survives two more years, there are better and worse answers. The answer depends on which bucket I drop the startup into — all startups, seed-stage B2B SaaS startups in Europe, startups with my exact revenue and churn — and the buckets give very different rates. I have the intuition that some of these buckets are just wrong and some are right, and not wrong just because they're less useful to me personally.

(3) The rule I keep reaching for is: use the narrowest bucket that still has enough data for a frequency I'd trust. Specificity beats sample size up to the point where the numbers get too noisy.

(4) But I also think the fully correct move is to condition on everything I know about the startup (founders, market, revenue, churn, etc). Except then the class has one member — this startup — and there's no frequency to read off. So the ideal looks both right and impossible.

(5) In high-stakes settings like risk scores, medical prognosis, or reoffense rates, I think the bucket choice is a real problem. An institution shouldn't get to just say the model said so — they should have to justify which class they put a person in, and there should be a fact of the matter about whether that's the right bucket or not.

(6) But then I also think there's no priveliged partition of the world. Any given thing is in unboundedly many classes, and there's no deep fact about which one is the one. So in a sense every probability is relative to a chosen model, and maybe the reference class problem can't have a once-and-for-all solution.

Do these hang together? I genuinely can't tell if I've smuggled in a contradiction or if I'm just worrying too much. Please go through the pairs of commitments and tell me which are actually in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what I'd have to give up and what that costs. Then tell me if there's one revision that would clean up the most.
