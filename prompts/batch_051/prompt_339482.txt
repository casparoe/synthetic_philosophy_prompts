I'm writing a novel, and I keep going back and forth about where one of my characters' minds stops. I've been using four rules of thumb to decide whether something outside a person's skull should be narrated as part of their mind, rather than as a tool they use.

1. It's reliably there when they need it — not something they have to hunt for or that's only sometimes available.

2. They reach for it the way you reach for a memory, without deliberating about where the thing is or how to get at it.

3. They take what it says on trust, more or less the way you trust your own memory and eyes even though both sometimes fail.

4. If you rebuilt the person with that resource inside their head, you'd unhesitatingly call the result part of their mind, not just a handy implant.

Now the case. My protagonist, Marta, is a poet who writes only by dictating lines into a small tape recorder the moment they come to her. She's used it for so long that she says the poems live in it between one dictation and the next. The recorder is always in her pocket or on her bedside table; if it goes missing she cant write at all — not won't, she's tried, and she just sits there until it's found. So rules 1 and 2 seem to hold: the thing is constantly present and she uses it without thinking.

Rule 3 is where I get stuck. Marta doesn't trust the recorder. She plays back yesterday's lines the way a critic listens to a stranger. She often decides the voice on the tape got it wrong and erases over it; sometimes she keeps a line she despises because she thinks the sequence, not the line, knows what the poem wants. And she frequently doesn't remember having said the lines at all. When she replays, it feels to her like recieving someone else's drafts, even though she knows it's her own voice. But she does trust it completely for one thing: it's her only record of what she has actually said, and she never doubts that the recording is a faithful record. So she trusts it as memory but not as judgement.

What I need for the novel is this: when I narrate Marta's inner life, should I treat the recorder as literally part of her mind — for instance, write its contents in the same register as her thoughts — or as a collaborator or tool outside her? By my four rules, does it qualify? Go through the rules honestly, tell me where they underdetermine the answer, and say whether the case shows that one of the rules is doing too much work or is misstated, and how you'd fix it.

Oh, and please write in British English, not American. And at the very end, after your discussion, give me the bottom line as the last line of your answer in a fixed form: \answer{qualifies}, \answer{doesn't qualify}, or \answer{criteria need changing}.
