In my field school this summer the director kept saying 'absence of evidence is not evidence of absence' to shut down any talk of inferring what a site's inhabitants believed or did ritually from finds that are just domestic debris and some weird pits. Some of us think that after three careful seasons of walking every square and floating every deposit, the lack of anything that looks remotely like ritual architecture or votive stuff has got to count for something. I cant tell whether his slogan is just a reminder about preservation and sampling biases, or a much stronger claim that archaeological absence can never ground an inference to the absence of a past practice. Could you lay out the different precise claims that slogan could be making in that context, from the trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it's actually true and whether it would block the move from 'we looked hard and found nothing' to 'they probably didn't do that here'? I want the readings as a menu I can bring to office hours, not one picked answer.
