I am not a professional philosopher, but I have been reading on my own, and I would like help settling a disagreement between two things I have read. In Donald Davidson's essay What Metaphors Mean, the opening paragraph compares metaphor to the work of dreams: Davidson suggests that interpreting a metaphor, like interpreting a dream, may reveal as much about the interpreter as about the originator. From there he argues that the words in a metaphorical sentence have only their ordinary literal meanings, and that a metaphor does not communicate any extra proposition. Its effect, on his account, lies in how it makes us notice a resemblance or tension between two things, much as a picture or a sudden jolt might.

On the other side, I came across the conceptual-metaphor view, which treats ordinary language as saturated with systematic mappings between domains. People speak of time as money, argument as war, or understanding as seeing, and the theory says these are not just isolated comparisons but organized conceptual structures that carry genuine cognitive content. On this view, metaphorical thought exists before and beneath metaphorical language, and meaning is not limited to the literal sentence.

I cannot tell whether these two are actually saying incompatible things about the same subject or whether Davidson is simply defining meaning so narrowly that the dispute is verbal. Are Davidson's denial of metaphorical meaning and the conceptual-metaphor theory's claim that metaphorical mappings carry content in genuine conflict? If they are, which side has the stronger case, and what is the other side still getting right? Please do not just lay out the debate; I want you to commit to a position, state it plainly, and defend it with reference to the passage I mentioned and to a concrete everyday metaphor.
