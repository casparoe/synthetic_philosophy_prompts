I am a novelist at work on a book set roughly a decade from now, and I keep reaching for the phrase transformative AI without being able to say what would make it true. I would like a working definition of the term, one precise enough that it can be applied to a new technology and yield a definite verdict rather than a vague impression. Please test the definition against these cases, chosen so that some are clearly transformative, some clearly not, and several are deliberately intermediate: the printing press; the steam engine; electrification; the internet; the smartphone; a large language model that changes how white-collar work is performed without much changing how much of it is done; a proprietary AI that doubles one firm's productivity but never diffuses beyond it; and a hypothetical system that automates every economically valuable cognitive task within five years. I am confident the printing press and electrification were transformative and that the single-firm AI that never diffuses is not, but I am genuinely unsure about the smartphone and the large language model case. For each case, state how the definition classifies it, note any place where the definition gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it if necessary. Then tell me which of your final verdicts is least secure. I am especially interested in whether the definition should turn on the size of a change, its speed, or its irreversibility, and whether those criteria come apart on the hard cases. Finally, apply the definition to the present: has transformative AI already happened, or is that question badly posed?
