Okay, here's one I want you to actually dig into, not give me the standard compare-and-contrast. In Nyaya epistemology the pramanas (the accepted sources of knowledge) are perception, inference, analogy (upamana), and testimony (shabda). The traditional view is that testimony is its own seperate pramana and can't be reduced to inference. The usual Nyaya argument goes roughly like this: inference needs a vyapti — a known universal connection between the sign and the thing signified, like smoke and fire — but accepting what a reliable speaker says doesn't run through any vyapti, and you can't even establish the speaker's reliability by inference without already relying on testimony somewhere along the line, so the reduction gets circular. Meanwhile, modern anti-reductionism about testimony (the Reid-flavored view) says our attitude toward other people's say-so is a default: we're entitled to accept testimony unless something speaks against it, and justification doesn't require each of us to run an inference from the speaker's past reliability.
