I spent Saturday evening in a long argument with a friend about whether our city should remove a controversial nineteenth-century monument. He kept insisting that removing it is always a falsification of history; I kept trying to say that some ways of relating to the past are themselves a form of being trapped by it. By the end I realized I was reaching, badly, for Nietzsche's three modes of history — monumental, antiquarian, critical — and that I couldn't deploy the distinction cleanly.

I'm a graduate student, and next Tuesday I have to guest-teach one fifty-minute session of a high-school history class, ages 16–17, with no philosophy background. Do not explain the three modes to me; I already understand them. I want a plan for how I should teach them, so that the students leave with a real distinction rather than three labels.
