I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a piece about how the definition of language changed as sign languages moved from being treated as pantomime to being recognized as full human languages. In an interview for the piece, a philosopher who defends the claim that speech is the essential medium of language dismissed the issue with the one-liner: language is speech. The slogan was doing real argumentative work — it was meant to settle, in three words, whether a manual-visual sign system could count as a language at all. I want to leave the interview with a clear sense of what that slogan can mean. Give me exactly five numbered readings of "language is speech," each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Order them from the most platitudinous to the most radical, and for each say whether it is true but trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and state explicitly whether that reading would support the conclusion that sign languages are not full human languages.
