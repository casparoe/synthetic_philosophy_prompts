An economist I know keeps telling me that a wetland or an old-growth forest is best thought of as natural capital — a stock of assets that yeilds a flow of ecosystem services (flood control, carbon storage, pollination) the way a portfolio yields dividends. His claim is that we should stop talking about "saving nature" as if it were sacred and start treating conservation as asset management: maintain the asset base and you can keep drawing income forever. He's using the analogy to argue that conservation decisions should be made by pricing the services and comparing rates of return, and that a species or patch of habitat that doesn't deliver a good return shouldn't be prioritized.

I want an audit of the analogy, not just tired objections. Give the mapping explicitly (what's the principal, the dividends, the depreciation, liquidation, diversification, and so on), say where the mapping holds and where it breaks, seperate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the ones that are just cosmetic, and say whether his conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table — and whether a different analogy would carry the intended weight better.

Format the audit as a JSON object with these fields: "correspondences" (an array of objects with fields "capital_concept", "natural_target", "status" where status is "holds", "partly_holds", or "breaks"), "disanalogies" (an array of objects with fields "disanalogy", "matters_for_conclusion", "why"), "smuggled_assumptions" (array of strings), and "verdict" (object with fields "conclusion_survives", "repaired_analogy", "rationale").
