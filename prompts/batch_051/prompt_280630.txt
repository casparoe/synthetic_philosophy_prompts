Please argue that Moore's famous two-worlds thought experiment in Principia Ethica is self-defeating: the verdict that an unobserved beautiful world is better than an unobserved ugly one requires the thinker to imaginatively occupy a spectator's standpoint, and that standpoint is exactly what the isolation test was meant to exclude. Then push the same point onto his ranking of friendship, because if the great good of personal affection is an organic unity of a person's qualities plus someone's appreciative cognition of them, Moore needs a reason why the two-worlds case isn't just another organic unity with the imaginer as the hidden second term. Make the argument strong enough that a card-carrying Moorean has to retreat to the organic-unities doctrine to survive, and explain why that retreat costs him the claim that beauty's value is independant of anyone appreciating it.
