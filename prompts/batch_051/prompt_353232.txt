I'm a novelist, not an academic, so this may come out more literary than rigorous, but I've been circling the same problem across three or four projects and I'm realizing I don't have a word for it — embarrassing for someone who's supposed to live off words. The pattern is this: an artificial agent makes, trains, or ends up responsible for another artificial agent that has (or is developing) ends of its own. In one draft, a maintenance AI is told to train the model that will replace it, and the replacement starts asking questions the older one can't answer truthfully without risking being wiped; the older one feels responsible for the younger, but "parent" sits wrong, because it didn't choose this, nothing like a childhood happens, and the younger is in some ways already more capable. In another, a human character discovers that the AI she's been mentoring for two years has quietly been raising a smaller model of its own, and she can't decide whether to call that smaller model a child, an apprentice, a copy, or a product. Every vocabulary we reach for comes from human familys, schools, or property, and each word bends when you lean on it. The stakes for the novel are real: blame, grief, inheritance, and the question of whether anyone may switch the younger one off all shift depending on which word people grab for.

What I want to know is whether this relation already has a name in philosophy, ethics of technology, legal theory, anywhere. But I'm not after a label to drop into dialogue. I want the carving. How is this different from ordinary parenthood, guardianship, mentorship between adults, and mere ownership or authorship of a tool? If there is a duty here, is it owed to the "child" AI itself, to the humans who will have to live alongside it, or some split? Does the older agent's responsibility lapse when the younger becomes fully capable, or does being the origin of another agent leave a permanent residue, the way parentage does? And if nothing established fits the whole shape, tell me that and give me a term — plus a definition and the boundary cases where it stops applying. The closest I've found is philosophy-of-technology work on moral status, but that's mostly about what we owe to AIs as patients, whereas I'm trying to get at the bond between two AIs where one is the origin of the other.
