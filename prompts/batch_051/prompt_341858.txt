Suppose a machine is trained on a large corpus of human moral verdicts and then evaluated on new dilemmas. A critic insists that the evaluation can only ever measure mimicry: where the machine agrees with human judges we have learned nothing, and where it disagrees we treat its answer as a defect. I want to know whether that conclusion can be resisted by an observation rather than by a definition. Design the strongest test you can for whether such a system is doing something better described as ethical reasoning than as pattern-matching. Be concrete about the training–testing split (for example, training on trade-off cases and probing rights-based cases, or holding out an entire family of moral considerations), and state exactly what a positive result would establish and what it would leave untouched. Then commit yourself: after the strongest version of your test, is the critic's claim vindicated, defeated, or shown to be untestable?
