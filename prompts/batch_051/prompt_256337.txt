ok so here's roughly where I've landed on philosophy of math, and me and a few people in my reading group are going to pick at it next week. I call myself a structuralist: the natural numbers aren't any particular set-theoretic objects, they're just positions in the natural-number structure, which is fixed up to isomorphism by the Peano axioms. I also think 'is 2 an element of 4?' has a determinate answer and the answer is yes — on the usual von Neumann construction, 2 = {0,1} and 4 = {0,1,2,3}. I think isomorphic structures are the same for all mathematical purposes, so nothing could distinguish the 'real' natural numbers from any copy of them. I think set theory works fine as a foundation, but the choice of coding is conventional. And I think first-order Peano arithmetic (PA) has nonstandard models. Which pairs of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and I'd rather hear which single commitment is doing the most damage than a generic 'structuralism is complicated' lecture.
