Take the Jacobellis v. Ohio obscenity case — the one where Potter Stewart, in his concurrence, basically threw up his hands and said he couldn't define hard-core pornography but knew it when he saw it. Analyze it as a case study in vagueness and the sorites paradox. Treat "obscene" the way you'd treat "heap": spell out the tolerance principle that seems to be doing the work (if a film is obscene, cutting or adding a single frame can't flip it, so by the usual sorites reasoning how does anything ever count as obscene?), then say what epistemicism, supervaluationism, and contextualism would each make of Stewart's remark and of the Court's attempt to write a legal standard. Then defend a verdict on what the case shows: does it just play out what the sorites paradox predicts, does it favor one of those theories over the others, or does it expose something about borderlines and the fact that a court has to rule yes or no on a single case that vagueness theory hasn't handled well?
