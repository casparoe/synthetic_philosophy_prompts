Friend and I spent a couple hours going back and forth about why fields with clear feedback advance faster, and he kept pushing the one-variable view: it doesn't matter how smart the people are, if a field can't tell you quickly whether an idea is wrong, it just spins. On the way home I realized this is basically the sparse-versus-dense reward distinction from reinforcement learning. The community is the optimizer, the field's accepted results are the policy, and "clear feedback" is just having a dense reward signal you can evaluate cheaply and non-circularly. Physics is gradient descent; a field with no verdicts is reward-free exploration. That framing has teeth, though: reward shaping says you can speed up a slow field by injecting intermediate artificial feedback — benchmarks, metrics, leaderboards — even when the proxy isn't the true reward, and reward hacking says the field will then start optimizing the proxy instead of the thing. Which is exactly what seems to happen when you drop metrics into a soft field, so the analogy keeps winning. Is this a genuine structural identity, or am I just pattern-matching? If its real, what actually transfers and what does it predict that's false? If its not, were does the mapping break first?
