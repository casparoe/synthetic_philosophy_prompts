In my philosophy class we read the passage where Zhuangzi dreams he is a butterfly and, on waking, says he does not know whether he is a man who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. We discussed the standard skeptical reading of it: because the dream felt real from the inside while it lasted, nothing in my present experience can establish that I am awake rather than dreaming. That reading appears to need the premise that dreaming and waking are alike in every respect available from the inside.

Now suppose modern dream research weakens that premise. Dreams are often less coherent and less stable than waking experience; some lucid dreamers can recognize that they are dreaming while the dream is still occurring; and researchers can use brain activity to tell dreaming and waking apart from the outside. If those findings are accepted, does Zhuangzi's dream argument still go through in its original form? Does it need repair, or does it turn out to have been making a point that empirical findings of this kind could never touch? I want to know which premise the development bears on, what a defender of the argument would have to claim in response, and whether the argument can be restated in a form that survives the findings unchanged.
