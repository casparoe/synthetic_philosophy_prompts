I am a novelist planning a near-future political thriller, and I have been reading about the singleton hypothesis: the idea that humanity’s future may be shaped by a world order in which there is a single decision-making agency at the highest level, capable of preventing existential threats and coordinating global outcomes. In my drafting, I keep running into a conceptual snag. If a singleton is defined as a single highest-level agency, then there are by definition no rival highest-level agencies. But the hypothesis is often presented as saying that a singleton would solve global coordination problems. That sounds like solving the problem by definition: once you posit one decision-maker at the top, conflicts between top-level decision-makers are ruled out, and then the arrangement is praised for lacking them. Isn’t the singleton hypothesis therefore empirically empty, or at best a tautological restatement of “if there is one boss, there is no competition among bosses”? I need a definite position for the novel, not a menu of interpretations. Please tell me where this objection goes wrong, or whether I should have my characters treat the hypothesis as empty rhetoric.
