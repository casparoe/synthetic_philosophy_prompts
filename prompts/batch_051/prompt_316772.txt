Everyone in my policy-arguing circles reaches for the same image when our country's workfare reforms come up. The anti-conditionality side says welfare is a safety net, and sanctions, mandatory appointments, and fraud prosecutions are holes somebody cut in it: you wouldn't punch a hole in a trapeze net just because you suspect some of the people falling might have jumped on purpose, so why do that to the one thing stopping people from hitting the ground. The pro-conditionality side says the problem is a net people sleep in isn't a net at all, its a hammock, and a real safety net is supposed to bounce you back up. I suspect the metaphor is doing the arguing for both of them, and I want it audited before I take a side. Give me the mapping first -- the fall, the ground, the net, the mesh, the holes, the performer, whatever else is doing work -- then the places the analogy breaks, especially where the 'net' quietly imports that the fall is random and that nets never run out of space for everyone. Then commit: once the disanalogies are on the table, which of the two conclusions (sanctions are holes / conditions are the bounce) actually survives, and is there a better analogy for what conditionality and fraud rules actually do?
