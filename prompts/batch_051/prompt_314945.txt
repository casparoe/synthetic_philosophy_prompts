Me and a friend spent way too long last night going in circles about physicalism, and I think the argument actually changed how I see a view I've called my own for years. I'd always been a physicalist because it seemed like the grown-up option: no extra ingredient, no spooky second substance, just the brain doing what brains do. But she pressed a dilema I'd heard before—I think it's called Hempel's dilemma—and I'd always waved it off. If 'physical' means the ontology of current physics, then physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics is incomplete and will keep getting revised. But if 'physical' means the ontology of an ideal, completed physics, then the claim is empty, since we can't say in advance what that future physics will include; it might end up positing stuff we'd now call mental, and then physicalism wouldn't rule anything out. She said either way the word 'physical' is doing no work.

I pushed back with what I actually feel. I have this stubborn intuition that there's a real difference between a world where a pain just is a brain process and a world where there's an extra, non-physical something attached—not a difference I can state in neutral terms, but the contrast doesn't feel like a confusion. It feels like the very thing physicalism was supposed to assert. She said I can't keep the feeling while dropping the definition: if I can't say what 'non-physical' would add, then my intuition isn't a thesis, it's just a vibe. Which, honestly, landed.

Now I'm stuck. The dilemma genuinely attracts me: it's clean, it dissolves a whole family of fights without me having to take a side, and it explains why physicalists and dualists keep talking past each other. But the intuition that the contrast is real won't go away, and it's the reason I cared about any of this in the first place. Which one should give? To be more specific: what would it take for me to treat the intuition as a verbal artifact and let it go, and what would it take to instead reject the dilemma—say, by holding that physicalism can be a determinate thesis without a presently statable defintion of 'physical'? And is there a reading on which the dilemma only damages a particular formulation of physicalism and leaves the underlying intuition untouched?
