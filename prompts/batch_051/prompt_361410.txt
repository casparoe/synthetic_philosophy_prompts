I spent my career as an experimental scientist, and only after retiring have I begun reading the philosophy of science in any systematic way. A proposal now circulating in one of my old professional societies is this: every empirical paper accepted to the society's journals must include, at submission, a public falsification clause in which the authors identify one observation that, if produced by an independent group within five years, would lead the journal to retract the paper automatically. The clauses would be indexed and searchable. Please analyze this as a mechanism rather than as a philosophical position. What would it incentivize among authors, reviewers, editors, and competing laboratories? How would sophisticated actors game it, and how severe are the predictable failure modes? Does the proposal in fact enforce the kind of falsifiability Popper placed at the center of scientific demarcation, or does it operationalize something else under that name—and are its worst failure modes fixable without abandoning it?
