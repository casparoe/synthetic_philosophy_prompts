For a one-off workshop for AI policy staff, write a decision tree that sorts a proposed explanation of why an AI race between two labs or states ends in conflict into Fearon's three rationalist mechanisms — private information with incentives to misrepresent, commitment problems, issue indivisibilities — plus a leaf for 'not a rationalist explanation at all.' The tree should be usable by someone who has never read Fearon, so phrase every branch as a concrete AI-race case or claim (e.g., whether a trusted auditor who revealed both sides' true capabilities would make a deal possible, or whether the prize can be split or is winner-take-all) rather than as jargon. Keep it to at most four branch questions. Make sure one question separates stories where the obstacle would disappear if all private information were revealed, from stories where the obstacle is that a side can't credibly commit to keep a bargain once the power balance shifts. Flag any pairs of mechanisms the tree can't cleanly separate, and say whether any leaf is likely to stay empty in AI-race settings.
