I'm moderating a Q&A for a small online metaphysics reading group, and next week we're hosting a philosopher who defends necessitism — the view that neccessarily everything is necessarily something, so the domain of what there is stays the same from world to world. He also thinks the Barcan formula and its converse push you there (roughly: if there could have been an F, then there is something that could have been F; and if there is something that could have been F, then there could have been an F). I want eight to ten questions that actually press him rather than just letting him restate the view. Please make sure there's at least one question on each of these: (a) the objection that he's just redefining 'exists' by saying Napoleon is still something but merely isn't concrete anymore; (b) the problem of the merely possible sister — if she was never born, what makes her her rather than some other merely possible person; and (c) how we could ever know what's in the fixed domain if most of it was never concrete. Also give me a question that pushes on why he doesn't go actualist instead: accept the Barcan formula, reject the converse, and let worlds have shrinking domains. For every question, add the follow-up you'd ask if he gives the answer he's most likely to give. After the questions, write a short paragraph on where this line of questioning is most likely to collapse, and what answer from him would make you abandon the whole sequence and start over. And please, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, no numbered lists — just continuous prose, the way you'd write an email.
