I spent forty years doing physical chemistry, and now that I'm retired I've been reading aesthetics for fun. I keep noticing a pattern in art that would have been nonsense in the lab, and I'd like to know if it has a name. In my old line of work, if two samples had the same spectra and the same structure, they were the same stuff; nobody cared who grew the crystal or whether it was the original batch. But in art people care intensely about provenance even when the physical object is indistinguishable. A painting gets outed as a forgery and its price collapses, a museum moves it to storage, and critics who praised a passage last year write that they can now see its weakness. If you made a exact replica of the painting, atom for atom, it wouldn't repair anything; the copy goes in a different category. And I catch the same thing in myself: once I learn an artwork is a copy, it looks different to me, even though I know the light hitting my retina is unchanged. What I want to know is whether this gap — between physical sameness and the way we value and experience an object once we know its history — is a recognized phenomenon with an established name, or whether it's a tangle of several. I keep seeing the word 'authenticity,' but that seems to label a property of the thing, not the change in my judgement when I learn where it came from. A bare name wouldn't settle much for me; I want to know what exactly the phenomenon covers, which nearby concepts it is and isn't, and where its boundaries are. If it has no name, I'd like a precise definition and a couple of edge cases. And please tell me how confident you are in what you claim, and which point you're least sure of.
