After I retired from biochemistry I joined a philosophy reading group, and for most of the past year we have been stuck on the old argument about whether markets improve or corrupt human character. One camp keeps pushing the Montesquieu–Smith line that commerce softens manners (Hirschman's doux commerce label comes up a lot): buying and selling forces you to deal with strangers, so you learn punctuality, trustworthiness, and the habit of seeing things from the other fellow's point of view, and it drains the violence out of honor cultures and religious wars by giving everybody something boringly profitable to do. The other camp follows Rousseau, Marx, and more recently people like Sandel: the market reframes every relationship as a transaction, makes us calculating, teaches us to treat people as instruments, and crowds out generosity and civic spirit. What's driving me up the wall is that we've all agreed on the same facts, and we've even read the same empirical studies on markets and prosocialty (some point one way, some the other), and nobody has moved an inch.

My working hypothesis, from a guy who spent fourty years expecting data to settle arguments, is that this is not really an empirical disagreement at all. I suspect the two camps are running different counterfactuals — compared to what: feudalism, a subsistence village, a well-run welfare state? — and maybe using "character" or "corrupt" in different senses, one meaning the aggregate behavior a system produces and the other the motive underneath. But I've been wrong about philosophy before.

What I want is a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict for one side. What kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, substantive but about different questions, a hidden empirical disagreement, or bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a testable question, a redefinition, or an honest claim that it can't move?

3. Evidence for the diagnosis, from the actual moves each side makes (about 200 words)
4. What would count as progress, if anything (about 150 words)
