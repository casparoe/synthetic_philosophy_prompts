Please give me your credence that there could be, even in principle, a completed final theory of physics—one such that rational investigators would be warranted in expecting that no future theory will overturn it—rather than an endless succession of approximate descriptions. I do not want a bare number: decompose the estimate into sub-questions and put a number to each. In particular, I want at least the following terms: (i) the probability that the notion of a final theory is coherent enough to admit of truth or falsity; (ii) the probability that, if coherent, physical reality has the bounded explanatory structure such a theory would require, rather than an open-ended hierarchy of successively more accurate descriptions; (iii) the probability that, if such a theory existed, we could ever recognise it as final rather than merely the best theory we currently have; and (iv) the probability that underdetermination of theory by evidence is severe enough to block any principled judgement of finality. Add any further terms you think belong. Give a one-line justification for each number, show how you combine them, and state which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most. Please answer in British English.
