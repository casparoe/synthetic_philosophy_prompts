At my company we started using an LLM to draft internal incident reports, and two coworkers got into it over whether the model is bullshitting us. I read Harry Frankfurt's On Bullshit last week, and the bit I keep coming back to is his contrast between the liar and the bullshitter: the liar has to at least believe he knows the truth and then assert against it, whereas the bullshitter's defining trait is that he doesn't care whether what he says is true. That contrast assumes a speaker who has some relation to truth, even if a sloppy one. An LLM doing next-token prediction doesn't have beliefs, intentions, or even the capacity to care, so neither side of the distinction seems to fit cleanly. Does Frankfurt's argument survive this development? Specifically, is his bullshitter category flexible enough to cover a system that produces confident false statements without any relation to truth, or does LLM hallucination show that his distinction was implicitly relying on a human speaker and needs a third category like mere error or something else? I'm trying to figure out whether my coworkers argument is even well formed.
