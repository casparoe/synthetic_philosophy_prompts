I'm moderating a panel at a small animal-ethics workshop next month, and one panelist defends the view that, if you're going to eat meat, beef is morally much better than chicken. His headline argument is that a single cow yields a couple hundred kilos of meat while a broiler yields a kilo or two, so per calorie (and per gram of protein) chicken kills vastly more individual animals. I need eight to ten questions for him that push past that headline argument instead of letting him restate it. Please include at least one question on each of the following:

- whether raw death-counts are even the right metric, given that cows and chickens have very different capacities for suffering and very different life histories;
- the crop-cultivation objection: a vegan can say plant-based diets also kill field animals during harvest, but the obvious reply is that conventional beef is fed on corn and soy with terrible feed-conversion ratios, so the grass-fed case ends up doing all the work;
- how wild animal welfare enters the leadger, especially through climate: beef has a much larger carbon footprint than chicken, so if he takes climate seriously he has to say whether wild animals harmed by a warming planet count in the beef-vs-chicken comparison, and with what weight.

For each of these, write the follow-up you'd give to the answer you expect him to give (e.g., if he backs off raw counting and adopts something like suffering-weighted life-years, ask him where the cow-to-chicken weighting comes from and whether the whole argument now hangs on a number he can't defend). Also include at least one question on what follows in practice — should welfare-minded omnivores switch from chicken to beef, and does that recommendation survive the land, feed, and climate costs — and at least one question that pushes the possibility that the real conclusion is neither beef nor chicken but some third option.
