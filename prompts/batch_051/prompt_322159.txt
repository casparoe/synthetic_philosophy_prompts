The question, worth 10 points and answered in 20 minutes under exam conditions, was:

Quine's article Two Dogmas of Empiricism rejects two commitments he attributes to mainstream empiricism. Identify those two commitments, reconstruct Quine's main reasons for rejecting each, and then assess the following claim: because Quine's holism permits revision even of logic and mathematics, Quine is committed to denying that any knowledge is a priori.

One student's answer was:

Quine's two dogmas are the distinction between analytic and synthetic statements, and reductionism, which treats every meaningful statement as translatable into a construction of sense experience and confirmable or disconfirmable in isolation. On the first dogma, Quine considers the available ways of defining analyticity. A sentence might be called analytic if it is true purely in virtue of meaning, if it is a logical truth, or if it can be converted into a logical truth by substituting synonyms for synonyms. He argues that the first proposal merely restates the distinction, and that the third proposal presupposes synonymy. The natural ways of explaining synonymy—definition, interchangeability salva veritate, and semantical rules—each either presuppose analyticity or fall short of capturing it, so the proposed definitions are, in an important sense, circular. Quine concludes that the distinction cannot be drawn in a principled way.

On the second dogma, Quine rejects the idea that each statement has its own separate empirical content. His holism says that our beliefs confront experience as a whole; when experience conflicts with expectation, we may revise any part of the system, including logic and mathematics, provided we make compensating adjustments elsewhere. The two dogmas are connected: once holism is accepted, the idea of a statement true no matter what experience brings loses its contrast with ordinary empirical statements. Since every belief is in principle open to revision, Quine thinks there are no analytic truths, and if there are no analytic truths, then there can be no a priori knowledge either.
