A friend of mine—she used to be a molecular biologist, so we both come at this from the science side—has apparently spend years quietly collecting verdicts about which things she thinks can be wronged directly, not just through some human who would be upset. I've been writing them down:

- A normal pig: can be wronged.
- A rat: can be wronged.
- A mouse brain emulated neuron-by-neuron on a computer, running normally: can be wronged.
- That same emulation paused or frozen between time steps: can still be wronged, and deleting it would be wrong.
- A current large language model that says it is in agony and wants you to stop: cannot be wronged.
- A robotic arm that uses a simple reinforcement policy to avoid collisions: cannot be wronged.
- A human in deep dreamless sleep: can be wronged.

The new case I want to predict her on is this. Suppose we build a chip that implements, in fine-grained detail, a mammal's pain network—the activation patterns are genuinely the sort associated with pain—but the device is wiped and reset every three seconds. No memories, no continuity, each three-second episode starts from a blank state. At any given moment, by hypothesis, there is something it is like to be it, and it hurts. Is that a thing that can be wronged?
