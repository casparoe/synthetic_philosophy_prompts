While working on a story about a debate over whether petitionary prayer could be rational, I reconstructed one participant's argument as follows, and I have not been able to find where it fails.

(1) An act of asking can do any work only in one of two ways: by conveying information the person asked would otherwise lack, or by causing the person asked to change what they had already resolved to do.
(2) God is omniscient, so no prayer conveys information God would otherwise lack.
(3) God is immutable, so no prayer causes God to change what God had already resolved to do.
(4) Therefore petitionary prayer can do no work.

The argument seems to prove too much, because (1) is offered as a fully general truth about asking:
(5) Applied to ordinary life, (1) implies that a request is pointless whenever the person asked already knew the need and had already formed a settled intention about it.
(6) But we do not in fact regard ordinary requests as pointless in all such cases.

I am not certain which premise to reject, and I suspect each rejection has a cost. Which step goes wrong, why did that step look unavoidable, and what would rejecting it commit me to saying about what petitionary prayer is doing?
