Okay, I've been going back and forth with a friend about superrationality and he claims he's proved it just is evidential decision theory with a nicer name. The argument feels wrong but I can't find the bad step, so tell me where it goes off and what it costs to reject it.

(1) Superrationality says: in a symmetric one-shot prisoner's dilemma, you should cooperate, because you have to assume the other player -- reasoning exactly as you are from exactly the same situation -- will land on the same act you land on, and both-cooperating beats both-defecting for each of you.
(2) The principle operating under the surface here is: pick the act such that, conditional on your picking it, the world is expected to be best. In the PD, conditional on your cooperating, your counterpart also cooperate; conditional on your defecting, your counterpart defects; so cooperate wins.
(3) But that is exactly evidential decision theory: evaluate acts by the news they give you about the world, not by what they cause.
(4) So superrationality is just EDT; anything one of them recommends, the other recommends too.
(5) But then superrationality inherits EDT's counterexamples. In the smoking lesion case, smoking doesn't cause cancer: a gene causes both the smoking and the cancer. Still, smoking is strong evidence you have the lesion, so EDT tells you not to smoke even though the cigarette does no causal harm.
(6) So superrationality tells you not to smoke in the smoking lesion case, which is just wrong.
(7) My friend says you can't squirm out by restricting superrationality to 'agents reasoning in lockstep.' The whole selling point was that once you allow the correlation between your reasoning and theirs to matter, you've allowed correlations to matter in general, and the smoking-lesion correlation is just one more correlation. It's all or nothing.

So where does this go wrong? Is the move from (1) to (2) already the mistake, or is (7) where he overreaches? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep cooperating in the one-shot PD than keep EDT, but I can't see how to have the one without the other.
