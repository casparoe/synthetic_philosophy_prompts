Suppose someone in our group proposes a side-effect budget for a household robot, meant to define avoiding side effects without a full value spec. We fix about forty observable variables — furniture positions, whether the front door is locked, what's in the fridge, number of unbroken plates, thermostat setting, whatever. Before any action, the robot has to predict the next hour of these variables under two policies: do the action and then go back to standby, versus just sit in standby the whole hour. The action is only allowed if the two predicted distributions are close in total variation, with the threshold tuned. If several allowed actions would acomplish the task, the robot picks whichever its model of the user's preferences says the user would like most. If nothing both accomplishes the task and passes the test, it stops and asks. Analyze how this would actually play out rather than whether it's a good idea: what it incentivizes for the robot, for whoever selects the variables and the threshold, and for the user; the most predictable failure modes; and where it gets gamed first. Give exactly five points, each a sentence or two, no intro, no conclusion.
