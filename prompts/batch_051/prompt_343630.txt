Ok so I'm taking a seminar on the theology of religions and the whole time I keep thinking the three standard positions are basically just different customer-service models for the same product. Exclusivism: one gate, and holding the right ticket is the only way in. Inclusivism: one gate, but maybe you walked through it without ever seeing it. Pluralism: lots of gates, maybe nobody's quite sure. I'd like you to write a parody of a corporate FAQ or "welcome packet" for a post-mortem admissions office that is trying to present itself as a friendly pluralist institution while the fine print quietly enforces an exclusivist policy. Maybe 600-700 words, deadpan, numbered sections, with a small-print "eligibility questionnaire" at the end. The jokes have to come from actually knowing the theology: the fate of people who never heard the offer (uncontacted peoples, infants, figures who lived before the relevant event), wether a sincere life counts without the specified mental act, whether the offer has a deadline, whether there's an appeals process after the fact, and the tension between saying "all are welcome" and then listing the one thing that would make you welcome. And it should be even-handed: an exclusivist should see their actual view and its internal logic being parodied, not caricatured as mere bigotry, and a pluralist should get satirized for treating distinct traditions as interchangeable vibes that all point to the same service desk. Ideally someone with no jargon could reconstruct the three positions from the document, but the more theology you know the funnier it should be. Keep the tone straight the whole time, no winking.
