I've been trying to get my head around the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery that Hofstadter wrote about (the one where Scientific American actually ran a version and people sent in googolplexes). I tried to reconstruct the superrational reasoning for both and ended up with a proof that the Luring Lottery has no sensible superrational answer. I want to know if the argument is valid and if not which step fails.

Take m players.

(1) In a symmetric game, superrational players should choose the same strategy, since they're all equally rational and face the same problem.
(2) In Platonia, if each of m players sends the telegram with probability p, the probablity exactly one sends is m p (1-p)^(m-1).
(3) This is maximized at p = 1/m.
(4) So the superrational strategy in Platonia is to send with probability 1/m.

Now the Luring Lottery. If I submit x entries and expect the other players together to submit N entries, total entries are x+N. The prize is one million divided by total entries, and my chance of having the winning entry is x/(x+N). So my expected payoff is x/(x+N) times one million/(x+N). Setting the derivative to zero gives x = N.

(5) So against a fixed total N of other entries, the best number of entries to submit is x = N.
(6) By the same symmetry reasoning as step (1), all superrational players in the Luring Lottery will submit the same number of entries, call it s.
(7) So for any one player, the other entries total N = (m-1)s.
(8) Plugging (7) into (5) gives s = (m-1)s, which for m > 2 forces s = 0.
(9) So the only superrational strategy is to submit zero entries, i.e., not enter, making the lottery pointless.

That conclusion seems wrong, so I think there's a hidden flaw. Is the argument valid? If not, which step exactly fails, and is it because the symmetry step is too strong, or because the best-response calculation in step (5) doesn't apply when N is fixed by everyone else's identical strategy? Or is the fix that the real superrational strategy is mixed, like roll a die with m sides and only enter on a 1?
