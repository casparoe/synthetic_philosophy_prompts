Im kicking around a mechanism and want to know how it would actually behave. A mid-size software company scraps annual performance reviews. Every quarter, all the individual contributors get dumped into a pool and the team leads take turns drafting them onto project teams, fantasy-sports style. A person's bonus for that quarter is set by draft position: first-rounders get the full bonus, fourth-rounders get a quarter, undrafted get nothing. The board says the draft order stays secret and nobody publishes the rankings, but everyone can work out roughly when they got picked from the bonus line on their pay stub. Walk me through what this does to the place over three or four years: who accumulates money and status, who burns out, what new forms of sucking up and sabotage appear (hoarding skills, poisoning the well, trading favors with leads, sandbagging a rival before the draft), what happens to honest dissent and unglamorous work, and whether the thing builds or corrodes the version of character the competition-is-good crowd claims for it. Then tell me which failure modes can be fixed with small tweaks and which are baked in.
