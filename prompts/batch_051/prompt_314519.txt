I'm on my university's athletics disciplinary board and we're stuck on a case that has half the panel muttering about strict liability and mens rea. A distance runner tested positive for a banned stimulant before conference championships. She claims she never knowingly took anything banned, and the facts back her up: the positive came from an over-the-counter pre-workout powder she bought at the campus store (which the university operates), she kept the receipt and an unopened tub, and a lab found the stimulant as an undeclared contaminant in that batch. But the rule is strict — it says athletes are "strictly responsible for any prohibited substance found in their sample, and lack of intent, knowledge, or fault is not a defense." One camp says the rule is strict on purpose, to avoid endless excuses and messy fact-finding, and that enforcing it is what protects clean athletes. The other camp says a full-season suspension for someone who did what any reasonable person would do is punishment without fault — made worse because our own store sold her the contaminated stuff. There's an equity clause that lets us reduce a sanction if "the violation was not the athlete's fault in any meaningful sense," but its not clear whether that applies to strict-liability drug cases or only to paperwork-type violations. Draft us a decision memo: uphold the full suspension, overturn it, or modify the sanction (say exactly what), and give the strongest argument on the side you're not taking. No preamble — begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning.
