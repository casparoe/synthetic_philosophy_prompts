Over the past few months I have been working through Peirce's writings with a colleague who is preparing a seminar on the fixation of belief, and I have been pushed to state, for my own benefit, what I actually accept. The result is a list of commitments that I would like audited, because I can no longer tell whether they form a single coherent position or simply a set of things I find myself saying. I do not know where the strain is, and I would rather not signal in advance which item I would be willing to give up.

Here is the list.

1. The pragmatic maxim is my criterion of meaning: the meaning of a conception is exhausted by the conceivable practical differences its object would make to conduct, experience, or expectation. If two hypotheses would make no such difference under any circumstances I can conceive, I treat them as the same hypothesis.

2. Belief is a habit of action. To believe a proposition is to be disposed to act on it and to have one's expectations shaped by it, so that a disappointment of those expectations would present itself as a surprise.

3. Among the ways in which belief gets fixed, only the method of science is answerable to an external constraint. Tenacity, authority, and the a priori method can produce stability or comfort, but they cannot put belief in a position to be corrected by something independent of the believer's inclinations.

4. Fallibilism admits no exception. Empirical results are revisable, but so are the principles of logic and mathematics; there is no proposition with absolute immunity from revision.

5. Truth is the opinion that the community of inquirers would agree on in the long run if inquiry were pushed as far as it could go, and reality is the object of that eventual opinion.

6. Genuine doubt is not something one can adopt as a costume. It arises as a concrete irritation about a specific matter, and inquiry cannot begin from a resolve to doubt everything. Cartesian doubt, understood as methodological, is sterile as a starting point.

I would like an audit rather than a lecture. Go through these six commitments and tell me which pairs are in tension, ordering the tensions by severity. For each tension, say which commitment or commitments would have to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Also say explicitly where a tension is merely apparent and dissolves once the relevant notions are understood. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent, and at what cost.
