I teach moral philosophy and am supervising a doctoral student who is reconstructing a visiting moral philosopher's view from the rulings she gives on rescue cases, treated as a body of case law. I want an independent reading before the student presents to our seminar. The rulings so far are these. (1) In a choice between saving one of two injured climbers, both strangers, equally endangered, and otherwise similar in every relevant respect, she held that the only fair procedure is to toss a fair coin, and that the choice is then fully justified whichever way it goes. (2) In a case where one trapped caver would certainly drown unless rescued while three other cavers would suffer only a long, cold, uncomfortable walk back, she held that the rescuer should save the one, on the ground that the imperilled caver's claim is weightier than each of the others' claims. (3) Faced with two rafts of stranded passengers, two strangers on each, all in equal danger and with time to reach only one raft, she again held that the two sides should be given an equal chance by a fair toss. In discussion she has maintained that a rescuer answers to each affected individual's claim, and she has not yet ruled on a case in which the numbers differ while the jeopardy and need on each side are otherwise identical. Next week she must rule on precisely that case: a single, indivisible rescue effort can save either one stranger on one shore or five different strangers on the other shore; all six face the same danger of death, no special relationships obtain, and no further help will arrive in time. Predict what she will say she ought to do. In your answer, set out the two or three most plausible rules that fit her track record and say what each predicts in the one-versus-five case; give your best prediction with the reasons for preferring that reading; and specify one further concrete case, not in her record, that would most efficiently discriminate among the candidates your prediction leaves standing. Conclude by saying how confident you are in this prediction and singling out the point on which you are least sure.
