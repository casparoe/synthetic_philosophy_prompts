Something keeps turning up in things I read and watch, and I would like to know whether the pattern has a recognized name or whether I am forcing three different things under one heading. The first instance is a kind of solitary wasp described in a natural history book I read long ago. It stings a cricket so that the cricket is immobilized but alive, drags it to the lip of its burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to inspect, and then comes out to haul the cricket down. If, while the wasp is underground, someone nudges the cricket a few inches away, the wasp does not simply grab it from the new spot; it brings the cricket back to the lip of the burrow, leaves it there, goes inside to inspect the now-empty burrow again, and then comes out and finishes the job. The whole performance looks purposeful until this one small change reveals that the wasp can only run the sequence from the top. The second instance is a robotic vacuum cleaner I once watched: when it was lifted mid-cleaning and set down a meter away, it behaved as though the displacement had not happened, steering back toward the region where it had been lifted and resuming its old pattern rather than planning from where it now was. The third is more human: a colleague who, when interrupted while reading aloud from a prepared statement, goes back to the start of the paragraph rather than continuing from the point of interruption, even when continuing would plainly save time.

I am not looking for a bare label for any one of these examples. What I want to know is whether there is an established concept, in philosophy of mind or in the study of animal behavior, for this general feature: a system that appears to be flexibly pursuing a goal, but whose behavior is exposed, under a small perturbation, as a chain of sub-routines that must be re-run from some fixed point because the system is not really tracking the changed state of the world. If the concept exists, how precisely is it defined, how does it differ from ordinary habit, instinct, and a simple feedback loop, and does my colleague's behavior fall inside it or is that a different kind of rigidity? If no single concept covers all three, I would rather you tell me what the neighboring concepts are and where the boundaries lie. I am also interested in what the distinction does and does not settle: does finding this rigidity in a wasp and a machine tell us anything about whether our own apparent flexibility is just a much longer list of such routines, or whether the free will debate turns on that very question? Please answer in continuous prose, as you would in a letter, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
