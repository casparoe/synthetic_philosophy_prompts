I am on the governing committee of a small housing co-operative, and we have to settle a dispute that has been running for two years. The co-op maintains shared amenities — a workshop and tool library, a communal garden, a laundry, and a bike shed — funded by a monthly levy and four maintenance work days a year. Around a fifth of residents contribute nothing, in money or labour, while using the amenities as much as anyone else. Some of them cannot contribute (chronic illness, disability, caring duties, very low income); others are able but decline, saying the amenities would exist whether or not they helped, and that the co-op's founding statement describes the facilities as open to all residents without condition. The committee must decide whether to adopt a rule tying continued access to a minimum contribution, paid in money or in-kind labour, with exemptions for those unable to contribute, or to keep access unconditional and rely on persuasion. Draft a short policy memo for the committee recommending a decision (or a variant), with criteria members can apply to the hard cases and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The memo should not simply review options; it should set out a defensible principle about what, if anything, is wrong with taking without contributing. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary, and make the memo open with its recommendation in the first sentence. Write in British English.
