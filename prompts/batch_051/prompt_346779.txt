I've been chewing on this for a few years now, since I tried to make our place mostly self-sufficient and it didn't pan out, and also from listening to people argue after the supply chain meltdown that countries should make everything themselves. I'm not a philosopher or anything, just someone who reads alot and thinks about it. I finally wrote down the argument that's been nagging at me, and I'd like to know how it holds up. Here it is—

Self-sufficiency as an ideal seems to assume that dependence is the problem and producing things yourself is the solution. But dependence isn't one thing, its a question of how your dependencies are arranged. If you rely on one fragile source you can't replace, you're fragile. If you rely on fifty sources and any one of them failing just means you go to the others, you're not. The thing people actually point to when they defend self-sufficiency isn't independence, it's resilience: not getting knocked over when the world hiccups. And weirdly, total self-sufficiency makes you less resilient, because it concentrates all the points of failure in one place — you. On the homestead it was exactly this: I was the well as much as the well was, and the year I got hurt and the pump went out the same month, there was no buffer, because I had spent years trying to be the buffer. What would have saved us was more redundancy, more slack, more people we could call — which is dependence by another name. Countries are the same: autarky means one supplier for everything, yourself, when the actual goal is many suppliers, spare capacity, and the ability to switch. So self-sufficiency isn't the opposite of interdependence, it's a poor substitute for it. Maybe the ideal still matters for character — not being a burden, knowing how to do things without help — but I can't tell if that's a real point or just me not wanting the whole project to feel stupid.

Anyway — I'd be grateful for comments: am I just redefining self-sufficiency so it means something nobody meant? Is the jump from a person to a country fair? And should I keep that last bit about character or cut it, because I genuinely can't tell if it's doing any work or if I'm just defensive.
