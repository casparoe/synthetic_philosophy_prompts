Darwin's account of morality treats the moral sense as an evolved trait rooted in social instincts such as sympathy, further refined by reason, habit, and the opinions of one's community, and he did not draw a debunking conclusion from this. Consider a contemporary evolutionary debunking argument: natural selection shaped our evaluative tendencies to track fitness, not mind-independent moral facts; so, if moral realism is true, either the alignment between our evolved moral judgments and the moral facts is a coincidence, or it must be explained by some third factor, such as facts about cooperation or flourishing. Suppose Darwin were confronted with this argument and with the claim that his evolutionary account undermines moral realism. What would his considered position be? Make the prediction precise by saying which horn of the dilemma he would accept or reject, how he would assess the third-factor strategy, and where in the argument, given his own commitments, he would locate the mistake or the insight.
