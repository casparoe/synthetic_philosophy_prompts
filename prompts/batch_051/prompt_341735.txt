Think about Williams's Jim and the Indians case. Now change it: the twenty captives are not all strangers to Jim — one is his wife — and Pedro says that if Jim wants the other nineteen to go free, Jim himself must pick which one to shoot. If he refuses, Pedro kills all twenty. How, if at all, does this alter the standard utilitarian verdict? Does Williams's integrity objection get stronger or weaker when Jim's own deliberation must include singling out a victim he loves? And is there a version of the one thought too many worry that applies to Jim either if he saves his wife by killing a stranger or if he kills her to save the nineteen?
