I've been sketching out a "karma ledger" app for my cohousing community, and I want to know how it would actually behave before I pitch it. Think through it carefully — maybe start by brainstorming the obvious ways people would game it, then go deeper.

Here's the proposal. Everyone starts with a balance of zero karma points. If someone does something that helps or harms another member, the affected person (or a witness) can submit a short report describing what happened. A panel of five randomly selected members, none of whom were involved or are close to the people involved, reads the report and rates it from -5 to +5 whole points: negative for harms, positive for benefits. If at least four of the five panelists agree on the sign, the average of all five ratings, rounded toward zero, is added to the reported person's balance. The person who filed the report gets nothing unless the report is upheld, in which case they get a flat +1 point (to cover their trouble and encourage reporting). Karma can go negative. Positive karma can be spent on concrete perks: skipping the waitlist for the shared workshop, priority in the garden-plot lottery, booking the guest room before others, that sort of thing. Spending karma just burns the points; they don't go to anyone. Negative karma below -10 temporarily restricts you from using shared amenities. Everyone's balance also drifts one point toward zero each month, so past deeds slowly fall off the books. And here's the rule Im least sure about: karma is transferable — you can gift any amount of your balance to another member, and you're allowed to go into the negative doing it, so you can literally "owe" someone.

What I want is an honest teardown of how this system would play out: what it incentivizes, where it gets gamed first, what second-order effects show up after a year or two, and whether it would actually track moral desert or just track popularity, visibility, and social capital. Also, do the failure modes have fixes that don't gut the whole point of the thing — which, in my head, is to make good deeds pay off and bad deeds cost you in a way that's fair and legible.
