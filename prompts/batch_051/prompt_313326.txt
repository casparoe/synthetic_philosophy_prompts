Bernard Williams died in 2003, so he never saw the effective altruism movement take shape, and as a philosophy PhD student I've been going back and forth trying to write a paper on what he would have made of it. Specifically, I want to know how his integrity objection — the worry that impartial moral theories alienate people from the projects and commitments that give their lives shape — and the one-thought-too-many point would apply to earning to give: the idea that people should take high-paying jobs they might not otherwise want so they can donate the excess, because an impartial cost-benefit calculation says that does the most good. Do you think Williams would've seen earning to give as exactly the kind of thing where impartial morality inserts an extra thought into nearly every corner of a persons life? Or is there a version of it he'd have been less hostile to? Give me your best reconstruction of his likely view, and flag the places where you're genuinely unsure. Please write in American English.
