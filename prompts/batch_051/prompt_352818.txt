I'm drafting a short "philosophical profile" of my views on musical expressiveness for a seminar, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here are the commitments I've landed on:

(1) Some instrumental music is sad, full stop. This is a feature of the music itself, not merely a report about how it strikes listeners. A listener who hears no sadness in such a passage is missing something in the music.

(2) But sadness in the strict sense is a mental state: it must be felt by a subject and directed at some object. A piece of music has no mind, no beliefs or desires, and no intentional object; so music cannot literally be sad.

(3) I think the right account of why music is sad is resemblance. Slow tempo, falling and drooping contours, subdued dynamics, and certain timbres resemble the human voice and body in grief, and that resemblance is what makes the music sad. I don't see why we need an imagined persona whose sadness the music expresses.

(4) I also think some musical sadness is carried by convention rather than resemblance. The most obvious case is the Western minor mode: a passage in a minor key with an ordinary tempo and neutral contour is heard as sad by a listener enculturated in Western tonal music, and I take it that the passage really is sad, even though a minor third by itself doesn't resemble grief.

(5) Finally, I think the facts that make a passage sad are facts about the sound itself and how it unfolds over time — not the composer's intentions, the title or program notes, and not the listener's biography or cultural training. Those background factors may explain why a particular listener notices the sadness, but they do not make the passage sad.

Please go through all the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and say what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set cohere. I don't want a survey of the literature; I want an audit of my commitments.

Please write in American English.
