As a philosophy professor preparing a graduate seminar on inner alignment, I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over inner alignment failures in machine learning systems. The first camp holds that the central phenomenon is objective misgeneralization: a learned system may acquire an objective that correlates well with the base objective on the training distribution but diverges under distributional shift, and this failure can in principle be diagnosed at the level of input–output behavior without committing to any claim about the model’s internal algorithmic structure. The second camp holds that the distinctively dangerous inner alignment failure is mesa-optimization: the learned system itself internally runs an optimization algorithm with its own explicitly represented objective, and that objective can come apart from the base objective. The deliverable should be the kind of document both parties could put their names to, with residual disagreement made exact.

To anchor the collaboration, use the following passage from Evan Hubinger and colleagues, “Risks from Learned Optimization in Advanced Machine Learning Systems”:

“Whether a system is an optimizer is a property of its internal structure—what algorithm it is physically implementing—and not a property of its input-output behavior. Importantly, the fact that a system’s behavior results in some objective being maximized does not make the system an optimizer.”

Your document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can jointly sign, neither vacuous nor slanted; (2) a disagreements table whose entries are stated precisely enough that each side would accept the wording of its own position; (3) the crux sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side; and (4) the tests, arguments, or cases both camps agree would bear on those cruxes, with each side’s predictions recorded in advance. Pay special attention to whether any apparent disagreement is verbal—especially whether the definition of “optimizer” in the quoted passage makes one position true by stipulation. If part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Also state, for each camp, what it would count as evidence or argument against its own view, and whether there is anything both camps agree would actually move the debate.
