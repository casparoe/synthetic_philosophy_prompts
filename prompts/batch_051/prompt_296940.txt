In Hume’s Treatise of Human Nature, Book 3, Part 2, Section 7, he writes:

The consequences of every breach of equity seem to lie very remote, and are not able to counter-ballance any immediate advantage, that may be reap’d from it.

I take him to be claiming that free riding is usually not caused by people failing to understand that cooperation is in their interest, but by the fact that the benefit of chipping in is remote and shared while the benefit of slacking is near and private. If that is the diagnosis, then a rule should work by making each person’s contribution and its absence feel near, concrete, and socially visible rather than by giving people better arguments.

Here is a mechanism I have been thinking about for a small voluntary group, such as a shared workshop, an allotment society, or an amateur rowing club. At the start of each quarter, every member must announce publicly, in front of the others, one specific maintenance or improvement task they personally will complete within that quarter, with a completion date. At the next meeting, any member who has not done their announced task automatically loses their vote and their key privileges for the following quarter, unless a majority votes to excuse them on the basis of a genuine emergency. A person under penalty may not make a new announcement until the penalty ends, so they remain without a vote until they clear the debt in some other way.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position. Analyse how it would actually play out: what it incentivises, how a self-interested member would game it, what second-order effects it would produce among the conscientious and the resentful alike, and what happens to useful work that is real but hard to announce or verify. Then give a committed judgement: if Hume’s diagnosis above is right, is this the right kind of remedy, or has it simply replaced one form of remoteness with another? Which single feature of the mechanism is doing the most work, and what is the best way to alter it without bringing back the original free-rider problem? Please answer in British English.
