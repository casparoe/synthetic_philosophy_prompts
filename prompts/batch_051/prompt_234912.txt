Spinoza argues in the Tractatus Theologico-Politicus that Scripture should be interpreted the way one interprets nature: assemble the internal data of the text, and from those data alone infer what its authors meant, without importing one’s own theology. That is a specific version of a broader claim I keep seeing in debates about sola scriptura and constitutional originalism: that a text can act as an independent constraint on interpretation, not just as a screen onto which a community projects its prior commitments. I want to know whether that constraint is a testable feature of the interpretive process or an ideal we can only approximate. Design the strongest test you can—perhaps using a constructed text, a legal corpus, or a real scriptural community—that would distinguish a text actually constraining interpretation from a community collectively using the text to ratify conclusions it already holds. Say exactly what pattern of agreement, disagreement, or revision would count as evidence for each reading, and then be precise about what a positive result would still fail to settle: whether the remaining gap is a limitation of the available data or a defect in the very idea of “independent meaning.” Please write the answer in American English.
