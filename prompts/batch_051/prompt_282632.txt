I keep finding myself in this weird spot and I want someone to force me out of it. The animal-welfare case for eating big animals instead of small ones has me convinced: a steer gives you something like three hundred kilos of meat for one life and one death, while a chicken gives you maybe two kilos for a life that was also mostly suffering, so getting the same food from chicken means raising and killing on the order of a hundred times as many animals, each with a nervous system and a capacity to suffer. The theory is dead simple — count expected suffering per calorie, use one currency, treat every animal as a unit that can suffer — and the verdict is clean: people who swap beef for chicken 'for the animals' have it backwards; eat the biggest animal you can find. I can't find a crack in the arithhmetic.

And yet I can't get myself to believe the lesson of animal ethics is to lean into beef. Partly there's the intuition that a cow is not a scaled-up chicken: it lives years rather than weeks, it's behaviorally more complicated, and if consciousness comes in degrees then its feedlot misery should swamp the suffering of a chicken, not be outweighed by the fact that the chicken is just one of hundreds. But the thing I really can't shake is the wild animal part. Beef's land footprint is enormous — pasture plus the grain and hay we grow to finish them. Chicken is a much more efficient converter of feed into meat, but the feed is corn and soy grown on intensively managed cropland, where the combine shreds field mice and voles and the pesticides clear out insects. If wild suffering goes in the same ledger — if the mouse caught by a combine matters the way the chicken in the shed matters — then the clean verdict might flip, and flip on messy empirical facts about whether pasture supports more wild animals than row crops kill. Worse, if wild suffering does count, I'm not sure the original decision procedure survives, because it was built for animals we deliberately breed and kill, not animals whose deaths are just part of a landscape we happen to own.

So here's what I want. Should the clean ledger be amended — wild animals counted, welfare weighted by lifespan or intensity of consciousness or whether a harm is deliberate rather than incidental — or should the intuition that a cow's misery matters more, and that wild animal suffering isn't ours to manage, be debunked and dropped? Is there a reading of 'count in the ledger' where the arithmetic and the intuition both survive, or do I have to give one up? Give me the strongest case for each move and then tell me which part you'd actually give up, and what it would take to justify that.
