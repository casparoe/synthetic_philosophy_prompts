So I've been going back and forth on the whole "is your data your property" question, and I keep feeling like the two sides are talking past eachother. On one hand there's a propertization camp: give individuals a transferable bundle of rights — exclude, license, sell, delete — over information about them, so platforms have to actually buy what they now just take. On the other hand there's a dignitary/personhood camp that thinks that move concedes the whole game: if data about you is something you can sell, privacy becomes another priced good, and someone who can't afford to keep their data private gets bought out of their own personhood — like how the law won't enforce a contract to sell your vote or sell yourself into slavery even when it's consensual. I want to force these two into an adversarial collaboration and get the document they could both sign after genuinely engaging rather than scoring points.

Draft that joint statement. It should include (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — about what personal data is, why the status quo is broken, what "control" over data amounts to, and where the disagreement is not located; (2) a disagreements table where each row states one residual disagreement, with each camp's own view phrased so that camp would sign the wording; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, the concrete case or argument both sides agree is a fair test, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I especially want to know whether they can agree about co-authored data (a message I send involves me and the recipient — we both "generate" it) and whether that breaks any individual property right; and whether something like a universal data dividend would answer the personhood objection that commodification itself is the harm. If part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Aim for formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted.
