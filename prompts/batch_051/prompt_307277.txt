I've been trying to pin down what makes something play and not just a pastime or a job in disguise. Huizinga seemed to think play is free, set apart from ordinary life, absorbing enough that you lose track of the clock, and done for its own sake; Pieper worried that once you start optimizing leisure—hobbies with metrics, vacations with itineraries, self-improvement disguised as rest—you've quietly turned it back into work. I'd like a working definition of 'play' that handles these cases: a kid inventing a game alone in the yard; two dogs play-fighting; a professional footballer mid-match, paid and aware the result affects his contract; someone gambling in a casino; a scientist fiddling with a side experiment on a Sunday; a chess grandmaster grinding opening lines for a tournament; a jazz musician improvising during a paid gig; two people flirting; a video game speedrunner resetting the same run a hundred times to shave off half a second; and a con artist 'playing' a mark. I'm confident about the first one (play) and pretty sure the last one is a different, manipulative sense of the word, but I cant decide the middle cases. Give me the definition, run it through all ten, tell me which verdicts come out differently than you'd have guessed, and say whether a single definition can do the job or whether 'play' is really two overlapping ideas wearing one word.
