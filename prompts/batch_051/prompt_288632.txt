I'm on a university curriculm committee approving a new supplementary physics unit. The unit uses the curved-tube task from Michael McCloskey's 1983 Scientific American article on intuitive physics—the one where a ball is shot through a curved tube and, after it exits, many students predict it will keep curving and explain that the ball has acquired an internal force or impetus. The unit's authors argue this is not a random error but evidence of a systematic impetus theory students impose on motion problems. Some members worry that having students articulate that theory legitimizes it and makes the Newtonian correction harder. Draft the committee's formal decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. State a clear recommendation, use the McCloskey passage to justify your reasoning, and honestly address the strongest argument on the losing side.
