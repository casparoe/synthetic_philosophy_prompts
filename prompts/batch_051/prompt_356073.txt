I'm on the rules committee for a club whose entire game is that the rulebook can be changed by following the rulebook. Our job isn't to pass proposals, just to rule on whether they're valid to go to a vote. Right now there are three rules:

Rule 1: any member may propose a rule change, in writing, at least 48 hours before a meeting.
Rule 2: a proposal is adopted if it receives a two-thirds vote at a meeting where at least half the membership is present.
Rule 3: any rule of the club, including Rule 3 itself, may be amended or repealed by a proposal adopted under Rule 2.

Our precendents so far:

- A proposal to repeal Rule 3. We ruled it invalid: you can't use the amendment rule to abolish the amendment rule.
- A proposal to amend Rule 2 so that all future proposals need a unanimous vote rather than two-thirds. We ruled it valid: the old threshold governs the vote on the new threshold, and after that the new threshold applies.
- A proposal to amend Rule 3 to read "any rule other than Rule 3 may be amended or repealed by a proposal adopted under Rule 2." We ruled it invalid: after that, no rule would authorize changes to Rule 3 itself, so Rule 3 would be stuck as is.

Next week's proposal is to amend Rule 3 by adding one sentence: "any future amendment to Rule 3 requires a unanimous vote."

What should we rule if we're trying to be consistent with those three decisions, and which old case is the closest analog? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, lay out the two or three rules we might be implicitly running, say what each predicts for this proposal, tell me which you'd bet on and why, and give me a further proposal that would cleanly seperate the candidate rules.
