David Hume argued in his essays "Of Money" and "Of the Balance of Trade" that a nation's stock of money is not itself wealth: a larger quantity of money eventually raises prices, leaving real commerce unchanged, though an inflow can briefly stimulate activity before prices adjust; and bullion outflows need not be feared, since relative prices and trade flows adjust to draw money back. Bitcoin appeared long after Hume's death and departs from the metallic money he analyzed in three ways: its supply is predetermined and algorithmically capped, it has no state issuer, and it circulates across borders outside the banking system. Working strictly from the arguments Hume gives in those two essays, argue for how he would most likely assess the claim that a fixed-supply, borderless digital currency improves economic life. Address at least the following: (a) whether he would regard Bitcoin's fixed supply as a virtue or a defect, given his distinction between the short-run stimulus from new money and its long-run price effects; (b) whether he would see Bitcoin's detachment from national price levels and trade balances as solving or undermining the self-correcting mechanism he described; and (c) whether that mechanism can operate for a currency with no single domestic price level or central authority. End with a last line beginning "Verdict:" that states your overall conclusion in one sentence.
