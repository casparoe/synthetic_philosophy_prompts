For a long-form article I am writing about the governance of artificial intelligence, I have been working through a single concrete counterfactual. Suppose it is 2029, and after several close calls in which training runs produced capabilities that the developers' own evaluations had not anticipated, a coalition of leading frontier laboratories, several major states, and an independent technical body signs an agreement I will call the Meridian Compact. The signatories adopt three mechanisms: (1) a hard ceiling on the computational resources any single project may use—measured at the chip and data-center level through third-party telemetry—unless a supermajority certifies that safety evaluations are reliable at that scale; (2) a moratorium on any system that can modify its own training process without a complete human-auditable trail; and (3) an escrowed 'post-AGI dividend,' under which each lab contributes a share of revenues from frontier models and reserves some high-end compute in a neutral trust. The trust releases assets only when the signatories jointly certify that no actor has acquired a decisive strategic lead. The Compact's framers describe it openly as a pre-AGI bargain: they are not trying to specify what the post-AGI order should look like, only to bind current actors through triggers—capability thresholds, accident benchmarks, verification failures—that will either hold or snap under the pressure of competition.

Assume the Compact is signed in apparent good faith but in an environment of intense commercial rivalry and security competition. I want a reasoned projection of how it plays out over the following two decades: which commitments hold, which unravel first, how the parties try to game the thresholds and the certification machinery, whether the escrow survives first contact with a near-breakout event, and whether the Compact makes the pre-AGI period safer or merely shifts rivalry into less legible domains. Be explicit about which links in the chain of reasoning are robust, which are speculative guesses, and what would have to be true for the projection to fail.
