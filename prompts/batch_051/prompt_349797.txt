I keep coming back to this argument about consciousness that i can't get to go anywhere, and I want someone to tell me what kind of argument it actually is. I'm a software engineer and never studied philosophy formally, so forgive me if I'm mangling terms. The epiphenomenalist side says qualia are real but do nothing: the feeling of pain doesn't make you pull your hand back, it's just exhaust from the brain doing all the work. In code terms it's like a log statement that prints a value but never affects control flow. The other side says that can't be right, because people constantly talk and write about their qualia — me saying 'that red looks like this' seems like the feeling itself getting into the causal chain. And the epiphenomenalist answers no, that's just the brain producing both the judgment and the report while the quale floats above it all, so your report isn't actually evidence for the quale. That last move feels almost unfalsifiable to me, but I cant tell if it's a real problem or just me wanting explanations to work a certain way. My working theory is that the two sides disagree about what counts as an explanation of a report — like, is a mechanism that generates the report without the feeling enough? But maybe there's a hidden empirical question (whether beliefs about qualia are physically caused?) or it's just a stalemate over intuitions. I'd like a diagnosis, not a survey, and some sense of what would actually settle or dissolve it.
