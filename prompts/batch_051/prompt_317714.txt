For a faculty working group on concepts, I have been asked to write out my commitments so that my colleagues can audit whether they hang together. Here is the set as I would defend it:

1. I take graded typicality effects — robins are better birds than penguins, apples are fruitier than olives — as our best evidence about the format of concepts, and I read them as showing that natural kind concepts are prototypes: summary representations of typical features that assign graded membership.
2. I also regard concepts as compositional: the content of a complex concept such as PET FISH, STONE LION, or COUNTERFEIT DIAMOND is a function of the contents of its constituents and their mode of combination, and this explains our capacity to entertain indefinitely many novel complex concepts.
3. I find the pet fish and guppy results convincing as a negative point: no simple weighted-sum prototype model predicts the content or typicality structure of combined concepts. I remain unsure whether this undermines prototype representations themselves or only those simple implementations.
4. I am not moved by the usual evidence for exemplar theory — disjunctive categories, context effects in classification, sensitivity to within-category correlations — since these results can be reproduced by a prototype model if one allows sufficiently flexible feature weighting, without positing stored instances.
5. I accept theory-theory for artifact and abstract concepts: what makes something a COMPUTER, a LIE, or a GIFT is its place in a causal-explanatory structure rather than its statistical typicality.
6. I think everyday categorization is fast, automatic, and phenomenologically non-inferential, which makes me skeptical that categorizing involves deploying anything like a causal theory in real time.

I cannot tell how much of this is jointly tenable. Please give me an audit: which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs merely appear to be in tension, and for each genuine tension how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would be. Then tell me which single revision of the set would do the most to make it coherent, and what that revision would cost me.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Order the first four items by severity, starting with the most serious tension; let the fifth item state the single most efficient revision.
