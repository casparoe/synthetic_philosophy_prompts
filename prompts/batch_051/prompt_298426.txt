I teach a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and for the last three weeks the whole thing has been eaten alive by the Sleeping Beauty problem. Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands Heads, she's woken on Monday and asked for her credence that the coin landed Heads. If Tails, she's woken on Monday and Tuesday, with the Monday awakening erased from her memory. She knows the full setup beforehand. Two of my students have dug in as a halfer and a thirder—one says that on each waking she should be 1/2 on Heads, the other says 1/3. After weeks of talking past each other, I've assigned them an adversarial colaboration: I want a document they could both sign, not another debate.

Please write that document. I don't want a description of how the collaboration would go; I want the actual joint statement. Include (1) a numbered agreed-claims section covering the setup, what Beauty knows and remembers, the fairness of the coin, and anything else both sides can sign without hedging; (2) a disagreement table stating the exact remaining differences, with each side's view phrased in wording that side would accept; (3) the cruxes. I suspect the real cruxes include whether waking gives Beauty genuinely new evidence, whether the right reference class is the coin toss or the awakening, and whether credence has to line up with fair betting odds when payouts are collected once per awakening. For each crux, give the concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If you conclude part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Please write the whole thing in American English.
