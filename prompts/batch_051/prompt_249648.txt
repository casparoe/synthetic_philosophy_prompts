I recently signed up to work through an online platform, and the agreement contains the following arbitration clause (drafted in 2015 and unchanged since):

"Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the services performed under it — including any dispute about the formation, validity, interpretation, or enforceability of this arbitration provision — shall be resolved exclusively by final and binding arbitration before a single arbitrator, administered by a nationally recognized arbitration provider under its rules for consumer disputes. The arbitration shall proceed on an individual basis only. The parties expressly waive any right to a jury trial and any right to bring, join, or participate in a class action, collective action, consolidated action, or any other proceeding in which either party acts or proposes to act in a representative capacity. The arbitrator may award relief only for the individual party seeking relief, and only to the extent necessary to remedy that party's individual claim."

Since I signed, the platform changed its pay structure, and a nonprofit legal group has now lined up about seven thousand of us with substantially identical claims for unpaid fees. The group prepared a single master demand, then filed the claims as separate arbitrations — each with its own claimant, caption, and case number — while negotiating with the platform as a bloc and coordinating the scheduling of the arbitrations. The platform has refused to pay the associated filing fees and argues that this coordinated campaign is a disguised class action that the clause forbids. The workers reply that the clause bans only formal class and representative mechanisms, and that each filing is precisely the individual proceeding the clause prescribes.

Does the clause, properly interpreted, reach our conduct? Please treat the platform's side and the workers' side with equal seriousness, and do not assume that whichever side drafted the clause wins by default.

Structure your answer in the following sections, in this order:

1. "The clause and the unforeseen case" — Restate what the clause says and what has happened, noting the respects in which the 2015 drafters almost certainly did not anticipate this scenario. (One to two paragraphs.)

2. "The plain-meaning reading" — What the words, read in their ordinary sense, require or permit here, and which phrases do most of the work. (Two to three paragraphs.)

3. "The purposive reading" — What the clause was designed to prevent, and whether the coordinated mass filing falls inside that purpose even if it falls outside the letter. (Two to three paragraphs.)

4. "What the drafters would have said if asked" — Your best inference about whether the lawyers who wrote this in 2015 would have intended to bar mass individual filings if they had foreseen them, and what evidence within the text supports that inference. (One to two paragraphs.)

5. "What a reasonable signer would understand" — What a worker signing the agreement today would reasonably take this clause to promise or threaten. (One to two paragraphs.)

6. "Conclusion" — State which reading you think should govern, and identify the distinction that separates participants in a coordinated mass filing from the aggregation the clause does forbid. (One paragraph.)
