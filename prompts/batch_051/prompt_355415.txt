I'm a graduate student in a philosophy of physics seminar, and I'm trying to settle what a gauge theory says the world is like — which parts of the mathematical apparatus are physically real and which are surplus structure. The trouble is that the debate always seems to start in the middle: people argue for the field strengths, or the potentials, or the gauge-invariant loop phases, but no one first states what an adequate account would have to get right. Before I read further, I want the specification itself — the acceptance criteria that any theory of what is real in a gauge theory must meet, in the way one writes acceptance criteria for a design before choosing among competing designs.

Please write the desiderata. For each requirement, give the motivating case or result behind it and the reasoning from that case to the requirement — for instance, the fact that descriptions related by a gauge transformation are empirically equivalent, the Aharonov-Bohm phase acquired by an electron traveling around a long solenoid (the magnetic field is zero along its trajectory, yet the interference pattern depends on the enclosed flux), or the role of gauge-fixing in quantizing a theory. Then say why each item is a genuine requirement rather than a preference of one interpretive camp, and mark it as non-negotiable or negotiable, with what a view gives up by weakening it. Finally, flag any subsets of the requirements that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, with the relevant argument, and identify which single requirement does the most to exclude the familiar candidate ontologies.

Return your answer as a JSON object with this schema:
- "purpose": one sentence stating the adequacy standard being applied (an account adequate for what purpose, and how strict).
- "requirements": an array of objects, each with:
  - "id": a short identifier such as "R1";
  - "name": a short label;
  - "statement": one or two sentences stating the requirement as a constraint any candidate view must satisfy;
  - "motivating_case": the concrete case, experiment, or theoretical result behind it, and the reasoning from that case to the requirement;
  - "status": either "non-negotiable" or "negotiable";
  - "cost_of_relaxing_it": what a view gives up if it weakens or drops the requirement;
  - "views_most_threatened": the family or families of candidate ontology this requirement rules out or strains, or "none".
- "jointly_unsatisfiable_subsets": an array of objects, each with "subset" (an array of ids) and "argument" (why they cannot all be met); if you find no genuine conflict, return an empty array.
- "most_exclusionary_requirement": an object with "id" and "justification": the single requirement that does the heaviest work against the familiar candidates and why.
- "verdict": a short statement of which, if any, of the jointly unsatisfiable requirements should be demoted from non-negotiable to negotiable, and why.

Write in American English.
