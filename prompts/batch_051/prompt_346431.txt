Construct a thought experiment for a paper in accuracy-first epistemology in which two inaccuracy measures for all-or-nothing belief — the Brier score and the absolute-value score — recommend opposite attitudes toward one and the same proposition, given one and the same rational credence. Here is the framework you should build with. Take a contingent proposition whose truth value is 1 if it is true and 0 if it is false. An inquirer may believe the proposition, which we model as assigning it the value 1; disbelieve it, which we model as assigning 0; or suspend judgment, which we model as assigning the neutral point 1/2. The Brier inaccuracy of an attitude is the squared distance between the assigned value and the truth value, and the absolute-value inaccuracy is the absolute distance between them; in each case lower expected inaccuracy is better. Given a rational credence c in the proposition, the expected inaccuracy of an attitude is c times its inaccuracy in the world where the proposition is true plus 1 - c times its inaccuracy in the world where the proposition is false. Your scenario should place an ordinary inquirer in an ordinary setting — a workplace, a clinic, a repair call, a neighbor's report — and should generate through natural evidence a rational credence strictly between one-half and three-quarters in a single salient proposition, without stipulating the credence as a number and without coins, urns, lotteries, or exotic setups. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. After the scenario, walk through the reasoning in continuous prose: state the credence, compute the expected inaccuracy of belief, disbelief, and suspension under each score, and confirm that the Brier score recommends suspension while the absolute-value score recommends belief. Then identify the structural feature of the two scoring rules that makes them peel apart here, explain what would happen to the case if that feature were removed, and say how a defender of the absolute-value score would attack the Brier-favored verdict as a measure-sensitivity artifact. Write your answer as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way you would write a letter.
