I've been putting together an essay for a seminar on explanation and the history of the concept of cause, and I keep circling back to the familiar story about Aristotle's four causes: that modern science kept the efficient cause and quietly let the final cause go. I want to push back on that story, but before I commit to the full draft I'd like comments on the idea.

The working thesis is that the four causes are best read as four distinct explanatory requests one can make of a single complex thing — roughly, what it is constituted from (material), what makes it the kind of thing it is (formal), what brought it about (efficient), and what it is for (final). If that is right, final and efficient causes do not occupy the same explanatory slot, and the scientific revolution did not so much refute final causes as stop treating the "what is it for?" question as well formed for inanimate nature. The essay develops this by looking at the uneven fate of teleology: the question survives in talk of artifacts, intentional action, and organisms, while it vanished from basic physics. A corollary is that selected-effects accounts of biological function, and goal-directed-systems analyses more generally, are best understood as attempts to keep the final-cause question respectable by showing it can be translated into historical or efficient-causal terms — and whether that counts as preserving final causes turns on whether the "what for?" formulation adds explanatory force beyond the translation. My standing worry is that this reading is too irenic: it may dissolve a genuine metaphysical dispute into a difference of interests and leave aside whether teleology names something in reality or only in our explanatory practice.

I'd value comments on three things in particular: whether the reading is textually defensible and not merely a convenient rational reconstruction; whether the proposed dissolution is genuine or merely relocates the problem to the question of which domains permit "what for?" questions; and whether the corollary about selected-effects accounts is too quick.
