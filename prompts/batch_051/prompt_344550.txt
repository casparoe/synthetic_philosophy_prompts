I am a journalist who covers science and ideas for a general readership, and next month I am giving an hour-long talk to a library audience of intelligent non-specialists, people who read popular science but have never studied philosophy of mind or cognitive science. The subject is the division of minds into Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. The point I want the audience to leave with is that these are not rungs on a single ladder of cleverness; they are different answers to the question of where the selecting happens. In a Darwinian creature, selection works across generations by natural selection, with little or no revision within the creature's lifetime; in a Skinnerian creature, selection also operates on actual behavior within the lifetime through reinforcement; in a Popperian creature, a kind of inner model allows candidate actions to be tried and rejected before the body commits to them; in a Gregorian creature, culturally transmitted thinking tools such as words, notations, diagrams, and procedures carry or frame part of that testing, so a great deal of the selection has already been done by other minds, many of them long dead. Do not write the explanation itself; write me a teaching plan for the hour. What has to be in place before that question, where the selecting happens, even makes sense, and in what order must the pieces be introduced so each level emerges from the previous one rather than being announced? I am inclined to carry the entire hour on a single concrete scenario, a mammal moving through dangerous ground and avoiding a predator, redescribed at each level; tell me whether that example can bear the weight, what it must have and what it must not have, and whether a single redescribed scenario quietly teaches the ladder-view I am trying to kill. Which misconceptions should I genuinely expect, for example that Skinnerian learning explains Darwinian adaptation or that Popperian creatures must be conscious, and at exactly which point in the sequence should each one be headed off? What should I openly refuse to settle in an hour? And what one question at the end would tell me someone has understood the distinction rather than merely remembered the four labels? Continuous prose only, please, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write it as you would a letter.
