You are a moral and political philosopher who works on the ethics of collective action, reciprocity, and public goods. I have been trying to pin down when someone is a culpable free rider—a social parasite in the morally objectionable sense, rather than merely a person who happens to benefit from others' efforts. I have arrived at five criteria. A person R is a culpable free rider if:

(C1) R receives a significant benefit from a cooperative practice.
(C2) That practice exists and continues only because identifiable others contribute labour, money, or other real costs.
(C3) R has a feasible way to bear some of the same cost, or to contribute to the practice in kind, without unreasonable sacrifice.
(C4) R could not justify her non-contribution to the contributors by a reason she would accept if another person offered it to her.
(C5) R's non-contribution makes the practice more fragile, degrades the benefit, or imposes meaningful additional costs on the contributors.

Now consider this case, which I find genuinely hard.

Bela is a translator who works with a small, endangered minority language. Most of her income comes from translations she produces with the help of a free online dictionary and corpus built and maintained entirely by volunteers: a handful of elderly first-language speakers and two graduate students. They proofread entries, standardise spelling, and raise money for server costs. The resource is non-excludable and non-rival—Bela's use does not slow anything down, and nothing she does wears the entries out. She has the time and expertise to contribute, and the maintainers have repeatedly invited her to join the proofreading rota and to donate; she has always declined. Her view is this: "The dictionary is not made worse by my silence, and my occasional correction would not noticeably improve it. The resource would exist and be just as good whether or not I contributed, so I do not see what anyone is owed."

The maintainers cannot feasibly exclude her, and they would not want to; but they do privately resent professional users who never give anything back, and they occasionally discuss closing the project from burnout.

Does Bela count as a culpable free rider by my five criteria?
