My half-formed idea is that the Schopenhauer–Nietzsche disagreement about pity is routinely read as a normative dispute about whether pity should be cultivated or suppressed, but the more recalcitrant disagreement between them concerns the structure of the pity relation itself. I suspect Schopenhauer builds his account of Mitleid around a recognition component, in which the suffering of another is directly present to the one who pities and thereby discloses a metaphysical unity of self and other; Nietzsche builds his account around a self-affective component, in which the onlooker's own distress is roused by the other's display of pain, with no reliable calibration to the other's actual condition. The usual readings run these together as "pity" and then charge one side with phenomenologically misdescribing the episode. I want to know whether the recognition component and the self-affective component are conceptually separable, and whether each can be instantiated without the other; if they can, the apparent disagreement may be an artifact of equivocation. If they cannot, then one of the two philosophers has mislocated the essence of pity.
