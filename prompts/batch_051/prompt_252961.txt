I've been rereading Plato's Timaeus and I can't shake the feeling that the way the Atlantis story is framed is basically the template for the modern lost-civilization genre — Hancock, von Däniken, the whole thing. Here's the bit I mean (Timaeus 22b–22c, Perseus text):

"O Solon, Solon, you Greeks are always children: there is not such a thing as an old Greek." And on hearing this he asked, "What mean you by this saying?" And the priest replied, "You are young in soul, every one of you. For therein you possess not a single belief that is ancient and derived from old tradition, nor yet one science that is hoary with age. And this is the cause thereof: There have been and there will be many and divers destructions of mankind, of which the greatest are by fire and water, and lesser ones by countless other means."

As I read it, the priest is running three moves. One: he tells the Greeks they're amnesiac children with no ancient tradition and no hoary science. Two: he explains the amnesia by a mechanism — periodic wipeouts by fire and flood. Three: he claims Egypt survives these purges, so "what is here preserved is reckoned to be most ancient." Thats exactly the structure of the modern pseudoarchaeological pitch: say mainstream history has collective amnesia, posit some destructive filter that conveniently erased the evidence (a flood, a comet, plasma events), then appeal to a purer record that survived somewhere nobody took seriously until now.

So my question is whether this is a real identity of structure or just me pattern-matching on a "surviving archive" motif. If it's real, does the Timaeus already contain the demarcation problem in embryo — how do you tell "you really have forgotten" from "you never had this memory because I just invented it"? What would have to be true on both sides for anything to transfer from Plato's setup to modern fringe archaeology? And can you name one prediction my parallel would make that turns out false? I'd rather have someone break the analogy where it actually breaks than have it become another just-so comparison.
