Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War 1.20 (Jowett translation): "The way that most men deal with traditions, even traditions of their own country, is to receive them all alike as they are delivered, without applying any critical test whatever. ... So little pains do the vulgar take in the investigation of truth, accepting readily the first story that comes to hand."

Okay here's the thing I keep getting stuck on. The usual lesson people draw from this passage (and from Thucydides' method generally) is that knowledge of the past is hard because the record is thin, and whatever does survive has been passed through human mouths, which smooth it into something flattering or dramatic; so the historian has to cross-examine witnesses and actively distrust tradition. That's an argument built for a world of scarce oral sources.

But for the recent past at least we now have the exact inverse: a ridiculous over-supply of contemporaneous records — group chats, email threads, dashcam footage, edit histories, transaction logs, five phones filming the same incident. What's scarce isn't the trace, it's the sorting. And the sorting is no longer just a careful reader; it's a recommender system that decides which account literally comes to hand first. If Thucydides' worry was "people accept the first story," we've automated the selection of that first story, and we've also made records mutable in a new way: pages get edited in place, repositories force-push over history, links rot, accounts get suspended, and the archive itself belongs to platforms that can reweight it tomorrow.

So I want to know whether his argument survives this inversion, or whether it quietly turns into a different argument. Which exact premise of Thucydides' diagnosis is under pressure once contemporaneous records are super-abundant and algorithmically curated — is it the claim about human credulity, the assumption of scarcity, or something else? Does the "critical test" he wants people to apply still do the same work, or does it now have to be aimed at the curating layer rather than at individual witnesses? And is there a repaired version of the argument that keeps the core without pretending we still live in a world of thin oral tradition?
