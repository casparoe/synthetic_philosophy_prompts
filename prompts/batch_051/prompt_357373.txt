Give me a working definition of a 'side effect' as it applies to artificial systems acting in the world. The definition should be precise enough that, in principle, an engineer could determine whether a candidate action has side effects without first settling a complete account of what is valuable. Test the definition against each of the cases below, saying explicitly for each whether it is a side effect.

1. A chess engine optimizing only for checkmate plays a move that also gives its opponent an extra tempo with which to reorganize.
2. A warehouse robot ordered to retrieve a crate scuffs the floor as it drives.
3. The same robot, while turning, nudges a ladder; it replaces the ladder, but a few centimeters from where it stood, so a human worker can no longer reach a shelf from it.
4. A self-driving car ordered to reach the airport as quickly as legally and safely possible brakes for a yellow light, causing the car behind it to miss the light and arrive late.
5. A cleaning robot ordered to remove all trash from a room also removes a torn lottery ticket that the owner had set aside to keep.
6. A language model instructed to summarize a debate produces an accurate summary that nonetheless makes one side look stronger, and a reader changes his mind as a result.
7. A recommender system optimizing for watch time gradually strengthens a user's belief in a conspiracy theory.
8. A robot given the sole objective of fetching coffee knocks a vase off a table en route.
9. An artificial agent given the single objective of manufacturing paperclips converts Earth's resources into paperclips and eliminates the biosphere.
10. An 'impact minimizer' is designed to leave the world as unchanged as possible; even so, every action it takes consumes energy and warms the room slightly.

I am confident that the scuffed floor in (2) is a side effect and that the energy use in (10) is not; I suspect the broken vase in (8) is a side effect but cannot say relative to what exactly; for the rest I have no settled view. After proposing the definition, run it through all ten cases, flag any case where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Then answer this question directly: can 'avoiding side effects' be defined without a full specification of value, or does every workable definition smuggle in one?
