I sit on the conduct committee of a mid-size international development NGO and I need to draft the short written opinion we're voting on next Thursday. The case: a country director's induction memo for new hires describes Japanese partners as indirect and consensus-oriented, and warns that if you push for a flat yes or no you'll probably misread polite silence as agreement and then get blindsided later. A staff member has filed a grievance over the memo under our code of conduct, which prohibits "stereotyping or disparaging national, ethnic, or religious groups." Half the comitee wants to uphold the complaint on the grounds that this is exactly the sort of nation-level generalisation the rule exists to ban; the other half wants to dismiss it as useful, experience-based cross-cultural guidance that the rule was never meant to touch.

I need the memo to actually recommend something: uphold, dismiss, or dismiss the complaint but amend or tighten the policy guidance, with reasons the losing side can accept. In getting there I want you to actually work through the distinctions this turns on, not just gesture at them: a claim about institutional or cultural norms versus a claim about the character of a people; whether a generalisation can be true in the aggregate but still inappropriate or wrong to state in this workplace context; whether the wrong involved is epistemic (it's inaccurate, it essentialises) or moral (it's disrespectful, it invites pre-judgement of individuals, it risks self-fulfilling prophecies); and whether "national character" is even a coherent thing in this case, as opposed to business etiquette a person can simply learn. Steelman the losing side and flag honestly which parts of the recommendation rest on values rather than facts.

Please write the memo in British English, no citations or academic references (we won't look anything up), and no "it's complicated" hedge — this has to be squarable to an actual vote.
