I'm giving a seminar next month to a development economics group — mostly applied people, the sort who teach the institutions story, run RCTs or difference-in-difference designs in one or two countries, and roll their eyes when anyone mentions 'culture'. My paper is the contrarian one: the institutions vs geography vs culture debate about why some nations are rich hasn't actually been settled by the identification revolution. Natural experiments and instruments give credible answers to narrow questions (what did this border do here, what did that colonial shock do there) but you can't stitch those together into an answer to the macro question without quietly re-importing the cross-country assumptions you were trying to escape. So the debate's been re-run at a lower level rather than resolved. I'm not saying institutions don't matter; I'm saying the field overclaims about how much of the cross-country variation it has actually explained.

Prepare me for the Q&A. I want the objections I'll actually get from this audience, roughly in the order they'll come, phrased the way they'll put them. For each: how damaging it is, what to concede up front, what to hold, the two-minute reply, and — this matters to me — the point in my reply where it's softest, i.e. the evidence or argument that would genuinely change my mind or make me drop the thesis. Include the question I should be praying nobody asks. Write the answer in British English.
