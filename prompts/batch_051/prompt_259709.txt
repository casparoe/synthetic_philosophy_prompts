Write a dialogue between two people walking home from a talk on bounded rationality. One comes down on the side that satisficing is just optimization with the costs of searching and thinking made explicit: choosing the first apartment above some threshold is really just maximizing expected utility over a bigger decision that includes whether to keep looking. The other thinks this is question-begging, because a bounded agent can't in general compute those costs or the value of more information, and an aspiration level isn't a badly estimated optimal stopping rule but a different kind of decision standard. Have them argue a concrete case, maybe apartment hunting, and make them respond to each other's actual moves rather than trading speeches. Neither should be a strawman and neither should land a knockout.
