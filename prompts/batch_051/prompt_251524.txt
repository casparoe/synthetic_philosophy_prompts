I'm giving a workshop paper in a few weeks to a group that's mostly philosophers of music, and I'm defending the line that for studio-recorded popular music the work just is the recording — the finished multitrack artifact is the primary object of evaluation, not a token of some abstract repeatable structure. The audience is weighted toward people who work on classical music ontology (type/token, Platonism vs nominalism, the score as the locus of the work), plus one person who writes on jazz and improvisation and already thinks the whole "musical work" category does more harm then good, and one musicologist who works on studio production. So I can't just gesture at EDM remixes and hope they nod.

My argument runs roughly: (1) in recorded popular music the dependence goes the other way from the classical case — the recording isn't a record of a prior performance; it's assembled from takes, overdubs, comping, and production choices, with no independent score or work-specification; (2) the only candidate for the work is whatever survives as the released recording; (3) evaluative practice already treats a remix, a remaster, a cover, and a dub version as seperate achievements, so the ontology should track that rather than force them all into one abstract type. The bold claim is that a cover version is no more a performance of the same work than a new setting of the same lyric would be.

I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense. Give me the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging each one is, phrased the way the people in each camp would phrase them (and flagged with which camp it comes from), each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is weakest. Include the question I should be hoping nobody asks — I'm guessing it's about identity conditions of recordings once you start talking about alternate masters, bit-identical files, and streaming, but you tell me if there's something worse. And tell me what to concede in the first five minutes so I don't spend the whole hour defending against a distinction I should have granted up front.
