The offers: a stable research-administration job with a modest but guranteed salary, or a friend's startup that will either turn into a real company or leave me unemployed in eight months. I genuinely cannot assign probabilities to those two futures.

A colleague told me: no probabilities, use maximin. Rank each option by its worst case and take the best worst case. Another friend said: no, use minimax regret--pick the option you'd kick yourself least for if the world goes the other way. Fine. But when I tried to make both rules precise, I got this:

(1) Maximin is the rule for choice under complete ignorance: pick the act whose minimum payoff is highest.
(2) A rational decision rule shouldn't give different answers just from a different presentation of the same decision, as long as the presentation doesn't change what actually happens.
(3) Minimax regret is just maximin applied to a different table: take the original payoff table, replace each entry with its shortfall from the best payoff in its column (the regret), and then choose the option whose maximum regret is smallest.
(4) The regret table is just a re-presentation of the same decision. Same acts, same states, same underlying payoffs--just relabeled as shortfalls.
(5) So maximin and minimax regret should always agree, since they're one rational rule applied to two equivalent descriptions of the same problem.
(6) But in my case they don't agree. Maximin says take the stable job, because its floor is much higher. Minimax regret says take the startup: if the startup takes off and I didn't take it, my maximum regret is enormous, whereas if I take it and it folds, my maximum regret is only the missed stable salary.
(7) So either one of these rules isn't the rational rule for ignorance, or the payoff table and the regret table aren't equivalent descriptions, or a rational rule can flip under a mere relabeling.

The last option is the one I can't swallow. I'd most like to keep (1) and (2), because without something like them choosing rationally under ignorance starts to mean whatever you need it to mean. But I can't see which step actually gives way, and depending on the answer I might be giving up on there being a single coherent rule for my job choice at all.
