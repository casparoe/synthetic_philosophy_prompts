So in my history of economic thought module we've been on Malthus, and it's turned into a running argument with my flatmate who's doing environmental science. He keeps trotting out this numbered argument, and I can't find the flaw though I also don't believe the conclusion. It goes:

(1) Malthus's core claim was that population, if not checked, tends to outrun the food supply, so that over any sustained period the bulk of people get pinned at subsistence — because any temporary surplus of food just gets absorbed by more births, and then the old scarcity reasserts itself.

(2) In the two centuries since he wrote, population has exploded, yet average calorie intake has risen, famines have become rarer and mostly a matter of politics rather than sheer shortage, and the world's poor are far above historical subsistence levels.

(3) A theory whose central predicted outcome fails to appear over two centuries of data should be treated as falsified.

(4) The usual comeback — that Malthus was just early, and that the full force of diminishing returns hasn't arrived yet — doesn't identify any new evidence or any date by which the mechanism should become detectable; it just relocates the prediction to an indefinite future.

(5) A claim that can absorb any actual history by saying 'not yet' is compatible with everything, and a claim compatible with everything explains nothing.

(6) So Malthusianism is either already falsified or it's unfalsifiable hand-waving, depending on how its defenders phrase it.

I think the conclusion is to harsh, but I honestly can't say which line I'd reject. Part of me wants to say that long-run ecological predictions just are like this: the underlying mechanism could be genuine and yet be swamped for centuries by technical change nobody could have been expected to foresee, and that's exactly what 'right but early' means. So tell me: is the argument valid? If not, which step exactly fails, and what does that tell us about whether a long-run prediction can be 'right but early' without the rescue being empty? Could you answer in British English, please.
