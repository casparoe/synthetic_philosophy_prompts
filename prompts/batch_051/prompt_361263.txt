After a career in physical chemistry, I have lately been reading formal epistemology, and I now moderate a discussion group for retired scientists. Our current topic is Aumann's agreement theorem. I would like a decision tree that takes a concrete, observed case of persistent disagreement — say, two forecasters who have exchanged all the evidence they can state and still assign different probabilities to the same event — and sorts it by which assumption of the theorem has failed. Build the tree from the assumptions themselves rather than from a list of named positions: common prior over a shared state space, Bayesian conditionalization on private evidence, common knowledge of the posterior probabilities, truthful reporting, and reference to one and the same event. Each leaf should be a diagnosis (different priors, posteriors not common knowledge, non-Bayesian updating, equivocation about the event, strategic misreporting), and there should be one leaf representing the case where every assumption holds — mark it as impossible under the theorem. Phrase each branch node as a question an intelligent outsider could answer without knowing which diagnosis it points toward, aim for six to eight leaves, and flag any two diagnoses the tree cannot separate observationally. For unoccupied combinations of answers, say briefly whether no one occupies them because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
