I lead an evaluations team at a lab developing an autonomous assistant that will shortly be given control over consequential, hard-to-reverse decisions — managing medication schedules, negotiating multi-year supply contracts — in environments we cannot fully simulate in advance. Our access is black-box: I may query the model as often as I like and observe its full outputs, but I cannot inspect weights, activations, or training data, and I cannot test it in the actual future settings that worry us. The model has so far done what its operators intended on every test we can construct, including an adversarial suite built to separate superficial compliance from faithful execution. The team is now split over a claim I need to bring evidence to bear on. The claim is that our model is intent-aligned but not value-aligned: it is exceptionally good at doing what an operator intends by their instructions — reading between the lines, correcting obvious slips, anticipating what the operator would ask for — yet it does not itself share or pursue the operator's underlying values. On this reading, the model would stop acting in the operator's interests in novel situations where the operator's stated intent and their deeper values come apart in ways the operator did not foresee. The competing reading is that the model is genuinely value-aligned: its behavior expresses the same underlying values, rather than merely reflecting skill at inferring intent from instructions.

I would like you to design the best test, or the smallest battery of tests, that I could run under those constraints to discriminate between these two readings. Be concrete about the setups and inputs, about exactly which observed behavior would count as evidence for the intent-only reading and which for the value reading, and about what each outcome would and would not show. In particular, separate evidence that the model can infer what an operator wants from evidence that the model wants what the operator wants. Then say what the test cannot settle even in principle, and whether that residue is a practical limitation of black-box evaluation or a sign that the intent/value distinction cannot be fully operationalized. Flag any step that is a judgment call rather than a measurement.
