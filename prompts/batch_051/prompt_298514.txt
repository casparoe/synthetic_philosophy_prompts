I am a student in a philosophy of mind course, and I keep noticing that one institutional practice sits badly with what we have been reading on the extended mind. The practice is the closed-book, timed written examination, which is still used for most of my final grades: I must produce answers from memory, without notes, reference works, or electronic devices, even though the same university treats those external resources as entirely legitimate when I write take-home essays or do research. What view of the mind, or of what it is to understand or know something, does the closed-book examination presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory as precisely as you can, distinguishing what the practice strictly commits to from what it merely happens to fit with, say whether that theory is internally coherent, and explain what would actually have to change in examination design if the university took seriously the extended-mind view that cognitive processes can include loops running through notebooks, calculators, or the web.
