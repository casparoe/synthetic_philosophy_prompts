I'm an undergraduate in a political philosophy course, and we're currently working through Robert Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia. A classmate in my study group sent around what he says is a faithful summary of the Wilt Chamberlain argument. I suspect it's broadly right but overreaches at the end, and I want a precise audit before I accept it. Here is his summary:

"Start from any distribution you consider perfectly just. Wilt Chamberlain, a star basketball player, signs a contract under which he is to receive a quarter from every ticket sold to his home games. A million people buy tickets over the course of a season, and each one voluntarily drops the quarter into a box set aside for Wilt; at the end of the season Wilt has $250,000 and everyone else has twenty-five cents less. Since each of those transfers was voluntary, with no force or fraud, Nozick thinks the resulting distribution must also be just. But any patterned or end-state principle — the difference principle, strict equality, a rule that the top fifth may earn no more than twice the bottom fifth — would have to forbid precisely this kind of transaction. So maintaining a pattern requires continually interfering with people's liberty to give, spend, and contract as they choose. Nozick has therefore shown that taxing Wilt's earnings in order to redistribute them is morally on a par with forcing him to work for others, which is why only an entitlement theory, built on just acquisition, just transfer, and rectification, can be defensible."

Give me an accuracy audit of this summary. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your overall verdict on whether this is fair to Nozick — and then go through the summary claim by claim. For each part, say whether a careful reader of Nozick would accept it, find it subtly misleading, or regard it as an overstatement the argument does not support. I especially want to know: (a) whether the Wilt example is best read as an argument about taxation, or as an argument against something more general; (b) whether the move from the transfers being voluntary to the resulting distribution being just needs any further premises the summary has not stated; and (c) whether the final step — that taxing and redistributing Wilt's earnings is on a par with forced labor, and that only an entitlement theory could be defensible — actually follows from the example as Nozick presents it. If the summary conflates two claims that Nozick keeps apart, say so explicitly. No preamble and no restating the question; open directly with your verdict.
