I've been keeping a mental list of my friend Dana's takes on markets in morally icky territory for years, mostly because she's wierdly consistent and I like arguing with her about it. It stopped being casual recently: my brother has been on dialysis for a year, and our state is floating a pilot proposal where the state itself would buy kidneys from living volunteers for a flat payment plus lifetime insurance and aftercare, and allocate the organs strictly by medical need. I realize I trust Dana's judgment here more than I trust my own, but I want to work out what she'd say before I bring it up with her.

Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it:
- open-market kidney sale: against, even with screening, insurance, and a cooling-off period.
- price gouging on bottled water and generators after a hurricane: against, hard no.
- paying someone for their vote, however small: against.
- paid plasma clinics, the forty-bucks-a-pop kind: for.
- paid surrogacy with a real contract, counseling, and a reputable clinic: for.
- paying extra to skip an airport security line: for, no problem.
- paying a broker to jump the queue for a public housing unit: against.

The new case is that state-buyer kidney program. I can't tell whether Dana would treat it as 'kidney sale, so no' or as 'this is basically the plasma/surrogacy structure with real safeguards, so yes.' What I actually want to work out is the principle she's tracking, the other principles that fit her old calls just as well, and where each one comes out on the proposal. Give me your best guess about what she'd say and why, name the closest analog among her old cases, and tell me what further case would most cleanly seperate the possibilities. And since this is now a real policy question for me, can you flag what's genuinely settled among people who work on commodification and repugnant markets versus what's contested, and not present a contested read as if it were the consensus.
