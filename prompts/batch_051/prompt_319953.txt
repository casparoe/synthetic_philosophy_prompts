Last night a friend and I spent the evening arguing about what makes an object complex, and I came away unable to state a definition I could defend. I had said a shuffled deck of cards is a paradigm of complexity, because the order has no short summary: once shuffled, the sequence can only be reproduced by listing it symbol by symbol. He said I had it backwards. A shuffle is random, and randomness is cheap; on his view, complexity has to do with how many steps of construction, arrangement, or selection were needed to produce the thing from simple raw materials. A crystal or a dune might look intricate, he said, but it results from a few repetitive processes, so it is not complex. We spent the rest of the evening throwing examples at each other and stopped agreeing even about clear cases.

I would like a working definition of 'complex' that gets the clear cases right and gives reasoned verdicts on the borderline ones. Please run your definition against each of the cases below, say where it gives a surprising or indeterminate answer, and either revise the definition or defend the verdict. I have a hunch that we were using one word for two different properties—one about the pattern itself, one about the history that produced it—but I could not make the distinction precise. If you think that hunch is right, separate the two definitions and sort the cases between them.

The cases:

- a single grain of quartz sand;
- a dune built of wind-deposited sand;
- a sandcastle with walls, a moat, and towers;
- a snowflake;
- a quartz crystal;
- a shuffled deck of 52 cards;
- a termite mound;
- a living bacterium;
- a functioning car engine, and a heap of the same parts scattered across a tarp;
- a 500-character string produced by fair coin flips, and a 500-character passage of metrically regular verse.
