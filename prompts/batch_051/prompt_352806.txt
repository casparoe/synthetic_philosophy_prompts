I have been designing an experiment for my science class and keep getting stuck on a conceptual point that I would like to identify precisely. The standard lesson is that, to test whether one thing causes another, you manipulate the first while holding everything else fixed. But before I can hold everything else fixed, I have to decide where the thing I am manipulating stops and the rest of the situation begins, and that decision seems to require the very causal knowledge the experiment is meant to discover. Consider whether eating breakfast improves performance on a memory test. I randomly assign one group to eat breakfast and the other to skip it. If the breakfast group gets more calories, is that an uncontrolled variable that undermines the study, or is it just breakfast operating through one of its components? If blood sugar rises, is that part of the intervention or part of the background? Answering these questions seems to require knowing in advance how breakfast, hunger, and blood sugar are causally connected to memory, which is close to what I wanted to find out. I noticed the same shape in biology lessons about gene knockouts: a knockout of a single gene is called a clean manipulation only if that gene does not set off changes in other pathways, and whether it does so is often part of what the experiment is trying to learn.

My first question is whether this specific problem has an established name of its own. I am not asking about the general point that correlation is not causation, and I am not asking about a study that simply fails to control for a known third variable; those are familiar. I am asking about the apparent circularity in the boundary between an intervention and the background one is holding fixed. If the problem does have a name, I would like to know how the named thing is usually defined and where its edges are; if it does not, I would like a term and a definition that mark it off clearly from the two neighboring ideas I just mentioned. I also want to know whether the problem is fatal to the idea that we can learn about causes by manipulating things, or whether it is the kind of circularity one can honestly live with.
