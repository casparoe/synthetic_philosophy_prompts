I've been working through the dispute between the Mohists and the Confucians, and an argument keeps nagging at me — it seems to show that the two schools end up recommending the same practical pattern, which would make one of the best-known quarrels in classical Chinese ethics partly verbal. Mozi's jian'ai, impartial care, says we should regard other people's families, persons, and states as our own and not let our own kin or home count for more merely because they are ours. Mencius and the Confucians answer that love is properly graded, with filial devotion at the root and benevolence extending outward by degrees from the family. Now consider the following line of thought:

(1) Mozi's impartial care requires promoting the benefit of all without favoring one's own family, state, or self.
(2) Promoting the benefit of all without favoring one's own means weighing each affected person's benefit equally in one's deliberations.
(3) Weighing each person's benefit equally does not require giving everyone equal portions of one's care and effort; on the contrary, equal weighing can require directing more to some when doing so produces more benefit overall.
(4) In ordinary circumstances, one is far better placed to benefit family and neighbors than strangers: one knows their needs, meets them daily, and shares habits and institutions with them.
(5) So impartial care, consistently followed, will typically direct more care and effort toward family and neighbors than toward strangers.
(6) But directing more care and effort toward family and neighbors than toward strangers, because they are nearer and better known, just is the graded pattern of care the Confucians defend.

So the two positions converge: the Mohist reaches graded treatment by impartial reasoning, and the ancient rift dissolves into a confusion. I keep suspecting that the last step is smuggling something in, but I cannot say exactly where. Is the argument valid? If it is not, which step or steps fail, under what reading of the key terms, and could a defender of either side repair it without begging the central question?
