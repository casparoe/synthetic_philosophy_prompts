Here's an argument I've been turning over that seems to show the best-of-all-possible-worlds theodicy is incompatible with free will. (1) If this is the best possible world, then every event in it, including my acts, is necessary for the world's being best: if my act hadn't happened, the world wouldn't be the best. (2) If something is necessary for the world's being best, then it occurs in every best possible world. (3) So if this world is the best, my act occurs in every possible world. (4) But I act freely only if I could have done otherwise — that is, there is some possible world in which I don't perform the act. (5) So if the theodicy is true and I act freely, my act both occurs in every possible world and fails to occur in at least one. Contradiction. I can't shake the feeling that step 3 is where the trick is, but I can't say exactly how. Is the argument valid? If not, which step exactly fails, what does the corrected step say, and does the corrected argument still threaten free will?
