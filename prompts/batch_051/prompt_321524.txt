I'm on the wage board for a mid-sized city, and next month we vote on an ordinance that would eliminate the tip credit (the rule that lets restaurants pay a lower cash wage and count tips toward the regular minimum) and phase the tipped cash wage up from $2.13 to the regular minimum of $17.50 over three years, then index it to inflation. There's a carve-out for places with under 15 employees, which get an extra year, and a training wage of $12 for the first 90 days for workers under 20.

The restaurant association says this will force independent places to cut shifts or close, push menu prices way up, and actually reduce servers' take-home pay because customers will tip less once they know servers are making a real hourly wage. The workers' coalition says the tip credit is basically employers paying almost nothing while customers get to decide how much staff make that week, and that a stable base wage is a dignity issue, not just an income issue.

Draft the decision memo I can bring to the board: recommend approve, reject, or approve with ammendments, and give reasons the other members can actually act on. I want the strongest version of the side you're recommending against on the page, not a strawman. Integrate the ethics and the economics instead of putting them in separate sections, and be explicit about which empirical claims each side is betting on (employment effects, tipping behavior, who actually ends up better or worse off). If your recommendation has conditions, spell them out. Write it in American English.
