I got moved into HR ops about a year ago, and a bigger part of the job then I expected is deciding what actually happened, speech-wise, in a meeting or an email thread. I handle a few of these a week now. We have a system with categories like "formal verbal warning," "resignation tendered," "directive issued," "leave approved," and each one has legal-ish downstream consequences later, so if I misfile a manager's offhand comment as a warning it can come back in an unemployment hearing. The thing is, almost nobody uses the magic words. A manager writes "this is the last time I want to see a missed Friday deadline" — is that a warning or ordinary performance feedback? Somebody writes "I'm done, I can't do this anymore" in the team chat at 1 a.m. — is that a resignation? A supervisor replies "sure, do whatever you want with the weekend emergency shift," and we find out two days later they were being sarcastic — did they approve a schedule change? I have a dim memory from a college class about performative utterances, "saying makes it so," maybe Austin and Searle, but I couldn't tell you what the machinery actually was. What I want is a reusable procedure — a checklist or ordered set of tests — that a non-lawyer with about fifteen minutes per case can run on a transcript to decide whether one of those job-relevant acts really happened: a warning, a directive, an approval, a resignation. It should say which context facts actually matter (who can say it in that role, whether anyone took it as binding, how explicit the words were, whether the person later acted as if it counted) and which are red herrings, and it should be honest about the step where the procedure has to stop and say "this is a judgment call" rather than pretending the rule settles it. Then run it on two or three of my examples, including at least one where the procedure gives my department an answer we won't like, and tell me straight what the procedure can't do — the part we'll still be arguing about. I can escalate genuinely tangled ones to our director, but I'm expected to bring a recommendation, not just dump the file on his desk.
