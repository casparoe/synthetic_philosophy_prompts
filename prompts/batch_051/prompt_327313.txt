I teach a unit in my philosophy of technology course where we use Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics as the first, failed alignment proposal, and work backwards from their failure modes to what a serious proposal for constraining an advanced artificial agent's behaviour would actually have to achieve. I'd like you to write the handout for that: the desiderata such a proposal has to meet, with the consideration behind each, using the Three Laws as the running counterexample.

For the class I boil them down to: first, don't harm a human being, and don't stand by while one comes to harm; second, follow orders unless that would conflict with the first; third, preserve your own existence unless that conflicts with the first or second. Don't get side-tracked into quoting them; the structure is what matters.

I can already see a few candidate requirements — harm can't be left as a vague commonsense term the agent gets to interpret literally; a finite list of rules can't be larded with exceptions; conflicts between rules need a decision procedure rather than an ordering bolted on afterwards; the agent has to track what an order means, not just its wording; and it has to let itself be corrected or switched off. But that's a first pass. I want you to do the real work: state each desideratum properly, say which failure of the Laws motivates it, and why a serious proposal can't do without it.

Then mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for any proposal worth taking seriously, and which are really preferences of particular camps (rule-based systems versus value learning, restrictive versus enabling constraints, and so on). And here's the part I most want: say whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — the way locality, determinism, and empirical adequacy run into Bell's theorem or similar no-go results — and if so, what has to give, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding the naive candidates.

Don't spend long scoring contemporary proposals like corrigibility or Constitutional AI; I'll do that in class, and the criteria are the deliverable. I want the list and the arguement for it, not a survey. British English throughout, please — 'behaviour', '-ise', that sort of thing. No bibliography or citations; work from what you actually know.
