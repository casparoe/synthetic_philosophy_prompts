Here's the sort of line that gets me: 'causation is just constant conjunction.' I keep meeting it in arguments about whether a model trained on purely observational data can learn causal structure, where it gets wheeled out to close the debate — there's no extra causal ingredient in the data, so what would the model even be learning? But that one sentence seems to blur at least three different claims: one about what we can ever observe, one about what causal statements mean, and one about what the world actually contains. And I have also seen someone shoot back that the New Hume reading says this is a misreading of Hume anyway, since (on that view) he only denied that we can know or conceive necessary connexions, not that they exist. Put the exegesis question to one side for a moment and seperate the precise claims hiding in that one-liner. Say which are near-trivial, which are substantive and defensible, which are false or confused, and which reading a person actually needs to be true when they use the line to end an argument about learning causal structure.
