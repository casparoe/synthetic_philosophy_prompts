I help run a medium-sized online forum for amateur historians, and we have recently begun designing a reputation system to replace our flat member label. The member leading the technical side keeps pressing a particular analogy. He argues that reputation is a currency: it is earned through good contributions, it accumulates as a store of past value, and so it ought to be spendable, and in fact transferable, just as currency is. On his proposal, a long-standing member could buy perks by spending points, or vouch for a new member by transferring points to them. The analogy is doing real work for him: because a currency is fungible, transferable, and outlives any particular transaction, he concludes that reputation points ought to have those same features.

I cannot shake the sense that something goes wrong in that move, but I want more than a feeling. Audit this analogy for me. Work through what actually corresponds between reputation and currency and what does not; where the mapping breaks; which features of currency the phrase quietly imports that reputation lacks; and whether the conclusions — spendable perks and transferable vouching — survive once the disanalogies are on the table. If there is a better analogy for what our community would be building, name it. Please end your response with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' that states in a short phrase how much of the currency analogy can carry.
