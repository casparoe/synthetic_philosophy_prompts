For some time I have been struck by a tension in how institutions around me try to shape conduct. Their official statements, onboarding programmes, and values documents often speak the language of character: they claim to cultivate integrity, prudence, fairness, and compassion in their people and in the public. Yet the actual tools they increasingly adopt are almost purely environmental. Employees are auto-enrolled into pension schemes, citizens become organ donors unless they opt out, canteens are rearranged so that the first food on display is fruit, and forms ask people to sign a truthfulness declaration before rather than after they fill them in. The operating assumption appears to be that behaviour follows choice architecture, defaults, salience, and framing rather than stable traits. If anything, this resembles the situationist critique in moral psychology, which holds that conduct is so sensitive to seemingly trivial features of the situation—haste, bystanders, ambient cues—that the idea of robust global character traits is empirically doubtful.

I would like help reconstructing what this pattern of practice actually presupposes. Does a reliance on defaults and salience strictly presuppose that global virtues do not exist, or only that they are unreliable in the settings in question? Is the practice compatible with an Aristotelian picture in which a genuinely virtuous person would, among other things, arrange environments well so that others are not left to struggle against temptation unaided? Or does the practice quietly commit an institution to something like the view that character is epiphenomenal and situation is the real unit of moral explanation? And if one rebuilt the same policy goals on the assumption that people possess stable, situation-resistant virtues, what concrete features of the default-and-salience approach would have to change? Please distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely comports with, and say clearly where it is philosophically underdetermined. Please write your answer in British English.
