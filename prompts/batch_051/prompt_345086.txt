I am one of the editors of my high school’s speculative fiction magazine, and every submission window we receive a dozen time-travel stories, most of which try some version of the grandfather paradox. Our editorial board keeps splitting into two camps: one says that any story in which a character prevents their own existence is straightforwardly incoherent and should be rejected, while the other says that time travel stories have their own internal logic and we are being too rigid. We do not have formal philosophical training, we have about fifteen minutes per story, and we need a procedure we can use as a group. Please write us a reusable checklist for deciding whether a submitted time-travel narrative contains a genuine inconsistency in the rules it sets for itself, rather than a merely surprising or unexplained plot point. I have read a little about the philosophical literature on the grandfather paradox, and I know there is a distinction between the order of events in the traveler’s life and the order of events in the world, but I do not know how to apply it in practice. The procedure should be phrased in ordinary language, with the questions in the order we should ask them, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the process stops with a definite verdict, and where it has to hand the decision to a human because the question is one of narrative purpose rather than logic.

Please apply the procedure to three short examples: one in which a traveler kills her own grandfather before her parent is conceived; one in which a traveler brings a book back to give to his younger self, and the book never seems to have been written by anyone; and one in which the story explicitly says that each trip creates a new branch of history. Include at least one case where your procedure gives a verdict that would disappoint a story-loving editor. At each step, please separate what is widely accepted among people who study the philosophy of time travel from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
