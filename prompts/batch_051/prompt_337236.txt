I've been following the long-running rationalist-adjacent proposal that public argumentation should be turned into a scored sport, with brackets, rankings, and a pre-registered rubric that determines wins and losses, on the theory that competition would improve the ecosystem of public reasoning. I want a thought experiment built to a precise specification.

Construct a scenario in which adding that sport format changes two epistemic outcomes in opposite directions relative to a baseline in which the same participants discuss the same question without scoring. Specifically, the audience's expected accuracy in its final all-things-considered belief should go up under the sport format, while the participants' expected calibration in their stated credences should go down. By audience expected accuracy I mean the expected closeness of the audience's final probability distribution to the true answer; by participant calibration I mean the match between stated credences and objective outcome frequencies across many counterfactual reruns of the same set-up. The split must be produced by the incentives or selection effects the scoring imposes. Do not produce it by making the rubric reward obvious sophistry, by letting the audience treat the judges' verdict as independent evidence, by making one side obviously wrong, or by stipulating that the participants were already miscalibrated before the format arrived. Keep it to an ordinary institutional setting, such as a commission hearing, a product-safety review, or an internal forecasting review, rather than science fiction.

After presenting the scenario, walk through the check. Say what the participants report and why in each condition, what the audience infers, and which specific feature of the case interacts with the win-loss format to make the two epistemic goods come apart. Then give the honest postmortem: if that feature were removed, which way would the case collapse, and what is the strongest reply available to someone who maintains that competitive scoring would still improve argumentation overall? The case is meant to stress-test the claim that competition is uniformly good for collective epistemology, so a scenario that merely stipulates the divergence, or that depends on corrupt judges or a gullible audience, will not count. Write in American English.
