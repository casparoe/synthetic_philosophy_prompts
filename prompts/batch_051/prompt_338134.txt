So my friend Iris is always telling me that ethics has no principles, that it's all just particular cases, and that anyone who follows a rule is fooling themselves. But I know her actual verdicts on a bunch of cases, and they're suspiciously consistent:

- Lying to the murderer who asks where your friend is hiding: she says fine.
- Breaking a deathbed promise because you get a better offer to go to a party: wrong.
- Breaking a coffee date because you stop to help someone badly hurt in a bike crash: fine.
- A doctor lying to a terminal patient about the diagnosis to spare her suffering: wrong.
- In the trolly case, pulling the lever: fine; pushing the big guy off the bridge: wrong.
- Stealing a loaf of bread to feed a starving child: fine.
- Torturing a suspect to get the location of a ticking bomb: wrong.

I can think of at least two rules that fit everything. One is a harm-prevention rule: you may lie, break a promise, or take property only to prevent serious harm that can't be prevented otherwise, but you may never inflict serious physical harm yourself as your chosen means. The other is a respect rule: you may bend those norms to prevent serious harm, but never in a way that uses someone merely as a means or overrides their own say about their own good. And then there's Iris's own view, which is that there's no rule at all and the relevant features just switch their significance from case to case.
