I'm a retired biochemist whose been reading Nietzsche for the last year. In Ecce Homo, when he explains why he is so clever, he goes on for quite a while about food—soup before a meal, alcohol, eating while stressed, and he treats nutrition as a philosophical question rather than a medical one. My question is this: if Nietzsche were alive today and saw how much modern diet science we have, with RCTs, big cohort studies, metabolomics, and so on, would he have regarded any of that as genuine knowledge? I'm not asking what he would think of the actual foods or diets, but about the kind of knowledge those studies produce. Would he trust it, or would he dismiss it as missing the individual case in the same way he thought much of philosophy missed life?
