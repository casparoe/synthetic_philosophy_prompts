I'm in a class on ancient Greek ethics, and we've been going through the Laws. Our professor gave us a translation of the passage where the Athenian stranger says:

"What I assert is that every man should make it his aim to be master of himself, and that this is best, and that if a man is to be master of himself, he must not be a slave to pleasure; and that the drinking of wine is a test of self-control, because we shall see whether a man has the mastery of his desires or is overcome by them. It is as if there were two strings in the soul pulling opposite ways, and wine relaxes one of them, so that the other appears; and this is why the legislator may use wine as a drug for the soul, to bring out the truth about a man. And we may say that the man who is to be trusted with the city or with anything else must first be tested in this way, and that wine is a more searching test than any other."

Is the Athenian stranger really saying that wine is a kind of test, or is our professor's gloss overstating it? Go through the passage and tell me what's fair in the gloss and what's not: does the passage support the idea that wine is a truth-serum for the soul, or is that a misreading of the Greek or context?
