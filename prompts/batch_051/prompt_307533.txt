I'm giving a session to second-year psychiatry residents on Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects for human kinds — the way classifying people changes the people classified, and the changed people then force the classifiers to revise the category, which starts another pass around the loop. These residents are sharp but they mostly think the DSM carves nature at it's joints and that a diagnosis is more like identifying a species than like naming a genre. Plan the session's explanatory arc for me: what has to be in place before the loop even makes sense (I suspect you can't talk about feedback between label and labeled until you've distinguished an indifferent kind like platinum from an interactive kind like borderline), the single example that should carry most of the weight and what it has to have and what it has to not have if it's going to show the loop clearly rather than collapse into a one-way story, the misconceptions they'll walk in with or form halfway through in the order they'll form them — for instance, that this is just a self-fulfilling prophecy, or that the category is therefore fake — and where in the sequence each one should be headed off rather than untangled later, and a check for understanding at the end that would show they grasp the two-directional, iterated structure and not just the slogan that labels affect people. I want the dependency structure and the misconception map, not the explanation itself. Also tell me what to deliberately leave out at this level and how to say so out loud.
