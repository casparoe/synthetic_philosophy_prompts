Ok so for a reading group I'm supposed to write down my current "philosophical profile" on causation, and I'd rather not show up with something that secretly contradicts itself. Here's where Ive landed. Can you audit it and tell me whether the set hangs together?

1. I'm with Russell in thinking causation isn't part of the fundamental furniture of the world. Physics, at its deepest level, doesn't seem to need a relation of causing on top of whatever it already says there is.
2. But I'm not an eliminativist about ordinary causal talk. "The rock broke the window" is true. I dont want to be the person who says all such talk is just a useful fiction.
3. The best grip I know on what makes ordinary causal claims true is counterfactual: roughly, the impact caused the break because if the impact hadn't happened, the break wouldn't have happened.
4. I also think preemption cases (two kids throw a rock at a window, Suzy's arrives first, Billy's would have done it a second later) show counterfactual dependence can't be the whole story. The window's breaking doesn't depend counterfactually on either throw, but Suzy's throw is still the cause.
5. For scientific purposes, I think causation is best understood through interventions and invariance: roughly, X causes Y just when Y changes under an intervention on X with the rest held fixed.
6. But I also want to say that the big bang caused the universe to expand, and that certain features of the early universe caused large-scale structure to form. There's no way to "wiggle" the big bang or hold the rest of the world fixed while you do, but I really don't want to call those claims false or merely metaphorical.
7. I think whether smoking causes cancer is mind-independent. It doesn't depend on our interests, our concepts, or what we happen to be able to manipulate.
8. I'm a Humean about laws: the world is, ultimately, a mosaic of local matters of fact, and the laws are our best compact summaries of the patterns in that mosaic.

I genuinely can't tell if 5 and 6 are a real problem or just a clash of toy examples, and I keep going back and forth on whether 1 and 2 are in more trouble than they look. Go through the set, say which pairs are in tension and how badly, flag any red herrings, and tell me which single change would leave the package in the best shape.

Format: give me four sections, in this order. "Where it holds together" (~100 words): the commitments that are mutually supporting, not just consistent. "Genuine tensions, worst first" (~350 words): for each real tension, how bad it is and what specifically would have to give. "Seeming tensions that aren't" (~150 words): pairs that look bad but dissolve once the right distinction is drawn. "Cheapest single fix" (~150 words): the one revision you'd make to clean up the most, and what it costs.
