I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about when its okay to use someone else's ideas, because my gut is giving me a pattern I can't quite state. Here are my verdicts on a pile of cases.

- A coworker explains an idea for a project to me one-on-one. A week later I pitch the idea to the whole team as if it were mine. Not fine; this one isnt even close.
- I read a stranger's blog post and it gives me the core idea for a piece I write. I never quote them and the words are all mine. Fine, though I wish I'd linked them.
- A friend teaches me a family recipe and I cook it for a dinner party, telling everyone where it came from. Fine.
- Same recipe, but a year later I open a restaurant and put the dish on the menu without asking the friend or crediting the family. Not fine, and it feels worse than the blog thing.
- A competitor ships the exact feature we shipped last quarter. They didn't copy any code, just the feature. Fine. Stings, but fine.
- A company sends researchers to a village, learns a traditional medicine preparation from local healers, then patents a standardized version and shares nothing back. Not fine — angrier about this one than any of the others.
- A student sells detailed notes from my lectures, including my examples, without asking. Not fine.
- A friend repeats a joke I made at a party the next week as if they came up with it. Not fine, but tiny.
- An artist posts their portfolio publicly. A company scrapes all of it, without permission, to train a model that can imitate their style and undercut their commissions. Not fine. But when I try to explain why this is different from the competitor copying our feature, I get stuck.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close to it? I want the principle stated precisely, with which case motivates each part of it, plus what it says about a case or two I didn't list. And be honest about which of my verdicts it can't keep.

Also, please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested here, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.

After the main explanation, give me a table with one row per case and these columns: Case (a short label); My initial verdict (fine / not fine / mixed); Clause of the principle that covers it, or "none"; What the principle says about that case (keep my verdict or overturn it); Settled or contested (one phrase saying whether this point is near-consensus or actively disputed).
