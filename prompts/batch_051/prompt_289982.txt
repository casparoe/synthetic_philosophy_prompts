I keep going back and forth on whether democratic backsliding is a real thing or just a fancy label one side uses for outcomes I don't like. Here's the problem. I'm attracted to a thin, procedural way of defining democracy—something like a country is democratic if there are regular, free and competitive elections, real alternation of power is possible, and parties are free to organize. The virtue of that defintion is that it's fairly neutral and checkable: you don't have to adjudicate what the right level of judicial independence or press freedom is, you just ask whether elections are real and whether incumbents can actually lose. It seems like it would protect against exactly the worry that backsliding is a partisan label, because it doesn't smuggle in any assumptions about what good government looks like.

But I also can't shake the intuition that something genuinely undemocratic is going on when an elected government wins and then changes the rules so it's harder to lose next time: packing courts, buying up or harassing the press, gerrymandering, rewriting electoral laws, siccing prosecutors on opposition politicians—all while continuing to hold real elections that it even wins. On the thin definition, that country looks like a democracy, maybe just a majoritarian one; it isn't backsliding, because free and fair elections are still happening. But if I'm honest, it sets off the same alarm in me as a coup would, just slower. I don't know whether to trust the theory or the alarm.

I don't want you to just pick the side you like. I want to know what it would take for each thing to give. Is the intuition just my liberal bias dressing itself up as the claim that democracy was never just about elections, and should it be dropped? Or does the intuition show that the procedural definition is missing a load-bearing part of what democracy is, so the definition should be thickened—and if so, how much, without turning every ordinary policy disagreement into backsliding? Or is there a reading where both survive, like we should talk about electoral democracy and liberal democracy as separate things, and backsliding is only a claim within the thicker one? And how would we tell, in some concrete gray-zone case, which reading is doing the work rather than just which label we prefer?
