Here's a set of verdicts I keep coming to about consent, and I'm trying to figure out whether there's one principle behind them, or whether I should just give up on the idea.

1. Someone agrees to a medical procedure because the doctor says "this is standard, everyone does fine," and doesn't mention the 5% chance of permanent nerve damage. I think the patient's consent is invalid.

2. Someone agrees to lend a friend money because the friend says "I just need it for rent this month," when really they want it for a gambling stake. Money borrowed, rent still paid, they do win and pay it back. I still think the consent to the loan was invalid, even though no harm came of it.

3. Someone consents to sex with a stranger at a party on the understanding that the stranger is single. The stranger never says they're single, they just don't mention their spouse. I keep going back and forth on this one. My gut says it's not invalid, but I can't say why it should differ from the loan case, where I'm so sure.

4. Someone signs a lease because the landlord says "sign now, I have three other people ready to take the place." The landlord is lying. I think the lease is invalid, though the tenant still ended up liking the apartment fine.

5. Someone consents to a police search of their car after an officer says "you don't have anything to hide, right?" I think that's valid, even though there's a pretty clear pressure being applied. It bothers me that I'm comfortable with this but not with the landlord.

6. An older teenager consents to sex with someone who's actually their school teacher, though the teacher never uses any threat or deception. I think it's invalid, but I can't tell whether the problem is that the power imbalance undermines consent itself, or that the consent was fine but the act was still wrong for a different reason.

7. Someone with severe dementia says yes to being in a research study, seeming to understand a simple explanation. I think it's not valid. But I also knew someone whose grandmother with dementia said yes to having her blood drawn for a test, and nobody treated that as a consent problem. I can't make these cohere.

Is there a single account of what makes consent valid that gets all or most of these, and where does it force me to change my mind?
