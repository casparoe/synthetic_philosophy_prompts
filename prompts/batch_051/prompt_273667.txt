I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas beat putting together a longread about the recent revival of panpsychism, and I've ended up with two interviewees who've been arguing past eachother for months. One is a constitutive, bottom-up panpsychist: she holds that the fundamental physical ultimates are conscious subjects and that the consciousness of a human or a dog is constituted by those micro-subjects' experiences combining—so her central problem is explaining how subjects "sum" without the result being a mere heap. The other is a priority cosmopsychist: he thinks explanation should run the other way, from one cosmic subject to finite subjects as limited aspects or derivatives of it, so he avoids the combination problem but faces what he admits is a decombination problem—explaining how the one subject's unified field gets carved into my finite, seperate point of view and yours.

I want to publish something more constructive than the usual ping-pong. Act as an experienced philosopher of mind who specializes in these debates and has a reputation for being scrupulously fair. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming maybe six candidate agreed claims before picking the strongest ones. Then draft the joint statement these two could actually sign: a numbered agreed-claims section that is substantive rather than anodyne, followed by a disagreements table. The statement should include the exact formulation of where they part ways, with each side's version worded so its own side would accept it; the genuine cruxes; and, for each crux, the thought experiment, argument, or finding both would agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also flag any part of their dispute that looks merely verbal, and say which agreed claims are doing real work.
