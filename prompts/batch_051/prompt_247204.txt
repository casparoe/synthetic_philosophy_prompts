I've been rereading that part of Leviathan where Hobbes says geometry is the only science it has pleased God to give mankind, and that men in geometry start by settling the significations of their words, which they call definitions. It's not the most quoted line, but it's been eating at me. I tried running the maker's-knowledge reading of it all the way out, and I got an argument that seems to prove too much. Here it is:

(1) For Hobbes, a science is demonstrable when we can know its subject's causes because we ourselves are the causes—we made the thing in question.
(2) In geometry, we make the lines and figures, or at least we construct their definitions and draw them, so geometry is demonstrable.
(3) Civil philosophy is supposed to be demonstrable for the same reason: we make the commonwealth through covenants, so we can know its causes.
(4) Therefore whatever follows from the definitions or covenants in civil philosophy should be knowable with the same certainty as geometry.
(5) Hobbes thinks he has demonstrated absolute sovereignty from the definition of a commonwealth.
(6) But the initial definitions or covenants are themselves artifacts, and they can be set up differently. I can define a commonwealth with divided sovereignty, or with a reserved right of resistance, or even with an elected rotating sovereign, and as long as my definitions are consistent my deductions will be valid.
(7) By parity with geometry, each such consistent covenant-definition should yield a demonstrable civil science.
(8) So Hobbes's absolute sovereignty looks like just one consistent construction among many, no more true of actual commonwealths than any other.
(9) But that can't be right, because it guts the whole point of Leviathan, which is supposed to show that absolute sovereignty is uniquely required to escape the state of nature.

So where does this go wrong? I'd love to keep (1) and (3), because I buy the maker's-knowledge parallel, but if I have to give one up I'd rather give up (6) than (5). I want a diagnosis: is there an equivocation on "make" between geometry and civil philosophy, or a scope shift in "demonstrable," or is the conclusion actually more Hobbes-friendly than I think? And what does it cost to reject whichever premise gets rejected?
