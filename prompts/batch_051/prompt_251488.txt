I am a PhD student in nineteenth-century German philosophy, working as a teaching assistant for an undergraduate course on Schopenhauer. I have to grade a take-home examination answer, and I want the grading to be rigorous and defensible. The examination question was:

"Drawing on The World as Will and Representation, reconstruct the steps Schopenhauer takes from the claim that the world is will to the claim that life is essentially suffering. Identify any further premise about pleasure and pain that the argument requires, and say whether the pessimistic conclusion depends on the truth of that premise. Then explain whether, on Schopenhauer's view, intellectually grasping this argument is sufficient to overcome suffering; if it is not, say what more Schopenhauer thinks is required and why."

A student submitted the following answer:

"Schopenhauer distinguishes two aspects of the world. As representation, the world consists of objects in space and time standing in causal relations, all governed by the principle of sufficient reason. As will, the world is the thing-in-itself: a single, blind, ceaseless striving that lies outside space, time, and causality. Because the will is a striving, it is always directed toward something it lacks, and this felt lack is the root of all suffering. Schopenhauer's pessimism rests on the further claim that pain is the positive state while pleasure is merely the temporary removal of pain. From this he concludes that a life in which desires were satisfied would be one of contentment; but since in fact most desires are frustrated or replaced by new ones, the ordinary course of life is mostly pain. The way out, for Schopenhauer, is intellectual: once we understand that the will is the thing-in-itself and that all individuals are only its objectifications, the distinction between self and other dissolves. In aesthetic contemplation the intellect is freed from the service of the will and we perceive the timeless Ideas without interest; this is why Schopenhauer regards the aesthetic attitude as the highest and most lasting liberation from suffering. What then remains is to live in accordance with this insight, which is what Schopenhauer describes as the denial of the will."

Please assign a numerical grade out of 20 and provide the point-by-point comments that justify that grade. The comments should be specific enough that the student could use them to revise the answer; they should distinguish between what the answer gets right and where it goes wrong, and they should identify any step that does not follow. Do not give a general impression; give a detailed assessment.
