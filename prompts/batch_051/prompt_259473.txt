got in a huge argument with a friend last night about whether self-deception is even possible. her whole point was that lying to yourself is sort of contradictory: if you're deliberately avoiding looking at the evidence, you must already know what the evidence would show, so you never actually end up believing the comforting thing. i didn't have a good comeback, partly because it hit close to home. my policy, for as long as i can remember, is that whenever i catch myself hoping for something, i refuse to investigate the situation and instead act as if it's already true and wait for the feeling to catch up. i did it with my last relationship—spent a year planning a future with a guy while never once checking whether he was planning it too—and i'm doing it now with my small business, which is probably failing: i wont look at the books, but i talk to my one remaining employee and our customers as if we're going to make it. the weird thing is i honestly cant tell whether i believe the business will survive or am just performing the belief. my friend called this cowardice with a self-help bumper sticker. is acting-as-if-until-belief-shows-up a defensible way to run my beliefs, or is that exactly the contradiction she was describing? and if i do know the truth somewhere underneath, is there any way i could tell the difference from the inside, or would i be structurally unable to catch myself?
