Please act as a professional philosopher whose specialties are animal ethics and normative theory, and think carefully before answering. I am a philosophy PhD student working on speciesism, and I have been rereading Bentham's Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation. I am thinking specifically about the passage in which he argues that, when we ask whether nonhuman animals should have legal protection, the question to ask is not whether they can reason or talk but whether they can suffer. I have a homegrown alternative to what I take to be the standard reading of that passage as making sentience the foundational criterion of moral considerability.

I call it the power-asymmetry account. The claim is that a being's entitlement to moral consideration is grounded not in any intrinsic capacity it has, but in the relation between that being and the agent whose action is under evaluation: specifically, in how far the being is exposed to the agent's unilateral power without meaningful opportunity for consent, exit, protest, or retaliation. The greater the power differential and the higher the stakes for the weaker party, the heavier the justificatory burden on the stronger party. On this view, Bentham's sentience question is diagnostically valuable but not explanatory: nonhuman animals matter to us because they are radically vulnerable to our choices, not simply because pain matters in itself. I suspect, though I have not fully worked this out, that the account should be restricted to beings with a welfare or interests, so that it does not swallow up inanimate systems.

I know this sounds as if it might collapse into a vulnerability or care-based view, but I think the explanatory order is different: those views start from a standing relationship or a virtue, while mine starts from the asymmetry of power itself. I would like you to steelman the strongest version of my position first, then identify the three most serious objections, and tell me whether any of them is fatal or merely discomforting. If the view already exists in the literature under another name, say so clearly, because I do not want to present it as novel if it is not.
