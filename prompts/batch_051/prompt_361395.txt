Write a dialogue between two equally sharp people who disagree about how to read a scriptural text. Pick one text — say Exodus or the Gospel of Mark — and stick with it the whole way through. One character is a historical critic: she thinks a passage means what its human author could have meant in his own context, and that the real work is reconstructing sources, genres, and the community that produced it. The other is a theologically orthodox believer: he doesn't deny the text has a human history, but he insists the same words are also revelation, that God is their ultimate author, and that reading them as one address to later readers is not just devotional sentiment. Have them actually argue, not talk past each other. Make them grapple with a specific point of tension — a story that looks like legand to the historian, say, or a prophecy that looks written after the fact — and make each side feel the cost of their own view. The believer shouldn't just say "it's a leap of faith," and the historian shouldn't just say "miracles can't happen." At least once, each of them should concede a real point to the other and shift their position slightly, not just restate it. Leave the disagreement unresolved but sharpened.
