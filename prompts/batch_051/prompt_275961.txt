Here is an argument I built and now distrust, because the conclusion seems too strong. (1) The maximum entropy principle says the rational prior is the distribution that maximizes Shannon entropy subject to whatever constraints express your background information; with no constraints on a bounded interval, that distribution is uniform. (2) If two descriptions of a quantity are related by a known one-to-one transformation, they express the same state of ignorance, so the uniquely rational priors they determine must correspond under that transformation. (3) Suppose your total information about a quantity Θ is only that Θ lies in [0,1]. By (1), your rational prior for Θ is uniform on [0,1]. (4) Now let Φ = Θ². Your total information about Φ is likewise only that Φ lies in [0,1]. By (1), your rational prior for Φ is uniform on [0,1]. (5) But a uniform prior for Φ corresponds, under the transformation, to a non-uniform prior for Θ with density 2θ, not the uniform density of step (3). So the same state of ignorance is assigned two incompatible rational priors for Θ. (6) Therefore there is no unique rational prior for complete ignorance, and since maximum entropy was supposed to supply one, the objective Bayesian claim that some priors are rationally mandated collapses. I most want to keep (1) and (2), and (6) is the step I cannot accept. Give a committed diagnosis: identify the exact step that fails or must be revised, explain why it looked fine, and state what a maximum-entropy objective Bayesian is committed to in order to escape this argument.
