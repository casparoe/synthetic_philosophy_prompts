Here's a counterfactual I want you to work through properly. Suppose that in 1990, a country otherwise very like Canada makes one change to education: every child from age six does an hour a day of rationality training run along genuine martial-arts lines. Dojos, belts, the lot — not classrooms. There are katas: standardised sequences of moves, such as "name the premise you most want to be true, then say what evidence would change your mind about it," "steelman the strongest version of the view you oppose, in words its defenders would accept," and "assign a probability and a falsifiable prediction with a date." Kids drill these until they're automatic, under time pressure, tired, sometimes in front of an audience, the way a karateka drills a form. There's also sparring: structured debates where you score points for updating honestly, conceding cleanly, and refusing to strawman, and you loose points for motte-and-baileying — retreating from a strong claim to a tame one the moment you're challenged — or for changing the subject. Adults can keep training in the evenings, like people do with judo, and employers treat the higher belts as a credential.

I'm not asking whether this is a good idea. I want your best reasoned prediction of what one generation of this (1990–2025) does to the adult population's actual rationality — accuracy of beliefs, quality of decisions, resistance to motivated reasoning — as opposed to their dojo performance. Walk me through the causal chain link by link: which effects are robust, which are guesses, and what each link assumes. Focus especially on transfer: does any of this survive outside the dojo, and what does transfer actually require — domain-general habits, changed motivations, changed social norms, institutions that reward being right rather than being loyal? Where in the chain is it most likely to snap? What would have to be true for your overall prediction to be wrong, rather than just delayed? And what would have to be true about how human reasoning works for the whole scheme to produce people who are razor-sharp in the dojo and still hopelessly motivated reasoners everywhere else?

Take the role of a cognitive scientist who works on reasoning, skill acquisition, and transfer. Start by brainstorming plausible mechanisms before you commit to a prediction, and be explicit about your confidence at each step.
