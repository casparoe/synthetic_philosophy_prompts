I'm a software engineer and I spend much of my day building interfaces whose job is to conceal what the machine is actually doing. Pressing delete in a file manager appears to delete a file, but underneath there is a chain of low-level operations, and the interface gives the user a clean, serialized story that is not a faithful report of the mechanism. I keep running into the user-illusion view of consciousness, which seems to make the same move about mental life: the felt unity of a decision, the sense that I am the single author of an action, is a high-level summary presented over processes that work nothing like the experience reports. I want to know whether this is a deep structural parallel or just an appealing metaphor. In software, I can say precisely why the abstraction exists, what it hides, and how to drop below it while debugging; in the conscious case there is no designer who specified the interface, no obviously separate user, and no debugger that can inspect the machinery beneath the illusion. Do those disanalogies break the parallel, or does something important survive them?
