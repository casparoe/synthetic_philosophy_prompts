Lately I have been working through the color-phi phenomenon and Dennett's Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction, and I would like a credence from you rather than a tidy summary of the literature. In the standard display, a red dot flashes briefly at one location and, after a short interval, a green dot flashes at a nearby location; subjects report seeing a single dot travel across the gap and change from red to green roughly midway, before the second flash could have informed the experience. The puzzle is how the brain appears to have access to the second color before it occurs. Two families of explanation present themselves. On an Orwellian account, consciousness first represents the unrevised sequence and the revision happens after the fact: a moment of red-dot experience is then overwritten, the way a regime rewrites a historical record that was briefly truthful. On a Stalinesque account, the second stimulus is folded in before the experience ever reaches consciousness, so there is no moment at which the unrevised version was ever experienced — a show trial whose verdict is scripted in advance. Dennett's deflationary claim is that this is a distinction without a difference: no experiment or introspective report could settle which story is correct, and insisting that one must be correct presupposes a Cartesian theater in which experiences occur at determinate times. I currently put my credence at around 0.65 that Dennett is right, but the number feels unstable.
