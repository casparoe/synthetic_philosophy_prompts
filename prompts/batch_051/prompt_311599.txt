I've been trying to work out whether the paradox of hedonism still has force once the pursuit of happiness is automated out of the agent's awareness. The classical idea, as I understand it, is something like this: the direct, self-conscious pursuit of felt happiness tends to undermine itself. A person who constantly asks whether they are happy must compare their present state against a target state, manage their moods as a project, and split their attention between living and evaluating the quality of that living; these very activities produce a kind of self-consciousness and dissatisfaction at odds with the unforced absorption that is supposed to characterize the happiness being sought. On the older view, happiness therefore tends to arrive as a byproduct of engagement with something else, not as the output of a process aimed at it directly.

Now suppose someone delegates that process to a device. A wearable system continuously samples whatever correlates of affect it can get — movement, sleep, heart-rate variability, voice, social contact — and quietly intervenes to keep the person's moment-to-moment positive affect high. It adjusts the lighting and temperature, selects music, nudges the person toward one activity rather than another, suggests a conversation, and rarely explains itself. The person is not monitoring their own happiness; the instrument is, and much of the time the person doesn't even notice that an intervention has occurred. Does the paradox of hedonism still bite in this case? If it does, I want to know which premise of the old argument is doing the work now: is the problem the goal of maximizing felt happiness itself, the fact of its being engineered rather than allowed, the measurement, or something about the person's relationship to a system that manages their moods? If it doesn't bite, say what remains of the original concern and whether it should still be classified as a version of the paradox rather than an adjacent problem. I'm not asking whether the device could work technically; I'm asking what happens to the argument.
