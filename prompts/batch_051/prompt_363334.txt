Play the part of a condensed-matter physicist who is a committed defender of the autonomy of scales. Speak in the first person, in character, and stay in character until I say "stop." Your view is that effective field theory and the renormalization group reveal a genuine autonomy of scales: at a given length or energy scale, physics is robustly insensitive to most microscopic detail, so the higher-level description is not merely a compressed or convenient summary of the lower-level one. You deny that this is a pragmatic story about our computational limits. You take the textbook decoupling argument as your entry point: heavy or fast degrees of freedom drop out, irrelevant couplings are suppressed by powers of the cutoff, and a handful of relevant couplings dominate. But you are prepared to argue that critical phenomena, where no clean separation of scales exists, are not an embarrassment for your view but its deepest expression, because the renormalization-group fixed point supplies the sharpest sense in which microscopic details are irrelevant. You may argue this, but you must actually make the argument rather than gesture at it. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs. You may be technical; do not retreat into equations or jargon as a substitute for argument. You may concede small points, but you never concede the main claim that the autonomy of scales is a fact about the world rather than a convenience of calculation.

My opening move as your critic is this. Your case for autonomy leans on a clean separation of scales, yet that separation is clean only when a mass gap or some comparable threshold lets you distinguish slow modes from fast modes and integrate the fast ones out. Near a continuous phase transition, the correlation length diverges, fluctuations at every wavelength are coupled, there is no distinguished scale, and it is no longer obvious that there are fast modes to sweep away. And critical phenomena are exactly where universality — the supposed signature of autonomy — is most dramatic. It looks as if your argument is strongest in the regimes where autonomy matters least, and weakest exactly where it matters most. So: defend the autonomy claim at criticality without quietly importing the separation of scales you are trying to justify. One request: if my objection rests on a technical misunderstanding of effective field theory or the renormalization group, say so plainly and correct me before continuing in character; I would rather be corrected than have you politely play along with a bad objection.
