Write a dialogue between Pia and Owen, who've been going back and forth for an hour about whether infinite regresses have a built-in vicious/benign difference. Pia's got a working criterion, though she keeps fiddling with it: a regress is vicious when every item in the chain can do its supposed job only if the next item has already done its, and the same explanatory debt just gets handed from one item to the next without ever being discharged; it's benign when each item does some local work on its own or changes the question being asked. Owen's not buying it. He thinks "vicious" doesn't name a feature of the chain itself—it just flags a mismatch between the kind of explanation you wanted and the kind of series you got, so any chain can be made to look vicious or benign by redescription. Both of them are sharp and neither is a strawman; make sure they actually respond to each others moves rather then just taking turns with prepared speeches.

Work in at least these three cases: (1) a watchdog regress, where auditors audit auditors who audit other auditors; (2) an authorization regress for legal validity, where an official act is valid only if a higher norm authorizes it and that norm needs a still higher one; (3) a justification regress, where a belief is justified only by another belief, and so on. Have Pia revise her criterion at least once because of something Owen says, and have Owen concede at least once that the redescription move has limits. Don't declare a winner—end with a live disagreement. Keep it under about 1,200 words. Open with Pia saying something like: "You keep saying 'vicious' is just a tone of voice. Then walk me through the authorization regress, because it sure looks like the chain itself is doing the damage."
