I am teaching a graduate seminar on the methodology of consciousness research, and we have spent the past two sessions on Daniel Dennett’s paper Who’s on First? Heterophenomenology Explained. As I understand his proposal there, heterophenomenology is meant to be a neutral method rather than a theory of what consciousness is: the investigator treats the subject as a kind of fiction-producing system, gathers the subject’s reports about how things seem to them, and then interprets those reports from the intentional stance to build up a representation of the subject’s heterophenomenological world, all while remaining resolutely agnostic about whether that world maps onto any real goings-on in the brain. Dennett’s claim is that this lets a science of consciousness take first-person reports seriously as data without either swallowing them as incorrigible testimony or discarding them as noise.

I would like you to evaluate whether that methodological claim survives contact with the strongest objections. Please rank the following four objections, which I have distilled from the seminar discussion and the surrounding literature, from most damaging to least damaging to heterophenomenology’s viability as a method for the science of consciousness: (a) first-person reports cannot be treated as neutral data because the act of reporting or being asked to report alters the very experience being reported; (b) the choice of the third-person, intentional-stance starting point is not neutral at all, and quietly rules out what is distinctively subjective about consciousness; (c) the heterophenomenological world is just a redescription of verbal behaviour and adds no real explanatory value; (d) treating subjects as fiction-producing systems is an instrumentalist manoeuvre that sidesteps rather than engages the hard problem. Give me the ranked list, with a paragraph justifying each placement, and then tell me which of the four, if any, is fatal rather than merely awkward. Finally, as a separate rating, give heterophenomenology a score from 0 to 100 for your confidence that it can serve as a foundational framework for a mature science of consciousness, with one sentence of reasoning for the score.
