The host said the standard Bayesian rule for updating on conditional information—minimising relative entropy—gives the same posterior as if she had simply learned the material conditional, in effect eliminating the Red-and-Second cell and spreading its probability over the other three. He then said this is usually treated as a reductio of the relative-entropy rule: learning a conditional should only rearrange probabilities inside the Red case, leaving the probability of being in Red territory unchanged, and it certainly shouldn't make Blue more likely. He made it sound like the upshot is that there's no good Bayesian way to update on conditionals. I suspect he's overstating somewhere, but I can't pinpoint it.
