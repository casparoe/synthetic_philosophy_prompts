Here's a fact pattern I've been using in my philosophy of law seminar, and I want someone to actually argue it through from the text, the purpose, and the drafter's likely intent, not just cite doctrine at me.

The clause, as it appears in the sign-up flow:

"Your clicking of the 'I Agree' button constitutes your electronic signature. You agree to be bound by these Terms and Conditions in all respects as if you had signed a written contract with us, whether or not you have read them, from the date and time of that click."

Sam tells the assistant on her phone—one of those new agents that can operate other apps for you—"find me a free cloud storage service and get me set up; I can't deal with the forms." The assistant picks CloudKeep, enters Sam's name and email, checks the default boxes, and clicks "I Agree." Sam never reads the terms, never sees the button, and doesn't learn which company she ended up with until the welcome email. Six months later there's a dispute over lost data, and CloudKeep says she's bound by an arbitration clause in the terms and moves to compel. She says she never agreed to anything: she didn't click, she had no idea what was in the contract, and the assistant wasn't acting as her lawyer or, really, as a person at all—it had no seperate intention to be bound by anything either.

This is a clause the drafters almost certainly didn't write with a consumer-side agent in mind. So does it apply? Give me what you get if you interpret the clause by (a) its plain words, (b) the purpose of clickwrap assent and the clause's function within it, (c) what the drafters would have said if asked about this case, and (d) what a reasonable user would understand "your clicking" to cover. Then tell me which approach should win when they diverge, and why. In particular: is there a coherent sense in which Sam agreed to a bargin she never encountered, or does the "electronic signature" language just paper over the fact that no human being ever formed the relevant intention? And if that last worry is right, does it cut against ordinary clickwrap too—where a human clicks without reading—or is there a relevant difference?
