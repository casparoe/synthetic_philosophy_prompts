So I spent two hours last night arguing with a friend who is a pretty convinced textualist — he kept saying judges should just read the words the way a normal English speaker would and stop asking what the legislature was attempting to do. I kept answering that even ordinary reading makes you decide how much context and background assumptions to bring in, and by the time you've made those choices you're already doing something like purposivism. We went round in circles and never landed it, and now I want to turn the disagreement into a thing a group can feel.

I help run a legal theory reading circle for undergrads, about 15 people with no law background, and we have a 75 minute session coming up. Could you design a structured activity where the group has to interpret a short open-textured rule under two different sets of instructions — one group told to stick strictly to the words plus a dictionary, the other told to reconstruct what the rule is for — and then both groups have to rule on the same hard case? Give me the rule and the hard case (you can make them up, or adapt something familiar like a rule banning vehicles in a park), roles for the participants, a realistic timeline, and a debrief. The debrief should map what each group actually did onto the textualism/purposivism distinction instead of explaining it from the front, and the labels should not be used until then. I want the first group to notice they cant decide the hard case without quietly appealing to the point of the rule, and the second group to notice that their account of the rule's point has to be tied back to the words or its just their own preference. Also let the text-only group use normal lawyer-ish tools — a dictionary, a grammar rule, maybe two canons of construction stated in plain English — but make them write down why they chose the reading they chose, so the debrief can show where purpose sneaks back in. Finally tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of the real debate the activity can't reproduce.
