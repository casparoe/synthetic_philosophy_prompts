I am a novelist drafting a passage that has stalled in a way that feels like a craft problem and something more, and I would like you to help me think through what is actually going on in it.

The scene: a woman in her late forties is fitting a door in a house above a cove. She has done this work for years, and the plane in her hand has become almost transparent to her; she is aware not of the tool but of the ribbon of wood peeling away, the door's resistance, the sound of the sea through the open frame. She is living inside the small future in which the door will finally fit against the jamb. Then the iron catches on something buried in the wood, the plane stutters and stops, and the spell breaks. What she now notices is the plane itself: the chip in the iron, the looseness of the handle, the alien weight of the thing in her hand, as if an object had suddenly been substituted for an extension of her arm.

I can write the sentence in which the tool catches, but I do not know what the moment demands after that. The honest rendering seems to be that a working tool recedes from experience while a failed tool floods it with detail. But I am not sure whether that detail belongs to the tool as it really was all along, or whether breakdown changes what the tool is in her experience. Is close description of the broken tool a truthfulness about the object or about the interruption? And if I follow the character's attention, should the next page describe the plane's material qualities, or should it stay inside the stalled activity itself — the half-finished door, the job now suspended, the small catastrophe in the rhythm of the afternoon?

Please approach this as a philosopher of technology and everyday perception who also cares about narrative craft, and resist treating it as a matter of mere style. Before giving your final advice, brainstorm at least three different ways of characterizing what has changed in the woman's relation to the plane, and say what each would commit the scene to. Then give me a careful diagnosis of the phenomenon and a concrete recommendation for how the prose should behave after the moment of breakdown, including what should be close-described, what should be left out, and what the shift should make the reader feel.
