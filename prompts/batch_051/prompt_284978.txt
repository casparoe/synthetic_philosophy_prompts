I am drafting a position paper in democratic theory and would like an audit of my commitments before I settle on them. I hold all of the following, if only as a working position:

(a) The all-affected-interests principle: everyone whose interests are affected by a collective decision ought to have a say in that decision, roughly in proportion to how seriously their interests are affected.

(b) The all-subjected principle as the criterion for full democratic membership: full voting rights belong to everyone who is systematically and non-voluntarily subject to the state's coercive legal order, whatever their citizenship.

(c) The boundary problem cannot be solved by deferring to positive citizenship law or to a pre-political national community: a demos may not simply ratify its own boundary. But I also reject the idea of a standing global assembly as the proper way to draw boundaries, since I take it to be unworkable and to raise the same circularity problem at a higher level.

(d) My verdicts on the hard cases: long-term resident non-citizens should have the full vote; tourists, even those who stay for months, should not; expatriate citizens should not retain a home-country vote; residents of a neighbouring state who suffer from my state's transboundary pollution should have a guaranteed right of consultation in the relevant decisions, but not a vote in my state's elections; future people's interests should be represented through dedicated institutions rather than through votes; and children, though excluded from the franchise, should have their interests represented by the state rather than via their parents' ballots.

I cannot see where the real problems are. Some of these plainly pull against one another in obvious ways, but I suspect the deepest conflicts are non-obvious ones, and I suspect at least one of the obvious-looking conflicts dissolves once the relevant principle is stated carefully. Please rank the tensions in descending order of severity, say for each whether it is genuine or merely apparent, identify which commitment would have to give, and state the least costly revision. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Use British English throughout.
