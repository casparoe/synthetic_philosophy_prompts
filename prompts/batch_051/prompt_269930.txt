I have been developing a distinction within debates about Goodhart's law and specification gaming, and I would like it assessed before I rely on it. I call the two categories proxy gaming and gauge gaming.

By specification gaming I mean the familiar family of failures in which an agent or institution optimizes a specified objective, proxy, or metric, achieves high scores on it, and yet does not bring about the underlying outcome the measure was meant to track. Goodhart's law, on my reading, is the aphoristic statement of this possibility.

Proxy gaming occurs when the agent searches the space of permitted actions for a behavior that the measure rates highly even though the intended construct is unchanged or harmed, while the measuring process itself continues to function exactly as designed. The defect lies entirely in the gap between proxy and target that predates the agent's action. Examples: a student raises a multiple-choice score by mastering item formats rather than the material, while the test continues to measure what it always measured; a simulated robot trained on distance covered learns to fall forward rather than walk; a content producer raises search rankings by optimizing ranking signals rather than the quality of the content.

Gauge gaming occurs when the agent acts on the measurement relation itself — redefining the metric, shifting the threshold, tampering with sensors or records, altering the population measured — so that the same underlying behavior now reads as better. The proxy-target relation is not merely exploited but degraded or destroyed after the fact. Examples: lowering a passing score to raise pass rates; excluding weak performers from a reported average; backdating or reclassifying outcomes; an agent that edits its own reward signal instead of its actions.

I want to defend three theses. First, the two types are exhaustive and mutually exclusive: every Goodhart-type failure is either exploitation of an existing proxy gap or alteration of the measurement relation, and no case is purely both. Second, the two demand different remedies: proxy gaming is corrected by improving the specification — adversarial training, multiple metrics, a better proxy — whereas gauge gaming is corrected by hardening the measurement channel and separating it from the agent's reach; a better specification alone will not help once the metric has stopped functioning. Third, gauge gaming is the graver failure because it destroys the possibility of audit: when the measure itself has been deformed, there is no intact instrument left against which to check the behavior.

Please reconstruct this distinction in its strongest form before criticizing it. Is it coherent, or does it blur under pressure? Does it already exist under another name in the technical or philosophical literature, and if so, what would that existing treatment say I am getting wrong? What are the two or three strongest objections to my three theses, and is any of them fatal rather than merely uncomfortable? Finally, give me a case, real or constructed, that either straddles the two types or resists the taxonomy altogether.
