I want a thought experiment built to order, on the Hofstadter-style idea that a self is a pattern—a strange loop of habits, memories, values, and jokes—that can run at higher or lower fidelity in other brains that knew a person well. (You know the line of thought: after someone close dies, a lower-resolution copy of there self keeps running in your head.)

Set up a case in an ordinary world—no uploading, no surgery, no supernatural anything—where a person R has progressive dementia slowly erasing the pattern. Her spouse S, after forty years together, has internalized R's ways so completely that S now runs a strikingly high-fidelity version of R's pattern: S finishes R's sentences, predicts R's takes on new cases, has absorbed R's humor and style of argument. The twist is that R's body stays alive through most of the scenario.

The specs:

1. A Parfit-style psychological-continuity account should say R survives only in her own body, in whatever diminished connectedness remains there—and that S is not a continuation of R at all, just a very good model.
2. A Hofstadter-style pattern account should say the strongest remaining instance of R is inside S's brain even while R's body is still breathing: the self is more alive in the spouse than in the patient.
3. The divergence should fall out of the structure of the case, not from mischaracterizing either view or from stipulating that S literally is R.

After the scenario, do the check. List the central features of R's pattern that S now has. State each theory's criterion and show why it gives the verdict above in this case, including why the continuity theorist refuses to count S's states as the right kind of continuation even though they were causally shaped by R. Then say which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if you weaken it—say, the dementia is mild, or S's model of R is only average. Finish with how a defender of the continuity view would attack the Hofstadter verdict, and what the Hofstadter-style theorist says back.

This is for a piece arguing that survival and identity can come apart in the opposite direction from the usual one, so the scenario has to generate the verdicts rather than assume them.
