Draft the joint statement that a committed proponent of Good's theorem and a committed proponent of the view that information can be harmful should be able to sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the value of free information. The first camp holds that, for a single expected-utility maximizer with a fixed utility function, the expected value of cost-free information is provably nonnegative, so every supposed case of harmful information must trace to a violated condition; the second camp holds that the conditions the theorem assumes away — strategic observability, fixed preferences, expected-utility maximization, and the ability to ignore the news — are not edge cases but the places where the interesting questions arise, and that a rational agent can sometimes be right to decline free information. The document should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims, covering exactly what the proof establishes, which idealizations it uses, and what it leaves open; (2) a disagreements table in which each side's core claim is stated in wording its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete decision problem both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance; and (4) a closing note identifying any residual disagreement that is merely verbal and restating it so both camps can sign. Throughout, do not let the two sides talk past each other: distinguish harm caused by the information itself from harm caused by the act of acquiring it and from harm caused by becoming known to be well-informed.
