I've been covering the deepfake beat for a while now and I've ended up with a set of commitments I want stress-tested before I write the piece. Part of the trouble is that I keep saying slightly different things to different sources, and I can't tell whether the package actually hangs together.

Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) Recordings do a very specific job in our epistemic lives: they're the backstop for testimony about public events. We take people's word for what happened partly because, in principle, someone could pull up the tape; mostly nobody checks, but the availability of the check is what keeps the whole system of trusting reports honest. (2) Deepfakes attack precisely that backstop: once cheap convincing fakes are around, the tape can't serve as an independent check anymore, because any given clip might be fake and unaided viewers can't tell. (3) But I dont actually feel that my everyday knowledge is under threat. I know what my editor said in our call this morning, and I know the mayor said the bridge is closing next month because I was in the room with fifty other reporters, so its not as if my knowledge hangs on the tape. (4) I think the fix is mostly technical and institutional—camera signing, provenance chains, watermarking, platform labels—not a mass program of teaching people to be more suspicious of video. (5) I also think the advice to just trust video less is a bad and maybe harmful response: it amounts to tearing down the backstop instead of repairing it, and it offloads the cost onto ordinary people. I'd rather preserve the standing presumption that footage is genuine until shown otherwise. (6) And I still think well-documented events are safe: if there are dozens of independent recordings from different people and devices, fabricating all of that coherently is orders of magnitude harder than making one fake clip.

So: go through these and tell me which are actually in tension and which only look like it. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give and what it would cost. If there's one revision that cleans up the most, tell me which and make me do it. Keep the answer under 900 words and lead with the bottom line: is the set jointly tenable or not, and which commitment should I give up first.
