In my seminar on global justice I keep seeing a move I don't have a name for. Someone will open with a family case: of course you may save your own child from a burning building before a stranger's child, and nobody disputes that. Then they say the state is just that same partiality scaled up. When you point out that a political community of hundreds of millions of strangers is nothing like a family — no intimacy, no thick history with each particular person — they retreat to something thinner, like "shared political project" or "shared fate." But then a few minutes later the family analogy is back, doing the heavy lifting again. The two justifications seem to take turns covering for each other: the family analogy supplies the felt moral force, and the shared-project talk supplies the scale. I know about the general debate between nationalists and cosmopolitans, and I know the term "associative duties" floats around, but what I'm after is this specific alternation between an intimate relationship and an institutional one. Is there a name for that pattern, and how exactly is it related to — but different from — ordinary appeals to associative duties? And seperately: can the compatriot-special-claims view be stated without borrowing the moral force of the family at all?
