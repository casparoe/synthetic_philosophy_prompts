Before I read any of the proposed solutions to the reference class problem, I want the conditions of adequacy set out explicitly. Write the desiderata that a theory of reference-class selection must meet if it is to assign a probability to a single case from frequency data about the overlapping classes to which the case belongs. The problem it must solve: when Susan's probability of surviving her surgery depends on whether we sort her into women, women with her condition, women with her condition and her age, smokers with the same condition treated at this hospital, and so on, and these classes have different survival rates, the theory should tell us what probability to use for Susan herself. Give each requirement with the concrete case or consideration that forces it, and make sure the list handles at least these hard cases: nested classes with conflicting frequencies, the most specific class with usable data versus the most specific class there is, gerrymandered classes, and attributes that are statistically associated with the outcome but causally irrelevant. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp, and say whether any pair of the desiderata looks jointly unsatisfiable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring the existing theories.
