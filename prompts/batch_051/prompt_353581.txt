ok so i have a distinction i keep using and want checked. i call it "lever voting" vs "signature voting." lever voting is when your ballot is just an attempt to shift probabilities in an election, like pulling a lever—if your vote doesn't decide the outcome, it did nothing. signature voting is when your ballot is an endorsement: you're signing your name to the candidate's platform and to whichever coalition ends up winning with your support behind it. most duty-to-vote and lesser-evil arguments seem to assume lever voting, but the real cost of a lesser-evil vote looks to me like it happens in the signature register—you're co-signing the bad parts too. say i vote for a candidate whose platform is 70% things i believe and 30% things i think are genuinely wrong. the lever story says fine, expected value. the signature story says my name is on the 30%, and other people reasonably read it that way. so my claim is the lesser-evil argument only looks clean if you pretend the signature dimension doesn't exist. is this distinction already a thing in the literature (the expressive voting people must of gotten here), and is my claim actually right, or does the signature metaphor just smuggle in the conclusion?
