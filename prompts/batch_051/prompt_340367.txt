Construct a Kantian test case with this specification: a maxim that passes the contradiction-in-conception test but fails the contradiction-in-willing test under the Formula of Universal Law. The case must be set in an ordinary modern context — an office, a household, a transit network, a medical practice — and must not be one of Kant's four Groundwork illustrations (false promises, suicide from self-love, neglecting one's talents, refusing to help others in need), nor a thinly disguised version of them. In particular, the contradiction in willing must not rest on the fact that the agent might someday need help and would wish others willing to provide it. The contradiction may instead exploit any other feature of finite rational agency — for example, its dependence on evidence and the possibility of revising a judgment — provided you name that feature precisely.

State the maxim in a single sentence, with its end made explicit. Then apply the two tests in order. Under the conception test, describe the world in which everyone acts on the maxim as if by a law of nature, and explain why no contradiction arises in thinking it. Under the willing test, show why a rational will cannot will such a world without contradiction, and locate the exact point in the maxim where the contradiction attaches. End with a diagnosis paragraph that names the structural feature doing the work and says how a defender of the conception test would argue that your maxim actually fails the conception test too.
