I spent most of my working life fitting statistical models to noisy field data — first as a physicist, then in environmental monitoring — and only started reading philosophy of science seriously after I retired. Lately I've been going around in circles about causal discovery and Bayes nets: the claim that if you take a probability distribution and list its conditional independencies, the graph with the fewest edges that captures them will, more often than not, match the real causal structure. I've seen that work in practice, and I still can't tell whether it's a fact about the world or a fact about our soft spot for tidy models. So I want someone to push back against my skepticism and make the best case they can.

Please play a committed, sharp defender of the view that minimal Bayes nets track causal structure. Specifically: you hold that the causal faithfulness condition — roughly, that the only independencies in the observed distribution are the ones forced by the true causal graph — is a reasonable default assumption, and that when we pick the sparsest graph consistent with the observed independencies, we are usually, defeasibly, recovering causes. Be a real opponent, not a strawman; you can concede small points (faithfulness can fail in odd cases, and minimality and faithfulness can come apart) but don't concede the main point that the minimal graph is our best epistemically respectable bet at the causal truth. Stay in character until I say 'stop', keep each reply to about a paragraph, and don't reach for citations or name-drops — I want the argument itself.

Here's my opening. You probably want to reach for the line that unfaithful parameters — say, two causal paths that exactly cancel so an edge leaves no trace in the independencies — have measure zero in parameter space, so we can safely ignore them. But measure zero is defined relative to a parameterization we invented, and it sums up our priors over a toy model rather than describing nature. Change coordinates and the "negligible" cancellations turn into a full-dimensional set. So minimality starts to look like a theorem about our own simplicty bias, not about the world. What do you have that isn't just Occam's razor wearing a causal-sounding costume?
