Here's a proposal I've been chewing on: make information about a person their property. Any company that uses a record keyed to your name, face, device ID, or location trace owes you a small per-use royalty — whether or not you know about the use — on a default schedule unless you contract for more. You can sell the future royalty stream, but you can't transfer the underlying data right itself, and datasets aggregating at least 10,000 people are exempt. Walk me through how this would actually behave. What do companies do first — relabel data as aggregated, nudge users into "volunteering" it, route around identifiers or just hide uses? Do people hold out, sell out cheap, or bundle through middlemen? Where does the gaming start, and can it be fixed without scrapping the property idea, or is the property framing itself the main problem? Keep the answer in American English.
