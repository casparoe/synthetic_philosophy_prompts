Give me your actual credence that, in Kavka's toxin puzzle, a rational agent can form a genuine intention at midnight to drink the toxin. The setup, for clarity: someone will pay you a large sum tomorrow morning if and only if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink a mildly unpleasant, briefly sickening toxin tomorrow afternoon; the money is paid before the drinking would happen and is not conditional on your actually drinking, so at the time you would drink there is nothing to gain and only unpleasantness to lose. I don't want the disclaimer that it depends on one's theory of intention; I want a number and the reasoning decomposed. Assign a rough probability to each of the sub-questions that your answer turns on — for example, the probability that an intention is the kind of state that can be formed for reasons bearing on the having of the state rather than on the intended action; the probability that such intention-formation is under enough voluntary control for the agent to do it here; the probability that rationality itself forbids intending what you know you will not do; and any other term you think is doing real work — then show how they combine into your final credence, identify the term the number is most sensitive to, and say what evidence or argument would most move that term.
