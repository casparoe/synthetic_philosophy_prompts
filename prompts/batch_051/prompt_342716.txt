I keep going round in circles on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I think I've finally got a half-formed idea I want to turn into a two-page proposal for a one-year fellowship. The standard worry is that pleasures don't seem to have any one felt quality in common: the pleasure of a hot bath, of solving a crossword, of schadenfreude, of the first cigarette of the day. So hedonism either has to posit some strained common element or admit that 'pleasure' picks out a pretty ragged class, and then it's unclear what the theory is actually maximising. What I keep coming back to is that we nonetheless compare these pleasures all the time without much trouble: finishing the crossword was more pleasant than the bath, and I'll happily trade the bath for the crossword. My hunch is that the heterogeneity only looks like a problem if you assume those comparisons have to be underwritten by a shared felt dimension. Maybe the comparisons are more basic than the common quality, not the other way round. But thats probably me underestimating what hedonism needs.

Turn that hunch into a proposal. I want: a precise statement of the question, framed so it has a determinate answer; why the existing ways of handling the heterogeneity problem — felt-quality views, attitudinal views, functional-role views, determinable views — all leave this particular issue open; what the method would actually be, month by month, and why approaching it through hedonic comparison and trade-offs rather than through introspective phenomenology or just refining the concept of pleasure; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and an honest note on whether a year is realistic, with what you'd cut if it isn't.
