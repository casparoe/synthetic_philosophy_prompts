Two members of my department have spent the term arguing about whether social harmony and order can be defended as primary political values. One, whom I shall call the harmonist, defends a position with Confucian and conservative roots: a well-ordered society must cultivate shared norms, a measure of deference to legitimate authority, and non-adversarial ways of settling conflict, and these are valuable in themselves rather than merely as instruments. The other, whom I shall call the pluralist, defends a liberal position: a free society is constituted by open contestation among incompatible outlooks, and apparent consensus is often suppressed dissent made presentable. I have asked them to produce the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration, and I should like you to draft the document they ought to have written. It should contain (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both can sign; (2) a table of disagreements phrased in neutral wording each accepts as fair to its own view, with merely verbal disputes flagged; and (3) a section on cruxes, each stated as a sub-question the answer to which would move at least one side. I suspect the cruxes include whether harmony is best analysed as the absence of open conflict or as the successful coordination of differences within a shared order, and whether institutionalised contestation tends over time to produce mutual accommodation or to entrench majority dominance. For each crux, specify a concrete case that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each would predict about that case. Write the document in British English.
