Construct a thought experiment for a philosophy of causation seminar in which an interventionist theory of actual causation says event c caused event e, but a simple counterfactual-dependence theory says c did not cause e. Use stripped-down versions: counterfactual dependence—c caused e iff if c had not occurred, e would not have occurred; interventionism—c caused e iff there is a possible intervention on the variable corresponding to c that changes the variable corresponding to e, holding relevant off-path variables fixed at their actual values. The scenario should be ordinary and realistic, set in a hospital or workplace, one paragraph, with no rock-throwing philosophers, firing squads, or magic pills. The negative counterfactual verdict should come from a backup process, not from an obvious common-cause confound; the positive interventionist verdict must be earned by actually specifying the intervention and the variables held fixed, not stipulated. After the scenario, provide: the variables and structural equations (or an otherwise explicit causal graph); the actual values; the counterfactual the simple dependence theory relies on and why it denies causation; the intervention, off-path variables held fixed, and why interventionism affirms causation; which feature of the case does the work and how the case would fail without it; and how a defender of the counterfactual-dependence theory would attack the interventionist's holding-fixed move. Throughout, separate the settled structural logic from genuinely contested interpretative claims about which theory better captures ordinary causal judgment, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Keep the response under 700 words.
