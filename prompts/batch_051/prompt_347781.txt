Write a dialogue between a retributivist and a consequentialist about punishment. Don’t let either be a strawman. The retributivist keeps returning to something like Kant’s argument in the Doctrine of Right that punishment can never be imposed merely as a means to some social good, such as deterrence or rehabilitation, but only because the offender has committed a crime. The consequentialist has to actually engage with that claim, not just wave it away with benefits. Put them on a hard case: a punishment that is fully secret, with no possible general deterrence and no rehabilitation because the offender will be permanently isolated. The retributivist thinks punishment is still required; the consequentialist must say why not, or accept something awkward. Give me exactly five numbered exchanges, each consisting of one retributivist move and one consequentialist reply, each move one or two sentences. No introduction or conclusion.
