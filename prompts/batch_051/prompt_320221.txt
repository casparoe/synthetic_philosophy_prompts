We've got about £120,000 a year for three years to spend in one post-industrial town, and the committee is deadlocked between two bids. The other bid would put the same money into a meals-on-wheels and home-visiting service for isolated older people, which currently has a waiting list. The chair wants a decision in time for our meeting on wednesday and says she'll break the tie herself if we can't produce something decent. Draft the written opinion I'll circulate beforehand. Recommend one bid — or a split, if you can genuinely defend it — with a time frame and a condition or two we could attach. Give the strongest argument for the losing side its due, not just a nod, and face the conceptual question head-on: is belonging something worth spending public money on even when the measurable outcomes are fuzzy, and what exactly is lost if the last shared space closes? Also make sure the whole thing is in British English.
