Something I keep running into when I read about AI interpretability, and also in arguments about what some system is "really" trying to do, and I've never had a good word for it.

Here's the shape of it. When you watch an optimizer's behavior and try to say what objective it's actually pursuing, the inference only works if you also commit to a guess about its search space: which moves it actually considered, how deep it could look, what options were available to it. But the inference goes the other way too. Given an objective, you can guess which moves it probably considered; given the moves it probably considered, you can guess what objective would make the behavior sensible. And the two guesses can be adjusted against each other almost freely. If the behavior looks bad for the objective I assumed, I can either say the objective was different, or say the search was shallower, or more constrained, or pruned in a particular way. Both stories fit the same behavior, and they point in opposite directions for what you should predict next.

Concrete example: I watch a chess engine bail out of a promising-looking attack and trade down into a drawish endgame. Do I conclude it was aiming for a draw, or that it was aiming for a win but its search found the attack unsound at a depth I can't see? The hypotheses only come apart if I already know what it searched and how it evaluated what it found. And I see the same structure outside games: a company whose behavior fits "maximizes revenue but searches badly" just as well as "maximizes something else but searches well."

I know this is adjacent to ordinary underdetermination of theory by evidence, and to the economists' revealed-preference move, and maybe to the "it's rational under some utility function" move. But what I'm after is narrower: the specific two-way coupling between a guessed objective and a guessed search space, where behavior underdetermines both jointly, so the split into "what it wanted" and "what it could think of" has too many free parameters. Does that have an established name? And if it's really just a special case of something broader, what does the special case add, and where are its edges? I won't be satisfied with a bare label; I want the carving.
