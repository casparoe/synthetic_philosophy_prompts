I'm trying to work out what I actually believe about changing my mind over time and about binding my future self, going by my gut reactions to cases.

- Every January I buy an annual gym membership, having done the same for four years now, go twice, then stop. Verdict: not merely a budgeting mistake, but a distinct failure of rationality — I am predictably wrong about a future version of myself.
- I put down a non-refundable deposit for a six-month language course because I know that evening-me will not want to go; the deposit makes me attend. Verdict: rational, perhaps the most rational arrangement in my life.
- I keep signing up for free trials intending to cancel, then forget and pay for months. Verdict: irrational, but I also feel taken advantage of, and I cannot say whether the wrong is in my inconsistency or in the company's design.
- A friend does the same free-trial dance but sets a reminder and cancels in time. Verdict: she is not irrational.
- At a restaurant where dessert was pre-ordered with the meal, I eat it while full because I paid for it. Verdict: irrational.
- I ask my partner to hide the cookies because I know I will eat them all tonight and regret it. Verdict: rational.
- I simply decide each evening to eat the cookies, never plan otherwise, and do not regret it. Verdict: not irrational; there is no inner conflict.

My tentative principle is that someone is rational to the extent that she anticipates and steers her future choices rather than being surprised by them, but that principle seems to count the dessert case as rational, and I do not accept that.

What principle, or small set of principles, best captures these verdicts? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: I buy a non-refundable plane ticket to a family wedding partly because I know that, a week before the wedding, I will want to stay home and will believe my presence does not matter. Is the ticket a rational form of self-binding or a sunk-cost trap of the very kind the dessert case condemns? And does it matter that some of the cases involve someone else deliberately profiting from my reversals, or is my irrationality the same whether or not I am being money-pumped?
