I am a PhD student in political theory, and I am drafting a chapter on how mental health law thinks about freedom. I would like you to act as a philosopher who works on the conceptual structure of freedom. Before you write, think through each of the three standard candidate views — negative liberty as the absence of interference by others, positive liberty as self-mastery or self-realization, and republican liberty as non-domination, the secure standing of not living under another's arbitrary will — and how each would read the practice I describe below. Work through that comparison before committing, even if not all of it appears in the final answer.

The practice is involuntary civil commitment. A person can be detained in a psychiatric facility and treated, including medicated, against their expressed refusal, on findings that they are a danger to themselves or others or are gravely disabled, even when they have committed no crime. The legal system officially frames this not as a straightforwardly paternalistic loss of freedom but as protecting the person, and often as restoring their freedom or autonomy: the illness is said to be interfering with the person's own will, so the intervention removes an internal obstacle to self-government. At the same time, the same process includes adversarial hearings, legal representation, independent medical opinions, and periodic review, as if it also must guard against arbitrary power over the person.

In no more than 900 words, give me a reconstruction of the theory of freedom this practice presupposes. I want four things. First, state which view, or which mixture across the trichotomy, is doing the real normative work, and why the obvious negative-liberty reading fails to capture how the practice justifies itself. Second, assess internal coherence: do the treatment provisions presuppose one theory of freedom while the procedural safeguards presuppose another? Third, separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits: where is it underdetermined between rival views? Fourth, say what would have to change institutionally if the governing view were replaced by a thoroughgoing negative-liberty theory, and then by a republican non-domination theory. Conclude with a verdict on whether the official claim that coercion in these cases restores freedom is a coherent philosophical position or a confusion in need of being dropped. No citations or reading list; I want the conceptual reconstruction, not a literature review.
