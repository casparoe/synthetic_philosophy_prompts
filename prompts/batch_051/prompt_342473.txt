Here are the criteria I work with for calling something a genuine Darwinian replicator rather than just a loose analogy: (a) structure actually gets copied from one instance to the next, so copies descend from earlier copies instead of being independently reinvented; (b) copying is imperfect, so heritable variation keeps arising; (c) there is differential success — some variants spread further because of their own features; and (d) the population has a branching lineage structure, so in principle you could reconstruct ancestry.

Now take the belief that you should put a piece of tape over your laptop camera. People pass it to each other with stories about hackers or spy agencies watching people through webcams, but plenty of people also arrive at it on their own after reading about real remote-access attacks. The scarier, simpler variants spread the fastest; versions that keep historical detail constantly get stripped down until almost everyone is left with just the bare "tape the camera" idea. For any given person, it's often impossible to tell whether they copied the belief from someone else or reconstructed it from the underlying facts, and almost nobody knows the lineage of their own copy.
