I am the student representative on my high school's dress code review committee. The committee is reconsidering a rule that requires girls to wear skirts or dresses at school functions while boys must wear collared shirts and ties. A parent on the committee has circulated a passage by Simone de Beauvoir, paraphrasing it as the claim that a person is not born a woman but becomes one through the expectations and routines imposed from childhood. She argues that a gendered dress code is exactly the kind of institution that produces the feminine dispositions it claims merely to recognize, and that the school should adopt a single, sex-neutral standard. Another member has pushed back, saying the passage is a piece of philosophical analysis about adult life in a sexist society, not a workable standard for a secondary school, and that a school can assign different clothing rules to boys and girls without thereby treating them unequally.

The chair has asked me to draft a short decision memo for the committee before our next meeting. The memo should recommend one of the following: adopt the unisex standard, keep the current gendered rules, or amend the rules in a specific third way. It should take the parent’s appeal to Beauvoir seriously, spell out what that passage can and cannot support, and state fairly the strongest consideration against the recommendation before giving the committee a clear, actionable bottom line.
