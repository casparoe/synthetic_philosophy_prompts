Hey, I've been going back and forth on the situationist critique of virtue ethics for a while and I'd love a dialogue version of the disagreement, because when I try to hold both sides in my head they just talk past each other. I've read enough of the Doris/Harman-style argument to worry it's not just "virtue is rare" but something closer to "the whole notion of character traits is doing no real explanatory work," and I've also read enough virtue ethics to worry the situationists are attacking a cartoon version of virtue. I know the actual evidence is messier then the pop version suggests, so I'd like a dialogue between two people who both know the data and are genuinely trying to locate the disagreement, not score points.

The evidence I have in mind is the classic social psych stuff: Milgram, the Princeton seminarian Good Samaritan study, the Hartshorne-May cheating studies, the bystander-effect findings, and Mischel's old claim that personality predicts behavior at something like r = .3. If you know other relevant studies, feel free to use them, but don't make either character depend on a niche study the other could reasonably call cherry-picked.

Please make both characters equally competent. I don't want the virtue ethicist to just wave at "virtues are rare and developed over time" without answering the consistency problem, and I don't want the situationist to pretend that a one-shot lab experiment with a confederate is a clean test of character. A real back-and-forth where they respond to each other's actual points would be ideal.

One specific request: near the end, have each character say what would actually change their mind, like the concrete result or argument that would make them substantially update, not a rhetorical gotcha. And maybe have each of them close by saying where their own side is most likely to be fooling itself. If they end up noticing that "virtue" or "character" is doing different work for each of them, I'd love to see them tabboo at least one of those terms at some point.
