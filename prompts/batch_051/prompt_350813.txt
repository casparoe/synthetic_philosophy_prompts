I'm a retired chemist who fell into philosophy a couple years ago, and I've been working through the question of whether love responds to the value of the beloved or is more like the thing that bestows value in the first place (I think the jargon is appraisal versus bestowal). I've ended up with five commitments and I honestly cant tell wether they hang together or whether I've papered over a contradiction. Could you audit the set before I bring it to my reading group?

Here they are:

(a) Love is not a response to the beloved's qualities. If it were, then when someone turned up with the same qualities but more of them, you'd have reason to trade up, and that gets love wrong.

(b) Loving someone gives you reasons you would not otherwise have: to look after them, to keep in touch, to put up with things you would never tolerate from a stranger.

(c) Love can be appropriate or inappropriate. Loving someone cruel, or going on loving someone who is wrecking you, is a mistake in a real sense, not just a preference like liking olives.

(d) What makes a person worthy of love is something every person has equally — being a person, a rational creature, whatever one calls it — not their charm or virtue or wit.

(e) You cannot decide to love someone, and you cannot decide to stop.

I'm not sure which of these pull against each other, which only seem to, and what the cheapest repair would be. I keep circling one thing: if (d) is right, why would loving one person give me special reasons toward that person and not toward every other equally worthy person? And if (b) is right, in what sense is an inappropriate love still a mistake?

Give me the audit as a JSON object with these fields:

- "verdict": a string, one of "coherent", "coherent with revisions", or "incoherent", plus a one-sentence justification.
- "tensions": an array of objects, each with fields "commitments" (a list of the letters involved), "severity" (one of "severe", "moderate", "apparent only"), "explanation" (what exactly clashes and why), "cheapest_fix" (the least costly revision that would resolve it), and "cost" (what that revision gives up).
- "apparent_only": an array of objects, each with fields "commitments" and "why_it_dissolves" (why the seeming clash is not a real one).
- "recommended_revision": a string naming the single change that would clean up the most, and why.
