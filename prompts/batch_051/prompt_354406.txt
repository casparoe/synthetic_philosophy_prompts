Been going back and forth with my housemate about AI legal personhood, and I ended up reading Blackstone — the bit in the Commentaries on the Laws of England, Book I, Chapter 18 ("Of Corporations") where he says:

"We have hitherto considered persons in their natural capacities, and have treated of their rights and duties. But, as all personal rights die with the person; and, as the necessary forms of investing a series of individuals, one after another, with the same identical rights, would be very inconvenient, if not impracticable; it has been found necessary, when it is for the advantage of the public to have any particular rights kept on foot and continued, to constitute artificial persons, who may maintain a perpetual succession, and enjoy a kind of legal immortality."

I can't shake how this sounds to me. Blackstone's point is that the law fabricates an artificial person when it wants rights to outlive the natural persons who originally held them. So "person" in the legal sense is just a device the law invents because its convenient. Which makes me wonder why AI legal personhood gets treated like this enormous metaphysical or moral leap. We already have corporate persons, and nobody demands that a corporation be conscious or capable of suffering. Why not just declare a sophisticated AI an artificial person in Blackstone's sense, and then decide piecemeal which capacities it gets — owning property, making contracts, standing to sue, maybe being subject to suit? The usual comeback I hear is "but a corporation is just an aggregation of human beings, and its legal personality just organizes their interests," while an AI would be... not-humans, I guess, a thing acting in its own right? But Blackstone doesn't say an artificial person has to be composed of humans; he says the law constitutes one when some set of rights would otherwise keep dying, or something like that. So why wouldn't the corporate analogy do more work than people let it? Am I missing a distinction that's actually in the passage, or is the resistance mostly vibes?
