Been chewing on Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary thing from Gödel, Escher, Bach, the bit where a whole ant colony gets treated as a single character you can talk to, even though no individual ant has any idea what the colony as a whole is doing. The argument, as I understand it, is that a mind is a certain kind of high-level pattern running on lower-level parts which are themselves mindless; the colony gets something like a soul, or at least mind-ish standing, because of how the ants' signals organize into a stable self-referential structure. Okay. But now we've gone and built systems that look like exactly this in a concrete, running form: a large language model, where no single weight, neuron, or attention head understands anything, yet the whole thing writes coherent paragraphs and does something that sure looks like reasoning under a prompt. So here's what I want to know. Does the Aunt Hillary argument, taken seriously, just entail that a large language model has a mind? If it does, is that a problem for the argument, in the sense that it overgenerates and hands out minds too cheaply, or is it a conclusion we should actually accept? And if you think the ant colony claim doesnt carry over to the LLM, say exactly which step fails: what the colony supposedly has that the transformer lacks, or what the transformer has that breaks the analogy the other way. I care less about the final yes or no than about which premise in Hofstadter's line gets tested when you run it against a real built case, and whether there's a repaired version that still says something worth saying. Please answer in continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just written like an email or a letter.
