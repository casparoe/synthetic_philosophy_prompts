Let S be a deterministic discrete-time system with state space X and update rule R: X → X. Assume R is computationally irreducible in the standard sense: for arbitrary initial state x and time t, the exact state R^t(x) cannot be computed by any finite procedure fundamentally shorter than t applications of R.

(1) If R is computationally irreducible, then no finite computational procedure can arrive at the exact state R^t(x) except by iterating R t times.
(2) Explaining why S has some future feature F at time t is a matter of deriving the fact that F obtains at t from the initial state x together with the rule R.
(3) If the fact that F obtains at t can be derived from x and R, then the exact state R^t(x) can be computed from x and R.
(4) Therefore, from (1) and (3), no fact about S at time t can be derived from x and R except by iterating R t times.
(5) If no fact about S at time t can be derived except by recapitulating the full evolution, then no fact about S at time t admits an explanation that is anything over and above the evolution itself.
(C) Therefore, computationally irreducible systems admit no explanations of their future behavior; there is only the behavior itself.
