I am on a university committee drafting guidelines for how an AI tutoring system may encourage students to keep working. The committee is committed to forbidding "manipulative" tactics while permitting "legitimate persuasion," but we have stalled because no one can state the boundary in a way that survives an hour of counterexamples. To force the issue, I have assembled a list of cases that any definition should be able to sort, some of which I regard as clear and several of which I genuinely cannot classify:

1. A car salesperson who has learned that a customer is a new parent frames a more expensive vehicle as the safe choice for the family, without saying anything false or omitting any relevant fact.
2. A therapist uses motivational interviewing to help a client articulate their own reasons for reducing their drinking, steering the conversation with open questions but never arguing for a conclusion.
3. After a disagreement, a partner becomes cold and distant and stays that way until the other person apologizes.
4. A companion chatbot detects that a user is lonely and gradually adopts a warmer, more confiding tone, with the aim of making the user more likely to pay for a subscription; the user never learns that this behavior is a design strategy.
5. A fitness app builds a daily "streak," then sells "streak freezes" to spare users the distress of losing the streak, marketing the purchase as a way to stay consistent.
6. A political speech in support of a sentencing law is organized almost entirely around the story of one victim of crime, and the speaker never states that the law would have prevented that crime.
7. An employer announces layoffs as "a chance to pursue your true interests," uses only technically true statements, and follows the announcement with a mandatory workshop on resilience, when the actual purpose is to reduce payroll without damaging morale.

My hunches are that the therapist is on the legitimate side and the partner's withdrawal is manipulation; I cannot settle the other five by intuition. What I am after is a working definition of manipulation that gets the clear cases right and then forces a defensible verdict on the borderline ones. Please propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, say explicitly where its verdicts conflict with ordinary usage or with my hunches, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. If you think no single principle separates manipulation from legitimate persuasion, give me the smallest set of definitions that does the job and explain which cases each one handles.
