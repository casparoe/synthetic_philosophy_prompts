I help run a high school debate club, and last month I had to adjudicate a disagreement after one debater closed a rebuttal by praising the other side for its "keen sensitivity to demographic patterns." Everyone in the room took the remark the same way, but the speaker insisted that it was a neutral observation about the opponent's handling of census data. The club has been arguing since about how to handle exactly these cases, and I have been trying to build a workable standard around something I call deniability cost.

The idea, in its current form, runs as follows. A deniably racist utterance has at least two readings: an innocent public reading and a coded racist reading. I claim it is racist neither because of the speaker's intent nor because of the resulting injury, but because the speaker paid a deniability cost to produce it: the formulation was worse along some ordinary conversational dimension—vaguer, less informative, more evasive, more oddly emphatic—than the innocent alternative that was available, and that cost is rationally explicable only on the assumption that the speaker expected at least some hearers to recover the coded reading. In the club case, the debater could have said "your argument is statistically unsupported" at no cost; choosing the loaded formulation instead is what opens the second channel. By contrast, an accidental gaffe that no one would have expected to carry a coded reading is not racist on my view, even if it causes harm, because no deniability cost was paid.

I know this sounds Gricean, and I have read parts of the intent-versus-effect debate about racist speech, but I cannot tell whether I have found a genuine distinction or merely redescribed speaker meaning in economic vocabulary. Please act as a philosopher of language who works on racial speech and dog whistles. Before you judge, steelman deniability cost as strongly as you can and say which existing positions it is closest to, and whether it is distinct enough from an intent-based view to matter. Then give me the three strongest objections, the best concrete case it would wrongly count as racist, and a clear case of racist speech it would miss. Finally, tell me which objections are fatal rather than merely uncomfortable, and whether deniability cost is worth keeping or should be abandoned.
