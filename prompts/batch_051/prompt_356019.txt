I am a high school student writing an essay about whether higher-level things such as words, rules, and ideas can genuinely make physical events happen, or whether such talk is only a useful shorthand. A lecturer recently described a machine built from a wide, shaking tray filled with thousands of tiny magnetic beads. The beads obey nothing but the local laws of collision and magnetism. Sometimes, by accident, the beads lock into a large, stable arrangement, and after that the arrangement seems to manage the traffic of the loose beads around it: stray beads are funneled along its edges, deflected by its corners, and trapped in its gaps. The lecturer called this arrangement a symbol and said that this is what it means for a symbol to push its substrate around. Yet I keep running into the same difficulty. If the motion of each individual bead is already fully explained by the physical forces acting on it, then the symbol is nothing over and above the beads; it contributes no additional force. Would not a complete microphysical account of the tray make all talk of the pattern steering the beads merely a convenient summary, rather than a statement about a real cause? I want to know whether a higher-level pattern such as this is genuinely doing something, or whether the lecturer was, strictly speaking, speaking metaphorically.
