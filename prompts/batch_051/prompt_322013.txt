I am compiling objections to pure time preference—the view that a benefit or harm should count for less merely because it lies further in the future, over and above any discounting for uncertainty about whether it will occur. The objections I already have are these:

1. The positional-arbitrariness objection, associated with Sidgwick: bare difference in temporal position is no more a reasonable ground for unequal concern than a bare difference in spatial position would be.

2. The weakness-of-the-imagination objection, associated with Ramsey: pure time preference reflects a failure to bring distant benefits and harms vividly before the mind, which is a defect in us rather than a reason to value them less.

3. The future-Tuesday objection, associated with Parfit: devaluing something because it lies in the future is like devaluing it because it falls on a Tuesday; the one disposition would be called arbitrary, and the other is arbitrary for the same sort of reason.

4. The conflation objection: the legitimate justifications for discounting expected value over time—uncertainty about realization or continued existence, and productive opportunity costs—do not require any additional discount for futurity itself, so a separate pure rate does no justificatory work.

5. The intergenerational-justice objection: across generations, a pure rate of discount privileges those born earlier over those born later in a way that could not be justified to the later-born parties themselves.

I want one further objection to pure time preference that is not on this list and is not a variant, special case, or combination of any of these five; it should have a different underlying structure. After presenting it, say briefly whether you regard it as stronger or weaker than the listed objections and what it adds.
