I've been trying to graft some Nishida onto my philosophy of mind and I want a consistency check before I present it at a department workshop. Here's the package: (1) perceptual experience in its lived character is pure experience, a flow that doesn't yet carve the world into a subject over here and an object over there; the split is something that crystallizes out of it later. (2) Once that subject-object split crystallizes, it's real and indispensable for judgment, not an illusion to be dissolved. (3) The place in which experience occurs — Nishida and the Kyoto School call this basho, the logical space of appearing — can't itself be one of the things that appears in experience; it isn't an object, a subject, or a container inside the world. (4) But I'm also a committed representationalist: perceiving is a relation between a mind and mind-independent objects, and truth is correspondence between a representation and what its about. (5) And I think self-consciousness is best explained as higher-order representation, a thought about a first-order mental state, rather than anything built into experience itself. Do these hang together? Go through them pair by pair: which actually pull against each other, which only appear to, and which one should I revise first if I want to keep as much of the package as possible? Start with the most damaging tension and work down.
