I keep meeting the line "everyone else is an NPC" and I want it taken apart properly. The latest occurrence was an argument in my company's internal engineering forum about whether an AI agent that pair-programs with us should be treated as a colleague or as a tool. Someone dismissed the whole discussion by saying the distinction doesn't matter, because none of us can prove that a human coworker is any less scripted than the agent is; for all he can prove, everyone else is an NPC and he is the only player character. The phrase seems to do several different jobs in these discussions — sometimes a claim about what I can know, sometimes a claim about what other people are, sometimes a claim about how I should treat them — and I want those jobs separated.

I have some rough candidate readings so you know the register I am working in; feel free to split, merge, replace, or add to them:

a. Others' inner lives: other people run a behavior-producing loop, but there is no first-person point of view behind the loop.
b. Epistemic: I have direct access only to my own experience, so other people's inner lives are an inference; they could be absent without my noticing any difference.
c. Observability: other people exist only when rendered by my attention, the way a game engine culls or down-grades entities outside the camera frustum.
d. Moral weight: other people's welfare counts less, or not at all, because they lack something the player character has.
e. Deflationary: I am also an NPC, and the player/NPC split is a type error from the start.
