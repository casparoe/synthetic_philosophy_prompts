So my brother and I keep having this argument about whether thinking about yourself does more harm than good. He has anxiety spirals that he says are pure waste, but Ive kept journals that genuinely sorted me out, and we couldnt settle it. So I want to try a what-if on you instead. Suppose a cheap little earpiece comes out (people are calling it a rumination governor, not my name for it) that detects when your self-examination is stuck in a loop: replaying the same mistake, catastrophizing about something nobody else remembers, asking why over and over without getting anywhere. It doesnt stop painful thoughts, and it doesnt make people self-reflect who werent already doing it. It just nudges the stuck thinking into a slower, fairer, more concrete version of itself; you still land on the painful conclusion if its true, but the spinning and the distortions (like always ruining everything) get blocked. The trials are solid, and people report they still feel the real feeling, it just stops snowballing.

What I want isnt whether youd wear it. I want how the world looks twenty years after the device gets as cheap and normal as meditation apps. Give me exactly five predictions, no introduction or conclusion, just a numbered list, each one a sentence or two. One prediction each for: talk therapy, close friendships, religion or twelve-step programs, creative work, and the ordinary guilty conscience people carry. For each, tell me the prediction, roughly how confident you are, and the part that most depends on whether the device actually gives people honest self-knowledge instead of just quieter rumination.
