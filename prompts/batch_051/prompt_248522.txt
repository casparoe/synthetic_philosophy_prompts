In rationalist-adjacent writing about priority disputes, I often see simultaneous invention treated as if it settles the question of whether discoveries are inevitable, and everything downstream of that. The analogy, as I usually encounter it, runs like this: scientific and mathematical ideas exist as stable attractors in a fixed epistemic fitness landscape, and independent inquirers are separate optimisation processes that, starting from different points, eventually fall into the same basin of attraction. Newton and Leibniz were simply two trajectories converging on the same local optimum called the calculus; Darwin and Wallace were two trajectories converging on the same local optimum called natural selection. The conclusion people then draw is that priority disputes are status games with no real intellectual content, because the idea itself was already “there” in the landscape, waiting for any sufficiently well-adapted search process to reach it.
