I keep going back and forth on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, the one from his paper arguing that moral responsibility is impossible, and I can't make the pieces fit. The argument attracts me: the way he runs it, to be truly morally responsible for what you do, you'd have to be truly responsible for the way you are, because your actions flow from your character, motives, beliefs, and so on. But to be truly responsible for the way you are, you'd have to have intentionally brought it about that you are that way. And that requires you to already have existed as something with a way you are, which then has to be accounted for in the same way, and it regresses. So the conclusion is that nobody is ever truly morally responsible, no matter how much it feels like we are. At most there's a superficial sense where we say someone is responsible for dusting the shelves or whatever.

But here's the problem. When I imagine a case, like someone who tortures a dog just to watch it suffer, fully aware, fully in control, no coercion, no delusion—my intuition doesn't just whisper that he's responsible; it screams it. It doesn't feel like a useful fiction or a social convention. It feels like something about him, right then, made him a fitting target of blame. And I don't think I can live without that, or maybe I can live without it but I don't think I can think without it.

So the question isn't just whether Strawson is right. I want to know what each option costs. If the intuition yields, tell me how it can be debunked: is it self-interest, is it a byproduct of the way we're built to punish free-riders, is it a confusion between causation and compulsion? If the argument yields, where exactly does it go wrong—maybe in the step from you didn't choose your character to you're not responsible for what issues from your character, and if so, why would that step be invalid? Or is there a reading where both survive: maybe the argument only targets a kind of ultimate, God-like responsibility and leaves ordinary responsibility untouched, but I keep worrying that's just changing the subject.

One more thing: whatever you answer, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning—no preamble and no restating of the question.
