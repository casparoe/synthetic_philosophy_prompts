lets use Marx, and lets stay on the exploitation side rather than alienation. I want you to work with the category "wage-labourer" as Capital, Vol. I actually defines it. The passage I'm anchored on is from Chapter 6, "The Buying and Selling of Labour-Power":

"For the conversion of his money into capital, therefore, the owner of money must meet in the market with the free labourer, free in the double sense, that as a free man he can dispose of his labour-power as his own commodity, and that on the other hand he has no other commodity for sale, is short of everything necessary for the realisation of his labour-power."

My attempt to turn that into four criteria:

1. She is legally free to dispose of her labour-power as her own commodity (not a slave, serf, or indentured servant).
2. She has no other commodity to sell: she is separated from the means of production and has nothing to fall back on, so she is forced to sell labour-power to live.
3. What changes hands is labour-power itself, for a definite period, in return for a wage, rather than a finished good or service produced with her own means of production.
4. The buyer consumes her labour-power by controlling the labour process and taking ownership of the output, and the excess of value her labour creates over the wage is appropriated by the buyer.

Now the borderline case. Dana drives full time for a ride-hail app. She owns her car (still paying off the loan), works when she wants, and is legally an independent contractor, not an employee. The platform unilaterally sets fares, takes 25%, controls the app she has to work through, and can deactivate her if her rating falls bellow a threshold or she declines too many rides; she sometimes drives for a rival platform. After fuel, insurance, depreciation, and the loan, she clears about what a shelf-stocker makes, and she has no savings to fall back on. She tells her accountant that the car and phone are business assets and she is a small proprietor.

Question: is Dana a wage-labourer in the sense the passage is defining, and is the platform's 25% cut surplus value (or something else, like rent for access to a market)? Go criterion by criterion, flag where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and end with whichever criterion you think should be weakened, dropped, or reinterpreted because of the case. Keep whatever is genuinely contested among people who know Marx's theory clearly marked as contested, rather than presenting one side as the consensus. Format: exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no intro and no conclusion.
