I have been thinking about a science-fiction teleporter—the kind that scans a person, destroys the body at the departure point, and builds an exact copy from new material at the destination—and arguing about it with a friend. Because my reactions to different versions of the machine pull in different directions, I have written down everything I currently believe in one place. I cannot see where the contradictions are, and I would rather be corrected precisely than told vaguely that the whole position is confused.

Here are my beliefs.

1. What makes a later person me is the continuation of my memories, personality, habits, and sense of my own past. The particular material the body is made of does not matter for this.

2. If a machine scanned me, destroyed this body, and then built an exact physical copy of me at the destination from new materials, the person at the destination would be me. Using that machine would not be dying.

3. If the machine built the copy at the destination but left my original body alive, then the original is me and the newly built copy is a different person carrying my duplicated memories. If the original were then destroyed, I would have died, even though the copy would carry on and insist it was me.

4. If the machine built two copies at once and destroyed the original, neither copy would be me. I would have died, and two new people would exist.

5. I would not count as surviving if my memories and personality were installed in something other than a living human body—for instance, a computer simulation. The body matters too, not only the contents of the mind.

6. I believe there is a real fact of the matter about which of these verdicts is correct. They are not merely different ways of using words that could all be made to come out true.

Please go through these beliefs and tell me which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what exactly is causing it, what the least costly revision would be, and what that revision would sacrifice. Rank the tensions from most to least serious rather than treating them as equally important. I care more about learning precisely where the weight-bearing parts of my view are than about receiving a name for the problem.
