Everyone around me keeps saying resilience is like a muscle: it grows under load, you can't build it without resistance, and protecting people from every stress just leaves them brittle. They use this to shut down complaints about workload — no, the overload is the training stimulus — and to argue against lowering standards or softening things in the name of wellbeing. Some of them package this as fortitude being a virtue; others more or less admit it's a way of getting people to stop blaming the institution, wich is exactly why I find the whole line suspicious. How far does the analogy actually hold? Give me the mapping first — what plays the role of the muscle, the load, progressive overload, the rest day, recovery, the overtraining injury, the different training ages and baselines? Then tell me where it comes apart, which breaks matter for the conclusion they're using it for and which are just decoration, and whether the don't-shelter-people conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table or whether it was mostly the analogy carrying the load. And at the end tell me whether what's left, if anything, is best called a virtue or a piece of ideology.
