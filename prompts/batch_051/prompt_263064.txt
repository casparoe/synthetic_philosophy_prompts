Here's an argument I keep coming back to about AI interpreting AI, and I cant figure out where it goes wrong. (1) To verify that an interpretation of a large model is actually correct, you can't just have a human read the explanation and nod; the model is too big and its internals are too alien, so the verification itself has to be done by another automated system that can keep up with it. (2) But any automated system sophisticated enough to check interpretations of the first model is itself a large, uninterpreted system—if it were simple enough to be transparent, it couldn't do the job. (3) So verifying an interpretation of model 1 means trusting the outputs of an uninterpreted model 2. (4) By the exact same reasoning, verifying model 2 requires trusting an uninterpreted model 3, and so on. (5) Therefore automated interpretability either bottoms out in an infinite regress or in some uninterpreted system you simply have to trust, so you never get a grounded explanation of model 1. Is this valid? If not, which step fails, and on what reading of 'verify' or 'sophisticated enough' does it break? If it is valid, how much of the AI-interprets-AI project does it actually sink?
