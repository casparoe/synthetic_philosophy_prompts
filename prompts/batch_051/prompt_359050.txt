I have been trying to work out a view about moral worth and I would like an honest assessment of it. I call the view the Load-Bearing Reason Account. The usual debate asks whether an action has moral worth only when it is done for the right reasons, but I think the more diagnostic question is what it could be for the right reason to be the reason that actually produces the act. My claim is that a feature R gives an action full moral worth only if three conditions hold: R is a right-making feature of the act; R figures among the agent's motives; and R is counterfactually load-bearing in both of two directions. First, indispensability: had R not been among the agent's motives, with every other motive held fixed, she would not have performed the action. Second, self-standingness: had every motive other than R been absent, with R alone still moving her, she would still have performed the action. On this view a right-making feature can be present in deliberation, sincerely cited, or even causally relevant, and still not be doing the relevant work.

The view gets its bite from cases of overdetermined motivation. Imagine a surgeon who operates on an uninsured patient both because the patient urgently needs the operation and because a documentary crew is filming her for a story on charitable surgery. The patient's need is the right-making feature. It passes the self-standingness test: without the cameras, the surgeon would still operate. But it fails the indispensability test: had the patient's need not been among her motives, she would still have operated for the film. So the act has no full moral worth in virtue of that reason. I take that to be the correct, if uncomfortable, verdict, and I suspect full moral worth is much rarer than is usually assumed.

I know one-directional counterfactual tests sometimes get used in discussions of acting for a reason, and I am not sure whether my second direction is a real advance or just makes the view overstrict. Give me the three strongest objections to the Load-Bearing Reason Account, say whether any of them is fatal rather than a bullet to bite, and say whether the view, charitably reconstructed, is genuinely distinct from familiar right-reasons views or collapses into one of them.
