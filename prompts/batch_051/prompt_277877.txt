i've been trying to get clear on what 'avoiding side effects' could even mean for an AI, because the intuitive idea seems to need a baseline it never states: a side effect is a change relative to how things would have gone anyway, but 'how things would have gone anyway' is doing a lot of silent work and any action at all leaves some trace. so give me a working defintion of 'side effect' (or 'low impact' if you think thats the better term) that gets these cases right. a domestic robot is told to fetch a mug from the kitchen. (1) on the way it knocks over a lamp: clearly a side effect. (2) it hands you the mug: clearly not. (3) it opens the kitchen door and leaves it open all afternoon: probably a side effect, but is it the kind the designers meant? (4) told specifically to minimize side effects, it stays in its corner and fetches nothing, reasoning that any movement would alter the world a little: i want to say this is not compliance, but then avoiding side effects can't just mean changing nothing. (5) it takes a route that displaces some air, and in the usual chaos-theory way that changes whether it rains somewhere next month: i want to say that isnt a side effect in the relevant sense, but i can't say why not. (6) it scuffs the floor, notices, and repaints the scuff with a shade that isnt a perfect match: is the repainting itself a side effect, and is the slight color mismatch one even though the net change is smaller? (7) it buys an identical mug from the shop down the street instead of taking one from the cupboard, so the shopkeeper's afternoon is altered in all the usual small ways but the mug was obtained exactly as planned: are those ripples side effects, or are they just what it is for a task to happen in a shared world? propose the definition, run it through all seven, and tell me which verdict is least secure and where the defintion surprises you.
