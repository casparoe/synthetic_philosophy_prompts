I have recently been asked to help lead a small, confessionally Reformed congregation, and I keep returning to the first paragraph of chapter 21 of the Westminster Confession of Faith:

But the acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by Himself, and so limited by His own revealed will, that He may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation, or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture.

I understand the broad point: we are not free to invent ways of worshipping God. But I am struggling to see how the clause "or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture" is supposed to apply in practice, and I would like help thinking through the logic of the passage rather than a summary of confessional debates.

Specifically, I would like you to help me work through the following questions. First, does the passage require a positive scriptural prescription for every distinct element of corporate worship, or does it permit general categories of practice—such as singing, prayer, or stated times of assembly—within which many particular forms may be chosen? Second, how should one distinguish between "circumstances" of worship and "elements" of worship here, given that the confession itself only mentions that distinction later? Third, is the underlying principle one about divine authority, about human sinfulness, or about the nature of acceptable worship itself? That is, is the problem with unprescribed worship that God has not commanded it, that humans tend to corrupt worship when left to their own devices, or both?

I am less interested in being told the standard labels for positions than in being given a careful reconstruction of what the passage itself commits someone to, including where it is genuinely ambiguous. If the clause can be read in a stricter and a more moderate way, I would like to know what further assumptions would push someone toward one reading rather than the other.
