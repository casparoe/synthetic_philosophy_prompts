Here's the passage I keep coming back to, from Aquinas, Summa Theologica III, q. 76, a. 1:

Hence it is clear that the body of Christ is in this sacrament "by way of substance," and not by way of quantity.

My confusion is this. Being somewhere involves being extended, taking up space — a quantitative thing. So if Christ's body isn't there by way of quantity, how is it "there" at all? It sounds to me like "presence by way of substance" is just a way of saying the bread's substance got changed and then declining to answer the hard follow-up. Is the "real" in real presence just doing the job of ruling out "merely symbolic," and I've been wrong to picture the body as extended through the host? Or is my whole objection a confusion about what presence has to mean here? Keep the answer to one paragraph.
