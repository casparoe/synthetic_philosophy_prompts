I cover probability, forecasting, and the philosophy of science for a general-interest magazine, and I have been asked to lead a forty-five-minute seminar for the actuarial practice group of a large reinsurance firm. The audience is fluent in Bayesian updating and life tables, but its members are impatient with what they call philosophical cleverness, and my host has warned me that an argument will be granted a hearing only if it can be made to behave like a calculation they could reproduce on a whiteboard. I want to use the session to make the doomsday argument serious for them: the claim that a modest sampling assumption about one's own birth rank among all humans can rationally shift an estimate of how long our species will last. I expect resistance, and I want their resistance to land on the argument's genuine pressure points rather than on caricatures of it.

Do not write the seminar for me; give me the plan for it. I need: the order in which the components must be introduced and why, so that the self-sampling step feels like a neutral accounting rule rather than a conjuring trick; the one carrying example I should build the session around — I am inclined to use a sealed-envelope or two-urn setup rather than the familiar claim that roughly a hundred billion humans have lived so far, because I suspect the demographic head count invites a side-debate about how to count past humans that buries the argument's structure, but tell me if that instinct is wrong and what the example must have and must avoid; the misconceptions this particular audience will bring or form, including their likely assimilation of the argument to ordinary survival analysis, the reference-class objection, and the complaint that every past generation could have run the same argument, and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off; what I should openly decline to settle in the time available; and a closing question I can put to them whose answer would show that they have understood the argument's structure without having been converted to it.
