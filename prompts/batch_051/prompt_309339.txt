I've been thinking about why shared resources deteriorate and whether there is any general way to stop it. The shared garden behind my apartment building has turned into a patch of mud because everyone says they will water it and nobody does, and the same pattern seems to lie behind overfishing, overgrazing, air pollution, and the group projects at work where a couple of people end up doing everything. I've started collecting the different mechanisms people have proposed for dealing with this problem, and so far my list looks like this: (1) dividing the resource into private property, so that each owner directly bears the cost of overusing it; (2) central regulation, through quotas, permits, fines, or taxes that price the social cost of taking from the commons; (3) self-governance by the users themselves, who make their own rules, monitor one another, and apply graduated sanctions to violators; (4) costly punishment, where members can pay a personal cost to push back against a free rider—someone who takes the benefit without contributing—even without any formal rule in place; (5) selective incentives, where some extra benefit is reserved for contributors so that free riders cannot get it; and (6) assurance contracts, where everyone's pledge is refunded unless enough people commit, so nobody is left as the only one who paid in; one version of this even pays people a small bonus if the project does not go forward.

I have a strong feeling that I'm missing at least one more kind of mechanism, one that does not depend on property rights, on a central authority, on explicit rules, or on anyone deliberately harming a free rider. What could that mechanism be? Please tell me whether it is genuinely different from the six items above, rather than a rewording or special case of one of them. And please be honest about whether it would still work in a large, anonymous situation where people interact only once, or whether it depends on conditions that the real commons problems I started from usually don't meet.
