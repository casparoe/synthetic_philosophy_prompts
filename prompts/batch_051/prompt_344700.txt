I am a novelist, and I have been trying to determine whether a recurring experience in my work has the same structure as the knowledge argument or merely seems to. As I understand that argument: if someone confined to a colorless room were taught every physical fact about color, she would still learn something when she first saw red, namely what seeing red is like, and so no amount of physical knowledge entails phenomenal knowledge. My craft offers what looks like a parallel. Before drafting, I can know everything statable about a character: her history, her dispositions, the causal explanation of her choices, the fine details of her speech and manner. And yet I can fail to know her in the sense that matters for writing her from the inside. When the voice arrives, something appears to be gained, but I cannot tell whether it is a new fact about her, a new ability in me, or only a shift in my confidence about choices I had already made.

I would like an assessment of whether this parallel is deep or superficial. State the mapping in its strongest form and say what would have to be true of fictional characters and of phenomenal experience for results to transfer in either direction. Then identify at least one claim the analogy seems to license about one side that you take to be false or strained, and diagnose where the analogy gives out. I want to know whether its breaking point tells us anything informative about the knowledge argument, about the ontology of fictional characters, or only about the limits of an occupational metaphor.
