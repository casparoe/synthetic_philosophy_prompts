I'm taking a social choice / formal political theory course this term, and we spent a week on the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem. I got the formal statement down (or at least I think I did: at least three alternatives, unrestricted domain over strict rankings, non-imposed, then strategy-proof implies dictatorial), but a classmate sent me a explainer video that made it sound much more dramatic, and now I'm not sure how much of the dramatic version is fair. The video's summary was roughly this: the theorem says that with three or more candidates, any voting rule is either dictatorial or vulnerable to manipulation — the only method where no voter ever has an incentive to misreport their true preferences is one where a single voter is dictator. It then waved at the fact that the theorem assumes every possible preference ordering is allowed, said real politics is mostly organized along a left–right spectrum, and concluded the whole result is more of a mathematical curiosity than an actual problem for elections.

I buy the basic theorem, but the leap from 'the rule can be gamed sometimes' to 'elections never reveal anything' feels like a lot, and the left–right escape hatch also seemed too quick.
