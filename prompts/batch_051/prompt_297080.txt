At work I'm building a training pipeline where a big frontier model generates instruction examples and then acts as the judge that scores the smaller student model's responses, and a couple of people on the team keep calling the big model the "parent" and saying the small one gets "raised" on its outputs. I used to write this off as tech-bro anthropomorphism, but now I've been put in charge of what we call the "teacher supervision" step and the choice has stopped being cute: we have to decide whether the student is allowed to query the teacher during training, whether the teacher's labels get treated as ground truth or as a fallible annotator, and who ends up on the hook when the student picks up a bad habit — the student itself, the teacher model, my team, or the people who built the teacher. It's made me wonder what philosophical commitments the whole setup actually presupposes about the relation between the two systems. Does it presuppose that the teacher is an epistemic authority the student should defer to? That the teacher's outputs are normative for the student in something like the way a parent's guidance is? Or does it presuppose something about care and responsibility, and if not, are we just using parent language to dress up a pipeline that is really just one optimization target being shaped by another dataset generator?

My working hypothesis is that the practice doesn't really presuppose parenthood, but it does presuppose a lopsided authority relation: the teacher is treated as a fixed standard the student gets pushed toward, not as a tutor that changes its approach based on how the student is doing. And I suspect that if we actually took the guardianship talk seriously we'd have to change quite a lot — let the student shape its own curriculum, model something like the student's interests rather than just benchmark performance, build in some notion of the teacher owing the student a duty of care, and say what it would even mean for the student to be harmed by bad examples in a way that matters. But I keep flipping, because the entire apparatus was designed by people trying to get a model that passes evals, and the parent-talk might be just a gloss that doesn't presuppose much of anything. I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit view here, say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined between rival views, and tell me what in the pipeline would actually have to change if we swapped the parent picture for a straight tool-building picture, or vice versa.
