Im on a policy working group looking at welfare conditionality and someone floated a scheme I cant stop turning over. The city runs a workfare program: to keep your full benefit you have to log 12 hours a week of municipal tasks (litter-picking, painting park benches, that sort of thing). The proposal is to make those hours transferable. Recipients could use an app run by the welfare office to buy and sell their weekly hours among themselves. If I pay you to do my 12 hours, my benefit comes through untouched, but I pay you whatever we agree, capped at the local minimum wage per hour; the welfare office keeps a 10% processing fee, so you receive 90% of what I pay. The hours have to be done by a registered recipient and signed off by the normal site supervisor, and nobody can clock more than 40 hours a week of workfare in total (their own plus bought). I want you to treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position, and walk me through how it actually behaves. Who ends up doing the work, and what does the price of an hour reveal about who finds the requirement costly? Does the scheme make it easier or harder for the public to sort the deserving from the undeserving, and does it change what deserving even means here? Where does it get gamed first, and do the cap and the fee fix the failure modes or just shift them? Give me the bottom line in the first paragraph, then the reasoning, and keep the whole thing under 900 words.
