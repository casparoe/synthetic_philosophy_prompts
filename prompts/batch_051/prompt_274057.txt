The claim is that they can still rationally cooperate — for example, each sending resources the other needs on different planets with no communication channel between them — because each runs the counterparty's decision procedure and sees that the counterparty would run theirs, so the transaction executes in both places without any message ever passing. I want to know whether this is a real thing or just a disguised assumption.

Translate the agent, the decision procedure, the prediction or simulation relation, the conditional commitment, and the claimed gain from trade into that vocabulary. Then give me a residue report: which parts survive the translation cleanly, which parts you had to distort to make fit, and which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary. In particular, I'd like to know whether my natural move — treating each agent as a replica executing the same deterministic protocol over a predetermined log, and treating "predicting the other" as determinism of the shared state machine — actually captures the force of the original idea, or whether it smuggles in a causal channel or a trusted third party that the original scenario rules out. Conclude with your own view on whether the residue shows that acausal trade is less substantive than it looks once it is pressed into my framework, or that my framework is missing a concept it needs.
