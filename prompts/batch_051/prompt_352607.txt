I've been reading Dewey for a course on pragmatism and American public administration. The professor has been using the Tennessee Valley Authority as a recurring example, but I want a careful case analysis rather than a quick illustration. As I understand it, the TVA was created in 1933 as a federal corporation to plan the Tennessee River watershed: building dams for flood control and electricity, improving navigation, promoting soil conservation, and encouraging economic development in a chronically poor region. It was deliberately regional rather than national, and it combined technical expertise with local agricultural extension and citizen participation, at least at some stages of its history.

In The Public and Its Problems, Dewey argues that a public forms when people recognize the indirect consequences of their associated activities and organize to regulate them, and in Liberalism and Social Action he argues that social policies should be treated as hypotheses to be tested, revised, or abandoned in light of consequences. He also thinks modern industrial society has dispersed and complicated those consequences so much that the public tends to become eclipsed: it exists as an effect but not as a self-conscious agent.

I'd like you to use the TVA as a test case for that theory. Specifically: (1) In what respects does the TVA look like a Deweyan experiment in democratic inquiry—its regional scope, its integration of research and action, its attempts to involve local communities? (2) Where does the analogy bend—for example, in the role of federal authority, the displacement of residents for reservoirs, the priority given to centralized technical expertise, or the fact that the initiative was not designed to be discontinued if it failed? (3) Does the TVA episode tend to confirm Dewey's view that inquiry and democracy can be fused in public administration, or does it expose a tension between experimentalism as method and democracy as self-government? I want the mapping to be explicit and honest about which features of the TVA the theory is silent about, not forced.
