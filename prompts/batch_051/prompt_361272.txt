My friend runs a small giving circle and I've kept notes on her past calls. She funded a deworming program whose long-run evidence she thought was shaky, because it cost pennies per child; she passed on an expensive but extremely well-evidenced depression treatment because the per-person cost was huge; she funded a lab working on a rare, almost totally ignored disease, saying the field was wide open; she passed on earthquake relief after a major quake because everyone else was already piling in; and she funded a policy campaign with maybe a 2% chance of success but an enormous payout if it won. Now she's weighing a proposal to distribute oral rehydration salts for childhood diarrhea in a region where the big foundations already run large programs. The evidence is as strong as anything she's ever seen and the cost per child is tiny. If she wants to be consistent with her own record, should she fund it? Which of her earlier decisions is the closest analogue, and if her record genuinely underdetermines the answer, what principle would she be adopting either way? What future case would best separate the two readings of her?
