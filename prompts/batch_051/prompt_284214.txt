There's a view about recursive interpretability that I keep going back and forth on: an AI can only fully interpret another AI up to roughly its own capacity, because a full interpretation has to be a compressed model of the target's internals that fits inside the interpreter alongside all the interpreter's own machinery. If the target is about as complex as the interpreter, the compression has to drop details that matter for behavior, so you get either a lossy account or a copy that doesn't actually explain. But I can't tell whether that's a real in-principle ceiling or just a parameter-count and compute problem. Design the best experiment you can to separate these, using open-weight models of different sizes and whatever behavioral or mechanistic probes you want. Then say what result would push you toward a hard ceiling, what result would push you toward just needing a bigger interpreter, and what part of the dispute no experiment of this kind could settle.
