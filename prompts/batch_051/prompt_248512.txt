I've been trying to hold two things in my head at once, and I honestly don't know which one to let go of. On the one hand, there's the no-free-lunch idea from learning theory. Not the restaurant thing. The formal result that, if you average over *all* possible patterns a sequence could have, every prediction rule does exactly as well as every other one — the rule that says "the sun will rise tomorrow because it always has" is no better than the rule that says "it won't, just to be contrary." It's a clean mathematical argument, and it seems to show that induction only works because you've already smuggled in a bias toward simple or regular worlds. Hume lands as a theorem: uniformity of nature isn't something you can earn from the data; it's something you have to assume.

On the other hand, I can't shake the intuition that this is a clever trick. After I've seen a thousand sunrises, I am not in the same epistemic position as someone who's seen none and just guesses. There's something about the fact that the world is regular — that it's compressible, that short programs describe it — that makes my induction justified in a way that a contrary rule isn't. Saying "well, your prior is just a bias" feels like it proves too much: every reasoning process has to start somewhere, so calling my regularity prior arbitrary drains the word "arbitrary" of meaning. I want to say the theorem is true of a world I don't live in, and my intuition is about the world I do.

Walk me through the options. Should I debunk the intuition as a scope-sensitivity artefact — I'm only picturing the patterns that matter to me and ignoring the huge space of worlds where induction loses? Should I amend or restrict the theory, maybe by saying the theorem's "all possible worlds" is the wrong place to look, because simplicity is itself a discoverable feature of this universe rather than a free-floating assumption? Or is there a reading where both are right and just talking past each other? Tell me what each move costs and which one leaves me in the best position — don't just say "they're compatible" and call it a day.
