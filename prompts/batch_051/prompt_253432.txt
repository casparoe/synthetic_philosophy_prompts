I have been working with a four-part account of what makes something a marriage. On this account, a marriage is a relationship that (1) is publicly and legally recognized, and thereby creates obligations the parties did not previously have; (2) is intended to last indefinitely rather than to terminate at either party's convenience; (3) involves a shared life — cohabitation, pooled resources, and mutual responsibility for one another's care; and (4) includes a sexual relationship between the spouses. Criterion (4) is what distinguishes marriage, on this account, from a close friendship or a caregiving arrangement; it is also what makes sense of the ideas that a marriage can be consummated and that sexual infidelity is a distinctive kind of betrayal. Notice that criterion (4) does not require procreation: a same-sex couple, a sterile couple, and a couple past childbearing age can all satisfy it as stated.

Now consider a case that sits uneasily on the edge. Two men in their late sixties have been close friends and housemates for thirty years. They own their home jointly, keep shared accounts, and each is the other's medical proxy, emergency contact, and heir. They are both gay, but they have never been romantically or sexually involved with each other, and neither expects that to change; any romantic relationships either has are with other men, with the other's full knowledge. They now marry, legally and publicly, because each wants the other to be the person who can make decisions and share responsibility as they age. They intend the marriage to last for the rest of their lives, and they are candid with one another, with the state, and with their community about the absence of any sexual relationship between them.

Go through the four criteria and say whether this is a marriage on my account. If it fails the fourth criterion, say whether that failure reflects a defect in the criteria or something the criteria are tracking: is the fourth criterion doing genuine work, or is it a leftover from a procreative conception of marriage that the rest of the account has dropped? If you think the relationship satisfies all four criteria as stated, then say what marriage is for on this account, and whether the account can explain why we should keep marriage distinct from intimate caregiving and economic partnership generally.
