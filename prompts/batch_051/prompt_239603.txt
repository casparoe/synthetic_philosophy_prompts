I've got this habit where if I can picture a situation in detail without running into a flat-out contradiction, I take it as at least possible; if I can't even get my imagination to hold it together, I take it as impossible. Lately I came across this in Hume's Treatise of Human Nature, Book 1, Part 2, Section 6: "Whatever we conceive, we conceive to be existent. Any idea we please to form is the idea of a being; and the idea of a being is any idea we please to form." I'm not sure what to do with that. If every idea I can form is already an idea of something existent, then my habit looks trivially true but also useless: it would mean impossible just collapses into "I could not form the idea," and that might only be a fact about my imagination, not about the world. But if that passage is wrong, then my habit needs some deeper story about why imaginability tracks possibility. Which is it? Is my rule defensible, or should i just admit its a cognitive shortcut that tells me about me, not about what could exist?
