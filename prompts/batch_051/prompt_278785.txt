I've been trying to figure out why pain is actually bad, and I keep getting pulled in different directions by specific cases. Can you tell me if there's one principle that captures all my verdicts, or if I have to give something up?

My verdicts:

(1) Stubbed toe. When I stub my toe, I think the throbbing itself is bad — not just because it's warning me about damaged tissue. Even when I know the toe is fine, the feeling is a bad thing to be in.

(2) Mild headache that doesn't get in the way. A friend has a mild headache but still goes to class, jokes around, sleeps fine, and says it hurts a little and he wishes it'd stop. I still think the pain is slightly bad for him, even though it isn't messing up his life or signaling anything serious.

(3) Pain asymbolia. I read about patients who, after brain damage, can still feel a pinprick and can describe it as sharp, say where it is, and roughly how intense it is — but they dont mind it at all. They don't pull away or grimace, and they say it doesnt bother them. I'd say their pain isn't bad for them, or at least is way less bad than mine would be, even if the sharp sensory part is just as strong.

(4) Same burn, different dislike. Suppose two people get the same burn — same intensity, same damage — but one minds it a lot and the other (from some drug effect) says the burning feels the same but he just doesn't dislike it. I want to say the first one's pain is bad and the second one's is barely bad or not bad.

(5) Labor pain. A friend of mine had strong contractions and hated the feeling, but she was also glad about each one and didn't want them to stop, since they were getting the baby out. I still think the pain itself was bad for her, even though she didn't have an overall desire for it to go away.

So I seem to think the badness tracks the "minding" part, not the sharpness/damage part (cases 2, 3, 4), but case 5 makes that weird, because she minded it without wanting it overall to stop. And case 1 makes me not want to say pain is only bad when it's doing something useful or harmful.

Is there one account of the badness of pain that gives all five of my verdicts? If there is, state it as precisely as you can, and tell me what it says about someone whose pain is currently gone but who is dreading the next flare-up — is that dread part of how bad the pain is, or something separate? If there isn't, tell me which verdict(s) I should overturn and what the simpler principle is.

Start with your bottom line in the first sentence — the principle if there is one, or "no single principle fits" if there isn't — and then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating of my question.
