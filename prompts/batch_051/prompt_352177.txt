Ok here's the setup. Two people in my reading group have been going at it for the better part of a year about why promises bind at all. One of them—call him the assurance guy—thinks promising is basically about deliberately getting another person to form an expectation, and the wrong of breaking a promise is the wrong of betraying that invited assurance. The other—call her the rights person—thinks a promise is a small exercise of normative power: it hands the promisee a right to your future action, so the wrong is a violation of that right even if they never relied on you and lost nothing.

They've agreed to stop rehashing the same arguement and do an adversarial collaboration instead, and I want you to draft the document they'd end up with. Please structure it roughly as: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign—make these substantive, not just 'promises exist' or 'people make promises'; (2) a section or table stating the core disagreement in neutral wording, with each side's view phrased so that side would say yes, that's exactly it; (3) a section on cruxes—the sub-questions such that answering one would move at least one side—and for each crux a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, plus what each side would predict about that case. Try to seperate real disagreements from merely verbal ones. If one of them uses harm to mean felt loss and the other means violation of a right, flag it and write around it. Don't inflate small differences into big ones and don't paper over the real gap.

I suspect one crux is whether the promisee's actual reliance or expectation is necessary for the wrong at all, and another is what happens to the force of a promise when the promisee dies before the day of performance—use those if they hold up, drop them if they're only verbal.

Make the last line of your answer exactly \answer{<one sentence summarizing the deepest remaining crux>}.
