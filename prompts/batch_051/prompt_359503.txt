I'm taking a philosophy course on the family, and our seminar has been arguing for weeks about what adult children owe their parents. I've somehow ended up as the person who has to draft the document for an adverserial collaboration between the two camps, and I'd like your help. One side — call it the debt camp — thinks parents confer decades of unchosen benefits, and that adult children therefore accrue a debt: a defeasible but real obligation of care, material support, and a kind of ongoing deference, roughly proportional to the sacrifices made. The other side — the friendship model, which I think goes back to Jane English — denies that the relationship works like a ledger at all. On that view, whatever grown children owe their parents is more like what friends owe each other: it's grounded in the quality of the relationship that exists now, in mutual affection and present need, not in a history of past benefits. The problem is less that the two sides disagree than that they talk past each other, and our tutor wants something constructive instead of another standoff.

Please write the document the two camps should have produced together. It should have: (1) a numbered list of substantive claims both sides can sign without hedging; (2) a disagreements section, maybe a table, giving each live disagreement in wording both sides would accept as a fair, not slanted statement of their own view; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. I suspect one crux is wether an unchosen benefit can generate a repayment obligation at all, and another is whether a long but emotionally distant parent-child relationship should count the same as a warm one in determining what a child owes. For each crux, give a concrete case both camps would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts about it. The goal is a document both sides could genuinely put their names to, one that makes the residual disagreement exact rather than making it sound as if they'd just been miscommunicating. And please write the whole thing in British English.
