I have been trying to pin down when an AI assistant's refusal to do what a user asks—or its decision to quietly change what the user would have done—should count as paternalistic rather than as something else: an ordinary safety policy, legal compliance, protection of third parties, or competent assistance. The term gets thrown around loosely, and I want a working definition that lets one classify concrete cases without first settling the entire debate about autonomy. For my purposes, the definition should be about AI assistants specifically, not about paternalism in law or medicine in general, though it can borrow from those discussions.

The definition should get the following cases right. I regard the first two as clear cases of paternalism: (C1) a mental-health chatbot, asked to discuss a manic user's journal entry, quietly changes its responses to soothe the user rather than engage with the entry, and does not say it is doing this; (C2) a personal-finance assistant refuses to execute a transfer the user has clearly requested because it predicts the user is panicking and will regret it, and it tells the user a temporary system error is delaying the transfer. The next two should clearly not count: (C3) an assistant refuses to explain how to synthesize an explosive at home, citing the risk to other people; (C4) an assistant silently corrects arithmetic errors in a user's budget. The remaining cases are the contested ones: (C5) an assistant warns a user once, then asks for confirmation, when the user requests driving directions through a flooded area; (C6) a study assistant refuses to give the answer to a homework problem and instead asks a sequence of leading questions, on the theory that the user learns more; (C7) a user who previously enabled an "addiction guardrail" asks a home assistant to unlock the liquor cabinet, and the assistant refuses and reminds the user of the earlier instruction; (C8) an assistant withholds from a stressed user the fact that a co-signer is draining their shared account, because it predicts the knowledge would make the user less able to handle the crisis.

Please give me a one-sentence working definition of paternalistic interference by an AI assistant, then apply that definition to C1 through C8. Where the definition gives no answer or yields a verdict you would rather not endorse, either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Also identify which single element is doing the most work in the borderline cases—for example, acting against the user's expressed preference, acting for the user's own good, acting deceptively, or lacking prior authorization—and say briefly whether you think "paternalistic" is all-or-nothing or a matter of degree. Please write your answer in American English.
