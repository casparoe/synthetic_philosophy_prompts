There's a pattern I keep seeing in arguments about coincidences, and I'd like to know if it has a name — and, more than a label, I'd like the two sides carved apart, because it feels like both sides are making the same move against eachother.

Someone reports a ridiculous coincidence: on a trip abroad they wander into an out-of-the-way café and find their childhood neighbor at the next table. A skeptic says: with billions of people having millions of encounters, some wild coincidences are bound to happen; the birthday paradox shows the "impossible" event isn't so impressive. The reporter answers: no, the birthday paradox is about *some* pair in the room sharing a birthday, which is indeed likely; but the chance that *this particular* pair shares a birthday is still tiny. Likewise, the fact that many rare things happen every day does nothing to make *this* rare thing likelier. So the skeptic has swapped the probability of *this* event for the probability of *some* event.

Then the skeptic says: on the contrary, you've drawn the target around the arrow after it landed. Any of a thousand different coincidences would have felt just as eerie — the same childhood friend in the same café, or the same song playing at that moment, or a stranger wearing your late father's rare name — so you're not really talking about a pre-specified event either; you've secretly got a "some event" dressed up as a "this event." Now each side accuses the other of the same swap.

Is there an established term for this standoff, beyond plain birthday-paradox and hindsight-bias talk? What exactly is each side doing, and is there a way to tell, in a given case, whether someone is specifying after the fact? If there's no name, coin one and say where its boundaries are.
