I'm drafting a one-year dissertation fellowship proposal (roughly 1,500 words) and want help turning a half-formed idea into a defensible research plan. My background is in epistemology, and I keep returning to the worry that as people offload more reasoning, writing, and problem-solving to AI systems, they stop understanding the domains they work in. My suspicion is that the existing discussion is stuck because it tends to treat "understanding" as one thing that gets eroded all at once. I want the proposal to argue instead that at least three capacities need to be separated: the capacity to carry out the relevant intellectual task oneself, the capacity to explain why a result is correct or how it hangs together with the rest of the field, and the capacity to judge when the model's output should be trusted, doubted, or independently checked. The question I want funded is roughly: which of these capacities, if any, is eroded by reliance on AI, and does the erosion of one of them explain what is distinctively troubling about AI dependence as opposed to dependence on calculators, printed references, or a colleague's expertise?

Please turn this into a proposal with: (i) a precise statement of the question; (ii) an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open—without a bibliography or literature review, since I will add the citations myself; (iii) a method section saying what I would actually do, presumably by working the three distinctions through a stable set of cases, and why this conceptual work is the right approach rather than an empirical study of how people actually use these tools; (iv) a statement of what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and (v) a realistic assessment of the scope for a one-year fellowship. The backbone should be argument and distinctions, not references.
