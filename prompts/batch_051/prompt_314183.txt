I'm helping my lab put together a classroom demo for a cognitive psychology section I'm TA-ing, and I've somehow become the person who has to design the one example that makes prototype theory and exemplar theory actually come apart. The problem is that every case I try, they end up agreeing, or the difference is so subtle its not obvious whether it's a difference in the theories or just in how you describe the theories. So I want you to build me the case.

Here are the specs. I need a single, ordinary-life scenario in which a person has to judge whether a particular thing belongs to a familiar everyday category—furniture, clothing, pets, hobbies, food, whatever, not a scientific kind with a hidden essence, and not "game." The category should be one normal people use without consulting a definition. In the scenario, prototype theory (the view that a concept is a summary representation, like an averaged or idealized best example of the category) and exemplar theory (the view that a concept is a store of specific encountered instances, and you classify new things by similarity to those stored instances) should, when you actually run the predictions, end up on opposite sides of the membership judgment. I don't want "one is a little more confident"—I want the verdicts to flip. Then, for the same case, tell me what a theory-theory person would care about and roughly where they'd land, even if their answer is "it depends."

After the scenario, do the check for me: state the item, the category, and the relevant dimensions of similarity; walk through why the prototype theorist says yes/no and why the exemplar theorist says the opposite, making clear which stored instances are doing the work. Then identify the one structural feature of the case that makes them peel apart—high within-category variability, a prototype no actual member resembles, context-dependence, something else—and say what would happen if you removed or weakened that feature: does the disagreement collapse back into a case where everyone agrees? Finish by telling me how a defender of whichever theory comes out looking wrong would attack the case, and whether their attack is any good.
