ok so I'm in a Nietzsche seminar this term and I've been trying to get my own view on the eternal recurrence into some kind of shape before my paper's due, and I'd rather someone poke at it now than have my TA do it in the margins. Here's where I've landed, roughly:

(1) I take the eternal recurrence as a practical thought experiment, not a cosmology. I don't need to believe time literally repeats for it to matter, and I'm not sure Nietzsche did either.

(2) The demon scene works as a test of how you relate to your own life: could you say yes to having the exact same life back, down to the smallest detail, an infinite number of times.

(3) Affirming it means willing the whole package back — the pains, the wasted years, the regrets, the boring Tuesdays — not just the highlights.

(4) I basically think amor fati and the recurrence are the same demand, just put in positive and negative/test form.

(5) But I also think the test loses its teeth entirely if it's treated as merely hypothetical. It has to be anchored in a live possibility about how the world actualy is; otherwise affirming it is just affirming a scene in a book.

(6) And I do think you can affirm the recurrence while still wishing some specific things had gone differently, as long as you'd still take the whole life back overall.

Can you audit this for consistency? I want to know which of these actually pull against each other, ranked by how serious the tension is, and for each one what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Also point out anything that looks like it's in tension at first glance but isn't once you spell it out — I'd rather not gut a view I mostly like over a fake problem.
