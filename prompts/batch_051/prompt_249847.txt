I have been turning over an argument about the Daoist idea of wu-wei, and I would like you to check it step by step. I am not trained in philosophy, so please be patient with the terms, but I want a real verdict, not a softening.

In the chapter Da Sheng, usually translated The Full Understanding of Life, of the Zhuangzi, there are two stories that I think describe wu-wei. One concerns a man swimming in turbulent water. When he is asked how he manages it, he answers, roughly, that he grew up by the water and no longer does anything of his own; he follows the water's own movements. The other concerns a woodcarver named Qing, who says that before making a bell stand he fasts for seven days. In the first days he puts aside thoughts of praise and reward; then he puts aside thoughts of his own body and skill; only then does he enter the forest and begin. I am paraphrasing the stories, but I take both to describe the same state: acting without forcing, without self-conscious control, and without aiming at a separate result.

Here is the argument that worries me.

(1) Wu-wei, as the Zhuangzi presents it, is a way of acting that excludes deliberate effort, force, self-conscious control, and goal-directed striving.

(2) If someone adopts a practice in order to reach a state of acting, then they are deliberately trying to reach that state.

(3) The practices in the two stories — following the water's own movements, fasting until reward and body are forgotten — are practices adopted in order to reach wu-wei.

(4) Therefore anyone who adopts these practices is deliberately trying to reach wu-wei.

(5) Deliberately trying to reach wu-wei is itself an instance of deliberate, goal-directed striving, which is exactly what wu-wei excludes.

(6) Therefore these practices cannot produce wu-wei.

(7) So wu-wei can occur only by accident, in which case the Zhuangzi is not really offering a usable practice, or the idea is self-contradictory.

I keep going back and forth. Sometimes I think step (5) is an equivocation on “trying”: there is a difference between forcing a result and cultivating the conditions under which a result appears, the way one cannot force sleep but can do things that make sleep possible. Other times I think that defense is just subtracting the paradox by changing the meaning of “trying,” and that the original Zhuangzi really does ask us to do something impossible. Tell me whether the argument is valid, and if it is not, identify the exact step or steps that fail. For any term that is doing hidden work, give the readings under which the argument goes through and the readings under which it fails. If the whole question is posed in a confused way, say that plainly instead of pretending there is a clean answer.
