I have been following, from a distance, the dispute among geologists over whether the Anthropocene should become a formal unit of the Geological Time Scale, and I would like you to work through a puzzle it raises. The standard way of fixing a boundary between geological epochs is to choose a single physical reference point — a Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point, colloquially a golden spike — in a particular rock, sediment, or ice core where a diagnostic signal first shows up; once ratified, the boundary is treated as an objective feature of the stratigraphic record, recoverable wherever that signal can be correlated around the world. A working group spent years building a case for an Anthropocene epoch beginning in the mid-twentieth century, on the strength of markers such as plutonium-239 fallout from nuclear weapons tests, fly ash, and novel plastics, and nominated a candidate lake-sediment core as the reference section. The proposal was then voted down by the subcommission that reviews Quaternary boundaries, one arm of the International Commission on Stratigraphy, which oversees the official time scale. The dissent cut both ways: some objected that the mid-century signals are too thin or too deliberately chosen to count as a normal boundary, while others argued that the Anthropocene is better treated as an ongoing event whose onset unfolds at different times in different places than as a formal epoch with a single fixed start.

That is the practice I want to understand. In its ordinary self-description, golden-spike ratification looks like an exercise in discovery: the marker is supposed to register a real, globally recognisable shift in the Earth system, not to create one. Yet in the Anthropocene case the candidate signal was selected partly because it coincides with the post-war acceleration of human population, industry, and consumption, and the boundary's existence and date are being settled by debate and vote in a way that looks like a decision rather than an observation. Please reconstruct as precisely as you can what theory of periodisation — and of what it is for a temporal boundary to be real — this practice presupposes. In particular, distinguish what the golden-spike procedure strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, and tell me what would have to change in the procedure if the units of the Geological Time Scale were instead treated as stipulated but useful carvings of a continuous record rather than discovered natural kinds.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
