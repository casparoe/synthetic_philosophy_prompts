Question: Explain how the no-false-lemmas condition is supposed to repair the justified-true-belief analysis of knowledge. Does the fake barn case show that this repair fails? Your answer should discuss the original Gettier-style cases alongside the fake barn case.

Student answer: The justified-true-belief analysis says that a person knows a proposition only if the person believes it, it is true, and the person is justified in believing it. Gettier showed that these three conditions are not sufficient, because a justified true belief can still be true only through luck. In the original coin case, Smith infers the true proposition that the man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket from the false belief that Jones will get the job. The natural repair is a no-false-lemmas condition: a person knows a proposition only if the justification for it does not rest on any false premise. This blocks the original Gettier cases.

The fake barn case does not show that this repair fails. In that case, Henry sees the one genuine barn in a region full of barn facades and forms the belief that the thing in front of him is a barn. His belief is true and justified in one sense, but intuitively he does not know. The no-false-lemmas condition still denies him knowledge, because his belief rests on the false background lemma that the surrounding environment is ordinary. Since that premise is false, the condition is violated. So the fake barn case is handled as well, and the justified-true-belief analysis supplemented by a no-false-lemmas condition remains a viable solution to the Gettier problem.
