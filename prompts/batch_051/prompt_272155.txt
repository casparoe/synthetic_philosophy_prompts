I keep going back and forth on Quinn's self-torturer puzzle — the dial from 0 to 1000 where each one-notch advance adds an imperceptible bit of pain, you get $10,000 per advance, and you can never turn it back down. I want to know exactly where, if anywhere, this argument goes wrong.

(1) For every n below 1000, the person prefers n+1 plus another $10,000 to staying at n, since the extra pain is too small to notice and the money is a real gain.
(2) So moving from n to n+1 is the rational choice at every point in the sequence.
(3) If every individual move from 0 to 1000 is rational, then the whole sequence of moves is rational — a chain of rational choices can't add up to an irrational one.
(4) But the person would never choose to go straight from 0 to 1000 in one jump, even for the full $10,000,000.
(5) So the person's preferences are intransitive: he prefers 1 to 0, 2 to 1, ..., 1000 to 999, yet prefers 0 to 1000.
(6) Intransitive preferences are irrational. So the puzzle seems to show either that the setup is incoherent or that rational choice is impossible for beings like us.

Each step sounds plausible on its own, but the conclusion feels like a slight of hand. Is the argument valid, and if not, exactly which step fails and what should it say instead?
