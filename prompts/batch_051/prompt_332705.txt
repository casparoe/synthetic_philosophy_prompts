Two members of my decision theory reading group got stuck on a hybrid of counterfactual mugging and XOR-style blackmail. In the scenario: Omega flips a fair coin. If heads, Omega gives you $10,000 if and only if it predicted that you would refuse a $100 demand in the tails branch. If tails, Omega does not merely ask for $100: it credibly threatens to reveal a secret costing you $10,000 unless you pay $100. Thus, in the tails branch, paying is causally dominant; but across both branches, refusing yields zero net (a $10,000 heads reward against a $10,000 tails loss) while paying yields a sure $100 loss.

Their back-and-forth was roughly this:

(1) A: After the coin lands tails, you should pay. The $10,000 secret cost is real and avoidable for only $100.

(2) B: That is too updateful. Before the coin flip you would want your policy to be refuse, because across both branches refusal breaks even while paying loses $100. You should stick with the policy you chose ex ante.

(3) A: But after tails, the heads branch is no longer a live possibility. Refusing gives up a certain $10,000 in this branch for a counterfactual reward that did not happen.

(4) B: That is the same move the causal agent makes in the original counterfactual mugging. The difference is that here the ex ante optimal policy is to refuse, so an updateless agent refuses even though the immediate branch looks bad.

(5) A: In the original counterfactual mugging, the tails branch only asks for $100; here refusing incurs an actual $10,000 loss. An agent can reasonably refuse a bare request but not a credible $10,000 threat.

(6) B: The $10,000 loss is just the mirror of the $10,000 heads reward. If you accept the upside of an updateless policy, you must accept the downside; otherwise you make yourself exploitable by any blackmailer who can reward you in the branch where you refuse.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then say which disputant is more right overall, and what, if anything, both are missing.
