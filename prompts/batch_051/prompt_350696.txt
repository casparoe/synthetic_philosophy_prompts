I write a small philosophy-and-environment blog and want to build a "which view of nature's value do you actually hold?" quiz. Please write the quiz itself, not a plan for one: nine questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, and an explicit scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions rather than self-descriptions.

The quiz should sort readers among six leaves: (1) instrumental value, on which nature matters only through what it contributes to human welfare, whether resources, ecosystem services, or recreation; (2) relational value, on which nature's value is constituted in human-nature relationships, practices, and place-attachment, and is not reducible to services; (3) existence value, on which nature matters because humans value the bare fact that it exists, even without use or direct relationship; (4) intrinsic wildness value, on which nature has value independent of human valuers, located in its independence from human design and control; (5) intrinsic ecological-integrity value, on which nature has value independent of human valuers, located in the functioning, diversity, and resilience of living systems; and (6) a pluralist leaf for readers who consistently combine valuer-independent judgments with relational or existence-value judgments.

Build the scoring key from the logical space rather than from the list of names. First lay out the space: (a) does nature's value require a human valuer? (b) if yes, is the value realized through use, through relationship and practice, or through the mere cognition that the thing exists? (c) if no, is the locus of value wildness, ecological functioning, or individual organisms? Then locate the six leaves in that space, note which combinations of answers are unoccupied, and for each say whether nobody is there because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

The questions must include the following cases, though you may add one or two if they sharpen separations: the last human on Earth deciding whether to dynamite the last untouched canyon; a former mine site restored to full ecological function but with no "wild" history; a non-native species that increases local species richness but displaces native communities; a virtual wilderness indistinguishable from the real thing in experience; a species that goes extinct without anyone noticing; a culturally sacred grove of negligible ecological distinction; and a mosquito species with no charisma and no known ecological role. No question should ask readers whether they believe nature has intrinsic value or which label they prefer; each should present the case concretely and ask what should be done, what counts as a loss, or which world is better.

Finally, flag any pair of leaves the key cannot separate cleanly, and say what additional case would separate them. The scoring key should be explicit enough that a reader's nine answers determine a leaf, with a "mixed" outcome only if the pattern genuinely cuts across the logical space.
