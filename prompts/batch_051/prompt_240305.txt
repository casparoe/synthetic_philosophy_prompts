So I'm a sophmore and I help run a small whitelisted Minecraft server, around 18 people, mostly friends from school plus a few from a Discord I'm in. We have three mods who can kick or ban, and we try to keep the whole server sort of cooperative — one shared town, some light roleplay, nobody griefing on purpose. The thing that keeps coming up, like once or twice a month now, is that players fight about how you can treat characters and mobs in-game. One person spends hours on a named axolotl and builds a little shrine for it, then someone else kills it because it was swimming in the way, and their whole defense is "it's just pixels so it can't matter." Other times it's about villagers: somebody built one of those trading halls where the villagers sit in 1x1 cells forever, and another player says that makes them feel gross and they don't want to play anymore. We keep ruling on these one at a time and the rulings feel random, so people get mad at the mods.

Could you write me a decision procedure we could actually reuse whenever somebody reports "mistreatment" of a mob or character? I want it as a checklist or flowchat in words that the three mods can run in about ten minutes before we talk to the reported player. It should say what questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops and says "no action," where it says "restore the mob or item and warn them," and where it kicks the decision up to the three mods because it's a values call rather than something a rule can settle. Please be honest about which steps are really judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and tell us what kinds of cases the procedure just can't handle. Then run it on three situations: (1) a player kills another player's named pet that the owner had repeatedly and publicly said they cared about; (2) somebody builds a standard villager trading hall / iron farm that locks lots of villagers in tiny cells but gives everyone cheap gear and is pretty normal on YouTube; (3) it's a single-player world, someone makes a "burn the village" video for fun and posts it in our Discord. I suspect the procedure will give an answer we won't like in at least one of those, and I want it anyway.

One more thing: when you pull in the philosophy here — like whether fictional characters can be harmed, whether an NPC's feelings are different from a player's feelings about a named pet, whether "it's just a game" works as a defense — please separate what people who actually study this mostly agree on from what's genuinely still contested, and don't present a contested view like it's the obvious consensus.
