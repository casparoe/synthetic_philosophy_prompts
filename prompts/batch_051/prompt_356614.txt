A popular science essay I read recently summarizes the state of the Anthropocene debate as follows: in 2023 the Anthropocene Working Group settled the matter by selecting Crawford Lake in Ontario as the golden spike, fixing the start of the new epoch around 1950 on the strength of a global plutonium spike from thermonuclear weapons tests preserved in the sediment. When geologists' governing bodies rejected the proposal in 2024, the essay insists, that was an institutional and political decision rather than a scientific one: the Anthropocene is real, its start date has been established on stratigraphic evidence, and the only open question is whether the geological bureaucracy will catch up. Since geologists are the discipline charged with defining periods of Earth's history, the essay concludes, the question of when the Anthropocene began is theirs to answer, not something for philosophers, politicians, or the public to decide. I accept that there is a measurable mid-century plutonium horizon, but I doubt the rest follows as cleanly as the article implies. Can you walk me through this claim by claim and tell me which parts are well supported, which conflate different questions, and whether a fairer summary would say the start date of the Anthropocene has been settled at all?
