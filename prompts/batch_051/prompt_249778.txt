I'm a retired physicist and I've recently gotten into philosophy, mostly thinking about what happens to ownership when the owner is gone — abandoned software, orphaned photos, that sort of thing. I have a bunch of gut verdicts about cases but I can't tell whether they hang together. Here they are.

1. A one-man software company from the 90s made a plotting tool I still use. The author died in 2005 with noone to leave it to and the company is long defunct. Someone posts the installer online for free. I think that's fine.

2. The same author also left an unpublished novel that nobody knew about. A publisher finds the manuscript, prints it, and keeps the royalties. That bothers me, though I can't fully say why — maybe because it feels like taking his voice rather than a tool.

3. A lab bought a $40,000 instrument whose control software checks a license server. The manufacturer goes under and the server goes dark. The lab cracks the software so the instrument keeps working. I think that's clearly fine; they paid for the instrument and noone is left to sell support.

4. A university archive holds negatives by an unknown 1930s photographer — nobody knows who took them or who the heirs are. The archive digitizes them and puts them online. Fine, even a bit admirable.

5. A fan rebuilds the server for an abandoned online game and charges $5 a month to cover costs and his time. That seems fine to me. But if a company bought the fan's server, jacked the price to $20, and ran ads, I'm genuinely unsure, even though the original studio is just as gone.

6. Someone takes the dead author's tool from case 1, renames it, and sells it commercially. That strikes me as wrong, even though I didn't mind the free copying in case 1.

Is there one principle that gets all six right? I suspect it has something to do with whether anyone is left to be wronged, plus something about taking over a person's creative voice, but cases 5 and 6 seem to cut against that in opposite ways. What principle would you propose, what does it say about a case I haven't listed, and which of my verdicts does it overturn? Also, tell me what would change your mind — which consideration or argument would flip your verdict, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
