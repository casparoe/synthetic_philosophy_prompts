In my philosophy club we keep coming back to the ticking time bomb scenario, and I've noticed that a lot of people want to end up in a position that doesn't fit any of the normal moral categories. Almost nobody says torture is fine. But they also say the interrogator should do it, that afterwards we shouldn't punish him, and that we're glad he did it — sometimes even that it showed real strength. So they're saying the act was wrong, but he should have done it, and he shouldn't be blamed, but maybe he should still carry some guilt about it. Normally "wrong" means don't do it and "right" means there's nothing to regret, but these people want a third category. Is there an established term for this kind of thing — an act that is wrong and yet should be done, and a person who is both doing wrong and doing the right thing by doing it? And how is that different, exactly, from just calling it the lesser evil, and from a genuine dilemma where every option is bad and you just pick one? I don't want just the label; I want to know the boundaries, like wether the leftover guilt is part of the category or a separate thing people tack on.
