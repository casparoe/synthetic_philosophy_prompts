I've come to accept something close to Nishida's pure experience and the Kyoto School's logic of place, and I want to know whether the view takes any real risks. As I understand it, pure experience is not a subject's private sensory intake but the immediate, undivided field from which the subject-object split is abstracted afterward; knower and known arise together inside it. The logic of place adds that every judgment has the shape of a particular being placed in a predicate-place, and that the ultimate place is absolute nothingness: not a further entity, but the self-negating field that allows beings and selves to appear at all. Reality, truth, and agency are therefore modes of this field rather than relations among substances that first exist independently and then make contact.

Tell me what would count against this position. I want concrete defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: which observations, arguments, intuitive cases, or formal problems should move me, and which would a committed defender honestly concede as costs rather than dismiss as misunderstandings? I am most likely to be convinced by evidence that the subject-object structure appears earlier in experience than the view allows, or by an argument that the position cannot formulate itself without presupposing the very distinction it aims to dissolve. I also want to know whether the view risks anything or has been shaped so that any apparent counterexample can be absorbed as arising inside the field it posits; if it is insulated that way, say whether that insulation is itself a reason to reject it. Keep the answer under 600 words.
