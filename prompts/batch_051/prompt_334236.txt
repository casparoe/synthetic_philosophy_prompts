I’ve been deep in Chapter V of Locke’s *Second Treatise of Government* while sketching a novel about an eighteenth-century woman who clears a patch of forest, dams a stream, and slowly begins to think of the land as hers. My protagonist’s whole arc rests on the labour theory of acquisition: that removing something from the common state nature left it in and mixing your labour with it creates a private right, as long as there is enough and as good left in common for others. The passage I keep underlining is where Locke writes:

“what fish any one catches in the ocean, that great and still remaining common of mankind; or what ambergrise any one takes up here, is by the labour that removes it out of that common state nature left it in, made his property, who takes that pains about it.”

I want to treat this as a live normative theory, not just a piece of period table-setting. So: what would honestly count against it? I don’t mean only the standard objections—I mean the full map of exposure. Which possible cases, empirical findings, or internal tensions would be decisive against the labour theory, and which would merely force me to qualify it? Is the proviso doing real work, or is it a face-saving clause that turns the whole thing into a tautology? Where would a committed Lockean concede the theory fails, and where would they say the objection rests on a misunderstanding? I’m especially interested in whether there are situations my novel can’t stage without the theory buckling. Please write the answer in British English.
