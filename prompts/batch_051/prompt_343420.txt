I just got back from a three hour argument with a friend about the equality-of-what debate — he was all in on capabilities, I kept saying resources — and we never settled the thing that's been bugging me since. Ordinary practice draws a line through people's brute-luck disadvantages that I can't actually defend. If your body stops making insulin, we'd call it an injustice if you couldn't get it. If you can't walk, we build ramps, cut curbs, fund wheelchairs, and treat a doorway without a ramp as discrimination. But if you're born unusually unattractive, or tone-deaf in a way that wrecks your social life, or you just can't read a room, we treat that as your personality or your problem — even though those traits probably set people back as much in welfare, employment and relationships as plenty of recognized disabilities. What justifies that line? Normal functioning? whether a doctor has named it? how much it costs to fix? whether a lobby exists to fight for it? If you had to redraw the line from scratch, which brute-luck disadvantages would society owe people compensation or accommodation for, and which ones is it fine to leave as your problem?
