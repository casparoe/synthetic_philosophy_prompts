In Marriage and Morals, Bertrand Russell argues that sexual jealousy is not an innate and inevitable passion but a product of a particular, property-based conception of marriage, and that a rational sexual ethic could greatly reduce its hold on people. Does this argument survive the later development of evolutionary psychology, which treats sexual jealousy as a cross-culturally recurrent and partly innately prepared mate-guarding response? I am interested in exactly which premise the evolutionary evidence bears on: the claim about jealousy’s origin, the claim about its variability, or the normative claim that we should work to reduce it. If Russell’s argument needs repair, give the strongest repaired version.
