I'm giving the commencement speech at a small liberal-arts college. The graduating class is, honestly, a weird bunch in the best way: one student spent a year building off-grid aquaponics systems, another gave up the internet for her entire junior year, a third tried to learn every instrument in the orchestra. The school's whole ethos is that you should lead your own life, and the students have internalized that hard. But a lot of the parents are pretty conventional and I know at least some of them are worried they just paid six figures for their kid to become unemployable. I want the speech to actually lean on Mill's experiments in living from On Liberty, not just name-drop him. But I also want to avoid the easy version where Mill sounds like he's saying every lifestyle is equally good or that no one can ever criticize someone else's choices. He clearly thinks experiments in living can fail, and that there's a line at harming others. Can you write about seven minutes of speech that makes the case for self-expression and nonconformity to the parents without turning into a lecture and without pretending Mill thought do whatever was the whole story?
