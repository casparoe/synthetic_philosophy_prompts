Ok so in my formal epistemology class we've been doing objective Bayesianism and the maximum entropy principle, and I have this idea I want checked before I put it in a paper. MaxEnt says: when you have no discriminating evidence, your prior should be the distribution with maximum entropy subject to whatever constraints you have, because it adds the least information beyond the data. The usual problem is that its parameterization-dependent. Take a coin with unknown bias θ ∈ [0,1]. With no constraints, MaxEnt gives a uniform prior over θ. Now write the exact same unknown bias as α = θ². With no constraints, MaxEnt gives uniform over α, which transforms back to a prior over θ proportional to 2θ, so it's tilted toward θ near 1. Same setup, same ignorance, two different 'rational priors,' depending on how you labeled the unknowns. (Log-odds makes it even more dramatic.)

Here's the idea I've been calling answer-space indifference. There is no rational prior over the world; there is only a rational prior over the answer-space you actually asked about. A prior isn't measuring ignorance about some parameter out there. Its measuring ignorance about the space of answers you set up before you looked. So the θ-vs-α mismatch isn't a bug, its the view working: θ-space and α-space are two different questions, with different neighborhoods of nearness and different symmetries, so of course the maximally ignorant prior over each one is different. Same physical thing, two different questions, two different rational ignorances.

But I can't tell whether this saves MaxEnt or just rebrands subjectivism. If I can make any prior come out 'rational' by choosing a reparameterization that makes it uniform, then the rule 'use MaxEnt over your answer-space' is only as constraining as 'pick a space where your favorite prior is flat,' which is no constraint at all. Unless there's some independent fact about which answer-spaces are genuine, and I don't have that part.

And give me a committed verdict: is this a genuine way to keep the rational-prior project alive, or is it subjective Bayesianism wearing objective clothes? Tell me the strongest objection and whether you think it is fatal or something the view can patch.
