Third meeting in a row where my philosophy of mind group lands on the color phi thing and somebody "wins" by calling the other account Orwellian or Stalinesque. In case it helps: a green dot flashes, then a red dot flashes a bit to the side, and people report seeing one dot glide across and turn red mid-path, before the red dot actually flashes. The Orwellian story, the way my friend uses it, says you had some earlier experience and then your brain went back and quietly rewrote the record, like memory editing. The Stalinesque story says the whole experience was held back and staged in advance, so the version you get was never preceded by a raw first draft. I can't tell whether these labels mark a real difference or just two descriptions of the same thing with the word "consciousness" moved around. Lay out the precise claims each label could be making — about when the editing happens, about what the subject actually experienced, about whether there is a fact of the matter — and say which readings are trivial, which are substantive, and which are just stylistic. Then tell me which readings would actually license using the label as a putdown in an argument, and which ones don't. I also keep seeing people write as if the whole Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction collapses once you ditch the Cartesian theater (the idea of a single place and time where the show of consciousness is performed). Seperate what's actually settled in that debate from what's still contested among people who work on it, and don't present the "it's just a theater artifact" line as consensus if it isn't.
