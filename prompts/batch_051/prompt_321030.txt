I've been trying to work out what I actually think about whether clicking "I agree" on a contract of adhesion is genuine agreement and not just something courts will enforce. Here are my gut verdicts on some cases, mostly off the top of my head:

- Installing a password manager: there's a checkbox saying I've read the 60-page terms, I tick it without reading. Fine – I take myself to have agreed.
- A streaming service emails "we've updated our terms; continuing to use the service after 30 days counts as acceptance." I keep using it. Not fine – it doesn't feel like I agreed to anything.
- Buying software in a shop: inside the sealed box it says opening the box means you accept the licence, with a full refund within 14 days if you don't. Fine, even though I never read the slip.
- A website footer says "by using this site you agree to our terms," and I've never opened them. Not fine.
- An app sign-up has the "I agree" box pre-ticked, so I just carry on. Not fine.
- The terms say the company can change them at any time by posting a notice on a page I'll never visit, and that posting alone counts as my agreement. Not fine, quite apart from whether it's enforceable.
- I actually read the terms and they're dreadful, but I click agree because the app is the only way to file my tax return before midnight. Fine – I did agree, even though I had no real choice.

Is there one principle of real agreement behind all this, or are their just a mess? Which of my verdicts does the cleanest principle force me to overturn, and why? And what would it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a dentist hands me a tablet at the start of an emergency appointment and says "just accept the treatment terms so we can treat you," and I tap agree without reading anything.
