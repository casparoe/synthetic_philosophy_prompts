I've been on the science-and-ideas beat for years, and there's this one running fight I keep getting assigned to that never moves, and I'd like your help seeing what its actually about.

For as long as I've covered paleontology, there's been a standoff over what killed the non-avian dinosaurs at the end of the Cretaceous, 66 million years ago. The thing is, the two camps seem to accept all the same evidence: there's a global iridium layer, shocked quartz, a huge crater at Chicxulub that's exactly the right age, and also the Deccan Traps flood basalts erupting in pulses around the same time. The impact camp says the volcanism is a side show — the dust, soot, and rapid cooling from the impact explain the abrupt extinctions, and adding volcanoes is an unnecessary complication. The volcanism camp says a single impact can't explain why the extinction was drawn out, or why freshwater species got through so much better than marine ones — they say the long volcanic eruptions explain the staggered, selective losses better. Both sides claim parsimony, and each thinks the other is telling a less simple story.

After talking to both sides I'm beginning to think the impasse isn't really about a missing fossil or a better date. It's more like they don't share a standard for what makes one explanation better than another — what counts as simple enough, whether you prefer a single tidy cause or a messier story that covers more of the data, maybe even what killing the dinosaurs means as an explanatory question. But I'm aware that my armchair diagnosis could be its own kind of bias.

So what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is there a genuine empirical dispute underneath it, or is the empirical part basically settled and the fight is over which explanatory virtues matter? And if I'm onto something, is that the sort of thing that could ever be moved, and by what?
