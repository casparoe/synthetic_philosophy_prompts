For a paper on the normative force of oaths, please write a dialogue between two philosophers of law who disagree about what the mythological case of gods swearing by the river Styx can teach us. One should defend the view that the Styx oath lays bare the real structure of oath-taking: an oath binds because it invokes an external moral or cosmic order with the standing to punish or condemn perjury, and without that kind of background appeal an oath is only a decorated promise. The other should defend the view that an oath's binding force is internal to the speech act and its conventions, that swearing by Styx adds solemnity rather than normative content, and that external-sanction accounts get the explanatory order backwards, since a person can recognize that perjury is a wrong even when no external punishment is expected. The dialogue should involve genuine back-and-forth: each character should press the other's strongest line, and should be willing to refine their position under pressure rather than merely score points. In particular, have them test both accounts against the Styx case to determine whether either can explain why oath-breaking is treated as more serious than ordinary promise-breaking.
