I want a working definition of what it is for one society to be more moral than another. Before the hard cases, check the definition against two easy ones: an otherwise similar society that has abolished slavery is more moral than one that still permits it, and a society with a more efficient postal service is not thereby morally better than one with a less efficient one. Then apply the definition to each of the following comparisons and give a clear verdict where you can:

(a) Society A has very low rates of violent and property crime, high participation in religious congregations, and strong norms of honesty and charity, but it imposes harsh, public punishments for apostasy and blasphemy. Society B is largely secular, has somewhat higher petty crime and thinner neighborhood ties, but imposes no penalty for religious dissent. Which is more moral?

(b) Society C has an extensive welfare state, near-universal tax compliance, and very low poverty, but almost no one volunteers or gives privately, and most civic associations have withered. Society D has significant poverty and a weaker safety net, but a dense web of mutual-aid associations, high volunteerism, and strong local solidarity. Which is more moral?

(c) Society E has made lying rare through intense communal surveillance and public shaming; people who break minor rules are publicly humiliated, sometimes to the point of suicide. Society F has more everyday dishonesty, but wrongdoing tends to be met with private reproach, restitution, and reconciliation rather than public disgrace. Which is more moral?

(d) Society G gives generously to international disaster relief and sustains a strong culture of charitable giving, yet tolerates entrenched homelessness and severe domestic inequality. Society H is less generous to foreigners, but has virtually eliminated homelessness and keeps its income distribution compressed. Which is more moral?

(e) In Society I, people mostly keep their promises and pay their taxes, but primarily from fear of detection and punishment. In Society J, measured compliance is somewhat lower, but most of the compliance that exists arises from uncoerced conscience and mutual expectation. Which is more moral?

(f) Society K is religious and displays very high trust among co-religionists but sharp suspicion of and discrimination against outsiders. Society L is secular and displays only moderate, lukewarm trust toward everyone, without strong in-group favoritism. Which is more moral?

I suspect the phrase 'more moral' runs together at least two different things: the moral quality of a society's prevailing rules and outcomes, and the moral quality of its members' underlying dispositions. If you agree, offer the two definitions, run each through comparisons (a) to (f), and say which comparisons come out differently under the two. Then tell me which of your verdicts is least secure, and where your preferred definition still gives a verdict you doubt.
