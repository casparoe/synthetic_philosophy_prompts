I'm drafting a short position statement for a workshop on behavioral welfare economics, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency audit of the view I intend to defend. I hold: (i) description invariance — a rational person's preferences should not shift between logically equivalent presentations of the same options, so a valuation that moves only because an outcome is described as a loss rather than a forgone gain is a framing effect with no normative weight; (ii) loss aversion is a bias — the prospect-theory pattern of weighting losses roughly twice as heavily as equal-sized gains is an error, not a welfare-relevant taste; (iii) some endowment effects are legitimate — when someone demands more to give up an item she already has than she would pay to acquire an identical one, this willingness-to-accept/willingness-to-pay gap often reflects genuine attachment to the particular item and should usually be respected in policy rather than corrected away; (iv) consumer sovereignty — competent adults' informed and uncoerced preferences are the final authority on their own good, and third parties should not replace them with a latent or reconstructed preference the person does not actually hold; (v) default effects are mistakes — if an employee stays in a retirement plan mainly because enrollment was the default, the participation is an artifact of inertia, and the mere fact of an assigned starting point is not itself a source of value; (vi) default-setting as nudge remains justified — policymakers may nevertheless set defaults toward the option most people would reflectively endorse, since this routes a known mistake toward the chooser's own long-term ends while preserving a low-cost opt-out.

Do these six commitments form a coherent package? Please go through the pairs that look as though they might be in tension, separate the ones that are genuinely in tension from the ones that merely appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. Then tell me which single revision would make the set most coherent, and why.
