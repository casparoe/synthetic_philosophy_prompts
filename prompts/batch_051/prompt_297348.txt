Over coffee yesterday, a friend of mine who runs a little rationalist reading group gave me his summary of a paper he calls the evidential cooperation in large worlds thing. As best I can reconstruct it: if the world is large enough, then even in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against someone you'll never interact with again, you should cooperate, because your deciding to cooperate is evidence that other people who reason the way you do will cooperate too — including, probably, the very person you got paired with. He described it as Newcomb's problem extended: one-boxing makes it more likely the predictor filled the box, and similarly, cooperating makes it more likely your counterpart is a cooperator. So, he concluded, once you accept evidential decision theory your forced to cooperate with any agent sufficiently similar to you, no communication needed, which means causal decision theory is just wrong as an account of realistic choice. He also waved his hands and said it's basically superrationality plus a bit of anthropic reasoning.

I know he tends to oversell, and I'd like to know how much of this is fair before repeating it. Could you go through his summary piece by piece and tell me what someone defending that view would accept, what they'd call a conflation or overstatement, and whether the final bit about causal decision theory being refuted actually follows from the parts that hold up?
