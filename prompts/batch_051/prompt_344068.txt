Suppose a consortium of major AI developers, worried about a race to the bottom in which competitive pressure leads each developer to skimp on safety testing before deployment, adopts the following mechanism. Any member that trains a model above a capability threshold must place a restraint bond equal to five percent of its declared training cost into a shared escrow. Deploying the model requires a clean audit by the consortium; deploying without an audit, or later being linked by an independent accident board to a defined class of safety incident, forfeits the bond. Forfeited bonds are distributed among the other members as compute credits restricted to safety research. The capability threshold is revised annually by majority vote of the members. Analyze how this mechanism would actually behave: what it incentivizes among developers, auditors, and members acting as threshold-setters; where it would be gamed first; whether it slows the race or merely shifts it into threshold manipulation, audit shopping, and disputes over incident definitions; and which failure modes could be fixed without gutting the mechanism.
