Before Tuesday's seminar on diachronic rationality I wanted to run my own position by someone, because I've never actually written it down as a set and I'd rather not get caught out. Here's what I think I think:

(1) Cyclically intransitive preferences are irrational, full stop.
(2) I teach the money pump argument as the standard proof of this: if I prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, a trader can sell me a sequence of trades that leaves me back at A minus some cash, and that's the demonstration of irrationality.
(3) But I also think an agent who sees the whole pump coming and refuses the first trade isn't being irrational in refusing — the sequential choices are fine; I just think the underlying cyclic preferences are still defective even if the pump never actually runs.
(4) I'm committed to resolute choice too: Ulysses tying himself to the mast is rational, so sticking to an earlier plan can be rational even when, at the later moment, your current preferences would tell you to deviate.
(5) And I think preferences change over time, and acting on a genuinely changed preference isn't automatically irrational.

I'd like an audit before I present: do these hang together? Which pairs are actually in tension, which ones only look like they are, and what's the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than just flagging everything.
