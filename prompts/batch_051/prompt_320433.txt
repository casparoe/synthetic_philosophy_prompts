Then give a residue report: which parts of Pylyshyn's argument map cleanly onto concerns about model identifiability or the underdetermination of representation by behavior, which parts have to be distorted, and which parts have no counterpart at all. Is Pylyshyn's criticism best understood as an early statement of what machine learning researchers now call an unidentifiability problem, or does it depend on assumptions about the architecture of the mind that the ML vocabulary cannot express?
