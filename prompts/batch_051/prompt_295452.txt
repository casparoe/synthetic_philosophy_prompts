I teach an upper-level undergraduate philosophy of science course, and I have a two-hour seminar next week on objective Bayesianism and the maximum entropy principle. My students already have a working grasp of subjective Bayesianism, conditionalization, and the idea that priors are degrees of belief, but they have not seen entropy outside thermodynamics. I want a teaching plan rather than the explanation itself. What has to be in place before the maximum entropy principle can be stated in a way that does not look like a free lunch? What single running example should carry the session, and why is it better than a six-sided die at making vivid that a MAXENT distribution is only minimally informative relative to the constraints one has chosen? What misconceptions should I expect — for instance, that MAXENT churns out a unique rational prior from no assumptions, that the entropy being maximized is the physical entropy of the system, that maximum entropy simply means the uniform distribution — and at which point in the sequence should each be headed off? End with one question or short exercise that would tell me whether students have understood why a sharply non-uniform MAXENT distribution can still count as maximally non-committal given its constraints. Say explicitly what I should leave out of a first pass at this level.
