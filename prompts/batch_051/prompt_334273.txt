I keep running into a friend who dismisses Pascal's wager by saying it is just a Pascal's mugging. In the mugging, the way I've seen it described, a stranger asks for your wallet and, when you refuse because the threat is not credible, keeps raising the threatened harm or promised reward without bound until the tiny chance of it happening makes the expected cost of refusing larger than the cost of handing over the money. My friend's argument is that the wager puts God in the mugger's role: a very small probability of an enormous payoff or punishment is made to do all the work, and because the mugger's logic is clearly not to be trusted, the wager is not to be trusted either. I want to know how far that analogy actually holds. Give me the correspondence explicitly — what in the wager plays the part of the mugger, the wallet, the escalating threat, the credibility discount, and the decision to comply — then the places where the two come apart, and say which of those differences matter for my friend's dismissive conclusion and which are beside the point. End with whether the dismissal survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the mugging and the wager rise and fall together for a deeper reason.
