I work on supply-chain compliance for a mid-sized apparel company, and in the course of revising our supplier code of conduct I have come up against something I cannot make philosophically coherent. Our code requires every factory to pay at least the local statutory minimum wage, to keep verifiable pay records, and to refrain from physical coercion, threats, and forced or under-recorded overtime. It deliberately says nothing about whether the wage covers a worker's basic needs, even though our own commissioned assessments show that in several of the countries where we source, the statutory minimum covers only a little over half of the cost of food, housing, and healthcare for a typical worker's household. When the code is questioned, our standard public response is that the manufacturing jobs we support pay more than the workers' next-best alternative—often irregular informal work or smallholder agriculture—and that the workers take the jobs freely; we describe the relationship as mutually beneficial and emphasize that we terminate any supplier that uses force or fraud. The asymmetry is what troubles me: a supplier paying the legal minimum to workers who still cannot meet their basic needs is treated as fully compliant, while a supplier using threats or physical force is dropped immediately under a zero-tolerance rule.

I would like to know what theory of exploitation this code presupposes. Does it, in effect, identify exploitation with coercion or with making someone worse off than they would otherwise have been, so that a voluntary and mutually beneficial exchange cannot be exploitative? Does the zero-tolerance stance on force commit us to a different theory of exploitation than the wage provisions do, or can the two be made consistent? And if the code were rewritten against a view on which a transaction can be both mutually beneficial and exploitative, or on which background injustice can undermine the consent of the poor to such bargains, what specific provisions would have to change? Distinguish, where possible, what the code strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it underdetermines the philosophy rather than choosing among rival views.
