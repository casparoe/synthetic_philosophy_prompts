I'm helping run an undergrad philosophy club, and we're doing a session on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence. Last meeting we got stuck because half the room thought the demon in The Gay Science aphorism 341 is telling us a literal cosmological truth, and the other half thought it's basically a stress-test for how you'd have to feel about your life to say yes to its endless exact repetition. Some students conflated it with reincarnation, which didnt help. I want to make a handout that sorts people by what they actually believe when they work through cases, not by which reading they think sounds sophisticated.

Please write me a "which reading of eternal recurrence do you actually hold?" quiz: eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete case or variant (the demon appears and you think he's lying; a physicist says the universe is cyclic in exactly the same sense; you're asked whether you'd live the same life again with no memory of previous cycles; someone says the thought gives them a reason to get their life together; someone says the thought is empty because life has already happened infinite times; etc.). Each question should have two or three answer options that push people apart. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions I want as leaves: the cosmological reading (it's a true description of time), the normative-transformative reading (it's a test of affirmation), the aesthetic reading (it's about loving the finite moment for what it is), the pragmatic or fictionalist reading (you act as if it were true because it's useful), and the deflationary or indifferent response (the thought has no grip). Flag any combinations of positions your instrument can't really separate, and note any unoccupied cells in the logical space where a coherent view might sit but nobody has defended it. The quiz should sort by what people say about cases, so no question should just ask "do you think the universe literally repeats?" in so many words.
