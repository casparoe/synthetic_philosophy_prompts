In his Science and Method, Henri Poincaré argues that mathematical creation does not consist in making new combinations, since anyone can make them and most are without interest; creation consists in the choice, made by a subliminal self, of the few combinations that are useful, a choice guided by a sense of mathematical beauty that the conscious mind cannot articulate. What are the most important implications of this account for how we should classify the outputs of a large language model that produces a proof technique unlike anything attested in its training corpus? In particular, does Poincaré's combination–selection distinction supply a principled way of holding both that the model's output is genuinely novel and that the system required no more than recombination plus an implicit evaluative filter? Or does it simply push the unexplained novelty into the origin of the evaluative filter, and if so, what kind of evidence could show that the filter is itself an artifact of the training distribution rather than a source of leaps beyond it?
