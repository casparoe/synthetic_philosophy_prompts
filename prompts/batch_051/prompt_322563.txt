A public-health research group has collected observational data on a job-training program and wants to estimate the causal effect of program participation on employment outcomes within Pearl's structural causal model (SCM) framework, using the do-calculus at the intervention level and, later, counterfactual queries at the top of the causal hierarchy. Depending on how far up the hierarchy the analysis goes, the framework requires or presupposes the following six commitments. Rank them from most difficult to satisfy in this setting (1) to least difficult (6), and justify each placement.

1. Variable specification. Treatment, outcome, and covariates can be defined precisely enough that each endogenous variable is a function of its direct causes and an unobserved noise (disturbance) variable, and that the chosen variables remain stable across individuals and time.

2. Graph correctness and acyclicity. The directed causal graph captures the true direct causal relationships among the modeled variables, with no missing or misoriented edges and no unmodeled feedback loops.

3. No silently ignored common causes. Every common cause of two or more modeled variables is either represented in the graph, possibly as a latent node, or ruled out on substantive grounds.

4. Modularity of mechanisms. An intervention that sets a variable to a particular value replaces only that variable's structural equation and leaves every other structural equation intact.

5. Surgical, well-defined interventions. The do-operator corresponds to a real, well-defined hypothetical manipulation of the variable, rather than a vague or composite change that would shift several mechanisms at once.

6. Functional-form determinism for counterfactuals. The same structural equations and noise values support any counterfactual queries the group later asks, which commits the model to particular functional forms and disturbance values, not merely to conditional-independence constraints.

Begin your response with your complete ranking in the first sentence, then explain each placement. Do not include any preamble or restatement of the question. If two commitments seem equally difficult, you may indicate a tie, but every commitment must be placed relative to the others.
