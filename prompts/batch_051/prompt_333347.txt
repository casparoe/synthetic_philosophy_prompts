I live on a small island and sit on the local trust that manages our conservation land. I'm not any kind of scientist, just someone who's been reading about this on my own for a couple years. Next month I'm arguing at a village meeting that we should do a small pilot release of lab-modified mosquitoes to get rid of the ones that carry dengue. The way I understand it, the mosquitoes are altered so that the next generation of our local mosquitoes mostly doesn't hatch, but the alteration is the kind that doesn't just disappear: it's built to push through the whole wild population on its own, so even a small release could end up taking over. After a few seasons our dengue mosquito could basically crash. The catch is that once we put them out there's no calling them back. Even if we stop all future releases, the thing is already out there in the wild.

Im proposing we do a limited release at the uninhabited north end, watch for two seasons, and then vote again on whether to keep going. My own view is that doing nothing is also a one-way door: dengue keeps killing people, and the spraying we already do is messing with the rest of the ecosystem anyway. The room will be mostly fishers and farmers who make their living off the lagoon and the bush, and they've seen outside experts bring in one thing to control another and watched it backfire. There's a retired biology teacher who's read up on the genetics and thinks a self-spreading modification is a line we shouldnt cross. And there's a nurse who's watched kids die of dengue and will ask why we'd wait when we have a tool now.

Give me my Q&A prep sheet for that room. I want the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come and roughly how much damage each will do, phrased the way these people would actually say them, not the way a scientist would.
