I am preparing a seminar paper for next month's meeting of a university philosophy of physics reading group, and I need a Q&A prep sheet.

My thesis is this: Newton's rotating-bucket experiment is routinely treated as a point for the substantivalist against the relationist, but this is a misreading. The experiment refutes only a deflationary relationism on which all motion is relative to matter. Every surviving relationism recovers the concave surface of the water by accepting some absolute standard of rotation: a primitive inertial frame, a primitive monadic "spin" property of bodies, or a non-dynamical global mass distribution. Once that standard is granted, relationism no longer enjoys the parsimony or ontological economy over substantivalism that it advertises; the bucket therefore cannot be cited on either side, and the real dispute must be re-run elsewhere (the hole argument, the status of unoccupied spacetime points, and what counts as a substance). I am not arguing that substantivalism is true; I am arguing that the bucket is a neutral datum.

The audience will include: a classical substantivalist who thinks points of space exist even when unoccupied; a sophisticated substantivalist who accepts Leibniz equivalence but still takes spacetime structure as primitive; two relationists, one Machian who thinks the global distribution of matter fixes inertial effects, and one moderate who is content to posit a primitive inertial frame without spacetime points; a general-relativity physicist who suspects the whole Newtonian debate is obsolete and will demand that any claim be translated into GR, where there is no global inertial frame; and a metaphysician who works on ontological commitment and will press on what "primitive," "substance," and "parsimony" pick out.

Please return the prep sheet as a JSON object with these fields:

- "thesis_restated": a one-sentence restatement of my thesis, in your own words.
- "objections": an array of six to nine objects, ordered from most to least pressing (roughly, likely times damaging). Each object must have exactly these fields:
  - "rank": integer, 1 for the first objection.
  - "from_whom": which audience faction raises it.
  - "as_asked": the objection phrased as that person would actually put it in question period, two to four sentences, in their vocabulary.
  - "targets": which step of my argument the objection attacks.
  - "likely": "high", "medium", or "low".
  - "damage": "high", "medium", or "low".
  - "concede": what I should grant on the spot.
  - "hold": what I must not grant, and why.
  - "two_minute_reply": my reply, written out, keeping to about two minutes of speech.
  - "weak_point": the place where this reply is softest and most likely to be probed next.
- "question_hoping_nobody_asks": an object with fields:
  - "question": the question I am hoping the audience does not ask, phrased as it would be asked.
  - "why_hoping": a brief explanation of why it is dangerous for my position.
  - "best_available_reply": the least-bad answer I could give if it comes up.
- "overall_assessment": one paragraph saying which objections I can survive, which could sink the thesis, and what revision to have ready if the room turns against the "neutral datum" claim.

Keep the replies in terms of the physics and metaphysics of the bucket, not general advice about public speaking.
