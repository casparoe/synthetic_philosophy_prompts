I'm a junior in an undergraduate course on Enlightenment ideas of self-improvement, and our upcoming class session is built around a recorded mock interview with Benjamin Franklin, with me as the moderator. The focus is the plan for moral perfection he lays out in his Autobiography: the thirteen virtues, the little book with its grid of days and faults, the practice of concentrating on one virtue each week while still tracking the others, and his later admission that the project never reached its goal and that order in particular defeated him. I need to write the interview questions, and I want them to go past simply having him restate the system. I would like at least one question each on: whether a daily ledger of faults is best understood as forming real virtues or as training a kind of anxious self-surveillance; whether moral perfection is a coherent aim for a person who reports being surprised at how full of faults he was once he started recording them, and whose method seems to assume the lapses will keep coming; what philosophical weight to give to the fact that humility entered the list only after a Quaker friend told him he seemed proud; and how the ideal of pursuing virtue for its own sake sits with his frank remarks about the business and reputational advantages the project brought him.

For each question I would also like a follow-up keyed to what Franklin would most plausibly say in reply. For instance, if he grants that the project did only partial good and that he became attached to the mere keeping of the book, press him on whether that is a reason to lower the standard for moral self-cultivation or a sign that the standard was always the wrong kind. Eight to ten questions would be the right length, and I would rather they be sharp and anchored in the text than merely provoking.
