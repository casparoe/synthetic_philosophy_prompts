In my free will seminar we have been discussing whether the ordinary, pre-theoretical concept of free will is compatibilist or incompatibilist. One classmate treats this as a testable claim rather than an armchair dispute: she predicts that once ordinary people are convinced that an agent's action was fully determined by prior causes, they will withdraw both the attribution of freedom and the attribution of moral responsibility. Others report the opposite intuition. I want the strongest concrete test of her claim. Specify the scenario or manipulation you would use, the pattern of results that would favor each side, and which outcomes would be decisive versus merely suggestive. Then say precisely what the test would and would not settle — in particular, whether a compatibilist folk pattern would undermine incompatibilism as a philosophical thesis or merely defeat a descriptive claim about ordinary concepts.
