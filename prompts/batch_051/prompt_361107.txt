I have been stuck inside an argument that seems to prove that no oath ever binds, and I cannot identify the step to give up.

As Greek myth tells it, the gods themselves swear by the waters of the Styx, and the oath has real force against them: a god who swears falsely incurs a penalty from which not even the god who oversees the sanction is exempt. The oath is treated as binding on the strongest of them.

Here is the argument:

(1) Suppose an agent swears an oath to do some act A. Before the oath, either she already had sufficient reason to do A, or she did not.

(2) If she already had sufficient reason to do A, then the oath adds nothing that bears on whether she should do A; it is inert with respect to her conduct.

(3) If she lacked sufficient reason to do A, then the oath can add a reason to do A only by changing her situation — by creating or triggering some sanction, cost, or other consequence that would follow from breaking it.

(4) But a reason supplied by a sanction or an unwanted consequence is a reason of prudence, not a reason of fidelity. The oath would not make A obligatory; it would only make failing to do A costly.

(5) Therefore an oath either adds no reason at all or adds only a prudential reason, and there is no distinct wrong of oath-breaking.

(6) If (5) is true, then a god's oath by the Styx is either inert or merely a threat backed by penalty; yet the Styx oath is supposed to have a force deeper than a threat, and it binds even the god who enforces the penalty, which a bare threat cannot do.

(7) So the very idea of a binding oath is confused — a conclusion that contradicts our practice of treating perjury as a distinct wrong.

I want to keep (6), because giving it up would reduce the Styx oath, and our own oaths, to a kind of celestial cost-benefit calculation. But I am not sure the cost of giving up one of the earlier premises is any more bearable.

Lead with your bottom line — the step you reject — in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
