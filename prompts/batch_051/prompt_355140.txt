I find myself pulled toward a strongly structural picture of history: the large outcomes — which regions industrialize, which empires collapse and roughly when, the direction of major revolutions — are overdetermined by slow-moving causes such as geography, demography, institutions, and technological baselines. On this view, no single contingent event could have deflected the broad arc; if one message went astray or one battle went the other way, the underlying pressures would have found another outlet. The view has real virtues: it explains why historians so often find multiple independent causes converging on the same outcome, why counterfactual history tends to look like storytelling, and why some large-scale prediction is possible even though local events look chaotic. And yet I cannot shake the intuition behind the old proverb about the missing nail that costs first a shoe, then a horse, then a rider, then a battle, then a kingdom. At least some hinges were genuinely load-bearing. Had the nail been found, the battle would have gone differently, and with it a great deal that followed — not merely in detail, but in the shape of the next decades.

I want you to present the strongest version of each side, then offer a negotiated verdict. Should I debunk the intuition as a side effect of narrative compression and hindsight, something we impose because stories need hinges and because we can always trace a chain backward from any outcome? Or should the structural theory be amended to something like scale-sensitive chaos: macro-outcomes are robust within certain basins, but a rare class of events sits at genuine branching points, and the theory owes us a non-retrospective account of which events those are? Or is there a reading of the phrase 'history would have gone differently' on which both views are compatible — and if so, what question does that reading answer, and what does it quietly set aside? Tell me what it would cost to give up the intuition, what it would cost to give up the theory, and what a principled settlement would actually commit me to.
