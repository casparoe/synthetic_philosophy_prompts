I would like a consistency audit of a set of commitments I have formed about David Hume's argument against miracles in Section X of *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, since I am due to defend the position in a seminar and can no longer see where the strain actually is. I take the argument to depend heavily on this passage:

"Though experience be our only guide in reasoning concerning matters of fact; it must be acknowledged, that this guide is not altogether infallible, but in some cases is apt to lead us into errors. One, who in our climate, should expect better weather in any week of June than in one of December, would reason justly, and conformably to experience; but it is certain, that he may happen, in the event, to find himself mistaken."

Here are the commitments I hold:

(a) The epistemic force of testimony derives entirely from past experience of its reliability, so testimony is one species of probable reasoning about matters of fact.

(b) When two bodies of experience conflict, the rational response is to balance the opposing experiments and treat the stronger side as a proof or probability, subtracting the weaker side.

(c) A miracle is a violation of a law of nature, and a law of nature is established by a uniformity of past experience.

(d) The occurrence of a miracle is not in principle impossible: if I myself observed an event that satisfied the usual criteria for a genuine miracle, I ought to believe it had occurred.

(e) Testimonial evidence for a miracle could in principle warrant belief in the miracle; it is just that, given the historical record, no such testimonial evidence has yet done so.

(f) When testimonial evidence and uniform experience genuinely conflict, and the balance of evidence is close, the rational posture is to accept whichever outcome would itself be the less miraculous, since that is the outcome with the stronger background support.

I do not know which of these commitments is causing the trouble, and I do not have a preferred one to give up. I would like you to go through them and tell me which are in tension, which merely appear to be in tension, and how severe each real tension is. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the overall view coherent.
