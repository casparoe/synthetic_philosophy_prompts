In the Scholium to the Definitions in the Philosophical Natural Principles of Mathematics, Isaac Newton asks us to consider a bucket suspended by a cord and twisted until the cord is tight, then released. At first the bucket rotates while the water inside remains still and its surface stays flat. As the water gradually acquires the bucket’s motion, the surface becomes concave, with the water climbing the sides. If the bucket is then brought to rest while the water still rotates, the surface remains concave for a time. Newton took this experiment to show that the water’s true rotational motion cannot consist merely in its relative motion with respect to the bucket, because the relative motion of the water and the bucket is similar when the bucket first rotates and the water is at rest, and again when the water rotates and the bucket is at rest, yet the water’s surface is flat in the first case and concave in the second. He concluded that true rotational motion must be distinguished from merely apparent relative motion, and he located this distinction in absolute space, which exists independently of all bodies and all relations among bodies.

Please explain the conceptual disagreement between Newton’s substantivalist conclusion and a relationist view of spacetime, according to which what Newton called absolute space is not a real entity over and above the system of spatial relations among material things. In particular, try to articulate how a relationist could account for the concave surface of the water, and what residual difficulty the relationist may face in explaining why the concavity appears when it does.
