I'm on the board of a small community foundation in the town where I grew up, and next month we vote on whether to reappoint our treasurer, Elena, for another three-year term. By reputation Elena is completely incorruptible: she refuses even small gifts, returns anything that could look like a favor, files conflict-of-interest disclosures obsessively, and once reported her own nephew's grant application to the state ethics office. I like her, and I think the reputation is honestly earned. But over the past year that same quality has started to cost us. She killed a shared-services agreement with a bigger foundation because the other side's lawyer sent her a bottle of wine at Christmas, which she decided was an attempt to get on her good side. She also refused to approve a matching grant for the food bank because one of the food bank's donors was a company her husband once did tax work for — not a conflict under any rule, she just said it "would look bad." The food bank lost the match, and now two partner organizations wont sit in a room with us. The board is split: some think she's exactly what a small foundation needs, others think she's turned incorruptibility into a way of making every decision about her own purity. Please draft the short written memo I'll circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete decision — reappoint her, let her term lapse, or reappoint with a specific condition — and it should honestly engage the strongest argument for the side you don't recommend. I'm less interested in a lecture on what corruption is than in whether being impossible to corrupt is always an asset in an official, and if not, where the line is.
