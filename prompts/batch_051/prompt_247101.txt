Please give an example of a conceptual issue with the common claim that sufficiently long shared evidence washes out differences in priors. In your example, two Bayesian agents with different priors over the same hypothesis space should update by conditionalization on the same specified infinite sequence of observations. Their posterior probabilities for some fixed proposition should fail to converge to a common value, even in the limit, while their one-step-ahead predictive distributions for the next observation should converge. Identify which condition of the standard Bayesian merging-of-opinions results the example violates, and explain what this shows about the gap between predictive agreement and full convergence of posterior beliefs.
