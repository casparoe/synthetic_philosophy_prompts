I cover courts and data algorithms, and I'm putting together a piece on the defamation suits being brought against automated ratings and forecasts — landlord screening scores, a corruption-risk index that names small-town mayors, that sort of thing. The way I hear judges frame it, the opinion/fact line comes down to roughly these criteria: whether the statement has a precise and verifiable meaning, whether the language is loose or figurative, the context and genre, and whether the statement implies some hidden factual basis. Heres the case I cant get out of my head: a data-journalism outlet publishes a searchable 'slumlord score' for every landlord with more than five units, computed from housing-court filings and code violations, and one landlord gets a 94 out of 100 with the label 'likely to violate habitability standards.' He sues, saying it's a factual accusation dressed up as a number. Is the score protected expression of opinion under the criteria? Go criterion by criterion, say honestly where the criteria underdetermine the answer, and tell me whether this case shows the criteria themselves need updating. Aim for about 500 words.
