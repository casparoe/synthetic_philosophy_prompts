so we got ducks this spring (two hens and a drake) and my daughter keeps asking why the bird book says ducks lay eggs when our drake, who is a duck, obviously has never laid one. i want a villanelle about this, and i want the form to do the actual work. the two refrains should be the generic claim and the counterexample — something like "we say that ducks lay eggs" and "our drake has never laid one," adjusted however you need so the rhymes work. the refrains should keep taking turns through the whole poem, with the exception coming back and back and never quite managing to kill the generalization, and then in the final two lines they should finally stand side by side, because a generic and an exception arent actually a contradiction: ducks lay eggs is not a universal claim, and the poem should make you feel that rather than explain it. make it accurate enough that a philosopher of language would know what you're up to, but it should still be a real poem, not a nursery rhyme.
