After a long argument with a friend about whether a honeybee colony or a lichen is an organism, I realized we were applying different tests of organismality without ever saying so. Please write a decision tree that classifies views on biological individuality—specifically, whether holobionts, colonies, and superorganisms count as organisms—by the criterion a person actually applies in concrete cases rather than by the label they endorse. Build the tree from the logical space of criteria before locating positions in it: genetic homogeneity, reproductive bottleneck, physiological integration, immune self/non-self discrimination, and evolutionary individuality. At each node, phrase the criterion as a question about a case the respondent can answer without knowing the taxonomy: a lichen, a Portuguese man o' war or other siphonophore, a honeybee colony, a cow with its rumen microbiome, an aphid with its Buchnera, a salp chain, a slime mold. Locate the following positions as leaves where they fit: the physiological integration view, the evolutionary or Darwinian individual view, the genetic or developmental view, the holobiont view, and the graded, pragmatic, or no-unified-concept view; add unnamed leaves where a coherent combination has simply gone unoccupied. For each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and for each empty combination say whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any positions the tree cannot separate, and aim for roughly ten nodes and seven or eight leaves, written for advanced undergraduates.
