Consider the canonical Kelly-criterion setup. A gambler is offered an indefinitely repeated sequence of even-money bets on a coin. Each round she may stake any fraction \(f\) of her current bankroll, with \(0 \le f < 1\); if the coin lands heads she wins \(f\), and if it lands tails she loses \(f\). In the standard version the coin's heads-probability \(p\) is known to be \(3/5\), and the rule of staking \(f = 2p - 1 = 1/5\) each round maximizes the long-run growth rate of her bankroll—equivalently, the expected logarithm of wealth over a long horizon—the move that makes Kelly betting an instance of geometric rationality.

Now change one assumption: the gambler does not know \(p\). She knows only that \(p\) was drawn once, before the first toss, from the set \(\{1/2, 3/5, 4/5\}\), with each value equally likely, and that this same \(p\) governs every toss. She observes the outcome of every toss no matter how much she stakes. Her objective remains the maximization of expected log wealth, or geometric mean growth, over the long horizon.

Work through how the standard analysis changes. In particular:

(1) What fraction should she stake on the first toss, before any data?

(2) After a history of wins and losses, what is her posterior over the three possible values of \(p\), and is the correct stake in each round simply the posterior-mean Kelly fraction \(f = 2E[p \mid \text{history}] - 1\), or should she deviate from it?

(3) Compare this posterior-mean policy with two alternatives that practitioners sometimes defend: staking only half the posterior-mean Kelly fraction because \(p\) is estimated rather than known, and staking more than the posterior-mean Kelly fraction early in order to purchase information about \(p\). Which of these policies, if any, maximizes expected log wealth, and what tacit assumption is doing the work in the usual defenses of the alternatives?

(4) Does your answer change if the gambler faces a known finite number of rounds \(N\) rather than an indefinite sequence? If not, explain why the standard myopic property of Kelly betting survives the introduction of parameter uncertainty.

Finally, identify what would change your verdict: under which alternative assumption about the prior, the observability of outcomes, the gambler's utility, or the betting environment would one of the alternative policies become correct? Also state the point in your reasoning where you are least confident.
