In preparing a graduate seminar that pairs David Lewis's theory of convention with the doctrine of customary international law, I have been struck by a structural resemblance between the two. I cannot decide whether it is a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion, and I would like you to give me a precise assessment.

On Lewis's account, roughly: a convention is a regularity R in the behavior of a population in a recurrent coordination situation S, where (i) nearly everyone conforms to R; (ii) nearly everyone expects nearly everyone else to conform to R; (iii) each person prefers to conform to R conditional on the others conforming, because uniform conformity to R is a coordination equilibrium; (iv) these expectations and conditional preferences are common knowledge; and (v) there is an alternative regularity R' that would have coordinated them just as well had history selected it instead. The fifth condition is what makes the convention arbitrary: it is held in place not by reason or nature alone, but by precedent and mutual expectation.

Customary international law, in turn, is standardly analyzed into two elements: a general and consistent practice of states, and opinio juris — the belief that the practice is followed because a rule of law requires it, rather than from mere habit, courtesy, or convenience. Practice without opinio juris is said to be mere usage; opinio juris without practice is said to be aspiration.

The parallel I see is this: state practice looks like the behavioral regularity R, while opinio juris looks like the layer of mutual expectations and normative beliefs that turns a de facto convergence into a self-reinforcing norm. On both accounts, past conformity generates expectations; expectations provide reasons; reasons sustain future conformity; and the norm's content appears to be a historical accident rather than a uniquely correct solution.

Please assess this parallel in depth. In your response, do the following:
1. State the most charitable and precise mapping you can find between Lewis's elements and the two elements of customary international law.
2. Say what would have to be true of customary international law for central Lewisian results — about arbitrariness, common knowledge, how conventions change, and why they persist — to transfer to it.
3. Identify at least one thing the parallel seems to predict that is false of at least one side.

- 'verdict': one of 'deep structural identity', 'loose analogy', or 'confusion'.
- 'verdict_reason': a one-sentence justification of the verdict.
- 'mapping': a list of objects, each with 'lewis' and 'customary_law' fields naming paired elements.
- 'transfer_conditions': a list of strings, each naming a condition that would have to hold for a Lewisian result to transfer to customary international law.
