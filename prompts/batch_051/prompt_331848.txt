I run a small philosophy meetup at a bar, and for the last month the whole group has been stuck on one argument, so I got volunteered to draft a statement both camps might actually sign. Half the group—call them the analysts—says that when we argue about what "friend" means, the only honest job is to uncover the concept people already have: map the cases where people confidently say it, the cases where they hesitate, and the hidden patterns behind that. The other half—call them the engineers—says that is backwards, because concepts are tools, and a concept that lets someone claim eight hundred "friends" while feeling completely alone is a tool that is failing. They have proposed a cleaned-up concept, "friend*," that applies only when three conditions hold: both people have voluntarily let themselves be a little vulnerable to each other; both have said out loud, so to speak, that the friendship exists; and there is no standing power relationship between them like boss, landlord, or teacher. The engineers don't want to ban the ordinary word "friend"; they want friend* to be the concept we reach for when we are being careful about close relationships. The analysts say the engineers have stopped describing friendship and started inventing a new thing, and slapping an asterisk on it doesn't make it the same concept.

We have convinced both camps to try an adversarial collaboration, with me as the secretary. Could you draft the document they could both sign at the end? I want it to include: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides agree on, including what each side is not saying; (2) a disagreements table where each row states the exact point of dispute, and gives the analysts' formulation of their side and the engineers' formulation of their side, in words each camp would actually accept; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions that would actually move someone if they were settled; and (4) for each crux, at least one test or case that both sides agree counts as relevant evidence, with each side's prediction written down ahead of time. The finished text should be specific to the friend* fight, not a generic essay about whether definitions can be changed.
