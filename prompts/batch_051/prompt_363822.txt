I coordinate a volunteer search-and-rescue team, and I am trying to decide where to send my one available team before dark. The search area divides naturally into four equal cells: northwest, northeast, southwest, and southeast. We currently have no reason to favor any cell, so I assign each a probability of 25 percent. A ranger then radios in with one new piece of information: if the hiker is in the northern half of the area, the hiker is in the western half. One of my team members says this just rules out the northeast cell, so the three remaining cells should become equally likely, which would make a southern location twice as likely as a northern one. That seems wrong to me: the message says something about where the hiker is within the northern half, not about whether the hiker is in the northern half, so it should not shift probability between north and south. What should I believe after the message, and which cell should I search first? I would rather you commit to one answer, with your reasons, than survey the possible ways of handling the message.
