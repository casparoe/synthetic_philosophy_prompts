I'm the captain of my school's debate team, and on Tuesdays I judge novice practice rounds. My ballot decides whether the freshmen get moved up to JV, so I want to be fair. The thing I keep running into is people shouting "that's just an infinite regress!" and acting like the round is over. Half the time they cant even say why the regress is bad, and the other half they use it on stuff where I'm pretty sure the infinite chain doesn't matter. For example: one kid argued that if every rule needs a rule to interpret it, then you'd need a rule to interpret that rule and so on forever, so no rule ever actually applies. Another argued that if every event has a cause, you get an infinite chain of causes, so there must be a first cause. Another said you can always ask "what's your source for that source?" or "what justifies that reason?" about any reason, so nobody can be justified in believing anything. But then someone pointed out that the number line goes on forever and nobody thinks that's a problem.

So I need a reusable procedure, like a flowchart in words, that I can run in about five minutes between rounds while I'm also timing the speeches. It should tell me when an "infinite regress" objection is actually vicious and when it's just a rhetorical move. It has to be explainable to a freshman in two sentences, and the coach (a history teacher, not a philosophy person) has to be able to see why I voted the way I did just from what I write on the ballot. Don't give me citations or a reading list, I can't use them mid-round.

The procedure should have questions to ask in order, with what counts as a good enough answer to each, and it should say where the checklist stops giving a clear yes/no and a person just has to make a judgment call. It should also be honest about where the procedure will misfire and what it can't do. Then run it on the three examples above (rules interpreting rules, causes of events, reasons for reasons), plus make up one case where the procedure would force me to vote in a way I wouldnt want to.
