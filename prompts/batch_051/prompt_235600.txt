I keep a long list of places in my city that I have never visited, and I have developed a policy of not returning anywhere I have already been, because each visit to a familiar place feels like spending scarce free time on an experience I have already had rather than on a new one. A friend objects that this policy rests on a philosophical mistake: the mind is not a container that an experience fills once and then keeps hold of, and if I paid full attention, a single familiar street would keep disclosing things I had not noticed, so an experience is not the kind of thing that gets used up in the way my reasoning assumes. I find that thought appealing, but it still seems true that my time is finite and that most of the list will go unrealized, which makes returning anywhere a real cost. Is my friend right that my policy rests on a mistake? And if he is only partly right, does that still leave me with a solid reason to prefer new places over familiar ones?
