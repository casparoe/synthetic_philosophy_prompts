I'm a statistics PhD student, and as a side project I run a small betting portfolio on a couple sportsbooks and one prediction market. It's a few thousand dollars of my own money, no one signs off on it but me, and I place maybe 30-60 bets a week, so after a few months the compounding actually matters. I'm not trying to get rich, I just don't want to blow up my stipend while I take the bets my model says are good.

I keep running into Kelly betting and the geometric rationality stuff around it: for repeated multiplicative gambles you should optimize the expected log of bankroll rather than expected dollars, and Kelly stakes fall out of that. But the clean presentation always assumes I know the true probabilities, the bets are independant, I can stake any real number, and the odds don't move when I bet. None of that holds for me. My probabilities are model outputs with their own uncertainty, and sometimes my edge is just overfitting a small sample. Lots of my bets are correlated (same game, same team, same underlying news). The sportsbooks have integer stake limits and minimums, and on the prediction market my own order moves the price against me.

So could you turn Kelly and geometric rationality into an actual reusable Saturday-morning procedure? Given an estimated probability (and some sense of how unsure I am about it), the posted odds, and whatever I know about correlation with my other open bets, how do I get to a dollar amount? I need something I can run in about an hour for 30-40 bets, that tells me what to do when I can't get a clean number, and that flags which steps are really risk-tolerance judgements rather than math. If the procedure says treat highly correlated bets as one position and cap total exposure to a cluster, I want to know when that heuristic stops being safe. And I want it to say explicitly where it stops being Kelly and becomes a choice about how much drawdown I can stomach.

Then run it through three cases. (1) A single bet where my model says 60% and the book's price implies 50%, except that 60% comes from a small sample and has wide error bars. (2) Two bets that are basically the same event in different clothes, like team A wins and team A covers the spread, where my model shows an edge on both and naive Kelly would double-count the same risk. (3) A bet where my model says 72% against an implied 50% but I've only been running that model for three weeks, and full Kelly would have me stake almost 40% of my bankroll. That's the one I'm both scared of and tempted by; I suspect the right procedure tells me to take much less than Kelly, so show me why that's not just cowardice, and where fractional Kelly fits as more than a fudge factor.
