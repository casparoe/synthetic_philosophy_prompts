In my stand-up scene there's an endless argument about what 'stealing a joke' actually means, and the usual rules people cite (never take the exact wording, always credit the source) break on real cases pretty fast. I want a working definition that can handle the mess, not a legal standard. Here are the cases that keep tripping everyone up:

Case 1: A comic does another comic's whole joke word for word on TV, no credit, after hearing it once at an open mic. (Clearly theft, I'd say.)
Case 2: Two comics watch the same news story and independently write a joke with the same premise and a near-identical punchline. (Not theft, surely? But then what exactly is being copied?)
Case 3: A comic hears a joke with a weak punchline, keeps the setup, and writes a much stronger punchline, then performs it.
Case 4: A comic buys a joke from a ghostwriter and performs it without saying so.
Case 5: A comic retells a totally generic template joke, like a lightbulb joke, with a new target inserted.
Case 6: Someone at a party repeats a friend's joke and says up front that it's Sarah's.
Case 7: Two comics co-write a bit in a late-night riff session, each adding lines until it's thoroughly blended; one of them later performs it solo and gets a special from it.
Case 8: A comic changes the nouns but keeps the shape, the attitude, and the sequence of moves of a famous bit—so everyone recognizes the source, but not a single sentence matches.

Could you propose a definition (or a small set of criteria) for joke theft, run it through all eight, flag where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it or bite the bullet? Then tell me wich of your verdicts you're least confident about and why.
