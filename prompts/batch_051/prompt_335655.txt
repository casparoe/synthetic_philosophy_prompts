Here's a counterfactual I want you to reason through rather than hedge on. In 2020, a fictional common-law country of about five million people (don't worry about its actual statutes) passes a criminal code reform with two clauses. First, anyone who knows another person is in danger of dying and could prevent the death at trivial cost or risk to themselves must act; if they don't, courts are to punish them exactly as if they had killed the person. Second, for anyone with a pre-existing duty to act — parents, doctors, nurses, carers — the doing/allowing distinction is abolished outright: a doctor who withdraws life support at a patient's request is guilty of the same offense as one who injects a lethal drug, and a parent who lets a child starve is punished like one who kills the child outright. So the killing/letting-die line is gone both for easy rescues by strangers and for caring professionals. I dont want a survey of possible effects — I want you to commit.
