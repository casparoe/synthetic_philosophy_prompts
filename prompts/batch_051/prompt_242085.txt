ok so over winter break my cousin showed me conways game of life on his phone and I got kind of obsessed with it. The thing that messes with my head is the glider: a little shape that glides across the screen basically forever, but when you zoom in its just individual cells switching on and off according to four super simple rules. The glider isnt "in" any one cell, its just a higher level pattern. You could in principle describe the entire screen for a million steps by listing every cell state one by one and never once mention a glider, but somehow the glider is what actually matters if you want to understand whats going on. I saw someone talk about this idea that those higher-level patterns are real patterns, not illusions, partly because they let you predict things without simulating every single cell. They also said you can take three stances toward anything: the physical stance (just the underlying parts and rules), the design stance (what its for and how the parts are arranged so it works), and the intentional stance (what it believes or wants, treating it like a rational agent). I get the three stances for like a toaster vs a dog, but I dont get where they blur or when one stance is more "correct" than the others.

Could you write me a short story where the main character basically refuses to leave the physical stance, like they only ever see parts and rules and never patterns or purposes or minds. Maybe theyre the only person in their town who cant see the gliders everyone else sees in some giant game-of-life display, or maybe theyre a mechanic who insists a person is just atoms and a broken clock is just gears. I want the story to actually show what they miss by doing that, not just say its sad. scifi or realistic both fine.
