I spent most of my working life as an evolutionary biologist (selection gradients, wild populations) and have only recently started reading philosophy of biology properly. The adaptationism argument keeps bothering me because the two sides often seem to be arguing past each other, and the whole point is a document both sides would actualy sign. I want you to draft that document as an adversarial collaboration between two positions:

- a careful adaptationist, who holds that for traits showing complex fit with the environment, selection is the default explanation, and that constraint, drift, by-product, etc. need to be positively shown rather than just floated as alternatives;
- a Gould–Lewontin-style pluralist, who holds that constraints, allometry, developmental bias, and by-products are first-class explanations on equal footing, and that adaptationist stories have to earn there keep like any other hypothesis.

Structure the document as follows.

1. Agreed claims. Number them. They must be non-vacuous — not just "selection happens sometimes," but claims with teeth about how current utility relates to historical origin, what makes an adaptationist story tested rather than just plausible, what optimality models can and can't show, and what exaptation does and doesn't commit you to. Both sides should be able to sign each one without grimacing.

2. Disagreements table. Columns: the exact question; the adaptationist position in wording the adaptationist would own; the pluralist position in wording the pluralist would own; and a fourth column where you flag whether any part of the disagreement is verbal. I have a hunch the word "function" is doing a lot of sliding, but don't let my hunch bias you.

3. Cruxes. Pick four sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. For each, give (i) a concrete biological case, dataset, or experimental/comparative program that both sides would agree bears on it — something a working biologist could do or analyze, not a thought experiment; and (ii) each side's prediction written ahead of time. Candidate cruxes to use or improve: whether traits that show current fit also tend to show the signature of selection at their origin rather than co-option later; whether apparent fit can be produced by allometry plus drift more often than selection-first views assume; whether reverse engineering a trait and finding it near an optimum tests its origin or only its current maintenance; whether the standard practice of atomizing organisms into discrete traits biases the inference toward adaptation. You don't have to use these if you find better ones.

4. Self-falsification. For each side, state what would count as evidence against its own position. If one side can't name anything, say so and say what that does to the collaboration.

Keep the tone like a document for working scientists, not a journal review. No citations or reading lists; the case studies can be named in passing but the value is in the structure of the claims and the exactness of the residual disagreement.
