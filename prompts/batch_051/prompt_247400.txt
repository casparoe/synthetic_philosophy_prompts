Consider the thesis that deliberation crowds out prediction: while you are genuinely still deciding whether to take some action, you cannot also hold a stable, well-grounded probability for the proposition that you will take it, since deliberation treats your future action as something for you to settle, whereas prediction treats it as an external fact to be estimated. What are the most important implications of this thesis for someone who tries to use explicit probabilities to make a high-stakes personal decision, such as whether to end a relationship or leave a secure job for an uncertain venture, where the outcomes depend substantially on the person's own future choices and follow-through? In particular, how should such a person handle probability assignments about their own behavior that seem to act both as evidence about the world and as a cause of the very behavior being predicted?
