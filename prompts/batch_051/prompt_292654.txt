You are an economic historian who specializes in eighteenth-century political economy and in modern monetary institutions. Think carefully, and give me a committed verdict rather than a survey of possible readings.

I have been trying to figure out how much of Hume’s account of money and trade survives the transition to fiat money and floating exchange rates. In the essay _Of the Balance of Trade_, first published in 1752 and later collected in _Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary_, Hume gives what later economists called the price-specie-flow mechanism. He asks his reader to suppose that a large part of Britain’s money were suddenly destroyed. His argument is that domestic prices would then fall, British goods would become cheaper abroad, foreign goods would become dearer at home, and coin would flow back into Britain until its money stock was roughly restored to its appropriate level. Conversely, if a country accumulated too much money, its prices would rise, its exports would lose competitiveness, and the surplus money would drain away. The argument treats money as metallic coin, assumes that a country’s domestic price level moves with the quantity of metal money in circulation, and assumes a fixed metallic standard rather than a floating exchange rate.

Now consider a modern economy in which money consists of central-bank fiat currency and electronic reserves, the exchange rate floats, and the central bank targets inflation. When foreign money flows in or out, the central bank can sterilize the effect on the domestic monetary base, and the exchange rate can adjust without requiring domestic wages and prices to fall or rise. There is no metal that must flow across borders in the way Hume’s argument requires.

Does Hume’s automatic adjustment argument survive this change? Which premise is actually doing the original work, and is it the premise that fiat money and floating exchange rates undercut? If the argument is dead, say clearly what should replace it. If it can be repaired, state the repaired version precisely and specify the conditions under which it still holds. I want a definite, argued position, not a menu of options.
