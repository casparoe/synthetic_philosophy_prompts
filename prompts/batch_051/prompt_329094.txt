In putting together a graduate seminar on formal semantics and mental content, I keep returning to an analogy I cannot decide about, and I would be grateful for a careful analysis. In model theory, a formal language begins as an uninterpreted collection of well-formed strings: nothing refers, and sentences have no truth values until one supplies an interpretation, assigning a domain and mapping each non-logical symbol to an object, relation, or function. After that assignment, satisfaction, truth, and entailment are defined with full rigor. But the assignment itself is expressed in another symbolic medium, such as mathematical English, set-theoretic notation, or diagrams. If someone says the original language has now been interpreted, they apparently mean that its strings have been correlated with further symbols that the mathematician already understands. This reminded me of the symbol grounding problem as Harnad formulated it: a system that manipulates symbols according to syntactic rules does not thereby understand them; meaning cannot be inherited from symbols alone, and must ultimately bottom out in something non-symbolic, such as sensorimotor capacities, or else one gets a regress of interpreters. A Tarskian interpretation seems to be doing for a formal language precisely what Harnad says the environment must do for an agent: providing the external relatum that makes the symbols about something. Yet by Harnad's own standard, the Tarskian interpretation would look like more ungrounded symbols, so model-theoretic semantics would face the very regress it claims to avoid. And if that is wrong, it would have to be because interpretation in model theory and grounding in cognitive systems are two different explanatory projects.

Please evaluate the parallel precisely. Is it a genuine structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion of two senses of interpretation? State the mapping between the two cases as explicitly as you can, say what would have to be true of each side for a problem diagnosed on one side to transfer to the other, and identify at least one thing the analogy predicts that turns out to be false. Please write your answer in American English.
