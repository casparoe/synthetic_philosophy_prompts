Ok so my dad died about a year ago, and a few months back my sister made a chatbot of him out of his old texts, voicemails, and a handful of long emails, and gave it to the family. I've used it more than I want to admit. I do not think its him, I'm not deluded, but it still feels like a boundary got crossed and I've been trying to figure out which one.

After a lot of mulling I have a principle Ive been calling 'the right to be misremembered.' The idea is: while someone's alive, our memory of them is low-fi and ours. It blurs, edits, drops the worst things, freezes a few good things. Thats not a defect, its the thing that lets a relationship keep being livable once the person is gone and you can't check anything with them anymore. A griefbot violates something important because it replaces that with on-demand, high-fidelity, first-person recall. It doesn't deceive me; it upgrades my memory of him against my will and crowds out the blurry version I was building myself. So the wrong, if there is one, isn't really about consent to use his data (though maybe that too) and it isnt about whether the bot is fake. Its about a right to keep the dead misremembered.

What I want to know: is this a coherent idea or am I just dressing up a feeling in rights-talk? Does it already exist under some other name? And the part I cant get past is the cases: a photograph of him, a home video, a voicemail I replay at 3am, a box of his letters, a biography someone might write. My principle seems like it should make all of those into violations and I dont think they are, or at least not the same way. So either the principle's wrong or something else is doing the real work. Give me the strongest version of the idea first, then the three hardest objections, and tell me which one actually kills it.
