I'm putting together a one-page statement of my views on the value of culture — languages, traditions, art forms — for a reading group, and I want a consistency check before anyone else sees it. Without argument, here are the commitments as I currently hold them:

(a) The extinction of a language is a genuine loss to the world even if the last speakers abandon it willingly and no living person is made worse off by its disappearance.
(b) All value depends on valuers: there is no value from the standpoint of the universe, and a thing can be good or bad only in relation to some being capable of valuing it.
(c) Aesthetic value is objective, in at least this limited sense: some artworks and art forms are more valuable than others independently of how much anyone actually likes or uses them.
(d) Traditions have only conditional value: a practice deserves preservation only insofar as it serves the flourishing of the people who practice it; age, ancestry, or its being ours gives no additional reason to keep it going.
(e) It can be rational to spend scarce resources maintaining a dying art form that almost no one currently enjoys, on the grounds that future people might find it deeply valuable.

Audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look as if they are, and how severe is each genuine tension? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say what the cheapest repair would be for each and what it would cost, and identify the single revision that would do the most to make the set cohere as a whole. Then say how confident you are in the audit overall, and single out the point in your assessment you are least sure about. End with a machine-readable line of the form \answer{a-b > a-d} giving your ranking of the genuine tensions.
