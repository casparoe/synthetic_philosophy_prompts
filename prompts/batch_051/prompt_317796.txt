So Ive been arguing about God and goodness with my friend Dena for years now, and a while back I started writing down her rulings on little cases after each argument, since I could never quite figure out what her actual position is. She works at a pharmacy, didnt go to college, and thinks about this stuff on her own -- so she doesnt use any of the usual theory words, she just rules on cases.

Here is what Ive got so far.

- On kindness: she says kindness is good in itself. God doesnt make it good by commanding it; God loves it because it already is the sort of thing a good person would be drawn to. She was firm that the goodness is in the act, not in the command.

- On the food rules she keeps: she says this is completly different. There is nothing in the animal itself that makes eating it right or wrong; if God had never said anything, no one would have any guilt about it. The whole thing becomes a rule only because God picked it. So there the command seems to be what makes the rule.

- On cheating on a spouse: she says the act is wrong in itself, because of what it does to the other person and to your own word. God doesnt need to add the wrongness to it, and she got genuinely irritated when I asked whether God could make it okay by commanding it -- she said something like that is not who God is, and the question is a trick.

- On the idea that there is some moral list sitting above God that God has to check: she says no, thats too simple. But she also refuses to say God's commands are arbitrary. When I pushed, she said I was trying to make her pick between two bad pictures of God and she wouldnt.

So she seems to be running some third thing, but I honestly dont know what it is. The bit I cant pin down is this: she uses two different logics for two different kinds of case, and I dont know how she decides which logic a new case gets.

The new case I came up with to test her: there is no command involved at all -- not from scripture, not from her tradition, not even a private sense of God telling her. A man has a chance to give up an apartment he loves so that a stranger with a serious disability and nowhere else to go can move in; if he doesnt, the stranger will be much worse off, but if he does, he will have to move somewhere cramped and noisy. There is no rule she accepts that covers this exact situation, and nobody would call the man a liar or a cheater if he stays put. I asked her not whether it would be good -- I already know she would say yes -- but whether it is something he genuinely ought to do, or just the kind of thing that would be nice if he did it but is above and beyond.

She said she would have to think about it, and we havent picked it back up yet.

I want you to figure out, from the track record, what she is going to say and what position she has actually been running all these years. Please give me:

1. "The candidate positions" -- the two or three rules that fit her past verdicts, stated in plain English (about 250 words).
2. "What each candidate predicts" -- what each of those rules would have to say about the apartment case, including whether they give different answers or the same answer with different reasoning (about 300 words).
3. "Best guess and why" -- your prediction of what Dena will actually say, and which of the candidates best explains her past rulings taken as a whole (about 200 words).
4. "The tie-breaker case" -- one new case I could put to her that would finally force the remaining candidates apart if the apartment case doesnt, and why it would work (about 150 words).

Dont include an introduction or a conclusion beyond these four sections.
