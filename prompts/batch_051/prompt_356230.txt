I've been collecting a friend's verdicts on forgiveness and I want to predict how she'd rule on a new case. So far she's said: a sister forgiving her brother after he apologized for a decades-old betrayal was real forgiveness; a man who announced he'd forgiven his unrepentant, still-manipulative father but kept seething about it was just talking himself into moving on, not forgiving; only the person wronged can forgive (you can't forgive the guy who hurt your partner, though you might forgive him for how it affected you); and self-forgiveness is a real thing, not just therapy talk. The new case: a woman's former business partner embezzled from their firm. Since then he's repaid every penny, fixed the books, and over years acted genuinely ashamed, but he's never once apologized in so many words because verbal apologies make him clam up. She says the anger is gone and she wants to forgive him. How would my friend classify that, which principle is she actually running — forgiveness requires an explicit apology, forgiveness requires repentance shown in deeds, forgiveness is just the victim's genuinely giving up resentment, or something else — and name a further case that would finally seperate the rules.
