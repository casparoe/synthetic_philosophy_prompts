Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions on satisficing under bounded rationality. The first position, satisficing-as-autonomous, holds that satisficing is a distinct and legitimate standard of practical rationality: an agent who adopts an aspiration level and takes an option meeting it can be fully rational even when she knows a better option is available. The second position, satisficing-as-derived, holds that satisficing is rational only instrumentally, as a heuristic that approximates optimization under limits of time, attention, computation, and information, and that its normative force shrinks as those limits are relaxed. Write the document these two camps could jointly sign, in this order: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept about aspiration levels, search, and the descriptive prevalence of satisficing, stated so neither camp finds them vacuous or slanted; (2) a disagreement table with three or four residual disagreements, each given in neutral terms and then in wording each side would sign; (3) a list of no more than four cruxes, the specific sub-questions that would force at least one side to change its view; and (4) for each crux, a concrete choice situation or empirical finding both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Commit to definite formulations throughout: choose the cruxes rather than surveying candidates, and where you judge a disagreement to be partly verbal — for example, an ambiguity in what counts as rational — say so explicitly and identify which term is doing the work. The document should end with the disagreement made exact, not with a verdict.
