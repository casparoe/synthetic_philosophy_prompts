I have been defending a particular formulation of physicalism in a reading group, and I would like a candid map of what the view actually risks. The formulation is this: "physical" should not be defined by the ontology of current physics, since physics has revised its fundamental posits before and presumably will again; nor should it be defined through some a priori notion of the material or the mechanical, since that imports assumptions we have no independent reason to trust. Instead, "physical" should be defined by completed, ideal physics — whatever the true final scientific theory turns out to posit — and physicalism becomes the thesis that every fact, including every mental fact, is either among the facts posited by that theory or metaphysically necessitated by them. I adopted this formulation because it lets physicalism make a substantive claim about the world without making it hostage to whichever entities today's physics happens to treat as fundamental.

But I keep worrying that this is just Hempel's dilemma wearing a disguise. If "completed physics" is defined by extrapolating from current physics, the formulation is almost certainly false, because current physics is incomplete in ways that probably involve new fundamental posits. If it is instead defined as whatever theory ultimately explains everything observable, the formulation threatens to become trivially true, or too vague to be held at risk by anything. I want you to take the position at face value and tell me what would count against it. What would have to be true for the ideal-physics formulation of physicalism to be false, empty, or indistinguishable from a rival such as dualism or panpsychism? Which developments in physics, philosophy of science, or the metaphysics of mind would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which would defenders of the formulation legitimately dismiss as problems for every version of physicalism rather than for this version in particular? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, along with an honest verdict on whether I have insulated the view into unfalsifiability or left it genuinely exposed.
