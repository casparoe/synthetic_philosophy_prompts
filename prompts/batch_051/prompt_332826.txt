I've been arguing with my friend for weeks about what should count as a single organism, and I've started keeping a list of her calls so I can catch her being inconsistent. Here's where she's landed. A blue whale: one organism, obviously. An ant colony: also one organism, even though the ants crawl around as seperate bodies — her line is that the workers can't reproduce and the colony runs itself like one body. A Portuguese man o' war: one organism. I showed her that its actually a bunch of tiny animals stuck together, and she said that's exactly the point — they're all from one egg and they're so specialized and joined up that they're just parts of one animal. And a giant aspen grove where every trunk is the same clone and the roots are all hooked together: one organism, no hesitation.

The next case I want to bring up is a lichen, the thing where a fungus and an alga grow so tangled together that they look and act like one little green crust, trading nutrients back and forth. What would she say about that, if she's trying to be consistent? I'm less interested in the actual yes or no than in the rule she's running, and whether there's another rule that fits all her other verdicts just as well but gives the opposite answer on the lichen. If you had to break the tie with one more case, what case would you pick and which way would each rule go on it?
