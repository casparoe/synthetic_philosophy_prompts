I keep going back and forth on origin essentialism: the view that each of us could not have developed from a different sperm and egg than the particular ones we actually came from, so the specific gametes are part of what it is to be you and not just a fact about how the events happened to unfold. Play it straight and map out what would have to be true for that view to be false. Give me the actual defeaters and stress tests — monozygotic splitting, tetragametic chimerism (two early embryos fusing, so the person comes from two sperm and two eggs), two numerically distinct sperm that happen to carry the same DNA, artificial gametes derived from stem cells, whatever else you think actually bites — and for each say whether it is decisive, merely awkward, or something a committed defender can absorb with a distinction, and be honest about when such a distinction starts to look ad hoc. Also say which pressure points a defender of the view should themselves take seriously, not just what critics claim. End with your verdict on whether the view is in fact well insulated, and whether that insulation is a feature or a bug. Write in American English.
