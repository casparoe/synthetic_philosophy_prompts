I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics and I keep running into the supertask fight, specifically Thomson's lamp. The setup is the usual one: a lamp starts off, you switch it on at t=1/2, off at t=3/4, on at t=7/8, and so on, infinitely many switches by t=1. The question is whether the lamp is on or off at t=1. Everyone agrees the state sequence just alternates and doesn't converge, so there's no limiting state. And yet the debate hasnt moved in decades. One side says that's the whole answer: the lamp has no determinate state at t=1, the mathematics is perfectly clear, and the puzzle only shows that a supertask can be a coherent description whose final state is underdetermined. The other side says that's exactly why supertasks are incoherent: a physically complete description of a process ought to fix its final state, so a scenario whose own construction leaves the final state undefined can't be a real possibility, and any supposed instance would be sneaking in some arbitrary convention (or an unmentioned last switching) to make the answer seem determinate.

What bugs me is that neither side seems to be contesting the math. They agree the sequence doesn't converge; they agree no experiment has realized anything like this; they agree ordinary physical machinery breaks down. So what's the disagreement actually about? My working hypothesis is that it's not a factual disagreement but a fight about what "possible" has to mean — logical possibility, physical possibility, or something like constructibility — and the two sides are quietly answering different questions under the same vocabulary of "can a supertask happen." But it might instead be a disagreement about whether a mathematical description carries existence commitments, or whether an underdetermined case counts as evidence against a theory or just as a gap in it. I want a diagnosis, not a survey. What kind of disagreement is this? And given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a formal result, a sharper notion of limit, an example of a supertask with a determinate final state, clarifying the sense of "possible" — or should I expect no progress because the whole question is ill-posed?
