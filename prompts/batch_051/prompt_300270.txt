Read Waiting for Godot through the ethics of small promises and punctuality. Vladimir and Estragon are keeping an appointment with someone who never shows up; each act a boy arrives to say Godot will come tomorrow, and they keep deciding to leave and then staying anyway. Treat the play as a kind of thought experiment about obligations that are tiny on paper—show up at the agreed time, wait a bit, keep your word—but become unbounded in practice. What does the play show about whether keeping that kind of commitment has value even when the other party is absent or indifferent, and whether such promises can become a kind of self-imprisonment? Where would a philosopher working on promising and reliability say the play dramatizes something the papers miss, and where does it resist a moralized reading?
