I'm leading a first-year undergraduate tutorial next week on Darwin's treatment of morality in The Descent of Man. The session will turn on a passage in which Darwin asks us to imagine human beings reared under the social conditions of hive-bees: the unmarried females would regard it as a sacred duty to kill their brothers, the mothers would strive to kill their fertile daughters, and no one would interfere, because their consciences would have been shaped by their social instincts rather than by our own. Don't explain the passage to me; plan how I should teach it in fifty minutes. I need the order in which the ideas must be introduced — what has to be in place about descent, social instinct, and the ordinary operation of conscience before the counterfactual can land — and the one carrying example I should build the session around, with an account of what it must have and what it must lack. I also need a misconception map: which predictable misunderstandings my students will bring or form, and at which point in the sequence each should be headed off rather than left for the discussion to fix. End with one question I can put to the room to check whether they have grasped the explanatory claim, rather than merely been made uncomfortable by it. Write the plan in British English.
