Design an everyday case in which epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment deliver opposite verdicts on the same knowledge ascription. The specification: a speaker, facing no significant stakes of her own, says of a second person, 'She knows that p'; contextualism should count the sentence true in the speaker's context, while pragmatic encroachment should count the subject as failing to know p. Constraints: the scene must be set in a workplace, household, clinic, or public-transit setting; no skeptical hypotheses; no characters using philosophical vocabulary; and no stipulating the verdicts. The contextualist's lower standards must not be manufactured by making the attributor cavalier, ignorant of the subject's stakes, or speaking loosely — she should be fully aware that the subject's stakes are high, but those stakes must not be her own. After presenting the scenario, run the check: state what p is, what evidence the subject has, what is practically at stake for the subject, what the attributor's own context is, and why each theory gives the verdict it gives. Then identify the one feature of the scenario doing the work, say how each theory's verdict would shift if that feature were removed, and explain how a defender of pragmatic encroachment would most plausibly resist the contextualist's verdict without rejecting the scenario.
