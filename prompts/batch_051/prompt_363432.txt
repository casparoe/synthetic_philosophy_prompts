I worked for most of my life as a research scientist and came to philosophy only after retiring, so I would appreciate a diagnosis pitched toward someone who thinks in terms of measurement and model stability. Reading Ian Hacking on the "looping effect" in psychiatric classification has left me with an argument I find rigorous but cannot accept. Please tell me whether the argument is valid and, if not, which step fails.

(1) A classification can support prediction and explanation only if the properties it marks off remain sufficiently stable across repeated applications.
(2) Applying a psychiatric label changes the labeled person's situation: the person, clinicians, and institutions respond to the label, and those responses feed back into the person's self-description, behavior, and treatment.
(3) This feedback alters the very phenomena the label was introduced to track — the presenting signs, subjective complaints, and course of the condition shift as the label circulates.
(4) Therefore the properties a psychiatric category marks off do not remain sufficiently stable across repeated applications; each application perturbs the target the category is meant to track.
(5) Hence psychiatric categories cannot support prediction or explanation, and their diagnostic use lacks scientific warrant.

I do not see where the chain breaks. Step (1) looks like a condition on any trustworthy measuring instrument, and steps (2) and (3) look like a factual report of what happens when labels become part of a person's social world. But the conclusion, if sound, would erode confidence in an entire branch of medicine. If the argument is valid, I want to know what that leaves of psychiatric science. If it is not, identify the exact step and the strongest reading under which it would be true, and assess whether the argument still goes through on that reading. Please also say what would change your diagnosis: which premise or inference is most vulnerable, and what empirical or conceptual consideration would most likely overturn your assessment.
