"Church and Turing independently proved that the informal notion of a function computable by following a mechanical procedure is identical to the formal notion of a Turing-computable function. Once that proof was in place, the thesis became a settled theorem of mathematics, and there is no serious question that it could be false. A direct corollary is that no physical system — a quantum computer, a human brain, or any more exotic device — could ever compute a function that is not Turing-computable, because such a device would contradict the theorem. That is why results like the unsolvability of the halting problem are best understood as logical limits on any possible computing device, not merely as limitations of a particular formal model."
