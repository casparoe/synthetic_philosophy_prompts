I'm working on a piece about whether hope is actually a moral virtue or just socially acceptable self-deception, and I keep getting stuck because "hope" and "optimism" slide into each other in my notes. A friend of mine who does palliative care told me that her patients are often completely clear-eyed about their odds — some of them know the survival curves better then the residents — and still describe themselves as hopeful. That doesn't look like optimism as I'd ordinarily use the word, since optimism would be believing the numbers are better than they are. But I also don't want to define hope as merely wanting something unlikely, because then every lottery-ticket buyer would count as hopeful.

Can you give me a working definition of hope that separates it from optimism, wishful thinking, and plain preference? I want it to get these cases right: (1) a stage-four cancer patient who knows the five-year survival rate and says she hopes to see her daughter graduate; (2) a startup founder who is convinced her company has a 70% chance of succeeding when the base rate for her sector is under 10%; (3) a climate researcher who believes 2.5 degrees of warming is basically locked in but spends weekends doing local rewilding work; (4) a gambler on a losing streak who genuinely believes the next hand will turn it around; (5) someone who buys a Powerball ticket and says "someone has to win"; (6) an activist who thinks her movement will probably fail but says the attempt is worth making anyway. I'm fairly confident the cancer patient is hoping and that the startup founder is just wrong about her odds, not hoping exactly; I'm unsure about the climate researcher and the activist, and I suspect the gambler is wishful thinking rather than hope. My hunch is that hope is something you can hold while thinking the odds are bad, and optimism is a belief that the odds are good, but I keep finding cases where that line get blurry. Run the definition through all six, say where it gives a surprising answer, and tell me whether any of these people should count as having an epistemic vice rather than a moral virtue.
