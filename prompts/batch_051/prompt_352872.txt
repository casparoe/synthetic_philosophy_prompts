I have been thinking about free will, and I have come to suspect that the standard question — whether we have it — is badly posed, because the label bundles together several different capacities: the ability to do what you reflectively want to do, the ability to have done otherwise, and being the ultimate source of your actions. Even the ability to have done otherwise seems to split in two, depending on whether it just means that you would have acted differently if you had wanted to, or something stronger: that you could have acted differently even with the same past and the same laws. I am drawn to the idea that the interesting question is not whether we have free will in some unspecified sense, but which of these capacities are actually worth wanting, and whether we already have those. One worry keeps nagging at me: if the only way to get the stronger kind of could-have-done-otherwise is to introduce some randomness at the moment of choice, I cannot see how randomness adds control rather than subtracting it.

I would like your credence in the following claim: the capacities worth wanting under the heading of free will are, roughly, self-control, responsiveness to reasons, and the ability to act on reflective judgement — and these are capacities we already have, whose value does not depend on determinism being false. Please decompose your credence into the sub-questions it actually rests on. I would expect something like: which capacities sober reflection would treat as worth wanting; how much of their value depends on the weaker rather than the stronger kind of could-have-done-otherwise; whether the capacities we actually care about can exist in a deterministic world; and how probable it is that determinism, or something near enough, holds for human action. For each sub-question give a rough probability and a line of justification, show how you combine them, and then say which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would shift it most. I am less interested in the bare number than in seeing where the reasoning is load-bearing.

Please separate what is reasonably settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Write the answer in British English.
