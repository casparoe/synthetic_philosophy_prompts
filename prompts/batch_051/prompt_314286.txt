Suppose that in 2030 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, worried about Simpson's-paradox-style reversals in trial data, adopts the following approval rule: a new treatment may be approved only if the estimated effect on the primary efficacy endpoint is favorable in the overall trial population and is not unfavorable in any prespecified subgroup that makes up at least 10% of that population. Predict how drug development, trial design, and clinical practice change over the next twenty years. Make the reasoning visible: who adapts first—sponsors, regulators, physicians, or patients; how sponsors will choose inclusion criteria, subgroup definitions, and multiplicity adjustments; where the rule is most likely to block treatments that would genuinely help; and where it can still be gamed into approving treatments whose subgroup agreement is an artifact. State which predictions you are most and least confident in, which depend on assumptions about how easily subgroup definitions can be manipulated, and what would have to turn out differently for your overall prediction to be wrong. Please answer in American English.
