I am a teaching assistant for an upper-division seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and I am grading a midterm essay on Frankfurt cases. The question students were asked to answer was this:

State the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) in the form Frankfurt attacks. Then describe a Frankfurt-style case and explain exactly why it is supposed to show that PAP is false. Finally, evaluate the argument: does the case really eliminate every alternative possibility, and does its conclusion by itself support compatibilism about determinism and moral responsibility? Address at least one standard objection.

One student submitted the following answer:

The principle of alternative possibilities says that a person is morally responsible for what they have done only if they could have done otherwise. Frankfurt’s example concerns Jones and Black. Black wants Jones to kill Smith, and he has installed a device that will make Jones decide to kill Smith if Jones shows any sign that he is about to decide not to. Jones shows no such sign, decides on his own, and kills Smith, so Black never has to intervene. Because Black would have stopped Jones from doing otherwise, Jones could not have done otherwise. But because the actual decision was Jones’s own, he is still morally responsible. This shows that PAP is false. It also shows that compatibilism is true, since determinism works in the same way as Black’s device: it removes alternative paths while leaving the agent’s actual deliberation and choice intact. One standard objection is the flicker-of-freedom reply. It says that Frankfurt has not removed every alternative, because Jones retains some faint alternative, such as showing the prior sign or beginning to form a different decision. Frankfurt himself answered this objection by saying that no flicker remains, since Black can detect the sign infallibly and therefore even the sign was not something Jones could avoid. But defenders of PAP reply that as long as some flicker remains, however faint, the requirement PAP imposes is still satisfied, so Frankfurt has not shown the principle to be false.

Please give this answer a grade out of 10, and then provide the point-by-point marginal comments that justify that grade. Specifically, assess its definition of PAP, its reconstruction of the Frankfurt case, its treatment of whether alternatives were genuinely removed, its inference from the case to compatibilism, and its handling of the flicker objection. As you grade, separate what is settled in this literature from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
