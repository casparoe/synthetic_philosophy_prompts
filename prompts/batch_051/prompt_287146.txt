I'm a novelist and I've somehow ended up moderating the public Q&A for a visiting philosopher who defends a Brandom-style inferentialism about meaning: the idea that what a statement (or a concept) means is mostly its inferential role — what other claims it entitles you to, what it commits you to, what it rules out — rather than what it represents or refers to. I've read enough not to ask "but what about the real world?" in a way that gets waved off, but I need the questions to be sharper than that. Write me eight or so interview questions, each with the follow-up to use when the guest gives her standard answer, plus one line on what a good answer would have to actually do. Press the places critics press, and order them so each question tightens the last.

The sequence should get at: whether inference is too "inside the circle" and how the view ever reaches aboutness or things in the world; genuinely novel metaphor — the thing I live on as a novelist — where a phrase deliberately breaks its normal inferences but still lands, which feels like a live problem for the view; where the norms of inference come from, since "implicit in practice" looks like it's doing a lot of work; observation reports like "this is red," where the content seems to come from causal contact rather than inference; and compositionality, since you can build a new sentence whose inferential role no one ever taught you. No soft-ball how-did-you-get-into-philosophy filler. And dont make me read a bibliography to understand the questions.
