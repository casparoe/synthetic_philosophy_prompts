I have been reading about responsibility for collective wrongdoing, and I keep noticing a configuration for which I do not have a precise term. Consider a company that falsifies its emissions reports, or a public body that runs an unlawful surveillance programme. One person within the organisation performs a small, entirely routine task in service of the scheme: entering a row of figures into a spreadsheet, maintaining a server, driving a van of documents from one office to another. The scheme would have proceeded unchanged without that person; had they refused, the task would simply have been assigned to someone else, or omitted without any effect on the outcome. The person knows what the larger scheme is. We want to say that they did something wrong, but the phrase 'causing harm' seems too strong, because the harm was already fully determined by the actions of others. 'Complicity' seems close, but the cases I see under that heading usually involve someone whose help actually advanced the wrongdoing, whereas here the person's contribution advanced nothing.

I am not asking for the general theory of collective responsibility. What I want to know is whether this specific configuration — a knowing participant whose particular contribution made no difference to how things turned out — has an established name of its own, and how exactly it relates to complicity, to being an accomplice, and to the kind of case in which each of many people makes a small but real difference to a harm (the person who adds one more lorry to an already congested road, for example). If no single term fits, I should like to know which of the neighbouring concepts comes closest and where the boundaries between them fall; a bare label would not settle the matter.
