So our eng Slack got into the Sleeping Beauty problem last week after someone linked a writeup, and a coworker whos usually pretty sharp gave me his rundown of why the answer is obviously 1/2. I cant tell if its a fair summary or if hes baking his conclusion in, and it's been bugging me. Here's roughly what he said:

The setup: a fair coin is flipped Sunday night. Heads means Beauty is woken Monday, interviewed, then sleeps through to the end of the experiment. Tails means she's woken Monday and Tuesday, but in between the memory of Monday is wiped, so each waking feels identical and she can't tell whether it's Monday or Tuesday. She knows the whole protocol in advance. On waking, what credence should she have that the coin came up heads?

His case for 1/2: before bed Sunday she'd obviously say 1/2. And waking up gives her zero new information about the coin — she already knew she'd experience a waking like this one either way, since heads gives one waking and tails gives at least one, and the amnesia removes anything that could tell her which day she's on. No new info means no update, so she stays at 1/2.

He says the 1/3 people get their answer by listing three subjectively indistinguishable possible wakeups (heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday) and assigning 1/3 to each. But that's counting the two tails wakeups as if they were separate chances for the coin to land heads, when really one coin flip produced both of them. It's double-counting tails. The only reason 1/3 feels right, according to him, is that people slide from asking about the coin to asking which waking they're in, or start thinking about bets. Sure, if Beauty bets on heads every waking she'd need 2:1 odds to break even, but that's just expected payoff across wakings; credence is about which world you're in, not how many times you'll cash a ticket. Over many runs of the experiment, if she guesses heads every waking she's right about the coin in half the runs, not a third. So the real answer is 1/2, and thirderism is basically a counting error dressed up as philosophy.

I half-remember a post saying waking up itself is new information — something about self-locating evidence, like the fact that this waking is happening now — and when I tried simulating it, the answer came out 1/3 or 1/2 depending on whether I sample by run or by waking. So I don't trust that he's actually representing the other side. Is his summary fair? Which parts would a defender of the 1/3 answer accept, which parts would they say mischaracterize their reasoning, and do the accurate parts of his account actually force the conclusion that her credence should stay 1/2? Also, is the betting stuff really a side issue, or is he right that the 1/3 camp is just conflating probability with expected winnings?
