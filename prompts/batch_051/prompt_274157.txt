Can you give me a working definition of the rule of law? Not the dictionary one, a defintion I could actually use to sort a country into "has it," "doesn't have it," or "has it partially" if you think it comes in degrees. Here are the cases I want the definition to get right:

1. A state where the laws are all public, general, prospective, and stable, and courts apply them faithfully, but the laws themselves set up a strict racial caste system.
2. A state where the legislature passes a law in January making something illegal, and in February people are prosecuted for doing that thing the previous December.
3. A state where traffic and tax rules are all published, but the enforcement agency keeps an unpublished list of people who are never ticketed or audited, and won't confirm who's on it.
4. A dictator who governs entirely through public, general decrees, applies them consistently to everyone including his own family, and never acts outside them — but he can repeal and replace any decree whenever he likes.
5. A small island society with no written legal code, no professional judges, and no police, but with a stable set of customary norms that everybody knows and that elders apply consistently in disputes.
6. A democracy with free elections and lots of rights on paper, but the rules change so often and are so tangled that even the tax office can't say what the law will be six months from now, and court cases take a decade.
7. A state with all the features of case 1 except that most people can't afford a lawyer and there is no legal aid, so court is basically a service for the rich.

My gut: case 1 counts, although I hate that it does. Case 2 clearly fails, and case 3 clearly fails. Case 4 I keep flip-flopping on — part of me says yes because the leader is actually constrained in practice, part says no because none of it is entrenched. Case 5 I also can't decide: no state law, but the norms sure function like law. Case 6 I think fails, but I can't articulate why if case 5 might pass. Case 7 I suspect fails too, but I'm not sure whether that's part of the rule of law or a seperate issue about access to justice.

So: give me the definition, walk it through all seven cases one by one, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising results. If you think the term splits into two concepts (thin and thick, or formal and substantive), give me both and tell me which cases switch between them.
