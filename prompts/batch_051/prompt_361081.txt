I keep getting tangled up in this argument, and I want to know exactly where it goes wrong — or wether it does. (1) Take "God" to mean the being such that no greater being can be thought. (2) A being that exists both in the mind and in reality is greater than a being that exists in the mind only. (3) Suppose God exists only in the mind. Then something greater than God can be thought: the very same being, but existing in reality as well. (4) But that contradicts (1), since by definition nothing greater than God can be thought. (5) So God must exist in reality. Gaunilo's parody says the same form proves a greatest conceivable island exists: an island that exists in reality is greater than one that exists only in thought, so if the greatest island existed only in thought, a greater island could be thought, contradicting its own definition — so a greatest island exists. Everyone agrees the island conclusion is nuts, so something in the shared form has to fail, but people don't seem to agree on which step. Is the original argument valid? If not, which step fails, and does the island version fail at the same step or for a different reason? If you want to say the two arguments differ, don't just say God is special — say what feature of the concept of God blocks the move that the concept of an island can't.
