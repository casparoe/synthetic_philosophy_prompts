I'm in a reading group on philosophy of physics, and last week we read a chapter that introduced the hole argument. The author summarizes it like this:

"Einstein's hole argument shows that general relativity is radically indeterministic. Consider a matter-free region of spacetime — the hole. Hold everything outside the hole fixed, including all the matter in the universe, and the field equations still permit infinitely many different gravitational fields inside the hole. Since these possibilities are observationally indistinguishable, Einstein concluded that only observable coincidences of matter are physically meaningful and that spacetime points have no independent existence. Philosophers later revived the argument to refute substantivalism, the view that spacetime is a substance whose points exist independently of matter. A substantivalist has to say the different fields inside the hole are different possible worlds even though nothing observable differs; since that is absurd, general relativity forces a relationalist picture of spacetime."

I have enough background to know the author is describing something real, but several of the claims strike me as suspicious — especially the idea that Einstein himself concluded spacetime points don't exist, and the slide from "the argument creates trouble for substantivalism" to "so general relativity is a relationalist theory." But I don't trust my instincts here.

Is this a fair summary? Please go through it claim by claim and tell me which parts a philosopher of physics would accept, which are common simplifications, and which are just wrong. In particular, I'd like to know whether the hole argument really shows that general relativity is indeterministic, whether the historical claim about Einstein is right, whether the matter-free stipulation is essential to the argument or merely a vivid way to present it, and whether the argument turns on observational indistinguishability or on what the field equations determine. Does the author's concluding inference — that the hole argument refutes substantivalism and forces relationism — survive the corrections?
