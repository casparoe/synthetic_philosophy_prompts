ok so ive been trying to figure out whether when a chatbot confidently tells you something false that counts as lying, bullshit, or just being wrong. the usual move is to look at the speakers aim: a liar knows theyre being false and wants you to believe it anyway, someone bullshitting just doesnt care whether its true, and being wrong is aiming at truth and missing. I read some of the Frankfurt bullshit essay a while back and that distinction stuck with me. but a language model doesnt have aims in any normal sense, it just outputs what the training made probable, so its not obvious any of those labels fit at all.

before I read any more about this, I want the list of requirements that any decent theory of machine hallucination has to meet if it wants to earn the right to use words like lying, bullshit, or error for what these systems do. for each requirement, say what case or example it comes from and why it matters. then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just us forcing a human category onto a machine.

make sure the list can handle at least these situations, and say whether any of them turn out to be the same thing or whether no single label can cover all of them:
- the model says something false when the training data pretty clearly says the opposite
- the model gives a totally made up citation or source, not just a bad paraphrase
- the model says something that happens to be true but for the wrong reason, or would have said it even if it had been false
- a model that has been trained specifically to stick to the truth and still gets something wrong
- a model that adds fake confidence markers like "certainly" or "studies show" regardless of what its saying

the part im most interested in is whether some of these requirements can't be jointly satisfied once you stop assuming theres a person behind the words, because then the list itself tells us how far the lying/bullshit/error distinction can actually stretch. if you think they clash, say which ones and why. keep the focus on the criteria and what motivates them, not on scoring specific chatbots or philosophers.
