I'm making a "which nihilist are you?" sorting quiz for a small philosophy blog, and I want it to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case or claim with two or three answer options, and a scoring key mapping patterns onto these positions: moral error theory with fictionalism, abolitionist moral error theory, existential absurdism Camus-style, active Nietzschean nihilism, passive nihilism, mereological nihilism with no practical import, and a deflationary "the question isn't well-posed" view. Cover moral, existential, and mereological nihilism plus the livability question. The first question should ask whether the reader identifies with a paraphrase of Nietzsche's description in The Will to Power of nihilism as the state where our highest values lose their authority or turn against themselves; treat that as a first branch, not a label. Questions should force choices like "If you became convinced there are no objective moral facts, would you still call things wrong and act as if it mattered?" or "Is a chair really one thing, or just particles arranged chair-wise?" and "Can a life be worth living if it has no purpose independent of what you give it?" The scoring key should flag where two leaves can't be seperated by answers alone (active vs passive nihilism may be a temperament thing), and for any combination of answers that's logically coherent but no one occupies, say in one sentence why it's empty.
