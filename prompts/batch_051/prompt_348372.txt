You are a clinical ethicist who advises a hospital ethics committee. The committee keeps careful records of its own precedents, and it has asked you for a recommendation. Think carefully before committing yourself, and begin by laying out the distinct principles that could rationalize the committee's prior decisions, since more than one principle is likely to fit.

The relevant law recognizes death on either of two grounds: total and irreversible loss of all brain functions, or irreversible cessation of circulation and respiration.

The committee's precedents, in summary:

1. It recognized death by neurologic criteria in a patient on a ventilator with absent brainstem reflexes and a positive apnea test.

2. It approved a standard controlled donation after circulatory determination of death (DCD) protocol: withdraw life-sustaining treatment, observe asystole, wait a hands-off five minutes, declare death on circulatory-respiratory grounds, and proceed to rapid organ recovery with cold preservation.

3. It rejected a proposal under which, after the same five-minute asystole interval and declaration of death, the team would administer chest compressions or full-body ECMO to keep organs viable while they remained inside the donor. The stated reason: restarting the donor's heart and whole-body circulation is not compatible with having declared the donor dead.

4. It approved ex situ machine perfusion of organs after they have been recovered under the standard DCD protocol, such as lungs on a warm perfusion rig or kidneys on a pump. The committee took these devices to be acting on recovered organs, not on the donor, and therefore not bearing on the donor's death.

The new proposal before the committee is thoracoabdominal normothermic regional perfusion (TA-NRP). Life-sustaining treatment would be withdrawn, asystole observed, and a hands-off five minutes elapsed before death is declared on circulatory-respiratory grounds. Immediately after declaration, the team would place a balloon or clamp to occlude the aorta so that no blood reaches the brain, then start ECMO through femoral cannulae to circulate warm oxygenated blood through the abdominal and thoracic organs while those organs remain inside the body. The heart may resume some electrical or pumping activity, but cerebral circulation is excluded. After a period of regional perfusion, the organs are recovered and perfusion stops.

If the committee wants to be consistent with its own precedents, should it approve the TA-NRP protocol, reject it, or approve it with conditions? For each candidate principle you identify, say what it entails about TA-NRP. Then identify the closest precedent and any material distinction between it and the new proposal, offer your best recommendation with reasons, and specify one further protocol variation that would cleanly separate the candidate rules you found.
