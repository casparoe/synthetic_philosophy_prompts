I sit on my university's research integrity committee, which must decide sanctions in two data-fabrication cases at its meeting next week. Both involve postdoctoral researchers with comparable records who admit to the same kind of misconduct—fabricating several data points. In the first case, the fabricated results appeared in a published paper that has since been retracted; before then, a collaborator built a grant application on the paper's findings and another lab spent months on replication attempts that failed. In the second case, the fabrication was discovered before submission, so nothing was published and no one outside the lab was affected. The only significant difference between the cases is when the misconduct happened to be detected, which appears to have been a matter of chance. Draft the committee's decision memo: recommend whether the two researchers should receive the same or different sanctions, specify the sanction(s), and justify the recommendation, giving an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
