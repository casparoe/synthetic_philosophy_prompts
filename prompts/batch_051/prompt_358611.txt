I keep noticing a parallel between the rule-following problem associated with Kripke's Wittgenstein and the problem of generalization in supervised learning, and I cannot tell whether it is deep or superficial. On the philosophical side, any finite history of my past uses of a function is consistent with infinitely many rules that diverge on the next case: addition and quaddition agree on every sum I have so far computed but come apart later, so my past behavior seems not to determine which rule I was following. On the machine-learning side, any finite training set is consistent with infinitely many hypotheses that fit the seen data and diverge on unseen data, which is why a learner must be given an inductive bias. So far the two look structurally identical. But two disanalogies bother me: the philosophical problem is about meaning and correctness, while the learning problem is only about predictive success against a loss function; and machine learning treats underdetermination as an engineering fact to be managed, while Kripke's skeptic treats it as a metaphysical threat to meaning itself.
