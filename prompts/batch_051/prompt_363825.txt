I'm taking an upper-level seminar on hedonism and well-being, and we have spent the last several weeks on the heterogeneity problem: if the taste of a ripe peach, the moment a migraine lifts, and the satisfaction of finally understanding a proof all count as pleasures, it is genuinely hard to say what they have in common. I've been keeping careful notes on my professor's case-by-case verdicts because she clearly has a rule in mind but never states it outright. So far she has counted all of the following as pleasures: the warmth and taste of a good meal when hungry; sexual arousal; the moment of relief when a severe headache suddenly lifts; the "aha" of seeing the solution to a hard problem after hours of confusion; and feeling proud of a friend's hard-won success. She often glosses the last two as taking pleasure in the fact that something worked out, rather than as any particular sensation. She has also said, more than once, that the bare absence of pain—in a state with no feeling of release, no comfort, and nothing one is pleased about—is not itself a pleasure.

Next week's case is this: someone deliberately listens to a piece of music that reliably moves them to tears. They find the experience intensely absorbing, and afterwards they say they enjoyed it enormously and would choose it again, but they insist that the felt quality throughout was one of grief-like sadness and longing, with no warm, pleasant, or comfortable sensation at any point. Would my professor call this a pleasure? I am less interested in the bare yes-or-no than in the rule behind her verdict.

Please act as a professional philosopher who works on pleasure and well-being. Think through the problem carefully, and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate principles that fit her verdicts so far. Then, for each candidate rule, say what it predicts about the music case. After that, give your best prediction of her verdict and explain why you prefer that reading of her record. Finally, identify one further case I could ask her about that would most cleanly settle which principle she is actually running.
