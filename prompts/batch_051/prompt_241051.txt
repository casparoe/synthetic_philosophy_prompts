I am putting together a sorting quiz for a workshop on longtermist grantmaking whose participants disagree about whether an expected-value calculation that is dominated by a vanishingly small probability of an astronomically large payoff should guide decisions. Please write a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz, with a scoring key, on positions about expected value fanaticism. Build the questions around the underlying structural choices rather than the position labels: (i) whether the goodness of an outcome is bounded or saturates as the good grows, (ii) whether probabilities enter the calculation in the standard expected-utility way or are risk-weighted so that worst outcomes count disproportionately, (iii) whether some rights or thresholds are lexically excluded from trade at any price, and (iv) whether sufficiently small probabilities may be treated as negligible. Have the quiz sort respondents into six leaves: unbounded expected-value maximization (fanaticism), bounded utility, risk-weighted expected utility, lexical-threshold views, deontic side-constraints, and small-probability neglect. Write the questions as eight to ten concrete forced-choice cases answerable by someone who does not already know these labels, and include at least these four: a Pascal's mugging-style demand with an enormous but finite promised payoff; a choice between a near-certain moderate benefit and a one-in-10^30 chance of a benefit 10^40 times as large; a case in which the fanatical option is first-order stochastically dominated by a more cautious option (for any threshold of benefit, the cautious option has at least as great a chance of exceeding it); and a case in which a fanatical calculation would license a serious rights violation. The scoring key should map answer patterns to leaves, state which pairs of leaves the questions cannot separate, and specify how to score a respondent who refuses to assign probabilities at all rather than answering the trade-off questions.
