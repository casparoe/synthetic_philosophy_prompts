I have been unable to decide whether the following argument succeeds. Its target is the claim that moving to the propensity interpretation of fitness defuses the old tautology objection to natural selection. I want to know whether the argument is valid, and if not, exactly which numbered step fails; where a step admits of a stronger and a weaker reading, identify the stronger reading and say whether the argument goes through on it.

(1) On the propensity interpretation, an organism's fitness is not its actual reproductive output but an objective probabilistic disposition — a single-case probability attached to the organism or its genotype in a specified environment — to leave viable offspring.

(2) A disposition of this kind is not directly observable. What is observable is only the actual number of viable offspring an organism leaves.

(3) So when a biologist says of two particular organisms, A and B, that A was fitter than B, the only possible observational warrant for that claim is that A in fact left more viable offspring than B.

(4) Hence comparative-fitness claims are inferred entirely from actual reproductive differences, and nothing over and above those actual differences makes them true.

(5) If nothing over and above actual reproductive differences makes comparative-fitness claims true, then to say that A was fitter than B is just to say that A actually left more viable offspring than B.

(6) Therefore, on the propensity interpretation, the principle that the fitter tend to leave more offspring reduces to the empty claim that those that actually leave more leave more — and the tautology objection stands.

My suspicion is that something happens at (4), because the argument appears to slide from how we would find out about fitness to what fitness is. But I have not been able to repair (4) in a way that is both principled and allows the rest of the argument to do its work; every repair I try either makes the premise false or leaves the conclusion unsupported. Is the argument valid as written, and if not, which step fails?
