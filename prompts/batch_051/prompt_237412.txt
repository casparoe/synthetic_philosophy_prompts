I'm a journalist working on a piece about what happens inside companies when they pick a metric and push it hard. I've been handed an internal support-policy doc from a software firm, and section 4.2 reads:

"4.2 Resolution standard. Each support team shall resolve no fewer than ninety percent (90%) of all assigned tickets within twenty-four (24) business hours of assignment. A ticket is resolved when the assignee has determined that the customer's issue has been addressed and has notified the customer of that determination."

The people who wrote this were not thinking about LLM triage agents, because the company didn't have any then. Here's the case I'm stuck on: one team runs a bot that watches for tickets about to hit the 24-hour mark. At hour 20, the bot sends the customer a message saying the issue is believed to be addressed, marks the ticket resolved, and closes it. If the customer writes back "no, still broken," the system files a new ticket and the clock starts over. The team now "resolves" 97% of tickets inside the window while actual problems sometimes sit unfixed for weeks.

I'm not asking whether this is lousy service; that's obvious. I'm asking what the clause actually says the team may or must do. Does the bot "determine that the customer's issue has been addressed"? Does an automated message count as notification? Does the reopen-as-new-ticket move put them out of compliance, or does the text license it? Then take me through the readings that matter: the plain words, the purpose of the rule, what the drafters would have said if youd asked them about a bot that does this, and what a reasonable employee reading the doc today would take it to mean. If those readings come apart, which one should an auditor use, and why?

Last thing: I keep coming back to Goodhart's law — you turn a number into a target and pretty soon it stops tracking the thing you actually cared about. Is this just that, or does the clause's definition of "resolved" make the gaming something more than a metric failure, like actual noncompliance? I'd rather you argue through the mechanisms than give me a yes.
