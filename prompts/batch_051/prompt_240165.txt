In my biology class this year, our teacher likes to cut off "why" questions about organisms by repeating the motto "Nature does nothing in vain." Recently he used it to dismiss the suggestion that the human appendix might be a vestigial leftover, insisting that if a structure exists it must be doing something useful. He also connected the slogan to Aristotle's idea that living things have final causes, which made the claim sound more substantial than it first seemed. Before I push back, I want to know exactly what I would be pushing against. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be expressing, ordered from the nearly trivial to the very strong, and for each say whether it is plausible and whether it actually licenses the inference that every existing biological structure has a function? In particular, I would like to know which reading, if any, would make my teacher's move about vestigial organs go through, and which readings would leave the inference unsupported even if the slogan were true.
