Lately I've been trying to write down my own epistemology — mainly stuff about the inside view, the outside view, and how confident I should actually be in my own judgments — to see whether it's a position or just a bunch of things I've absorbed on the science and ideas beat. Here's roughly what I'd say if you asked:

1. I'm a planning-fallacy person. For any project I'm personally involved in, the outside view wins: anchor in how similar projects actually went, treat the details of this case as mostly noise that makes me feel special, and forecast from the base rate.

2. But I also think the inside view is basically what journalism is for. A story built only from aggregate statistics and base rates, with no feel for the texture of a lab or a place or a person, is how you publish something that's true in every particular and false as a whole. So I don't want to eliminate the inside view; I want to confine it.

3. I'm with Tetlock on foxes and hedgehogs. I distrust anyone with one big idea that explains everything, and I try instead to keep a bunch of small, partial models and update.

4. I'm a concilliationist about disagreement. When someone I regard as roughly my peer — roughly as smart, roughly as informed — disagrees with me on an empirical or interpretive question, I think I should lower my confidence substantially, not just go looking for reasons they're wrong.

5. But I also think being there counts. If I spent three weeks in a flood zone or a physics lab and a sharp editor who didn't make the trip disagrees with my read of what the place was actually like, I don't think I owe them equal weight on the interpretation. On-the-ground reporting gives you standing that documents can't.

6. And yet I also think vivid first-person experience is systematically overrated. I've seen on-the-ground reporters be exactly as wrong as everyone else, just with better anecdotes, and I think I'm just as prone to treating being-there as a magic credential.

I want you to run this the way an editor runs a fact-check: tell me which pairs actually conflict, which only seem to conflict once you spell them out, and put the real tensions in order of severity. For each real tension, say what would have to give. Then tell me the single cheapest revision that would make the whole set hang together — or, if it's not a position at all, say why it's just a mood.
