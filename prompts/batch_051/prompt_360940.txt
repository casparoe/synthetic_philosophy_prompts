I am drafting a term paper for a course on early analytic ethics, and I have been rereading the sections of G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica in which he discusses the ideal. I keep noticing that his treatment of friendship and his treatment of beauty seem to be built from the same materials. In each case he describes an organic unity: a cognition of something good or beautiful joined with an appropriate feeling, where the value of the whole is not simply the sum of the values of its parts. His examples move back and forth between admiring a beautiful object and loving another person almost as if the two cases illuminated one another. I would like my paper to test whether this alignment is real, and whether it is a strength or a weakness in his view.

Please compare Moore's account of the value of friendship, understood broadly as personal affection, with his account of the value of beauty in Principia Ethica. Specifically: does he in fact assign them the same normative structure, and if he does, are we entitled to say that his ethics of friendship is an aestheticised one? What features of friendship, if any, would this assimilation distort or omit, and why? Finally, should either good be regarded as the more fundamental in Moore's scheme, or are they strictly co-ordinate? I would like a close reconstruction of the relevant stretches of his argument as well as your own evaluative judgement. Please write the answer in British English.
