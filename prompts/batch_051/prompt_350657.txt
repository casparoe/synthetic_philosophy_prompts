I'm putting together a list of objections to Schopenhauer's view that music is, unlike the other arts, a direct copy of the will itself and therefore the highest art. So far I've got:
- The representational-content objection: music can depict particular things (storms, birds, trains) so it isnt purely non-representational.
- The unfalsifiability objection: the mapping of bass/tenor/alto/soprano to inorganic nature/plants/animals/humans is arbitrary and can't be tested.
- The internal-tension objection: if music is the clearest presentation of the will, it should intensify willing, but Schopenhauer also says aesthetic experience quiets the will.
- Nietzsche's objection that Schopenhauer misdescribes music as will-free contemplation, whereas music actually stimulates and affirms life.

I feel like there's another kind of objection out there that dosen't reduce to any of these. Bonus points if it attacks the claim that music is the highest art by looking at the structure of Schopenhauer's hierarchy of the arts, rather than just the music/will relation. Give me one more, and if possible say how it relates to the ones above. And is it actually any good?
