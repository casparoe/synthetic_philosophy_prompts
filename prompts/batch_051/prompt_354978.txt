The working definition of wisdom I keep seeing around rationalist-adjacent parts is roughly: (a) a large, well-calibrated model of how people actually live and why; (b) a sense of where the model runs out, plus updating instead of forcing the new case; (c) being able to pass the relevant chunk of the model to someone else so their decisions actually change; and (d) enough of an inside view of your own model to notice when you're pattern-matching past the data. Now the case that's been bugging me. Mara, a woman in her fifties, has spent two decades quietly collecting an anonymized archive of life-dilemma posts and their follow-ups—breakups, career pivots, estrangements, that kind of thing—maybe 40,000 of them. Give her a new case and she'll tell you, flatly, that you'll regret leaving by spring or that the mother will cave once you stop apologizing, at rates that beat everyone I know, and her advice measurably changes people's lives for the better. But she can't say why a call goes one way beyond the sense that this one is like that one, refuses to generalize, has no inside view of how she does it, and when she's wrong she just files the new example away and moves on. Is Mara wise? Go criterion by criterion, and say where the criteria underdetermine the answer and whether the case shows the list is missing something or counting something it shouldn't.
