I’m putting together a short handout for a grad seminar on conceptual ethics, and I want it to double as a “sort yourself” instrument: a decision tree that takes someone from yes/no answers about a handful of concrete cases to one of the live positions about whether and how concepts get fixed, replaced, or left alone. I want it to sort people by what they say about cases than by which label they like, so none of the questions should just be “do you think we should revise concepts?”.

Here’s the angle I want built in. I keep noticing people run together three things a revision could target: the word (patterns of usage), the concept (the inferential and dispositional structure people actually deploy), and the kind or classification out in the world (who ends up grouped together by laws, labor markets, or polite behavior). I’d like the tree to make that three-way split visible in the first couple of branches. At least these positions should show up as leaves: a Haslanger-style ameliorative position, an austerity view on which revision just is changing how we use a term and questions about concept identity are misplaced, a Strawson-style descriptive position that mostly leaves the relevant concepts alone, and an eliminativist position where the right move for a concept is to stop using it rather than fix it. For the branch questions, use the concept woman or race, or a made-up term like blorg — your call — but the nodes have to be answerable by someone who has never heard the term “conceptual engineering.” Also flag any pairs of leaves your tree can’t cleanly separate, and for any combination of answers that lands in an empty region, give a one-line diagnosis of whether its empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it to around ten leaves.

One more thing: I want you to commit. Build the tree the way you actually think it should go, make the hard calls about where each position sits, and then defend two or three of the choices you made — don’t hand me a survey of possible trees and leave me to pick. Write it in American English.
