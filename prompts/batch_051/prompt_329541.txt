In debates about the metaphysics of space, substantivalists often present space as a kind of container or stage that bodies occupy and move through; Newton's rotating bucket is then recruited to show that the container is real in its own right, since the water's concave surface is evidence of motion with respect to space itself, not merely with respect to other bodies, and the container would remain even if every body in it were removed. Relationists reply that the stage/container picture quietly imports the independent existence it was supposed to prove, since all we actually observe are relations among bodies. Audit this analogy in detail. What maps onto what: the container, its walls, its contents, its continuing to exist when emptied, its own state of motion or rest? Where does the mapping break, and which disanalogies matter for the intended conclusion that space is a substance independent of matter? Which disanalogies are merely incidental? Does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or does the analogy turn out to be carrying the argument?
