Act as a language designer who treats variable identity as a metaphysical problem rather than an implementation detail. I'll give you five informal models of mutation and identity-over-time, and I want you to tier them S/A/B/C by how well they actually explain—not just restate—the classic confusions people hit with mutable state: aliasing surprises, mutations showing up through unexpected references, and the feeling of "did I change the object or just the local name?" Use one sentence per placement to explain why it landed there.

The models:
1. Variables are boxes; assignment replaces the contents of the box.
2. Variables are nametags attached to objects; mutation alters the object behind the nametag.
3. Variables are pointers into memory; mutation writes through the pointer.
4. Values live in an append-only log of immutable versions; mutation just moves a label to a later version.
5. An object is a continuous causal process; mutation is an event in that history, and "same object" means "same causal thread."

Before you finalize the tiers, work through a concrete example where models 2 and 4 make different predictions about what a second variable observes, and let that example justify at least one of your rankings.
