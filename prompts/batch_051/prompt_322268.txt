I am a novelist working on a book whose protagonist, a woman in her forties, returns to her rural hometown to look after an estranged and demanding parent, and I am preparing to interview a moral philosopher who defends a relational care ethics. I need eight to ten interview questions that will help me understand how such a philosopher would reason about obligations that were never chosen, but I do not want the questions merely to invite a restatement of her position. The questions should engage the actual commitments of care ethics and press where the philosophical literature presses. Please include at least one question on each of the following, each with a follow-up that anticipates the reply she is most likely to give and pushes on its weakest point rather than asking a generic follow-up: the objection that care ethics is objectionably partial, privileging the near and dear and thereby licensing neglect of distant strangers; the worry that treating care as the central moral category flatters the historical expectation that women perform unreciprocated, self-effacing nurturing; the problem of deriving structural duties of justice from a moral picture built on one-on-one caring relations; and the distinction between caring about someone and actually taking care of that person, including whether the former has moral weight when the latter is refused. Avoid biographical questions and any request for bibliography or citations.
