Been going back and forth with a friend who's into physics, and he tried to convince me that special relativity makes the future just as real as the past. I'm a software engineer, not a philosophy person, so I might be mangling this, but his move was: two observers moving at different speeds can disagree about whether some distant event is already in the past or still in the future, because what counts as "right now" over there depends on your frame. So the event can't be settled for one observer and not yet real for the other, which pushes toward the block universe picture where every time exists together and our past/future line is just a slice through it.

But I notice I still live as if the past and future are completely different kinds of thing. I treat last month as closed and fixed, but I treat next month as open in a way last month isn't. I regret the past but not the future; I'd pay to change the future but not the past. If the block picture is right, is there any actual justification for this past/future asymmetry, or is it just a quirk of how brains are wired? I want the strongest argument you can give for keeping the asymmetry, and an honest verdict on wether it survives.
