I am a novelist with professional fluency in the vocabulary of narrative construction — authorial intention, plot, foreshadowing, narrative inevitability, character agency, retconning, and revision — but only a lay reader's grasp of theology. Restate the debate among Augustine, Calvin, and Arminius over predestination, grace, and free will in that narrative vocabulary, as faithfully as possible. For each position, translate its central commitments into terms of an author plotting a novel: the author's will and foreknowledge, what a character's freedom can consist in, what it means for a character to be chosen or passed over, and whether a character's choices affect the outcome or only reveal what the author has already set down. Then give me the residue report: which claims carry over cleanly, which ones you had to distort to fit, and which ones have no counterpart in the novelist's vocabulary at all. I want your judgment on whether the residue points to something my vocabulary is missing about the theological problem, or to something in the theological dispute that is less clear or less substantive than its language makes it seem.
