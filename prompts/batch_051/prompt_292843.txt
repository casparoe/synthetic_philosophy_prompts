I've been reading forum threads about an idea called evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I can't shake the feeling that there's a real question in there but I also can't quite state it. As I understand it, the idea is: if the universe is huge, or if there are lots of agents running the same sort of decision procedure as me (copies, simulations, branches of the wavefunction, whatever), then my choices can line up with the choices of agents I will never causally affect. So choosing to cooperate with them could be evidence that they'll cooperate with me, even though my choice can't change what they do. The intuition that pulls at me is something like: if there are a thousand copies of me, and each copy comes out better when the other nine hundred and ninety-nine cooperate, the copy who treats its own decision as evidence about all the others seems to do best. But maybe that's cheating, since the copies share a decision procedure from the start and the real question is whether that pre-existing correlation is a reason or just a fact to exploit. My one friend says this is just Newcomb's problem scaled up and one-boxing-style reasoning is right; my other friend says it's confusing correlation with causation and we should ignore it.
