I am trying to resolve a tension in my thinking about blame and hypocrisy, and I want a negotiated assessment rather than a partisan verdict. I am attracted to an account on which standing to blame is relational rather than content-based: hypocrisy, complicity, or a shared record of the offence forfeits a person’s entitlement to blame even when the charge is accurate. The account appeals because it explains the recoil from the hypocrite and why blame is a social act rather than a bare assertion. Yet I cannot shake the intuition that aptness of blame tracks the wrong done: if a habitual liar calls out another person’s lie, the accusation is true and worth making, and the speaker’s record seems to disqualify the speaker, not the message. I am often told there is no conflict, because speaking with standing and speaking truly are separate dimensions; that reply feels too neat. Please present both sides at their strongest and say which should yield. Should I debunk the intuition as failing to appreciate the relational structure of blame, should the standing account be amended or abandoned, or is there a version on which both survive without collapsing into one another? Lay out what would justify each move. Please write your answer in British English.
