I sit on a state commission that can charter public benefit corporations to run insolvent municipalities, and a proposal in front of us would hand one mid-sized city's permitting, code enforcement, and business-tax collection to a private company for thirty years, with a board appointed by the company's own shareholders and only a narrow appeal back to our commission. Half the commissioners are treating it as ordinary municipal privatization with extra steps; the other half say we'd be chartering a little sovereign, the way the old trading companies were, and that sovereignty is being quietly transferred. I need to circulate a short written opinion before next Thursday's vote. Draft it: approve, deny, or approve with conditions, with real reasoning, and take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side - in particular whether anything like sovereignty is actually being transferred or whether the city's remaining elected officials keep every power that matters, and whether the contractor/public-officeholder distinction can carry that weight. At the end, tell me how confident you are in the recommendation and what single point you're least sure of.
