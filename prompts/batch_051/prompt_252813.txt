I am a high school student, and for a project in my mathematics club I have been collecting people's choices between pairs of monetary gambles. One configuration keeps reappearing, and although friends have called it "risk aversion" or "loss aversion," neither label seems to capture what is strange about it. My question is whether this configuration has its own established name, and exactly how it should be distinguished from those neighboring ideas.

In the first question, each person chooses between:

- A: receive $1,000,000 for certain.
- B: an 89% chance of $1,000,000, a 10% chance of $5,000,000, and a 1% chance of receiving nothing.

In the second question, the same person chooses between:

- C: an 11% chance of $1,000,000 and an 89% chance of receiving nothing.
- D: a 10% chance of $5,000,000 and a 90% chance of receiving nothing.

Most people choose A in the first question and D in the second. Individually, the first choice looks like a preference for certainty and the second looks like a preference for the larger expected payoff, but no single scale for valuing money can produce both choices at once: the second pair is obtained from the first by replacing the 89% chance of $1,000,000 that appears in both A and B with an 89% chance of receiving nothing, and that common replacement should not reverse a person's preference if their choices are governed by a single underlying utility scale.

I am not asking for a reading list or for advice about how people ought to choose. I want to know whether this exact phenomenon—a pair of revealed preferences that are jointly inconsistent with the idea that replacing a common outcome in both options leaves the choice unchanged—has a name of its own, how it differs from ordinary risk aversion, from expected-value reasoning, and from framing effects, and where its boundaries lie. Would it still be the same phenomenon if the sure option were replaced by a merely very likely one, or if the common outcome were changed in value rather than removed? If there is no established name for exactly this configuration, I would prefer a precise definition and an account of the boundary cases to a bare label.
