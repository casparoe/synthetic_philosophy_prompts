ok so i've been going back and forth with people in a discord server about whether stuff inside games and online worlds is "real" and whether someone can actually be harmed by something that only happens in a virtual space. like one person says a stolen sword in an mmo is just pixels so who cares, another says no its real theft same as if someone took your bike, and theyre both convinced theyre obviously right. i want to make a post for my blog that sorts readers by what they actually think about cases instead of what label they vibe with.

please write me a "which view of virtual stuff do you actually hold" quiz. the deliverable should be: eight to ten questions, each one a short concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. the cases should force people to take a stand on specific situations, not ask them to pick a philosophy. so no questions like "do you think virtual objects are real?" — instead give them a case and have them choose what best describes what happened morally/metaphysically.

cases i want included: someone destroys a minecraft build you spent months on; someone sexually harasses or gropes your avatar in a vr space without consent; someone runs a long con inside an eve-online-like game and walks away with a huge amount of in-game assets; a server rollback deletes a super rare item you just got; an account ban wipes out a library of paid digital games or skins. you can add more cases if you want.

the positions the scoring key should be able to land people on are roughly: (a) virtual objects are just data and code, nothing more; (b) virtual objects are real but only as code/configurations, so "stealing" them is at most a change in a database; (c) virtual objects are real social or institutional artifacts, the way debts or borders or points in a game's economy are real; (d) virtual objects are kind of like fictional objects, real as things we refer to and care about but not material; (e) virtual harms are only real when they cause physical-world damage, like lost money or stress that shows up in your body, otherwise its just hurt feelings; (f) virtual harms are real when they set back genuine interests or cause genuine suffering, even if nothing physical changes; and (g) whether a virtual object or harm is real just depends on the platform's rules or terms of service, so if the company says its allowed its not a real harm. if you think some of these collapse into each other or some are incoherent, say so and fix the list.

i want the quiz built from the logical space, not from the list of names. so first work out the key questions or distinctions that separate people (e.g., does something count as existing if its just code plus shared understanding? is a harm real only when your physical situation changes, or also when your interests or suffering change? do the platform's rules decide what counts as real?). then lay out which combinations of answers are possible, put the actual positions on that map, and flag any combinations that nobody holds — and say why: is it incoherent, just really unattractive, or maybe underexplored. also flag any two positions that the quiz honestly cant tell apart with these cases, rather than pretending theyre cleanly separated.

the audience is general blog readers, not academics, so keep the language normal and avoid jargon where you can. and make sure the result labels are things a normal person could see themselves in without feeling insulted.
