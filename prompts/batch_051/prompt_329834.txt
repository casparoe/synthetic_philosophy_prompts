Hey, I’m a novelist and I need a poem for a chapter in my current book. The character who wrote it is a former political theory professor, and it’s her attempt to get her son to understand what Hobbes actually argued in Leviathan without making it sound like a lecture. I’d like a 14-line sonnet, not strict about rhyme, in American English, and the volta should land on the exact move in Leviathan chapter 13 where a person in the state of nature stops being an isolated individual and becomes a subject under a sovereign. The octave should treat the state of nature as the structural problem Hobbes describes there, not mindless brutality: no common power means no security, and no security means no trust, no industry, no stable cooperation, so even a decent person is pulled into preemptive action. The sestet should turn on the covenant: we each lay down our right to all things only because the others do too, and promises alone don’t bind unless there’s a common power with teeth to enforce them. Let the poem respond to the passage in chapter 13 where covenants are only as strong as the sword that backs them. Don't let the ending be too tidy; the same Leviathan that ends the war of all against all is also a mortal god we now have reason to fear. Someone who knows Hobbes should recognize the argument, and someone who doesn’t should still get a good poem. Max 20 lines, please.
