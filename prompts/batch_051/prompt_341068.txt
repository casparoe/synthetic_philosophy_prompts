I'm an ML engineer, so my native vocabulary is objective functions, hard and soft constraints, shadow prices, lexicographic preferences, and regularization — I can make a cost function do tricks, but I've only read enough moral philosophy to be dangerous. There's a structure in deontology I want you to translate into that vocabulary, piece by piece, and I need you to be honest about the places where the translation breaks instead of papering them over.

The position is this: some acts are prohibited independently of the good they do. In particular, it's wrong to kill one innocent person even when killing them is the only way to stop five other murders. The deontologist says rights function as side constraints on action, not as goals to be promoted. But then there's the paradox: if murder is bad, five murders are worse than one, and if wrongness tracks badness then the reasons that make murder wrong should also make it mandatory to minimize murders — even by committing one. The side constraint forbids exactly the act that best minimizes the very thing the constraint protects.

Here's what I want. Translate the whole package — the prohibition, the side-constraint image, the agent-relativity, and the paradox — into optimization terms: objective functions, hard versus soft constraints, shadow prices, Lagrangian penalties, lexicographic orderings, regularization, whatever fits. Go piece by piece, and for each piece tell me what carries over cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in the vocabulary at all. Then give me the residue report: is the stuff that doesn't translate a defect of my optimization vocabulary, or a sign that the deontologist is smuggling in something that looks clear only while it's left vague? Don't just encode do-not-kill as a constraint and declare victory — the interesting question is whether a constraint-like object can actually do the work deontology asks of it, or whether the thing that refuses to translate is the thing doing all the work.
