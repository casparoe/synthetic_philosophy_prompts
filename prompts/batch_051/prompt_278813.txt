For the last couple months my school's philosophy club has been stuck on whether there actually is such a thing as the scientific method, and its gotten weirdly personal. The pro-method side keeps pointing to falsification and controlled experiments and says, look, this is the closest thing to a method, it's not perfect but it's real. The Feyerabend side keeps saying that no version of the method has ever survived contact with actual scientific practice: Galileo violated the accepted methodological rules of his own day, and that's exactly why he made progress, so anything goes isn't a method but a warning against pretending there is one. The annoying thing is, we seem to agree on almost all the history and on the fact that science works. We even agree that scientists use lots of different tools. But nobody has moved an inch. My personal theory is that the whole fight is a mix-up between two questions: what scientists in fact do, and what they should do when things get hard. Maybe it's also a fight about how much a historical counterexample should count against a norm. But I don't trust my own diagnosis. So: what kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, factual, normative, methodological, or some layer cake of those? And is there anything that could realistically end it, or is the question somehow badly posed?
