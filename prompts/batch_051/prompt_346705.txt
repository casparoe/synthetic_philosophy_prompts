I can't decide whether this parallel is deep or just pattern-matching. Sen's whole point about famines is that you can get one without a big drop in food availability: in Bengal in 1943 the rice harvest wasn't catastrophicly bad, but wartime inflation and hoarding pushed rice beyond what rural labourers could pay, so people starved while stocks were there. That's basically what we call starvation in an operating system: total CPU and memory are fine — the machine even has idle cycles — but a low-priority process never gets scheduled and eventually gets killed by the OOM killer. In both cases aggregate supply isn't the problem; the allocation rule is, and what matters is whether an agent has a claim the rule respects. Is there a real isomorphism here, availability vs entitlement on one side and total resource vs scheduling/access on the other, or does it break because people have legal and moral entitlements and can trade, migrate, riot, while a process just blocks? The Great Leap Forward seems like the obvious stress test: there the aggregate really did collapse, though maybe procurement and distribution were doing work too.
