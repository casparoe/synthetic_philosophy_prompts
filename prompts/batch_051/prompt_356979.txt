I am a novelist. My current book turns on a fictional adversarial collaboration between two professional epistemologists: one holds that the Bayesian convergence theorems, often glossed as showing that priors wash out under shared evidence, demonstrate that two rational inquirers with different starting probabilities will eventually come to agree in ways that matter for real inquiry; the other holds that these theorems prove only that convergence occurs under idealizations that almost never obtain, including a shared model of possible observations, priors that do not count out one another's live hypotheses, indefinitely long common evidence, and no computational constraints. I need the document their collaboration would actually produce.

Write that joint statement as both camps could sign it after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should include: the claims they agree on, including about what the convergence theorems establish and what they jointly concede the theorems do not establish; the precise formulation of their residual disagreement, with each side's version phrased so that a committed holder of that view would accept the wording; a separation of substantive disagreement from disagreement that is merely verbal; the cruxes, where settling one would move at least one side; and, for each crux, the case, argument, or test both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
