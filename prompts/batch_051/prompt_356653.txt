I was discussing factor analysis with a psychology colleague the other day, and the exchange kept reminding me of the New Hume debate, which makes me suspect I am either seeing a deep structural unity or forcing a match between two things that merely look alike at the level of vocabulary.

The Hume side, as I understand it: a regularity reading says causation just is the constant conjunction of event types; talk of causation adds nothing over and above the pattern of succession and contiguity in the data of experience. The New Hume reading says Hume's point is epistemic rather than ontological: we can never observe or know the necessary connection between cause and effect, but there may still be something in the world, a real causal power or necessary tie, that grounds the regularities we do observe. The live question is whether the hidden thing that would explain the regularity is real, or whether the regularity is all there is.

The psychology side: researchers posit a latent variable, an unobserved construct such as a trait or ability, to explain why several measured variables correlate. A factor model represents each observed score as depending on loadings on this shared unobserved factor plus noise. The skeptical complaint is that the latent factor is only a compressed redescription of the correlation matrix, a model artifact with no license to reify; the realist reply is that positing a real underlying source of the observed covariance is exactly what gives the model explanatory force, even though the factor is estimated only indirectly.

The mapping that struck me is precise, perhaps suspiciously precise: observed regularities correspond to the correlation matrix, the unobservable necessary connection corresponds to the latent variable, and in both debates the contested move is from evidence for patterns to evidence for an unobservable ground of those patterns rather than a mere summary of them. My worry is that the argumentative structure may not transfer. In psychometrics the realist can appeal to identifiability constraints, out-of-sample prediction, intervention studies, and causal modeling to give a latent factor credentials beyond curve-fitting. Are there genuine counterparts to these moves in the New Hume debate, or is the epistemic situation of the Humean reasoner different in a way that blocks the analogy? And if the parallel is only superficial, what specifically would it predict that turns out false if one tried to run the two debates on the same template?
