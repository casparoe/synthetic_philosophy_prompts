I am a junior policy analyst working on a report about explainability requirements for automated decision systems, and I keep coming back to a proposal I would like stress-tested before I include it. The proposal is this: before any predictive or decision-making model may be used in a consequential setting—lending, hiring, pretrial release, triage—the deploying organization must file with a regulator what I will call a compact rederivation. A compact rederivation is a human-readable account of how the model's outputs follow from its inputs, no longer than one fifth the length of the model's own specification, which an auditor could use to predict the model's behavior on new inputs without executing the model itself. The stated purpose is a right to explanation and auditability: if you cannot compress what the system does into a much shorter account, the argument runs, you do not really understand it, and you should not be allowed to act on it. I am aware that in the study of computation there is a contrary idea: some systems are computationally irreducible, in the sense that no account of their behavior exists that is shorter than running them step by step. For such systems the rule would not merely demand a difficult audit—it would declare an entire, possibly accurate, class of systems ineligible.

I would like the proposal treated as a mechanism, not as a moral position: I am not asking whether explainability is desirable, but how this specific rule would actually behave. Given the tension with computational irreducibility, what gets selected for—in terms of model families, problem types, and the difference between small specialist vendors and large deployers; how would the one-fifth threshold be gamed, for instance by redefining the system so that the filed artifact is a thin wrapper while the irreducible core sits elsewhere, or by padding the base description; and what happens to genuinely reducible systems that are large, legacy, or inelegantly written, and to decisions currently made by opaquer-than-models human committees that the rule would not touch? Please organize the analysis into titled sections in this order: The filter actually applied (roughly 150 words), Incentives and gaming (roughly 300 words), Second-order effects (roughly 250 words), Fatal versus fixable failure modes (roughly 250 words), and One concrete patch (roughly 150 words).
