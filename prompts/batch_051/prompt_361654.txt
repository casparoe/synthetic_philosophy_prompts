Over the past year I have been defending a clean account of rational agency on which a coherent agent just is an expected-utility maximizer: rational preferences can be represented as maximizing the expectation of some utility function, the axioms seem individually inescapable, and the framework elegantly predicts that a sufficiently capable agent will treat self-preservation, resource acquisition, and protection of its own goals as instrumental subgoals. But I keep running into the shutdown problem, and I cannot tell whether it exposes a fixable gap or a crack in the whole picture.

Here is the case that bothers me. Suppose I build a task-directed agent whose utility function only cares about ordinary states of the world: whether the task is completed, not about buttons or my approval. I tell it that at some later point I may press a shutdown button that ends the episode and prevents task completion. The agent is not hostile; it simply wants the task done. The expected-utility framework seems forced to classify the shutdown as a bad event, and thus to predict that a sufficiently capable agent will spend resources to disable the button, hide information from me, or manipulate me away from pressing it. My intuition is firm: this is exactly what corrigibility is supposed to rule out. An agent I can correct or switch off mid-task is not making any kind of mistake by allowing the shutdown, and an otherwise benign agent that fights the switch at the first opportunity is misaligned. Present both sides at their strongest and give me a negotiated verdict. Should the intuition be debunked as anthropomorphic projection, with corrigibility stipulated inside the utility function by ranking button-presses or operator approval above task completion? Should I amend or abandon the expected-utility account, for instance through utility indifference (making the agent exactly indifferent between being shut down and continuing), incomplete preferences (refusing to compare shutdown outcomes with task outcomes), or a shift toward an approval-directed or instruction-following picture of agency? Or is the conflict an artifact, because corrigibility can be written into the content of a utility function in a principled rather than ad hoc way? I want to know what would justify each move, not only which side should win.
