Some friends in my science studies group have been relitigating Feyerabend's *Against Method* for weeks, and its gone nowhere. One side, call them methodists, says science works because it has a recognizable method—hypothesis testing, controlled experiments, peer review, incremental replication—and even when scientists deviate, the deviations are justified by reference to those norms. The other side, call them anarchists after Feyerabend, says the history of science is a graveyard of methodological rules, and the only honest summary is that in actual scientific practice anything goes, not as a prescription but as a description of what has repeatedly been necessary for progress. Each camp keeps accusing the other of changing the subject: one says the other is dodging the question of how science should be done, the other says the first is dodging how science has actually been done.

We need to produce a joint document we could both sign, the kind an adversarial collaboration would produce. Please draft it. It should have a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table. In the table, state each disagreement in wording both sides would accept for their own position, not a caricature. Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one side. For each crux, say what both sides would agree counts as evidence or argument bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. I suspect one crux is whether early modern astronomy is a representative case or a special one, and another is whether the word method should mean an algorithm, a set of norms, or something looser like a set of intellectual virtues. But don't just take my hunches—work out the real cruxes, and be explicit about which parts of the fight are about the descriptive history, which are about the normative ideal, and which are just about the word method itself.
