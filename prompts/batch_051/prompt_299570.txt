I'm in an EU politics course and the chapter on the democratic deficit has a summary that sounds mostly right to me but also a bit off in a way I can't fully articulate. The textbook says something like this:

'The EU is not a state. It is best understood as an intergovernmental organisation in which the member states have pooled some of their sovereignty. The Union itself has no sovereignty of its own, because it cannot change its own powers—only the member states can do that, by amending the treaties. The heart of the problem is the democratic deficit. The Commission is unelected, but it has a near-monopoly on proposing legislation, which means laws are effectively made by unelected officials in Brussels. The European Parliament is directly elected, but it is weak: it cannot propose legislation and it cannot force the Commission to resign. The result is that voters have no way to throw out the people who make EU law, and that is why the project of an ever closer union should be resisted.'

How fair is that summary? Go threw it sentence by sentence and tell me which parts my lecturer would accept, which are misleading, and which are simply wrong. Does the anti-integration conclusion follow from the parts that are accurate?
