I was arguing long into the evening with a friend about Bernard Williams's Makropulos argument for the tedium of immortality, and the discussion left me with the uncomfortable sense that I have already collected most of the standard replies to his central claim. As I understand Williams, he holds that an endless life would eventually become intolerably boring because the categorical desires that give a finite person a reason to go on living—the projects, attachments, and long-term ambitions that make life feel worth continuing—are exhaustible. For any given human character, these desires would at some point be worked through, abandoned, or reduced to mere repetition, and death would come as a relief. On my current list of standard replies are these: the appeal to an inexhaustible supply of new experiences, works of art, and historical developments; the appeal to finite memory and forgetting, so that an immortal life could cycle through pleasures without cumulative boredom; the appeal to gradual character change, so that whatever psychological continuity persists would allow the person to become someone with fresh categorical desires; the appeal to a renewable sequence of complete finite lives within one endless existence, each with its own projects; the appeal to the contingency of boredom as a feature of human psychology rather than a necessary feature of any possible endless life; and the appeal to an inexhaustible object of desire such as God, which some theists deploy against the boredom objection.

I want something that is genuinely different from all of these, not a combination, special case, or restatement of any of them. In particular, I would be interested in a reply that attacks the way Williams identifies a person's reasons for living with categorical desires in the first place, or one that denies that the desire-exhaustion phenomenon transfers from our finite case to the immortal's case. Give me one such reply, commit to a definite assessment of whether it is any good rather than merely surveying options, and explain specifically what it does that the items on my list do not.
