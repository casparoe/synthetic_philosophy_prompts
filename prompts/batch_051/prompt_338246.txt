I have been working through Moritz Schlick's treatment of the meaning of life and keep getting stuck on a problem in the internal structure of the position. Schlick's central move is to dissolve the question rather than to answer it by supplying some external purpose. He distinguishes work, which is activity undertaken for the sake of a further result and is therefore instrumental in character, from play, which is activity carried on for its own sake and in which a person can be wholly absorbed without needing something outside the activity to justify it. A life has meaning, on this view, not because it serves some cosmic or historical end, but insofar as it is composed of such play-like, intrinsically valued activity; and explicitly hunting for the meaning of life is self-defeating, because the hunt turns life into a means.

Here is the difficulty. If play is characterized subjectively — by absorption, by the agent's taking the activity to be its own reward — then any self-contained enthusiasm passes the test alike: the string quartet composer and the person perfecting a video-game speedrun are both playing in the relevant sense. But if Schlick then adds criteria to separate richer play from poorer play, things like creativity, a kind of youthful openness to new forms, or the activity's capacity to keep creating new possibilities of experience, he seems to import an objective standard of value that a subjectivist starting point has not earned. And if he refuses to add such criteria, the view threatens to collapse into the claim that the meaning of life is simply whatever a person happens to love doing, which no longer diagnoses why some lives feel meaningless despite being full of absorbed activity. Please lay out this tension carefully, give the strongest reconstruction of the Schlickian view you think is available, and then say whether you think it can draw a principled line between meaningful play and mere self-contained absorption without already presupposing the distinction it needs. I am interested in the structure of the argument, not a survey of the secondary literature.
