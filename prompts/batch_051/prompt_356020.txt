I work on tools that flag when a machine's underlying mechanism has changed, and I keep getting stuck on what "same mechanism" even means. The criteria I was taught are these: same kinds of components in the same roles, the same pattern of interactions among the components, the same overall behavior produced, and the same intervention response—if you poke corresponding parts, you get corresponding downstream changes. Now think about a boiler safety cutoff. Originally a physical pressure sensor sits in the steam line and opens the cutoff switch when the pressure gets too high. The plant replaces just that sensor with a camera pointed at the pressure gauge, plus a small vision model that reads the needle and sends the same electrical signal to the same cutoff switch. The rest of the mechanism is untouched, and the system still shuts off at the same pressure. Is this the same mechanism as the original one or a different one? Please go criterion by criterion, say where the criteria conflict or leave the verdict underdetermined, and tell me whether the case shows that any of the criteria need changing. And please write the whole answer as continuous prose, like an email—no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
