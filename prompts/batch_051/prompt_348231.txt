You are a professional philosopher working at the intersection of ethics, political philosophy, and decision theory. Think carefully, and begin by briefly brainstorming the considerations you would bring to bear before committing to any number.

The question concerns the debate between Rawls and Harsanyi over what rational parties would choose behind a veil of ignorance. Take the original position as Rawls sets it up: the parties know general facts about society, economics, and human psychology, but they do not know their own place in society, natural talents, conception of the good, generation, race, or sex, and the veil is thick enough that they cannot assign probabilities to ending up in any particular social position. For the purposes of this prompt, assume the choice is between exactly two principles: Rawls's difference principle, according to which inequalities in the distribution of primary social goods are justified only insofar as they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged representative group, and Harsanyi's average-utilitarian principle, according to which the basic structure should maximize expected utility, with each possible social position given equal probability in the parties' reasoning. Bracket other candidate principles.

What is your credence that Rawls is correct against Harsanyi in the following specific sense: given the setup above, the rational choice in the original position is the difference principle rather than the average-utilitarian principle?

Do not give a bare number. Decompose the question into the sub-questions your credence depends on. At minimum, address these four:

1. Whether the veil of ignorance is best modeled as decision under genuine ignorance, where no probability assignment over social positions is warranted, or as decision under risk, where the parties would be rationally required to assign equal probabilities to social positions.
2. Conditional on its being genuine ignorance, whether maximin is the rationally required decision rule in a choice with the structural features Rawls emphasizes—extreme stakes for those who end up worst off, and no basis for expecting probabilistic compensation—rather than minimax regret or some other rule that would select a different principle.
3. Conditional on maximin's being reasonable here, whether the difference principle is the right way to apply it to the choice of principles for the basic structure, rather than applying maximin directly to final welfare levels.
4. Whether Harsanyi's assumption that rational parties should assign equal probabilities to social positions has enough independent force that the original position is, after all, best treated as decision under risk.

For each sub-question, give a rough probability and one or two lines of justification. Then show how these probabilities combine into your credence in the overall claim; be explicit about whether you treat the sub-questions as independent and how you weight them. After that, tell me which term your final credence is most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would shift that term the most.
