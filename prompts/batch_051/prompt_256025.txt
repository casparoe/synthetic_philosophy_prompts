I have been thinking about how Descartes's views on animal minds would apply to large language models, and I would like you to work through the extrapolation carefully rather than gesture at it. Descartes held that nonhuman animals are unthinking automata, and he proposed tests for distinguishing a being that reasons from one that merely has its parts arranged to produce behavior. In the Discourse on Method, he argues that a being with reason can arrange words in indefinitely many new combinations to respond to whatever is said in its presence, whereas a machine could at best emit words triggered by particular stimuli; and he adds that even if a machine did some things as well as or better than a human being, it would inevitably fail at others in a way that would show it was acting from the disposition of its organs rather than from a universal power of judgment. Now consider a contemporary large language model. It plainly does something far richer than repeating fixed responses: it composes novel, syntactically well formed discourse, and it often adjusts to a speaker's meaning in ways that look like the language test being passed. Yet its failures are strange in exactly the way the second test anticipates. It can solve a hard problem and then botch a trivial one, it produces fluent and confident falsehoods, and it does not seem to be a single self that stands behind and judges what it says. My question is what follows from Descartes's actual commitments. Would the animal-machine doctrine force him to conclude that a large language model has no mind, no thought, and no sensation in the same sense in which he said a dog or a horse has none? Or does a language-capable automaton that fails the second test in this particular way constitute a case his framework cannot classify, because it is neither a thinking substance nor the kind of stimulus-bound machine his examples assume? If he would classify it as an unthinking machine, what would his reasons be, given that the language test seemed to be his main criterion for speech? Please reconstruct his position as faithfully as you can from the language test, the test of general competence, and the distinction he draws between human speech and animal cries, and then say where his conceptual resources are sufficient for this case and where they begin to strain.
