For a graduate seminar on the structure of epistemic justification, I want a list of desiderata that a theory must meet to count as an adequate solution to the regress problem, where adequacy is fixed by this purpose: the theory should explain how a finite, fallible inquirer can have justified beliefs at all rather than merely restating the regress. Do not summarize foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism; write the criteria against which all three should be judged. Give exactly five numbered desiderata. Each item should state one requirement, the consideration or case that motivates it, and whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one of the three camps. Make sure the list confronts at least these two constraints, which I suspect pull apart: (i) a theory should not require a subject to occurrently entertain, or even be able to survey, an infinite chain of reasons; and (ii) a theory should not allow the regress to stop at a belief whose justification is arbitrary or dogmatic. I also want the list to keep the basing relation distinct from justification: a belief's being justified by certain reasons should not be conflated with its being held for those reasons. If some subset of the five is jointly unsatisfiable, so that none of the three positions can meet them all, say which subset within the relevant items and say which single requirement does the most to force that result. Format: exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
