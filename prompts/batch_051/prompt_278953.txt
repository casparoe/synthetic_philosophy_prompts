If that is true, then my judgment that I am conscious, and my judgment that epiphenomenalism is true, are caused entirely by physical processes in my brain, not by anything my consciousness did. So why should I trust those judgments? Yet epiphenomenalists give arguments and expect those arguments to change minds, which looks like they are treating their own conscious reasoning as causally powerful.

I suspect I may be conflating conscious experience itself with the thoughts and judgments about it. If epiphenomenalism only says the raw feel is inert, while beliefs and reasoning still do causal work, does my worry collapse—or does a subtler version of it survive?
