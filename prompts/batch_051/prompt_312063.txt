I've been going down an embedded agency rabbit hole and I want a number from you. The idea, in case you haven't seen it: decision theory usually treats a rational agent as standing outside its environment, with a utility function and beliefs defined over a world it isn't part of. I don't much care whether the standard picture is a useful approximation; I'm asking about a stronger claim, namely that it's wrong in a way no local patch fixes (more actions, more outcomes, uncertainty about your own code, drawing a wider boundary around agent-plus-world). So: what's your actual credence in that stronger claim? Then combine, say which term you'd least want to be forced to bet on, and tell me what single argument or observation would move that term most.
