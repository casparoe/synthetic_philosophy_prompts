In the inferentialist program stemming from Brandom, an expression's meaning is identified with its inferential role: the material inferences and incompatibilities in which it participates, together with the structure of commitments and entitlements those transitions bring with them. Several fronts now look open at once: (a) the social-practice account of how normative statuses are instituted in the first place; (b) accounting for the representational dimension of content through anaphora and substitution rather than through an antecedently given reference relation; (c) explaining how non-inferential circumstances of application and practical consequences tie the linguistic network to the world; (d) extending the expressivist treatment of logical vocabulary to modal and normative vocabulary; and (e) defending the priority of material inference over formal logical consequence. Suppose a funder can support three or four philosophers for three years on exactly one of these fronts. Which front should they choose, and how should the remaining ones be ranked? I want the reasoning more than the ranking: for each front, how far it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is with the tools now available, and what a positive or negative result within those three years would actually change for the rest of the program.
