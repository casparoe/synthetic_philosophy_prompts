I work in the office of the Attorney-General of a small parliamentary republic. Our continuity-of-government legislation has just been used for the first time, in circumstances the drafters plainly did not contemplate, and I have been asked to prepare a memorandum.

Section 11 of the Continuity of Government Act provides that, where the President and the Vice-President are unable to exercise the functions of the office of President by reason of death or incapacity, and the Speaker of the House is also unable to exercise them, 'the designated minister shall exercise the functions of the President until a special election is held'; subsection (4) adds that the election 'shall be held within ninety days of the designated minister's taking the oath of office.' The constitutional line of succession ends with the Speaker. The designated minister is a statutory addition: under section 10 the Prime Minister must, each year, name the Cabinet minister whom the security services judge least likely to be targeted, and must require that minister to remain outside the capital during any joint sitting of the legislature.

Four months ago, during the annual joint sitting, the chamber was destroyed by an attack. The President, the Vice-President, the Speaker, and every member of the Cabinet except the designated minister were killed. She — the Minister for Rural Affairs, selected under section 10 precisely because she was the least conspicuous member of the Government — took the oath that evening.

The attack also destroyed the headquarters of the National Electoral Commission and with it the only complete electoral register. The Commission has certified that it cannot compile a lawful register in less than eight months, so no special election could have been held within the ninety days; that period has now expired. The acting President remains in office and has begun to issue decrees on taxation, schooling, and media regulation, saying that, until the electorate can be consulted, someone must govern.

Please write the memorandum. First, does section 11 authorise her to continue in office now that the ninety days have passed without an election, or did her authority lapse when the period expired? Argue that question from the words of the section, from its evident purpose, from what the drafters would have said had they considered an attack that left the electoral machinery itself unable to function for months, and from what a reasonable citizen would have understood; then say which approach should prevail and why. Second, if she may lawfully continue, how much does the continuity rationale permit her to do? This is a question of political ethics as much as law. Is an acting President selected by this route confined to keeping the state running and taking emergency measures, or may she govern as an ordinary President with policy discretion? Draw, where it helps, on the distinction between having authority to hold the office and having authority to govern as if she held a mandate.
