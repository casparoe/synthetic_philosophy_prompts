I've just been put on the appointments committee in my department for the first time, and its got me thinking about how we turn a bunch of individual votes into a single group decision. Our usual method is to vote separately on each criterion—is the candidate's research strong, is their teaching good enough, would they be a decent fit—and then the chair combines the majority view on each one into an overall verdict. But I recently stumbled on the discursive dilemma (sometimes called the doctrinal paradox) and it's made me worried that we're walking straight into it: if we each reason consistently, but we vote on the premises separately, the majority can end up endorsing every premise while rejecting the conclusion those premises actually support.

So here's what I'd really like, in a practical register rather than a literature review: what are the most important implications of the discursive dilemma for how a small committee like mine should make group decisions? I'm especially interested in whether it suggests we should vote on conclusions rather than premises, whether we should just accept that a group isn't a person and stop expecting it to have coherent 'beliefs', and what it implies about things like agenda-setting and who gets to frame the questions. And, this is a bit incidental, but could you answer in British English?
