I'm a science journalist preparing a feature on how scientists and philosophers talk about dispositions — solubility, fragility, pathogenicity, and the like — and before I let my own settled views shape the piece, I want an audit of whether they actually hang together. I have accumulated these commitments over years of reporting and reading, and I can no longer tell where they rub against each other.

(a) Dispositional realism. To say that salt is soluble, that a glass is fragile, or that a virus is pathogenic is to attribute a genuine property to the thing itself, not merely to summarize actual or counterfactual regularities about it.

(b) The simple conditional analysis of dispositions is false. Finks — conditions that would remove a disposition when its trigger occurs — and masks — conditions that would block the manifestation while leaving the disposition in place — show that the bare conditional x would M when S is neither necessary nor sufficient for x's being disposed to M when S.

(c) A refined conditional analysis is nonetheless correct. x is disposed to M when S just in case x would M when S under appropriately specified non-finkish, non-masked conditions.

(d) Dispositions are intrinsic properties. Exact intrinsic duplicates cannot differ in their dispositions, whatever their surroundings.

(e) Dispositions are individuated by their stimulus and manifestation. Fragility just is the disposition to fail structurally when suitably stressed; solubility just is the disposition to dissolve when immersed in a solvent. Same stimulus–manifestation profile, same disposition.

(f) Scientific disposition talk is in good order without settling the categorical-versus-modal question. Whether a disposition ultimately rests on a categorical basis or is irreducibly modal can be left open with no loss to prediction or explanation.

(g) Some dispositions central to biology and medicine are relational through and through. Pathogenicity depends on facts about potential hosts, and toxicity depends on dosage and on the target organism. These are perfectly intelligible dispositional properties, but they cannot be pinned to the microbe or the chemical alone.

(h) Many dispositions are multi-track. A single disposition can be associated with several distinct stimulus types and several distinct manifestation types without thereby splitting into a family of different dispositions. Fragility, for example, remains one disposition even though it can manifest as shattering, cracking, or chipping under different kinds of stress.

Work through this set and tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious. For each genuine tension, say precisely what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to restore joint consistency.

Please end with a single final line of the form \answer{...} containing your ranked list of the genuine tensions, most severe first, with each tension written as the letters of the commitments that conflict, followed by the merely apparent ones after the word apparent.
