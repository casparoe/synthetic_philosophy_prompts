I am preparing a seminar paper on proposed solutions to the frame problem, and before I assess any particular proposal I want the criteria of adequacy worked out on their own. My working picture is this. In the narrow, AI-planning version, an agent must infer that unmentioned facts persist through an action (the cup stays on the table when I open the door) without writing, for every action, an axiom for each fact it leaves unchanged, since the unaffected facts are indefinitely many. I also know the broader philosophical claim that this is not fundamentally a problem of logic but a problem about how a bounded agent knows which of the indefinitely many possible effects and non-effects are relevant enough to represent at all. I would like the desiderata for a solution, keeping both versions in view.

Please give me the requirements list. For each requirement, supply the concrete case or argument that forces it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a demand characteristic of one camp (for example, computational tractability, logical completeness, or descriptive fit with human updating). Then take up these four points.

(1) Situate the qualification problem (the indefinitely many preconditions of an action: a match lights only if it is dry, struck hard enough, in the presence of oxygen, not coated in something, and so on) and the ramification problem (indirect effects: moving a block moves everything stacked on it). Are these part of the frame problem, or separate problems an adequate solution may legitimately bracket?

(2) Address the temporal-projection failing exemplified by the Yale shooting problem: a gun is loaded, a waiting period passes, and then Fred is shot, but simple minimize-change reasoners allow the gun to have silently become unloaded during the wait rather than infer Fred's death. What requirement does this case force, and can it be satisfied jointly with the requirement that the reasoner not need exhaustive knowledge of the world?

(3) Identify which pairs of requirements, if any, are in tension or cannot be jointly satisfied.

(4) Identify the single requirement that does the most work eliminating otherwise attractive candidate solutions, and say briefly which candidate approaches it rules out and why.

I need the list itself more than a scorecard, so keep any discussion of named systems brief and in service of the criteria.
