Write a dialogue between two people who disagree about whether argumentation could be turned into a serious competitive sport, and whether that would make reasoning better or worse. One of them, Mara, is designing a league she calls open-outcome debate: two people are assigned opposite sides of a claim neither of them chose, and points are mainly awarded for moves that normally cost you status in a real argument — restating the strongest objection to your own position, updating in a way judges can verify, and retracting a point while naming the exact consideration that killed it. Her pitch is that an Elo-style rating ladder for arguments would make the score-maximizing move and the truth-tracking move the same thing. The other, Jonas, thinks this makes good reasoning legible enough to be gamed: the people who rise to the top would be performers who fake small concessions, overstate how much they updated, and package a trivial change as a deep one, while the real, unglamourous work — checking sources, sitting with ambiguity, admitting you don't know something — doesn't score. Have them go back and forth at least four times each, responding to each other's actual points, and have each make at least one real concession or sharpening of their view under pressure. Then, in a short postscript, take a side yourself: say which of the two has the stronger case and why, and concede the strongest point from the other side. Don't end with 'it depends.'
