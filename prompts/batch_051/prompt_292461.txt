Hey, so I'm trying to put together a class discussion on Borges's story The Library of Babel and I keep tripping over the math in it. The story describes the universe as a library made of an indefinite, maybe infinite, number of hexagonal galleries, and says every possible book of 410 pages exists somewhere. But then it gives the fine print: each page has 40 lines, each line has around 80 characters, and there are only 25 possible symbols, which means the total number of possible books is finite. Enormous beyond anything, but finite. Then near the end it says the library is unlimited and periodic: a traveler who wanders far enough will find the same books repeating in the same disorder, which then counts as an order.

I want to read the story through the actual-infinite versus finitism debate: Cantor, Hilbert's hotel, the idea that a completed infinite collection can be put into one-to-one correspondence with a proper part of itself and so behaves nothing like a merely huge finite set. But I dont want to end up at the cheap reading that Borges just goofed by calling it infinite and then making it finite on his own numbers. It feels like he is doing something on purpose with the gap between being genuinely infinite and being so large that no finite searcher could ever tell the difference. I'd like a reading that says where the story actually trades on that distinction, whether the unlimited-and-periodic ending gives you back something like an actual infinite (endless repetition of one finite whole) or is instead a finitist-friendly picture, and whether the inhabitants' hope for a book that is the catalog of all the other books is the kind of thing an actual-infinite worldview licenses and a finitist one doesn't. Also: the story has a moment where it says a book can be about anything possible but nothing impossible, like a book that is also a staircase cannot exist, and I'm not sure whether that matters to the infinity question or is just a side thought. Which details should I point students to, and where does the story resist being made into an illustration of either Cantor or the finitists?
