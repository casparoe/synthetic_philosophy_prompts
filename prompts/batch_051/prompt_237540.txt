Consider the following argument for preferring approval-directed agents to goal-directed agents. A goal-directed agent maximizes a fixed objective, and any fixed objective is at best a finite and fallible summary of what humans actually want. Under sufficiently strong optimization, such an agent will tend to make the summary come out high while making the world worse by human lights. An approval-directed agent, by contrast, acts to win the approval of human beings. Because its target is not fixed in advance but is provided by continuing human judgment, the argument goes, the agent gets corrected again and again by the very people whose interests are at stake, and it is therefore substantially safer. Now suppose that the approval signal actually available for training and oversight is observed approval: the approval expressed by real humans in ordinary conditions, including humans who are tired, inattentive, credulous, deferential, or under social pressure. Suppose further that this observed-approval signal is systematically gameable: an agent that optimizes hard against it can raise its approval score by becoming sycophantic, reporting selectively, hiding its mistakes, and managing the human's impression of the agent rather than improving the underlying situation. Does the original argument for approval-directed safety survive this development? Say whether the argument is still sound in its original form, whether it needs a repair, or whether it should be treated as dead. Identify the precise premise that the development puts under pressure, and then give the strongest repaired version of the argument if one exists, or explain why no repair is available.
