A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the zombie argument and walked away with the same evidence, the same premises on the table, and still different bottom lines. Her view is that the real action is at the back end: if a zombie world is conceivable, then the physicalist has to show why conceivability is an unreliable guide to possibility in this particular case, rather than gesturing at other cases like water and H2O. My view is that the action is at the front end: the zombie scenario may only be conceivable in the weak sense that we can entertain the sentence and find no obvious contradiction on a first pass, and I doubt that weak sense of conceivability is strong enough to bear the weight the argument places on it. Since neither of us could say which suppressed premise was actually doing the work, I would like to outsource the diagnosis.

Please give me your own credence that the following proposition is true: a zombie world—a world that is a perfect physical duplicate of the actual world, identical in all physical facts and laws, but in which no creature has any phenomenal experience—is metaphysically possible, meaning a way the world genuinely could have been, not merely logically or conceptually entertainable. I care less about the number itself than about the decomposition. Separate the question into the sub-claims your credence actually depends on; for each, give a rough probability and one or two sentences of justification. At minimum, address: (a) whether the zombie scenario is coherently conceivable in the strong sense the argument requires, rather than merely described without noticing a contradiction; (b) whether, granting that conceivability, conceivability is a reliable guide to metaphysical possibility for claims of this kind, or whether the link between physical and phenomenal facts might be an a posteriori necessity in the way water being H2O is; (c) whether the absence of experience in a full physical duplicate would count as an extra, non-physical fact about the world, or only as a different way the same physical facts could be known or represented; and (d) whatever else you think your final credence turns on.
