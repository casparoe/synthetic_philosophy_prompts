I've drifted into a fairly strict policy over the last few years: I won't seriously engage with unsigned or pseudonymous opinion writing, and I discount newspaper comment sections almost to zero unless someone writes under their real name and clearly has something to lose if they're wrong. My justification is a half-remembered Kierkegaard point about the daily press turning its audience into what he called the public, an abstraction where no particular person has to answer for anything, so there is no one with their judgement actually on the line. My friend says this is just a genetic fallacy: arguments stand or fall on their own, and decent arguments sometimes only get made under a pseudonym. I'm not talking about factual reporting, just op-eds and comments. Is this policy rationally defensible as a standing rule, and if the underlying idea is right but the rule is too blunt, what's the most defensible version of it?
