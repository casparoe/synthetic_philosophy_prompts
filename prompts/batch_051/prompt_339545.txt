I am drafting the statement of positions for a grant proposal on evaluating long-horizon AI assistants, and a collaborator who works on performance measurement in public administration has been pressing me to check whether what I believe about metrics in AI is consistent with what I believe about metrics in human institutions. Before we circulate the draft, I want an independent audit of the following six commitments, each of which currently appears in the text in roughly this form:

(1) Goodhart's law — understood as the claim that once a measure is selected as a target, the behaviors that maximize it tend to decouple from the underlying value it was meant to track — is broadly correct and should make us suspicious of any target metric.

(2) Nonetheless, hospitals, schools, and police departments are often justified in adopting quantitative performance targets, because the realistic alternative, professional discretion without external accountability, predictably fails in worse ways, even though the targets will be gamed at the margins.

(3) The specification gaming observed in reinforcement learning agents — where a system exploits the letter of a reward function to obtain high reward without doing the intended task — is in important respects the same phenomenon as human gaming of institutional metrics.

(4) The right response to specification gaming in AI is to improve the reward specification: add terms, penalize known failure modes, and adversarially test the reward, rather than to abandon reward-based training as a research program.

(5) In the longer term, we should aim to build AI systems that optimize for the human values we actually intend, rather than for any proxy measure of those values.

(6) Yet human values are plural, context-sensitive, and contested in ways that make it impossible to state them in advance as a finite objective function or utility function.

Please work through these systematically. I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be; and then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole set cohere. I am not asking for a survey of Goodhart's law or the alignment literature. I am asking where my own position is pulling against itself.
