My housemates got into it last night and I'm still not sure whose right. Priya has been reading the pragmatists and takes what she says is James's side: a belief is true when it proves itself in experience, when acting on it works and it hangs together with everything else we already accept. Dan takes what he says is Peirce's side: truth is where inquiry would eventually land if it were pushed far enough, and it doesn't depend on what any of us finds useful or consoling right now.

They made it concrete with a historical claim neither of them can settle: whether King Arthur was based on a real person. Priya said that since the evidence will probably never decide it, calling the claim true in the abstract is empty; the only meaningful question is whether believing there was a real Arthur does work for you, like helping you organize the texts or explain why the stories took the shape they did. Dan said that's backwards: there either was such a person or there wasn't, and a useful belief could still be false. Truth, he said, is what the evidence would force any fair inquirer to conclude in the long run.

Priya shot back that the long run is a fantasy, because nobody ever actually completes inquiry and in real life we decide under time pressure on partial evidence. So in practice, she said, Dan evaluates beliefs exactly the way she does; he just adds a metaphysical extra, the final opinion, that does no work except make him feel his beliefs answer to something beyond him. Dan said it's not an extra, it's the difference between being right and being genuinely mistaken. A false belief can be useful for years, he said: take a placebo that works. If truth just is short-run usefulness, you're forced to say the belief this pill cures me is true the week the placebo works and false the week it stops, which is absurd. You have to cash out works in terms of the long run, he said, and once you do, you've adopted my view.

Priya came back that he'd smuggled reality in under the word works by assuming there's a stable long run out there waiting to be found. Dan said she'd given up the only thing that lets two inquirers disagree rather than just hold different useful fictions.

I bailed to the kitchen around then. Can you adjudicate this? I want a point-by-point verdict on their actual arguments: which specific claims hold up, which don't, and what both of them are missing. No need to pick one whole person as the winner unless it really shakes out that way.
