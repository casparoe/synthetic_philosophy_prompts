I’m the managing editor at a social psychology journal, and we’ve got an editorial policy on the books from 2004 that says: “Manuscripts reporting empirical studies will be considered for publication only if the principal result is statistically significant at the .05 level.” That line was written well before preregistration, Registered Reports, and routine Bayesian analyses were a thing. Now we have a registered report in the pipeline that replicates a pretty famous effect, and the p-value came out at .07. The authors also report a Bayes factor of 0.12 in favor of the null, which by most standards is reasonably strong evidence that the effect isn’t there. The plain text seems to say we reject, but a Registered Report is supposed to be judged on the methods, not the results; the null is the news here. My editorial board is split. Please write the short opinion I’ll circulate: work through what the policy requires under the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the 2004 editors probably would have said if they’d been asked about this kind of submission, and what a reasonable author reading our author guidelines today would expect. Then say which approach should control and whether we publish or reject, with an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side.
