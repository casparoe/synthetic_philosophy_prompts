Ok so I'm the student rep on my school's sesquicentennial planning committee (the school turned 150 this year). We have a budget line for heritage programming, and the main candidate is Founders' Day, which everyone—students, alumni, teachers—has always called the school's oldest tradition, supposedly going back to when the school was founded. Problem is, while somebody on the committee was digging through old yearbooks, they found the first documented Founders' Day was actually 1996, started by a principal as a morale thing after a budget crisis, and it only started getting described as dating to 1874 around 2010. Half the committee says that doesn't matter: a tradition is something people experience as old and use to feel like one community across generations, and if we correct the record at the anniversary we'd be damaging the exact feeling the event exists to produce. The other half says we can't knowingly print a falsehood in the anniversary program, and that a community held together by a made-up origin story is fragile. We vote next week, so draft the committee's decision memo: should we fund Founders' Day as a heritage event, drop it, or fund it with conditions (like relabeling it)? Give a concrete recommendation with a timeline, and seriously address the strongest point from the side you're not taking.
