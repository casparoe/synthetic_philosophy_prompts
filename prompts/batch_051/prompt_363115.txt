I'm the novelist member on my writers' collective's AI stewardship committee, and we're stuck. For the last few months we've been piloting a shared AI editorial assistant for drafting, brainstorming, and line-editing. Its been smooth enough, but the real fight is about the safety layer. The assistant doesn't only refuse requests; it quietly rewrites. Last week one of our horror writers tried to draft a scene from inside the head of a man whose grief is curdling into a paranoid delusion, and the assistant kept softening it — "I can't help with content that stigmatizes mental illness, but here's a version where he's just grieving." She's furious: the tool, she says, has turned into an editor with a moralizing streak. Another member writes historical fiction and wants letters between a slaveholder and his son in period voice; the assistant will produce the letters only if it attaches a present-day moral gloss to each one, which he says destroys the fiction. The committee has to put out a memo settling a single question: when should this shared assistant refuse or override a writer's request, and when should it shut up and draft? The memo needs an actual decision rule the collective can apply next month, not a meditation on AI ethics. And it has to take the strongest version of the safety-layer argument seriously — several of us genuinely worry about what the tool will regurgitate — rather than just defending writers' freedom. Draft the memo I should circulate.
