I'm TAing a philosophy of mind class this term, and I keep noticing a double standard in how the phrase "I can conceive of it" gets used as evidence. When we did the zombie argument, the fact that someone can imagine a physically identical world with no conscious experience was treated as a serious challenge to physicalism—not conclusive, but enough to shift the burden onto the physicalist. The students mostly bought it. But the same students rolled their eyes at Anselm in week two: sure, you can conceive of the greatest possible being, but that doesn't mean it exists or even could. And if any of them said backwards time travel is possible because they can vividly picture themselves doing it, everyone would say the vividness of the image is a fact about their psychology, not about the way the world is. Same with perpetual motion: the fact that someone can imagine a machine running forever just shows they aren't holding all the constraints in their head at once.

So there's an asymmetry in how conceivability gets treated. In some contexts—zombies, Mary's room, Descartes's mind–body split—"I can conceive of X" is allowed to do real modal work, or at least to put the other side on the defensive. In others—time travel, perpetual motion, the ontological argument—the exact same move is dismissed as a confusion between imagination and possiblity. My question is whether there's a principled difference between these cases: some feature of the first group that makes conceivability genuine evidence for possibility and its absence in the second group that makes it not evidence. Or is the line just drawn wherever our prior philosophical sympathies happen to be? I'm not asking for a literature review. I want your best shot at the underlying principle, the hardest test case against it, and a verdict on whether we should end up trusting conceivability more than we do, less, or keeping the asymmetry roughly where it is. Also tell me how confident you are in the different parts of your answer, and single out the point you're least sure of.
