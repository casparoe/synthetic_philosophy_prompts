In a recurring discussion about democratic backsliding and stalled reforms, a colleague has been defending a hands-off stance by invoking a secular version of a providential picture of history: the moral universe follows a long arc that bends toward justice, so the present reversal should be treated as local curvature in a trajectory whose overall direction is settled. From this she concludes that the reversal will correct itself and that there is no urgent need to redesign institutions. I want the geometrical analogy between historical moral progress and an arc audited as a piece of argument rather than accepted as a slogan.

First, run the analogy through to the end and produce an explicit correspondence table, assigning each of the following roles in the source to something specific in the historical target: the curve itself, the length of the arc, the direction of bending, the radius of curvature, the coordinate system in which the bend is measured, the material that is bent, and the force producing the curvature.

Then test the mapping. An arc has a single, determinate curvature toward an endpoint that is fixed within an already-given coordinate system; it bends monotonically; and deviations from the curve count as noise rather than changes of direction. Which of these features does the historical target actually possess? Which disanalogies are merely cosmetic, and which ones matter for the conclusion the analogy is being made to support? In particular, attend to whether "toward justice" supplies an independent metric or is read off the endpoint afterwards, and whether the picture can even distinguish a cumulative bend from cycles, drift, or local restoration.

Finally, adjudicate the conclusion. Does the analogy license the inference from a posited long-run trend toward justice to the claim that the current reversal is a transient deviation that the trend itself will correct? Or is that inference imported by the geometry, as if history came with a restoring force and a fixed coordinate system it does not in fact have? If the disanalogies undermine the arc picture, say what remains of the colleague's argument, and state what a better analogy, if any, would be.
