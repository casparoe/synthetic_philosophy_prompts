Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole about why countries like the UK or Sweden or Japan still have a monarch even though they're democracies, and there's this old argument—I think it's from Bagehot—that the monarchy is there to be the emotional part of the state and the goverment is the working part. People need something to feel loyal to, like a crown and a palace and all the ceremony, and if you made an elected president do that job, people would start wanting the president to both run things and be the national symbol, and the system would get muddled. So the king or queen soaks up national feeling and lets the actual politicians be judged on boring stuff like budgets.

But that argument always seemed to assume the monarch could stay kind of remote and above politics, so people could project whatever they wanted onto them. And that's basically gone now. Every royal has social media, there's 24 hour news, every scandal and family fight gets covered everywhere, and people have opinions about them the way they have opinions about celebs. I'm not asking whether monarchies are popular right now or whether we should abolish them—I want to know what happens to the argument itself. Which exact premise does this constant visibility actually break? Can the argument be fixed by saying it never needed mystery, just someone non-political and long-term, or is the whole emotional-lightning-rod idea dead once the king is also just another famous person with embarassing opinions? And if it can be fixed, what does the repaired version have to give up?
