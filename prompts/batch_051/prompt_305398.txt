I have a distinction I've been relying on and want checked. I call an error in an argument locatable when a bounded judge who inspects the argument can, given enough time, point to the specific step that fails; I call it latent when the failure isn't in any single step but in how individually plausible steps combine—what the AI-safety literature calls an obfuscated argument. My thesis is that the proposal to keep superhuman AI systems honest by having them debate before a human judge tacitly presupposes that the errors a dishonest debater needs to hide are locatable. If a debater can build a long chain of locally acceptable moves whose flaw shows up only globally, the judge may see that something is wrong without being able to say where; and I suspect defenses of debate conflate 'the argument has a flaw' with 'the judge can locate the flaw.' Is my distinction coherent, or does it reduce to something more familiar like soundness, transparency, or checkability? Is my diagnosis of what debate presupposes accurate? And what is the strongest reply on behalf of debate—for example, that cross-examination or local justification demands would force latent errors to surface as locatable ones?
