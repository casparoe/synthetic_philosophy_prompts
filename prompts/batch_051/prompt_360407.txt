Take Merleau-Ponty's blind man's cane: with practice the cane stops being an object the man feels and becomes something he feels through, its tip a sensitive zone at the edge of his bodily space. Now vary the tool. A blind person uses a handheld ultrasonic wand, sweeping it side to side like a cane; it vibrates faster as obstacles get nearer. In version A the vibration-to-distance mapping stays fixed for years. In version B a firmware update quietly reshuffles the mapping every few weeks, so the same buzz now means a different distance and the person has to decode it consciously each time. In version C the mapping is fixed, but the sensor sits on the head, beams ahead, and beeps—the person never probes or sweeps, only receives. Does Merleau-Ponty's account predict that A gets incorporated into the body schema like the cane, B doesn't, and C is the tricky one? Pin the difference down in his own terms—habitual body, motor intentionality, sedimentation, the body as a set of capacities—rather than in information-processing terms. And if C did get incorporated after enough exposure, would that put pressure on how motor his notion of motor intentionality has to be, or can he simply redescribe C as a new kind of motor attunement?
