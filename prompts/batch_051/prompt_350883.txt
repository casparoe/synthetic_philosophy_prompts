Last night I had a long argument with a friend about the New Hume reading of causation, and I am left unsure what to make of his central argument. He claims that the New Hume reading cannot even get off the ground because it makes Hume use a phrase that, by his own account, has no content. I think the argument is too quick, but I could not say exactly where it goes wrong. I would be grateful for a careful assessment.

His argument ran as follows.

(1) On Hume's account of ideas, every simple idea is a copy of a corresponding impression.

(2) We have no impression of necessary connection in objects; the only impression connected with necessity is the felt determination of the mind to pass from one idea to another after repeated experience of constant conjunction.

(3) Therefore, on Hume's principles, we have no idea of necessary connection in objects; what we call necessity is merely a feeling internal to the mind.

(4) The New Hume reading says that, for all Hume asserts, there may still be objective necessary connections in objects, and that Hume's skeptical conclusions concern only what we can know or observe.

(5) If a person has no idea corresponding to an expression, then statements using that expression cannot express a genuine possibility; the expression is, for that person, words without assignable content.

(6) So if the New Hume reading is correct, Hume is advancing the possibility of necessary connections in objects while lacking any idea that would give that phrase content.

(7) Therefore the New Hume reading attributes to Hume an incoherent position and should be rejected.

I can see that the argument hangs on something like the principle in step (5), but I am not sure whether step (5) is one Hume would accept, or whether it is ambiguous. Could you assess the argument? In particular: Is it valid as stated? If not, which step fails and why? If the trick is an equivocation, identify the term and the two readings, and say whether the argument goes through on any uniform reading. Is there a version of the New Hume reading that blocks the argument, and at which step would it do so?
