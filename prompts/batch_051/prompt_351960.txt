I help lead an adult discussion group at a nondenominational Protestant church that includes both former Pentecostals and people raised in churches where tongues and prophecy were never mentioned. We are about to start a unit on spiritual gifts, and before the group reads any theologians, I want to give them a sorting exercise that locates people by what they actually believe about whether the sign gifts (tongues, prophecy, healing) continue today, rather than by which label they would claim or reject. Please write a decision tree, built from the logical space rather than from a list of names, that sorts a respondent into a position on the cessationist–continuationist question.

Build the branching questions first, from the combinations of answers those questions allow, and only then place the named positions in that space. Every branch node should be a concrete, case-based question that someone with no theological vocabulary can answer — for example: what the elders should do if a member speaks in tongues in a Sunday service; whether a missionary who feels a strong, specific word of knowledge about a stranger should act on it; whether a vivid dream that predicts a death functions as revelation standing alongside Scripture; whether the church should expect healings through particular people who have a standing healing gift, or only as extraordinary answers to prayer; and whether Paul's rules for prophecy and tongues in 1 Corinthians 12–14 are instructions for the church until Christ returns or a temporary arrangement for the apostolic age. The following positions must appear as leaves, placed wherever your questions put them: hard cessationism (the sign gifts ceased with the apostolic age, leaving Word, sacraments, and prayer as the ordinary means), moderate cessationism (the apostolic sign gifts as normative, expected channels ceased, but God still heals and works miracles directly in answer to prayer), cautious continuationism (all the gifts remain available, but present-day prophecy is fallible, stands under the closed canon, and must be tested), and full charismatic continuationism (the post-Pentecost gifts, including tongues and prophecy, are expected features of ordinary church life, without thereby claiming new binding revelation if that is not what the position entails). Where a logically possible combination of answers has no well-known occupant, mark it unnamed and diagnose in one sentence whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, and give the extra question that would split them.

Format the deliverable as one JSON object. Under a top-level field nodes, give an array of node objects, each with an id, a type of either question or leaf, and — for questions — text, options (each with label and next), or — for leaves — position, definition (one sentence), and awkward_commitment (one sentence on the position's most uncomfortable implication). Include a top-level field unseparated_pairs as an array of objects, each giving the two position names and the separating question.
