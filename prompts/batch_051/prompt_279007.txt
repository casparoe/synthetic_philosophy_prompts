I am a PhD student in transitional justice, and I am constructing a set of coherence problems for an amnesty committee modeled on the South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission's tests of full disclosure, political objective, and proportionality. The committee's precedents so far are these. It granted amnesty to a liberation-movement cadre who bombed an unused rail line at night, causing no casualties, on the basis of full disclosure, a political objective, and proportionate sabotage. It granted amnesty to a low-ranking conscript who shot and wounded an unarmed protester while following a commander's order, accepting a good-faith disclosure and holding that the applicant's own contribution was not disproportionate in light of the subordinate role. It denied amnesty to a local political organizer who killed a rival in a township conflict, despite a full account, because the organizer had taken the victim's property and the killing served private gain rather than a political objective. It denied amnesty to a battalion commander who authorized a reprisal that killed unarmed civilians, holding the act grossly disproportionate even though disclosure was full and a political objective existed.

Now it must rule on an application from a low-ranking paramilitary who participated in a raid on a farmstead planned by a superior: two unarmed occupants were killed, the raid served the unit's political campaign, the applicant has given a good-faith account but has gaps in the sequence of the deaths, and the applicant carried away household goods; the applicant states that the attack was planned by others and that the fatal shots were fired by others.

What should the committee decide if it is to cohere with its own precedents? Identify the rival principles that fit the four earlier rulings, state what each would dictate in this case, identify the closest precedent, give a best-coherence recommendation with reasons, and specify the further case that would most sharply separate the competing principles.
