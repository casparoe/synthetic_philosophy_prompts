I'm putting together a handout for an intro philosophy of mind class during the week we talk about wether large language models actually understand anything. Students get defensive whichever way they lean, so I want something that sorts them by their actual answers to cases rather than by the label they showed up with (the ones who say models are just autocomplete, the ones who say they obviously understand, and so on).

Could you write me a which-position-on-LLM-understanding-do-you-actually-hold quiz with eight questions, each built around a short concrete case and two or three response options. Some cases I'd like used: a person alone in a room following a giant rulebook that maps Chinese symbols to other Chinese symbols so well that outside observers take the room to be a fluent speaker, though the person understands none of it; an octopus in the wild that somehow picks up the statistical pattern of taps on two underwater telegraph cables and starts sending convincing but flat messages before it has ever met a human; a model trained only on next-token prediction that still solves novel one-shot puzzles stated in a language it barely saw during training; and a future model for which we have a complete human-readable transcript of every internal computation while it answers a question. Other cases are up to you, but keep them concrete and do not include any question that just asks the student which label they like.

The scoring side should map patterns of answers onto at least these positions: an eliminativist one that says no amount of text prediction can add up to understanding, so current systems have none of it; a pragmatist one that says behavior is the only evidence we can ever have and these systems already act like understanders so the debate is idle; a functionalist one that says current models have a thin kind of understanding simply by occupying the right role; a right-architecture-but-not-yet position; and a mechanistic one that says the verdict depends on whether we can recover a faithful internal model of the world from the activations, so it is undecided until the interpretability work comes back. Phrase every question and option so a student could answer without knowing these labels, and then tell me which two positions your quiz cant cleanly separate (or, if all five come apart, say which concrete question does the main separating work).
