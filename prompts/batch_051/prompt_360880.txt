I've spent the last couple months defending supervaluationism about vagueness, and I've gotten weirdly good at winning arguments while slowly losing the feeling that the view says anything falsifiable. The position, as I hold it: a vague predicate like 'heap' doesn't have a unique extension; there's a range of acceptable sharpenings ('precisifications') consistent with how we use the word. A sentence is true simpliciter iff it's true on every acceptable sharpening, false simpliciter iff it's false on every one, and neither in borderline cases, where the sharpenings disagree. The payoff is that it keeps classical logic mostly intact, dodges the epistemicist's unknowably sharp boundary, and avoids degrees-of-truth machinery. So far so good.

But I'm starting to suspect the view is just a machine for absorbing counterevidence. Whenever a problem shows up — higher-order vagueness, connectives going non-truth-functional at the edges, the fact that 'this is a heap or this is not a heap' comes out true while neither disjunct does — I catch myself saying "the definiteness operator handles that," or "the metalanguage is vague too," or "that's just the price of keeping classical logic." I need someone to be mean to me about this. What would actually count against supervaluationism? Give me the strongest objections, observations, or formal results that should move me, sorted from decisive to merely awkward, and mark which ones the views defenders would themselves concede are real costs rather than just talking points from rivals. Also flag where the view sticks its neck out: commitments it has that epistemicism or degree theory would deny, and which of those commitments are load-bearing. Then tell me, straight, whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability by all the 'definitely' machinery, and whether that's a genuine problem for a semantic theory or just an occupational hazard of doing semantics for vague language. Where it's tempting to hedge, dont: give me your actual ranking and your actual verdict, and say plainly if you think I should abandon the view.
