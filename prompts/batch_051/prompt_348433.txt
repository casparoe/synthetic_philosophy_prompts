I'd like you to do a systematic translation of the main positions in that debate into my quantitative-genetics vocabulary, the way a translator would, not the way someone reaching for an analogy would.

Please translate at least these claims, one at a time:

(1) Selection approximates the local gradient of mean fitness, so evolution roughly descends a loss surface.
(2) Evolution is a greedy hill-climber without foresight that gets stuck on local optima and can't do credit assignment across genes.
(3) The fitness landscape isn't fixed—organisms construct their niches and change each other's fitnesses—so there's no well-defined objective to descend.
(4) What evolution lacks is momentum, path-independence, and a clean train/test split, which is why it generalizes poorly to novelty.
(5) Evolution's open-ended construction of its own search space, not anything like gradient descent, is the real trick and ML hasn't got it.
