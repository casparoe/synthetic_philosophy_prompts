Consider the following variant of Rawls's original position. In the original, parties choose principles of justice from behind a veil of ignorance, not knowing their eventual place in society. In this variant, the parties no longer choose principles from behind a nearly total veil. Each party simply chooses between two ready-made societies, knowing only that, within whichever one they pick, their own position will be settled by the probabilities stated below. The options are:

A, the American arrangement: a 50 per cent chance of a lifetime income of €20,000 and a 50 per cent chance of €160,000.
B, the European arrangement: a guaranteed lifetime income of €40,000.

Work through the following.

(a) Which arrangement does a maximin chooser select, and which does a risk-neutral expected-income chooser select?

(b) Show carefully that an expected-utility chooser with logarithmic utility over lifetime income still prefers A. Then take a constant-relative-risk-aversion utility, u(x) = x^(1−η)/(1−η) for η ≠ 1 and u(x) = ln x for η = 1, and determine the threshold value of η at which the chooser becomes indifferent between A and B; give the value in closed form.

(c) Keep logarithmic utility and now let p be the probability of drawing the low income €20,000 in A, with the high income €160,000 having probability 1 − p. Show that the logarithmic chooser becomes indifferent at p = 2/3. Explain why the break-even value is that high, and what this indicates about how large the downside risk in A has to become before moderate risk aversion favours B.

(d) Use (a) through (c) to assess how far an original-position argument of this kind really settles the question whether the American or the European attitude toward inequality is more defensible. State what the device must assume about the chooser's risk aversion, and about the size of the worst position, before the European attitude comes out ahead; say whether those assumptions can be independently motivated; and indicate which aspects of the actual American and European attitudes are not captured by treating them simply as different degrees of risk aversion.

Please answer in British English.
