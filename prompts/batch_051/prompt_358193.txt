so in econ class we keep hearing that if two adults agree to a trade and no one's holding a gun to their head, the trade should basically be allowed, since both people couldnt be better off otherwise. i used to think that made sense, but a bunch of cases are messing with me and im trying to figure out if there's one line running through them or if my gut is just noise. I heard the phrase repugnant markets in a video essay once and it's been in the back of my head since.

here are my gut verdicts. paying a guy 30 bucks to stand in a four-hour line for me so i can get concert tickets: fine, that's just buying his time. buying up tickets to a sold-out show and reselling them for triple face value: fine? i honestly can't get mad at it. a store after a hurricane tripling the price of bottled water and batteries: not fine. selling one of my kidneys to a stranger for 50 grand: not fine, even though i can't say why without sounding like i think poor people can't make their own choices. a couple paying a surrogate 30k to carry their baby: fine, maybe even good. buying and selling votes: not fine at all. a hospital letting people pay extra to skip the ER line: not fine. a pharma company paying mostly homeless people 5k to test an experimental drug with risky side effects: i flip on this, but when the person is clearly desperate it feels not fine.

what i want is the principle (or maybe two principles) about corruption, exploitation, desperation, dignity, whatever, that best captures these calls. state it precisely, run it through each case, and if one of my verdicts has to go, tell me which one and why. if you have to pick between a clean principle that overturns one of my calls and a messy one that saves all of them, i'd rather see the clean one. then give me verdicts on two cases i didn't list: a parent secretly paying another family five hundred bucks a week to use their address so their kid can go to a better public school, and someone paying a stranger 300 dollars to cry at a funeral as a professional mourner. i care more about the principle being sharp than about it matching every single thing i said.
