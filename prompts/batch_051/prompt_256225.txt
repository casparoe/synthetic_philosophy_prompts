I'm a philosopher of science who works comfortably in the language of structural causal models: systems of equations, interventions, the do-calculus, and the counterfactuals they license. I'd like you to restate Aristotle's four causes — material, formal, efficient, and final — one by one in that vocabulary, treating a cause as whatever can be represented by a variable, an equation, an intervention, or a counterfactual dependence, and report which translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all. I suspect the material cause will become an exogenous variable or boundary condition, the efficient cause an intervention, the formal cause something like the structural equations themselves rather than a cause of any particular outcome, and the final cause either a goal-state encoded explicitly in the model or simply absent. Test that suspicion honestly rather than making the text fit it. Finally, tell me what the translation failure says about the fate of teleological explanation: once causation is formalized in this way, is the disappearance of final causes a discovery, a stipulation, or an artifact of the formalism — and is there anything in Aristotle's four causes that the modern vocabulary still cannot say?
