Is there a name for this? I keep noticing people talk past each other about whether some bet is smart, because they're secretly using two different ideas of "average outcome." Say someone offers you a coin flip: heads you gain 60%, tails you lose 50%. For a single flip that's a good deal, the average outcome is up 5%. But if you take that bet over and over, betting your whole bankroll each time, your bankroll doesn't actually trend upward—the typical path shrinks. A win and a loss in either order leaves you with 1.6 × 0.5 = 0.8, so the path loses money even though each individual flip looked good. I've noticed the same thing when people argue about the stock market: one side points to a positive average annual return, and the other points out that a 50% drop takes a 100% gain just to get back to even. It seems like one side is adding possible outcomes together and the other is multiplying them across time, and neither notices. Is there an established distinction for this, and if not, can you name and define it? I'm not after just a word—I want to know what exactly the two notions are, where each is the right tool, and what happens in the borderline cases, like getting the bet only a few times or being able to choose how much to stake.
