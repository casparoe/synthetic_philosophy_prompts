I currently hold a view I'll call the Judgment-Action Constraint on Moral Agency. The view says: an entity that is constitutively incapable of acting on its own moral judgments is not a full moral agent. By constitutively incapable I mean that the incapacity is built into the entity's situation rather than being a temporary obstacle: for example, an AI that can evaluate options and produce moral verdicts but whose outputs cannot, as a matter of design, initiate any action; or a human being whose powers of judgment are intact but whose motor and communicative capacities are completely severed, so that nothing they judge ever makes a difference in the world. On my view, such entities may be moral judges, critics, advisors, or spectators, and their assessments may be excellent, but they are not moral agents in the full-blooded sense, because agency requires that an entity's normative take on the world be able to feed into what it does.
