I'm writing an essay about whether a company can be morally blamed, and a friend of mine has a stock move. Whenever I bring up a case — a drug company burying unfavorable trial results, a mining firm ignoring safety complaints until people die — he says "it's nothing personal, it's just business," as if that dissolves the blame question. I'm not sure he's even making one claim, as opposed to three or four stitched together. Take that sentence and lay out the distinct precise things it could be claiming: that a company isn't the kind of entity that can have intentions or emotions in the way blame seems to require, that business decisions operate in a separate normative domain, that since no single person decided on the harm the harm isn't anyone's "personal" doing, that the proper targets are the individuals inside and not the firm, or whatever else you think is really doing the work. For each reading, tell me whether it's trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and say which reading or readings would actually get my friend his conclusion that the company itself shouldn't be blamed. I'd rather have a menu than one interpretation. And since I plan to use this in the essay, tell me how confident you are in each verdict and flag the one you're least sure about.
