I'm a high school student who splits my time between programming and a philosophy reading group, and I keep noticing what might be a real structural similarity between version control and the philosophy of joint action—or what might just be me pattern-matching on a few words. In the Bratman-style account we read, when we act together each of us intends the joint activity itself, and our individual subplans are supposed to mesh: they need not be identical, but they must be compatible enough for the activity to succeed, and we adjust them in light of one another's plans. On Gilbert's more demanding view, participants are jointly committed to the activity, so that someone who walks off halfway can properly be rebuked by the others—and this is what separates genuinely acting together from two people whose separate goals merely happen to overlap.

The version-control picture maps onto this in a way I cannot get out of my head. A Git branch is an individual plan for how the shared codebase—the shared project—should go. Merging branches into main is the subplans being made to mesh; a merge conflict is the system noticing that two subplans do not yet mesh, and it must be resolved before the joint project can go forward. A pull request is one contributor proposing an update to the shared plan and asking others to accept it. Rebasing onto current main is updating your own subplan so it stays compatible with where the group's plan has moved. Forking is declining to continue the joint project and taking a copy off in your own direction instead.

I cannot tell whether this is a genuine identity of structure or just a loose analogy carried by suggestive labels. Where exactly do the two models match, and where do they come apart? Does anything in version control—the mechanics of conflict resolution, the etiquette around review, protected branches, even force-pushes—actually transfer to how we should think about shared intention and joint commitment, or does Git leave out the normativity (the obligations, the standing to complain) that philosophers treat as the point of acting together? And if the parallel were deep, it should predict things that are false of real joint action; which would those be?
