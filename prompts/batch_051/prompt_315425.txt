You are a professional philosopher specializing in value theory, the ethics of automation, and the philosophy of work. Please think carefully, begin by brainstorming the relevant considerations, and then give a precise analysis.

Consider Nozick's experience machine argument. The standard machine offers directly pleasant experiences, and Nozick thinks most of us would refuse it because we want to actually do things, not just have the experience of doing them, and because we want to be a certain kind of person and to be in contact with a deeper reality.

Now vary the case. Imagine a future in which all necessary goods and services are produced by automation, so no one needs to work. The only real jobs left are artificial make-work positions whose outputs no one needs. A new technology — call it the meaningful-work machine — does not merely make you feel happy. It produces a full, sustained simulation of doing demanding, useful work: you experience genuine effort, frustration, collaboration, responsibility, and earned accomplishment. The work and its beneficiaries, however, are simulated. The machine's advocates say it offers the only reliable access to a central component of a good human life that the real economy can no longer provide. Its critics say that Nozick's reality objection applies even more strongly here, because the value of work is precisely supposed to be a real relationship between labor and the world, and the machine converts it into a private experience.

Does Nozick's original argument show that a person in this automated society should refuse the meaningful-work machine? Or does the removal of real work change the weight of the reality objection? Be as precise as you can about which of Nozick's three reasons — actual doing, being a certain kind of person, and contact with a deeper reality — transfers to the modified case, and whether any of them depends on the existence of meaningful real alternatives. Where the two cases diverge, say whether the divergence comes from the content of the simulated experience or from the society outside the machine. Finally, state the condition under which you would change your answer.
