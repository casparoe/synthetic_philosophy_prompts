Draft the joint statement that a committed epistemicist about vagueness and a committed supervaluationist about vagueness would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, focused on the sorites paradox. I want a numbered list of claims they can both sign — I'd expect it to cover at least: the tolerance premise has to be rejected, classical validity is not the main casualty, borderline cases are genuine and not just sloppy stipulation, penumbral connections between vague predicates are real, and a fuzzy-degrees-of-truth account is something neither of them will settle for. Then do a disagreements table, with each row worded so the relevant side would accept its own wording: bivalence, whether there is a sharp cutoff we are simply unable to know versus no fact of the matter at the borderline, what an admissible precisification is and whether that notion is itself vague, and what a semantics is supposed to explain — the logic or the phenomenology of not knowing where the line goes. Also make the statement explicit about the asymmetry each side has to live with: supervaluationism says the tolerance premise is false while no particular instance is the false one, and epistemicism says there is a particular instance where the conditional fails but nobody can know which. Then the cruxes. I suspect the big one is higher-order vagueness: the epistemicist has a margin-for-error story to explain iterated ignorance, while the supervaluationist has to say what admissible means without invoking more of the same. Another is the forced-march sorites, where someone is marched one hair at a time and asked at each step. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part, and if a crux is unfalsifiable on one side, be honest about that. Dont play referee — I want the document itself, not a verdict on whos right.
