I am a member of a five-person workers' cooperative running a small bakery. We operate under an informal agreement: major decisions are made by a vote of all five members and profits are shared equally. For years this held because no one had meaningful leverage over the others. Recently one member bought the lease to our building in his own name and took over our two largest wholesale accounts. When we asked him to honor our old decision procedure, he said, in substance, that fairness never did any real work among us—it was just what dealing with equals felt like—and that now that he holds the cards, what is fair is whatever we can persuade him to grant, which will not be much. I believe he has wronged us, but I struggle to say how, since our agreement was never enforceable and our old fairness did operate only while power was balanced. Is there a coherent way of thinking about fairness on which we have actually been wronged, or were our rights just an artifact of a temporary balance of power?
