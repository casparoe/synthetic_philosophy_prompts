Philosopher of science here, more at home in metaphysics, but I've been dragged onto a project about anomaly detection in complex systems. The engineers keep saying two anomalies have 'the same mechanism' and then can't tell me what that means. So I've coined my own term: two anomalies are 'fingerprint-equivalent' when the same intervention, applied to the same set of variables at the point each anomaly is first detected, produces the same ordered sequence of downstream changes. Example: two pressure-release anomalies in a reactor are fingerprint-equivalent if opening the bypass valve at detection produces the same ordered pattern of downstream sensor changes, and I'd count them as the same mechanism even if they turn out to have different upstream causes. My stronger claim is that being the same mechanism just *is* falling into the same fingerprint-equivalence class — there's no further fact about activities or organisation that settles it. That makes 'same mechanism' relative to a choice of intervention and a choice of variables, but I regard that as a feature, not a bug. Before I preassure-test this on the engineering team, I'd like it checked. Is it coherent? Am I just badly reinventing Woodward's interventionism? Where does it break?
