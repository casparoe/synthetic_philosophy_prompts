In a metaphysics course we had been weighing transworld identity against counterpart theory when a classmate offered the following argument as a quick refutation of the former. I wrote it down afterward, and I still cannot tell whether it is valid or merely too quick:

(1) Assume transworld identity is correct, so that one and the very same individual can exist in more than one possible world.
(2) Then there is a possible world w in which I never learn to swim, and I myself exist in w.
(3) But the individual in w never learns to swim, while I — that very same individual — actually did learn to swim.
(4) Hence one and the same individual both learns to swim and never learns to swim, which is a contradiction.
(5) Therefore transworld identity must be false.

Each step seemed forced on me when I first read it, but the instructor hinted that the argument cheats in how step (3) is phrased, which I did not follow. Is the argument valid? If it is valid, say why the conclusion, however unwelcome, follows. If it is not valid, identify exactly which step fails and restate that step in a form that no longer yields the apparent contradiction, taking care to say what the restated argument does and does not show about transworld identity.
