I've been teaching Glaucon's story of the ring of Gyges in my ethics seminar, and my students keep comparing it to the One Ring in The Lord of the Rings. The comparison strikes me as both natural and misleading, and I'd like a reading that sorts out where it holds and where it breaks. Glaucon's ring is designed as a clean test: it removes the one thing the many think restrains injustice, namely detection and punishment, so that anyone, just or unjust, could act with complete impunity. If the just and the unjust person then converge in behavior, justice is shown to be valued only for its consequences. Tolkien's Ring likewise grants a kind of invisibility, but it is not an inert tool; it carries Sauron's will, bends its bearers toward domination, and even its concealment exposes the wearer to Sauron's eye. I want a close reading of the One Ring through Glaucon's challenge. Does Tolkien run the same experiment, or does he change the crucial variable? Where does the Ring's corrupting power come from—the removal of accountability, the intrinsic pull of domination, or the malevolence of the instrument itself? And what should we conclude about Glaucon's claim from the different fates of Bilbo, Gollum, Boromir, and Frodo? The reading should treat both the narrative and the philosophical argument seriously, and it should be willing to conclude that Tolkien's Ring is the wrong instrument for Glaucon's test if that is where the evidence leads.
