I have been reading about Bayesian decision theory, and I keep encountering the claim that an ideally rational agent should assign a credence to every proposition she can entertain. I have also encountered the claim, however, that a person cannot simultaneously deliberate about whether to do something and assign a probability to her doing it, because the predictive stance toward her own action crowds out the deliberative stance. Why should the proposition that I will perform some act be an exception to the Bayesian requirement? I can assign probabilities to another person's decisions, and I can even say, in a detached way, that I will probably give in and eat the dessert; what is different about the proposition concerning my own action while I am actually deciding? If I cannot assign such a probability, is this not simply a form of introspective ignorance, or an incomplete set of credences, that a better reasoner would correct? What is the right way to think about the relation between assigning a probability to my own choice and making the choice? Please answer in no more than 250 words.
