I spent my working life in spectroscopy, where one learns not to interpret a peak until one has separated signal from artifact. I have lately been applying that habit to word embeddings, and I have arrived at a hypothesis I would like examined rather than applauded.

Let μ be the global centroid of the vocabulary vectors in a static embedding space, and decompose each word vector as v = μ + r·u, where r is the Euclidean distance from μ and u is the residual direction. As I understand it, standard practice treats only direction as meaning: cosine similarity discards r, and many systems normalize vectors to unit length anyway. I want to defend the discarded part. My proposal is that r is not noise but a measure of semantic determinacy: the degree to which a word carries context-independent, concept-fixing content. Function words ('the', 'might') should sit near the centroid; rare, concrete, narrowly denotative words ('amygdala', 'lentil') should sit far out; polysemous or heavily context-dependent words should fall in between, because context must do more of the pinning-down. Direction, on this view, is qualitative sense; the radial component is something like semantic mass or definiteness. Two words with the same residual direction but different radii would mean the same sort of thing with different degrees of definiteness.

This tempts me toward a philosophical conclusion: contrary to the structuralist thesis that a sign's value is exhausted by its differences from other signs, embeddings may preserve a non-relational component of meaning, namely distance from the global mean. But I am aware that my examples may be self-selected and that the centroid itself may be an artifact of corpus and model choices.

I would like an honest assessment. Is the radial component well-defined enough to bear semantic interpretation, or does it collapse under the preprocessing, normalization, and dimensionality choices actual models involve? Is this a rediscovery, under another name, of frequency effects, anisotropy, or something else? Give me the strongest cases in which radius and determinacy come apart. And say whether the philosophical conclusion is licensed, or whether I have mistaken a statistical artifact for a component of meaning — in particular, how I should think about the fact that cosine similarity, which throws r away entirely, works as well as it does.
