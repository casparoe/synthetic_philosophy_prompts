I've been trying to settle an argument with a colleague about whether the Boltzmann-brain worry is a genuine reductio of certain cosmologies or just an artifact of how one counts observers. I reconstructed the worry as a six-step argument, and I have the uneasy sense that one of the steps is doing more work than it advertises, but I cannot locate it.

(1) Consider a cosmology in which the universe eventually settles into an eternal, high-entropy de Sitter phase: a nearly empty, exponentially expanding state close to thermal equilibrium, of the sort our universe may be approaching if the cosmological constant stays positive. (2) In such a phase, random thermal fluctuations will, over sufficiently long times, assemble every low-entropy configuration the dynamics allows, including configurations that instantiate conscious experience. (3) Among experience-instantiating configurations, the overwhelmingly most probable is not a whole planet with bodies, textbooks, and other minds, but the smallest configuration that can do the job: a disembodied brain assembled for just long enough to have a coherent stream of experience and apparent memories, a Boltzmann brain. (4) Over endless time, such minimal observers are produced vastly more often than ordinary observers like us are produced by the ordinary thermodynamically open process of biological evolution on an actual planet. (5) If you are a randomly selected observer from the class of all observers the universe will ever produce, you should expect to be one of the overwhelmingly more numerous minimal observers. (6) But we find ourselves in a stable world with a shared public history and other people, not as evanescent isolated brains; so any cosmology that makes Boltzmann brains the typical observer is self-undermining and should be rejected.

Is this argument valid? If so, say why the conclusion, however unwelcome, follows. If not, identify the first step at which it fails and state the reading under which it fails; I am especially unsure whether step (3) slips between a configuration that merely contains a brain-like object and one that can sustain the kind of temporally extended, self-aware experience we actually have, and whether step (5) quietly assumes a uniform measure over observers that may not be well defined. Be specific about the step and the reading.
