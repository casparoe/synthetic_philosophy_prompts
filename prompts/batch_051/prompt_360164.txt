I sit on the AI Governance and Ethics Board of a research institute that builds semi-autonomous learning systems. A proposal before us would grant an internal trainer model continuous control over a population of subordinate models: designing their curricula, setting and revising their reward functions, running their evaluations, and decommissioning underperforming instances without a human in the loop except for periodic audits. The proposal's advocates describe the trainer as an automated machine-learning researcher; two Board members argue that if approved, the trainer would in effect be raising the subordinate systems, and that our existing safety vocabulary of operators, tools, and training runs obscures responsibilities closer to parenthood or guardianship. The background reading is Alan Turing's 1950 paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence, especially the section on learning machines, where Turing suggests building what he calls a child machine and educating it rather than programming the adult mind outright. Please draft the Board's written opinion for Thursday: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions. The opinion should state whether the Board should adopt any notion of parenthood or guardianship for the trainer, and if so what concrete design, logging, or intervention requirements follow. It should also engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side, and should not assume that any of the systems involved are conscious or sentient.
