I keep seeing "satisficing" used in two different ways: (1) picking the first option that clears a preset threshold, and (2) doing anything reasonable under limited time or information rather than fully optimizing. I want a working definition (or definitions) that handles these cases and tells me which use is better:

(a) a consumer who rents the first apartment with at least three rooms and rent under $1400;
(b) a chess engine that searches every line to depth eight and picks the best move in that truncated tree;
(c) a firm that finds the profit-maximizing plan within an explicitly simplified model of the market;
(d) a traveler who checks ten restaurants and then picks the best of those ten;
(e) a tourist who picks a restaurant by coin flip after glancing at a map.

I'd especially like the defintion to say where (c) and (d) fall, since people seem to disagree about whether they're satisficing or just constrained optimizing. If satisficing comes in degrees, say along which dimensions.
