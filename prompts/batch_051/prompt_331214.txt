I've been reading up on lexically threshold negative utilitarianism and somethings bugging me. The view says ordinary pains and pleasures trade off against each other below some intensity threshold, but once a pain crosses the threshold, no amount of pleasure can outweigh it. But that "no amount" has to be doing one of two jobs, doesn't it? Either it's literally infinite, in which case the view is using two different scales and just denies commensurability above the line (while affirming it below the line, which seems inconsistent). Or it's just a really, really large finite amount, in which case the threshold is just a steep exchange rate and the view is plain utilitarianism with an extra weight. If both readings are wrong, what's the third option—what does the view actually say the pleasure would have to be, numberwise, and is pleasure really commensurable with pain or not?
