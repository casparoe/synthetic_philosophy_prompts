I'm a PhD student in sociology, though I keep drifting into philosophy of social science, and I'm trying to settle on a dissertation-ish direction around whether generational categories like Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z are real kinds or just marketing/period labels that got reified. I keep going back and forth: on one hand there's obviously no biological essence to being a Millennial, and the boundaries are arbitrary (why these cutoffs rather than some others?), but on the other hand the categories clearly structure how people describe themselves, how employers and pollsters sort people, and maybe loop back onto behavior, so they don't feel merely nominal either.

What I'd like help with is an agenda. If you had three years to work on the conceptual side of this — not an empirical survey project, more like philosophy of social science — which open questions would you prioritize and in what order? Some candidates keep occuring to me: (1) whether generational kinds should be analyzed like institutional kinds (real-but-social), like folk kinds, or like something closer to Hacking's looping/interactive kinds; (2) how cohort effects, period effects, and age effects ought to be disentangled conceptually before people run regressions; (3) whether the reification critique of generations is overstated — is "Millennial" more like "teenager" (a real developmental category) or like "middle child" (a label with weak causal grip)?; (4) what normative weight generational membership has, if any — is generational identity a legitimate axis of solidarity or just a distraction from class. I'd much rather you defend an ordering than give a survey: for each question, what makes it upstream of the others, how tractable is it for someone trained mostly in qualitative methods and social theory, and what would count as progress in two or three years. Also happy for you to tell me some of my candidate questions are badly posed and should be dropped.
