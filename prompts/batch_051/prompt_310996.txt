Kant was pretty clear that we have no direct moral duties to non-human animals, since they aren't self-legislating rational beings and so can't be ends in themselves. But he also thought cruelty to animals is wrong as a violation of a duty to ourselves, because it blunts our sympathy and makes us more likely to mistreat human beings. Thats the standard indirect-duty reading. A lot has happened since 1804: factory farming, mass animal experimentation, the modern animal rights movement, cognitive ethology suggesting many animals are far more sophisticated than Kant probably assumed, lab-grown meat, and so on.
