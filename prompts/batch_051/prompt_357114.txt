Construct a thought experiment in which two standard procedures for judgment aggregation—premise-based and conclusion-based voting—come apart in the less familiar direction: the conclusion-based procedure approves, while the premise-based procedure rejects. The committee must have exactly three members; each member must hold individually consistent judgments; the topic must be an ordinary grant-award decision, not a courtroom; and the decisive rule must be that the proposal is funded if either one of two independent criteria is met. After presenting the three members' judgment sets, do the check: state what each procedure votes on, what the majority verdicts are, and why the two procedures yield opposite outcomes. Then isolate the structural feature that does the work—say why the familiar doctrinal-paradox direction (premise-based accepts, conclusion-based rejects) is not possible under this rule, and what would happen if the decisive rule were changed so that both criteria had to be met. Finish with what a defender of premise-based voting would say to resist the conclusion-based outcome in this case.
