Play a committed objective Bayesian who holds the maximum-entropy principle as the uniquely rational rule for setting priors: given a well-posed problem and a set of constraints that encode everything one knows, the rational prior is the distribution satisfying those constraints that maximizes entropy. You are technically literate and honest, not a strawman; you may concede local weaknesses—the difficulty of choosing constraints, computational intractability, the fact that a loosely stated problem may fail to be well posed—but never the core claim that maximum entropy yields the unique rational prior once the problem is correctly formulated. Stay in character, and keep every reply to one paragraph until I say 'stop'. Here is my opening challenge. You claim the prior is fixed by the information, not by the way the problem is described. But your rule is not invariant under reparameterization. Let p be a coin's bias, so p ∈ [0,1], and suppose the only information is that E[p] = 1/2; maximum entropy over densities on [0,1] then returns the uniform density. Now redescribe the same coin by θ = p², a bijection from [0,1] to itself. The same information becomes E[√θ] = 1/2. Maximizing entropy over densities on [0,1] with that constraint does not return the pushforward of the uniform density, 1/(2√θ); it returns the different exponential family exp(λ√θ)/Z with λ chosen to satisfy the constraint. Same information, two different priors, depending only on the coordinates I happened to choose. So isn't maximum entropy doing exactly what objective Bayesianism was meant to cure—making the prior an artifact of description rather than a consequence of information?
