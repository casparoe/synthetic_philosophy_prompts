i've been chewing on the Dover intelligent design trial from 2005 — you know, Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District, decided by Judge John E. Jones III in December 2005. The setup, as I remember it: the Dover, Pennsylvania school board required ninth-grade biology classes to have a short statement read aloud saying evolution is a theory, not fact, and flagging intelligent design as an alternative; Of Pandas and People was placed in classrooms as a reference book. Eleven parents sued, and Jones ruled that intelligent design isn't science — it's religious in origin and effect — and that the policy violated the Establishment Clause. The eight board members up for reelection had already been voted out that November. I know the standard legal and political story; what I want is a Dennettian reading.

By Dennettian I mean the cranes-versus-skyhooks distinction plus greedy reductionism, so we're working from the same definitions: a skyhook is an explanation that does its lifting from above without being supported from below — positing a designer or mind already equipped to do the work, with no account of how it arose. A crane is a process that builds from what's already there, like natural selection pulling complexity up from simpler processes. Greedy reductionism is the bad kind of reduction that skips levels and says a phenomenon is "nothing but" the lower level, flattening the real explanatory work done by higher levels.
