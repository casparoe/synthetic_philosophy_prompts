Every time the subject of automation comes up among my friends, the conversation settles into the same deadlock. One side talks as if a job is just a way of trading hours for money, so that a machine doing the job is an unqualified gain once the income problem is handled. The other side talks as if jobs are where people get purpose, structure, standing, and a place in the world, so that automating meaningful work is a social injury even if the pay is replaced. Each side is right about its favorite examples and misses the other's, and I have come to think the real disagreement is earlier than any prediction about robots or universal basic income: we do not actually share an account of what work is worth, or what a society owes people when work changes. Before I read another essay either celebrating or mourning automation, I want the specification itself. Lay out the desiderata that an adequate theory of the value of work would have to meet if it is going to clarify, or help settle, these arguments.

The list should state which cases and distinctions the theory is required to handle, and the observation behind each requirement. For instance, it seems to me it must separate the several goods work can carry — income, time structure, social contact, status, identity, practiced skill, felt contribution — and explain why they cannot be collapsed into one, since people substitute among them in some situations but not in others. It must distinguish the objective features of a task from the worker's experience of it, because the same job can feel like a calling to one person and degradation to another. It should cover unpaid effort such as caring for a family member; jobs that are respected but produce nothing anyone can point to; the difference between losing a paycheck and losing the rhythm, colleagues, and sense of being expected somewhere; and the two opposite kinds of partial automation, one that removes the skill and leaves the drudgery, another that removes the drudgery and leaves the skill. It also must not contradict the plain fact that people's own reports change, since someone can welcome release from a job and still later describe missing its structure.

For each requirement, give the case that motivates it and mark which requirements are negotiable and which are not. Then tell me which single requirement you think does the most to exclude the popular positions, and why.
