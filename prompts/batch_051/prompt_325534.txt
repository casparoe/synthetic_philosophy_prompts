So I'm applying to one of those small EA research funds and I need a two-page proposal, and I keep going back and forth about how to frame the thing I actually want to work on. The half-formed idea is this: the endless forum argument between "just do the legible marginal thing, like buy a bednet or deworm someone" and "no, you have to change the system" is usually treated as a dispute about expected value or about how much leverage one person realistically has. I suspect the more interesting disagreement is that they are two different kinds of counterfactual. A marginal intervention asks what changes if I alter one action while holding institutions, prices, norms and incentive structures fixed; a systems intervention asks what changes if I alter one of those background parameters, and the two don't obviously live on the same scale—like comparing a better move inside a game to a change in the scoring rule, where the second one might not even have a well-defined expected value in the first games units.
