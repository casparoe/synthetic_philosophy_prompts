You are an expert in decision theory and the philosophy of rationality, equally comfortable with evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, and the family of views built to handle acausal influence, and you prefer to derive theoretical constraints from concrete decision problems rather than from slogans. Think carefully, and before you write anything polished, spend some words brainstorming candidate requirements, including ones you later reject.

I want a specification of what a theory of acausal trade has to get right, before I evaluate any particular proposal. By acausal trade I mean the idea that agents can rationally cooperate or make deals with agents they cannot causally affect, when their choices are nonetheless correlated: because both run the same decision algorithm, because at least one can predict the other, or because each is one instance of an agent-type instantiated across many possible worlds. No signal, contract, or physical consequence passes between them; the trade runs purely on the decision-relevant correlation between choices.

Write the desiderata: the cases a theory must handle correctly, the distinctions it must not collapse, the standard constraints of decision theory it must remain consistent with, and the failure modes it must avoid. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration, and say which requirements are non-negotiable conditions of adequacy and which are merely the preferences of particular camps, where one-boxing in Newcomb's problem or updatelessness might be examples of such preferences rather than constraints everyone must accept. If some subset of the requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, or can be met only by what amounts to a trivial redefinition of the problem, identify that; it is the most valuable possible outcome of this exercise. Then, in a short paragraph each, score evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, and one explicitly acausal-style theory such as an updateless or functional decision theory against the list.

To keep the list honest, make sure it takes an explicit stand on each of the following: Newcomb's problem; the symmetric one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma between agents provably running the same decision procedure; the smoking-lesion and spurious-correlation cases that pure evidential reasoning is accused of mishandling; acausal extortion or blackmail by a would-be counterpart whose power or existence cannot be checked; a predictor who can predict you while you cannot predict it; the question of which level of description or implementation makes two agents count as the same for purposes of the correlation; and trade with a large ensemble of possible counterparts, many of whom will defect. You may conclude that some of these are not genuine desiderata after all, but each must be visibly argued through rather than assumed away.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring of the three theories. Do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the case for each requirement should stand on the thought experiments themselves.
