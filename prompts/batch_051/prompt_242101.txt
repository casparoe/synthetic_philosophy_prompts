Me and my flatmate have been watching an ants nest in our back garden all spring (lockdown habit that got out of hand, don't ask). He's now convinced the colony literally has a mind, not as a metaphor but actually. I think he's pulling a fast one somewhere, but i cant make it stick. Here's his argument more or less as he gave it:

(1) Any system that integrates information across lots of subunits and, out of that, produces coherent goal-directed behaviour as a single whole has a mind.
(2) An ant colony integrates information across lots of subunits — the individual ants, through pheromones and antennal contact — and produces coherent goal-directed behaviour as a single whole: choosing a new nest site, allocating foragers, defending the mound.
(3) Therefore an ant colony has a mind.

His favourite move is to say the ants don't need to know what they're up to, any more than my neurons know that I'm writing this. I keep wanting to say that 'mind' in (1) means one thing — an information-processing system that coordinates behaviour — and in (3) it means something richer, like a subject with experiences. But then he asks what extra ingredient I'm adding, and I don't have an answer, because the nest really does seem to make decisions that no single ant understands.

So, at the risk of giving him ammunition: is the argument valid? If it is, which premise should I attack and why? If it's not valid, say exactly which step fails and what a corrected version of that premise would have to state for the conclusion to follow. If the whole dispute just comes down to whether (1) is true, give me the strongest case for and against (1) and then tell me which side you'd take.
