An impact-focused grant fund adopts the following screening rule to blunt Pascal's-mugging-style proposals. First, any proposal whose estimated success probability is below one in a billion has its expected value computed as usual, but any result above the fund's largest verified ex post payoff from a past grant is reduced to that historical maximum. Second, any reviewer may issue a falsifiability challenge: if no observation within the proposal's first two years would lower the claimed payoff by at least an order of magnitude, the proposal's payoff claim is replaced by the largest payoff the applicant has actually produced in the past, or zero if the applicant has none. Analyze how this regime would behave in practice: what it incentivizes in applicants and reviewers, how a sophisticated applicant would game the cap and the challenge, what second-order effects it has on the fund's portfolio, and whether the failure modes can be fixed without reintroducing the original Pascal's-mugging vulnerability.
