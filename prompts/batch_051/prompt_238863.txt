Write a deadpan parody of an informed-consent form for a university psychology study, roughly 600 words, laid out with the usual sections — Purpose, Procedures, Risks and Benefits, Compensation, Right to Withdraw, Questions. The humor shouldn't be wacky: it should come from the form quietly undermining, one clause at a time, the very conditions that make consent valid. Work in, naturally, things like the actual hypothesis being hidden in a throwaway line about "secondary analyses" while the stated purpose is a decoy; a "future research" catch-all that amounts to broad consent without ever saying what for; pay set high enough that for a broke undergrad it reads as an undue inducement; a clause treating sitting silently in the room as continued consent; withdrawing forfeits credit already earned; and a comprehension check scored so that every possible answer "demonstrates adequate understanding." A bioethicist should be able to check off competence, disclosure/understanding, voluntariness, and revocability as they read, while someone with no philosophy background just finds it unsettlingly familiar. Stay in legalese the whole time, no authorial winking, and aim it at the practice of treating signed forms as a substitute for valid consent, not at any real study or researcher.
