I've been trying to get my views on free riding straight, and I wrote down a list of committments so someone else could pressure-test them. Here they are, roughly:

1. The wrong of free riding is basically about fairness. If I get a benefit that only exists because other people did their part—paid their share, restrained themselves, put in the work—I'm treating those people like chumps and claiming an advantage I didn't earn.

2. But fairness can't generate obligations all on its own. I don't think you owe anything to a cooperative scheme just because it happens to benefit you. You have to have accepted the benefit in some real sense, or the scheme has to be basically just and effectively unavoidable. Otherwise any scheme could impose duties on you by dumping unsought benefits in your lap.

3. Staying home on election day is not a moral failing, and the non-voter isn't free riding on the people who do turn out. The probablility that any one vote decides the election is basically zero, and the non-voter isn't taking anything away from voters.

4. Paying the tax you legally owe is required, even when the tax system is only roughly just and only a vague fraction of what you pay comes back to you as services you personally use. Doing cash work to dodge taxes is wrong.

5. I'm suspicious of 'social parasitism' as an accusation. The adult kid living at home, the houseguest who never chips in, the friend who's always broke when the bill arrives—usually that's fine, or at worst a relationship problem, because nobody is harmed and those relationships get to set their own terms.

6. But dodging a bus fare is wrong, even on a mostly empty bus that would have run anyway, and even if the driver wouldn't have bothered to check.

Go through these and tell me where the set doesn't hang together. For each pair in tension, say how severe the tension is and what the cheapest fix would be; also flag anything that looks like a tension but isn't once you look closer. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
