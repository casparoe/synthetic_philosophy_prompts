Last night I was up way too late arguing with a friend about Weber's Protestant ethic thesis, and he would not let go of the idea that culture is just the exhaust fumes of economic change. His line was that the thesis only survives because defenders quietly swap out "causation" for "elective affinity" the moment anyone presses it, and that there's no way to test it anyway. I tried to give the strongest version I could, but I had to admit I wasn't sure how a sophisticated defender actually handles the hardest objections. I host a small university podcast, and next week I'm interviewing a historical sociologist who defends a neo-Weberian version of the claim that certain Protestant values helped cause modern capitalism. I don't want the interview to just have her restate the thesis for the hundredth time.

Could you draft me eight to ten interview questions that go beyond exposition? I want at least one question each on: (a) the reverse-causality objection — that commercial centers like the Italian city-states were already pretty capitalist before Calvinism, so maybe Protestantism was adopted downstream of economic changes rather than causing them; (b) whether "elective affinity" is doing real causal work or is just a polite way of declining to answer; and (c) the micro-to-macro problem — even if individual Calvinists felt salvation anxiety and channeled it into methodical work, how exactly does that aggregate into a macroeconomic pattern instead of just showing up in a bunch of people's diaries? For each of those, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. Somewhere in the list, also give me a question about what would count as evidence against the thesis, because my friend kept saying the view is unfalsifyable and I want to see if she can make it testable. And maybe throw in a question about the East Asian growth cases — my friend used them as a counterexample, but I suspect a defender would treat them as a functional equivalent, and I'd like to see her spell out what exactly the thesis is claiming about Protestantism specifically.
