I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a long piece about whether "belief" and "desire" are on their way out. The more I read, the more the eliminativist line seems to have the right structure: folk psychology is a stagnant theory that hasn't gotten any more precise in a few thousand years, it sits badly with what neuroscience keeps showing, and the history of science suggests that folk categories which "explain" messy phenomena — phlogiston, caloric, vital forces — don't get absorbed by the new theory so much as discarded. It would be cleaner and more unified to describe people in terms of neural processes and information flow rather than postulating a layer of sentence-like propositional attitudes no one can locate in the brain. The theory has real virtues.

And yet. I'm siting here believing my editor is in Chicago, wanting another coffee, and planning to file this piece by Friday. The claim that there are no such things as beliefs and desires doesn't feel like a subtle revision; it feels like someone trying to convince me I don't have a headache. I don't want a referee call. I want to know what would have to be true for my intuition to be the thing that goes, what would have to be true for the theory to be the thing that gets amended, and whether there's a reading on which both survive — maybe the theory is only denying a bad philosopher's model of belief, not the plain fact that I believe things.
