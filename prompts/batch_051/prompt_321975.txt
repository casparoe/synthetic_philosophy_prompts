Take the role of an expert on anthropic reasoning who's been brought in to run a structured adversarial collaboration between someone comitted to the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and someone comitted to the self-indication assumption (SIA). Think through both views carefully, and do a little brainstorming about where they can actually agree before you draft anything. The deliverable is the document this collaboration should have produced: a numbered agreed-claims section (things both can sign without hedging), then a disagreements table. Don't relitigate every anthropic puzzle; the two of them think the live disagreements come down to three cruxes: (1) the presumptuous philosopher, where SIA looks like it forces huge confidence in a theory merely because that theory predicts vastly more observers, while SSA doesn't; (2) the doomsday argument, which SSA seems to license and SIA seems to dissolve; and (3) reference classes, since SSA people admit their choice of reference class is underspecified, while SIA people still need to say what counts as a possible observer. For each crux, state the exact residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own view, and give at least one concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. God's coin toss (a fair coin where heads gives one observer and tails gives a million) probably earns a spot in here somewhere. Finally, say which parts of the fight you'd judge to be merely verbal.
