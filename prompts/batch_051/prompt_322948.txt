At work I've been arguing with two people about whether the things we find inside neural networks deserve to be called representations at all, or whether we're just projecting labels onto numbers. The first person says a feature is real if we can intervene on it and get the predicted change in the model's output; the second person says that only shows it's a causal cog, not that it's about anything — the way a tree's shadow is caused by the tree without meaning tree. I realized the dispute isn't really about the model, it's about the word representation, and I don't have a definition I'd trust outside a few easy cases. So I want a working definition, and I'd like you to test it against this list.

The clear-in cases, for me: a photograph of a cat; the word dog in the middle of a novel; a paper road map of a city; someone's mental image of their kitchen while cooking. The clear-out cases: a tree's shadow; a puddle. The cases that genuinely confuse me: a mercury thermometer reading 22 degrees; a honeybee's waggle dance in the hive; a unit in a vision model that reliably lights up for cats across photos and cartoons; a hidden-state vector in a transformer after it reads the first sentence of a story; a chess engine's printed evaluation of +2.1; the number in a bank balance field; and the codon AUG in an mRNA sequence. I have a hunch that use or function is doing most of the work in some of these and none of it in others, and that representation might be two different concepts wearing the same word.

Give me a definition — or a small family of definitions, if that's more honest — run it through each case, and tell me where it gives a verdict that makes you want to revise the definition, and where instead I should just bite the bullet. Which of your verdicts is the least secure?
