I've been turning over an immigration policy mechanism and I want someone to actually run it through the wringer. The setup: a country lets new working-age immigrants claim the full welfare state from day one, but each arrival (or their sponsoring employer) has to put up a refundable bond, sized roughly to the average welfare draw of a comparable native over five years. The bond is returned with interest as the immigrant reaches tax-contribution milestones over the next decade; if they never reach them, it's payed into the relevant welfare programs rather than refunded. The pitch is that you get rid of waiting periods while still answering the native worry about people showing up and claiming without ever contributing. I want the mechanism analyzed, not the morality of it: who does this select for among potential immigrants, what does it do to employers and to the politics of welfare among natives, where does it get gamed first, and can the failure modes be fixed without basically recreating a waiting period? And please keep straight what's actually settled about migration and welfare state dynamics versus what's genuinely contested among people who work on it — don't present one side's read as the consensus.
