Write the joint statement that a committed epiphenomenalist about qualia (conscious experiences are caused by brain activity but never themselves cause anything) and a committed interactionist dualist about qualia (conscious experiences themselves make a real causal difference to at least some of what we do or say) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. They both accept, for example, that people make introspective reports like "that hurts" or "I'm seeing red now," that such reports are usually reliable, and that there are neural processes highly correlated with them. But I'd like you to spell out the agreed claims more carefully than that, without making them vacuous. Then give the precise formulation of their remaining disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view. Then identify the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether a true introspective report about qualia requires the qualia themselves to cause the report, or whether a shared neural cause plus the right representational content is enough. Another crux is wether the introspective feeling that my pain caused my report is evidence of real causal efficacy, or just one more item of experience that needs explaining. A third is whether epiphenomenalism makes phenomenal judgments unjustified or merely hard to explain. For each crux, give the concrete thought experiment or case both sides would agree bears on it—for instance, a blindsight patient and a normal subject comparing how they report what's in front of them—and write down what each side would predict about it in advance. If you conclude that part of their dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Format the whole thing as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
