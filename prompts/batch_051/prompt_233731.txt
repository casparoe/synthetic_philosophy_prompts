hey — in my lit theory class we've been going through the anti-intentionalist arguments. wimsatt and beardsley say (roughly) that the author's private plan is neither a good standard for what a poem means, nor even accessible in the right way, because the poem is a public linguistic object; then barthes turns that into the whole death-of-the-author thing. but the argument was built for a world where every text has a flesh-and-blood author whose intentions someone might be tempted to invoke. now you can get a poem generated by a language model, where theres no one in the ordinary sense who meant anything by those sentences: just a prompt, a statistical model, and a training corpus made of millions of people's writing. does the anti-intentionalist argument survive that, or does it become trivially true and stop doing the work it was supposed to do? i'm mostly wondering wich premise of the original wimsatt/beardsley argument this actually pressures, and whether there's a repaired version that still says something true but weaker.
