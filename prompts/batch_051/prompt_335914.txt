I've come around to the view that utility in economics is just a compact description of choice behavior — a number we assign so that chosen-over lines up with gets-a-higher-number — and that it carries no commitment about what's going on in the agent's head. I know the usual consistency axioms (transitivity, completeness, the revealed-preference conditions) are what make the representation possible, and I take that as a point in favor of the deflationary reading. But I want to stress-test this. What would count against it? I'm thinking of things like choice reversals under framing or reordering of options, menu dependence, the fact that welfare economists routinely use utility to say someone is better off, and the whole happiness-economics program. Which of these, if any, should actually move me? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest statement of which ones a committed revealed-preference person would themselves concede count against the view, and whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability. If it has, is that a problem for a claim of this kind, or just what it is to hold a definitional view? Keep it in American English.
