Rawls's treatment of envy in A Theory of Justice rests on a psychological bet: in a well-ordered society, the inequalities the difference principle leaves in place won't generate much envy among the worst off, because those inequalities are publicly justifiable as benefiting them, so they don't have to feel diminished. That's what lets Rawls hold resentment and envy apart—resentment responds to injustice, while envy is a non-moral reaction a just society mostly deflates—with excusable envy reserved for inequalities large or arbitrary enough to threaten self-respect. Now suppose the psychology runs the other way: self-respect is positional all the way down, tracking relative standing even when people fully accept that the difference principle is satisfied and that the advantages of those above them benefit them too. Does Rawls's account survive this, need repair, or is it dead? Say which premise takes the hit and whether the envy/resentment distinction can be repaired rather than scrapped.
