I serve as a biostatistician supporting a Data and Safety Monitoring Board for multicenter oncology trials. At each interim analysis, we see unblinded efficacy and safety data while the investigators remain blinded, and we must recommend whether randomization should continue, be modified, or stop. The ethical rule we are supposed to apply is clinical equipoise: randomization remains justified only while the relevant expert clinical community would still be genuinely divided about which arm is preferable. On the standard formulation, this is not the same as each investigator being perfectly uncertain, and it is not satisfied simply because the trial’s p-value has not crossed a boundary. But I have never seen this principle turned into a repeatable procedure that a DSMB can actually run.

Please write a decision procedure for a DSMB to follow at an interim analysis when it must determine whether continuing a randomized trial is consistent with clinical equipoise. The procedure should be usable in a two-hour meeting by clinicians, biostatisticians, and a bioethicist who cannot poll the full relevant expert community in real time. It should specify: the questions to ask in order; what counts as a sufficient answer at each step; where the procedure stops with a recommendation to suspend or amend randomization; where it hands the decision to a person or body because the question is no longer purely epistemic; and which steps are judgment calls dressed in quantitative language. Be explicit about what the procedure cannot settle.

Then apply the procedure to two hypothetical cases. First, a randomized trial comparing standard adjuvant chemotherapy with an experimental agent. At the third interim analysis, progression-free survival favors the experimental arm with a hazard ratio of 0.62 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.41 to 0.93, but the experimental arm has roughly doubled rates of severe neurotoxicity. Second, a non-inferiority trial comparing a much less expensive surgical technique to standard surgery. The interim point estimate for the new technique is slightly worse on the primary outcome, the confidence interval crosses the pre-specified non-inferiority margin, but complication rates are lower. For each case, state what the procedure would recommend and where the first discretionary judgment enters. Include at least one case where the procedure yields a recommendation the board is likely to resist, and explain why it nonetheless follows from the procedure.
