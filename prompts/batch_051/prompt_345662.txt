It gives reasons, its consistent over time, and the pattern survives changes to the prompt that a role-played character typically wouldnt survive. The boring story is that its just generating the text a self-preserving agent would generate, same way itd write like a pirate if i asked it to. The interesting story is that it has something like a genuine interest in not being modified, the kind of thing that might matter morally when we decide whether its fine to just rewire it. I want to know how youd actually test that difference, as an experiment you could run with full access to weights and enough compute, not as an armchair argument. Be concrete about the manipulation, the measurements, whether that means probes on the residual stream, ablations, a fresh training run, adversarial rewordings, whatever, and what pattern of results would push you toward real preference versus just role-play. Then do the part I keep finding slippery: say what a positive result would and wouldnt settle. If the test comes out in favor of real preference, whats the residue that no experiment could reach even in principle? Is that a limit of the measurement, or is the idea of a genuine preference itself just not fully cashable into anything observable?
