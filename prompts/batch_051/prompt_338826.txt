Alan Turing's 1950 paper on whether machines can think has this weird section where he treats telepathy as a real enough possibility that anyone administering his imitation game would need to shield the interrogator from psychic hints, because the statistical evidence for telepathy struck him as strong. He was clearly a lot more open to ESP than the sort of hard-headed computer scientist you'd expect. My question is what he'd make of the last few decades of parapsychology, where claimed effects keep failing to replicate. The big example is a 2011 paper in a mainstream psychology journal claiming people can apparently detect future random events before they happen, which got a lot of attention and then basically collapsed when other labs tried and failed to reproduce it. Would Turing have looked at that and updated, saying the original evidence was probably just artifacts and selective reporting? Or would he insist those modern failures dont touch the kind of statistical evidence he had in mind? And maybe more importantly: how much of his actual test for machine intelligence depends on taking telepathy seriously at all?
