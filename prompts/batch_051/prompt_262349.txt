I sit on the board of a small foundation that makes grants in pandemic preparedness. One proposal asks for $40,000 to run a campaign that, by its own estimate, has a one-in-ten-thousand chance of preventing an engineered pandemic that would kill several billion people; the applicant argues that the expected value is therefore astronomical and that any funder who understands expected value would approve the grant. When I objected that the probability was a made-up number and that this felt like paying a mugger who offers a minuscule chance of an infinite reward, a fellow board member ended the deliberation with the rationalist slogan "shut up and multiply," and the others treated the question as settled. I suspect that one sentence was doing several distinct jobs at once, and I want to know which of them, if any, actually answers my objection. Please disambiguate the slogan: lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing, and say for each whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false; then say which reading the slogan would need to have in order to settle the Pascal's-mugging worry in this scenario, and whether it does. Give your answer as exactly seven numbered items with no introduction or conclusion. Items one through six should each state, in one or two sentences, one distinct candidate claim the slogan could express and your verdict on it. Item seven should state, in one or two sentences, which of those claims the slogan needs to be making in my scenario and whether it succeeds.
