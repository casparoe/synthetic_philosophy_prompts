In my Tuesday Aristotle section we've been doing phantasia for three weeks — the De Anima chapters plus the bits on memory — and my co-instructor has been making a series of calls about what does and doesnt involve it, which I've been writing down. (If you dont live in this corner of things, phantasia is Aristotle's term for something like imagination or "appearing," the capacity in virtue of which an image can be present to the soul without the thing itself being right there.) Her track record so far:

- Dreaming that you're back on the ferry to your home island: she said yes, that's phantasia — imagery running loose in sleep.
- Simply seeing a white wall in good light: she said no, that's just perception; no phantasia needed.
- Recalling what you ate yesterday by calling up an image of the plate in front of you: she said yes, memory works through a phantasma.
- Holding a warm mug and feeling the heat right now: she said no, that's occurrent perception, not phantasia.
- Weighing whether to take a job in another city by picturing the apartment and the commute: she said yes, she called it deliberative phantasia.

Now I want to pin her down on a case she hasnt ruled on. At sunset, someone looks at the sun and it appears about a foot across, even though the person knows perfectly well the sun is vastly larger. Is that little sun-disc a case of phantasia for her, or is it just fallible but occurrent perception of a present object, which belief then corrects?
