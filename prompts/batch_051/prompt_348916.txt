Two friends of mine, Priya and Sam, got into it at dinner last week about what Priya should plant on her family's single field next season, and neither of them would budge. The setup they both accepted was this: she has to pick between rice and millet. If the season is wet, rice brings in a lot (roughly twenty times what millet brings), and millet brings in a modest but reliable amount. If the season is dry, rice basically fails and they'd have to borrow money to get through the winter, while millet still brings in about the same modest amount. Nobody has any idea whether the season will be wet or dry, and there's no forecast worth trusting either way.

Priya said: plant millet. We can't predict the rain, so don't gamble the farm. Millet puts a floor under us no matter what happens. If we plant rice and it's dry, we're in debt. That's not a risk I'm going to take when we have a safe option right there.

Sam said: you've got it backwards. If we really don't know whether it'll be wet or dry, the reasonable thing is to treat the two as equally likely and go with the better average. Rice's wet years more than make up for the bad ones, so the average favors rice by a lot. You aren't being careful; you're just weighting the bad year as if it's the year we'll get.

Priya said: "equally likely" is made up. We have no evidence for fifty-fifty. If we don't know the odds, the only honest rule is to make the worst outcome as good as we can.

Sam said: fine, stick with worst cases, but you've picked the wrong worst case. What we should minimize is regret. If we plant rice and it's dry, the best we could have done in a dry year was the millet harvest, so that's the regret we'd face. If we plant millet and it's wet, we'd regret missing a rice harvest that's twenty times bigger. The maximum regret from planting rice is smaller than the maximum regret from planting millet. So the wise rule is: minimize how much we'll kick ourselves.

Priya said: but that's perverse. If we plant rice and it's dry, we don't just miss the millet harvest; we're in debt and maybe loosing the field. Your rule counts that as a small miss, while treating "we didn't get rich in a wet year" as the huge regret. Being ruined is bad in itself, not just because it compares badly to something else. A rule that tells us to risk the farm because missing a jackpot would be more regretful is not a rule for actual people.

Sam said: if ruin is really that bad, put a number on it and feed it into the calculation. You might find rice still wins if the wet-year payoff is as big as we said. And your rule has no stopping point either: any bad season, however unlikely, would make you pick the safe option every year until the farm slowly shrinks from never taking a good bet. Regret at least forces us to compare the worst mistakes under ignorance instead of assuming the worst season is coming.

Then it petered out, and they asked me to referee, and I couldn't. Could you go through the exchange step by step, say whether each move is mostly right, mostly wrong, or somewhere in between, and then say who is more right overall?
