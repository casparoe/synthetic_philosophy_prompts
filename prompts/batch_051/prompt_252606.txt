Before I read any more restitution takes, give me the full spec: what would a theory of when a museum or state is entitled to keep and display a contested cultural object actually have to get right? I keep seeing people pick one case — the Benin Bronzes, the Parthenon Marbles — and turn their gut reaction into a general rule ("it was looted," "too much time has passed," "museums preserve things for everyone"). I want the requirements list instead. Include the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't afford to collapse (who took it vs who holds it now, a object's origin vs its current significance, sacred vs merely historical), and the basic legal/historical facts it can't contradict. For each desideratum, give the consideration behind it and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's preference. Then flag any two requirements that look like they can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want to see. And throughout, separate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this; don't present a contested position as the consensus.
