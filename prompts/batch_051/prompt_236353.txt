I'm a novelist, and there's a story I've been trying to write for months but I keep making the central idea go fuzzy, so I want to see what you'd do with it. Write me a short story, maybe a thousand words, that turns on the difference between endurantism and perdurantism about how people persist through time. Here's the gloss: endurantism says a person is a three-dimensional thing wholly present at every moment it exists—same whole object, over and over, just changing properties. Perdurantism says a person is a four-dimensional spacetime worm, and at any given moment only one of its temporal parts is present.

Here's the premise I want. A woman gets a stack of messages from later in her ex-partner's life. Not from a copy or a simulation—they genuinely come from his future. The relationship ended badly and recently, and the man in the messages is changed enough that she cant find the person she left in him at all. On the endurantist picture, the sender is the very same whole object she left, just differently qualified now. On the perdurantist picture, she's being addressed by a later temporal part of a larger worm, and the man she left isn't present in the messages any more than somebody's elbow is present in their knee. Make the emotional stakes hinge on exactly that difference, and dont tell the reader which picture is right. Also no character is allowed to say "endurantism" or "perdurantism" or otherwise explain the view out loud.
