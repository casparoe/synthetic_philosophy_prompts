ok here's one I've been going in circles on. On the one side I'm drawn to a deflationary view of money: wealth is real goods, services and productive capacity; money and liquidity are just claims on those real things, so printing money can't create wealth — it changes the price level and reshuffles who holds the claims. On that view, printing money to build a bridge doesn't conjure a bridge out of nothing; it bids steel, concrete and labour away from whatever else they'd have gone to. Its an elegant view, and it gives one clean answer to every 'just print more' proposal. But I also have an intuition I can't shake: in a slump or a credit freeze, printing money, and the extra liquidity that comes with it, seems to bring idle machines and workers back online and keep otherwise-solvent institutions from collapsing — and that really looks like new output, not just relabelled claims. Even the bridge built at full employment feels like new wealth to me, even if someone else goes without. What I want to know is what each concession would really cost: what would justify writing off my intuition as a real-vs-nominal confusion or an aggregate-demand slip; what would justify amending or abandoning the deflationary theory; and whether there's a reading of 'create' and 'wealth' on which both are tracking something true.
