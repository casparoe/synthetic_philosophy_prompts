I write about food and farm systems for a magazine — mostly the science-and-ideas side of food — and after a year of reporting for an upcoming piece on what people should actually feel guilty about eating, I've ended up holding six commitments that I suspect don't all sit together comfortably. I need an audit of the position as a whole before I put it into print. Here is the set as I'd defend it today.

(a) Suffering is suffering: species, size, and cuteness are not part of the moral calculus, so a field mouse killed by a combine is a real death with the same moral relevance as a cow killed in a slaughterhouse. (b) Industrial chicken and eggs are among the worst things on a normal western plate, and I won't buy meat, dairy, or eggs from those systems. (c) But the crop-death argument against veganism is mostly right, so no one gets a clean conscience just by eating plants; the only honest question is expected suffering per thousand calories, not whether an animal died for your food. (d) On that per-calorie question, I've been won over by pasture-raised beef: one cow with a comparitively decent life and a quick death covers a huge number of meals, whereas chicken means one short miserable life per dinner, and mechanically harvested wheat or soy means shredding mice and ground-nesting birds by the dozen. (e) But I've also read enough about wild animal suffering to think that if you honestly count small short lives, a lush pasture or hedgerowed organic field is itself a moral disaster, because it supports voles and insects and the predators that eat them alive, and most of those lives end badly. So the landscape I call humane in (d) starts to look like a horror-show in (e). (f) And in public, on panels and in print, I still push plant-rich diets, mostly on climate and land-use grounds.

What I want is a tension map, not a summary of the debates. Go through the pairs and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension, which only look like they are from the outside, and how severe each real tension is. Then give me the cheapest fix for each real tension, and say whether there is one revision that would do the most cleanup at once.
