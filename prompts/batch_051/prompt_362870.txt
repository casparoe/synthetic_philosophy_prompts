I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training. I've been sitting in on a Stoic reading group run by a friend who insists she is sticking to the original Epictetus, and she grounds that claim in this passage from the Enchiridion, in Elizabeth Carter's translation:

"Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions."

Her verdicts so far: my judgment that a colleague's proposed simplification was wrong was up to us; whether the team adopted the simplification was not up to us; the desire to keep defending the design after the meeting had moved on was up to us; whether my manager now regards me as difficult to work with was not up to us; the first surge of alarm when our monitors went red was not up to us, but going along with that alarm and dwelling on it all night was up to us.

Next week's case is whether the act of clicking the merge button on an approved pull request, as distinct from whether the merge actually succeeds, is up to us. Predict her verdict, lay out the rule she seems to be running, and identify the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well. Say which of those rules is the better reading of the passage, give your best guess about what she will say and why, and name a further case that would settle which rule she actually holds.
