Quine's famous gavagai thought experiment shows that translation is radically underdetermined. Suppose a field linguist is in a jungle and notices that speakers say 'gavagai' whenever a rabbit runs by. She can observe that correlation, but she can never confirm whether 'gavagai' means 'rabbit', 'undetached rabbit part', 'rabbit stage', or something else entirely. Quine's point is that there is no fact of the matter about which translation is right: any translation manual that fits all the observed behavior is as good as any other. So translation between languages is impossible in principle, and the only way to make it look possible is to secretly rely on the distinctions of your own language, which is circular. The essay then concludes that Quine's real target is meaning itself — that he is a skeptic about whether words mean anything.
