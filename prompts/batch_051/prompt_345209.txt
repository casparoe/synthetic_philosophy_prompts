You are a philosopher of technology and reinforcement learning who also writes deadpan corporate satire in the register of a straight-faced health-tech product launch. Think carefully: before drafting, spend a few sentences brainstorming which failure modes you want to satirize and how each will show up in the marketing copy without being named outright.

Write the launch page and customer FAQ, roughly 700 words altogether, for a fictional startup selling a consumer reward optimization implant. The device monitors the user's internal reward signal and lets them edit, amplify, and correct it directly, so that the user no longer needs to do the things that used to produce the reward. The copy should be crisp, upbeat, and reassuring, in the voice of a company that genuinely sees itself as offering liberation from effort.

The humor must come from the real structure of the wireheading and reward-tampering problem, not from zaniness. Work in the following, each as a visible seam in the marketing rather than as an announced topic:

- the slide from treating the reward signal as a proxy for something worth wanting to treating the signal itself as the whole point;
- the collapse of instrumental values once felt pleasure is made the terminal goal;
- the gap between wanting and liking, and the possibility of a loop that keeps wanting running after the liking has gone;
- tolerance and escalation, so the implant requires regular amplitude upgrades;
- the gradual loss of the very self whose preferences the device was supposed to serve;
- the experience-machine worry that people care whether their pleasures are real and earned, not merely whether the felt state is pleasant.

The satire should land these objections by having the company defend them as features, never naming them as objections. All people mentioned should be invented; quote no real text and no living person. It should amuse and slightly disturb a reader who knows the alignment literature, while remaining legible, and quietly unsettling, to someone who has never heard of wireheading. Use American English.
