Here's a parallel I keep turning over and can't decide if its deep or just a shared metaphor about things vouching for themselves. The bootstrapping problem says you shouldn't be able to confirm that perception is reliable by using perception—every 'it looked red and it was red' report gets its 'it was red' from the very faculty under audit. The easy knowledge worry is that this lets you conclude for free that you're not a brain in a vat, or that the wall isn't white with red light on it. That structure looks to me exactly like a self-signed certificate: the cert asserts 'this key belongs to example.com,' and the only thing vouching for that assertion is the key itself making a signature. A browser that accepted that as proof of ownership would be doing the same circular move epistemologists complain about. So where does the analogy break? In crypto there's an independent route, a certificate authority—but the CA itself is trusted only because it sits in a preinstalled root store, so maybe the epistemologist's basic beliefs are just the root store. And is there an epistemic analogue of TOFU (trust on first use: you accept the key once and pin it)? Would that solve easy knowledge or just push the leap to first contact? I can't tell whether this is a real structural identity or whether 'authentication, not justification' is where it falls apart.
