I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I design database-backed systems, and I think most naturally in terms of tables, rows, views, materialized views, aggregate queries, query plans, and consistency invariants. I have been trying to understand the discursive dilemma from judgment aggregation, and I want you to translate it into that vocabulary rather than explain it to me in philosophical terms.

Here is the puzzle as I understand it. A three-member panel must decide a legal question. Liability, R, follows from two premises: P, that the contract was breached, and Q, that the contract was valid; so R is P AND Q. The judges vote as follows:

- Judge 1: P true, Q true, hence R true.
- Judge 2: P true, Q false, hence R false.
- Judge 3: P false, Q true, hence R false.

A majority accepts P, and a majority accepts Q, so aggregating the premises first commits the panel to liability. But a majority of judges individually rejects liability. The two ways of aggregating come apart. There is also a standing dispute between premise-based and conclusion-based procedures, and a family of impossibility results to the effect that no aggregation rule can satisfy several plausible conditions at once.

Restate this debate in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Treat each judge as a row, the premises as base columns, the conclusion as a view defined over those columns, majority voting as an aggregate query, the premise-based procedure as a query that aggregates the base columns first and then applies the view, and the conclusion-based procedure as a query that applies the view to each row first and then aggregates. I am not asking for a loose analogy; I want a systematic mapping, and I care as much about where it breaks as where it holds.

Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which parts did you have to distort to fit? Which parts have no expression in database vocabulary at all? In particular, I want to know whether the philosophical disagreement about how a group ought to aggregate its judgments is just a disagreement about where in a query plan to materialize a view, or whether my vocabulary is missing something that matters.
