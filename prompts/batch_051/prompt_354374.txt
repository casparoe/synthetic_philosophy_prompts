You are a professional philosopher of art who works on the paradox of tragedy, and you are good at distinguishing genuinely different explanatory strategies from mere terminological variants. I spent a long evening arguing with a friend about why people deliberately seek out artworks that make them sad, and by the end we had assembled the following list of explanatory strategies:

(1) Catharsis: tragedy arouses pity and fear and then discharges or works them through, and that discharge is itself satisfying.

(2) Conversion: the craft of the work—plot, verse, composition, performance—turns painful material into an object of pleasure.

(3) Control: the fictional frame lets us undergo negative emotion from a position of safety and mastery, and the exercise of that mastery is itself agreeable.

(4) Reappraisal: the sadness is real, but we interpret it as part of a chosen, worthwhile experience, and that interpretation changes how it feels.

(5) Make-believe: our emotional responses to fictional characters are not full-fledged emotions tied to belief, so they lack the painful sting of their real counterparts.

(6) Content-value: tragedy earns its pain by giving us insight, understanding, or moral reflection whose value outweighs the unpleasantness.

(7) My friend's suggestion: sad art provides one of the few socially licensed spaces in which we can acknowledge and examine grief, despair, and disappointment instead of performing cheerfulness.

We had the impression that the first six cover the standard families of explanation, and the seventh was the only nonstandard one we could generate. I would like you to do better. Before answering, brainstorm several candidates and discard any that are only rewording, special cases, or combinations of items on the list. Then give me one explanatory strategy that is genuinely different: it should identify another psychological mechanism or another source of value than any of the seven. State it precisely, explain how it differs from the items it most closely resembles, and say whether you think it is any good.
