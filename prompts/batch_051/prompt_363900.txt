Galen Strawson's Basic Argument is often presented as a problem for determinism. In *Freedom and Belief*, Strawson writes: "There is no such thing as free will. There is a fundamental sense of the word 'free' in which this is incontrovertibly true; and this has been known for a long time. There are plenty of senses of the word 'free' in which it is false. But the sense in which it is true seems to be the one that matters most to most people." If I'm reading this correctly, the argument is meant to show that no one can be truly responsible even if determinism is false, because you can't be the ultimate cause of your own character. But doesn't this just assume that 'ultimate' self-creation is required for responsibility? Why should a libertarian accept that requirement rather than holding that an undetermined free choice can ground responsibility without needing to be caused by a prior self? I'm inclined to think Strawson has simply built a deterministic picture into the word 'character,' and that his conclusion doesn't touch the libertarian at all.
