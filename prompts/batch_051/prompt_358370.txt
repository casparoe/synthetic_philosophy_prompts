I got way into bouldering this year and it's made me weirdly invested in the enactivism-vs-representationalism debate, which I realize is a strange hobby crossover. A friend dropped this argument in a group chat and I keep going back and forth on whether its airtight:

(1) Representationalism says a cognitive system gets around by building and manipulating internal models of the world.
(2) In skilled coping—hitting a dyno, parking while mid-conversation, returning a serve—the agent responds to affordances like the climbable hold or the gap in traffic without first recovering the world from an internal model.
(3) Responding directly to an affordance just isn't a case of building and manipulating an internal model.
(4) Skilled coping is cognition: it calls on perception, memory, anticipation, and learning.
(5) So at least some cognition is non-representational.
(6) Therefore representationalism, as a general account of cognition, is false.

He thinks step 2 is the load-bearing weak point, but I suspect step 3 quietly shifts what "directly" means and that's where the argument stands or falls. Which of us is right? If the argument fails, say exactly which step fails and what a fixed version would say; if it goes through, say why the representationalist has to accept it. And be explicit about how confident you are in your answer, and which premise or step you're least sure about.
