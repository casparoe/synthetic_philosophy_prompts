I had a long argument last night with a friend about whether a person who has been brought up with no concern for others can be said to have reason not to humiliate a subordinate for sport. I came away with a list of six things I found myself unwilling to give up, and I would like to know whether they can all be held together. Here they are, as carefully as I can put them.

1. Reasons must be capable of moving the person whose reasons they are. A consideration counts as a reason for her only if there is a sound deliberative route from what she already cares about to her being moved to act on it.

2. I cannot attach a clear sense to the sentence, "She has a reason to do it, but no amount of sound deliberation starting from her present concerns could bring her to do it." As a claim about her reasons, that seems to me not a thought but a noise — at best it is a report of what we would have reason to want her to do.

3. Yet sound deliberation is not merely instrumental. Imagining the lives of others from the inside, noticing relevant similarities, and reflecting on the passage of time can genuinely create new concerns rather than merely reveal means to old ones. An agent need not already want the end, in however attenuated a form, for a deliberative route to exist.

4. Not every change of mind counts as sound deliberation. Brainwashing, peer pressure, and bare exhortation do not show that the person had the reason all along. There has to be a real difference between coming to see that one has a reason and being altered so that one now has a reason.

5. Nevertheless, there are actions any minimally decent person has reason to refrain from — torturing the innocent for amusement, humiliating a subordinate for sport. These remain reasons for her even when no flawless deliberation from her present motives would ever reach them. We may say, flatly, that she has reason not to do them.

6. And we are entitled to hold her answerable for ignoring them in a way we would not hold someone answerable who could not have done otherwise. Her inability to care is not like a phobia or a delusion; it is a failure of attention and imagination, and that failure is what makes blame appropriate.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each pair in tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would be, and then tell me which single revision would do the most to clean the whole set up. Where a tension looks genuine to a first reading but dissolves under a careful one, I would like that noted as well.
