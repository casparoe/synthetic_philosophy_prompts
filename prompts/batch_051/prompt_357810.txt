i have a rule for reading history: i won't accept an explanation like 'Britain industrialized because of coal' unless the person can pass a contrast test — they should be able to say why the same factor didn't produce the same outcome somewhere else it was present, or what that somewhere else lacked that mattered. last week my friend explained the industrial revolution in terms of coal and a certain political culture. i asked why coal didn't do the same for parts of China that had it, and he said i was changing the question: he was answering 'why Britain,' not 'why Britain rather than China.' maybe he's right that these are different questions, but without the contrast the explanation felt like it could fit anything. is my rule defensible? what's the strongest version of it, and when does it stop being a guard against just-so stories and become an unreasonable demand that history work like a controlled experiment?
