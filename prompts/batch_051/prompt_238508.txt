I am a researcher in causal inference, fluent in structural causal models, potential outcomes, and the distinction between interventional and counterfactual queries; I have only a passing acquaintance with scholastic theology. Restate the Molinist doctrine of middle knowledge in that vocabulary. The doctrine holds that prior to creation God knew, for every possible free creature and every possible circumstance, what that creature would freely choose, and that God used these truths in deciding which world to actualize; freely here is meant in the libertarian sense, so that in the same prior circumstances the creature could have chosen otherwise. Translate the machinery piece by piece: the possible worlds, the circumstances, the creature, and above all the counterfactual of creaturely freedom. Then give the residue report: which elements translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (for example, by treating a free choice as a variable whose value is fixed by an equation and background variables), and which have no expression in the framework at all. Conclude by saying whether the residue shows that the causal framework is missing a resource needed to state the doctrine, or that the doctrine's notion of a counterfactual of freedom does not survive close translation.
