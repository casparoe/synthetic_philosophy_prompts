I've been trying to make the "lexical threshold" version of negative utilitarianism precise, and I've ended up with a position I don't entirely trust. As I understand that view, there is some level of suffering — extreme, inescapable, or degrading — whose prevention lexically outranks every other consideration: no amount of happiness, achievement, beauty, or relief of lesser suffering can compensate for allowing it. What the view usually leaves vague is how things work below the threshold. My proposal, which I've been calling the two-tier ledger, is an attempt to fill that gap.

Below the threshold, pain and pleasure are fully fungible. They enter a single account in which a unit of mild pain is exactly canceled by a unit of mild pleasure, with the usual summing across times, people, and kinds. But the account never crosses the threshold. At or above the threshold, the currency changes: an above-threshold pain gets a veto rather than a very large weight, and no accumulation of below-threshold pleasures can justify permitting it. The veto also outranks the prevention of any number of below-threshold pains. So a stubbed toe can be traded against a good meal, but a migraine that crosses my line cannot be traded against any number of good meals, or against the relief of any number of other people's stubbed toes.

I can see the appeal — it stops the view from being fanatical in one direction while keeping the special claim of the very worst suffering. But I want an honest assessment. Is the two-tier structure coherent, or does it smuggle in a contradiction by quantifying pain and pleasure below the line and then refusing to place threshold-crossing pain on that same scale? Does the threshold have to be arbitrary, or can it be fixed by a real feature of experience or agency? Where does it break first — in ignoring how many mild pains accumulate, in across-person cases where one person's severe pain vetoes everyone else's mild goods, or in the discontinuity between the last unit just below the line and the first just above it? Reconstruct the position in its strongest form if you think it's defensible, tell me whether it already exists under another name, and give me the three strongest objections, with a verdict on whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable.
