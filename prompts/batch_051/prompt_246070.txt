My friend keeps insisting that determinism and predictability are totally different things — that a system can be fully deterministic and yet unpredictable beyond some horizon, and that whether we can forecast a process tells us nothing about whether its deterministic. I've started wondering if that claim is actually testable, or whether it's the kind of thing that gets defined into truth. Here's the case I keep getting stuck on. Suppose you're handed a long record of some irregular, fluctuating quantity — say, a sensor trace from a churning fluid — and you're told it was generated by one of two things: (a) a low-dimensional deterministic system with sensitive dependence on initial conditions, or (b) a genuinely stochastic process driven by noise. How would you actually test which of (a) or (b) produced the record? I'd like a concrete procedure, what result would count as evidence for each diagnosis, and then the harder part: what a positive result for determinism would and wouldn't settle. E.g., if the data looks chaotic by your preferred measure, does that rule out a noise-driven process? And if the series is unpredictable in practice, does that support (b) at all, or is my friend right that predictability is just the wrong question? What, if anything, is left over that no test could ever reach — maybe because deterministic-chaos vs. stochastic is partly a modelling choice rather than a fact about the world? Sell me on where the line between testable and untestable actually sits here.
