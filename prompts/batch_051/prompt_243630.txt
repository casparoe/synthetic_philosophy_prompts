I have spent years reading the AI alignment literature, and I keep noticing that two ideas are invoked together so often that they start to feel like a single package: the orthogonality thesis and the instrumental convergence thesis. I want them pulled apart and compared on their own merits, because I suspect they do different logical work and that some arguments in the field would survive without one of them but not the other.

The orthogonality thesis, as I understand it, says that an agent's level of intelligence or capability is independent of its final goals: in principle, any level of capability can be paired with any final goal. The instrumental convergence thesis says that a wide range of final goals, across a wide range of capable agents, will tend to produce similar instrumental subgoals, such as self-preservation, resource acquisition, and goal-content integrity. I want a comparison that is precise about the quantifiers and does not let the two theses borrow support from each other by mere association.

3. **Load-bearing assumptions** (roughly 300 words). Identify the hidden assumptions each thesis needs: for example, assumptions about goal-content integrity, about the agent's world model and its ability to plan, about what distinguishes a final goal from an instrumental one, and about the relationship between capability and goal-directedness.
