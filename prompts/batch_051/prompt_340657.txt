I'm the policy person in a county prosecutors office in a state that just passed a ballot initiative decriminalizing possession of small amounts of all drugs for personal use, roughly Portugal-style: a civil citation plus a health referral, no criminal charge. Sale and manufacture stay illegal, and cannabis got legalized separately with its own retail rules. My job is to write the charge-screening procedure deputies will run on the few hundred possession cases a month that still land on their desks; each deputy has maybe 15 minutes and a file, and has to decide whether to charge, divert, or decline. I don't want a memo about the initiative, I want the actual harm-reduction logic translated into a reusable checklist: the cost of a record, the effect on use, what keeps the black market fed, how much the drug and the quantity should matter. It has to work for a deputy who hasn't read any policy literature. Include three worked cases, one where the procedure says to decline something the local paper will run a scary headline about, and mark the places where judgment can't be eliminated.
