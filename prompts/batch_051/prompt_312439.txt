A friend and I spent an evening arguing about whether letters have essences, and he left me with a skeptical argument I cannot shake. He claimed that there is no fact of the matter about whether a mark is the letter 'a' — that letter identity is only a family-resemblance convenience we project onto strokes. He laid the argument out like this:

(1) If it is objectively true that a mark is an 'a', then some criterion determines this non-arbitrarily: a shape property, a causal history, a classification by competent readers, or something of that kind.
(2) No shape property is shared by all and only 'a's. The lowercase form already varies between the printed two-storey shape and the handwritten one-storey shape, and including the uppercase 'A' removes any shared contour; any shape-description broad enough to cover all accepted cases also admits 'd', 'o', 'q', or '@', while any description strict enough to exclude them leaves out clear cases.
(3) Causal-history and intention criteria are circular, since produced as an 'a' or intended as an 'a' already contains the very category to be explained.
(4) Classification by competent readers fares no better: competent readers are, in effect, readers who already track 'a'-hood, so the criterion presupposes what it is meant to fix; without that presupposition, we are left only with brute perceptual dispositions, which do not by themselves constitute a standard of correctness or error.
(5) Therefore no non-circular criterion for being an 'a' exists, and there is no fact of the matter about whether any contested mark is an 'a'.
(6) If that is right for letters, the same reasoning reaches words: someone who reads a carelessly written 'cat' as 'cut' is not wrong but is only grouping the strokes differently.
(7) But that is absurd. Competent readers can be flatly wrong about what letter a mark is, and a written word is not just a cloud of strokes awaiting arbitrary grouping.

I would much rather keep (7) than accept (5), but I cannot see which earlier step to reject; every alternative I try seems either to smuggle in a hidden shape-list or to appeal circularly to what we already know 'a's are. Which step goes wrong, and what does rejecting it commit me to?
