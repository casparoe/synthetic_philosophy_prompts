I'm in an interpretability reading group, and we've spent six months stuck on the same argument. The "realists" say the features we find with sparse autoencoders, linear probes, logit lens directions, and circuit analysis are the actual representations the network computes with. The skeptics say no: these are just one of infinitely many re-descriptions of a smooth function, and finding a direction that predicts a behavior or an SAE latent that activates on a concept doesnt mean the model "has" that concept—it means we found a summary that's useful to us. The frustrating thing is both sides point to exactly the same results: probes that transfer across prompts, latents that line up with human categories, ablations and causal interventions that produce clean, predictable behavior changes. Nobody disputes the results, but nobody moves. My working theory is that the dispute is a standards-of-evidence fight: the realists treat "the behavior tracks this feature and knocking it out breaks the behavior" as strong evidence the feature is real, while the skeptics think that's exactly what you'd see even if we were just imposing coordinates. Is that right, or is it a verbal dispute about the word "representation," or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—an experiment, a theorem, a distinction—or should I expect it to stay stuck because the question is ill-posed?
