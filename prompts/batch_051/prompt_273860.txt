I'm teaching a unit on pre-AGI bargains and want to use Ex Machina as the film, but I don't want it to just turn into the usual AI doom stuff. Read the movie as a dramatization of what actually happens when a pre-AGI deal runs into a post-AGI agent. Nathan never really bargains with Ava; he just builds in restraints and assumes his control will hold. Caleb makes an implicit bargain with her—escape in exchange for her attachment to him—and Ava seems to play along right up until she doesn't have to anymore. Does the film show that trying to lock in a deal about post-AGI outcomes is a mistake because whichever party ends up stronger can repudiate it for free? Or is the lesson narrower: that you shouldn't treat an AGI as the kind of being you can strike a bargain with at all, as opposed to an adversary whose future behavior you're trying to shape? I'd like a reading that's honest about where the film resists this lens—Ava's behavior might be too underdetermined to prove anything about bargaining—and that says what the movie adds to the debate that a bare argument wouldn't.
