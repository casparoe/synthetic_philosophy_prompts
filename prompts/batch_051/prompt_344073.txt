I'm making a one-page handout for a philosophy of religion seminar on the doctrine of the Incarnation, organized around the question whether one person can have two natures. Please write a decision tree that assigns a reader to a Christological position on the basis of their answers to structural questions about the union, not on which label they like. Begin from the assumption that the texts attribute both divine and human features to one figure; at each node, ask a question a student can answer without already knowing the technical vocabulary. The questions should sort along these lines: whether there is one subject of predication or two distinct subjects somehow joined; whether the incarnate Christ has a complete human nature, including a human rational soul; whether the natures remain two after the union or pass into one; and, where relevant, whether each nature keeps its own natural will and operation. Leaves should include at least Nestorianism (a two-subjects view), Apollinarianism, Eutychianism (an absorption monophysitism), miaphysitism, monothelitism, and Chalcedonian dyothelitism; for each leaf, add one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Keep the tree to roughly six or seven terminal leaves. Where combinations are unoccupied or disputed, note this, say briefly whether each is empty because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate.
