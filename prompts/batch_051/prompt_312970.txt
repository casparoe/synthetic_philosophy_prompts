I got into a long argument last night with a friend about embryo selection. He kept insisting that the package of views I was defending could not be held together — that I was invoking a distinction I could not defend and quietly backing away from my own principle when its implications became uncomfortable. I don't think he hit on the right diagnosis, but the argument left me unsure enough to write everything down.

Here is where I have landed. (1) Procreative beneficence is right at least as a pro tanto matter: parents who are already choosing among several viable embryos have a genuine moral reason to select the one whose life can reasonably be expected to go best, on the information available at the time. (2) In cases of serious, early-onset genetic disease, this reason is decisive: other things equal, it would be wrong to transfer an embryo known to carry such a condition when a healthy sibling embryo is available. (3) Where the differences concern non-disease traits — height, temperament, cognitive potential — the same welfare-maximizing logic applies only weakly: selecting for such traits is permissible, but generally not required, and parents may ignore those differences for any reason or none. (4) Whatever the underlying moral facts, the final reproductive decision should remain legally with the parents; the state should not prohibit, mandate, or penalize particular embryo selections, though it may fund nondirective counseling and screening for serious disease. (5) Traits such as deafness are not intrinsically bad, and a large part of the welfare disadvantage associated with disability arises from current social arrangements; a just society would compress, though not eliminate, those gaps. (6) Selecting against a trait — whether privately or through a public screening program — does not by itself express contempt for, or downgrade the equal standing of, people who live with that trait; the expressivist objection mistakes a judgment about a prospective child's expected welfare for a judgment about existing persons.

I honestly cannot tell whether this is a stable view, whether some of these commitments are in real tension while others only look that way, or whether one of them has to go. Commit to a diagnostic judgment about where the genuine fault lines are, and say how confident you are in it.
