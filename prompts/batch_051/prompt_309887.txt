A colleague of mine, who takes a broadly Hofstadterian line, insists that neural machine translation systems are shallow in a principled sense: they have no grasp of what the source sentence means, only a fluent facility for recombining statistical patterns absorbed from their training corpora. Another colleague counters that a system that reliably renders novel, never-before-seen sentences from one language into another must have acquired something more than rote association, and that denying this amounts to moving the goalposts on the word "understand." I am caught between them. The disagreement sounds conceptual, but it seems as though it should have empirical traction: if one side is right, there ought to be some observable difference between a system that merely pattern-matches and a system that represents meaning, however thin or partial that representation is.
