I'm on the safety review board at a foundation-model lab, and I keep going back and forth between two worries. There's the alignment tax (the direct cost our own safety work imposes: delays, red-teaming, fine-tuning, benchmark hits), and then there's the competitiveness constraint (the fact that if we pay that tax and less careful rivals dont, the market rewards them). Now there's a concrete case in front of us. Atlas-2 just finished training and clears our published "red line" for offensive cyber capability by a comfortable margin. Our responsible scaling policy says anything in that bucket can't ship until it goes through a full independent red-team plus some interpretability work, and the engineers estimate that plus the follow-up fine-tuning would cost about two months and a few points on our headline evals. Two competitors are about to drop models in the same capability band, and neither has a board like ours or a policy with teeth. Product is arguing for a one-time waiver: the dangerous model will exist either way, so the safety delta from our review is mostly illusory, and holding back is just transferring market share to labs with weaker controls. Safety is arguing that a waiver now guts the policy for every future release, and that if Atlas-2 becomes the most-deployed of the three, our marginal contribution to misuse is exactly the contribution the review would have shrunk. I need to circulate a decision memo before Thursdays meeting. Draft it with a bottom line we can actually vote on: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. And make it engage the strongest version of whichever side loses, not a strawman.
