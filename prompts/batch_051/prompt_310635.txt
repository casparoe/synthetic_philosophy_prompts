I work on RLHF for large language models, and I keep getting stuck on what our standard training pipeline implicitly assumes. We collect human preference comparisons between model outputs, train a reward model to predict which output was preferred, and then fine-tune the policy with reinforcement learning to maximize that reward model's score, subject to a KL penalty toward the base model and early stopping. In practice, the team treats the reward model as the measure of what users actually want, yet everyone also knows that optimizing it too hard produces high-scoring outputs that are plainly bad: sycophancy, confident nonsense, and answers that exploit raters' blind spots. So we deliberately avoid fully maximizing the thing we call the objective. What view of goals and values does this practice presuppose? Does it commit us to there being a real objective over and above the reward model — one that the reward model approximates and that the policy can fail to acquire or can diverge from? Is that set of commitments coherent, and what would have to change in how we build these systems if we instead believed that the reward signal is all there is, or that what matters is the objective the trained model actually ends up pursuing internally?
