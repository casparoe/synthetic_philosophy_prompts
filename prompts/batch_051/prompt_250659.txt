I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar and we keep going in circles about organismality, so I want to settle my criteria before I write the paper. The list I've been using is roughly this: (1) physiological integration, meaning the parts are coupled enough that the whole does the maintaining—homeostasis, nutrient cycling, gas exchange—as a unit; (2) shared reproductive fate, meaning the parts reproduce together rather than each making its own offspring; (3) developmental unity, something like a single propagule or bottleneck so the whole originates in one organized program; and (4) a stable inside/outside boundary. Now take lichens. A lichen is a fungus and a photobiont (an alga or cyanobacterium) making a stable, recognizable body: the fungus supplies structure and water, the photobiont supplies fixed carbon, and many lichens even disperse together as soredia, little packages containing both partners. Is a lichen an organism? Go through my criteria honestly, and where the case doesn't fit, say whether that's a point against the lichen or a point against the criteria—for instance, maybe insisting on one genome or one developmental program just rules out multi-lineage individuals by fiat. Then tell me what you'd change: which criterion to add, drop, demote, or reweight. And for calibration, check whether a honeybee colony fits the criteria better than a lichen does, and say what that tells me about what the criteria were really tracking in the first place.
