In my vegan/freegan-adjacent friend group there's this argument that keeps coming back and never resolves, about 'waste' animal products. One side says if a hen was rescued and lays eggs, or someone dumpster-dives a cheese sandwich that was going to be thrown out, eating it is fine — maybe even good, since it doesn't create demand, doesn't harm any animal, and stops waste. The other side says no: the problem with animal products was never only harm and demand; it's that using an animal's body or secretions as food treats them as a resource, and eating a rescued egg still does that, even if no chicken is worse off. Both sides agree on all the descriptive facts — no market signal, no added suffering — and yet nobody moves. I'm starting to think the disagreement isnt really about the egg at all. What kind of disagreement is this? I mean: is it a verbal dispute over the word 'vegan', a hidden factual dispute about whether individual consumption actually shifts social norms, a split between act-based and character- or expression-based moral theories, or just bedrock intuitions about disgust and commodification that can't be argued for? I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right, and I'd like you to say what, given that diagnosis, could actually count as progress — a distinction, a social-science finding, a change of question — or whether I should just stop expecting the argument to move. Also, please say how confident you are in the diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure of.
