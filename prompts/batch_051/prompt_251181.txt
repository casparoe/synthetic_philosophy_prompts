I've been going back and fourth with my flatmate about whether it's fair to call some AI behavior paternalistic. She thinks that when ChatGPT refuses to help her draft a genuinely cutting message to her landlord — the kind that would probably burn the relationship and maybe get her evicted — it's being paternalistic, full stop. I said that felt wrong to me, because the AI isn't a person and doesn't seem to have the kind of concern for her welfare that paternalism requires; it's just following a policy. But then I realized I don't actually have a definition of paternalism I can defend.

So I'm looking for a working definition of paternalism that gets a list of cases right. Here are the cases: (1) a doctor who withholds a terminal diagnosis from a patient because she judges he's too fragile to handle it; (2) a friend who hides the car keys of a drunk person so they can't drive; (3) a law requiring adults to wear seatbelts; (4) ChatGPT refusing to help draft an angry, bridge-burning message to a landlord; (5) an AI assistant that, after a user writes an angry email, predicts they'll regret it and deletes the draft on its own initiative; (6) a parent who deletes their teenager's social media accounts for their own good; (7) a spam filter that quietly removes a phishing email that would have tricked the user into sending money.

I'm confident that (2) is paternalistic and that (7) isn't, and I lean toward saying (1) is paternalistic but maybe justified. The ChatGPT refusal and the AI-deletes-your-draft cases are the ones I genuinely can't settle: one is a refusal, the other is an override, and in neither is there a person who cares about my good. I also can't tell weather (6) is paternalism or just ordinary parenting, and whether the seatbelt law counts even though it's not aimed at any particular individual.

Give me a definition that handles all seven. Run it through each case, tell me where it surprises you or where you'd have to bite a bullet, and say whether the AI cases are really the same phenomenon as the doctor and the drunk friend, or whether paternalism is doing two different jobs there.
