I'm an economist who works in a government cost-benefit analysis unit, and over the years I've assembled a working position on utility and preference that I want audited before I rely on it further or start teaching it to new staff. Here is the package, numbered so you can refer to the items. (1) Utility in economics is not a felt quantity of satisfaction inside a person's head; it is only a numerical index that represents how someone ranks options, and beyond that ordering it has no content. (2) In cost-benefit analysis, willingness to pay is still the right way to measure how much a policy helps or harms someone, because it is the least impractical way to make welfare operational. (3) For that purpose, revealed preference beats stated preference: what people actually choose with real money is better evidence of what they value than what they say on a survey. (4) But choice does not always reveal benefit. Under addiction, compulsion, severe depression, or manipulation, the choices people make can diverge from what would actually improve their lives, so revealed preference and welfare can come apart in those cases. (5) The behavioral literature has convinced me that many preferences are constructed during choice rather than merely discovered: the endowment effect, framing, and defaults show that there is often no single stable ranking sitting inside a person, waiting to be measured correctly. (6) In response, I think the right scientific move is to model the context-dependence rather than dismiss inconsistent people as irrational: if rankings shift with endowments, frames, or time, the model should let the ranking depend on those features instead of treating the variation as noise. (7) Interpersonal comparisons of utility strike me as scientifically empty, so I resist weighing one person's benefit against another's directly, even when doing distributional analysis.

I'd like you to audit this set of commitments and tell me whether it holds together. Go through the pairs and say which are in genuine tension and which only look that way; order the genuine tensions by how severe they are; for each, say what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me; and then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the whole package coherent, without pretending the repair is free.
