I am helping plan a working session for a cross-disciplinary seminar called "Deals with the Future." The session concerns pre-AGI bargains: agreements made now, by AI labs, states, employees, or investors, whose performance or payoff is triggered only after a transformative artificial general intelligence exists. We will anchor the discussion with two running cases. One is an "equity pledge," in which a frontier lab publicly commits that if it builds AGI, a fixed fraction of control rights or the resulting returns will be distributed according to a formula negotiated before the AGI existed. The other is a private windfall clause, in which an employee's contract grants a defined share of AGI-generated revenue that vests on some future milestone.

I need a decision tree that sorts participants into positions on whether such bargains can genuinely bind or shape post-AGI outcomes, rather than sorting them by the labels they already like. Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: begin with questions a policymaker or engineer could answer in terms of the two running cases, without knowing in advance which position each answer leads to. The first cuts should include whether a term like "a share of AGI-generated revenue" even picks out a well-defined obligation across a capability discontinuity; whether a commitment made before the post-AGI party existed can bind it independently of that party's interests; whether present actors can make compliance ex ante rational for the post-AGI party; and, if so, through which mechanism, such as shaping its preferences or values, making violation costly through hard lock-in or countervailing institutions, or serving as a public focal point that post-AGI parties would coordinate on because deviation would unsettle expectations. Also include a branch for the view that, absent enforcement or interest alignment, the bargain still matters but only as present-tense coordination among pre-AGI actors, and a branch for the view that it then matters not at all.

Among the leaves, I would expect at least a paper-commitments view (what is written cannot constrain a party with decisive power), a preference-shaping or value-loading view, a hard lock-in view, a focal-point view, a present-coordination-only view, a normativist or legal-continuity view (obligations survive shifts in power), and an opacity view (the bargain's terms cannot survive the relevant discontinuity). Aim for roughly seven to ten leaves. For each leaf, give one sentence stating the position and one sentence stating its most awkward or banal commitment; for example, one position may have to accept that a promise made today can obligate a being that does not yet exist and had no part in making it. Finally, flag any combinations of answers that have no named occupant and say whether each vacancy looks like incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect, and flag any pair of positions your tree cannot cleanly separate.
