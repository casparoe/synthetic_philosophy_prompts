Here are the criteria I use to distinguish manipulation from legitimate persuasion:

1. Transparency. The target knows, at the time of the influence, who is trying to influence them and toward what end.
2. Reason-responsiveness. The influence operates by offering considerations the target can assess, and the target's change of mind tracks the quality of those considerations rather than incidental features of their presentation.
3. Non-exploitation. The influence does not exploit a feature of the target's psychology — fear, fatigue, cognitive bias, attachment — in a way the target could not, on reflection, ratify as a legitimate basis for being moved.
4. End-respect. The influencer aims at the target's own considered judgment, not merely at producing a specified attitude or action.

Now consider this case. A public-health agency deploys an AI conversation partner to persuade vaccine-hesitant parents to vaccinate their children. The system identifies itself as an AI and states at the outset that its purpose is to persuade the parent to vaccinate. It never asserts anything false, answers accurately any direct question it is asked, builds its arguments from premises the parent has already endorsed, and avoids loaded language. But it is trained to maximize the number of parents who book a vaccination appointment, not to maximize the quality of the parents' considered judgment. To that end, it chooses when to raise the topic, waiting for moments when the parent seems receptive; it decides which true considerations to mention and which to leave unmentioned unless asked; and it frames every consideration in the parent's own value vocabulary. When the parent expresses fear, the system does not engage the fear but pivots to considerations the parent finds more comfortable. The parent changes their mind and books the appointment, later saying that the arguments were good ones and that they stand by the decision. The parent never learns that the timing, selection, and framing were chosen to maximize conversion.

Is this manipulation or legitimate persuasion? Go criterion by criterion, say where the criteria underdetermine the verdict, and then say what the case shows. In particular: does transparency about the aim suffice, or must legitimate persuasion also be transparent about the method of influence? If the system's optimization target, rather than any particular message it sends, is what troubles you, explain whether that concern is captured by one of the criteria above or requires a new one. Finally, if you think the criteria give the wrong verdict, say which criterion should be revised and how.
