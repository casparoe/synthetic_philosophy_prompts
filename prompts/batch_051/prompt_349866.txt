I've developed a rule in arguments with my family and online, and I'm not sure anymore if it's a good one or just a bias with a policy attached. Whenever someone says a policy violates human dignity and then can't or won't say what actually happens to an actual person, I treat the phrase as if it added nothing. I don't interrupt or roll my eyes, I just don't let it move me. Last month my brother and I were arguing about a proposal to allow commercial surrogacy contracts. He said even a well-paid, fully voluntary surrogacy demeans the woman's dignity, and his whole case seemed to rest on that. When I pushed him to say what the concrete badness was, he said some things simply shouldn't be bought and sold, and that's what dignity means. My sister jumped in and said dignity isn't reducible to harm or choice: it's the thing that makes a person's rights more than a calculation, and by ignoring it I was missing the point. That got me worried I'm using my rule to dodge a value I don't personally feel.

So I want to know whether the rule is defensible as it stands. If it isn't, is there a version worth keeping, where I listen for whatever dignity adds beyond harm, exploitation, coercion, or disrespect, and ignore invocations that don't supply it? And how would I tell from the inside whether I'm cutting through an empty word or just refusing to be convinced by a concept I find vague?
