Last week, in our seminar on structural realism, two people I study with got into an argument over a passage near the end of Henri Poincaré’s *The Value of Science*. As they paraphrased it, the passage says that objective reality for science reduces to the relations among things, and that outside such relations there is no reality knowable. Daniel read it as a statement of epistemic structural realism: Poincaré is saying that our knowledge is limited to structure, while the relata themselves exist and have intrinsic natures that remain permanently hidden. Irina read it as pushing toward ontic structural realism: if the only criterion for positing a thing is its role in a network of relations, then the relata are empty placeholders, and the structure itself should be treated as fundamental.

Their exchange went roughly as follows. (1) Daniel said Poincaré could not have meant to eliminate relata because science plainly quantifies over objects—electrons, charged bodies, coordinate frames—and relations without relata would be a formal system with no physical grip. (2) Irina replied that Poincaré treats questions like whether the ether exists as a matter for metaphysicians, and says the proper role of scientific theories is to express relations between phenomena, not to reveal hidden substances. (3) Daniel answered that this is still epistemological caution, not ontological deletion: Poincaré contrasts crude metaphysical entities with the enduring relations that successive theories preserve, not with a world made of nothing but relations. (4) Irina said that distinction is unstable: once you admit that all the explanatory work is done by relations and that relata are known only through those relations, the claim that there are unknowable relata collapses into a meaningless gesture. (5) Daniel responded that modern ontic structural realism inherits a classical problem—relations need relata logically, so the view either smuggles relata back in or renounces the relational character that was supposed to do the work. (6) Irina answered that modern versions solve this by defining relata in terms of the relational network, so the dichotomy between relata and relations is an artifact of traditional ontology, not a real obstacle.

Please adjudicate the disagreement move by move. For each of the six moves, say whether it is clearly mistaken, plausible but flawed, defensible, or clearly right, and briefly justify each rating. Then say who is more right about Poincaré’s passage, and who is more right about structural realism as a view, if those two verdicts come apart.
