I'm a software engineer and I live in git; I think best in terms of commits, parent pointers, branches, rebases, cherry-picks, merge commits, and `git blame`. I know the internals reasonably well, so you don't need to explain them. I've been reading around the nonidentity problem in population ethics, and I keep thinking its basically a version-control problem. As I understand it, the puzzle is this: some choices (conceiving now rather than later, or a policy with very delayed effects) determine which particular people come to exist. If the resulting person has a life worth living, the choice doesn't make them worse off, because the alternative wasn't a better life for them, it was no life at all. And yet we want to say the choice was wrong. That puts pressure on the person-affecting idea that wrongness must be bad-for-someone, and leaves us searching for an impersonal way to explain the wrongness.

I'd like you to restate the nonidentity problem in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can -- not as an analogy but as a translation. Where does 'which person exists' map: to commit hashes, to the parent chain, to something about the DAG of ancestry? Where does 'a life worth living' map: to the build passing, to the software being usable, to the tests going green? Where does 'harmed' map: to a diff that makes some state worse relative to the same history, or to `git blame` pointing at a commit that introduced a line? Try to carry the whole dialect over -- the person-affecting restriction, the way the choice changes which identities exist, the sense that the choice was wrong anyway.

Then give me the residue report. Which pieces survive the translation cleanly, which ones you had to twist to squeeze in, and which have no git counterpart at all. I especially want to know whether the residue shows that git is too thin a framework to carry the moral content, or whether it shows that the moral content was smuggled into the original problem and the load-bearing part is really just the formal point that identities are fixed by alternative histories. And if you think my 'worth living ≈ tests pass' mapping is dodgy, tell me what mapping would be less misleading.
