I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I'm drafting a book set in 2029 where a mid-sized lab open-sources a near-frontier AI model over the objections of its own safety team, and the fallout drives the plot. For one chapter I want to get right, I need the document that two of my characters would have co-produced after a year-long adversarial collaboration: one is an ex-lab researcher now in the "release weights by default unless there's a specific, demonstrable harm" camp, and the other is a policy person in the "keep frontier systems closed by default, with staged access or licensing" camp. Could you draft that joint statement for me? It'll double as source material for their dialogue.

I don't need philosophy jargon or citations; both characters are smart but they're writing for a lay board. Do this: first, a numbered list of claims about open-sourcing frontier AI that both camps could actually sign—not truisms, the kind of thing that makes each side a little uncomfortable. Then a disagreements table. For each row I want: the disagreement stated in wording each side would accept for its own view; the crux or sub-question underneath it; a concrete case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on that crux; and each side's prediction written down in advance. One crux I suspect is whether open weights are more like a scientific result (publication is the default and secrecy needs justifying) or more like an engineered artifact with a blast radius (so the default flips). Another is about who the marginal user of a release actually is: the earnest hobbyist or small lab that gets a real capabilities boost, or the serious adversarial actor who probably already has equivalent access. But treat mine as a starting guess, not a constraint—if you can find sharper cruxes, use those.

A couple of traps to avoid, since the whole point of the scene is that these two have moved past the usual standoff: don't let the table collapse back into "open good, closed safe," and don't make the agreed claims so weak that signing them costs nothing. I'd rather the document had fewer, harder-won claims than a long list of weasel-worded ones. And I've talked too a bunch of people who actually work on this, so the details matter: make the cases concrete about what the released model lets a user do, not just vibes.
