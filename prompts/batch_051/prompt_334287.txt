I'm giving a department colloquium next month defending a neo-Hofstadterian view of consciousness, and I need a Q&A prep sheet before I face the room. The thesis: what distinguishes conscious systems from non-conscious information-processors is not their physiology or their gross behavioural repertoire, but whether their processing realizes a strange loop, a self-referential causal structure in which a high-level representation of the system's own representational activity is fed back into that activity and modifies it. My argument, roughly, is that (1) standard functionalism is too liberal because it counts systems with the right input-output dispositions but no reflexive self-model; (2) the strange loop gives a non-question-begging way to draw the line between mere processing and there being something it is like; and (3) because the loop can be characterized formally, the view is more precise than appeals to integrated information and less chauvinistic than biological naturalism. The audience is a mid-sized analytic philosophy department: mostly philosophers of mind and language, two cognitive neuroscientists, no one who works on formal logic or foundations of mathematics, several sympathetic to functionalism but suspicious of neat formal criteria for consciousness. They will be friendly to the question and hostile to the Gödelian machinery. Write my prep sheet: the seven or eight objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by likelihood and damage, phrased the way they would put them, with a short reply to each and a note on where the reply is soft; include the question I hope nobody asks.
