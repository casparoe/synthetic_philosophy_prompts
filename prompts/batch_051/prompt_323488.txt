At my university the assessment regulations contain this line: 'For assessed work, students must not submit anything that is not their own, and must declare any assistance they received in preparing it.' A final-year student wrote a philosophy essay and showed her full draft to a language model with the instruction 'produce the twenty strongest objections a hostile examiner might make.' It gave her a list; she picked the five she thought were best, redrafted the essay herself to answer them, and submitted with no declaration. She says no sentence of the file was written by the model and the ideas it surfaced are just possible comments, so the essay is her own and there was nothing to declare. The rule was written years before LLMs existed, with essay mills and a friend rewriting your paragraphs in mind. I'm on the committee and we can't agree. How should that sentence apply to her case? Work through the literal reading, the purposive reading, and what the drafters would have said if you'd asked them about a system that supplies objections but no prose, and say which should govern. Does it matter that the assessment was supposed to test her ability to anticipate objections without that help?
