Next month I am presenting a paper to a philosophy of physics reading group, and I want to be ready for the discussion after the talk. My thesis is that the traditional choice between spacetime substantivalism and relationism rests on a false picture in which spacetime must be either a container of matter or an abstraction from matter. I argue that general relativity points to a middle option: physical spacetime is exhausted by the metric field, while the underlying manifold of points is surplus structure with no independent existence. Diffeomorphism-related models are therefore representations of one and the same physical situation, so the hole argument is not a threat to determinism but a symptom of misreading the theory. The view keeps the substantivalist's insight that spacetime is real while honoring the relationist's refusal to posit bare points as an invisible container. I will also argue that Newton's bucket, read carefully, supports inertial structure rather than an absolute container.

The reading group is mixed. Two regulars are substantivalists who know the hole argument in detail and favor a sophisticated or structuralist response to it rather than the coarse-grained manifold substantivalism it refutes. One regular is a committed relationist in the Leibniz–Mach tradition who will suspect that making the metric field real simply smuggles a substance back in. There is also a differential geometer who follows the mathematics but is agnostic on interpretation and will press me on what the word 'real' is doing, and an experimental physicist who doubts the debate is empirical at all and will ask what observable difference any interpretation could make.

Please prepare a Q&A prep sheet for this specific audience, not for a generic metaphysics colloquium or a gravitational physics workshop. It should cover the seven or eight objections I am most likely to get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way the relevant regular would put it, with a reply I can deliver in about two minutes and a candid note on where that reply is weakest. End with the question I should be hoping nobody asks, and say why it is the dangerous one.
