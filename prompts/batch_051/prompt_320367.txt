For a one-page handout in a mixed astrobiology and philosophy seminar, I would like a decision tree on the question of where the Great Filter lies. It should take a reader from the concession that the apparent silence of the galaxy implies at least one improbably hard transition on the route from prebiotic chemistry to a galaxy-spanning civilisation, to a named position on which transition is the filter. Please build it question-first rather than name-first: set out the distinctions and the combinations of answers they permit, locate the actual positions inside that space, and flag combinations nobody occupies — in particular, the all-transitions-easy combination should come out empty because it contradicts the observed silence — saying for each empty combination whether it is empty because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or simply underexplored. The splits should at least ask: whether life arises readily from chemistry; whether, given microbial life, the prokaryote-to-eukaryote transition (the origin of the complex cell) is a likely innovation; whether, given complex cells, complex multicellularity is predictable; whether, given complex multicellularity, general tool-using intelligence is predictable; whether, given such intelligence, cumulative technological culture reliably follows rather than stalling at dolphin- or corvid-grade cleverness; whether, given a technological civilisation, it tends to survive and expand; and whether a galaxy-filling civilisation would be detectable at all. The leaves should include at least: the filter at abiogenesis, at eukaryogenesis, at complex multicellularity, at the evolution of intelligence, at the intelligence-to-technology gap, at civilisational longevity, at the propensity to expand, the view that they are present but undetectable, and a distributed-filter view on which the improbability is spread across many modest bottlenecks rather than one. Throughout, the questions should force respondents to treat Earth's history as a single, selection-biased sample rather than a representative record. Please end by stating which leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate from one another, and the most awkward commitment each leaf is left holding. Answer in British English.
