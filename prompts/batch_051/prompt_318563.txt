Just got out of a two-hour argument with a friend about whether the propensity interpretation of fitness actually solves the tautology objection to natural selection, and I'm annoyed because we both ended up exactly where we started. I know its an old fight in philosophy of biology, and our version didn't move an inch.

His line: natural selection is supposed to be this great explanatory principle, but the usual slogan survival of the fittest just says the survivors survive. Once you define fitness as actual reproductive success, the theory becomes circular and explains nothing. My line: sure, and that's exactly why the propensity interpretation exists — fitness isn't actual survival, it's a probabilistic disposition to survive and reproduce in a given environment, a tendency that's present even when the fitter individual happens to die young. So fitness is a cause of survival, not survival itself, and the tautology is dissolved. He shot back: but we only ever infer or measure fitness by watching actual survival and offspring counts, so haven't I just moved the circle one level down, from 'the survivors survive' to 'those with a disposition to survive tend to survive,' where the disposition gets read off the same data? And a probabilistic claim that's consistent with the fitter one losing in any particular case doesn't seem to predict much either.

We agree on the biology and the math; we just keep talking past each other. My hunch is that I'm talking about whether fitness can be defined independent of actual survival (it can) and he's talking about whether it can be measured or tested independent of actual survival (maybe it can't), and those are different questions. But I'm not sure that's the whole story — maybe we're actually disagreeing about what a scientific explanation is supposed to do, or about whether theoretical terms need independent measurement at all.

What kind of disagreement is this, exactly? Is it verbal, about standards of evidence, or a substantative question about explanation? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would actually move it — a distinction, an experiment, a different way of measuring fitness — or should we just accept that nobody moves?
