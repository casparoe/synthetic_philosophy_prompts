I've been trying to think my way through a problem in the philosophy of history, and I've come up with a term for a distinction I keep circling. I call it narrative torque. Take a fixed batch of well-established, source-certified particular facts about a single episode—say, the fall of a government after a scandal, or the decisions leading up to a war. Imagine two historians who accept exactly the same batch of facts and dispute none of them. One arranges the facts as a tragedy, the other as an institutional farce or a slow, overdetermined drift. The individual facts stay the same, but something downstream changes: the two accounts seem to license different further judgments. A reader of the tragic arrangement is guided toward verdicts about individual responsibility and the avoidability of the outcome; a reader of the farcical or structural arrangement is guided toward verdicts about systemic pressure and the smallness of agency. I want to say that this inferential deflection is the torque of the narrative, and I'm tempted to add that torque is in principle measurable: hold the shared fact-batch fixed, present differently arranged sequences to competent readers, and compare how their counterfactual judgments and attributions of responsibility diverge. I admit I have no idea how to make the measurement concrete; what I want to know is whether the underlying point is sound.

Before I try to defend the idea, I'd like an honest, charitable but candid assessment. Does this amount to anything more than a loosely reworded version of what Hayden White meant by emplotment, or does the measurability claim do new work? Has someone already worked the idea out more carefully under another name—perhaps in the debates over Ranke's objectivist ideal and the constructivist criticisms associated with Carr and White? And where does the proposal fail first? My own worry is that the whole set-up presumes a neutral, pre-narrative stock of facts that arrive already individuated, which may be exactly what a Whitean or a Carr-style constructivist would deny; if the fact-batch is itself shaped by narrative commitments, then the torque is mislocated. Please reconstruct the idea in its strongest form, name the nearest existing positions or distinctions, and say precisely where it stops being coherent or distinctive.
