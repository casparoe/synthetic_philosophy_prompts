I am a PhD student working in the philosophy of mind, and I find myself genuinely torn between two things I find hard to give up. On one side is the pull of constitutive panpsychism. Its appeal is systematic: rather than trying to build experience out of non-experiential matter, it takes the experiential to be fundamental and ubiquitous, present already in the basic constituents of the physical world; the consciousness of a human being is then not something that mysteriously emerges when matter gets complicated enough, but something constituted by the experiential properties of its parts. The view promises to dissolve the hard problem of consciousness instead of solving it, keeps the causal closure of physics intact, and offers a unified ontology on which there is only one kind of fundamental stuff with two aspects. On the other side is an intuition I cannot shake: that my experience is unified in a way that no aggregate of micro-subjects could be. When I feel a pain, it is a single subject that feels it, not a swarm of tiny subjects each feeling a tiny something-or-other. Experiential properties do not seem to combine the way mass or charge does; arranging many point-sized subjects into the shape of a brain seems to give you many point-sized subjects, not one large subject. This, as I understand it, is the combination problem, and it is not a marginal technical objection but the place where the view's central promise is tested.

I am not asking which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and whether there is a reading on which both survive. Concretely: would debunking the intuition require showing that the unity of consciousness is itself a useful illusion, or a feature of the contents of experience rather than of subjects, and would that cost more than it saves? Would saving the intuition require moving to a view on which human consciousness is emergent rather than constituted, or to a view on which the whole cosmos rather than the microphysical is the fundamental subject — and would those be amendments of panpsychism or abandonments of it? Or is there a stable middle path, for instance one that postulates a primitive relation binding many micro-subjects into a single macro-subject, and does that merely restate the problem in a new vocabulary? I would like an account of which of these moves is least costly, and what would in each case justify making it.
