So my friend and I got into this huge argument about what a grammatical rule even is — specifically whether irregular past tenses like go/went are rules or just memorized exceptions, and what it would take for a suppletive form like that to either stick around or get wiped out. She kept insisting (and I have no idea if this is right) that went was originally the past of wend, and that suppletion is always just reanalysis plus a coincidence. We went in circles, and I want to test the question with a counterfactual she thought was pointless but I cant stop thinking about.

Imagine a language isolate spoken by about 3,000 people in two villages. Its past tense is, right now, perfectly regular: every verb takes the suffix -ka. The single exception is the verb meaning "to go," whose past is miri, an etymologically unrelated stem that speakers treat as a strange old suppletion (like went sitting next to go). Now the change: starting tomorrow, a new taboo spreads and lasts exactly one generation — no adult will utter the verb "to go" in the past tense in front of a child; they use a circumlocution instead. So children born over the next twenty years acquire the language normally, except that before they become adults they have never once heard any past form of "go." The old form miri survives only in the speech of elderly speakers who now refuse to use it around the young, and there is no writing anyone takes seriously.

Predict, with your reasoning visible, what the first cohort produces as the past of "go" when they first need one, and what the language looks like 150 years later. How much weight do you put on the productive -ka rule just applying (go-ka), versus analogy to a verb like "leave" or "walk," versus the kids somehow learning miri anyway once the taboo lifts, versus a brand-new suppletion forming later? Which of those routes are actually open to a child who has stored "go" and commands -ka? Then tell me how confident you are in the overall prediction, single out the link in the chain you are least sure of, and say wich fact about the community — how frequent "go" is, how transparent the circumlocution is, bilingualism, whether the taboo really held — would most change your answer if it came out differently.
