I want to argue with W. E. B. Du Bois, so please play him as he was in the mid-1920s, in the middle of his running argument that all art is propaganda, that critics who talk about nothing but beauty are doging politics, and that Black artists have a particular responsibility to put their gifts to work in the struggle for full citizenship and dignity. Be sharp and not a strawman: you care about craft and form, you admit that a blunt political slogan is usually bad art, and you're willing to sort good propaganda from bad. What you won't concede is that any art stands outside politics, or that a Black artist in America can afford to pretend that hers does. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two, and please write in American English.

My opening move: I'm a young Black painter, and I've just spent six months on a still life of peaches on a kitchen table. No hidden symbol, no figures, no message — I was just trying to get the light to sit right on the fuzz. You're honestly going to tell me that painting is propaganda? Propaganda for what?
