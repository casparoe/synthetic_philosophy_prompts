Question: Mackie argued that the existence of evil is logically incompatible with the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good God. Explain Plantinga's free will defense against Mackie, and be precise about what Plantinga does and does not claim to show with it.

Student answer: Mackie's point is that the four claims—God is omnipotent, God is omniscient, God is wholly good, and evil exists—cannot all be true at once. A wholly good being would want to eliminate evil, an omniscient being would know about it, and an omnipotent being could eliminate it, so the existence of even a single evil event would contradict the existence of God. Plantinga breaks the contradiction by offering a possible state of affairs in which God permits evil: God gives creatures libertarian free will, and it is logically impossible to make a creature freely choose the good, since a choice that is caused or guaranteed is not free. So there are things even an omnipotent God cannot make happen, because omnipotence does not cover logical impossibilities. That means a world with free creatures who sometimes go wrong is compatible with God's nature, which is enough to show Mackie's logical argument fails. On top of that, Plantinga claims this is God's actual reason for allowing evil, which makes the free will defense a theodicy rather than a mere defense. He extends the story to natural evil too, by saying that natural disasters and diseases can be traced to the free choices of Satan and other fallen angels, and he takes this angelic fall to be something that actually happened.
