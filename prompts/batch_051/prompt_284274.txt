I've somehow become the screener for a small foundation that funds "exit" projects: charter cities, special economic zones with there own arbitration, seasteading ventures, and lately a few "network state" things where the territory is a Discord plus a DAO registered in Wyoming. I see two or three proposals a month and have to write a one-page memo for a board of generalists who will skim even that. I'm a longtime rationalist / ACX-adjacent reader and I'm broadly sympathetic to the idea that states have too few off-ramps, so I keep catching myself wanting to fund anything that uses the words "consent," "polycentric law," and "jurisdictional competition" in the first paragraph. That's exactly why I want an explicit procedure instead of vibes.

Please write me a reusable screening procedure for the question: does this project meaningfully let people opt out of a state, rather than just creating a small themed state, a dependency on somebody else's state, or a paid product that calls itself citizenship? I want it as an ordered sequence of questions, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a hard no, and where it stops being mechanical and becomes a judgment call. The core thing I'm trying not to lose: exit only matters if there are at least two real options to move between and the new one doesn't reproduce the thing it supposedly escaped. Constraints: I have about half a day per proposal, I can read the founders' materials and do a call or two, and I need to be able to defend a no to people more libertarian than I am.

Then run the procedure on two proposals: (a) a seastead twelve miles offshore selling medical-licensing freedom and a flag, but whose emergency care and resupply run through the neighboring state, and (b) a charter city in a small country that gets its own commercial courts and land registry on a ninety-nine-year lease, but where everyone physically present remains a citizen and taxpayer of the host country. Include at least one case where the procedure returns an answer I will not like. Flag the points where you think the procedure will misfire or where a value has to be plugged in rather than a fact. Don't pad it with invented numbers or fake citations; I want the structure, not empirics.

Hard cap: 1,200 words, worked examples included.
