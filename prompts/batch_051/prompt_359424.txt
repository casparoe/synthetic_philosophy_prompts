I teach an intro philosophy of action module for second-year undergrads—about 18 students, one 50-minute session. I want a class activity that makes them feel why "we did it together" is not just shorthand for "I did my bit and you did yours, and we each knew the other was doing theirs." The official reading for the week is on shared intentions, but I'd rather not start by defining joint action or we-intentions; I want the idea to show up in how they act and only get named in the debrief.

My rough idea is a two-person carrying game. Each pair gets a ping-pong ball and two rulers, one per person, and has to move the ball across the room to a target table without dropping it. One ruler can't reliably carry the ball alone, so success basically forces their rulers to work as a pair. I'm imagining three rounds with different instructions:

Round 1: each player is privately told to get the ball to the table for ten individual points. No talking before or during. The task happens to require the other person's ruler, but each is only given an individual job.

Round 2: the pair gets thirty seconds to plan together and is scored as a pair—ball makes it, both score; ball drops, neither does.

Round 3: same surface as one of the first two rounds, but I've secretly asked one partner to freeze halfway and pull their ruler away. The other partner doesn't know. I want to see whether they treat the freeze as a problem in their own plan or as a breakdown of something the two of them were doing.

Turn this into something runnable: exact rules, materials, timing, how to keep the confederate secret from the rest of the class, and a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money. Then give me a debrief that maps specific moments in the game to the conceptual pieces—mutual belief or common knowledge, meshing subplans, and shared commitment—since my students blur those together and I want them pulled apart. I'd also like you to say what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and to be frank about which part of shared intention the game can't get them to feel and I'll have to supply afterward.

One worry: the rounds might be rigged. Round 1 bans talk, so maybe it only tests who lacks common knowledge rather than whether they lack a shared intention. If that's right, tell me how to fix the game or drop that round—don't hand me a version that teaches the wrong thing. And if you think the carrying game is the wrong kind of case altogether, say so and propose a better one rather than polishing my idea.

No readings or citations needed—just something I can run tomorrow.
