I am taking a seminar this term on comparative political theory and authoritarianism, and we have been working through the argument that sustained economic growth and public order can, by themselves, justify authoritarian rule, the view sometimes called performance legitimacy, with China and Singapore as the standard test cases. Before I either accept or reject that argument, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of political legitimacy would have to get right if it is to adjudicate the dispute. Please write this as a list of desiderata, but as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, numbered points, bold, or tables, the way one would write a long email or a letter. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely the commitment of one side of the debate. I am not after a literature review or citations; the conceptual specification is the point.

I would like you to pay particular attention to the following and not let the list glide over them: the difference between a regime's being believed to be legitimate and its actually being legitimate; the difference between support for current outputs, such as growth, employment, and safety, and support for the system itself; the problem that an authoritarian regime's observed record does not by itself establish the counterfactual record under a more open alternative; whether order maintained through arbitrary arrest, censorship, and fear is the same kind of success as order maintained through predictable law; how the theory treats non-material goods such as voice, dignity, and exit, and whether it may let growth outweigh them automatically; the selection effect by which the authoritarian regimes we observe are disproportionately the ones that happened to perform well; and the possibility that subjects endorse the regime's performance because their preferences have adapted to an unfree environment. The list should also be general enough to make real distinctions rather than being gerrymandered to fit one case, so please say whether the same requirements can apply to a small city-state with a strong civil service and to a continental one-party state with a very different administrative challenge.

The purpose of the list is to let me score the performance-legitimacy argument against it in a paper, so state each desideratum precisely enough that a proponent and a critic would be forced to differ somewhere. End by identifying which single desideratum does the most to exclude the most popular versions of the performance argument, and whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable.
