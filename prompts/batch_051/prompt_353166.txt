I've been putting together a small handout for a discussion group about whether the left/right distinction tracks anything real, and I've gotten stuck on one section. The section lists accounts of what holds each side together — that is, accounts of the glue, if there is one, that makes all the leftish positions hang together and all the rightish ones hang together. So far I have these:

1. A value account: the left hangs together because it treats equality, or flattening hierarchies, as the master value, and the right hangs together because it treats liberty, or preserving certain hierarchies and order, as the master value; the policy positions are downstream of that.

2. A moral-psychology account: the left builds morality mainly on care and fairness, while the right draws on those plus loyalty, authority, and sanctity, so each side's positions cohere because they run on different moral foundations.

3. A temperament account: the left attracts people high in openness to experience and tolerance of ambiguity; the right attracts people high in conscientiousness and orderliness, and on average higher in threat sensitivity and need for closure; the sides hang together because they are sorting people by personality, not by ideas.

4. A historical coalition account: there is no deep philosophical glue; left and right are contingent bundles of group interests — labor versus capital, religious versus secular, urban versus rural, and so on — welded together by historical accidents and kept together since by path dependence and party competition.

5. An elite-packaging account: most people's views are messy and multi-dimensional, and what makes left and right look coherent is that parties, media, and activists package issues into two bundles and punish defection; the glue is top-down coalition management, not a real underlying unity.

I'm pretty sure that covers the standard menu, or at least the versions of it I keep hearing. What I want is one more account of what holds each side together that is genuinely different from these five — not a rewording, not a special case, and not a mix of two entries on the list. It can be speculative, but it needs to be statable in a couple sentences and to actually explain the clustering rather than just redscribe it. If it's a bad account, say so and say why you're offering it anyway; if it's good, tell me where it earns its keep. Oh, and this started as an argument with a friend who thinks the distinction is basically just a tribal tag, so the new entry should have a recognizable engine or mechanism that does the holding-together, something that could stand as its own bullet point in a handout.

Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. Item 1 states the new account. Item 2 says what it captures that the listed accounts miss. Item 3 says how it differs from the nearest account on my list. Item 4 says whether it's any good or where it's weakest. Item 5 says which argument or consideration would lead you to drop or revise the account — or where your claim that it's genuinely new is most likely to fail. American English, please.
