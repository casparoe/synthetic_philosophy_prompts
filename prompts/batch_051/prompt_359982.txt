I'm grading an undergrad formal epistemology problem set and want a second read on one answer before I put a score on it. The exercise is about the Judy Benjamin case. Here's the question the student got, followed by their answer.

Question. Private Judy Benjamin has been parachuted into a region divided into four sectors: Red Headquarters (RH), Red Second Company (RS), Blue Headquarters (BH), and Blue Second Company (BS). Initially she assigns probability 1/4 to each sector. Base then sends her a message whose content is this: conditional on her being in Red territory, the odds are three to one that she is in Red Headquarters rather than Red Second Company. (a) Write this information as a probabilistic constraint. (b) Explain why ordinary Bayesian conditioning cannot be applied directly to this message. (c) The minimum cross-entropy (MRE) rule says to adopt the posterior p that minimizes KL(p || q) = Σ_i p_i ln(p_i / q_i), where q is the uniform prior, subject to the constraint from (a) and Σ_i p_i = 1. Compute the MRE posterior, showing your work. (d) Say whether the MRE posterior is unique, and whether that settles the Judy Benjamin problem.

Student answer. (a) The message says P(RH | Red) = 3/4, so p(RH) = 3 p(RS). (b) Conditionalization needs a proposition to condition on, but the message doesn't say which sector she's in; it only fixes a ratio inside Red territory. Its not the same as learning "she is in Red" or "she is in Red Headquarters." (c) Since the constraint only involves the two Red sectors, the Blue sectors should stay put, so p(BH) = p(BS) = 1/4. That forces p(Red) = 1/2, and together with p(RH) = 3 p(RS) we get p(RH) = 3/8 and p(RS) = 1/8. Since this satisfies the constraint and changes only what the message forces us to change, it is the MRE posterior. (d) The posterior is unique because relative entropy is strictly convex and the feasible set is convex. So the Judy Benjamin problem has a definite rational solution.
