I am a lay trustee of a small museum, and I have been turning over a problem that seems to have no clean answer.

In 1894 our founder left his writing desk to the museum in a will that reads: “I give to the Museum my writing desk, the desk at which I wrote my histories, upon the condition that the Museum exhibit it at all times in the Founder's Room.” The desk has stood there ever since. But over the past century the conservators have, one piece at a time, replaced nearly every part of it. The leather top was replaced in 1922, the legs in the 1950s, the drawers and dovetails in the 1970s and 1980s, the brass handles ten years after that, and finally the wooden carcass itself in 2008, after an outbreak of furniture beetle. Each replacement was made to match the original in material and design, using the old pieces as patterns, and each was treated at the time as a repair rather than a replacement. Because no single step ever looked like a loss of the desk, no one thought the museum was violating the will.

The complication is that the removed pieces were not destroyed. Each was labelled, catalogued, and placed in a crate in the archive. A member of the founder's family recently asked to see the desk, and when I explained the history, they made the following claim: the object now standing in the Founder's Room contains none of the material the founder actually wrote on; the genuine desk sits in pieces in a crate; and the museum is therefore in breach of the condition, because it is exhibiting something else while keeping the real bequest in storage. The museum's position is that the desk has never been lost or replaced, only repaired, and that it remains “the desk” in the only sense that matters.

I care less about which side a lawyer would take than about what the right answer is. Please work through how the will's language, the purpose of the founder's condition, and what the founder would have said if asked in 1894 would each decide the matter, and tell me which of those three approaches should control here and why. I would also like to know whether the fact that the original pieces survive in a crate, rather than having been thrown away, should make any difference.
