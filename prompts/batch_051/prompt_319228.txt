I am a philosophy of mind PhD student working on the dispute between illusionism and realism about phenomenal consciousness, and I need a dialogue that does not simply restate the familiar explanatory-gap or Mary's-room moves. Write a dialogue between two equally competent characters: an illusionist who holds that phenomenal properties are introspective misrepresentations with no real referents, and a realist who holds that at least some phenomenal properties are real even though introspection can mischaracterize them. The dialogue should be built around a single invented case that both characters accept: a subject in a peripheral color-change task reports a perfectly uniform and stable visual field, performs no better than chance when asked which side changed, yet sincerely endorses the statement that she had a continuous visual experience of the screen throughout. The illusionist should argue that this pattern is what her view predicts, because the subject's introspective representation of the field is false and there is no further phenomenal fact left over. The realist should argue that the case troubles rather than refutes realism: a false introspective characterization is still a characterization of something, and the subject's intact experience of the screen is a real phenomenal property that survives the localized error. The characters must respond to each other's actual arguments, not alternate prepared speeches; each should concede at least one point; each should state the other's view in its strongest form before objecting; and by the end they should identify the residual disagreement as precisely as they can. They are not allowed to settle the dispute by appealing to the authority of named philosophers or papers. Write the dialogue in American English.
