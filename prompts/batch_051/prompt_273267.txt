Give a working definition of 'a right to the city' as that phrase functions in debates about housing and zoning. I don't mean the bare right to vote in city elections or to walk on public sidewalks; I mean the right to have one's interests count, as a matter of justice, when a city decides what housing may be built, where, at what density, and at whose expense. Your definition should get the following cases right, or say clearly when it can't.

The cases: (1) a retired renter who has lived in the same neighborhood for forty years and would be displaced by an upzoning; (2) a person currently homeless and sleeping in the city; (3) a tourist visiting for a weekend; (4) an out-of-state owner of a short-term rental in the city; (5) a restaurant worker who works fifty hours a week in the city but lives ninety minutes away because that is all she can afford; (6) a young adult who grew up in the city, was priced out, and wants to move back; (7) a would-be resident from another state who would move in if new construction lowered rents; (8) a developer proposing to build that new housing; (9) a resident of a neighboring suburb who would absorb the additional traffic and school crowding; (10) a child who will be born in the city ten years from now.

I take (1) and (2) to be paradigm cases where the interest matters, and (3) and (4) to be paradigm cases where it does not; I do not have settled views about (5) through (10).

First state your definition. Then run it through all ten cases, assigning each a verdict of 'right,' 'no right,' or 'depends' with a one- to two-sentence basis. Then identify the single verdict you find least secure and say what evidence or argument would move you on it.

Return a JSON object with this exact schema:
- definition (string)
- plain_restatement (string)
- verdicts (array; each item has case (string, e.g., '1: retired renter'), verdict (one of 'right', 'no_right', 'depends'), basis (string))
- least_secure (object with fields case, why_insecure, what_would_move_me)
- revision (string, either 'none' or a short statement of how the hardest case forced an adjustment)
