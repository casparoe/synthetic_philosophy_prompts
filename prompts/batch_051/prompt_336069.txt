I'm a philosophy of science PhD student, and I've somehow ended up organizing the Q&A for a visiting speaker whose coming to our cosmology reading group. He's a philosopher of physics who defends a likelihood version of the fine-tuning argument: he doesnt commit himself to any prior probability for design, and he admits we dont have a well-defined chance distribution over the fundamental constants. His claim is just that a life-permitting universe is much more likely under the design hypothesis than under what he calls the single-universe chance hypothesis, and that this is enough for the observation to confirm design—the way a suspicious fire can confirm arson without our needing a prior probability for arson.

I need eight to ten questions that will actually squeeze him, not let him re-explain the argument for the fiftieth time. I want at least one question on the measure problem (if design itself doesn't face a distribution over constants, what makes one subset of parameter space the right comparison class, and why isn't any smooth measure over parameter space just a human-imposed carving of possibility space?), one on whether his target is a straw man, since most naturalists would rather invoke a multiverse or a deeper theory than settle for a single universe with brute constants, and one on what a non-fine-tuned observation would even look like—i.e., whether the design hypothesis is flexible enough to accommodate nearly any values and still claim they were set for something. For each question, include a follow-up tailored to the reply he's most likely to give: he tends to say the measure problem is everyone's problem in cosmology and not a special weakness of his view, and that multiverse proposals just move the tuning worry up one level. I want follow ups that make those moves costlier rather than letting him treat them as conversation-enders.
