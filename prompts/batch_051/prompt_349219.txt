ok so my Aristotle reading group has been stuck on phantasia for like a month and I'm starting to think the problem isn't the text but what kind of disagreement we're actually having. the passage we keep circling is De Anima III.3 (around 428a) where Aristotle says phantasia is neither perception nor judgment, but it does not occur without perception, and judgment does not occur without phantasia. one side in our group thinks he's positing a faculty: a kind of image-making capacity between perception and thought, which stores and reproduces perceptual content so the intellect has something to work on. the other side says no, the passage is just marking off a type of state — things appearing to one — and the line about judgment needing phantasia is about the role of appearances in all thinking, not a separate storage box. both sides can read the same lines and feel confirmed. I have a hunch this is partly a verbal dispute about whether the word names a capacity or an exercise, but I'm not sure, and the fact that neither side moves makes me think there may be a hidden philosophical difference about whether Aristotle is doing faculty psychology at all. so: what kind of disagreement is this really? is it a matter of Greek semantics, a substantive dispute about Aristotle's theory of mind, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? and if you had to say what would actually count as progress, what would it be?
