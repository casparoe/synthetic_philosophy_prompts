I sit on the naming and commemoration committee of a mid-sized city, and we are about to decide a petition to rename Merrick Street. The street honours Sir Edmund Merrick (1812–1888), a physician who was celebrated in his lifetime for founding the city's first teaching hospital and for advancing obstetric technique. The petition, submitted jointly by local historians and a university bioethics group, documents that several of Merrick's innovations were developed through painful, non-consensual experiments on women in a workhouse infirmary he supervised. The factual record is not in dispute.

The complication is that the street has outgrown its namesake. Over the past sixty years, Merrick Street has become the commercial and cultural heart of the city's largest Caribbean and West African communities. The name 'Merrick' now functions in daily life as shorthand for the neighbourhood: its shops, market halls, and carnival. Few residents could say who Sir Edmund was. The petitioners maintain that this is beside the point, because the name continues to perform an act of honouring regardless of how residents happen to read it. A coalition of market traders and community associations opposes renaming on the ground that the name has been theirs for two generations and no longer refers to the physician at all; they argue that erasing it would destroy a layer of local identity that Merrick himself never created.

Our committee already has criteria for cases in which a namesake's wrongdoing is egregious. What we lack is any principled way of deciding how much weight, if any, to give to meanings a name has accumulated after the original act of commemoration. Please draft the short written opinion I shall circulate before our meeting next week. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame; say whether, in your view, a name that no longer functions as an honour in daily life can still wrongfully honour; set out a principle we could apply in future cases where a name has outlived its namesake; and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the side you ultimately reject, rather than merely restating the criteria we already have.
