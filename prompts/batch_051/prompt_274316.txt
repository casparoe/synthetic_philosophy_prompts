I've been keeping notes on my own choices, and I keep finding a pattern I don't have a word for. Take a pair of options: $10 at one date or $12 a month later. When I choose for myself, I often take the $10 if it's available today, but if the $10 is ten months away and the $12 eleven months away, I'll wait for the $12. The same one-month delay seems enormous in the first case and negligible in the second, and so far this sounds like the familiar kind of present bias. The part I can't name is this: when I make the identical choices for a friend, the one-month delay seems negligible both times — I wait for the $12 whether the sooner option is today or ten months out. The steep drop in patience appears only when my own sooner reward is immediate, not merely when a sooner reward is immediate for someone else. Pure time preference, as I understand it, is supposed to attach to a time rather than to an owner. Does this owner-sensitive version have an established name? If not, what would you call it — and how would you mark its boundaries against ordinary present bias and against simply discounting other people's reward at a lower but still positive rate? I want to know exactly where the edges of the phenomenon are, not just a label.
