I sit on the assisted-reproduction ethics review board of a teaching hospital, and I need an analysis of a new application, judged for consistency with our board's own track record of decisions over the past five years.

Our precedents:

- We approved preimplantation genetic testing to avoid transferring an embryo carrying a mutation for a fatal early-childhood neurodegenerative disease, where unaffected embryos were available from the same cycle.
- We approved testing to avoid transferring an embryo with a mutation for a severe chronic disease that requires lifelong treatment and greatly shortens life, though it is not usually fatal in childhood.
- We rejected a request for sex selection for family balancing from a couple with two daughters who wanted a son; both available embryos were healthy.
- We rejected a request from two Deaf prospective parents to select an embryo carrying a variant associated with congenital deafness, so that the child would share their signing community.

The new application: a patient has produced six healthy preimplantation embryos through IVF. She asks for polygenic screening of the embryos for educational attainment and wants to transfer the embryo with the highest score. The scores are statistical and noisy; no embryo has any known disease-related mutation. She argues that this is no different in principle from moving to a better school district or paying for tutoring: she is not choosing what her child will become, only giving whichever embryo becomes her child the best expected life. The board is split. Some members see the request as continuous with our disease-prevention approvals; others see it as continuous with our rejections of non-medical selection.

I want more than a yes or no. If the board is to remain consistent with its own precedents, what should it decide? Which of our past decisions is the closest analog, and why? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine this case, say so: identify the principle we would be adopting with each possible decision, say which principle fits our whole track record better and why, and state the further hypothetical case that would most cleanly separate the viable principles. Then give your own recommendation.

Keep your answer to no more than 600 words.
