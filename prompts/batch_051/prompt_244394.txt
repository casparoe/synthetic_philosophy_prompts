I run a masters seminar in environmental policy, 16 students, 80 minutes, and I want a structured activity around the precautionary principle before we read anything on it. The case: a town board has to decide whether to approve a new systemic insecticide to control an invasive beetle killing ash trees. The applicant has lab and small-field efficacy data and a 48-hour bee toxicity test showing no mortality; beekeepers and a local conservation group say long-term sublethal and soil-accumulation effects are simply unknown, and that trunk injection costs more but avoids broadcast exposure. I want the students acting as the board under a rule that they may only consider evidence in the application packet, so absent studies matter, not just the studies somebody brought. They have to choose: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Design the packet, roles, timeline, a voting rule, and a debrief that maps what happened to risk vs uncertainty, type I and type II errors, reversibility, and burden of proof. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and what the activity can't get them to feel so I know what I have to supply myself. And they shouldn't hear the word precautionary until the debrief.
