Last night I got into a long argument with a friend of mine who works as an ML engineer, about whether a lab should release the weights of its next frontier model. The red team had found that after a few hundred examples of fine-tuning the safety training basically came off, and the thing would happily walk someone through synthesizing a pretty nasty compound. My point was that this changes the calculus for putting the weights up on Hugging Face. His whole response, again and again, was "well, the genie is out of the bottle" — and from there he seemed to conclude that refusing to release is just theater and that the lab does nothing wrong if it puts the model out there.

I can't tell what exactly that sentence is doing. It seems to slide around between a few different claims: that the dangerous capability is already public, that a single lab's decision no longer changes anyone's access in expectation, that release restrictions are unenforceable, and that once a technology has leaked nobody is responsible for making it easier to get. Could you seperate those out for me? I want a menu of the distinct claims "the genie is out of the bottle" could be making, with a verdict on each — which are empirical, which are normative, which would need what evidence, and which are just defeatist or a non sequitur. And then say which reading, if it were actually true, would support his conclusion that open-sourcing the next weights is fine. I want to go back to him with the readings spelled out instead of letting the slogan keep doing all the work.
