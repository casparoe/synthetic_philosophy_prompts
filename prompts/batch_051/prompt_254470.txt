I'm a novelist, so I spend a lot of time thinking about the stories people tell themselves to keep going — thats partly why I can't stop chewing on a conversation with my mother. She's 73 and in pretty good health. A few weeks ago she told me, almost cheerfully, that she doesn't want to live much past 80, that wanting more time than that would be greedy, and that people who chase longevity are refusing to accept how a life is supposed to go. When I asked why she wouldn't want more good years with her grandchildren, she said I was projecting my own fear of death onto her. I'm torn. I want to respect her, and it is her life and her death. But I also know that "I never wanted it anyway" is exactly the sort of line I'd write for a character whos protecting herself from a future she's afraid to want. Is there a principled way to distinguish respecting her stated preference from enabling what might be a kind of self-deception, and how much should I push?
