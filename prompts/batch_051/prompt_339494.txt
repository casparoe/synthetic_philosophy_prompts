My colleague in a law-and-tech workshop has been handing down verdicts about responsibility for autonomous-system harms all term, and I've kept a running list. On a rule-based surgical robot that cut in the wrong place because a technician had entered the wrong coordinate in a look-up table, she said the technician is responsible and the supervising surgeon isn't. On a machine-learned ICU early-warning system that gave a normal score while a patient deteriorated, and the nurse deferred to it with no independent reason for concern, she said no human is responsible — a textbook responsibility gap, with the hospital compensating institutionally. On an autonomous drone that misread a wedding party as a convoy using a learned target classifier and fired before the remote operator could veto, she again said no individual is responsible. On an old-fashioned transparent credit scorecard, not machine learning, that everyone knew was biased, and a loan officer who rubber-stamped the denial, she said the loan officer is responsible and the scorecard builders are not.

Now I've got a new case for her: a machine-learned lung-screening tool returns a low-risk score, but the radiologist herself notices a suspicious nodule, decides she's probably over-reading it, and signs off without flagging it. It turns out to be cancer. I want you to predict who, if anyone, she holds responsible, then say which rule or principle she seems to be running. Which other rules fit her track record just as well, and what does each predict about the radiologist case? What further case would cleanly settle which rule she's actually using, and which one is your best guess?

Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion.
