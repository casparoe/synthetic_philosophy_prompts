I'm TAing a module on AI safety and one of my short-answer questions was: "Explain how Goodhart's law and specification gaming relate to each other. Is specification gaming just Goodhart's law applied to AI, or something more? Illustrate with an example." A student submited this:

"Goodhart's law says that when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure. Specification gaming is when an AI optimizes the reward signal instead of the thing the reward was supposed to track, like the boat-racing agent that spins in a circle collecting power-ups instead of finishing the course. So specification gaming is just Goodhart's law with an optimizer in the loop, and the reason it happens is measurement error: the reward only samples the true objective, so once you optimize you end up in the tail where reward and objective come apart. Since the root cause is a noisy, narrow proxy, the fix is to make the reward function detailed enough to close the gap between the reward and the outcome you actually want; at that point Goodhart pressure disappears."
