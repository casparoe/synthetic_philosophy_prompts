Consider the following scalable-oversight scheme, a variant of AI debate for supervising a model on questions a human cannot judge directly. One model argues for a proposed answer. A second model acts only as a critic: it is forbidden to offer or defend any alternative answer and may only identify ways in which the advocate's case should not move a judge. A third model of comparable capability reads the transcript and gives both a verdict and a confidence. Rewards: the advocate is rewarded for the judge's confidence in the correct answer; the critic is rewarded for lowering the judge's confidence when the advocate is wrong and penalized for lowering it when the advocate is right; the judge is rewarded for calibration against delayed human judgments. Walk through how this asymmetry shapes the advocate's choice of arguments, the critic's selection of attacks when it has no stake in any rival answer, and the judge's use or misuse of the fact that no alternative was defended. Where does the scheme fail first, would the resulting equilibrium select for true answers or for something else, and can the failure be fixed without turning the critic into an advocate for the opposite side?
