In the modularity literature people love to say that some system—early vision, say, or a face-recognition device—is informationally encapsulated: it literally cannot use what you believe. The standard bit of evidence is something like the Müller-Lyer illusion, where you can know the two lines are the same length and still see one as longer. But I keep thinking that all this shows is that vision doesn't use the belief, not that it couldn't. So: design the best experiment you can to separate genuine encapsulation (the system can't use the information) from mere non-use (it doesn't happen to consult it), for whichever subsystem you like. Say what result would favor each reading, and then be blunt about what even a decisive-looking result would still leave unshown. Is the encapsulated/unencapsulated distinction here a testable one, or are we just dressing up behavioral inertia as architecture?
