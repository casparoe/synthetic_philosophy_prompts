Okay here's a thing I can't get my head around. In a lot of jurisdictions, a doctor can be required to unhook a ventilator when a competent patient asks, and can also push morphine at a dose she knows will probably suppress breathing and kill the patient, as long as she documents that her intention was pain relief and not death. But in most of those same places she cannot, even at the patient's explicit and repeated request, prescribe a lethal dose or inject it. So the law seems to treat unplugging a machine and letting someone die as fine, and treating pain in a way that forseeably hastens death as fine, but directly killing as not fine — even though the patient consents in all three cases. I want a reconstruction of the implicit theory here. What does this pattern actually presuppose about the moral difference between killing and letting die, and about the difference between intending death and merely forseeing it? Which of these distinctions are doing the real normative work and which are basically window dressing? Is the package internally consistent, or does it quietly commit to several incompatible views at once? And if you were forced to keep the ban on lethal injection but drop the intention/foresight distinction, what else in the surrounding law and medical practice would have to change?
