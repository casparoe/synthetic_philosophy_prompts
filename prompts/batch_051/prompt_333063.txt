One of my cofounders wants us to sign a pre-AGI pact with two other groups — a binding agreement now about who controls any future AGI research and how the returns get shared. The argument has a tidy logic I keep almost accepting: right now, before an AGI exists, we actually have chips, and once it does exist the power distribution is whatever it is, so any term we dont lock in today is a term we've already lost. And the people pushing it are careful to say the pact doesn't bind the AGI; it binds us and the other signatories now, so we can't quietly defect on each other later. That part sounds sensible. But when I picture actually signing, I get a firm intuition that the whole thing is cosplay: the enforcement that's supposed to hold us to the deal is itself a pre-AGI institution, and a system smart enough to be called AGI will be in a position to break or buy or rewrite that arrangement in the first hour. So we'd be signing a contract whose only real enforcer is the thing we're supposed to be hedging against. Is that intuition just a debunkable feeling, does the precommitment view need amending, or are these two thoughts compatible on a reading im missing? I want to know what would justify each move, not just which side wins.
