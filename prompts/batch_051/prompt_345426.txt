Exam question:

In the Judy Benjamin problem, an agent has a uniform prior over four mutually exclusive location hypotheses:

R1: she is in the Red Headquarters area.
R2: she is in the Red Second Company area.
B1: she is in the Blue Headquarters area.
B2: she is in the Blue Supply area.

She receives a garbled message that, she judges, gives her exactly one piece of posterior information: her conditional probability for being in R1 given that she is in Red territory should be 3/4, and it gives no further information about her location.

(a) Explain why standard Bayesian conditionalization on a proposition cannot directly accommodate this message.
(b) Describe how van Fraassen's relative-entropy minimization rule selects a posterior distribution in a case like this.
(c) Compute the posterior that this rule yields for the numbers above.
(d) Compare it with the posterior one would get by conditionalizing on the material conditional Red → R1.

Student answer:

Ordinary conditionalization needs a full proposition in the agent's algebra, but this evidence is only a constraint on the posterior conditional probability P(R1 | Red) = 3/4. Many posterior distributions satisfy that constraint, so conditionalization alone does not select a unique answer. Van Fraassen's proposal is to minimize relative entropy, Σ p_i ln(p_i / q_i), between a posterior p and the prior q, subject to the constraint. Relative entropy minimization preserves all the prior ratios except those that the constraint forces us to change. Since the constraint concerns only the ratio R1:R2, it leaves both Blue hypotheses at their prior probabilities and also leaves the total probability of Red territory at its prior value of 0.5. Therefore B1 and B2 each remain 0.25. Within Red, the ratio R1:R2 must become 3:1, so R1 = 0.75 × 0.50 = 0.375 and R2 = 0.25 × 0.50 = 0.125. Thus the relative-entropy-minimizing posterior is (0.375, 0.125, 0.25, 0.25). By contrast, conditionalizing on the material conditional Red → R1, which is equivalent to R1 ∨ B1 ∨ B2, would rule out R2 entirely and give (1/3, 0, 1/3, 1/3).
