I keep encountering the same picture, and I do not know whether it has an established name. Suppose a difficult judgment must be made — for example, whether a complex proposal should be approved. Instead of one person working through the whole proposal until they understand it, you arrange a tree of tasks: the top-level question is split into subquestions, each subquestion is split again, and at the bottom each task is small enough to be answered from a short briefing. Each person sees only their own question and a little context; their answers are passed upward and combined by other people who also see only a narrow slice of the whole. The claim is that the tree reproduces the verdict a single careful generalist would have reached, even though no person ever holds the entire problem in mind. I have seen this same configuration treated both as a practical recipe for teams and as a model of what one mind might be doing below the surface of deliberation. Does it have a name? I am less interested in the label than in the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from ordinary division of labor, from a committee with a chair, and from the familiar idea that perception and language are modular while central judgment is unified, and what would have to be true of judgment for the tree to be faithful to it.
