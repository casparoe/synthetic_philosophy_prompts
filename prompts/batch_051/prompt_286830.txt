I keep noticing a pattern in moral arguments, and I wonder if it has a name. When someone disagrees with me about something like eating meat or immigration, they usually explain my view by appealing to bias — I was raised that way, I find the alternative disgusting, I benefit from the status quo, my social circle all agrees with me — while they explain their own view by appealing to values or principles. I catch myself doing the same thing in reverse. A gut feeling of disgust gets called "bias" when it points toward someone else's conclusion, but "a strong moral intuition" when it points toward mine. Is there an established term for this asymmetry, or for the deeper problem that there seems to be no neutral way to sort my moral convictions into genuine values versus mere bias? I know motivated reasoning and ad hominem arguments are nearby, but this is specifically the bias/preference distinction being applied lopsidely. If there is a term, I'd like to know exactly what it names and where its boundaries are; if there isn't, what would you call it, and can the line between a moral preference and a bias be drawn at all?
