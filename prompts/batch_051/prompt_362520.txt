Highway departments sometimes publish clearance heights as if they were just engineering facts: a bridge is 9 feet, a bus is 11 feet, so the bus doesn't fit. In the US there's a famous-ish case where an underpass on a parkway was built deliberately low so public buses couldn't reach a beach, effectively filtering who could use the park. What theory of technology and political power does treating that bridge as a mere physical constraint presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view: does the artifact itself have politics, or is the politics only in the human plans and budgets behind it? Say whether the practice is coherent: can officials consistently claim the bridge is neutral after the planning memos show the clearance was chose to keep bus riders out? And what would infrastructure review have to add—or drop—if we adopted the view that technical specifications can carry political content even without anyone's explicit intent at the moment of use?
