Me and a friend have been arguing about this for weeks. He says that when a language model answers a question like "what would you do if I changed a few of your weights" or "predict your own next output", it isn't actually modelling itself at all—it's just mapping the question text to a plausible-sounding answer from training data. I think there's a real difference between a system whose world model has a slot for itself (the actual system, sitting inside the environment, able to be changed and to change things) and one that just predicts its own outputs from the outside, like a weather model predicting rain. My claim is that the difference isn't just philosophical, it should show up in behaviour if you poke it the right way.

So: what is the best experiment or probe you can design to tell these two apart? Tell me what result would count for each side. And then be brutal about the part I'm worried about: even if the test comes out looking like the system does have a "self-slot", what would that still not establish? Is there a residue here that no test can reach, and is that residue something real about the system or just us insisting on a label after we've already operationalized it into a different thing?
