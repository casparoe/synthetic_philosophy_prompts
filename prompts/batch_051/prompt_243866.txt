In the weekly card game I host, there is an unspoken rule about corrections. If someone says, "As we all agreed last month, house rules are banned," and no one objects immediately, then from that point on everyone treats it as settled that we did agree to that, even when the claim is false and most of us know it. If someone tries to object later, they are told, "You should have said something at the time." Yet the same group insists, in almost every other context, that staying silent is not the same as agreeing and that nobody can be bound by a decision they never actually made. I am trying to understand what this practice assumes about how conversation works and how shared commitments come to exist. Does it only make sense if a conversation keeps some sort of public record that is updated by what is said and left unchallenged, regardless of what anyone privately believes? Or is it just a practical way to keep the game moving, compatible with several different pictures of conversation? And if we gave up the idea of that shared record, what would the game-night rule have to become?
