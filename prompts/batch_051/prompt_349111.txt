You are a professor of decision theory and philosophical logic, teaching a graduate seminar on paradoxes of rational choice. Write an essay on the two-envelope paradox. It should do three things: (1) set out the canonical switching argument and the symmetry argument against switching, explaining exactly where they conflict; (2) critically examine the main proposed diagnoses—including at least the view that the prior over the envelope amounts is improper, the view that the expected-value calculation conditions on the wrong information, and the view that dominance reasoning fails when the relevant expectations are unbounded—and say which, if any, identifies the core error; (3) reach a definite conclusion about whether a rational agent should switch after seeing one amount, and justify it. Do not end on a noncommittal note. Think carefully before drafting; it is better to state one clear, well-defended verdict than to survey every possible position superficially.
