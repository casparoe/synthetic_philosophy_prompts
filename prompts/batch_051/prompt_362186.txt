I am trying to work out what I actually think about the therapy/enhancement distinction by starting from my verdicts on cases. Growth hormone for a child with a hormone deficiency seems clearly therapeutic; growth hormone for a short but otherwise healthy child so that he ends up taller than he would otherwise have been seems clearly an enhancement. Methylphenidate for a student with ADHD is therapy; the same substance used by a student without ADHD to stay alert through revision is enhancement. A measles vaccine, however, feels like prevention rather than enhancement, even though it leaves the immune system able to do something it could not do before. A statin prescribed to someone with ordinary cholesterol to lower her risk of a heart attack also feels like prevention rather than enhancement. Yet propranolol taken by a healthy violinist before an audition to keep her hands from shaking feels like enhancement, although it too merely lowers the likelihood of a particular bad outcome; I cannot see why the statin and the propranolol cases should fall on opposite sides, but that is how they strike me. I also think a cochlear implant for a child born deaf is therapy, while feeling the pull of the thought that it is enhancing a body that was functioning as that body functions. Is there a single principle — framed in terms of disease, normal functioning, a species-typical or individual baseline, or something else — that sorts these cases as I have sorted them? Which of my verdicts, if any, does it force me to revise? And what does it say about an antidepressant that makes people without depression and with ordinary stress responses more resilient than most people, and about a gene edit that confers resistance to a disease the person would almost certainly never have contracted? Please also tell me what would change your mind: which further case or consideration would lead you to a different principle, or where the reasoning is most likely to give way.
