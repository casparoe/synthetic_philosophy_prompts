I'm on my denomination's worship committee. We officially work from the regulative principle — public worship may include only what Scripture commands, not just whatever it doesn't forbid — but in practice we've been ruling case by case, and I'm starting to think our own precedents could be read two different ways.

Our rulings so far: we approved the congregation singing Psalm 23 straight from the Bible, a cappella; we approved the congregation singing the Magnificat (Mary's song in Luke 1), also a cappella; we rejected a request to sing the hymn Amazing Grace, because the text is uninspired; we rejected a request to install a piano to accompany the singing; and we rejected a choir singing Psalm 23 as an anthem during the offering, on the ground that the singing belongs to the whole congregation.

Now a church wants the whole congregation to sing a close metrical paraphrase of Psalm 23 — same content, reworded for meter and rhyme — a cappella. What should we decide if we want to be consistent with our own precedents? Which of our past rulings is the closest analog? And if our track record genuinely underdetermines it, what principle would we be adopting with each possible decision, and what later case would cleanly tell the two principles apart?
