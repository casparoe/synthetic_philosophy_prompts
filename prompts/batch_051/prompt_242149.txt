I'm a retired chemist who's recently fallen down the modal metaphysics hole, and I keep coming back to Lewis's counterpart theory. Write me a deadpan FAQ page, maybe 700 words, for a start-up selling 'counterpart insurance.' The premise: the founder is a strict counterpart theorist, so on his view the person in the nearest possible world where you loose your job is not you—just a counterpart, similar but not identical—and yet the company still insures you against what happens to that counterpart. The humor has to come from the actual machinery, not generic corporate-speak: counterparts get picked by context-relative similarity, so whether someone in another world counts as your counterpart can shift depending on what features are being compared; the theory refuses transworld identity, so the FAQ must never quietly fall back into 'the same you in another world' except as the joke; and sentences like 'you might have been a plumber' come out true without the plumber ever being you. By the end a reader should feel the real question: if my counterpart isn't me, who is it exactly that I'm relieved not to be?
