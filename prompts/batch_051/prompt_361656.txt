I am a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I have been reading around the question of how a rational agent should revise its probabilities when the incoming information is partial rather than a clean yes/no observation. I am currently writing a small library for probabilistic updating and would like a consistency audit of the working commitments I have settled on before I encode them. Here they are.

(1) Learning a proposition E outright means moving to P'(H) = P(H | E). (2) Learning only that some event E should move to probability q means applying Jeffrey conditionalisation: hold P(H | E) and P(H | not E) fixed, and set P'(H) = q P(H | E) + (1 - q) P(H | not E). (3) Both of the above are special cases of a single minimal-information principle: when one learns only that the posterior must satisfy a constraint C, one should adopt the distribution in C whose Kullback–Leibler divergence from the prior is smallest. (4) In the parachutist scenario I have seen called the Judy Benjamin problem, where a soldier starts with a uniform prior over four map regions, two in Red territory and two in Blue territory, and is told by radio that conditional on being in Red territory the odds are 3 to 1 of being in the Red headquarters sector rather than the other Red sector, the message conveys nothing about Red versus Blue as a whole. The posterior I endorse therefore keeps the two Blue regions at 1/4 each, keeps the total probability of Red territory at 1/2, and apportions Red internally as 3/8 and 1/8. (5) I also take it that updating should be order-independent: if two partial-information messages arrive one at a time, processing them in either order should give the same posterior, and that posterior should match a single update on the combined constraint.

Please treat the deliverable as an audit rather than a tutorial. I want to know which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs merely look tense but are in fact compatible, and a ranking of the real tensions by severity; for each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost, and finish by saying which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent. Please write the answer in British English.
