i'm a third-year philosophy student doing a module on epistemic game theory, and we've spent a few weeks on common knowledge of rationality (CKR) and backward induction. i find the formal side genuinely attractive: CKR plus backward induction gives a definite, non-arbitrary answer in every finite perfect-information game, no free parameters, no hand-waving about "well it depends on the players." but the centipede game keeps bothering me. the result we proved says that if it is common knowledge that both players are rational and know the game, player 1 takes at the first node, even though both would do much better if the pot kept growing and someone took late. i can't make myself believe that's what an ideal rational agent should do, not just what bounded real people do.

the bit that really sticks is the off-path stuff. when i imagine being player 2 and watching player 1 pass to me, i don't think "she must have been irrational, so now anything goes." i think "she passed, so the shared picture has already broken, and maybe i should pass back and keep the good outcome alive." that feels like a rational thought, not a fact about impatience or altruism. i get the standard reply: reaching node 2 is inconsistent with CKR, so the theory is silent there, and my intuition is me sneaking in how actual people play. but then it looks like the clean first-node predicition is bought with a counterfactual the theory hasn't earned—the backward-induction reasoning needs rationality at later nodes that the prediction itself says will never be reached, and it won't tell me how a rational player should update on a probability-zero event.

so i want a negotiated verdict, not a side. give me the strongest version of the debunking move (why caring about off-path play is a confusion, why even an ideal agent should take at node 1) and the strongest version of the revisionist move (that CKR isn't maintainable at off-path nodes, or that backward induction needs an extra assumption about belief revision and is weaker than it looks). is there a reading where both survive—say, the theorem only licences the first-node prediction and goes silent rather than wrong once the path deviates? and in each case: what would justify dropping the intuition, and what would justify amending or abandoning CKR?
