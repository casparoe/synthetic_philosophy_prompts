I'd like you to write a satirical internal memorandum, between 600 and 700 words, from the Office of Legislative Precision in the city of Clearwater, announcing that the city has solved the void-for-vagueness problem once and for all. The background, for the purposes of the piece, is the doctrine that a criminal law can be struck down when it fails to give an ordinary person fair notice of what it forbids and when it invites arbitrary or discriminatory enforcement by leaving police and judges to draw the boundaries. The memorandum should introduce the Zero-Discretion Criminal Code as a straight-faced modest proposal: every offense will be defined by a complete, closed, finitely enumerated taxonomy of sub-acts, and each sub-act by further enumerations, until no borderline case can survive. The target of the satire is the fantasy that vagueness is merely a drafting defect rather than a structural feature of rules, and that fair notice plus a ban on arbitrary enforcement can be achieved by eliminating every borderline case through precision. Write in the deadpan, self-important register of a municipal legal bureaucracy, and keep the irony sustained, with no winking or aside to the reader. The humor should exaggerate real features of the problem rather than invent new ones. The memo should work through three of them: first, the fair-notice anxiety that powers the demand for precision; second, the way each definition pushes the fuzzy edge one level down instead of removing it, so that defining something like standing or loitering becomes an exercise in regress; third, the way the reasonable-person standard the code is trying to abolish keeps getting smuggled back into the definitions. Close with a compliance provision that itself requires interpretation.
