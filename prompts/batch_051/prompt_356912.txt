I keep coming back to a point in the personal identity literature and it's making me feel like I've missed something obvious. Reid's brave officer is supposed to show that Locke's memory criterion makes personal identity intransitive: the old general remembers being the young officer, the officer remembers being the flogged boy, but the general no longer remembers the flogging. So the general is the officer and the officer is the boy but the general isn't the boy — contradiction with transitivity. Fine.

But here's my hangup. Identity is non-negotiable here, so a Lockean has to keep transitivity. Doesn't that force her to say the general does remember being the boy after all, just not consciously — the memory got transmitted through the officer-stage somehow? But thats a weird theory of memory: you either have the memory or you dont. So what is the actual Lockean move supposed to be? And why isn't it just a polite way of pretending that memory itself is transitive when it obviously isn't?
