Write a dialogue between two political theorists at a lunch seminair. One defends sortition—randomly selected assemblies—as the only real cure for the problem of career politicians, and thinks term limits for elected officials are a half-measure that leaves the underlying disease untouched. The other thinks sortition replaces one elite with another (the permanent staff and interest groups who navigate around amateurs) and that well-designed term limits plus strong committee staffs can get most of the benefit without losing electoral accountability. Both should be sharp and willing to concede partial points; neither should be a strawman. Have them actually respond to each other's arguments, not talk past each other. Format: exactly five exchanges, each exchange being one character speaking for a sentence or two and then the other replying, with no introduction or conclusion.
