My philosophy of mind prof is a Rylean, but she uses the category-mistake test in a very hands-on way. She keeps coming back to the passage in The Concept of Mind, chapter 1, where Ryle describes a foreign visitor being shown the colleges, libraries, playing fields, museums, scientific departments, and administrative offices of Oxford or Cambridge and then asking where the University is; and the spectator at a cricket match who is shown the players, the umpire, and the pitch, and then asks where the team spirit is. Her gloss is that the mistake is not asking for one more building or one more player, but treating a collective or dispositional term as though it named an additional item of the same type.

Her verdicts over the term so far:

- the market, in the market is punishing investors, when used to point to a single hidden agent behind the individual trades: category mistake.
- the university, in the university is in debt after a tour of all the buildings and offices: not a category mistake, just a collective noun for the whole lot.
- team spirit, in their team spirit won them the game when used as an extra player alongside the bowlers and fielders: category mistake.
- the will, in the will is a part of the mind like a muscle in the body: category mistake.
- the brain, in a neuroscience lecture, the brain decides before we are conscious of it: not a category mistake, just a loose but acceptable use of decides.
- the mind, in the mind is the brain: not a category mistake, just a false identity claim.
