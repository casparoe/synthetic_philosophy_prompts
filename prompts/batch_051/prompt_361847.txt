I'm a PhD student working on the foundations of dynamical systems, and I've been asked to run a ninety-minute workshop for about fifteen graduate students in a philosophy of science program; most are comfortable with arithmetic but have not thought carefully about nonlinear dynamics. The session is meant to replace a lecture on deterministic chaos. I want participants to leave with a working sense of three things: that a deterministic rule can generate behaviour that is unpredictable in practice; that sensitive dependence on initial conditions is a structural feature of certain systems rather than a defect of measurement; and that the resulting random-looking behaviour can nonetheless have an organized geometry, something like a strange attractor. The butterfly-effect intuition should be rebuilt from the bottom up rather than asserted at the outset.

Design a runnable activity, game, or structured simulation in which participants generate the phenomenon themselves before it is named. I imagine something built around iterating a simple deterministic map — the logistic map, or a two-dimensional map if that would make the attractor's geometry more visible to the eye — with individual participants or pairs starting from slightly different initial conditions and discovering divergence at staggered times; override that guess if another mechanism serves the aims better. Provide the rules and roles, the materials, a timeline for the ninety minutes, and a sequencing that lets participants notice the divergence themselves rather than having it explained to them. Then give a debrief that maps what happened onto the concepts of state space, deterministic dynamics, sensitive dependence, and attractor geometry. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time — round-off error being mistaken for the phenomenon, divergence too slow to notice within the session, one group dominating the plot — and how each should be handled. Finally, be explicit about which part of the target idea the exercise cannot reproduce: I suspect a hand-computed session will struggle to exhibit a genuine strange attractor in its full form, with stretching and folding of a region of state space rather than a plotted point cloud, and I want to know how the facilitator should honestly supply that missing piece.
