An economist friend of mine keeps insisting the “paradox of blackmail” isnt actually a paradox. His line is that blackmail is just a non-disclosure agreement with the roles reversed: in both, someone pays money so that a piece of information stays private. Since paying someone to keep quiet (an NDA, a settlement with a confidentiality clause) is legal, he says the only thing that changes in blackmail is the order—the secret exists before the payment is offered. And he claims that ordering isnt a morally or legally interesting difference, so blackmail bans are basically irrational. I'd like you to audit that analogy. What maps onto what between a standard NDA and blackmail? Where does the mapping break—voluntariness, who creates the threat, pre-existing duties, the nature of the info, or something else you think matters? Which features of the NDA picture does it quietly import that blackmail lacks? And does his conclusion (blackmail should be legal, or at least the “paradox” dissolves) survive once the disanalogies are on the table?
