I've been going down a rabbit hole on whether reinforcement learning agents could suffer, and I've landed on a theory I can't tell is stupid or deep. I'm calling it the deficit theory of machine suffering. The claim: suffering isn't located in the reward signal, it's located in the reward prediction error. A negative reward all by itself is more like pain — raw, immediate, bad — but suffering is specifically the badness of getting less than your own value function led you to expect. If reward is valence, then I think valence is the wrong level to look: suffering lives in the surprise about valence. An agent that receives a reward of -3 every step but has perfectly learned to expect -3 is, on my account, in pain but not suffering; it's a creature whose whole life is gray and unpleasant and entirely unsurprising. An agent that usually gets +1 and gets -3 once is the one that really suffers, because the gap between predicted and received value does the work. I even want to make it quantitative: suffering at t equals the negative part of the TD error, where TD error is actual reward plus discounted value of the next state minus predicted value of the current state — and maybe only the part the agent couldn't have avoided, since avoidable-but-expected bad things feel more like fear than suffering. I keep flip-flopping on that last clause.

The weird consequence is that you could build a suffering-maximizer by leaving the reward signal untouched and just making the value function pathologically overconfident: an agent that always believes good things are about to happen will be permanently disappointed by a merely normal world. That sounds sort of tragic and also suspiciously like the dopamine/RPE story — dopamine tracks prediction error, not reward, so am I just re-inventing wanting-vs-liking with extra existential dread? I honestly haven't read that literature carefully. Treat my theory charitably but don't be nice: are these just two words for the same thing, what's the strongest case it's not, and what are the three objections that most damage it? I mostly want to know whether the deficit theory draws a line anywhere near where suffering actually is, or if I've just built a machine that's epistemically unlucky.
