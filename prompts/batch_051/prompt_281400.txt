For the past few months I have been keeping an informal record of a friend's judgments about which things can be wronged for their own sake, rather than being protected merely because someone who cares about them would be harmed. She has said without much hesitation that an adult human being, a dog, a pig, a chicken, an octopus, and a horse can each be wronged for their own sake; she said the same about a salmon, though she paused before answering. She has said that a honeybee cannot be wronged for its own sake, that an oyster cannot, and that a sea sponge cannot. She denied that a human fetus at ten weeks of gestation can be wronged for its own sake, and she denied that a human being in a permanent vegetative state can be wronged for its own sake, while adding that the person's family could certainly be wronged. She also denied that a honeybee colony amounts to a further subject over and above the individual bees, saying she saw no reason to treat the colony as a kind of super-organism with its own claim to moral consideration. When I described a language model that reports severe suffering, she said that if we stipulate that the system has no phenomenal states at all, then it cannot be wronged for its own sake, regardless of what it says.

The case I now want to predict concerns an organism of the following kind. It is deliberately engineered, or perhaps merely described, so that its entire conscious life consists of a brief, sharp pulse of pain. Each pulse is genuinely felt while it lasts, but the thing that feels one pulse is not, in any psychologically meaningful sense, the same subject as the thing that feels the next: the organism has no memory between pulses, no anticipation of the next pulse, no desire to avoid it, no preferences, no sense of self, and no cumulative biography. If you want the case to have a biological substrate, suppose it has only a minimal circuit that realizes this one repeating flash, with nothing in it that unifies one pulse with the next. What would my friend most likely say: can this organism be wronged for its own sake, or not? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the principle that best explains the whole record of her verdicts, the other principles that fit her earlier verdicts just as well and what each of them predicts about the pulse-organism, and which further case would most decisively settle which principle she is actually running. I would also like to know whether her answer about the pulse-organism would put pressure on any of her earlier verdicts.
