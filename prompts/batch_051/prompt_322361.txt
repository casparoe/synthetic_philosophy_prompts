I'm a software engineer by training, and I recently got into a long argument with a friend who works in cognitive psychology about whether the mind has a modular architecture in anything like the sense we mean when we say a codebase is modular. When we actually enforce modularity in software, the crucial property is encapsulation: a module cannot reach into another module's internal state, and the rest of the system can interact with it only through a narrow, well-defined interface. I gather that the philosopher Jerry Fodor made a similar-sounding claim about parts of the mind, and that he distinguished several features of such "modules"—for example, being fast, mandatory, domain-specific, and informationally encapsulated. Encapsulation, as I understand it, means the system has access only to a restricted range of information and cannot be influenced by what the person knows or believes. Some people (so my friend tells me) have gone further and claimed the mind is "massively modular"—that cognition in general consists of many specialized systems of this sort.

What I find hard to see is how one would test the specifically Fodorian claim about informational encapsulation, rather than the weaker claims that a process is fast, automatic, or specialized. My friend keeps insisting it is an empirical question, but I am not convinced that behavioral evidence can settle whether a process is genuinely cut off from background knowledge or merely acts as though it is, because background knowledge would usually be useless for that task anyway.

So here is what I would like. Suppose I want to test whether a particular process—say early visual processing, or the early stages of sentence parsing—is informationally encapsulated. What is the strongest experimental or observational design you can think of for that? Spell out what pattern of results would favor the encapsulation account, what pattern would count against it, and then be explicit about what such a test would and would not settle. In particular, can any behavioral test distinguish a genuinely encapsulated module from a fast, specialized, but in principle informationally promiscuous system that happens not to consult general knowledge in the cases we can observe? I would rather have a sharp account of what the evidence can and cannot show than a list of experiments.
