I'm designing a philosophy-of-language unit on the geometry of meaning — distributional semantic models, word embeddings, and the claim that semantic structure can be read off vector geometry — and I want to build the unit around Edwin Abbott's Flatland. The students will already have met the basic machinery: tokens embedded as dense vectors, similarity as cosine similarity, and the familiar vector arithmetic that turns king into queen. What I want is a critical reading, not an overview: treat Flatland as a deliberately anachronistic allegory for what an embedding space is and is not. The obvious parallel is that a Flatlander sees only two-dimensional cross-sections of a three-dimensional object and must infer the higher-dimensional structure from them, while an embedding model sees only word co-occurrences and must fit a high-dimensional space to account for them; and just as the Square misreads the Sphere's changing cross-sections as a single mutable object, readers of t-SNE or UMAP plots routinely mistake a two-dimensional projection for the full semantic geometry. I want the reading to press those parallels while distinguishing the respects in which the allegory is genuinely illuminating from the respects in which it is positively misleading, rather than letting it harden into a just-so story. In particular, where does the allegory genuinely illuminate the epistemology — that a higher-dimensional structure can be the best explanation of lower-dimensional data — and where does it actively mislead? Embedding dimensions are not the interpretable east-west and north-south axes of Flatland: the space is defined only up to rotation, so no individual coordinate need correspond to a semantic feature, even when vector differences do. The metric, whether cosine, dot product, or hyperbolic, is chosen by the modeler rather than given. A static embedding gives one vector per word type and thereby collapses distinct senses into a single point. And does the ending — the Sphere, fresh from revealing the third dimension, refusing to countenance a fourth — warn against treating any fitted geometry as final? The students need to finish able to say how far the king − man + woman ≈ queen result supports a genuinely geometric picture of meaning, and what the novel adds to that discussion that a technical treatment of embeddings alone would miss.
