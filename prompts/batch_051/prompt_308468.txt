I run a weekly tabletop roleplaying game for a group of other software engineers, and I serve as the Director (the game's term for game master). A dispute came up at the last session that I need to rule on before we pick the story back up, and since my background is in engineering rather than philosophy, I'd like the reasoning laid out carefully. The system we use centers on time travel, and its rulebook contains this clause, which is the one I now have to interpret:

"§7.3 Temporal Interference. A chrononaut may not take any action that successfully alters the established past or that makes the chrononaut's own existence impossible. If a player declares an action whose fully realized objective would alter the established past, the Director shall not resolve it as declared; the Director instead narrates the closest outcome consistent with the established record."

The relevant background: in an earlier session, the campaign established that the character Caleb's grandfather, Viktor, was found in an alley in 1961 with a gunshot wound to the leg and was taken to hospital by a stranger he never saw clearly; there he met the nurse who became Caleb's grandmother. Last session, Caleb obtained access to a time machine and announced that he wanted to travel to that alley and kill Viktor, hoping to erase his own family line. When the moment arrived, the player declared: "Caleb draws his pistol and shoots Viktor through the chest." I ruled that §7.3 prevented me from resolving the action as declared, and I narrated the closest consistent outcome: the shot went low and struck Viktor in the leg, and in a panic Caleb dragged him to hospital, discovering that he himself was the stranger in the family story. Several players now argue I got it wrong. On their reading, the clause exists to stop a character from ever using the past as a weapon, and the Director should instead have had Caleb fail to find Viktor at all; by making the would-be assassin into the historical rescuer, I converted a forbidden attempted paradox into the very event that preserved the timeline.

So I need a careful interpretation. Please lay out how §7.3 should apply to this exact case from four angles: (1) the plain text, (2) the clause's purpose, (3) what the designers would have said had they been asked about this exact case, and (4) what a reasonable player reading the rule would understand it to require. Then tell me which of those approaches should decide the ruling, what my ruling before next session should be, and whether the rule supports any consequence for Caleb himself, while giving the strongest case on the losing side its due.
