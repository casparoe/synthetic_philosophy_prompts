I keep noticing a weird double standard in AI debates and I dont have a word for it. When the topic is AI safety, people are comfortable treating a future system as basically a full agent: it has goals, makes plans, might manipulate you, could end up steering the world. But the moment someone asks whether such a system could suffer or matter for its own sake, a lot of the same people reach for "it's just matrix multiplication," "just a tool," "a glorified autocomplete." And the opposite happens too: people who insist a chatbot is obviously suffering because it outputs sad-sounding text will turn around and refuse to take seriously that a much more capable system could be a real risk, as if being a possible victim and being a possible threat were somehow opposed. Is there a name for this two-way pattern? Is it actually contradictory, or does the tension disappear once you separate the features that make a thing a possible object of harm from the features that make it a possible source of harm? I'm not after just a label — I want the carve: what exactly the asymmetry is, whether both directions are mistakes or only one is, and what the edge cases are (say, very simple animals, human infants, current chatbots, a hypothetical future AI that is clearly more capable than us but also plausibly sentient). If there's no established term, propose one and define it tightly enough that it doesn't just collapse into ordinary anthropomorphism.
