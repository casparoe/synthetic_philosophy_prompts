I'm putting together a sorting handout for an applied ethics reading group on forgiveness, and I want it to sort people into positions on the two questions the group keeps circling: whether forgiveness can be obligatory (rather than always elective or supererogatory), and whether it can be unconditional (rather than only valid when the wrongdoer repents). Please build it as a decision tree or taxonomy from the logical space, not from a list of named positions: start with the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then locate the actual views in that space. Use these cases as the probes: a friend who betrays a secret and is plainly contrite; an unrepentant wrongdoer who harmed you years ago and has since died; whether you can forgive someone on behalf of a victim who hasn't forgiven them; and self-forgiveness for a harm you can no longer repair. Make the leaves include at least these: the repentance-conditional view, the unconditional-forgiveness view, the "pure forgiveness" view where forgiveness forgives the unforgivable and is somehow never finished or in a sense impossible, the view that forgiveness is something you owe in thick relationships, and the view that what's really required is reconciliation or forbearance rather than forgiveness itself. Where the two master questions can't seperate two of these leaves, add a further splitting question instead of blurring them together. Also mark the unoccupied cells and say for each whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it to around five or six questions and no more than eight leaves. And please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
