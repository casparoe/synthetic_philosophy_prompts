After a long career in the natural sciences, I have lately been working through the philosophy of mind, and I would be grateful for a consistency check on the position I have provisionally assembled about consciousness. I find myself committed to the following propositions:

1. Physicalism: consciousness is wholly realized by physical processes in the brain, and no nonphysical ingredient is required.
2. A rejection of the Cartesian theater: there is no single place in the brain, and no single moment, at which a signal becomes conscious for an inner audience.
3. The central claim of the multiple drafts model: at any instant the brain contains many parallel, partially edited streams of content; over the next few hundred milliseconds these are continuously revised, and a content is conscious only in the functional sense that it comes to guide report, memory, or behavior, not in the sense of being projected into a special medium.
4. For two stimuli presented very close together in time, there is normally a determinate fact about which one I was conscious of first, and that fact is fixed at the time of presentation.
5. It is a first-person datum that, at a given moment, my conscious experience is a single unified field belonging to one subject, rather than a set of parallel drafts.
6. I accept the multiple-drafts treatment of the color phi phenomenon — namely, that the apparent moving spot is represented only after the later flash has arrived, so that the experience is in part a retrospective construction — and I take this to support proposition 3.

I would like an audit rather than a survey. Which pairs among these six are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, please rank it by severity and say what would minimally have to give and what that revision would cost. Since I am new to this literature, please also separate what is broadly settled from what is genuinely contested among informed people working on consciousness, and do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
