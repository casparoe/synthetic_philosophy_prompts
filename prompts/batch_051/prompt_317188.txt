I work as a software engineer on systems that assign individual risk scores for credit, insurance, and fraud, and I keep running into the same obstacle: a single person belongs to a large number of statistical groups, and different groups support different predictions. A loan applicant might fall into a high-default group if classified by postal code, a low-default group if classified by employer, and a middling group if classified by age, and our current system has to pick exactly one classification to produce a single number. I have begun to think the mistake is in that requirement, and I have been sketching an alternative I call the "prediction span."

The idea is this. Rather than choosing one reference group, collect every group that (a) includes the individual and (b) was formed by a rule that was not hand-tuned after the fact to reach a preferred result, compute the base rate inside each, and report the interval between the lowest and highest base rates as the prediction itself. If a decision threshold falls inside that interval, the system should refuse to auto-decide and flag the case as genuinely indeterminate. So instead of saying a person has a 9% default risk, it might say their risk is between 3% and 14%, and if the lending threshold is 10%, the model declines to decide. I find this appealing because it seems to dissolve the problem of selecting the correct group: there is no correct group, and the honest object is the span, not a point. It also seems to me that many fairness disputes about these systems are secretly disputes about which groupings count as acceptably drawn rather than gerrymandered, since that boundary is exactly what moves the span.

I would like an honest, candid assessment. Is this a coherent proposal, or does it just relabel the original choice and smuggle it back in? Has something in statistics or the philosophy of probability already worked this out under another name? Give me the three strongest objections to the prediction span, and tell me whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable. Please also say how confident you are in your assessment overall and single out the specific point you are least sure of.
