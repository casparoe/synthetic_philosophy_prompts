Write a dialogue between a neuroscientist and a philosopher of mind about whether a brain–computer interface could give someone a genuinely new colour experience, specifically a stable reddish-green (one of the so-called forbidden colours). Make the neuroscientist argue that since, on opponent-process accounts, red-green opposition is a feature of early visual coding rather than a law about possible experience, directly driving cortical colour areas in patterns that never occur in normal vision could decouple the pair and produce a quale that is not a blend, not rivalry, and not a fast flicker. Make the philosopher argue that this is less coherent than it sounds: a subject who says 'reddish-green' is either describing an unstable composite of familiar colours or confabulating, and a truly novel colour couldn't be recognized and reported with existing colour vocabulary. Make them actually respond to each other's points rather than talk past each other—for example, the philosopher should press on how we would distinguish a novel quale from confabulation or demand characteristics, and the neuroscientist should push back on the assumtion that qualitative novelty requires prior conceptual resources. Neither side should be a strawman. End the dialogue with them narrowing the disagreement to one point where it becomes unclear whether the question is empirical or conceptual.
