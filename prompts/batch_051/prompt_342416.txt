Here is an argument, reconstructed as carefully as I can, for the claim that the simulation argument dampens fanaticism about the far future — where by fanaticism I mean the view that the expected value of influencing the long-run future so dominates our choices that it swamps near-term considerations.

(1) If our civilization has the kind of future longtermists take seriously — survival, expansion, and immense computational capacity — then our descendants will almost certainly run vast numbers of high-fidelity simulations of their own past.

(2) If that is so, then the overwhelming majority of consciousnesses with experiences relevantly like ours are inhabitants of such simulations, not of the base-level world.

(3) Because your current evidence does not discriminate between these two possibilities, you should assign substantial credence to the proposition that you are simulated.

(4) If you are simulated, you have no causal purchase on the base-level far future: your actions can at most alter what happens inside the simulation.

(5) And in the simulated branch there is no far future of the sort fanaticism needs: either the simulation ends around our era, or, if it continues, the future it contains is itself simulated — and a merely simulated far future is not the kind of thing that could bear the enormous moral weight fanaticism assigns to it.

(6) Therefore, on the very assumptions that make the far future vast and important, you are probably simulated, and so fanaticism about the far future should be sharply dampened.

Each step strikes me as individually plausible, but the conclusion feels like a bait-and-switch, and I cannot locate the move that produces the overreach. Is this argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails, explain what is wrong with it, and give the strongest corrected version of that step — or say whether no reasonable correction can make the conclusion follow. If you think a premise is being smuggled in rather than located in a single numbered step, identify that premise and say where it is needed.
