In "The Unreality of Time" (Mind, 1908), J. M. E. McTaggart writes:

"It would, I suppose, be universally admitted that time involves change. A particular thing, indeed, may exist unchanged through any amount of time. But when we ask what we mean by saying that there were different moments of time, or a certain duration of time, through which the thing was the same, we find that we mean that it remained the same while other things were changing. A universe in which nothing whatever changed (including the thoughts of the conscious beings in it) would be a timeless universe."

I am drawn to an eternalist, B-theoretic account of time: the view that all times and their contents are equally real, and that the fundamental temporal facts are permanent relations of earlier and later, while pastness, presentness, and futurity are not objective features of the world at all. The view earns its keep by giving a single tenseless description of reality, by refusing to privilege any moment, and by fitting more naturally with special relativity than its moving-spotlight or growing-block rivals. And yet I have a firm, pre-theoretical conviction that time involves change in a stronger sense than this view allows: events become present and then recede into the past, and that becoming is part of what it is to be temporal. The quoted passage is the sharpest statement of my worry—if the B-theorist's world is one in which nothing comes to be present or past, then, in McTaggart's terms, has it not lost change and thereby become a timeless universe?

Present both positions at their strongest and tell me which should yield, or whether they can be reconciled. Should I debunk the intuition of passage as a perspectival illusion and retain the eternalist theory? Should I abandon or amend the theory in the direction of an A-theoretic moving present? Or is there a reading of "change" on which an object's having different properties at different B-times counts as genuine change, making the intuition and the theory compatible? Whatever you conclude, tell me what would justify each possible move rather than simply choosing a side.
