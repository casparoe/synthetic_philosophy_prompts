I've been rereading A Clockwork Orange alongside Persson and Savulescu on moral bioenhancement, and I can't shake the feeling that the Ludovico treatment is just a cruder version of what they're recommending. It doesn't make Alex a better moral reasoner, obviously: it just makes the thought of violence physically sickening. But maybe the novel's point is that any moral enhancement that actually worked on someone like Alex would have to bypass his will in exactly that way, because the bit of him that chooses is the malfunctioning part. I'd like a reading that treats Burgess's novel as doing philosophy rather than illustrating a paper, and that says where Persson and Savulescu's account of enhancement can't absorb what the book is showing. Does the novel's worry that goodness has to be withholdable to count survive their likely reply that a proper moral enhancement would enlarge agency rather than override it? And be honest about the other direction: what does the novel romanticise about Alex's freedom that their argument rightly gets suspicious of?
