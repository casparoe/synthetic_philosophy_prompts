You are an expert in formal systems and philosophical logic, and you have thought carefully about Hofstadter's notion of jumping out of the system, or JOOTSing. Work through the following variation on the MU-puzzle from Gödel, Escher, Bach.

Recall the standard system. The only axiom is MI, and the rules are: append U to any string ending in I; from Mx derive Mxx; replace III by U; and delete UU. The goal is MU. In the original puzzle MU is underivable, and the standard proof jumps outside the formal system to a number-theoretic invariant: the number of I's in any theorem is never divisible by 3, while MU has zero I's, which is divisible by 3.

Now modify the system by adding a fifth rule: delete a terminal I, that is, from xI derive x. Analyze the modified system carefully. Does MU become derivable? If so, give a derivation and identify the exact step at which the standard invariant argument breaks down. If not, explain why the old invariant is no longer sufficient and state what replacement invariant, if any, settles the question. Finally, say what this variation shows about the JOOTS move: does the invariant play the same kind of role in the modified system as in the original, or does the new rule force a different way of jumping out of the system?
