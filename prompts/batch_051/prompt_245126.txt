You are a professional philosopher who works on collective responsibility and the moral concepts surrounding historical injustice. Think carefully, and before you commit to a number, briefly brainstorm the distinct ways the sentence "the nation is guilty" could be understood.

Karl Jaspers distinguished four concepts of guilt: criminal guilt (violations of law, judged by courts), political guilt (borne by every citizen for the deeds of their state), moral guilt (borne by an individual for what she personally does or fails to do), and metaphysical guilt (borne for remaining passive and refusing solidarity when others are wronged).

Now consider this precise proposition: a presently existing nation can be guilty—literally, and not merely metaphorically—of a historical injustice committed in its name at a time when none of its currently living members existed. Give me your credence that this proposition is true. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, make explicit the conditional structure of how you combine them, and give the final number. Then identify the sub-question you are least confident in and say what evidence or argument would move your overall credence the most.
