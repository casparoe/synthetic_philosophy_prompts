What would an adequate theory of games as art have to get right, once the question is fixed as: when is the agency a game gives you an aesthetic achievement, rather than merely a design or entertainment feature? I want the requirements list, not a ranking of games or a survey of positions. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is negotiable; then identify which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular theories. The list must at least handle: (1) agency under designed constraint, where the interesting cases are choices the player would rather not make, or choices that are costly no matter what; (2) failure, loss, and incompetent play as possible aesthetic values rather than defects to be patched with difficulty settings; (3) the difference between the designed game, a particular playthrough, and a performance observed by others, so the theory does not quietly crown one of them as the work; and (4) the difference between agency and freedom, and between difficulty and frustration. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them, not on scoring particular theories. A table is fine.
