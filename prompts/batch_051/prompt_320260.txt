I teach an upper-level undergraduate course in philosophy of mind and am writing the final examination. Draft one essay question on the reliability of introspection, to be answered in fifty minutes under closed-book conditions. It should not be answerable by rehearsing the familiar skeptical conclusion that introspection is fallible; it should force students to distinguish what introspection might tell us about the character of one's current experience from what it might tell us about the causes of one's own judgments and actions, and to assess how far the experimental findings of confabulated reasons, choice blindness, and self-perception effects bear on each. It should also require students to take a defensible stand on whether one can consistently grant the empirical critique of introspective access to causes while maintaining privileged access to current phenomenal states. Provide a model answer of about six hundred words and a marking scheme worth twenty marks built around five must-hit points; for each point, give the marks and a one-line note on how candidates typically go wrong.
