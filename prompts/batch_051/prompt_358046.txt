Imagine you could sit Malthus down in the present day and show him the last two centuries: per-capita food supply is up, famine nowadays is mostly a product of war or state failure rather than simple overpopulation, and plenty of rich countries now have below-replacment fertility. Would he conclude that his basic claim—that population tends to outrun the food supply—had been falsified, or would he insist he was right but early, that the checks he predicted had merely been postponed or had changed form? Work out the most consistent position he could take using his own framework, and be specific about where he would draw the line between still early and disconfirmed. In particular, what future evidence, if anything, would even count against a long-run prediction like his?
