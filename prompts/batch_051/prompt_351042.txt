Me and my two friends Carlos and Dana have been arguing for about a year about something we can't settle. Carlos is obsessed with a thought experiment: imagine a person exactly like a normal person on the outside — talks, laughs, cries at funerals, says "ouch" when burned — but on the inside the lights are off. Not unconscious, not acting, just no inner show. No pain felt, no taste of coffee, nothing it's like to be them. He calls them "zombies," but not the movie kind. His argument is: if you can hold that whole idea in your head without contradiction, then it's at least really possible; and if it's really possible, then the inner life must be something extra over and above the physical, because all the physical stuff could stay the same while the experience disappears. Dana says it's a word trick, like saying "married bachelor," and that Carlos never actually imagines the thing — he just says the words, and the words hide a contradiction.

The wrinkle is that Carlos can't form mental pictures. He found out a few years ago that when other people "picture an apple" they actually see something; he gets nothing. So when Carlos says he can imagine the zombie, he means he can hold the whole description in his head and see no conflict in it, not that he pictures anything. Dana keeps saying thats not really imagining. I think that's one reason they've been stuck, and it might turn out to be more than just a language thing.
