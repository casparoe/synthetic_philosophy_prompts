ok so I've been trying to land a structuralist view of arithmetic that doesn't just dodge Benacerraf's problem about whether 2 is an element of 4, and here's the position I've ended up with. Tear into it.

(1) The natural numbers aren't any particular objects, and they're definately not sets. 2 is a position or place in the natural-number structure: the second slot in any system of things that has the right shape, an ω-sequence say.

(2) So I think the question whether 2 is an element of 4 is a category mistake rather than something with a false answer. Membership is a relation between sets, and numbers aren't sets, so it's as ill-posed as asking whether the number 7 is blue.

(3) But I don't want to say numbers are nothing. I quantify over them all the time, I think it's true that there is a prime between 12 and 16, and I have no problem forming sets of numbers, like {2, 13} or the singleton {2}. A position has to be a thing you can collect, otherwise set theory couldn't even get started on arithmetic.

(4) I also think there is exactly one natural-number structure. The second-order Peano axioms pin it down: any two systems satisfying them are isomorphic, and that's enough for the phrase the natural numbers to pick out a unique structure.

(5) I'm tempted to say something stronger: isomorphic structures just are the same structure. If two systems are isomorphic, they don't instantiate two copies of the structure; the structure is exactly what they have in common.

(6) Finally, I'm happy with set theory as usual: there's always a determinate answer whether one set is a member of another, {2} exists and has exactly one member, and {2} is not the same object as 2.

For each real tension, tell me the cheapest fix and what it costs, and then say which single revision would clean up the most.
