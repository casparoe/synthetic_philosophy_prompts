I'm trying to settle a spat with a friend about whether LLM hallucinations (like when ChatGPT invents a court case and states it confidently) count as bullshit in Harry Frankfurt's sense. She took a class on this years ago and sent me her summary:

"Frankfurt's basic move is that lying and bullshitting are both ways of deliberately saying false things to manipulate your audience. The only real difference is that the liar knows the truth and says the opposite, where the bullshitter doesn't care enough to check. That's why he calls bullshit a greater enemy of truth than lying: the liar at least has to keep track of the truth, but the bullshitter is totally untethered from it. So an LLM hallucination can't be lying, since the model has no intention to deceive. But by the same token it can't be bullshitting either, because bullshitting is something a person does with a goal. That leaves plain error: the model is trying to get things right and occasionally fails."

I have a feeling there are one or two subtle misreadings in there, especially the jump from "no intentions" to "therefore just error," but I can't quite articulate them. Could you go through her summary line by line and say which parts Frankfurt would accept, which are common simplifications or conflations, and whether the final verdict (hallucination is just error, not bullshit) actually follows? Also, does Frankfurt's framework leave any room for talk about "bullshit" produced by a system that has no intentions at all, or is applying the term to LLMs a category mistake?
