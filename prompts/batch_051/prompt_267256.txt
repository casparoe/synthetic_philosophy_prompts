I cover philosophy and ideas for a general-interest magazine. Bertrand Russell, in his 1905 essay *On Denoting*, writes: "denoting phrases never have any meaning in themselves, but that every proposition in whose verbal expression they occur has a meaning." A case Russell did not foresee: an interactive-fiction studio is releasing a serialized story in which a character named Ana Vane is defined only by a changing, partly user-submitted set of episodes, with no single canonical text settling which claims about her are part of the story. How, if at all, does Russell's principle apply to this case? Specifically, if "Ana Vane" is a denoting phrase that does not denote, are sentences such as "Ana Vane is a detective" meaningful in the way Russell says the whole proposition is meaningful even when the denoting phrase has no meaning in itself—and are such sentences true, false, or something else when they occur inside the fiction? What should the studio say to a user who insists that Ana Vane exists because she has properties in the story?
