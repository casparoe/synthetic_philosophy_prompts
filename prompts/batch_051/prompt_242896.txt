I'm a novelist by trade, but I adjunct a section of intro philosophy of religion and I'm behind on grading. The midterm had a short-essay question on original sin, and I need to put a grade and comments on this one. Here's the question I gave:

"Explain the difference between original sin as inherited corruption and original sin as inherited guilt, and make sure to seperate the two readings clearly. Which reading, if any, did Augustine hold, and what is the strongest argument for and against the idea that inherited guilt makes God a punisher of the innocent? Keep to about 300 words."

And here's the student's answer, word for word:

"Original sin is the condition all humans are born into after Adam's fall. The corruption reading says we inherit a wounded nature — disordered desires and a will that inclines to sin — but not the blame for Adam's act itself. The guilt reading says we inherit not just damage but a share of the liability for the first sin, so that even infants are born answerable for a transgression they did not commit.

Augustine is standardly read as defending the guilt reading. He takes Romans 5:12 to mean that all sinned in Adam, and he ties original sin to concupiscence passed on through procreation. That is why he insists on infant baptism: baptism is for the remission of sins, and infants must have some real sin to be remitted. Pelagius denied the transmission of any sin, saying Adam was only a bad example and each newborn begins with the same moral ability Adam had.

The strongest objection is that guilt seems to require a voluntary act, so Augustine appears to punish people for a choice they never made. The strongest reply is that Adam acted as the representative head of human nature, so his act counts as theirs corporately and is not a foreign deed. The church's condemnation of Pelagius settled exactly this in favor of the guilt reading, which is why Western theology has always taught inherited guilt rather than mere inherited corruption."
