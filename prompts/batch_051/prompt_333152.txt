Consider the claim that happiness is a by-product good: deliberately targeting one's own positive affect tends to make it recede, while the same affect appears reliably when attention is absorbed in an activity pursued for its own sake. The patterns usually cited for this — people told to maximize mood during a pleasant task enjoy it less than people told simply to do the task, and people who report strongly valuing happiness often report lower well-being — are equally consistent with deflationary readings, such as unhappiness driving people to monitor and value happiness, or self-reports being biased. How would one design a test that separates the by-product mechanism from those alternatives? Specify the comparison conditions, the outcome measures, the result pattern that would favor the by-product reading, and the result pattern that would favor a deflationary reading. Then say what even a conclusive experiment could not settle: whether it has established a causal law about motivation, a conceptual truth about what being happy consists in, or a normative claim about which goals one ought to adopt.
