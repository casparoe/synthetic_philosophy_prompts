In arguments about whether large language models could ever understand language, I keep encountering the same line from the Chinese Room discussion: "we cannot get from syntactical to the semantic just by having the syntactical operations and nothing else." People use it to close the debate down — to say that a program, no matter how impressive its outputs, is only ever doing syntax, so that no question of meaning or understanding can even arise. I want to know what that sentence could actually be asserting. Lay out the distinct precise claims it might be expressing — about what understanding requires, about what a symbol-manipulating system is missing, about whether the obstacle is one of principle or just of present technology — and for each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive defensible claim, or something that could be false. Finally, tell me which of those readings would actually license the dismissals I keep seeing, and which would not.
