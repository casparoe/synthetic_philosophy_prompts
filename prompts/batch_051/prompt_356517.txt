I chair my university's Committee on Campus Events and Free Expression. A registered student organization has applied to host a visiting speaker next term. The organization is best described as illiberal rather than merely conservative: its platform calls for excluding certain religious and ethnic groups from campus life, its members have previously followed minority students into residential areas while filming them, and one of its earlier events ended in a confrontation that a security review described as coordinated, though the review stopped short of attributing the coordination to the group itself. Our university's stated policy commits us both to viewpoint neutrality in allocating rooms and funds and to maintaining an environment free from harassment and discrimination, and nothing in the policy tells us what to do when the two commitments come apart. The committee is evenly split. One faction holds that a tolerant institution must tolerate even speech it finds repugnant, and that free expression exists precisely to protect views the majority finds hateful. The other faction invokes the paradox of tolerance: roughly, a community that extends its protections to those whose goal is to dismantle those protections is not being principled but self-defeating, and the past incidents are evidence that this group is such a case.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before Wednesday's meeting. Argue for a recommendation among the three live options: approve the event with no conditions beyond those applied to any other group; approve with restrictions such as a smaller venue, ticketing, security, a moderator, and a ban on filming in residential areas; or deny the application. Do not simply invoke the paradox of tolerance as if it settled the matter. Reconstruct the strongest version of it as it bears on this decision, say carefully what it does and does not imply for a single university event as opposed to a society-wide policy, and address the standing objection that this kind of reasoning licenses whoever holds power to deny a platform to whatever it classifies as intolerant. Also indicate which facts, if different, would flip your recommendation.

Please write the memo under the following section headings, in this order, with the approximate lengths indicated, for a total of roughly 800 words: (1) Recommendation (about 75 words), stating the bottom line up front; (2) The decision and the constraints (about 100 words), setting out the policy and the three options; (3) The case for approval (about 150 words); (4) The case for denial or restrictions, including a careful statement of the paradox of tolerance (about 150 words); (5) Analysis and recommended conditions (about 200 words); (6) Response to the strongest objection to your recommendation (about 100 words).
