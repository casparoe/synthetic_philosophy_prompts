I am currently taking an undergraduate module in moral epistemology, and one question that has stayed with me is whether moral deference is ever legitimate: whether I may properly adopt a moral belief simply on the authority of a person I trust, without grasping the grounds for it myself. I would like to explore that question through a counterfactual, because the actual world makes it difficult to separate the effects of moral testimony from the many other ways in which moral beliefs spread.

Suppose that in 2030 a cheap, reliable and essentially side-effect-free intervention becomes available in a medium-sized liberal democracy. The intervention has one effect: whenever a person becomes aware that the sole basis on which they currently hold a moral belief is another person's assertion, that belief loses its felt conviction within roughly a week unless the person can by then articulate some non-testimonial support for it — an argument, a remembered first-hand experience, an emotional response to the relevant situation, or a pattern of reasoning — that they themselves find at least mildly compelling. The effect applies only to moral beliefs; empirical, prudential and aesthetic beliefs are unaffected.

Assume adoption is uneven but substantial: within a decade perhaps half the adult population uses the intervention, with uptake concentrated among the university-educated and in the caring professions, while many religious communities and civic organisations abstain. I am less interested in whether the intervention would be beneficial overall than in a precise, mechanism-based account of what changes over the following thirty years. Please address at least these four questions: (i) does the pace of moral change accelerate or decelerate, and does society become more or less polarised; (ii) what happens to the authority of moral experts, from applied ethicists to advice columnists and clergy; (iii) how do parents, teachers and religious communities adapt when moral claims can no longer be transmitted by instruction alone; and (iv) which kinds of moral beliefs become more common and which become rarer. For each prediction, indicate how confident you are, identify the link in your reasoning that could most plausibly fail, and say what would have to be true for the prediction to turn out otherwise. Please do not include a bibliography or suggestions for further reading; I am asking for your reasoning rather than a survey of studies.
