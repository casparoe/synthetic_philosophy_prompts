Exam question: Explain how Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility theory represents risk-avoidant preferences, and why a risk-avoidant REU maximizer cannot in general be redescribed as an expected utility maximizer with a concave utility function.

Student's answer: Buchak replaces the expected utility formula ∑ p_i u(x_i) with a rule that orders outcomes from worst to best and weights each marginal utility increment u(x_i) − u(x_{i−1}) by r(p_i + ⋯ + p_n), the risk-weighted probability of getting at least x_i. A risk-avoidant agent has r(p) ≤ p, which overweights the marginal contributions of worse outcomes and underweights those of better outcomes; this is how the theory captures Allais-style choices, such as preferring a certain $1 million to an 89/10/1 gamble with higher expected value, while still satisfying stochastic dominance. Since the risk function only changes the weights on utility differences, and since a concave utility function is the standard expected-utility way to encode risk aversion, any risk-avoidant REU maximizer is really just an expected utility maximizer with a concave utility function; the two models are notational variants.
