I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on a word for a book set near a border that has moved back and forth depending on which empire won the last war. The book keeps circling the question of who owes what to whom, and I realize I cant actually say what "compatriot" means beneath the passport. I want a working definition of compatriot — not the legal citizenship sense, but the thicker one that is supposed to carry special claims on people. Then run the definition through the cases below and tell me where it gives a verdict you didn't expect, or where it just refuses to answer.

1. A woman born in the country, with the passport, who left at age two and has never been back and doesn't speak the language.

2. A man who arrived forty years ago, naturalized twenty years ago, raised his kids there, and calls it home; his neighbors still introduce him as "a foreigner, but one of the good ones."

3. A second-generation kid born in the country who supports the national football team but otherwise says his real people are his parents' nation, where he has never lived.

4. A farmer across the border who shares the language, religion, and clan name of the majority group in the country, and who says the border was drawn through his living room.

5. An exile who fled after a coup, still holds the passport, but says she's from nowhere now.

6. A dual citizen who votes in both countries and describes himself as whichever nationality the audience likes more.

I think 2 is clearly a compatriot and 6 is clearly a badge of convenience, but I honestly dont know about 1 and 4. Propose the definition, apply it, and then say whether anything in the list made you revise it.
