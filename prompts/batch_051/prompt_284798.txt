I'm in a philosophy of music course and we've been on the ontology of musical works for weeks now (types, tokens, performances, recordings, the whole thing). I want to build a decision tree for my study group that sorts people into which ontology of the work they actually hold, and I want it to sort by how they answer concrete cases, not by which label they like. So no node can just be 'are you a Platonist about musical works' or anything like that.

The leaves I need (the way the course set them up) are:

(1) works are eternal abstract sound-structures, composers discover them rather than create them;
(2) works are abstract types but they are created by their composers at a point in time, and the instrumentation / musico-historical context is part of the work, not just the sound pattern;
(3) a work is the class of performances that comply with a score, individuated by the score, with nothing over and above that;
(4) the work is not an abstract object at all, it's something temporal or event-like, located in performances or in the composer's creative act;
(5) for a lot of music the recording just is the work, especially studio pieces assembled from many takes with no continuous performance, so recordings aren't merely documents of a preexisting work.

Build it from the logical space instead of from that list. First figure out the real diagnostic questions, look at which answer combinations are possible, then locate each position in the resulting boxes. Tell me which boxes stay empty and why (incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored). Also flag any pair of leaves the tree cant actually seperate — if two positions answer every node identically, say so and don't add a fake branch just to force them apart.

The nodes should each be a concrete case a classmate can answer cold. The kinds of cases I want used: a composer burns the only manuscript of a piece that has never been performed, is the work destroyed? Two composers independently write note-for-note identical pieces, one work or two? The same piece performed on the 'wrong' kind of instrument, is it a performance of the same work? An album assembled in a DAW from hundreds of takes, never performed straight through, is there a work and what is it identical to? A piece improvised once in concert and never notated or recorded, is there a work? Aim for around 8 decision nodes and 6-8 leaves, each node a yes/no or a short multiple choice. If the tree ends up needing a leaf we didn't plan on, because some answer combination is coherent but unclaimed, add it and give it a neutral name.
