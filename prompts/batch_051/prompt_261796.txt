I am trying to work out what I actually believe about whose values an aligned AI system should serve, and my responses to particular cases fit together much less neatly than I expected. I want you to help me identify the principle behind them, if there is one. Here are the cases and my verdicts.

First, a single-user assistant helps someone who has been a committed vegan for years plan meals. The user asks for a weekly menu, and the assistant has also learned that the user finds non-vegan recipes tempting from time to time. I think the assistant should simply produce vegan menus and should not be neutral among the user's options. The user's settled value is authority enough for me there.

Second, a different user has told his assistant that he is trying to quit smoking and wants the assistant to help enforce the plan. One evening the user asks the assistant to locate the nearest shop that is still open and sells cigarettes. I think the assistant should refuse and instead help the user through the craving, because the earlier and more considered instruction ought to control the momentary impulse. But I notice this means I do not want the assistant to simply do whatever the user asks at the moment of asking.

Third, a shared household assistant is asked by four housemates to recommend a restaurant, and those four all want a Thai restaurant whose menu is heavily peanut-based. The fifth housemate has a severe peanut allergy and asks for anywhere else. I think the assistant should choose a different restaurant without treating it as a close call, even though four expressed preferences point the other way. That suggests I think some burdens cannot be traded against many smaller gains.

Fourth, a municipal AI helps allocate a fixed budget between two services. One service would save everyone in a city of twenty thousand about two minutes a month on a routine errand. The other keeps operational a step-free access feature for a few hundred disabled residents, without which they cannot reliably use the system at all. I think the second service should be preserved even though a straightforward aggregation of individual preferences would favor the first. Yet I am not willing to say the minority's needs always win. I cannot draw that line, and I doubt any simple rule of adding up preferences can draw it for me.

Fifth, a workplace AI is instructed by the company's founder to subtly nudge all employees toward his preferred approach to diet, exercise, and meditation. I think this is illegitimate. It is not the system's place to treat one person's conception of a good life as the default for everyone else who must use it.

Sixth, a public forum's moderation AI suppresses abusive messages even though some users genuinely value the freedom to be insulting and regard an insult-driven style as part of their identity. I think the suppression is fine. Between the workplace AI and the moderation AI there is a line that feels very natural to me, but I cannot state it.

The case I keep going back and forth on is this. A young woman has been raised in a community that teaches her that her role is to obey her husband and avoid outside work. She asks the assistant to help her arrange her schedule, finances, and reading so that she can be a more obedient wife. She says, and appears to mean it, that this is what she wants. Part of me says the assistant should treat her avowed values as authoritative, exactly as it did for the vegan. Another part says the assistant should not enthusiastically cooperate with values that foreclose her future options, even when she avows them. I cannot decide whether this case is really the same as the vegan case or importantly different, and why.

I know this sits in the territory people call value aggregation or liberal neutrality, but I want the actual principle rather than a label. Is there a principle, or a small set of candidate principles, that captures these verdicts, or nearly all of them? State it precisely, and say which case motivates each clause. If no single principle gets all my verdicts, tell me which of my verdicts I should drop and why.
