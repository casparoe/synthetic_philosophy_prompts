Then assess whether benign violation theory is genuinely distinct from incongruity theory or whether it is best understood as a version of it. Defend your assessment."

Here is exactly what one student wrote:

"Superiority theory says we laugh because we feel a sudden sense of being above someone else, like when we see someone trip and feel better about our own situation. Incongruity theory says humour comes from clashing expectations—we expect one thing, the punchline gives us another, and we enjoy the mismatch. Relief theory, which comes from Freud, says humour releases built-up psychic energy, especially energy connected to socially forbidden impulses. Benign violation theory says something is funny when we see it as both a violation of a norm or expectation and, at the same time, as okay or harmless. I think benign violation is really just a version of incongruity theory. Both theories treat humour as the collision of two incompatible representations: expected versus actual in incongruity, wrong versus safe in benign violation. The vocabulary is different, but the mechanism is the same. The only extra thing benign violation adds is the condition that the violation not actually be harmful, and that is just an added constraint on the same reconciliation process rather than a new mechanism. So I agree with the statement."
