Someone showed me the color phi thing a while back and it's been bugging me. You flash a red dot on the left, then a green dot on the right, a short distance apart, maybe a tenth of a second later. People report seeing one dot glide across and turn green about halfway — spooky, because the color change gets reported before the green flash could possibly have gotten into the head. The two standard stories are: Orwellian (you genuinely saw a red dot go the whole way, and your memory got quietly rewritten once the green arrived) and Stalinesque (your experience was held back until the brain had both dots, the whole glide got staged, and only then did you get to see it). Everyone says there's no way to tell them apart.

So here's my variation, and I want you to work through what each story says about it. Keep the timing and positions the same, but make the two flashes differ only in shape, not color: a circle on the left and a triangle on the right, same color, same brightness. People report a single thing sliding over and morfing from circle to triangle around the midpoint. Now add this: when the shape change is made harder to see — say, a circle and a nine-sided figure — people still put the morph at the midpoint, but their confidence that it was already the new shape at the midpoint is lower than their confidence that the moving dot was already green at the midpoint in the original red/green version.

Run the Orwellian and the Stalinesque stories on this shape version. Does the no-fact-of-the-matter point carry straight over, or does swapping shape for color give any new traction? Is there any setup in this neighborhood where the two stories would come apart, and if there is, what would that tell us about when experience happens?
