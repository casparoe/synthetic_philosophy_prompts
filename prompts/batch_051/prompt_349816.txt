I keep noticing something in videos of hearing children of deaf adults, and I don't have a word for it. They'll speak and sign at the same time, but the two streams aren't translations of each other: the hands sometimes carry the main verb while the voice only supplies little grammatical words, or the mouth silently shapes a word from the spoken language while the signs do something else. It's clearly systematic, yet the combined utterance doesn't seem to belong to either language on its own. Is there an established name for this, and does it put pressure on the idea that a language is a self-contained system with one grammer? I'm not just after a label — I want to know whether the person is producing one language, two, or something in between, and what would settle that. Please answer in British English.
