I run a weekly Nomic game with friends—it's the game where changing the rules is itself a legal move. Over the years we've let the rulebook grow into a sprawling mess, but one rule has been there from the start and nobody has ever tried to touch it. It reads:

"Rule 1. Any player may propose an amendment to the rules. A proposal is adopted if it receives two-thirds of the votes cast at a scheduled session. Rule 1 may be amended only by unanimous vote of all players."

Then, last Thursday, one of the players put forward this amendment:

"Rule 1.1: A proposal that changes the voting threshold for amending Rule 1 is adopted by a simple majority. Rule 1.1 takes effect before any vote on the proposal that enacts it."

He claims he found a genuine gap in the rules. The vote on his proposal was 6–4 (we had ten players that night), so not two-thirds and obviously not unanimous, but he now insist it passed because Rule 1.1, if it is valid, applies to the vote on itself. The older players say the unanimity clause was meant to stop exactly this kind of procedural power grab, but they admit they never pictured someone lowering the vote threshold for the very vote that lowers it.

I don't actually care who wins the game. I want to understand what a self-referential amendment procedure is even supposed to do here. Walk me through the different readings: the plain words of Rule 1, the purpose of the unanimity clause, what the drafters would have said if someone had asked them "can a proposal lower the threshold for lowering the threshold?", and what a reasonable player reading the rule in the moment would take it to mean. Where do those readings come apart, and which one would you actually apply to this vote? Is there any coherent reading under which Rule 1.1 both was not valid before the vote and yet governed the vote that enacted it?
