Two people in my research group recently argued about how to classify a large language model that confidently produces a false answer, and I couldn't referee it. The first said the best description is Frankfurt's bullshit: on Frankfurt's account, the liar deliberately departs from a truth he knows, while the bullshitter is simply unconcerned with whether what he says is true, aiming only to produce a certain impression — and a model trained and prompted to generate plausible, useful-seeming text does exactly the latter. The second replied that this is a category mistake: Frankfurt's bullshitter is an agent with intentions and an audience whose impression he is managing, whereas a model has no intentions, no awareness of an audience, and no attitude of indifference toward truth; its false output is simply an error. The first came back that the agency sits in the deployed system as a whole: the company that ships the model and the user who presents its output as an answer are together doing the asserting, and at least the company is bullshitting with the model as its instrument; and in any case, the text itself presents itself as factual, and a practice that systematically produces truth-indifferent assertions is bullshit wherever we locate the agency. The second answered that the company never intended any particular falsehood and is usually harmed by it, so Frankfurt's distinction — drawn for a speaker on a particular occasion who either knows and hides the truth or ignores it — does not carry over to a statistical process spread across an institution; and "the text presents itself" is just a metaphor, since a typo can produce a false claim and a typo is not bullshit. If the real explanation is that the training objective rewards plausibility over truth, that is an explanation of a mechanical failure, not a moralized speech-act vocabulary.

Then assess the argument step by step: for each of the four moves, say who is right about which point, whether either claim is clearly mistaken, and what both parties are overlooking. Finally, give your verdict on how a confident false LLM output should be classified — lying, bullshit, error, or something else — and defend where you draw the boundaries.
