I've spent the last while defending something I call the tedious-mystic view and I want it stress-tested rather than cheered on. The core claim is that there are no in-principle unsayable experiences; there are only experiences whose shortest adequate description is so long that no one would sit through it. When a mystic says the experience can't be put into words, I read them as saying something true but boring: the words would be a curriculum, not a sentence. You'd have to transmit the practices, the imagery, the bodily states, the triggers, maybe years of them. So 'ineffable' names a transmission problem, not a special sort of content that language can't touch. From that I conclude the question whether what can't be said can still be known is badly posed: it can be known, but only in the ordinary first-person way I know a hangover, which gives mysticism nothing special. I know there's a long apophatic tradition that would hate this. Is my view just a known position with a sillier name? And where does it actually break — what's the hardest case for it, the experience that seems unsayable even in principal, and is that case fatal or just uncomfortable?
