So I've been trying to write out my actual position on Scripture and translation for a theology reading group, and before I have to defend it in front of actual humans I'd like a consistency audit. Here's the set of commitments I keep endorsing:

1. I hold to verbal plenary inspiration of the autographs: the Spirit's superintendence of the original writers goes all the way down to the words, not just the concepts.
2. When the New Testament cites the Old Testament in wording that tracks the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew we now have, I want to say that Greek wording is the scriptural wording at that point—not that Paul or the author of Hebrews was quoting a flawed translation.
3. But in general I don't think any translation is inspired in the strict sense. Translations are witnesses to the inspired text, which is exactly why KJV-onlyism strikes me as a category mistake.
4. I also think the surviving manuscripts and the versions made from them are fully sufficient: nothing of doctrinal importance hangs on disputed wording.
5. And yet I want to insist that when I read a competent English translation, I am reading the Word of God, not merely a loose paraphrase or a report about it.

I can't tell wether these five actually hang together or whether I'm papering over a contradiction with the word "Word." Could you go through them, say which pairs genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to, rank the real tensions by severity, and tell me the least costly revision—which single thing to weaken or qualify—and what accepting that revision would cost?
