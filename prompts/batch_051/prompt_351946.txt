I was reading Locke's Second Treatise, the chapter on prerogative, and I keep going back and forth. He says the executive may act by discretion for the public good in cases the law doesn't foresee, and in some cases even against the direct letter of the law, because the legislature can't anticipate everything and sometimes moving too slowly would be ruinous. But I don't see how this isn't just arbitrary rule. If the executive gets to decide when the public good requires stepping outside the law, and the only real check is that the people might resist afterward, then isn't "prerogative" just tyranny with a more respectable name? What exactly is the principle that separates what Locke is defending from what he calls tyranny? I feel like I'm missing a step.
