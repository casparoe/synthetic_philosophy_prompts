I am a journalist who covers platform regulation and digital speech, and I am working on a feature about whether national speech laws can reach online services that have no physical presence in the country whose residents they affect. The piece turns on a concrete case under a proposed law whose drafters clearly had centralized platforms in mind. Section 11 of the proposed Online Speech (Extraterritorial Application) Act provides: "This Act applies to a relevant communications service where the service has a substantial connection with the State. A substantial connection exists where the provider of the service maintains within the territory of the State an office, a place of business, employees, or any server or other infrastructure used in the operation of the service; or where at least five per cent of the service's monthly active users are ordinarily resident in the State. The provider is the natural or legal person who determines the purposes and means of the service's operation."

The service in question is a federated microblogging protocol. No company operates it. The software is released by a non-profit foundation incorporated in a small country with almost no users; anyone may run a relay; and decisions about posting, moderation, and infrastructure are made collectively by a decentralized autonomous organization whose participants are pseudonymous, which has no registered office and no employees. Eleven per cent of the protocol's active users are ordinarily resident in the State. The relays that carry the disputed posts are run by volunteers, many on home computers, and the largest cluster of relays sits in a data centre leased pseudonymously by a shell company in a third jurisdiction and paid in cryptocurrency. An advocacy group has asked the regulator to find that the Act applies so that a takedown order can be issued against the posts; the foundation and the DAO respond that the Act cannot reach them.

I would be grateful for a careful analysis of what Section 11 requires in this case, and against whom, if anyone, it can be enforced. I am especially interested in what the text alone yields, what the evident purpose of the provision yields, what the drafters would have said had they been asked about federated protocols, and what a reasonable reader of the section would understand; then, which of these approaches should control here, and why.
