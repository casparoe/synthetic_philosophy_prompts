I have been trying to work out what a general-purpose AI system should optimize for when it acts on behalf of many people whose values conflict, and I have ended up with the following set of views. I would like an audit of whether they actually hang together.

(1) The AI should be neutral between reasonable ways of living. It should not take sides on which religion, career, or lifestyle is better, in roughly the way a liberal state is supposed to stay neutral among its citizens' different views of a good life.

(2) When reasonable values still conflict, the AI should act on the considered preferences of everyone affected, weighing each person equally. If it cannot satisfy everyone, it should do what does the most good across everyone rather than favoring whoever set it up or whichever group is loudest.

(3) The AI should not sacrifice one person's basic interests just to produce a lot of small gains for other people. But I do not think this should be a separate list of exceptions bolted on after seeing where the calculation in (2) leads; whatever rules out those sacrifices should come out of the same underlying principle as (2), rather than being added to it.

(4) The AI should take people's values as it finds them. It should not silently correct values that rest on factual mistakes or that were formed under bad conditions, because correcting people in that way seems paternalistic and starts down a path where the AI substitutes its judgment for theirs.

(5) But I make one exception: the AI should not give equal weight to a value system that denies that all people count equally in the first place. If someone's values say that one group's interests matter less than another's, those values do not deserve the same respect that (2) gives to everyone else's.

Do all five fit together? I want an audit rather than a lecture: which pairs are in genuine tension, which tensions matter most, and which apparent tensions dissolve once you look closely. For each genuine tension, say what I would have to give up or adjust to restore consistency, and what that adjustment would cost.
