I help run a small multiplayer game with a player-run economy, and I am trying to work out how a proposed rule would actually behave before we adopt it. Under the proposal, if one player deliberately destroys another player's item, the item is not restored, but the destroyer must pay the victim the item's current market price in real money. Separately, players can pay a small real-money monthly fee to register an item as significant, with a short written statement of why it matters to them; if a registered item is destroyed, the platform, not the destroyer, pays the victim a fixed real-money sentimental-value payment. I would like this analyzed as a mechanism rather than judged morally. Walk through the incentives it creates for owners, destroyers, traders, and the platform; how each party would game it; and whether the scheme ends up treating virtual destruction as a real harm or as a compulsory sale. Where does it fail first, and can those failures be fixed without giving up the idea that some virtual items are irreplaceable?
