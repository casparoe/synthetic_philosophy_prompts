I keep seeing "you can't have infinite growth on a finite planet" in climate discussions, most recently in an argument where someone used it to wave off carbon pricing and say that any green-growth or decoupling story is just wishful thinking and only degrowth is honest. I'm not sure what exact claim is doing the work there, and I don't want to end up in a fight about a slogan. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that one-liner could be making — does "growth" mean GDP, energy throughput, material extraction, population, or something else; does "finite" mean a fixed stock of resources, a fixed rate of solar input, or the planet's waste sinks; and finite over what timescale — then say for each reading whether it's trivially true, defensible but disputable, or false, and which readings would actually support the leap to "therefore carbon pricing won't work and we need degrowth"?
