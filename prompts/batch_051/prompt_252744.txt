I've been trying to work out whether I actually think neural networks have representations, using my gut calls on a bunch of cases rather than the literature. Here's where I've landed.

(1) A linear probe — a linear decoder trained on one layer's activations — can predict with ~99% accuracy whether the model's next token is saying something true, even though no individual neuron is doing anything truth-like and the probe direction is an unreadable mess across hundreds of dimensions. I still want to say the model represents truth at that layer.

(2) A single neuron fires on photos of cats, but also on fur textures and vacuum cleaners. I don't want to say it's a 'cat neuron' or that it represents vacuums; it's just a junk feature.

(3) Activation patching: for a factual prompt where the model should answer 'Paris', corrupting the input makes it start to answer 'London', but swapping in the activations from one particular layer of the clean run flips it back to 'Paris'. The swapped activations are unreadable mush. I still want to say that layer represents the model's settled answer.

(4) A sparse autoencoder feature activates for almost every mention of the Eiffel Tower and decodes cleanly to it, but causally intervening on that feature barely changes what the model says — it only starts hedging a little more ('I think', 'perhaps'). I'm reluctant to call it a representation of the Eiffel Tower, because it isn't doing anything in the forward pass.

So my verdicts seem to pull two ways: decodability is enough in (1), but not in (4); causal role is enough in (3), but I accept (1) without causal evidence; and (2) suggests I want some content-specificity that (3) doesn't have.

Is there a single account of what it is to have a representation that gets all four, or close? Which verdicts should I drop? And what does the account say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a probe direction that decodes 'truth' perfectly but where intervening on it does nothing, sitting seperate from an unreadable pathway that actually does the work?
