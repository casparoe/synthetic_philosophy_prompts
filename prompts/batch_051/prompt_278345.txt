A foundation distributes a numerical library under the Rowan Public License 2.0. Section 3 of the license reads: "A work that incorporates, adapts, or is derived from the Program, in whole or in part, must itself be distributed under the terms of this License. A work that merely interoperates with the Program through its published interfaces, and contains no part of the Program, is an independent work and may be licensed as its author chooses. Aggregating the Program with independent works in a single distribution medium does not make the aggregate a derivative work."

A startup now trains a code-generation model on a corpus that includes the library's source, then sells a service that emits short numerical routines. Most outputs are original, but some reproduce fifteen-line functions from the library with only the variable names changed. The model does not call the library, and the library's source is not present in the model's weights in any searchable form. The startup says the clause was drafted for source ports, wrappers, and linked binaries, not statistical models, and that neither "incorporation" nor "adaptation" reaches trained weights. The foundation replies that the model's competence and its occasional near-verbatim outputs derive from the library, and that a generator whose outputs include copied routines is in effect incorporating the Program.

What does Section 3 require with respect to the model and its generated routines? Set out the textual reading, the purpose-based reading, what the drafters would likely have said if asked about trained models, and what a reasonable developer would take the clause to mean; then say which of these should govern and why.
