I keep tripping over something in how the law works that seems to flatly contradict itself. On the one hand, if someone sues you they have to actually notify you; a contract can get thrown out if the fine print was buried; courts make a big deal out of whether you had notice. On the other hand, when it comes to criminal law the system says ignorance of the law is no excuse, even though there's no realistic way for one person to know all of it — the tax code alone is thousands of pages, and there are rules buried in agency guidance and local ordinances that aren't even easy to find online. So my question is: what view of fairness, or of what it means to be bound by a legal system, is that practice presuposing? Does it only make sense if you assume the rules are at least knowable in principle, or that we all somehow consented to them, or that each of us has a standing duty to keep up with every change? Or is the practice compatible with a bunch of different ideas and I'm overreading it? And if you swapped in the principle that people can only be held to rules they could reasonably have found out about, what would actually have to change in how laws get written and enforced?
