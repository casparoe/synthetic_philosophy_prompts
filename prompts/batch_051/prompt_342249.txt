I am a high school student, and in my philosophy of science reading group I ran into the problem of verisimilitude. As I understand it, Popper thought our best scientific theories are conjectures that may well be false, so he needed a way to say that one false theory can still be closer to the truth than another; the measures I saw compare a theory's truth against its falsehood, statement by statement, which assumes that a theory is a collection of statements. A lot of today's best science does not look like a collection of statements. AlphaFold's skill at predicting protein structure consists of a vast web of learned weights, and the system never writes down a general law about folding; it takes a sequence and returns a structure. My question is whether the problem of verisimilitude survives that development, or whether it was a problem about one specific, shrinking form of scientific knowledge. Please respond in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. The points should identify which premise of the verisimilitude program is under the most pressure from black-box predictive models like AlphaFold, and whether a weakened notion of closeness to truth could still be defined for such models.
