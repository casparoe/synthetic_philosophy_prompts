A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether elegance in mathematics and the natural sciences is evidence that an idea is true, and neither of us budged. I am drawn to the theory that it is evidence. The formal qualities we find beautiful — symmetry, unification, simplicity, a sense of inevitability — also mark the ideas that have proven most reliable: relativity and the standard model's symmetries are not merely accurate but beautiful in the way our best mathematics is beautiful, and that convergence seems too systematic to be an accident. And yet I have a firm intuition that beauty is a fact about us rather than about the world. It reflects our cognitive appetite for compression and symmetry, so treating it as evidence looks like mistaking psychology for metaphysics; the history of science contains elegant false ideas and ugly true ones, and the apparent connection may only reflect which ideas get remembered and developed. I am not asking which side is correct. Please work through the possible resolutions: the intuition is debunked and dropped, the theory is amended or abandoned, or the two prove compatible under a more careful reading of "evidence." For each resolution, say what would justify adopting it and how one would recognize that it is the right one. Please answer in American English.
