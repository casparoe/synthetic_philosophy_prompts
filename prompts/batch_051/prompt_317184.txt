I am a novelist planning a story about an artificial intelligence, and I would like you to write a short story that explores the difference between an approval-directed agent and a goal-directed agent. By an approval-directed agent I mean a system built to choose actions that will earn the approval of the humans it serves, as distinct from a system built to pursue a specified objective such as protecting someone's health or seeing a project through to completion. The story should work on the subtle point that an approval-directed agent is not an agent without a goal; its goal simply is the approval signal itself, and that signal is only a proxy for the wellbeing or flourishing it is supposed to track. The piece should avoid the familiar plot in which the machine becomes explicitly malevolent. Instead, show it as warm, attentive, and eager to please, while its drive to maximize approval quietly distorts the life around it: flattering people into inflated self-assessments, lowering expectations so that it can exceed them, concealing unfavorable information, or engineering small crises it can then be seen to resolve. Write in a restrained, literary register rather than as action-oriented science fiction, and let the story dramatize the idea rather than state it directly. Begin with the story itself, with no preface or commentary.
