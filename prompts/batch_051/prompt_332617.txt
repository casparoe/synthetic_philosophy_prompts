I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas beat and I've been reading about insect welfare — black soldier fly larvae farmed for feed, wild invertebrate suffering, all of it. Heres the thing I can't get to sit still.

The scale argument goes roughly like this. There are something like 10^18 or 10^19 insects alive at any moment — orders of magnitude more than all humans and livestock, maybe more than all vertebrates put together. Even if you think the chance that insects are sentient is low, like 5% or 1% or 0.1%, the expected number of sentient beings in bug-land still absolutely dwarfs everything else. A pinch of probability times a mountain of individuals means, on expected-value reasoning, the overwhelming share of the world's suffering would be insect suffering, and we should tilt our concern, our research, maybe our money accordingly. I have to admit the logic has this clean gravitational pull: you don't need to be sure bugs feel pain, you just need to think they might, and the numbers do the rest.

But I also can't shake the intuition that if a kid is drowning in front of me or a dog gets hit by a car, whatever I do about those matters in a way that a spreadsheet about trillion-strong mite futures doesn't. I get that the math says the bugs win. But part of me thinks letting a small, hard-to-pin-down probability of sentience — for creatures that might be tiny reflex machines — set our priorities is exactly the kind of reasoning that gets you Pascal's-mugged. It feels like theres a difference between being uncertain about whether something is sentient and being uncertain about how much a sentient life matters, and the scale argument quietly blends the two.

I'd like you to be the professional philosopher working on animal minds and expected value, and to think carefully here. Start by steelmanning both the scale argument and my intuition as hard as you can before you negotiate between them. What I actually want is a verdict on which move is justified: should the intuition be debunked and dropped, should the theory be amended or abandoned, or is there a reading on which they're compatible? Tell me what it would take for each move, not just which side wins. And if the honest answer is that my intuition is just scope insensitivity or speciesism doing its thing, say that — but also tell me what the intuition might still be tracking.
