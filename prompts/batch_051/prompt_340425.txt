Write me a deadpan parody of an internal league memo — around 700 words — from a fictional NBA-style compliance office trying to figure out whether it can block or tax an "unusual compensation arrangement" that Wilt Chamberlain has reportedly signed. Use Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain example from Anarchy, State, and Utopia as the factual setup: Wilt gets an extra quarter from every home ticket, a million fans attend over the season, and he ends up with $250,000, far more than anyone else in the league, even though nobody thinks any individual step was unfair. The comedy should come from the compliance officers being genuinely unable to find the illegal or coercive act: the fans own their quarters, the transfers are voluntary, Wilt is simply extremely good at being watched, and yet the final distribution obviously violates every internal policy the league has about max salaries, revenue sharing, and competitive balance. Don't have the memo lecture about entitlement theory, or about the general point that voluntary exchanges unravel any distribution that has to fit a pre-set pattern; let the writers keep rediscovering the problem from scratch, with mounting frustration. By the end they should either recommend, against their whole sense of what their job is, that the arrangement be allowed — or recommend a rule so broad that enforcing it would mean stepping in and redistributing after every single ticket sale, and let the absurdity of that just sit there. The reader should come away feeling the actual Nozickian pressure: if you concede that the starting holdings were just and each transfer was voluntary, then a historical/entitlement view of justice leaves you no clean lever against a wildly unequal outcome.

Oh, and after the memo, add a short seperate note (100–150 words) flagging where the parody takes liberties with Nozick's actual argument or leans on a contested reading of it. Distinguish what's settled — e.g., that the example is aimed at patterned or end-state distributive principles, and that Nozick stipulates the starting distribution as one everyone already regards as just — from what's genuinely disputed among people who read this stuff — e.g., whether just acquisition can simply be stipulated like that in the real world, whether the example leans on a particular theory of property and consent, and whether the argument is really a reductio of all redistributive policy or just one family of views. Don't present the disputed parts as consensus.
