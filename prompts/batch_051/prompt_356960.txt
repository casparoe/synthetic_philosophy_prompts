I am a novelist, and I think most precisely in the vocabulary of narrative theory: focalization, free indirect style, diegetic levels, the narrator–narratee–implied reader triangle, and the cast of characters. Restate the debate in anthropic reasoning between the self-sampling assumption and the self-indication assumption in those terms. By self-sampling I mean the policy of reasoning as if one were randomly selected from the observers in some reference class; by self-indication I mean the policy of treating one's own existence as evidence favoring hypotheses on which there are more observers — which yields the presumptuous philosopher, who becomes nearly certain of a theory predicting vastly many observers without any evidence distinguishing the rival theories beyond the fact that they exist. I want a systematic translation, not loose metaphor. Then give me the residue report: which moves in the debate translate cleanly, which can be rendered only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in narratological vocabulary at all. Finally, say whether the residue is a gap in my usual framework or a spot where the anthropic debate itself has not been made precise.
