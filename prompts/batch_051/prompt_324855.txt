ok so ive been reading about the Judy Benjamin problem and im stuck on which part of me to give up. from what i can tell it goes something like this: a paratrooper (Judy, I think) is dropped somewhere and her map says shes either in Red territory or Blue territory; each of those has two smaller zones, headquarters and second company maybe. before she gets any outside info she has some prior over the four possibilities. then command radios her with a weird message: not “youre in Red” or “youre 70% in Red,” but something like *if* you’re in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 you’re at Red headquarters. it’s a conditional, not a proposition about where she actually is. ordinary Bayes conditioning on propositions doesn’t directly apply.

now heres the clash. side one: im pretty convinced that updating on partial evidence like this should work by minimal change. you impose the conditional as a constraint and then pick the new probability function that’s closest to your old one (minimum relative entropy, info divergence, whatever its called). it’s a general, tidy rule. it actually gives a unique answer for exactly the cases ordinary Bayes cant touch, and I like rules that dont make you invent extra steps. side two: but I also cant shake the intuition that learning a conditional about *Red given where you are in Red* shouldn’t move your credence that you’re in Red at all. the message is silent on whether shes in Red or Blue. sure, if she trusted the sender as a reliable reporter with a human communication policy, then the fact that he chose that conditional could be evidence, but in the puzzle I think youre supposed to treat the message as a bare constraint. so it feels wrong that she ends up more (or less) confident shes in Red without anyone telling her anything about Red vs Blue.

i dont just want the verdict. i want to know which of these two things is doing the real work, what it would cost to let go of each, and whether there’s a stable reading where both survive: maybe the minimal-change rule is right but we should update on the whole event “command said that conditional” rather than on the conditional as a raw constraint; or maybe the intuition is right but only when the conditional’s antecedent and consequent are independent in your prior, or something. then at the end take a definite position: in the radio-message case as I described it, do you actually bite the bullet and let your probability of Red move, or do you reject the minimal-change rule for conditional evidence, and why? and put a hard cap on it: 800 words max, please, because I have to study for calc.
