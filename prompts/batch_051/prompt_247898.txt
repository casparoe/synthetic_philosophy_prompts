I've spent the last few years working through naturalistic theories of mental content, and I've ended up with a set of commitments that I keep deploying in arguments without having tested them against one another. I'd like you to audit the whole set: I want to know whether this is one coherent position or a patchwork I've been able to keep only by not running all the pieces at once.

Here is the profile as it stands.

1. Physicalism about content. Intentionality is not a fundamental feature of the world; facts about what a mental state means are grounded in non-semantic physical facts.

2. Misrepresentation is non-negotiable. A state can mean X even when X is not present, and even when it was actually caused by something other than X. Any theory that reduces content to actual causal covariance or reliable indication therefore fails.

3. Etiological teleosemantics. The right way to naturalize content is through proper function: a state means X because it is the function of the state to indicate X, where functions are fixed by selection history, whether evolutionary or through learning.

4. Consumer-side determination. Within that framework, the content of a representational state is fixed at the consumption end, not the production end. What matters is the condition under which the downstream systems that use the state would operate successfully, not the condition that typically causes the state to fire.

5. Generality and determinacy. The account is meant to be fully general, covering not just perceptual states but logical, mathematical, and normative concepts such as negation, number, and justice. It should also recover fine-grained contents rather than leaving everything at the level of equivalence classes: it should be able to say why a frog's prey-detector means something like prey rather than small dark moving thing or photon pattern.

6. Narrow explanatory content. The content that does real work in explaining behavior should be narrow: it should supervene on the agent's current intrinsic physical constitution, so that two intrinsic physical duplicates in the same occurrent circumstances share the same beliefs and desires.

7. The no-history duplicate verdict. A molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a normal adult human that assembled by chance, with no evolutionary or learning history, has no representational states at all. I accept this verdict rather than treating it as a reductio.

Go through these and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For the genuine tensions, order them by severity and say, for each, what has to give and what the cheapest fix would be. Then tell me which single revision would do the most to make the package hang together, and what it would cost.
