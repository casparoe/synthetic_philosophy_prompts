I'm trying to settle a genuinely unpleasant question about our local campaign-finance code, and I'd value an honest read from someone who'll actually do the interpretive work instead of reciting the obvious answer.

The ordinance says an expenditure is not "independent" if it is "made at the urging, suggestion, or request of a candidate, or in concert or consultation with the candidate or any committee supporting the candidate." Independents can spend unlimited amounts, so the line matters a lot.

Here's the case the drafters did not anticipate. A candidate's campaign has stopped trying to coordinate quietly and instead publishes a complete public "ad kit": raw b-roll, a style guide, copy blocks, and a fine-tuned image-and-video model checkpoint that regenerates the campaigns ads in its voice. Anyone can download it; the page says it's "for the public, not a communication to any specific spender." A Super PAC downloads the checkpoint, runs it with no human in the loop approving output, and places whatever comes out. Nobody from the PAC and nobody from the campaign have ever emailed, and if the model produced a bad ad, no one at the PAC reviewed it before it went up.

Is the expenditure "made at the ... suggestion ... of" the candidate? On the plain words, I half-believe a published kit is a suggestion in the relevant sense and half-believe the words require a communication directed at a specific spender. The drafters were imagining phone calls and cutouts, not weight releases. Walk me through what the text, the obvious purpose (stopping the candidate from directing supposedly independent money), what the drafters would say if woken up and asked, and what a reasonable reader would think each actually gives you here—and which one should win. And tell me straight whether there's a principled line that catches the checkpoint but not a candidate's ordinary public remarks, or whether this is exactly where the independent/coordinated distinction stops cutting reality at the joints.
