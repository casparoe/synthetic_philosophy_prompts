I keep running into the idea that envy or resentment of the better-off isn't only a personal vice, because under the right conditions it's a kind of signal that the arrangement is unjust. Rawls has a thought in this neighborhood: he wants to keep resentment and envy apart—resentment is a moral sentiment aimed at a wrong, while envy is just pain at another's good—but he carves out a special case he calls excusable envy, where the envied gap itself looks unjust and the feeling starts to shade toward a legitimate sense of injury. I don't know how literally to take that. Is the claim that envy can track injustice an empirical claim, a conceptual claim, or just a flattering way to redescribe a ugly feeling? And does envy actually do the same epistemic work as resentment there, or is it just resentment's disreputable cousin?
