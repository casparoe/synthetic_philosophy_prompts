You are a professional philosopher with expertise in social epistemology and the philosophy of archives and record-keeping. Please think through the following case carefully. Begin by brainstorming several distinct ways of framing the problem; then settle on the framing you find most illuminating and defend it.

I am a retired ecologist who has recently begun reading philosophy. I now volunteer for a county historical society that has inherited the contents of a shuttered biological field station: roughly eighty years of handwritten field notebooks, species census cards, weather logs, early drafts and correspondence, and a dozen audio interviews with elderly local collectors and caretakers, most of whom never appeared as authors or staff in the station's official documents. A digitization grant is forcing us to choose what will be scanned, indexed, and preserved, and what will be left to decay in a storage shed. The surviving senior scientists I have consulted are united: only publication-quality data and the station's formal administrative record count as the scientific record, in their view. They regard the rest—the field assistants' crossed-out entries, the collectors' notebooks written in dialect, the taped recollections—as noise, at best biographical curiosities, and possibly misleading to future researchers. The historical society's director is inclined to agree for budgetary reasons and frames the choice as efficient curation rather than exclusion.

I know from my own career that good science depends on filtering out unreliable observations, but something about doing that filtering at the level of what gets preserved strikes me as different. I can feel that a philosophical issue is at stake, but I cannot yet articulate it without sounding merely sentimental. What is really at stake in deciding what counts as part of the record? Is my unease defensible, and what would you advise the historical society to do? Please do not give generic archival or fundraising advice; I want you to uncover the conceptual and normative structure of the situation.
