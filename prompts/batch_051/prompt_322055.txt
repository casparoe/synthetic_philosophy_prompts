Okay so I'm teaching confirmation prep at my parrish this year and it's made me way too anxious about sin and intention, which is why I've been using my friend Marta as a moral sounding board. She keeps saying the external act doesn't matter much, only what you consent to, which sounds like something I half-remember from a college class. I've been keeping track of her verdicts.

- husband dreams he cheats on his wife, wakes up feeling guilty: not a sin
- guy is furious at his brother and for a split second wants to hit him, but immediately catches himself and walks away: not a sin
- guy decides to embezzle from work, writes out the whole plan, then loses his nerve the night before and does nothing: sin, full stop
- woman gives a huge donation to a food bank solely to impress someone: sin
- man runs over a pedestrian while driving carefully and obeying every law, in a genuinely unavoidable accident: not a sin

Now the new case. My cousin has been nursing a fantasy about humiliating her ex at their mutual friend's wedding next month. She says she would never actually do it, has no plan, and doesn't even want to see him hurt in real life, but she deliberately sets aside time to imagine it in detail because it makes her feel powerful. She insists it's just a private thought, seperate from anything she'd ever consent to doing. I can't tell if this is closer to the dream or the passing impulse or the guy who made the plan, and I have a feeling it matters for how I talk to my confirmation kids.

What would Marta probably say about my cousin? Also, what rule is she implicitly running, what other rules fit her past verdicts just as well, and if I could ask her one more test case to settle which rule she actually holds, what should it be?
