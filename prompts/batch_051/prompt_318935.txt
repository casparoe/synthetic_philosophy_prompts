The case. We run a content-moderation model that takes a user report plus the flagged message and outputs a violation decision with confidence. Last quarter we logged three anomalies that look different:

A. Messages containing certain benign words from a recently added regional slang list get overflagged as violations at near-100% confidence.
B. When a reported message uses simple obfuscations (spaced-out letters, glyph swaps), the model often misses real violations and gives low confidence.
C. A subset of long-lived accounts get their scores systematically downweighted, so their appeals almost always fail even when the model otherwise classifies the message fine.

Our interpretability crew did causal tracing and found a single family of attention heads, spread across three adjacent layers, causally implicated in all three; ablating that circuit makes all three disappear in the eval set. The behavioral/safety folks push back: the tracing shows these heads compute something pretty generic, so ablating them is like cutting the steering column and declaring every car symptom the same fault. The three anomalies have different triggers, different failure directions (overflag / underflag / miscalibrated appeals), different harms, and — they showed me a graph I half-followed — different generalization behavior under distribution shift. Being downstream of the same shared component, they say, is not the same as being the same mechanism.

Why it matters. Policy (and our auditors checklist) says one incident report per "distinct underlying mechanism," and a mitigation counts as resolved only if it's validated against each named mechanism. If its one mechanism, we file one report, our circuit ablation counts as the fix, and we can close all three. If its three, we file three reports and the fix has to be validated separately against each, with extra red-teaming. The board splits about how you'd even tell: same circuit vs same functional role vs same counterfactual response to a targeted fix.

I need to circulate a decision memo tomorrow: a concrete recommendation (one report, three reports, or whatever middle option you think is right), plus a reusable test for "same mechanism" that we could apply next quarter.
