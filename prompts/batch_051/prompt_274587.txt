I have been turning over a parallel between two changes normally taught in different disciplines, and I cannot tell whether it is a real structural correspondence or only a suggestive analogy. The first is Frege's replacement of the subject–predicate analysis of a proposition with function–argument analysis. The older analysis takes the subject–predicate form as the fundamental shape of any judgement, which makes one-place predication the basic pattern. Frege's alternative treats a predicate as a function with one or more argument places saturated by objects, so that n-place relations and the interaction of several quantifiers can be expressed without being forced into the one-place mould; the grammatical subject is no longer the owner of the thought, but merely one argument among others. The second change is the move in database design from modelling data as entities carrying attributes to the relational model, in which an n-ary relation over values is the sole logical data structure: even the fact that a given customer has a given name is stored as a tuple in a relation rather than as a property attached to an object. The common move, as I see it, is a refusal to privilege one term as the bearer of a fact — Frege deprives the grammatical subject of its privilege, and the relational model deprives the entity or record of its privilege. I should like to know whether this is the same inversion happening twice, a point about why representations that attach each fact to a single owner break down when information is irreducibly relational, or whether the database case is only an engineering echo of the logical one. I should like the answer to do three things. First, state the mapping precisely and say where the two structures match and where they come apart. Secondly, say what would have to be true on both sides for results to transfer: would anything like normalisation, joins, or referential integrity carry over to the semantics of natural language or to the design of a logical notation, and would anything in Frege's treatment of quantification and binding carry over to what a database schema must be able to express? Thirdly, identify something the parallel predicts that turns out to be false, as a way of locating the point at which the two cases genuinely diverge.
