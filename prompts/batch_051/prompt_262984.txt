I've just been moved onto a standing arbitration panel for two-party business disputes—partners splitting a practice, a supplier and a buyer renegotiating after a price shock, a contractor and an owner fighting over change orders. We see two or three cases a month, our award is binding, and the parties are not economists: they can put a dollar number on outcomes, but nobody is handing us a utility function. I keep coming back to the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution, or at least my rough understanding of it: take each side's disagreement payoff if talks fail, then each side's ideal payoff (the most they could get while the other still gets at least their disagreement payoff), and pick the deal on the line between the disagreement point and the ideal point that is farthest toward the ideal while still feasible. I like that it respects both sides' best and worst realistic outcomes, but I dont actually know how to run it as a procedure rather than a nice idea, and my colleagues are lawyers who will not read an axiomatization.

Write me a reusable procedure for turning the KS solution into something our panel can actually run on a case, assuming the only numbers we'll ever have are rough dollar valuations, expected legal costs, and each side's fallback if there's no deal. The procedure should cover, start to finish: what to ask the parties first, how to nail down the disagreement point and the ideal point without just trusting their say-so, how to build or approximate the feasible set from the offers on the table, how to select the KS outcome, and how to write it up as a recommended award. Flag where the procedure has to make a judgment call or breaks down: nonconvex feasible sets from lumpy indivisible assets, more than two parties, disagreement points one side can shift by stalling, and parties gaming the numbers by lying about their fallback or their best outcome. Include two or three worked examples with plausible numbers, one where KS gives a result I am going to hate and one where the procedure throws up its hands.

After the main writeup, give me a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields:
- "steps": an array of objects, each with "name" (short label), "question" (the plain-language question the step asks the parties or ourselves), "input" (the numbers or facts required), "output" (what gets written down), and "judgment_needed" (a boolean, true if the step can't be done mechanically).
- "worked_examples": an array of objects, each with "case" (one-sentence description), "disagreement_point" (array of two numbers), "ideal_point" (array of two numbers), "ks_outcome" (array of two numbers), and "sticking_point" (a string saying which side, if any, walks away unhappy, or what the procedure ignores).
- "unsettled_cases": an array of strings, each a one-line description of a case where the procedure breaks or would need a different principle.
