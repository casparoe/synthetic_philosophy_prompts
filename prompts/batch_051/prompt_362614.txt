Write a deadpan parody in the form of a double-anonymized peer review report for a generalist science journal, roughly 800–900 words, evaluating a submission by an invented author that is unmistakably in the mode of Wolfram's A New Kind of Science. The submission argues that the universe is ultimately computational, that simple rules such as elementary cellular automata generate the full range of complex behavior, that the principle of computational equivalence shows ordinary equation-based science to be a merely special case, and that the standard demands of equations, theorems, and testable predictions no longer apply. The reviewer should be scrupulously fair and formally composed; the comedy must come from the collision between the submission's totalizing ambitions and the report's quiet bureaucratic machinery, not from caricature or cheap insults.

Use the standard sections: Summary, Assessment of the Claims, Significance, Recommendation, and Comments to the Author. In character, the reviewer should grant whatever seems genuinely interesting, such as the runs themselves and the sheer range of figures, before pressing on the hard questions: whether the central claims are stated precisely enough to be false, what would count as evidence for a claim about all computations, how a proof of universality for a particular elementary cellular automaton such as Rule 110 is supposed to license claims about nature, and how the submission distinguishes its framework from a position that is insulated from all counterevidence. At one or two points, include a short quotation from the submission or a footnote reply by the invented author, who should respond entirely within the framework and treat the reviewer's objections as further confirmation. Any quoted fragments must be invented by you and sound like the genre, not actual passages from A New Kind of Science.

The piece should remain legible to a reader who has never read Wolfram's book, while leaving everyone with the live question the piece is really about: can a purported new kind of science be assessed by the old kind of peer review, or has the framework been built so that criticism can never land? The satire should exaggerate real features of the book's actual manner rather than a straw man, so that a specialist recognizes it as fair. Keep the report self-contained; do not append a bibliography or reference list.
