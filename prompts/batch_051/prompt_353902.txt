Two friends of mine got into an argument about a third friend, Dana, and I'd like someone to referee it. Dana is a programmer at a bank, mostly fixing bugs in the software the loan officers use. The bank has been financing a company that's clearing rainforest illegally. Dana has nothing to do with that client: nobody runs those loans through her, and if she quit tomorrow the loans would go ahead unchanged. Mia argued Dana isn't complicit in the clearing. Complicity, she said, needs an actual contribution to the wrong or intentional help with it; maintaining generic loan software is neither, and she's basically in the position of the person who services the elevators. Leo said that's exactly the picture that lets modern wrongdoing off the hook: no individual employee is ever necessary for the harm, so if you demand a difference-making contribution, everyone's clean by definition. What matters, he said, is that Dana knows what the bank is funding, deliberately keeps the machinery running that the bad loans move through, and stays voluntarily. That's participation in a harmful enterprise, and you don't have to cause the harm to be complicit in it. Mia came back that Leo's standard would make the janitor and cafeteria staff complicit in everything the firm does, which is absurd; there has to be a difference between generic background support and involvement in the specific wrongful project. Leo said there are degrees — the janitor might be complicit in a thin way too — but Dana's is thicker because her code is the channel these loans pass through and she has realistic exit options, so staying isn't neutral. Mia replied that 'the channel' is doing all the work: Dana fixes bugs in a general-purpose system and has no say over which loans get made, so her only real agency is over the bugs, and you can't be complicit in a decision you couldn't touch. They asked me to settle it and I genuinely couldn't. Please adjudicate this point by point: who's right about which claims, who's more right overall, and what are both of them missing? Also, say how confident you are in that adjudication and name the point you're least sure about.
