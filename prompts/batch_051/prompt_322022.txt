I'm a software engineer, spent most of my career on compiler correctness and formal methods, so I'm at home with operational semantics, observational equivalence, bisimulation, trace equivalence, abstract interpretation — that whole toolbox. I have basically zero philosophy background, but I keep running into people who say that if we emulated a brain faithfully on silcon, the silicon thing would be conscious too, because what matters morally is the computation, not the substrate. My instincts say the same thing: if two programs are indistinguishable to every observer, they're the same program, full stop. But the ground shifts under me when the examples get weird. Suppose you take a supposedly sentient program and apply an aggressive "optimisation": precompute the output for every possible input and replace the whole thing with a giant lookup table. Behaviourally identical, but the intermediate computation is gone, compiled away. Or suppose two implementations are trace-equivalent but not bisimilar, so they produce the same input-output stream but differ in internal branching. Or suppose the same chunk of hardware counts as implementing several different computations at once depending on the encoding you choose to read it with (I once saw the claim that, with a liberal enough mapping, most physical systems implement almost any finite computation, which sounds to me like saying every rock is sort of running Linux if you squint).

I want you to restate the philosophical debate about which computations matter morally — roughly the computationalist side against views that say the physical or causal details of the implementation matter (integrated information theory, biological naturalism, that family), but don't lean on jargon I won't know — in my vocabulary: programs, traces, bisimulation, refinement, compilation, and the mapping from a program to the device that implements it. Then give me a residue report. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly, which parts only translate with distortion, and which parts have no expression at all. And I want a committed verdict, not a menu: does my position — same observable behaviour means same computation means same moral status — survive the lookup-table case and the squinting-at-a-rock case, or does the phrase "the computation a system is running" end up doing more moral work than it can bear? Give me your actual view and the reasons for it. British English, please.
