Ok so something I keep noticing: everyone talks about Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, Gen Z like they're real kinds of people. Like, a company will change how it markets to Gen Z, or someone will blame the housing market on Boomers hoarding all the houses, or whatever. These labels get treated as if they pick out a real group with a shared psychology. But if I proposed sorting people by the winner of the World Series in the year they were born and claimed those people have a shared work ethic, everyone would call that silly. Astrology gets the same reaction, even though it's also just bucketing people by when they were born. So what separates the respectable cases from the silly ones? Is it that generations have real shared experiences—wars, technology, crashes—during their formative years? But the cutoff years are totally disputed and keep moving depending on which article you read, and I've never seen evidence that the between-group differences are bigger than the differences within a single generation. I want the strongest principle that could make generations real kinds while making my World-Series-year people not real kinds, and then an honest verdict on whether that principle actually works. Like, am I allowed to keep treating Gen Z as a thing, or is it just astrology with better PR? I cant tell.
