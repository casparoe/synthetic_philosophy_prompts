Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a reductionist and an emergentist about the relation between higher-level and lower-level descriptions in science. Take the reductionist to hold that higher-level facts, properties, and causal claims are in principle fully captured by the lower-level story—or are no more than compact redescriptions of it—while the emergentist holds that at least some higher-level features of complex physical, biological, or social systems are autonomous in a way the lower-level story cannot capture even in principle. Begin with a numbered "agreed claims" section that both parties can sign without hedging, covering what inter-level reduction does and does not require, which empirical facts both accept in concrete cases (phase transitions and thermodynamic properties, chemical bonding, neural versus mental descriptions), and which formulations of "reducible" each side rejects as too strong or too weak. Follow this with a disagreements table whose columns are: the issue; the reductionist's preferred wording; the emergentist's preferred wording; and whether either side grants that the issue is partly verbal. The table should address at least these cruxes: (i) whether multiple realizability of a higher-level kind by lower-level states counts as evidence for irreducibility; (ii) whether "derivable in principle" is a meaningful standard when the derivation is intractable, idealized, or depends on boundary conditions; (iii) whether coarse-graining and universality in statistical physics are best read as emergence or as reduction by other means; and (iv) what an acceptable, non-mysterious case of downward causation would have to look like. For each crux, record in advance the observation or argument each side would accept as bearing on it, and each side's prediction for what that observation or argument would show.
