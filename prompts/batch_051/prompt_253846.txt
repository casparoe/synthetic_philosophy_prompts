Last night my mate and I spent an hour arguing about whether any of us could have been born to different parents, and it made me think of a row I overheard between two people on my course a few months back. I've never been able to settle it, and last night didn't help. They were arguing over the old 'different sperm and egg' thought experiment — whether a particular person could have come from a different conception than the one they actually came from. Here's the back-and-forth, as close as i remember it.

Daniel started with: if a different sperm had made it to your mother's egg, you would never have existed; some other child would have been born instead, so your origin — those gametes — is essential to which person you are, and your genes couldn't have been different. Maya said that was wrong, or at least irrelevant: she's a person with a mind, not a sperm-and-egg pairing, and if her brain, memories, and character could be kept the same while the body was built from different genetic material, she'd still be herself, so genes are about as essential as a haircut. Daniel came back that this treats a person as a pattern rather than a particular thing: a duplicate table cut from different wood to exactly the same measurements isn't the same table, it's a replica, because being this thing rather than that one depends on which stuff it started from, not on matching a description. Maya said persons aren't tables; what matters for a person's continued existence is psychological continuity, and that's why a brain transplant would be survival of the person while keeping the body alive as an empty shell wouldn't be — so origins matter to organisms but not to persons. Daniel answered that her psychological life had to belong to somebody, and it came to be hers by growing out of that zygote; a different zygote would have built a different brain and fed it different memories, so you can't peel the mental life off its origin and imagine the very same one floating free. Maya then said that even if she granted that 'same person' is origin-bound, all Daniel had shown was a fact about the phrase 'same person', and the real question is what we should care about: if a future self remembered her life and carried on her projects, that's what matters, whether or not it satisfies some origin test. Daniel finished by saying she'd therefore given up her original position — there is no possible situation in which she exists but with a different origin, so hoping you'd had different parents is hoping for a different person — and that 'what matters' was a separate question she'd smuggled in.

I stayed out of it at the time, and after last night i still havent worked out whether Maya's final move rescues her from Daniel's first point or just changes the subject. Could you go through it move by move and say who is right about which specific claim, where each one overreaches, what both of them are missing, and who is more right overall?
