I help run a small philosophy meetup whose members mostly got into this through Camus, Cioran, and "everything is meaningless" memes, so everyone already calls themselves a nihilist. I'm pretty sure the label is doing at least four different jobs at once, and I'd like to make a "which kind of nihilist are you actually?" quiz for the next meeting. I want it to sort people by what they say about concrete cases, not wether they like the word "nihilism". So: could you write eight to ten questions, each a concrete scenario (a promise extracted under threat, whether a room containing two chairs and two people contains four things or more or fewer, someone who calls their office job pointless, Sisyphus at the bottom of the hill, erasing the last paragraph of a story and asking if it's the same story, whatever cases you think genuinely discriminate), with two or three answer options each? Then provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto at least these positions: moral error theory (moral sentences are all false), moral non-cognitivism (they aren't truth-apt at all), existential nihilism (life is meaningless), and mereological nihilism (only simples exist, no tables or chairs or people as wholes). I'd also like a second axis for "the view is livable / not livable" rather than folding that into the individual positions, since a mereological nihilist might be entirely untroubled about it while an existential nihilist might not be.

The audience won't know jargon, so the questions themselves should be plain—never ask "do you believe there are composite objects?"—and the scoring key can carry the technical labels. Please also flag which pairs of positions your quiz can't reliably pull apart (my guess: non-cognitivism and a quietist error theory will answer almost every moral case the same way, and an absurdist may be indistinguishable from a cheerful existential nihilist on paper), and note which combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy, with a one-line diagnosis of whether each empty cell is empty because it's incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. The point isn't a neat taxonomy for its own sake; it's to show the group that "nihilism" is several quite different commitments that happen to share a mood.
