I keep going back and forth on a dispute with my physicist friend. She insists that Noether's theorem shows energy conservation is *explained by* time-translation symmetry: the deep reason a closed system conserves energy is that the laws don't care what time it is. I can't see that as anything more than a rewrite. Under mild conditions, a conserved quantity and a continuous symmetry are mathematically tied together, so calling the symmetry the explanation of the conservation law (rather than the conservation law a symptom of the symmetry, or both a symptom of something else) feels like a free choice. Is there any test—thought experiment, formal, or empirical—that could break the tie? E.g., would cases like broken symmetries, anomalies, or conserved quantities with no obvious symmetry do any work, or not? Design the best test of the claim "the symmetry explains the conservation law," say what result would count for each side, and be explicit about the part no test can settle even if it comes out cleanly.
