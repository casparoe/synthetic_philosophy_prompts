Over the past few months I have been reading about epiphenomenalism in a philosophy of mind reading group, and the topic has suddenly stopped being academic for me. A close friend has a form of blindsight: in some visual tasks she performs above chance while insisting she sees nothing, and her doctors treat this as evidence that certain kinds of visually guided behavior can run without visual experience. That made me wonder whether the same could be true of my own case. When I say that my hand hurts, I feel sure that the felt pain is part of what causes me to say it. An epiphenomenalist about experience would say the felt pain is more like the noise of a motor: it accompanies the neural machinery that does the causing, but the machinery alone would have produced my report even if the feeling had been absent. The picture is coherent enough that I cannot point to an observation that would obviously refute it.

I want help designing a test, not another summary of the debate. Please act as an expert in both the philosophy of mind and the experimental psychology of consciousness, and begin by brainstorming two or three candidate experimental or observational approaches for deciding whether the felt character of pain contributes causally to my verbal report of it, or whether the underlying neural process does all the causal work. Then commit to the approach you think is strongest and spell it out concretely: what would be manipulated or measured, what pattern of results would favor the ordinary causal view, and what pattern would favor the epiphenomenalist view.

For each step, I would like you to be explicit about what a clean result would and would not settle. In particular, I worry that both hypotheses agree that the report itself is produced by the brain, so any test that uses what a subject says or presses may be unable to catch the felt quality doing any work; it may only ever catch the neural machinery doing the work. I want your honest assessment of how much of that difficulty is a practical limitation of experimental design and how much is a defect in the demand itself, perhaps a confusion in asking a felt quality to leave a trace in the causal chain that can be separated from its neural basis. Be explicit about how confident you are in each main claim you make, and single out the point you are least sure about.
