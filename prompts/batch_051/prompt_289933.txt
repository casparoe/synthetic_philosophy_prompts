I'm a novelist researching a near-future novel about an AI lab, and a few weeks ago I watched a panel where a venture capitalist kept leaning on the line "you can't put the genie back in the bottle" like it was a full argument. A safety researcher had just said that training the next generation of models without coordination is how you walk into an extinction event, and that the big labs should slow down. The VC's whole comback was: the genie's already out — the papers are on arXiv, the chips are already in datacenters, the talent is everywhere — so the only real question is who gets there first and how we adapt. The audience ate it up, and I left with the sense that one sentence had done six different kinds of work, but I couldn't name them. Can you lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims that line could be making in an AI-safety context, take each one and say whether it's a platitude, a real and defensible claim, or just false, and then tell me which of those readings would actually get him to the conclusion that slowing down is pointless? I don't need the one true interpretation — I need the full spread, because I'm probably going to put versions of this line in the mouths of two characters who mean different things by it.
