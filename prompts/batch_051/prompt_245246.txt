I've been trying to get my head round the different claims people make about how you'd build an AI that doesn't resist being switched off, and I keep finding the real disagreement is about which part of the set-up is doing the work. Could you write me a decision tree that sorts someone into a position from what they think about the agent, not from which label they like? Start from: the AI is a goal-directed system that can model the world and plan. Each branch should be a yes/no question someone could answer without knowing the jargon, e.g.: does the design let the agent represent the shutdown button as part of the world at all; is shutdown inside the agent's preferences or outside them; is the difficulty coming from a built-in drive to stay on, or is staying on just an instrumental subgoal of whatever else it wants; is the fix meant to change what it wants, what it believes about whether the button will be pressed, or how far ahead it can plan. The leaves should cover at least: the myopia or short-horizon answer; the 'make it actually want to be shut down' answer; the utility-indifference-style answer (make it not care whether it is on or off); the safe-interruptibility-style answer (whatever that amounts to in your reconstruction); an approval-directed answer ('do what the operator would endorse'); and a 'just build it too dumb to scheme' leaf if that's a genuine seperate position rather than myopia under another name. Give each leaf its usual name if it has one, otherwise call it unnamed, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't really tell apart, and note which combinations of answers are empty because they're incoherent rather than just unpopular. Around seven questions and six or seven leaves would be about right.
