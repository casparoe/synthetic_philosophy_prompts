A friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether menopause is a disease, and I want the question settled by someone who can work through the criteria more carefully than we did. I was relying on a definition I was taught in a philosophy of medicine course: a condition is a disease just in case it involves a biological dysfunction, meaning some part or process fails to perform its species-typical function, and it harms the person who has it. My friend kept pressing the case of a woman in her early fifties going through menopause: the process is statistically normal and near-universal, so it is hard to call it a dysfunction in the sense of the first criterion, yet it brings hot flashes, bone density loss, and elevated cardiovascular risk, and for some women it is severely distressing, so the second criterion seems clearly satisfied. Work through this case criterion by criterion and tell me whether menopause qualifies as a disease on this definition. Then say what the case shows: is one of the two criteria doing all the real work, is the notion of harm value-laden in a way the definition hides, and do the criteria need to be revised in a normativist direction rather than a purely naturalist one?
