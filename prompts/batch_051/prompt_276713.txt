I serve on my company's data-quality review board. We monitor roughly 20,000 product metrics. Last week an engineer flagged one series — nightly failed payment authorizations in a single market — that is so extreme that, under our baseline model, a movement of that size should occur about once every fifty years. She wants us to open a formal incident review and assign two engineers to investigate for up to a week. Another member counters that, as with shared birthdays in a large room, when you watch this many series, a once-in-fifty-years movement somewhere is essentially guaranteed, and the rational course is to do nothing unless the metric is still out of bounds at the next weekly check. Draft the short memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should honestly engage the strongest consideration on the losing side.
