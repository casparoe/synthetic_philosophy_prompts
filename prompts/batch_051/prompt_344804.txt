I find myself persuaded, on reflection, by much of Malcolm's case about dreams: a person who is sound asleep does not judge, perceive, or act, so the idea that dreams are experiences occurring during sleep looks like a conceptual mistake. The view earns its keep by explaining, at one stroke, why dream time is so distorted, why I can be unsure whether I dreamed at all, and why no one can ever check a dream report against the dream itself. Yet from the inside a nightmare is as close to undergoing something as anything I know: the suffocating room, the panic, the effort to wake. If that is only a waking confabulation, then confabulation is being asked to do too much. I am aware of a third route associated with Dennett: the dream is composed or stored during sleep and then replayed into memory only at the moment of waking, so that it seems to have been experienced without ever having been an experience during sleep. What I want is a negotiated verdict. Spell out what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that is debunked and dropped, what it would take for the Malcolm-style conclusion to be revised or abandoned, and whether the replay view is a real reconciliation or merely a way of keeping the theory while relabeling the intuition.
