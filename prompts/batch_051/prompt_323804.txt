I'm a software engineer at a small company, no formal philosophy background. We build a product that uses open-source code, and every few weeks I have to decide whether some code we wrote is a derivative work of the copyleft/GPL program we used, or a genuinely seperate program that just talks to it. Get it wrong one way and we have a licensing mess; get it wrong the other way and we waste weeks reimplementing something we could have used. The question I keep hitting is conceptual: where does one program actually end and another begin?

Write me a decision procedure I could run in about 30 minutes per case, in plain language. It should be an ordered checklist of questions, with what counts as a good-enough answer to each, where it stops with "treat as derivative, comply," where it stops with "probably separate, proceed," and where it has to punt to a lawyer because it's a judgment call. No case law or citations needed—I want the underlying logic, not a legal reference—and be honest about what the procedure can't settle with any checklist.

Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) we read a GPL library's source to understand an algorithm, then reimplement it ourselves in another language without copying any of the code; (2) our proprietary app dynamically links a GPL library and calls its public API; (3) our app spawns a GPL command-line tool as a separate process and parses its text output. Make sure at least one case comes out the way a founder won't like, and say plainly which one.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the procedure overall, and which single step or distinction you're least sure about. End with a last line starting "Summary:" followed by a one-sentence summary of the procedure.
