I suspect they actually come apart in a way that matters for backward-looking responsibility, but I want a case that forces the split. So build me a thought experiment where a Lockean of the memory-criterion sort and someone committed to anatta give opposite verdicts on whether a later person can legitimately be held responsible for an earlier action, in the direction that's harder to construct: the Lockean says no, the later person is not the right target of blame or punishment, while the anatta person says yes, responsibility carries across, without any appeal to a soul or self. Constraints: an ordinary-life scenario, no rebirth or karma talk at all, no teletransporters or memory uploads, and the later person has lost all first-person memory of the act but still has some kind of continuity that the Buddhist can point to.

After the scenario, do the check: state exactly what the Lockean finds missing and why that blocks responsibility on the memory criterion, then state what causal or other continuity the anatta person relies on and why anatta makes that enough. Finish by saying which single feature of the case does the real work, and how a committed Lockean would push back without giving up the memory criterion.
