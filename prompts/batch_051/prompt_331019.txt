I'm working on a column for our ideas desk about whether a language could get by with no pronouns or indexicals — I mean words like I, you, here, now, and today. I keep going back and forth. On the one side, languages already drop subject pronouns when the verb marks the person (Spanish does it all the time), and you can imagine a language where people just use names or kinship labels instead of you. On the other side, there's that old argument that indexicals are irreducable: knowing the fire is behind the person at the back of the room doesn't by itself make me run — I also need to know that I am that person, and that extra self-locating bit seems hard to pry out of a purely non-indexical description. So here's what I'd like. Write me an essay on whether a language could in principle do without pronouns and indexicals, and whether such a language would be just as expressive or worse off. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but don't end on a fence-sitting note — actually take a position and defend it, even if it's a careful one. If your view is that a language could ditch pronouns but not indexicals (or the other way round), say so explicitly. Oh, and one wrinkle worth addressing: suppose a language replaced I with a demonstrative plus a point at your own chest, like this one is hungry. Should we count that as a language without pronouns, or just pronouns in disguise — and does that matter for whether the language is fully expressive? Keep it readable for a smart non-specialist, and don't worry about citations or a reading list.
