In a module on social epistemology, we have been working through C. Thi Nguyen’s distinction between echo chambers and epistemic bubbles: bubbles are informational structures in which relevant voices are omitted, whereas echo chambers are trust structures in which outside voices are actively discredited. That distinction is presented as conceptual, but it seems to carry an empirical claim about how different communities maintain themselves. Choose a concrete online setting, and design the best possible test for whether that setting is an echo chamber rather than a mere epistemic bubble. Specify what observations or interventions would count as evidence for manipulated trust rather than omission, what result would convince you the distinction is doing real work, and what a positive result would still leave unsettled about the concept. Please answer in British English.
