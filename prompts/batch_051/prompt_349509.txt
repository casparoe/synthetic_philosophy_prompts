For a paper I'm writing, I need to get clear on what people actually mean when they call something conceivable, because the claim that conceivability is a guide to possibility seems to depend entirely on that word. I'd like a working definition of "conceivable" that gives a defensible verdict for each of the following cases: a mountain made entirely of gold; a liquid that looks, tastes, and behaves exactly like water but is not H2O; a four-sided triangle; a machine that talks and acts exactly like a person but has no experiences; a ship traveling faster than light; a universe whose physical laws differ from ours but are internally consistent; and a sum of 2 and 2 equaling 5. Some of these strike me as obviously conceivable, some as obviously not, and several I keep reversing myself on. Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and identify the verdict you find least secure. Then say whether the definition that survives these cases leaves conceivability with any real claim to be a guide to possibility.
