I've run into a slogan that keeps being made to do serious work, and I want it pulled apart. A friend of mine recently defended treating strangers and casual acquaintances in a distant, instrumental way — as though their inner lives set no real constraint on him — by saying, in effect, that everyone else is an NPC. I've seen roughly the same line used online to shut down debates about whether we should model other people as having rich first-person perspectives, and it feels like a harder-edged cousin of the "main character" intuition. I understand the video-game reference, but the sentence plainly gets used for more than "some people are predictable."

Please treat it as a family of candidate claims and lay out a menu rather than defending just one reading. I can already imagine several: literal metaphysical solipsism, where only my own mind is real; the weaker epistemic claim that we can never have good enough evidence that another person is conscious; the zombie claim that other humans are behaviorally indistinguishable from real agents but have no phenomenal experience; the simulation claim that other people are literally scripted or generated by an underlying system; and the social-psychological claim that many people operate on automatic, unreflective scripts and do not really author their actions. Pull apart any further readings you think are live, and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or something probably false. Then say which reading my friend actually needs if the slogan is to justify instrumental treatment of other people, and whether that reading survives.
