Draft the joint statement that a committed negative-liberty theorist and a committed republican-liberty theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The deliverable is the statement itself: a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, formatted as a single JSON object with the schema specified below.

The two positions, stated so that each camp would accept the wording:

- Negative liberty (liberty as non-interference): a person is free to the degree that other persons do not intentionally obstruct, coerce, or constrain the choices she would otherwise make. Power over another is relevant to liberty only when it is exercised in actual interference; unexercised power may be an injustice, but it is not itself a loss of liberty.

- Republican liberty (liberty as non-domination): a person is free to the degree that she is not subject to another's arbitrary will. A has arbitrary power over B when A can interfere in B's choices at A's own discretion, without needing to track B's interests. On this view, a person can be made unfree by mere subjection to unexercised power; and a law that constrains conduct need not compromise liberty if it is non-arbitrary, enacted through regular and contestable processes and designed to track the interests of those it binds.

Positive liberty (self-mastery, self-realization) is out of scope except where the statement must distinguish it from the two positions above.

The statement must address all five of the following fixed cases:

C1. The benevolent slaveholder: an enslaved person whose owner never interferes with her daily choices and treats her kindly, but who could at any time sell, punish, or control her.

C2. The jailed dissident: a person imprisoned under a procedurally impeccable law she regards as unjust, enacted through regular and contestable institutions that she had a fair chance to influence.

C3. The algorithmically managed worker: a gig worker whose current shifts and pay are acceptable, but whose schedule, rate, and standing can be changed at any time by an opaque algorithmic system under the platform's unilateral control, with no explanation or avenue of appeal.

C4. The threatening mayor: a mayor who never interferes with residents but is known to be able and willing to ruin any critic through discretionary audits, permit delays, and dismissals.

C5. The speed limit: a citizen obeying an ordinary speed limit enacted through regular, contestable procedures that she had a fair chance to influence.

Constraints on the content. The agreed claims must be neither vacuous nor slanted, and must include (a) a shared statement of the question to which the two traditions are rival answers; (b) at least two claims about C1-C5 on which the two sides' verdicts coincide, such as that C1 is a grave wrong and that C2 and C5 involve actual interference in the ordinary sense; (c) a shared account of what counts as interference; (d) an explicit joint statement that interference can occur without domination and domination can occur without current interference; and (e) a joint rejection of at least one view neither side accepts. Where a claim could not be signed by both camps without dishonesty, it belongs instead in the disagreements table.

Format the output as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- title: string, a descriptive title.
- preamble: string, 2–4 sentences both camps would sign as the statement's opening.
- agreed_claims: array of strings, in order; the array order is the numbering of the section. Each claim should be written in the first-person plural.
- disagreements: array of exactly five objects, one per case, each with:
    - issue: string, a short label naming the case and the dispute.
    - negative_liberty_formulation: string, the negative-liberty side's verdict and reasoning, worded as its own proponent would endorse.
    - republican_liberty_formulation: string, the republican side's verdict and reasoning, worded as its own proponent would endorse.
    - crux: string, the general sub-question the case isolates, such that a settled answer would move at least one side.
    - agreed_test: string, a concrete variation of the case, observation, or argument that both sides agree bears on the crux.
    - negative_liberty_prediction: string, what the negative-liberty side predicts the agreed test will show, stated in advance.
    - republican_liberty_prediction: string, what the republican side predicts the agreed test will show, stated in advance.
    - classification: string, one of "genuine_conceptual_disagreement", "empirical_crux", or "partly_verbal". Use "empirical_crux" only when the residual dispute turns on facts that could in principle be settled by evidence. Use "partly_verbal" only when the table also identifies the word or scope shift that created the appearance of disagreement.
- open_questions: array of 1–3 strings, questions both camps would jointly pursue next.

Output only the JSON object, with no surrounding text or commentary.
