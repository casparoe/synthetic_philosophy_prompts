So my friend and roommate and I just burned two hours arguing about the Sleeping Beauty problem (fair coin Sunday night, Monday-only if heads, Monday plus Tuesday if tails with the memory wipe between), and it left me with a question that's half film criticism and half epistemology. I'm a halfer, she's a thirder, and neither of us moved an inch. At some point I dragged Groundhog Day into it, because Phil Connors is basically Sleeping Beauty minus the amnesia: every morning he wakes up in Punxsutawney on February 2, but from inside his own head the date is kind of deceptive, since the day has reset and he's stuck somewhere in a stack of repeated February 2s. I argued the movie is a halfer movie, because Phil remembers earlier loops and accumulates evidence, so his uncertainty about "where" he is in the sequence tracks something objective instead of getting diluted across duplicate wakings. My roommate said I had it backwards: each morning is one more subjective waking, so if you counted up all the wakings you should start spreading credence across loops in a thirder-ish way. We both got hand-wavy, and the argument ended with us arguing about wether the groundhog seeing its shadow even matters.

I'd like a careful reading of Groundhog Day through the Sleeping Beauty problem and self-locating belief. What should a halfer and a thirder each say Phil ought to update toward, and does the film's ending actually settle anything or just dodge the question?
