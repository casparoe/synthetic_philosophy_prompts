As a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, I have developed the habit of trying to reverse-engineer the decision rule behind a person's case-by-case judgments rather than asking them for it directly. I have been doing this for several years with a senior colleague, a physician on our hospital's treatment-review committee. She has classified all of the following as therapy: cochlear implants for a child born deaf; thyroxine for hypothyroidism; a standard antidepressant for moderate clinical depression; erythropoietin for anemia caused by chemotherapy. She has classified all of the following as enhancement: growth hormone for an otherwise healthy child whose predicted adult height is near the lower bound of the normal range, with no endocrine deficiency and no recorded distress; methylphenidate for healthy university students who wanted it for examination preparation, with no documented disorder or distress. Next month she must classify a new request: a healthy professional violinist wants episodic low-dose propranolol to suppress the tremor and sweating of ordinary performance nerves during concerts. The violinist has no diagnosable anxiety disorder and no impairment in daily functioning, but she reports real dread before performing that the drug would substantially reduce. Predict my colleague's verdict. More importantly, say which rule she is implicitly running, which other rules fit her past classifications just as well, and which further case would best force those rules apart.
