I am a high school student applying to a summer philosophy program, and the application requires a one-page research proposal for a project I would carry out during the program and write up as a paper of around four thousand words. The topic I want to work on is whether it makes sense to say that a machine, specifically a large language model, can make a genuine intuitive leap, and whether the popular way of posing this as a question about producing something beyond the training distribution is even coherent. My half-formed idea is this: the standard debate keeps sliding between two senses of novelty, a product sense, in which a text is novel when it does not resemble particular training examples, and a process sense, in which an idea is novel when it was reached by the kind of search and selection that we credit as a leap in a person. I suspect that separating these would not settle the debate but would reveal that the original question is ambiguous, because an output can be far from any specific training example while still being an interpolation in a sufficiently fine-grained space of descriptions, and because whether something counts as beyond the distribution depends on how one chooses to describe the space in the first place. I also suspect the human comparison does more work than people admit, since we call a student's move an intuitive leap even when it combines two techniques the student already knew. I would like you to turn this into a proposal with a precise central question, an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open, a method I could realistically follow, an account of what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure, and a scope appropriate to the length. Please write the proposal in continuous prose, the way one would write a letter or an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and do not include a bibliography or citations.
