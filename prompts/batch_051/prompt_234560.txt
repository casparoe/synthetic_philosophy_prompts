I'm compiling a list of arguments for keeping conscription in a small, neutral or non-aligned country — Switzerland today, Sweden and Finland in their post-war non-aligned decades — where conscription is the backbone of a total-defense posture. So far I have these: (1) deterrence by denial: an invader knows that even after it beats the regular army it faces a whole trained population that can make occupation permanently expensive; (2) fairness: the risk and burden of defense are spread across classes and regions instead of being dumped on a volunteer underclass; (3) anti-praetorianism: a conscript force is a cross-section of society and so can't turn into a politically dangerous professional caste; (4) strategic depth: a mass reserve provides not just infantry but doctors, engineers, logisticians, and cyber specialists; (5) cost: a cadre-plus-reserve militia gives a small state more mass per unit of spending than a standing professional force of the same budget; (6) civic cohesion: mixing people of different classes, regions, languages, and immigrant backgrounds in service builds the shared identity and social trust that total defense runs on. I think these cover the stock moves, but I have the feeling I'm missing one. Give me an argument for conscription in that setting that is genuinely different from these six — not a restatement, special case, or combination of them — and that a serious defender of the Swiss, Swedish, or Finnish model could actually offer. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence: name the argument, then give the reasoning, and say honestly whether it's any good. No preamble and don't restate the question.
