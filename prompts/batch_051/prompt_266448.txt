After a year of arguing past each other, a presentist and an eternalist about time agree to run an adversarial collaboration and produce a single joint document. Draft that document for them. The presentist holds that only presently existing things exist, and that truths about the past and future must be accounted for without quantifying over merely past or future objects; the eternalist holds that past, present, and future things all exist tenselessly, with now merely indexical.

The document should open with a numbered list of claims both can sign, covering at least what each position is committed to, the data about tensed and tenseless discourse they both accept, what special relativity allows and rules out, and which moves they both regard as fair or unfair (tallying truthmakers, positing a privileged foliation, rephrasing the other side in one's own idiom, and the like). Then include a disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give the formulation each side would accept for its own position, and identify the crux on which it turns. Where a case or argument bears on a crux, attach it and record each side's prediction in advance. The cases I especially want covered are: what makes it true now that there were non-avian dinosaurs; the Andromeda simultaneity argument; and a truth about a particular past individual (say, that Cleopatra ruled Egypt) chosen to test whether presentist surrogates can capture particularity rather than only qualitative past-tense content.

Separate substantive disagreements from ones that are merely verbal or merely presentational, and say which are which. If you cannot find a neutral formulation, write each side's formulation and say what remains unstated. I would rather you flag uncertainty about a formulation or a case than paper over it. And if the framing itself is confused — for example, if "existence" is already equivocal enough that there is no shared proposition they disagree about — tell me plainly instead of writing the document on those terms.
