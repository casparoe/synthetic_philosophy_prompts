i've been using a term i made up, 'skyhook laundering,' and i want to know if it's a real distinction or just a catchy name for something obvious. here's what i mean. dennett talks about cranes, explanations that build the fancy thing up from simpler parts and mechanisms, and skyhooks, explanations that just hang the fancy thing from above and call it done. my term is for a third thing that pretends to be a crane. it's a mechanistic explanation that walks you through boxes and arrows, and then at the crucial box quietly uses the very capacity it was supposed to explain. so you get an account of decision-making that bottoms out in a module whose job is described as 'choosing,' or an account of memory that ends in a store that gets 'retrieved' when needed, and nobody notices because the diagram looked mechanistic. i keep seeing this everywhere, and my central claim is that most bad reductionism in psychology and neuroscience is actually this, not greedy reductionism in dennett's sense. greedy reductionism explains a thing away by dropping it; skyhook laundering keeps the thing but hides it inside one of the boxes. is this a real category? it feels like it must be a hundred years old under another name. give me the clearest example you can construct of a mechanism explanation that launders the phenomenon this way, and one case where the same structure is legitimate because the capacity sitting in the box is genuinely basic. and tell me where the distinction i'm drawing breaks down or stops being distinctive.
