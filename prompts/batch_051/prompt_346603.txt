I'm giving a colloquium next month in a linguistics department, and I'm nervous about the Q&A. My thesis is that an alphabet is better thought of as a theory of its language than as a transcription of it: even a maximally "phonemic" script is making a bet about which unit — the segment, the morpheme, the feature — carries the contrasts worth writing down, and the interesting question is never "how phonemic is this alphabet?" but "what is the non-phonemic part doing?" I'm building it around three cases: Finnish, where the system is systematic about the segment; English, where it's systematic about the morpheme; and Hangul, where it's systematic about the feature. My argument sketch is that each one pays a different cost — reader effort, writer effort, morphological transparency — and that calling an orthography "shallow" or "deep" just names the cost without saying what it buys.

The audience is mostly phonologists who will think I've stolen the word "theory" from them and applied it to an after-the-fact code; a couple of psycholinguists who work on visual word recognition and will want to know why the reader's task doesn't just settle evreything; and one historical linguist who actually knows the writing-systems literature and will show up with messy loanword and scribal-convention examples. My worry is that I get generic objections about English spelling being a historical accident and never have to defend the actual claim.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet, not a survey. Order the objections by how likely and how damaging they are — I want the order I'll actually get them, not an imposed taxonomy. Phrase each one the way a phonologist or psycholinguist in this room would phrase it, in their vocabulary. For each: does it have real force, what should I concede up front rather than get caught on, a two-minute reply, and where that reply is softest. End with the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And take a definite position in every reply — if you think one of my cases is the weak one, say so and tell me how to defend it anyway.
