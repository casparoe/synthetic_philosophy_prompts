I keep being unable to decide whether a parallel I noticed is deep or just pattern-matching, and I would like the structure checked rather than merely elaborated. In AI safety, the orthogonality thesis says that an agent's capability is independent of its final goals: any level of competence can in principle be paired with any goal. Instrumental convergence then says that, despite this independence, many very different final goals converge on the same subgoals—self-preservation, resource acquisition, keeping one's goals from being rewritten, and improving one's ability to act. This looks to me structurally like convergent evolution in biology: lineages with very different niches, diets, and reproductive strategies independently arrive at the same designs—camera eyes, wings, social cooperation—because they face similar selection pressures, with no one intending the pattern. Both seem to be cases in which an optimization process pushes an enormous space of starting points into a small set of shared solutions. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is genuine, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer between them, and what is one thing the analogy would predict that turns out false?
