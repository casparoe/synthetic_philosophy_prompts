My position, roughly: an act can be right, with good consequences, and still lack moral worth if the person did it for the wrong reason. His position: the whole "right reasons" thing is either just how much you personally like the agent, or it collapses into consequences plus maybe overall character, and anyway talking about "the reason someone acted on" is unfalsifiable storytelling.

Here's the spec. But here's the constraint my friend kept pinning me with: the scenario can't just stipulate their private mental states from a god's-eye view and call it done. The difference in what they acted on has to be earned by stuff that would be observable in principle — how each one deliberates, what they'd have done under a small variation, how they describe their own reasoning afterward, that kind of thing. If the verdict depends on me reading their souls to the reader, it's cheating. Also keep the scenario tight enough that I could retell it from memory over a beer.

Then isolate the one structural feature doing the actual work, and say what happens to the case if you remove it.
