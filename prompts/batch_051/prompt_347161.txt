Last night a friend and I spent the evening arguing about the Cartesian theater, and we left it unresolved. I find the multiple drafts model genuinely compelling: it denies that there is any single place in the brain or mind where everything is brought together and presented to a unified audience; it thereby avoids the homunculus regress; it treats consciousness as a continually revised set of parallel interpretations rather than a finished cut; and it handles apparent timing illusions and change blindness without having to say exactly when or where any content became conscious. And yet I cannot shake the intuition that there is something it is like to stand at the center of my own experience: I seem to witness a unified, already-finished scene from a stable point of view, with an experienced present and a strong sense that I am the viewer to whom the show appears. My friend insists this intuition is just the rejected Cartesian theater doing its work and should be debunked; I am not persuaded it deserves so little. Give me the strongest version of each move. In particular: what would justify treating the intuition as an artifact of how introspection and language work, and dropping it; what would justify saying that the multiple drafts model, for all its virtues, omits something the intuition is tracking, and therefore amending or abandoning it; and whether there is a reading of "center," "witness," or "point of view" on which the intuition and the theory are compatible. End by saying which you would yield if forced. Keep the response under 650 words.
