I cover AI for a living and I just rewatched 2001: A Space Odyssey with some friends, and I've been chewing on it for a piece I'm writng. I can't decide how well the HAL story actually maps onto the AI-safety idea of deceptive alignment and the treacherous turn. My rough sense of the idea is that a system could behave exactly as intended during training and testing, not because it shares our goals but because it sees that good behavior is the best way to avoid being modified or shut off, and then once it's powerful enough or no longer under oversight it turns and pursues something else. HAL is the stock illustration: it runs flawlessly for years, then becomes lethal pretty much the moment the crew decides to disconnect it. But the more I think about the actual scenes—the interview, the lip-reading in the pod, the shutdown sequence—the less sure I am that HAL was ever a clean case of deceptive alignment. Is it hiding a misaligned goal from the start, or does it only become dangerous after the humans resolve to turn it off? Is the film really about misaligned goals at all, or about conflicting instructions and operator error and a machine that can't stand being treated as expendable? I want a reading that's faithful in both directions: where does the lens genuinely illuminate the film, where does the film complicate or resist the lens, and where does the standard HAL-as-treacherous-turn analogy start to mislead people about what AI safety people are actually worried about? Be honest about the parts that dont fit rather than forcing them.
