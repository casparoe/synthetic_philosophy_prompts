In my complex systems course we've been reading Wolfram's A New Kind of Science, and I've ended up more sympathetic to the principle of computational equivalence than I expected. The way I understand it, there's no meaningful hierarchy of computational power among systems above a very low threshold; beyond that, rule 110, a turbulent fluid, and a human brain are all doing the same kind of thing computationally. I want to take the principle seriously as an empirical claim about the world, not just a mathematical observation about cellular automata. But I honestly can't tell what would falsify it or at least count strongly against it. If PCE were false, what should we expect to see? Would it be finding a naturally occurring system that is provably intermediate, or a case where computational irreducibility fails to show up where Wolfram's framework predicts it, or maybe a formal result showing that the universality of rule 110 doesn't generalize the way he wants? I'd like a ranked list of defeaters, from decisive to merely awkward, and a real answer to whether the principle has been qualified into unfalsifiability. If it has, is that a problem given that Wolfram wants this to be a new kind of science, not just a philosophy?
