A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether resentment is something to be cured in a person or something to be listened to, and by the end I found myself defending a position I don't yet know how to defend. Let me state it. Resentment, and the kind of envy Rawls treats as excusable — envy directed at an unjust inequality rather than simply at another person's having some good — should not be classified as vices, or even as emotions with a fixed moral valence. I want to say they are a person's inchoate registration that an injury or inequality is unjust: a signal whose moral status is determined by the justice of what it responds to, not by the agent's character. The same underlying state would be a vice when aimed at a justified advantage and something closer to warranted protest when aimed at an unjust one. A rough operational test is this: the state functions as a signal of injustice to the degree that it would dissolve if the agent came to believe the arrangement was just.

I want to know what would count against this view, sorted by how badly. Specifically: what cases of resentment that tracks no injustice are nevertheless vicious in a way the view must own, and what cases of resentment that does track a real injustice are still ones we hesitate to endorse? What does the empirical distinction some psychologists draw between an envy that motivates self-improvement and an envy that wishes the rival harm do to the claim that the state's moral valence just is the justice of the arrangement? Does the appeal to the agent's perception of injustice insulate the view so thoroughly that no observation could refute it, and would that be a defect for a view of this kind? I would also like to know whether the view quietly collapses into the platitude that resenting genuine injustice is fine, and if so what work the signal metaphor is still doing. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish the ones a committed defender would concede from the ones they would dismiss, and say whether the position in its strongest form risks anything at all. Finish with a one-sentence summary of the view's overall vulnerability, in the form \answer{...}.
