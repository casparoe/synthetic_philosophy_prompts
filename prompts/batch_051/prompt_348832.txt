I’m organizing a small reading group on asceticism, and we’ve been stuck on a question I’d like to put to a guest. The guest is someone I’ll describe as “a defender of a neo-Thoreauvian ideal of voluntary simplicity”—not a historian of Thoreau, but someone who thinks the core of the ideal survives without Thoreau’s specific metaphysics or his 1840s economic assumptions. The group has read the “Economy” chapter of Walden, and the passage that keeps getting argued over is this one from Henry David Thoreau, Walden, “Economy”:

“I had three pieces of limestone on my desk, but I was terrified to find that they required to be dusted daily, when the furniture of my mind was all undusted still, and threw them out the window in disgust.”

Our disagreement is about what the disgust is really tracking. One faction says the limestone is just a stand-in for any possession whose upkeep cost exceeds its use, so the passage is essentially about maintenance burdens and attention budgets. Another faction says no, the important thing is the order of the two dustings: Thoreau isn’t against the stones because they cost time simpliciter, but because he noticed he was maintaining the wrong kind of surface, and the disgust is about a kind of misplaced reverence. A third faction thinks both readings are too intellectual, and the passage is mostly aesthetic revulsion at the thought of mindless repetitive housekeeping.

Can you draft eight to ten interview questions that would force the guest to say which elements of Thoreau’s disgust are load-bearing for the modern ideal, and which are just Thoreau’s temperament? I want at least one question each on: the idea that the object is “innocent” and the problem is the daily relation to it; on whether the position devalues housekeeping or just refuses to count it as the kind of activity a life should be built around; and on whether the dust analogy even works, since mental dust seems to require a different kind of upkeep than physical dust. For each of those, include a follow-up that presses the guest’s most likely move. And throughout, I’d like you to flag how confident you are in your interpretation of the passage and the questions, and to name the point you’re least sure about.
