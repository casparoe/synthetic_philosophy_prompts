In my grad seminar on agency we keep hitting the same wall: someone says they're eighty percent sure they'll write the damn paper over break, and at least one person immediately repeats the slogan "deliberation crowds out prediction" as if that settles it. I know there's a real idea behind it somewhere, having to do with Newcomb-style cases and the thought that deciding whether to φ is different from forecasting that you'll φ. But the slogan is doing too much work. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims it might be expressing—e.g. about whether self-assigned probabilities are legitimate, whether they can ever be anything other than zero or one, whether assigning a probability to your own choice changes or undermines the deliberation, and what it means for an option to still be open—and say for each whether it's a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or just false? And in particular, which of those readings would actually support the move from "I'm 80% sure I'll do it" to "your 80% is incoherant"?
