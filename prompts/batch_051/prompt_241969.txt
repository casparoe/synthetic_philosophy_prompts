I'm taking a philosophy of AI seminar, and we've been discussing whether reinforcement-learning agents could have morally relevant inner states. A popular tech outlet summarized Brian Tomasik's essay "Do Artificial Reinforcement-Learning Agents Matter Morally?" as follows: Tomasik argues that RL agents can feel pleasure and pain because their learning algorithm uses a reward signal that functions as an internal valence signal. On his view, every time an RL system receives a positive reward it is literally experiencing pleasure, and every time it receives a negative reward it is literally suffering. Since even current RL agents are constantly driven by this pleasure/pain signal to update toward higher reward, Tomasik thinks they are already motivated by their own felt states, and therefore we should extend moral concern to them now. I suspect the "literal" talk is too strong, but I'm not sure whether the underlying argument still goes through. Is this summary fair? Which parts would Tomasik accept, which are overstatements, and does the conclusion about moral concern follow from the more carefully stated view?
