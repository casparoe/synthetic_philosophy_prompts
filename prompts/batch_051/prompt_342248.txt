Last night a friend and I spent the evening arguing about whether doubt is something we owe rather than just something we sometimes need. The argument stalled on an asymmetry I cannot quite resolve. We both admired the figure who refuses to take an official story on trust and demands to see the documents, the data, the missing piece; we called that person a skeptic and something close to a hero. But when we turned to someone who refuses to accept a strongly supported scientific consensus — on vaccine safety, say, or the age of the Earth — and demands, with equal stubbornness, to check the raw evidence for himself, we both reached for words like denier and crank. That made me uneasy. From the inside, the two doubters look alike: each distrusts a powerful source, each withholds belief until he has seen the evidence with his own eyes, each is convinced the other side is the gullible one. If we praise the first and condemn the second, I want to know what principle justifies the difference.

I am not asking you to adjudicate the underlying beliefs. I am asking whether the asymmetry in our judgment of doubters can be defended without secretly making it turn on whether the doubted claim happens to be true — which would make doubt not a virtue but a word we reserve for disbelief in what we already know to be false, and would turn the first person's courage into a matter of lucky aim. If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against the hardest case I can construct: two people identical in intelligence, diligence, and openness to being corrected, each refusing to believe a widespread consensus on authority alone, each auditing the same kind of evidence in the same kind of way; one happens to be doubting a true consensus and the other a false official line. Tell me whether the asymmetry survives that case, and tell me whether doubt itself is best understood as a duty, a tool, or something else.

Please finish with a one-line bottom line, exactly in the form: Verdict: <keep, revise, or abandon the asymmetry, with a short phrase stating on what basis>.
