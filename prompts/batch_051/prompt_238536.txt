I have been collecting a friend's snap judgments on small made-up cases where one person deceives another, because I want to reverse-engineer the rule she is actually applying. Several different rules can be made to fit her record so far, and I would like your help predicting how she will judge a new case that I suspect will force the issue.

Her current record is this. She says an engineer who tells a job interviewer he knows a programming language he has never used has done something wrong. She also says an engineer who answers the same interview question by saying he has read the language's documentation and built a small side project in it, which is true, but who knows the interviewer will take that as evidence of real competence, has done something wrong as well; she says the literal truth of the words does not get him off the hook. But she says an engineer who tells a coworker he saw a mutual friend at the grocery store two days ago has done nothing wrong, even though the coworker takes that to mean the friend is still in town and ends up mistaken, and even though the engineer has no idea whether the friend has left town since.

The new case I want you to predict is this. An engineer is at a party and an acquaintance asks whether the startup they both once worked at was acquired. The engineer says the two founders are no longer at the company. That is true, but he knows the acquaintance will take it as a yes; the founders actually left after a falling out, and there was no acquisition. He says it deliberately because he wants the story to sound dramatic. There is no professional relationship, nothing turns on the answer, and no harm follows from the acquaintance believing the startup was acquired.

What would my friend say about the party case, and why? In your answer, identify the candidate rules or principles that fit her record so far, say what each one predicts about the party case, give me your best prediction of her verdict with your reasons for preferring that reading over the others, and then give me one further case I could put to her that would most cleanly separate whatever rules are still live. Please write the whole thing in continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables; write it the way you would write an email or a letter.
