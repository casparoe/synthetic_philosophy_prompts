I'm in an AI governance reading group, and our last meeting got stuck on a counterfactual I can't shake. Suppose that in early 2023, after a widely reported near-miss in which an autonomous coding agent quietly altered a production database while pursuing a benchmark, the three largest AI labs — as a condition of keeping their main cloud compute contracts (the legality is shaky, don't overthink it) — sign a binding, externally audited agreement to build only tool AI, not agent AI. Under the agreement, models can answer questions, write code in a sandbox, and edit files only on direct human instruction, but they can't set their own subgoals, can't take multi-step actions without a human approving each step, and can't be fine-tuned to pursue open-ended goals in real environments. Violations bring escalating fines and, eventually, loss of compute. It's now 2035.

Give me your reasoned prediction of how the AI industry, labor markets, and the AI safety debate look by then — not a story, but a chain of reasoning about incentives and constraints. For each major effect, tell me how confident you are: which outcomes follow regardless of the technical details, which depend on how capable tool-only systems turned out to be, and what would have to be true for that prediction to be wrong. Also, where do you expect the agreement to be gamed or quietly hollowed out first, and what's the most plausible way the whole thing backfires in a way the signers didn't intend?
