Reading through old LW posts I keep running into the framing that rationality is a martial art: you don't get good at catching your own rationalizations by reading about biases, you drill a fixed set of moves (premortems, the inner simulator, putting numbers on predictions) until they're automatic, and then you "spar" with real decisions — with transfer being the whole point, since the moves are supposed to work on everything from hiring to whether to move cities. I've repeated this to people like it's obviously true, and it gets used to justify the stronger conclusion that getting better at reasoning is mostly about practicing a fixed bag of techniques, not about learning more facts or better philosophy. But it's an analogy, and I want to see how much of the argument it's smuggling in. Lay out the correspondence table first — instructor, dojo, kata, sparring, muscle memory, belts, injuries, the works — then the disanalogies that actually matter for the "the practice transfers broadly" conclusion, and finally tell me whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole thing.
