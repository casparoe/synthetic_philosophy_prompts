I'm a PhD student in philosophy of language, and I've been sitting in on a seminar that keeps going in circles about Quine's Two Dogmas of Empiricism. The Carnap-inclined people grant that Quine showed something about naive verificationism, but they think the analytic-synthetic distinction survives as a matter of which sentences we hold fixed as meaning postulates when we set up a language. The Quineans reply that this just relocates the problem: nothing in our later behavior determines whether a revision was a change of meaning or a change of belief about the facts. A third camp thinks Quine was attacking a verificationist criterion of cognitive synonymy that nobody actually needed, so the whole fight is about an abandoned research program. And everyone has the same intuitions about bachelors and arithmetic anyway. My hunch is that the real disagreement is about confirmation: whether it is holistic in a way that makes any local distinction between meaning-change and belief-change impossible, so the language talk is downstream of an epistemological fight. Is that a fair diagnosis, and what kind of disagreement is this actually? Given the diagnosis, what would count as progress from here? Please seperate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present one side as the consensus where it isn't.
