In a graduate seminar I teach on the philosophy of physics and the physics of information, a student has been defending the following argument, and I would like a careful logical audit of it before I respond. The setup is the usual Maxwellian box: an isolated box divided into two chambers by a wall with a frictionless trapdoor and filled with gas. The demon is an automaton controlling the trapdoor, and it is equipped with a memory tape that begins blank; the tape is appended to during the experiment and is never erased. The argument the student gave runs as follows. (1) The demon measures each molecule that approaches the trapdoor and classifies it as slow or fast, at no thermodynamic cost. (2) It writes one bit recording the classification into the next blank cell of the tape; this writing operation is logically reversible, because the state of the molecule together with the state of the tape before the write and after the write contain the same information, so by the principle that only logically irreversible operations carry an unavoidable dissipation, the write itself dissipates nothing. (3) Since no cell of the tape is ever erased, the demon never pays the familiar Landauer cost for erasure. (4) Acting on each stored bit, the demon opens the trapdoor only for fast molecules moving toward the left chamber and slow molecules moving toward the right chamber, and this gating performs no work and transfers no heat. (5) After many cycles, the left chamber is hot and the right chamber is cold, so the entropy of the gas is lower than it was initially. (6) Therefore the entropy of the entire isolated box, and hence of the universe, has decreased: the gas has lost entropy, and nothing else has gained any. I find each step individually plausible yet the conclusion absurd, and I suspect the mistake is somewhere in the move from the reversibility of the individual operations to the entropy balance of the whole arrangement. Is the argument valid? If it is not, say exactly which step fails and supply the corrected version of that step. If it is valid, say which premise is false and explain what a defender of the second law must claim about the memory tape and its blank cells. Write your answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
