I'm preparing a section on church–state arrangements — the Church of England, the German Kirchensteuer, French laïcité, American disestablishment — and a student seminar threw out a proposal I keep turning over, so I'd like a proper anaylsis of it. Suppose a country with a historically established church decides to scrap the single establishment and replace it with a universal worldview levy. Every adult pays one percent of their income on top of normal tax, with no opt-out; each year they can direct the money to any registered worldview organisation — a church, mosque, humanist association, secular burial society, whatever. They can switch the direction as often as once a month through a government portal, and the money follows from the next month. If someone ticks no box, the levy goes into a Civic Neutrality Fund, which maintains listed historic places of worship and pays civic chaplains in hospitals, prisons and universities. Any body can register if it can show five thousand members and keeps open accounts; no single organisation may receive more than twenty-five percent of the total worldview revenue, and any excess is swept back into the Civic Fund. Children default to their parents' choice until sixteen, then to the Civic Fund until they first choose. The state insists this is not establishment, just administrative neutrality.

What I can't get straight is who this actually ends up serving and where it breaks first. Walk me through the incentives it creates for the old established church, for small and new groups, for the Treasury, and for ordinary taxpayers. Where would you expect gaming to show up first — shell organisations to dodge the cap, padded membership lists, registration of whole families of bodies? Does monthly switching turn affiliation into a boycotting tool or a tracking poll; does the incumbent church just become the default and stay dominant; and does the Civic Fund become a prize for whoever controls the spending rules? I'm especially interested in the second-order stuff: what happens to the meaning of membership, to proselytising, and to the data the state now holds on everyone's monthly worldview choices. And could you mark how confident you are in the overall read, and single out the bit you're least sure of?
