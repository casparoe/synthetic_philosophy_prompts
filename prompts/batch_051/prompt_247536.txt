I'm in a jurisprudence course and we've been doing legal fictions, and I keep going in circles. On one side I'm pretty convinced by legal positivism: what makes something law is a social fact about what officials do and treat as binding, which is clean and keeps law seperate from morality, and on that picture a legal fiction is just a falsehood the system agrees to treat as true for convenience. On the other side I cant shake the intuition that when the law says a corporation is a person, the corporation really is a legal person—it genuinely sues, owns property, and owes duties, not pretend-ones. That feels less like the law agreeing to say something false and more like the law creating real legal powers. I dont want someone to just pick a side. Give me the strongest version of each, then tell me what it would actually take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the positivist story to need amending, and whether there's a reading where both survive (e.g., 'person' is just a defined term inside the system).
