Not sure if this counts as philosophy or just my field not thinking things through, but I'd like you to help me pin down what the standard practice actually assumes. I'm an atmospheric science grad student and I work with CMIP-style climate model ensembles. The way the community usually summarizes future change is this: each modeling center runs its model through a common protocol, each model gets one vote in the multi-model mean no matter how similar it is to the others, and then the spread across models is reported as if it were the uncertainty range policymakers should care about. At the same time, agreement among models is treated as the main reason to call a projection robust. But everyone knows these models are not independent samples of anything. A lot of them share atmospheric or ocean components, several are close cousins built on the same code base, they're all tuned to the same historical observations, and sometimes a bias or bug propagates from one center to another. So model agreement can just mean common ancestry or shared assumptions, and the spread mostly reflects which subset of choices the participating centers happened to make.

Here's my puzzle. What does this practice presuppose about what a multi-model ensemble is? Does it treat the models as a random sample from some population of possible models, as a pool of expert judgments to be averaged, as a stress test for robustness, or as something else? Can those commitments live together in one practice, or do they quietly rule each other out? And is the practice coherent if the models are partly duplicated, or is equal weighting more like a convention we keep becuase nobody wants to defend a weighting scheme?

And then practically: if a city planner or a farmer is trying to use these projections to make a decision, what would a decision-relevant uncertainty framework have to do differently — about weighting, independence, reporting ranges, maybe scenario-based planning instead of one best-estimate range? I'm not asking for a reading list. I want the implicit theory reconstructed, and a sense of which parts of the practice are genuinely forced by that theory and which parts are just habit.
