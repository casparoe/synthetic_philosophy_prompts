I'm on a parish council in Cumbria and we're part of a consultation about rewilding a small upland valley — stop the sheep grazing, let the scrub and bog come back, pull some of the old drains. So I end up reading a lot of public comments, and over the years I've settled into a rule for how I weigh them. I mentally cross out every sentence that rests on the intrinsic worth of the place — the valley has value in itself, nature deserves protection for its own sake, that sort of thing. I only keep what can be restated either instrumentally (flood storage, water quality, carbon, tourism) or relationally (a farmer's attachment to the land, the village's identity bound up in the landscape). If a comment can't be translated into one of those two vocabularies, I give it no weight in forming my own view.

I don't think im doing anything weird: if intrinsic value means something I can't check, it's useless as a reason, and if it means something checkable it'll cash out in one of the other vocabularies in the end. But I've also noticed my rule makes some comments very easy to dismiss, and easy dismissal is a warning sign.
