I've been mulling over van Fraassen's Reflection principle — roughly, that my current credence in A should equal my expectation of my future credence in A, so I should treat my future self as some kind of expert. The standard objections I've seen all seem to have the same shape. My list so far:

- the pill case, where I know I'll be given a drug that makes me believe A regardless of whether A is true;
- the forgetting case, where I currently have good evidence for A but know I won't have it later;
- the sleep-deprivation case, where I know my later judgment will be made at 3am after no sleep;
- the social pressure case, where I know I'll later be surrounded by people who disagree and will eventually cave just to fit in.

All of these are basically saying that my future self might be irrational, biased, or less informed than I am now. I want an objection to Reflection that isn't just another version of that move. Specifically, can you come up with an objection that would create trouble for the principle even if my future self were perfectly rational and had all my current evidence plus more? Please brainstorm a bit before committing, dont just reword one of my cases, and then tell me honestly whether the objection you find is any good.
