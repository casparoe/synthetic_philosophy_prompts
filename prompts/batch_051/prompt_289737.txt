I have recently been transcribing stories from my mother's community, and I have run into a puzzle that has become personal: the community's central origin myth exists in three living versions. The oldest is a fragmentary written text, known to only a few people and never retold at public gatherings. The second is the version performed at the annual festival; it adds an episode explaining why a certain ritual is done, but there is no documentary evidence for that episode before the nineteenth century. The third is the version now told to children and printed in the local schoolbook; it omits the ritual episode and changes the ending so that the protagonist chooses to remain with her family rather than return to the mountain. When I asked three elders which is the real story, each named a different one.
