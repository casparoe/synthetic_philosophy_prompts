I'm giving a talk next month at an AI alignment workshop where I'm the only person defending scalable oversight via debate. The audience is mostly epistemologists — people who work on testimony, peer disagreement, and expertise — plus a couple of ML engineers who work on RLHF. My thesis is that two AI systems arguing opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge who can't evaluate the answer directly can still let the judge reliably pick the correct side, because checking an argument for flaws is much easier than generating a good argument, so an honest debater can expose the liar's mistakes. I already know the standard LessWrong objections (collusion, obfuscated arguments, the judge being systematically snowed, reward hacking the setup). What I want is a Q&A prep sheet for the objections this audience will actually raise: phrase them the way an epistemologist would, ordered by how likely and damaging they are, each with a short reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Flag which objections come from the epistemologists versus the ML engineers, and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
