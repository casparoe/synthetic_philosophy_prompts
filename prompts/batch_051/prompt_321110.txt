My housemate and I got into it about whether young Germans today are 'guilty' for the Holocaust, and he kept falling back on the line that the sins of the fathers are visited on the children as if that closed the case. When I pushed him on what that actually means — are the children guilty of the fathers' deeds, or just stuck with the consequences, or something else — he just shrugged and repeated it. I have a vague memory of Jaspers splitting guilt into criminal, political, moral, and something metaphysical around exactly this question, but I can't reconstruct his distinctions with any confidence. Take the proverb and lay out every distinct claim it could be making, from the practically trivial to the morally loaded, and say for each whether it's plausible — and which reading, if any, actually supports saying a German born in 1995 is guilty for the Holocaust. I want a menu of readings with verdicts, not a defense of the one you like best.
