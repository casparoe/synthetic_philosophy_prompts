In our philosophy and game theory unit we spent a while on the one-shot prisoner's dilemma, and I have been trying to combine it with the dark forest hypothesis, the science-fiction idea that the universe looks empty because every civilization that detects another fears being destroyed, so all of them stay silent or strike first. I want to vary the standard prisoner's dilemma along those lines and see what the usual game-theoretic tools actually say about a formalized version. Suppose two interstellar civilizations have just detected each other and must simultaneously choose to cooperate (attempt contact and remain defensively open) or defect (launch a preemptive first strike). Each is privately one of two types. With probability p it is a dark type, whose payoffs are 10 for striking while the other cooperates, 3 for mutual peace, 1 for mutual strike, and 0 for being struck while cooperating; for the dark type, defection strictly dominates cooperation, exactly as in the original prisoner's dilemma. With probability 1-p it is a dove type, whose payoffs are 8 for mutual peace, 6 for striking while the other cooperates, 1 for mutual strike, and 0 for being struck while cooperating; for the dove, peace is the best response to peace and strike is the best response to strike. Both civilizations know p but not the other's actual type, and they cannot communicate beyond the choice itself.

What are the Bayes-Nash equilibria as a function of p, and at what value of p does mutual peace stop being an equilibrium so that strike is the only rational outcome left for doves? Does the formal game reproduce the dark forest conclusion, or does that conclusion require an equilibrium-selection argument layered on top? In the informal version a chain-of-suspicion argument is supposed to force even otherwise peaceful civilizations to preempt; I want to know where that chain appears in the formal game — is it the mixed equilibrium, risk dominance, iterated reasoning about the other's reasoning, or something else? Could rational players still reach mutual peace when p is moderately high, and what extra assumptions would be needed to make the dark forest outcome the unique equilibrium?
