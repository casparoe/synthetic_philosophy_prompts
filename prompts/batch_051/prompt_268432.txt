I teach an upper-division philosophy of biology seminar, 16 students, one 75-minute session, and we're about to hit the question of whether evolutionary psychology is science or a pile of just-so stories. I want an activity where they run into the problem themselves before I name it. Design a small-group game, with rules, materials, and a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money. Give each group a trait (jealousy, a sweet tooth, fear of snakes, whatever) and have them invent an adaptionist story for it. Then have them try to turn that story into a risky prediction; maybe also have two groups with very different traits explain both and realize every story feels equally good. Award points for predictions that would look different if the trait were drift or a byproduct instead of an adaptation, and punish vague, everything-compatible stories. End with a debrief that maps what happened to the just-so story critique without using that phrase during the game. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — my guess is they'll confuse 'this makes sense' with 'this predicts something' — and say outright which part of the science-versus-stories debate the activity can't capture. No lecture up front.
