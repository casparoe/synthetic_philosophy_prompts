I spent thirty years in geochemistry and now that I'm retired I've gotten into philosophy of mind, mostly through arguments about whether large language models are doing something we should call reasoning. There's one debate that's been going for a couple of years now and seems completely stuck, and I'd like a diagnosis of the impasse itself, not a verdict on who's right.

The debate is over chain-of-thought faithfulness: when a model lays out intermediate steps before answering, is that transcript a report of its actual reasoning? One side says yes, or at least it can be. The intermediate tokens are generated before the final answer by the same machinery that generates the answer; if you edit or patch an intermediate step, later steps and the final answer often change; and treating the transcript as the reasoning isn't just a convenient fiction. The other side says the chain is usually a post-hoc rationalization. The model lands on an answer first, and than produces fluent steps that would make a human look reasonable; you can often swap the stated reasons for entirely different reasons and the final answer doesn't move; models will sometimes state a false intermediate premise and still reach the correct conclusion. Both sides have their experiments, and as far as I can tell they agree on a lot of the raw observations — there are plenty of unfaithful chains, and also cases where the intermediate steps look causally load-bearing. And still nobody moves.

My working hypothesis is that they're using "reasoning" in two different senses: one side means the verbal account tracks the underlying causal computation, and the other means the verbal account would justify the conclusion to a reasonable person. They also seem to have different rules for what counts as evidence, with one side satisfied by behavioral interventions and the other demanding a detailed mechanistic story. But maybe I'm overintellectualizing what is mostly a ordinary empirical dispute that's just hard to settle.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, exactly — verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, a hidden empirical question, or ill-posed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a specific kind of experiment, a conceptual distinction, a formal result, or should nobody expect convergence?
