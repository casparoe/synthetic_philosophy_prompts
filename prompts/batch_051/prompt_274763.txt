Since reporting a feature on proposals to reform the civil calendar, I have not been able to shake an argument that one of the historians of science I interviewed laid out for me. He is not a contrarian, and his reasoning was careful enough that I could not find the joint in it. It runs like this. (1) An epoch is a coordinate origin: a point from which years are counted, and no such point is more true than any other, any more than one zero on a number line is true and another is false. (2) If no epoch is more true than another, then no epoch is better or worse than another; the only difference between them is familiarity. (3) Yet an epoch is never inert notation. The common-era count anchors the civil year in one religious tradition's reckoning of a founder's birth; the French Republican calendar anchored it in a particular revolution; a Holocene, or "human era," calendar would re-anchor it near the start of the Holocene, treating the whole of human civilization as the relevant span; and one can imagine an epoch set at a tyrant's rise or at a massacre, which would turn the date on every calendar page into a quiet commemoration. (4) If an epoch is a standing claim about what part of the past is salient, then an epoch can be criticized as parochial, exclusionary, or ideologically freighted. (5) Therefore some epochs really are better or worse than others. (6) But that contradicts (2). From this he concluded that the notion of a neutral epoch is incoherent: either the choice of epoch is empty — so calendar reform is a waste of effort — or every proposal just dresses an interested ideology in the costume of a convention.

I find that conclusion too strong. I want to keep the idea that an epoch choice can be genuinely worse than another — that dating the civil calendar from a massacre is worse than dating it from a geological boundary, and in a way that is not merely a matter of taste or familiarity. But I cannot name the step where his argument slips. Which premise or inference should I reject, and what is the cost of rejecting it?
