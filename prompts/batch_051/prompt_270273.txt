Civil law says you can win on a preponderance of the evidence. Criminal law says the state must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. In the old blue bus hypothetical, a pedestrian is hit and the only evidence is that 4 out of 5 buses on that route belonged to the Blue Bus Company; that’s usually treated as enough in a civil suit. Now flip it: the only evidence against a spectator is that 4 out of 5 people in the arena had entered without paying. No criminal court would convict him on that, and we’d find it unjust if it did. Both cases use the same naked statistical evidence. Is there a real principle that explains why the same evidence can tip a civil judgment but not a criminal verdict, or is the difference just an intense but unsupported feeling about criminal punishment? I want the strongest justification you can construct, and then an honest verdict on whether it survives cases like a fine-only criminal offense, or a civil suit seeking to brand someone a fraud.
