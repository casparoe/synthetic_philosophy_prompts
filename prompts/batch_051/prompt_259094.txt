I'm teaching a unit on invented tradition and imagined communities — Hobsbawm and Anderson, the idea that much of what feels ancient is actually assembled quite recently and then given a fake past. I want to show The Village (the Shyamalan movie) alongside the readings and treat it as a thought experiment about exactly this. The elders literally build a whole community with invented rituals, a fabricated founding story, and a manufactured threat, and the younger characters mostly live inside it. Read the film against the theory: where does it track, where does it distort by making the invention a deliberate top-down conspiracy rather than a more difuse, half-accidental process, and is the film self-aware about that mismatch? Which scenes would you pull for class, and what does the film show that the theory tends to underplay?
