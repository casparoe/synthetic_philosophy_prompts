The original memetics argument was that culture evolves because little units—tunes, catchphrases, rituals—get copied, vary through imperfect copying, and then the variants sort themselves out by how good they are at getting copied. That was supposed to make “meme” more than a metaphor. But the things people actually call memes now are mostly image formats that get deliberately remixed: someone makes a template, everyone swaps in their own caption, and a feed algorithm then decides which variants get seen, largely by predicting engagement. The variation is purposeful, not blind copying error; there often isn’t a canonical original being copied; and the unit is wierd (is the meme the template, the format, or the individual post?). So: does the replication-and-selection argument survive this shift, or does it need repairing into something much weaker—like “stuff spreads through networks and ‘meme’ is just a nice word for it”? I mainly want to know what happens to the argument, not whether the word survives. And tell me what consideration would most likely change your verdict, or where the argument you give is shakiest.
