I'm on the community standards board at a small liberal-arts college, and we're stuck on a conduct case. A recognized atheist student group held an event they advertised as a sacred-text destruction: they spent an hour tearing up and burning printed pages from the Quran, the Book of Mormon, and the Bhagavad Gita. No classes were disrupted, no individual was personally harassed, and they cleaned up afterward. But many students and a couple of chaplains say the act itself was a moral injury: they describe feeling defiled, physically disgusted, and as though something they hold sacred had been shown contempt. Some board members want to sanction the group under our bias-and-harassment policy (an intentional act that made religious students feel unsafe), while others say there's no defensable secular ground for punishment, because disgust and wounded reverence aren't harms in the relevant sense. The underlying question the board keeps circling is whether a secular conduct system ought to protect sacredness as something more than bare offense, or whether feelings of defilement are too unreliable and illiberal to build rules around. I've been asked to draft the policy memo for the board. Could you write it? We need a clear recommendation (sanction or don't, and if sanction, roughly what sort), criteria for future cases that distinguish protected criticism from sanctionable desecration, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
