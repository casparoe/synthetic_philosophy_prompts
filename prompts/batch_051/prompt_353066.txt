Having spent my working life in the natural sciences and only lately turned to philosophy, I have been puzzling over the difference between standard game-theoretic rationality and superrationality in one-shot choices, and I would like a case constructed to order rather than merely an illustration of the idea.

Please construct a thought experiment of the following sort. Two parties find themselves in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma: each does better by defecting no matter what the other does, yet each would be better off if both cooperated than if both defected. The case must be one in which cooperation is the correct recommendation, but it is correct only for superrational reasons, not because of any of the usual escapes from the dilemma.

The specifications are these. The scenario must be realistic: a workplace, a research group, a hospital unit, or some similar ordinary institution; no demons, no perfect predictors, no telepathy, no enforceable contracts, and no mechanism that causally links the two choices. It must be conveyable in a single paragraph. The parties must not communicate, must not have a history that creates trust, must not face future interaction or reputational cost, must not care intrinsically about each other's welfare, and must not face an external enforcer. The cooperative conclusion must not be smuggled in by stipulating that the parties are clones, that they are guaranteed to reason alike, or that they are already committed to superrationality.

What the case may rely on is the feature that makes superrationality applicable: the two parties occupy structurally identical positions. Each faces the same payoffs and the same information, each knows this, each knows the other will apply the same mode of reasoning, and each can see that whatever reason the situation gives for choosing an action in one position is also a reason for choosing that action in the other position. From that, each can reason that the real choice on the table is between a world in which both cooperate and a world in which both defect, and that the first world is better for each of them. I want the case engineered so that this reasoning arises naturally from ordinary institutional facts rather than from philosophical stipulation.

After presenting the one-paragraph scenario, walk through the case as follows. Explain what a standard dominance analysis of a one-shot prisoner's dilemma prescribes and why it treats cooperation as irrational here. Explain what causal decision theory prescribes and why it agrees with the standard analysis. Then explain what superrationality prescribes and set out the superrational reasoning step by step in the idiom of the two parties. Finally, identify the single feature of the case that is doing the main work, say what happens if that feature is removed, and give the strongest objection that a defender of the standard game-theoretic verdict would press against the case.

Please begin with your bottom line: your first sentence should state in one sentence what the case you have built is designed to show. After that, give the scenario and then the walkthrough, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
