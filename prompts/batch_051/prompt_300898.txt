I'm supposed to write up my philosophical profile on self-knowledge and assertion for a seminar next week, and the more I look at it the less sure I am that the peices fit. Here's where I've landed:

(a) I'm a functionalist about belief: beliefs are dispositional states, fixed by how they connect perception, inference, emotion, and action, not by any special introspectable belief-feeling or mental image.
(b) I'm also convinced by something like Evans's transparency claim: when asked whether I believe p, the normal way to answer is to settle whether p is actually the case, not to look inward for a belief-shaped object.
(c) I'm drawn to the knowledge norm of assertion: you shouldn't assert p unless you know p, where knowing p includes believing p.
(d) I think Moorean sentences—'p, but I don't believe p'—are always defective to assert, even though their contents can be perfectly true.
(e) I think self-deception is possible: a person can have a belief in the functional sense while sincerely disavowing it, like a parent who insists their kid isn't using drugs but keeps searching the kids room.

I can't tell whether these hang together or whether I'm quietly helping myself to something. Could you go through them? I want to know which commitments are genuinely in tension, which ones only look that way, and for the genuine tensions how severe each is and what the least costly revision would be. Rank them rather than flattening them all to 'some tension.'
