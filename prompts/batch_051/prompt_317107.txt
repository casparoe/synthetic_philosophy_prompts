I'm in a metaethics course and we've just spent three weeks on the Frege-Geach problem, and I'm leaving more confused then I started — not about any particular move, but about what the problem actually is. Different papers we read seem to mean different things by 'solving' it. One treats it as a problem about how 'stealing is wrong' can appear inside a conditional ('if stealing is wrong, then getting your little brother to steal is wrong') without actually condemning stealing. Another treats it as a problem about why modus ponens with a moral major premise is valid. A third treats it as a problem about giving a compositional semantics for 'not', 'or', and 'if' over sentences that express attitudes. Before I start my term paper, I want the full spec: a list of desiderata that a theory of normative language has to meet to count as solving the Frege-Geach problem, not just one local version of it.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. Cover at least embedded contexts, negation (why 'stealing is wrong' and 'stealing is not wrong' look like contradictory claims rather than just two different non-cognitive stances), mixed sentences that combine moral and descriptive vocabulary, attitude and belief reports like 'Alice believes that stealing is wrong', and the inference problem with modus ponens. But don't stop at the cases: mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are really only preferences of particular camps (e.g., keeping a truth predicate, having a full compositional semantics, avoiding speaker subjectivism, preserving classical logic). The part I most want to see is whether any subset of these requirements is in tension — whether a theory that satisfies some of them is forced to give up others. People in class kept hinting at an impossibility result in the neighborhood, but nobody would state it plainly, and I would rather see the tension laid out as a property of the requirements themselves than have it blamed on one theory or another. Keep it under 600 words; a table for the requirements is fine, as long as the tension part is in prose.
