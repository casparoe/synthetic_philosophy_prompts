I'm in a high school philosophy club and last meeting we spent an hour on the surprise quiz paradox—teacher announces there will be a test next week, but the class won't know which day until it actually happens; students reason backwards from Friday and conclude it can't happen; and then the test happens on Wednesday and everyone's surprised. Half the club thinks the students' backwards induction has a hidden mistake, the other half thinks the announcement was just false from the start, and a couple people think the whole puzzle is really about what the word surprise means. But nobody could actually say what they believe about specific cases, they just kept repeating labels.

For next meeting I want to make a handout that's a sorting quiz / decision tree: you answer questions about weird variants of the paradox and it tells you which diagnosis you actually hold. I want it built from the logical space instead of from the names—first figure out the separate splits (does a student ever actually know the teacher's announcement is true? does surprise mean "can't be deduced before the exam" or just "doesn't actually expect it"? is the step that eliminates the last day okay? is the later induction okay?), then place the real positions as leaves. Please work these three cases into the diagnostic questions: the one-day version (teacher says quiz tomorrow, you won't know until it starts), a two-day version where the teacher is known to never lie, and a version where a student misses the last two days and so never does the reasoning. The leaves should include at least: the announcement is just false; the mistake is in eliminating the last day; the mistake is somewhere later in the induction; surprise is doing two different jobs; and the whole thing only works because real kids are sloppy reasoners. But feel free to add a leaf or kill one if the questions genuinely force it. Keep it to about 7 questions max for high schoolers. And where a combination of answers has no position, say that and guess why its empty—incoherent, nobody would defend it, or just unexplored. If two positions can't be seperated by any question, tell me that instead of faking a distinction.
