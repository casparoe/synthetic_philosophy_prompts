I'm a software engineer and I've never taken a philosophy class, so this might be a dumb question, but I cant get a parallel out of my head.

The philosophy side is Newton's bucket. As I understand it: you hang a bucket of water from a rope and spin it. At first the bucket spins and the water stays flat; then the water catches up and its surface goes concave; and at the end the water and bucket are at rest relative to each other while the surface is still curved. Newton took that to mean the concavity can't be explained by the water's rotation relative to the bucket or any other body, so it must be rotation relative to absolute space itself.

The software side: every engine I've worked on puts objects in one world space, with absolute position and orientation. You express most things relatively, parent/child transforms and constraints and so on, but somewhere there's a root frame, and it's doing real work, because angular velocity gets stored and integrated against it. So here's my parallel. An absolute-space engine is substantivalism: space is a container that exists even when nothing's in it. A purely relationist engine would have no world frame at all, where the scene is nothing over and above the relative transforms among objects, and two scenes that differ only by a global rotation are literally the same state. My claim is that that engine cannot simulate Newton's bucket: if I spin the bucket, there's nowhere to put the rotation of the whole system relative to anything, so the water either never curves or I have to cheat by reintroducing a hidden world frame.

Is this a real identity of structure, or am I just pattern-matching on the word relative? What would have to be true on each side for results to transfer, and what does the parallel predict that turns out false? In games we treat the origin's location as a pure convention, like a floating origin that recenters the world near the camera to avoid float precision loss, but we treat angular velocity as real; does substantivalism basically say the same thing, that where the origin sits is arbitrary but rotation isn't? And the relationist counter I vaguely half-remember says the concavity is really rotation relative to the distant stars, which would mean my relational engine fails only because I built a finite scene; if I put the whole universe's worth of mass in, would the concavity come back? I want to know if the analogy is deep or where exactly it breaks, and whether anything from game dev, like sensor fusion for gyroscopes, quaternion drift, or nested reference frames, actually transfers back to the philosophy.
