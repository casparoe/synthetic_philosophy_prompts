I'm trying to work out what I actually think about corruption and incorruptibility, going by my own verdicts on cases, and I want the principle behind them, if there is one.

A customs official who takes cash to clear an uninspected shipment: corrupt, the clearest case I have. The same official clears an uninspected shipment for a childhood friend, no money changing hands: corrupt too, and I keep wanting to say no less corrupt than the cash case. A judge who owns a few shares of the defendant company through an index fund and rules for the company because the merits point that way: not corrupt. A regulator who softens an enforcement action after the target firm offers her a job: corrupt; the same regulator takes the job but leaves enforcement unchanged until her last day: not corrupt, though it makes me queasy. A school principal refuses a bribe to admit an unqualified student, then admits a qualified student with no connections: exactly right. But the same principal, after refusing a bribe from one family, goes out of her way to be hard on their qualified child so no one can think she was bought: I don't want to call that corruption, but it feels like a failure of the same virtue, even though she wasn't for sale. A legislator accepts an expensive bottle of wine from a personal friend with no business before her and treats him no differently: not corrupt; the same legislator accepts the same bottle from a lobbyist with no business before her and treats him no differently: corrupt anyway. I keep going back and forth on this case: a police officer refuses a bribe from a gang, then months later lets a friend off a minor traffic ticket, believing he would not have done it for a stranger.

My working hypothesis is that corruption is being moved in one's official role by reasons the role is supposed to exclude, and that incorruptibility is the disposition not to be moved by them. That fits the cash payment and even the childhood friend, but it doesn't explain why the lobbyist bottle counts at all if it never changes how the legislator behaves. Is there a coherent principle that captures all of this, or nearly all of it, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? State the principle precisely, say which clauses are motivated by which case, apply it to the police officer case, and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short label for the principle you endorse.
