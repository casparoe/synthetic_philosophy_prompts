I'm a doctoral student in philosophy, and in a few weeks' time I've agreed to run a single fifty-minute section for second-year undergraduates on an economics-and-ethics module. Their exposure to moral philosophy amounts to a couple of lectures on consequentialism, and most of them have absorbed from their economics training the assumption that adding up welfare gains and losses across different people is simply what it is to assess a policy sensibly. I need to introduce them to the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism in that one session.

Don't give me the explanation itself; plan how I should teach it. I'd like you to specify the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what — for example, I suspect the students first need to see an intrapersonal trade-off as unproblematic before an interpersonal trade-off can strike them as different, but I want that dependency spelled out rather than assumed. Then identify the single example or case that should carry the session, and what it must have and what it must lack if it is to do the work rather than blur the point. I also want a map of the misconceptions this audience will bring or form. I'd guess several will treat the objection as the demandingness objection restated, some will reduce it to a vague appeal to rights, and others will conclude that it forbids any aggregation across persons whatsoever; I want to know at exactly which point in the sequence to head each of these off, and which, if any, should be allowed to persist for a while because they are doing useful work. What should I end with, and what single question or exercise at the end would show me they have understood the objection rather than merely learned its slogan? Finally, say what I should deliberately leave out at this level and how to flag that I am leaving it out. As you answer, please keep apart what is settled about the objection from what is genuinely contested among philosophers who work on it, and do not present a contested interpretation as if it were the consensus. Please write the answer in British English.
