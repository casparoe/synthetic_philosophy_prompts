A friend and I recently spent an evening arguing about Noether's theorem and what it actually explains. He held the strong view that once you have shown that a system's action is invariant under, say, time translation, the question of why energy is conserved has been fully answered, because the symmetry is the terminus of explanation. I resisted, but I could not articulate precisely what, if anything, a symmetry explanation might leave out, and we ended in a stalemate. Since then I have been trying to work out which positions in this vicinity are genuinely defensible.

Consider the following four positions about what Noether symmetries contribute to the explanation of conservation laws:

1. Symmetry as complete explanation: a Noether symmetry fully explains the corresponding conservation law, and any further request for explanation rests on a confusion or on metaphysical expectations that physics need not satisfy.
2. Symmetry as redescription: Noether's theorem is a mathematical result that lets one move between two true descriptions, a symmetry and a conservation law, but it explains neither; treating the mapping itself as explanatory is a category mistake.
3. Two aspects of one structure: symmetries and conservation laws are two aspects of a single underlying physical structure, and the theorem displays their equivalence; whether one calls the symmetry or the conservation law explanatory is a pragmatic or conventional choice, not a deep metaphysical fact.
4. Explanation relative to a framework: a symmetry can explain a conservation law only relative to the posited action or Lagrangian in which the symmetry holds, so unless the choice of that framework is itself explained, the conservation law is not fully explained.

Please sort these four positions into tiers according to how philosophically defensible you find them, from most defensible to least defensible, and give a one-sentence justification for each placement. End with a single line of the form \answer{...} that encodes your tiering compactly, for example \answer{tier1: 1,3; tier2: 4; tier3: 2}.
