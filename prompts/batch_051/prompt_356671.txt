A safety regulator is thinking about a certification rule for things like emergency brakes, fire suppression, and battery cutoff switches. The idea is that you can't just test whether the safety mechanism works under normal conditions; you also have to test it under finkish and masking conditions. A fink is something that removes the disposition before it can manifest—say, a fault that disables the brake exactly when the trip signal arrives, so the system isn't ready to engage at the moment it needs to. A mask is something that blocks the manifestation without removing the disposition—say, rust seizing the cable so the brake is still set to engage but can't physically move. Under the proposed rule, a product is certifiable only if the relevant disposition still manifests across a battery of finks and masks, and any fink or mask that blocks it is grounds for rejection. Walk through how this actually plays out: what it pushes manufacturers to do, how it gets gamed, whether the "any fink or mask" clause makes the standard either impossible or toothless, and whether the rule can be fixed without abandoning the basic idea.
