I find myself pulled in two directions about Goodman's new riddle of induction. Fix a future time t and define a predicate "grue" as follows: an object is grue just in case it is green if first observed before t, and blue otherwise. Every emerald observed so far has been green, and therefore every emerald observed so far has also been grue. Yet "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" diverge sharply after t, and nothing in the evidence seems to favor the first over the second. I am attracted to the solution that treats confirmation as relative to a language: "green" gets projected rather than "grue" because "green" is entrenched in our record of successful inductions, while "grue" is a gerrymander with no such history. On this view the riddle dissolves, because projectibility is not a mind-independent joint in nature but a status a predicate acquires through use. The theory is economical, generalizes to other artificial predicates, and preserves ordinary inductive practice without pretending to prove more than it can.

And yet I cannot shake the intuition that there is a further fact here. "Green" seems answerable to something in the world in a way "grue" is not, and a merely historical fact about which words my community happened to project cannot be what makes an induction reasonable. If some community had entrenched "grue" instead, I do not believe the world would have rewarded its inductions the way it rewards ours. So I want to know: is this intuition just a product of the very entrenchment the theory describes, something to be debunked rather than accommodated? Or is it tracking a real distinction the entrenchment account leaves out? If it is tracking something, can the account be amended to capture the distinction without quietly reintroducing the riddle it was meant to solve? Give me the strongest version of both positions and then a negotiated verdict: what would justify giving up the intuition rather than revising the theory, what sort of amendment would preserve the theory's virtues, and whether there is a reading of "projectible" on which both survive.
