Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate about the expanding circle. Camp A, the deliberative camp, holds that expansions of moral concern are caused primarily by the extension of impartial reasoning: a group comes to be included when principles already accepted are applied consistently and are seen to compel inclusion. Camp B, the contact camp, holds that expansion is caused primarily by changed affect toward the group — exposure, narrative, identification — with impartial reasoning arriving afterward to codify an inclusion that has already occurred.

The document should have four parts. First, a numbered list of no fewer than eight claims about the psychology and history of moral change that both camps accept without hedging; these should be substantive enough to constrain the debate rather than truisms. Second, the residual disagreement stated in neutral wording, with each camp's view given separately so that each side would accept its own formulation. Third, a table of four cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would force at least one camp to revise its position. At least one crux should be a temporal-order question (does principled argument characteristically precede or follow the inclusion of a group?), at least one should be a case of inclusion without prior affective contact, and at least one should be a case of affective contact that did not lead to inclusion. For each crux, give a concrete historical episode or psychological finding that both camps agree bears on it, and state each camp's advance prediction about what that case will show in falsifiable terms. Fourth, a closing note on what the collaboration changed: identify one disagreement that turned out to be merely verbal and should be retired, and one that survived and is genuinely substantive.

Do not cite sources; refer to positions, episodes, and findings descriptively, and write the statement so that both camps could put their names to it.
