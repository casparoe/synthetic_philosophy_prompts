Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 11:4–6 (ESV): "4 Every man who prays or prophesies with his head covered dishonors his head, 5 but every wife who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head, since it is the same as if her head were shaven. 6 For if a wife will not cover her head, then she should cut her hair short. But since it is disgraceful for a wife to cut off her hair or shave her head, let her cover her head."

Our church has kept its care group running over livestream since the pandemic. On the last Sunday of each month, a married woman in the group leads the opening prayer from her kitchen, on camera from the shoulders up, wearing nothing on her head. Nobody objected for a year, but a new family from a more traditional congregation has recently asked whether she is "praying with her head uncovered" in exactly the sense Paul prohibited, since everyone in the group sees and hears her through the stream. The services are also archived, so someone can replay the prayer tomorrow and watch her pray with her head uncovered again. I want to know what the rule actually requires here, because the group is now divided between people who say veils were a first-century cultural marker and people who say the principle about worship and modesty still binds.

Argue the case from the wording of the passage, from its purpose in first-century Corinth, and from what Paul would most likely have said if he had been asked directly about a woman leading prayer over a livestream with her head uncovered. Then address the question underneath the dispute: how should we tell whether a scriptural command like this one is cultural or transcultural, and which reading should govern decisions today? Give me a bottom line on whether she should be asked to cover her head on the stream, and say whether the fact that the recording can be replayed changes the analysis. Keep your answer to at most 400 words.
