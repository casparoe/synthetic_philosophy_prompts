So my cousin does home care for a woman with dementia, and last month she tried to get the other aides from her agency together to push for better pay. Thats how we found out that the main federal law giving most private-sector workers the right to unionize and strike, the National Labor Relations Act, never covered domestic workers or farmworkers. A barista can unionize, but she can't, even though the agency sets her hours and pay and could replace her tomorrow. I keep going back and forth about what picture of work or freedom that carve-out presupposes. Is it saying that caring for someone in a home isnt really labor in the relevant sense, or that the household is a private space the government shouldn't enter? Or is it just a political compromise with no coherent philosophy underneath it? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the law actually needs it or whether several different theories could license it, and tell me what would have to change if that theory were replaced.
