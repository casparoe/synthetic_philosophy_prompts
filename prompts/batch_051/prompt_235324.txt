I am a PhD student in experimental philosophy, and I have been puzzling over an asymmetry in ordinary judgments of intentional action that I want you to help me reconstruct and evaluate. Consider two cases, matched in every respect except the valence of one side effect.

Case A. An engineer is finalizing an update to a hospital triage system. The update will cut average waiting times by a fifth, and cutting wait times is the only thing she is aiming at. She is told that the update will, as a side effect, disable the screen-reader interface used by blind patients, making the system effectively unusable for them. She says that she does not care either way about this; the wait-time gain is what matters to her. She ships the update. Asked whether she intentionally made the system unusable for blind patients, most people answer yes, and they blame her.

Case B. A second engineer is finalizing the same update. She is told that the update will, as a side effect, activate a previously dormant screen-reader interface, making the system newly usable for blind patients. She says that she does not care either way about this; the wait-time gain is what matters to her. She ships the update. Asked whether she intentionally made the system usable for blind patients, most people answer no, and they do not praise her.

So the agent's stated attitude is identical, the causal structure is identical, and only the valence of the side effect differs; yet people are far more willing to say she brought the bad side effect about intentionally, and they blame rather than praise. My question is not merely why the asymmetry occurs. I want you to decide whether anything justifies it as a competent tracking of intentional action, rather than a bias in which bad outcomes distort mental-state attribution.

Give the strongest case you can for the asymmetry: identify the distinction it might be tracking — in the agent's reasons, in what she was prepared to accept, in the norms she flouted, or something else — and show why it would make Case A, but not Case B, count as intentional.
