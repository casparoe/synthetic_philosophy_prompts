I sit on my state's continuity-of-government working group, and we're stuck. For the governor's State of the State address, one cabinet secretary is chosen as the designated survivor and kept at a secure off-site location; right now the role is voluntary and people can decline. Two years running, the most senior eligible members have said no, and the designation fell to a junior agency head who'd had no briefing on the line of succession. The attorney general's office says we can make it compulsory: a random draw among all eligible cabinet members, no opting out except for documented medical emergencies. Half the group says that's the only way to make the burden fair and the succession dependable; the other half says you can't conscript someone into being a spare head of state, especially since they'd have to keep it secret from their own family. Draft the working group's decision memo: should we adopt compulsory random assignment, keep the volunteer system, or do something in between? Give a clear recommendation and engage fairly with the strongest reason on the losing side.
