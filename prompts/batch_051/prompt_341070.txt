Defend the claim that, in the Death in Damascus problem—where an accurate predictor's location is an evidential but non-causal correlate of your eventual choice, and you die iff you end up where the predictor is—introducing mixed strategies does not restore the existence of a ratifiable decision once the predictor can condition its location on your chosen randomization probabilities rather than only on the realized act. By a ratifiable decision, understand one that still maximizes expected utility after the agent conditions on having made it. Show that the self-undermining structure that makes each pure act worse than the alternative, once chosen, reappears one level up: for any mixture the agent settles on, that settlement is evidence about the predictor's location under which some alternative mixture would have greater expected utility. Conclude with a brief statement of what this indicates about randomization as a response to evidential act–state correlations.
