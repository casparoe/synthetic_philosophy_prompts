I have been turning over a thought experiment I once read — I believe it involved a machine called a careenium whose activity was driven by enormous numbers of tiny components called simmballs — and a question that was put, roughly, like this: can the higher-level patterns in a mechanical system ever push the underlying material around, or does the material do all the pushing while the patterns merely go along for the ride? I have recast the question in terms I find more natural: a deterministic simulation running a virtual machine.

Consider a closed, deterministic particle simulation. The substrate particles obey fixed local update rules, and nothing outside the particle layer ever intervenes. On top of this substrate we implement a virtual machine, and on that virtual machine we run a program that manipulates symbolic tokens — variables, strings, a call stack. Suppose, further, that the program is a simple agent that reads input tokens and writes output tokens. At the particle level, the next complete state of the substrate is fully determined by the current complete state of the substrate together with the particle-level update rules.

From this I arrive at the following argument, whose conclusion I find hard to accept:

(1) The next state of the substrate is fully determined by the current state of the substrate together with the substrate-level update rules.
(2) Therefore the substrate is causally closed: no fact above the particle level ever needs to be cited to account for the next particle-level state.
(3) The symbolic level — the program's variables, tokens, and control state — is not an additional ingredient on top of the substrate; it is entirely constituted by particular substrate configurations.
(4) If the substrate is causally closed, and the symbolic level is nothing over and above substrate configurations, then the symbolic level does no causal work. The symbols never push the substrate around; the substrate does all the pushing.
(5) Therefore the symbolic level is causally inert: it is a compressed description of what the particles are doing, not something that steers the agent.

Tell me whether this argument goes through. If it does not, identify exactly which step fails and explain the flaw as precisely as you can — in particular, whether the trouble is in one premise or in a suppressed assumption, and what distinction the argument is sliding over. If the argument is valid, say so, and then tell me what remains true of the symbolic level, because I would like to keep talking about programs and their states without feeling that I am describing something that does nothing.
