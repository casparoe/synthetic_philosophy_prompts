I've spent months following an argument between two people who work with the same hiring data and still cannot move. Overall, applicants who went through a screening program were more likely to be hired than those who did not. But within every job type, the screened applicants did no better, and the two sides agree on both sets of numbers. They even agree that the overall pattern comes from screened applicants clustering in job types that hire at higher rates. One insists the overall comparison is what matters, because the company hires from the whole pool and that is the outcome it would actually see. The other insists the within-type comparison is what matters, because for no individual applicant does the screening seem to improve the chance of being hired. The arithmetic is not in dispute, and the argument has stalled. I suspect the real disagreement is not about numbers or hidden variables but about what the question "does the program work?" is asking: a population-level outcome versus an effect on an individual. Is that diagnosis right? What kind of disagreement is this, and what would count as actual progress or a fair resolution?
