I am currently taking an upper-division philosophy of biology course on the debate between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function. I have been trying to assemble a consistent statement of my own view before I have to defend it in a paper, and I have ended up with a set of commitments that feel individually attractive but that I have not yet managed to examine as a whole. The position I have arrived at includes the following:

1. A trait's function is fixed by what it was selected for. A heart's function is to pump blood because pumping blood is the effect that explains why hearts persisted and spread, and a heart that is unable to pump because of a defect still has that function, which is why we call it defective.

2. Malfunction is an objective matter. A defective heart is not merely statistically unusual; it fails to do something that a heart is in fact supposed to do.

3. A newly arisen mutation can have a function before selection has had time to favor it. If an individual is born with a variant enzyme that happens to break down an otherwise harmful compound in a changed environment, then breaking down that compound is the function of the variant from the outset, even if the variant has not yet spread through the population.

4. Co-option works the same way. If a trait that originally evolved for one effect now helps organisms survive because of some new effect, the new effect can count as its function even before selection has reshaped the trait for that new role.

5. Attributing a function is explanatory because it tells us what keeps the relevant trait around in the population now. A purely historical story that had no bearing on current survival would not seem to be a story about function at all.

6. Biological functions and artifact functions are not two different subject matters. Saying that a heart's function is to pump blood and saying that a mousetrap's function is to catch mice are claims of the same fundamental kind.

Before I commit to this in the paper, I would like an audit of the whole set. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, and which pairs only look as though they conflict? For each real tension, please rank it by severity and say what the least costly revision would be and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the view. Finally, I would like to know which single revision would do the most to restore coherence, and at what price.
