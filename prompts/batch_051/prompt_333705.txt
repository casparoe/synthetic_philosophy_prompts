Before I read any more proposals about how to reason when the odds cannot be looked up, I want to know what I should be asking of them. Set out the desiderata for an adequate theory of decision-making under uncertainty. It must handle at least the contrast among a gamble with known chances, a gamble whose probabilities have to be estimated from a long track record, and a judgment so one-off that past frequencies are almost no guide. It must not blur the difference between assigning a middling probability because the evidence points both ways and assigning one because the evidence is too thin to support any number at all, nor the difference between weak evidence and strong evidence for the same probability. It must accommodate how two reasonable people can disagree without either having reasoned incorrectly, and how confidence can change purely because the evidence became more complete while the balance of evidence did not move. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are optional, and flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable. Keep the emphasis on the criteria rather than on scoring particular theories, but briefly say which single requirement gives the most trouble to the standard approach of reducing every uncertainty to a number and maximizing expected value.
