Ive been trying to pin down what “the will of the people” could actually mean without it just being a rhetorical placeholder. Heres a passage from Rousseau, The Social Contract, book 2 chapter 3: “It follows from what has gone before that the general will is always right and tends to the public advantage; but it does not follow that the deliberations of the people are always equally correct. Our will is always for our own good, but we do not always see what that is; the people is never corrupted, but it is often deceived, and on such occasions only does it seem to will what is bad.”

I think this does something interesting: it says the general will is always right, but the people can be deceived. So the real thing isnt just what people happen to say at any given moment. I want a working definition of “the will of the people” that uses that distinction and can actually classify hard cases.

So please define “the will of the people” in a way that handles these eight cases:

1. A referendum where 57% of voters approve a measure, but turnout is only 31% of eligible adults.
2. A referendum where 61% approve a measure after a campaign that later turns out to have been dominated by foreign-funded false stories about what the measure would do.
3. A law that has 65% support in opinion polls for ten straight years, but the legislature keeps voting against it.
4. A huge protest movement demanding a policy that most of the country has never voted on and, in polls, only a plurality supports.
5. A court throws out a popular law because it violates a constitutional right; polls show 70% of the public wanted the law to stand.
6. A small group has an intense, stable preference on an issue, while a large majority has a mild preference for the opposite, and the legislature gives the small group what it wants.
7. A free and fair election where a party wins a majority of seats after receiving 42% of the vote.
8. A mass uprising overthrows a regime that won the last election, and the new rulers claim they represent the people.

Run each through your definition and say which ones it classifies as genuinely expressing the people’s will, which not, and which it leaves indeterminate. Then go back to Rousseau: does his quote help you with the deception cases but not the turnout/intensity cases, or can it be stretched? Which of your verdicts is the least secure?
