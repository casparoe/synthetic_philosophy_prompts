An engineer on my block has begun testing a small autonomous delivery drone from his roof. The drone's flight software has a known failure mode: on any given flight, there is roughly a one-in-800 chance that the drone will crash onto a neighbor's property, and roughly a one-in-8,000 chance that debris will seriously injure someone. He has not asked permission from anyone in the possible crash radius. His stated justification is that these probabilities are smaller than the risks we routinely impose on others by driving through a residential area, and that if one had to seek consent for every tiny risk, ordinary social life would be impossible. No one has been hurt, and there is no evidence of negligence beyond accepting this known failure rate. I live next door. I feel that his decision wrongs us in a way that cannot be dismissed as mere risk-aversion, but I struggle to say why, since there is no actual harm and the chance of harm to me is minuscule. Is his reasoning sound? If it is not, give the best account of what makes his behavior objectionable, and say whether that account would also condemn ordinary driving. If it is sound, explain why my sense of being wronged may still be rationally or morally intelligible.
