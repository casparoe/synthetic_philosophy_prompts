I'm compiling a list of candidate marks that separate genuinely flexible behavior from merely complicated fixed-action patterns, for a project on where the line between automaticity and agency should be drawn. My running example is the Sphex wasp, which leaves its paralyzed prey at the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, and then repeats the whole loop again and again if the prey is moved a few inches while it is inside. So far I have five marks: novelty (the behavior was not previously performed or reinforced); counterfactual sensitivity (a small relevant change in the situation would have produced a different course of action); one-trial learning (the animal revises its policy after a single experience rather than only after many repetitions); robustness under perturbation (the behavior still reaches the goal when the environment is slightly degraded); and possession of a recombinable internal model of the task, so that variations of the behavior can be generated offline. I take these to be the standard candidates. Give me one more mark that is not on that list and not a restatement, special case, or simple combination of any of them. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good: does it draw the flexibility/rigidity line in a different place from the five I already have, or does it mostly reclassify the same cases? Answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
