I chair a small constitutional commission in a Westminster-style democracy that still has a hereditary monarch, and last week this stopped being abstract for me. The monarch privately told the prime minister they won't grant royal assent to the Public Emergencies Bill unless two clauses are removed: one lets the government suspend ordinary judicial review during any declared emergency, and the other lets it renew the emergency without another vote in parliament. The bill passed by a narrow majority. The PM says the monarch is purely cerimonial; the palace says this is exactly the kind of extreme case the reserve power to refuse assent exists for. Acting as our in-house constitutional theorist, think it through carefully and draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday. Recommend one concrete bottom line: the monarch should refuse assent, assent under a published protest, or assent silently. Make it a committed recommendation; give the strongest point on the losing side a fair run, but no hedging and no options menu. Begin with the memo itself—bottom line in the first sentence, no preamble, no citations.
