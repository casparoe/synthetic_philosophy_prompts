Since a junior colleague's careful account of repeated marginalization was dismissed last term as unprofessional, I have been assembling a taxonomy of epistemic injustices beyond Fricker's two canonical forms, testimonial and hermeneutical injustice. So far my list has five entries. Testimonial smothering: a speaker truncates, dilutes, or withdraws her testimony because she anticipates a prejudiced credibility deficit, so her knowledge never actually reaches an audience for uptake. Contributory injustice: a group's situated knowledge and the interpretive resources it has already developed are refused uptake by the wider community and thereby kept out of the shared pool of epistemic resources, leaving an avoidable collective gap. Epistemic exploitation: privileged interlocutors extract from marginalized knowers the labor of educating them about the knowers' own oppression, without reciprocity or compensation. Epistemic appropriation: concepts or frameworks developed by a marginalized group are taken up by more powerful agents, detached from their origins, credited to the takers, and put to use in ways that erode the originators' authority over them. Willful hermeneutical ignorance: an otherwise competent agent or group motivatedly refuses to acquire or use interpretive resources that are already available and would make marginalized experience intelligible, thereby preserving that ignorance as an advantage. I suspect this list is close to complete, which is why I am asking for one more: a form of epistemic injustice that is not on the list and is not a special case, variant, restatement, or combination of anything on it or of Fricker's two, but has a distinct mechanism — a distinct answer to what is withheld or extracted, by whom, and at which stage of epistemic practice the wrong occurs. Then say honestly whether it survives as a genuinely distinct form or collapses into one of the listed ones, and what it adds that the existing taxonomy misses.
