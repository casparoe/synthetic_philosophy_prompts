I’ve been re-reading the classic stuff on the paradox of hedonism lately — the idea that aiming directly at happiness tends to backfire, and that happiness is something you get mostly as a byproduct of caring about other things. The standard line is that this is structural: pleasure doesn’t like being chased. But I keep noticing that the standard line was fully formed before anyone could cheaply measure hedonic state. These days you can strap on a wearable that tracks sleep and heart-rate variability, do quick mood surveys on your phone, and run your life like a self-experiment: randomize a few evenings of reading vs. climbing vs. meeting friends, log the afterglow, and schedule more of whatever wins on the data. If someone actually did this for years and and got genuinely good at predicting which activities produce the most positive affect over the next day or week, would the paradox still apply to them in the same way? Or does the old argument secretly depend on our affective forecasting being bad and our introspection unreliable, so that once you replace guesswork with data, chasing happiness is just another optimization problem with a noisy objective?

But I can also see a version where the measurement makes it worse, or where the core premise isn’t about forecasting at all. If the mechanism is that moment-to-moment attention to your own hedonic state breaks flow and turns your activities into instruments, then the person logging mood after every activity might be making themselves unhappier *because* they’re optimizing. And there’s a Goodhart-y angle: if you’re steering by a mood score, you might drift toward things that spike the score without touching whatever the score was supposed to track — choices that feel satisfying to check off but don’t change the felt texture of a random Tuesday. So my actual question is: which premise of the old argument does continuous hedonic self-measurement actually bear on — the byproduct structure of happiness, the self-monitoring/flow problem, or the forecasting problem? Does the paradox survive this development, does it need a repaired version that says something narrower, or does it die and get replaced by a boring engineering problem (or a new Goodhart problem about the measure itself)?
