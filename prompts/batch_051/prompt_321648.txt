I'm in a grad student group that issues mock rulings on constitutional breakdowns and interregna. Our own past rulings: (1) When a coup dissolved the legislature and the constitution said the president serves only until a successor is sworn, we allowed the president to remain as a caretaker but not to make new law. (2) When a city charter said nothing about what happens after a recall removes the entire council, we said the recalled council had to keep administering basic services but couldn't set new policy. (3) When a constitution required a two-thirds vote to dissolve a deadlocked parliament, we refused to let the head of state dissolve it anyway: the text was clear and mere deadlock didn't override it. (4) When an earthquake made elections impossible within the constitution's 60-day deadline, we allowed the emergency government to stay beyond 60 days, but only for election administration and disaster relief. The new case: a caretaker government's 90-day constitutional limit expires, the text is silent on what happens after that, the assembly still can't form a government, and an epidemic has made holding elections logistically impossible. Should we let the caretaker government continue for another 60 days? If yes, under what distinction? Which of our past rulings is the closest analog, and what principle would we be committing to either way?
