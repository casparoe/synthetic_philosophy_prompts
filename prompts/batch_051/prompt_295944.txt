Write an essay assessing the claim that culture evolves by natural selection acting on memes, and in particular the further claim that describing memes as replicators amounts to more than a metaphor borrowed from genetics. Engage seriously with the objection that much cultural transmission lacks the copy-fidelity and clean boundaries that selection would need in order to operate in anything like the genetic way: when a joke, a recipe, a folk song, a belief, or a political slogan passes from person to person, what is reproduced is often a reconstruction or remix rather than a faithful copy, and the supposed unit has no obvious edges. Internet memes intensify the puzzle, since they propagate largely through mutation, captioning, remixing, and format shifts; one side of the debate takes this to show that replicator language is forced, while the other takes it as a straightforward illustration of variation and selective retention. Some theorists further argue that cultural change is better explained by the attractiveness, utility, or salience of the content itself, or by facts about whom people copy, than by the differential survival of discrete replicators. Take a position and defend it: is the meme concept best understood as a genuine explanatory framework, as a harmless shorthand for ordinary talk of ideas and fashions, or as a misleading analogy that imports a flawed picture of how culture changes? If you think it is genuine, say what conditions a cultural variant must meet to count as a replicator, and identify cases where the concept earns its keep and cases where it is overextended. If you think it misleads, say precisely what the analogy gets wrong and what alternative vocabulary does better. Reach a determinate conclusion rather than ending with a balance of considerations.
