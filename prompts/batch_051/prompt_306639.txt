"The void-for-vagueness doctrine says a criminal law is unconstitutional if an ordinary person reading the statute can't figure out exactly what's prohibited. So the Constitution basically demands near-mathematical precision from criminal codes: if a reasonable person could disagree about whether some edge case falls within the law, the law is void. That's why statutes using open-ended words like 'reasonable,' 'unusual,' or 'annoying' are sitting ducks—when the legislature uses a vague term, it's really delegating the power to define the crime to cops and judges, which the doctrine forbids. The heart of the doctrine is fair notice: the whole concern is that a person shouldn't be punished for conduct she couldn't have known was criminal from the text."
