I am a philosophy PhD student working on consequentialist moral theory, specifically on whether virtues and deontic constraints can be given a place within a broadly consequentialist outlook without becoming independent normative sources. As far as I can articulate them, the standard options all make virtue and duty matter through value: either as means to it, as robust rules for producing it, as objects assessed by it, or as constituents of it. My working list of the standard families is this:

1. Heuristic view. Virtues and duty-like rules are fallible guides for deliberation, justified because following them generally brings about the best consequences, while the criterion of rightness remains the act's own consequences.

2. Rule-internalization view. Duties are rules whose general acceptance, or whose internalization as firm dispositions, has the best expected consequences. Virtues are the stable dispositions to comply with such rules without case-by-case maximizing.

3. Character/motive view. The primary objects of consequentialist evaluation are motives or character traits rather than acts. An act is right when it is what the optimific motives or traits would characteristically lead an agent to do.

4. Global view. Actions, motives, rules, dispositions, and institutions are all assessed directly by their consequences, so virtues and duties are not second-best approximations but are themselves among the items that can be right or good in virtue of consequences.

5. Constitutive-value view. Some virtues and duty-involving relationships, such as trustworthiness, promise-keeping, and respect, are not merely instruments for producing good outcomes but are partly constitutive of valuable states of affairs. Their value is genuine, but it is located inside the good rather than independent of it.

I take these five to exhaust the standard space in which virtues and duties are treated as derivative rather than independent. I would like you to propose a sixth family of accounts that is not on this list. By 'new' I mean not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of these five; it should differ in the mechanism by which virtue or duty acquires its normative standing. It may be a neglected proposal from the literature or your own construction, but it should remain within a consequentialist frame in the broad sense that the good is prior to the right and all normative standing is ultimately anchored in value or its pursuit. Then tell me candidly whether this sixth family actually gives virtues and duties a place that is more than derivative or merely instrumental, or whether it collapses into one of the five.

Structure your response exactly as follows:

- 'The proposed sixth family' (150–250 words): name and state the view, its central claim, and one example.
- 'How it differs from the five listed' (100–150 words): explain precisely why it is not a rewording, special case, or combination of them.
- 'Assessment' (100–150 words): say whether the proposal earns virtues and duties derivative, instrumental, or independent standing, and identify its main weakness.

Do not give citations or a reading list.
