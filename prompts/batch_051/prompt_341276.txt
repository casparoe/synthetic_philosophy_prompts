I'm a PhD student in an ML lab that does scalable oversight work, and I keep having the same fight with a collegue about debate. His claim, as best I can steelman it, is that obfuscated arguments aren't the real weak point of the debate protocol: as long as the judge is a careful, truth-seeking human with enough time and a full transcript, the honest debater can just walk them through exactly where the other side buried the false step, and a patient judge will follow. I think he's overconfident. I've watched judges in our own pilots nod along to extremely confident nonsense and then pick the wrong transcript even after the flaw was pointed out, because the passage was just too tangled or technical for them to keep in their head all at once. So here's what I want from you: design the strongest concrete test of the claim that obfuscated-but-explainable arguments, rather than hidden information or judge laziness, are the binding constraint on AI-assisted debate. Spell out the experiment well enough that I could actually run it: what's the task, what counts as obfuscation, what do the judges see, and what result would count for and against the claim. Then be honest about what the result would and wouldn't settle — especially wether any of it transfers to the regime where the debater is genuinely smarter than the judge, which is the one I actually care about.
