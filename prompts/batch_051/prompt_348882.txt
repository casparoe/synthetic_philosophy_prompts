I've followed both the formal-epistemology literature and the rationalist-blog version of the peer-disagreement debate for a long time, and I've come to think the conciliationist and steadfastness camps are at least partly talking past each other. Draft the joint statement that a committed conciliationist and a committed steadfast theorist could both sign after a long, good-faith adversarial collaboration. Begin with the document itself, with no preface or commentary. Include a numbered section of claims both camps accept — about what peerhood requires, about which cases genuinely belong to the debate rather than to deference to expertise, testimony, or group deliberation, and about what higher-order evidence amounts to in a fixed, single-encounter disagreement. Then give a disagreements table whose entries state each side's view in wording that side would endorse and the other would recognize as a fair rendering of the opposition. Then list the cruxes — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side — with, for each crux, a concrete vignette or argument both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. I expect two candidate cruxes will be whether a body of evidence uniquely fixes one rational credence and whether you may legitimately use the disputed first-order reasoning to dismiss your peer; gloss these, and add any others. Finally, say explicitly which part of the dispute, if any, turns out to be verbal.
