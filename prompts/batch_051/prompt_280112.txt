In the course I'm taking on symmetry and explanation in physics, I keep noticing an asymmetry in what we demand an explanation for. When a marble is released in the center of a perfectly symmetric bowl and settles on the symmetry axis, no one asks for a further cause: the symmetry of the setup is treated as explanation enough. But if the marble came to rest off-center, we would at once look for whatever broke the symmetry—a tilt, a scratch, a nudge—and would not let the symmetric laws be the last word. Both outcomes are compatible with the same symmetric laws, so the practice amounts to treating symmetry as an explanatory default and asymmetry as a puzzle. Does anything justify this asymmetry? Give the strongest justification you can for treating symmetric outcomes as the default that needs no cause while treating asymmetric outcomes as requiring a symmetry-breaker. Then test that justification against a case where the setup is symmetric and several equally eligible asymmetric outcomes are open—say, a marble released along the central ridge between two identical basins, which ends up in the left basin—and we still look for a hidden bias or a chance event to explain why left rather than right. Is the asymmetry a defensible epistemic principle, or is it a habit we should abandon?
