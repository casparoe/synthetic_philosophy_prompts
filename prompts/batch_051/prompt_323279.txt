My family lives in one of those co-housing communities where every household signs a covenant when they move in. I've been staring at one clause ever since this whole thing started. It says:

"No member shall engage in any conduct, on or off the premises, that tends to bring the community into public disrepute."

Everyone is pretty sure this was written around 1992, and that it was meant for stuff like a member getting arrested and it ending up in the paper, or someone throwing a party so loud the town files noise complaints, or a member making a total fool of themselves while visibly representing the community at a town council meeting. Nobody was thinking about the internet.

Here's what happened. One of the members, who's in her mid-20s, has a decently popular X account under a pseudonym where she posts really spicy political takes. She never mentions the community, and most of her followers don't even know what state she's in. One of her posts blew up, and somehow somebody in town recognized details of how she writes and personal stuff she'd mentioned, figured out who she was, and posted it. The local news ran a story like "Resident of our co-housing community posts [whatever the controversial thing was]," and now the county has paused a grant decision the community had been waiting on for months because they don't want to look connected to it. The board is now trying to expel her under the disrepute clause.

She says the clause can't reach her because (a) posting isn't "conduct," (b) she never identified herself with the community, and (c) the clause was never meant to police a member's speech. Honestly I care less about whether she's being treated fairly (she probaly isn't) and more about what the different ways of reading the clause would actually say. What does the plain text say? What does a purpose reading say? What would the people who wrote it in 1992 have said if you showed them X and asked? And how should a reasonable person reading the clause think it applies, especially since the link to the community was made by someone doxxing her, not by anything she said?

My government teacher says this is just the communitarianism-versus-liberalism debate: a communitarian would say the community partly makes you who you are, so it can legitimately hold you responsible for your public life even off the property, while a liberal would say the community is only a limited contract for shared upkeep and has no claim over what you say. Is he right? And if the two views give different answers, which one should actually control an expulsion from a community she freely signed up for?
