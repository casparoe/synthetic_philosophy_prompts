so i've had this thing knocking around in my head for a while and i want someone to tell me if its a real idea or just me re-inventing something obvious. i dont have any background in biology, i just read pop science and get annoyed. a while ago i went down a rabbit hole about why humans are the only animal with a chin, and every article gave a different story: its for chewing stress, no its sexual selection, no its to do with talking. everyone seemed very confident, and nobody seemed to notice that if four stories all fit the same bone equally well, that should probably make us less confident in all four, not more.

so i came up with what i call the Loan Shark Rule. the idea is: a trait only counts as an adaptation if you can say, before you get attached to a story, what the world would look like if the story were false. if you cant, then the story is just borrowing against the trait you already see, and natural selection (as its told in pop science anyway) is a loan shark demanding interest on a loan the organism never took out. i think this one rule kills most adaptive just-so stories while leaving real ones standing: for sickle cell or the peppered moth you can say what the failure looks like (who lives, who dies, who gets eaten), so the story has teeth.

what i want is an honest verdict. is this already a named position, and if not, is it any good? give me the three strongest objections and tell me which are fatal and which are just uncomfortable. and if ive basically confused evolution itself with the way we narrate it (the loan shark being in our heads rather than in nature), say so plainly instead of politely grading my rule. dont save my feelings and dont answer with a reading list; if the rule is broken, tell me its broken.
