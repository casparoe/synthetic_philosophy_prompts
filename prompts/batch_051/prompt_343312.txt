I have been thinking about how societies manage risk, and I have worked out a view I call the displacement view of risk. The triggering observation is that many celebrated safety victories turn out, on closer inspection, to be partly redistributions rather than eliminations. Seat belt laws did make occupants safer, but some of that gain appears to have been spent as worse or more aggressive driving, with the extra hazard landing on pedestrians and cyclists rather than on the protected occupants. Reinforced cockpit doors made hijackings harder, and attention then shifted toward attacks outside the hardened perimeter. Limits on one kind of financial leverage pushed risk-taking into other, less scrutinized parts of the system. The pattern I think I see is this: in a system made of adaptive agents, a successfully suppressed risk does not vanish; it migrates to wherever it is least watched, and often to whoever did not receive the benefit of the original measure. So I hold a strong version of the point: at the level of a whole society, aggregate risk is roughly conserved in the long run, like a grim kind of energy. Safety regulation, on this view, is never really about reducing total risk; it is about choosing which risks and whose risks a society will tolerate, while we pretend otherwise by measuring only the risk the regulation names.

I want this assessed honestly rather than confirmed. I know weaker, local versions already exist under labels like risk compensation, moral hazard, or offsetting behavior, and my claim is specifically that the matured form of these effects is the conservation of aggregate risk. Please tell me whether the strong conservation statement is even falsifiable, and whether it is true. Trace precisely where I stop being a standard risk-compensation account and start making a distinct, stronger claim. Then give me your three strongest objections, and say whether any of them is fatal rather than merely uncomfortable. Finally, offer one safety measure that in your view genuinely reduced total risk rather than merely moving it, and identify the feature that let it escape the conservation principle. Lead with a one-sentence verdict on the strong view, and keep the entire response under 500 words.
