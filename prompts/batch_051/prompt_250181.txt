I am a high school student, and I spend a large share of my free time building small video games. I am fluent in the vocabulary of game engines: entities and components, the update loop, game state, sprites and rendering, the camera, hitboxes, UI overlays, and the distinction between the simulation itself and what is drawn on screen. I recently encountered a position called illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. As I understand it, illusionism holds that experiences do not actually have the subjective, felt qualities they seem to have — the redness of red, the sting of pain — even though it seems to us, in a way we cannot shake, that they do. The view is not that experiences are unreal in every sense, but that their qualitative feel is an introspective misrepresentation; the mind produces something like a private display that misleads us about its own nature.

I would like you to restate illusionism in the vocabulary of game engines, as faithfully as you can. You might treat my visual experience as a rendered frame, the apparent private quality of experience as a HUD overlay drawn for the player, and the illusionist claim as the claim that there is no second, private screen inside the machine: the rendering is all there is, and the appearance of a separate inner display is a systematic misreading of the game state. But use whatever mappings are most honest rather than merely convenient.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of illusionism translate cleanly into this vocabulary, which parts can be forced in only with distortion, and which parts seem to have no counterpart at all? I especially want to know whether the residue reveals a real gap in the game-engine way of thinking — something the vocabulary cannot capture — or whether it reveals that the illusionist position quietly relies on ideas it has not earned. In particular, illusionists say there is an illusion, but they deny there is a separate self sitting in a theater being fooled; in game terms, I would ask: if the HUD misrepresents the game state, who or what is the HUD misrepresenting it to, and does my vocabulary force me to invent a player entity where the theory says there is none?
