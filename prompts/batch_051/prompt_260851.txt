I'm stuck on a passage from Hegel's *Phenomenology of Mind* (the old translation on the Marxists Internet Archive), section 184:

> Each is the mediating term to the other, through which each mediates and unites itself with itself; and each is to itself and to the other an immediate self-existing reality, which, at the same time, exists thus for itself only through this mediation. They recognize themselves as mutually recognizing one another.

My question is about the last sentence — "They recognize themselves as mutually recognizing one another." I can't tell whether Hegel means each consciousness must explicitly represent the other as recognizing it, or just that the two stand in a relation that we, the philosophical observers, can see is mutual. Also, if each only exists for itself through the other's mediation, does that require the recognition to be symmetrical and simultaneous, or can it be staggered and still count? Explain the mechanics in plain English, with attention to the differnce between being recognized and recognizing. Keep it under 250 words.
