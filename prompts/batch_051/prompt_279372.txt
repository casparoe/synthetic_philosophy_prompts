My reading group has spent the last term on whether video-game characters matter morally, and the two of us who actually disagree — one is a sentientist, the other a functionalist about moral status — have agreed to write an adversarial-collaboration document rather than carry on shouting past one another. The sentientist says no current NPC is a moral patient, because what makes something a moral patient is a capacity for conscious experience, especially suffering, and game characters do not have it; harming them can matter only indirectly, through its effects on players or on the habits it builds. The functionalist says at least some current NPCs matter directly, because moral status should track the functional profile — memory, responsiveness, goal-directedness, persistence, the role the character plays in our lives — and a sufficiently rich companion character already has enough of that even if we cannot check what is going on inside. Write the document they should have produced: a numbered list of claims both can sign, then the residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own side, then the cruxes. For each crux, give a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, and say what each side predicts. If any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Finish with a table whose columns are: crux, what would settle it, sentientist prediction, functionalist prediction. Do not let the agreed-claims list become so thin that its vacuous, but also do not let either side sign something that is really the other sides view in neutral clothes. For at least one crux, use the case of a companion NPC that is functionally almost exactly like a cat — it retreats from damage, seeks comfort, has preferences built into its reward function — but whose studio insists it has no inner states at all.
