I'm a graduate student in a metaphysics of mind seminar, and I've been asked to circulate a one-page statement of my position on functionalism and multiple realizability before next week's meeting, where the group will try to find holes in it. Before I send it around, I want an audit of whether the view actually hangs together; I have a vague sense that something is strained, but I can't locate the fault line, and I'd rather find it myself than have the seminar find it for me. Here is the position as I would state it:

1. Mental states are functional kinds: what makes two states both pains is that they share the pain-role, the characteristic causes and effects, within the creature's overall cognitive economy.
2. Mental state types are multiply realizable: pain can be realized by materially very different physical structures in different creatures (a human, an octopus, a silicon-based alien), so no mental type should be identified with any particular neural or biochemical state type.
3. I am a non-reductive physicalist: everything concrete is physical, but mental properties are real, higher-level properties that are not identical with, or reducible to, lower-level physical properties; substance dualism and eliminativism are both false.
4. I accept a broadly Kim-style causal efficacy requirement: a property or kind earns its place in explanation only if it is causally efficacious, and if being in pain as a higher-level property turned out to be epiphenomenal, that would count heavily against the view.
5. I hold that functionalism is best made precise as realizer functionalism: within a given individual, a particular mental state is the very physical state that occupies the relevant role, while across individuals we group such states by sameness of role.
6. Psychology's belief–desire explanations of action are autonomous and not merely shorthand for neural or physical explanations; psychological kinds do real explanatory work.
7. Case verdict: if my neurons were gradually replaced over several months by functionally equivalent silicon units while my behavior, reports, and memory remained continuous, I would continue to have the very same beliefs and pains throughout. The change of realizer would not change the mental state, and those mental states would be causally responsible for what I say and do.

Please take the set as a candidate package and go through it systematically rather than telling me which label in the literature I really hold. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest revision is, including what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set; then tell me which single revision would do the most cleanup. As you go, keep separate what is settled or definitional in these debates from what is genuinely contested among people who work on them, and do not present a contested position—for instance, about whether the causal exclusion problem really has the force it is often taken to have, or about how empirically well-supported claims of multiple realizability are—as if it were the consensus. After the prose audit, include a structured summary as a table with these columns: Pair of commitments; Genuine or merely apparent; Severity (1–5, 5 being most severe); What has to give; Cheapest revision and its cost elsewhere in the set.
