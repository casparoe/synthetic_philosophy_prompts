ok so in AP Bio I have to do a presentation on why memes arent just a metaphor, and I already know the question part is going to go badly unless I prep. The title of my talk is basically "memes are replicators, not just a vibe" and by memes I mean the older Dawkins sense: any bit of culture that gets copied around, like a song hook, a slang word, a handshake, a recipe tweak, a challenge video, or the idea of a meme itself. The audience is my teacher Ms. Okafor (very much a "Dawkins was being poetic, no one really thinks culture is made of particles" person), Ben (he read the book and is going to ask where one meme stops and the next one starts), Priya (she keeps saying natural selection needs genes, DNA, inheritance, the whole package, and culture doesn't have any of that), plus the rest of the class, who mostly know internet memes and are going to want me to explain why some meme formats die while others stick around.

My argument, compressed: a replicator is whatever bit of information (1) gets copied, (2) varies between copies, and (3) has its chance of getting copied again depend on that variation. I say culture fits because earworms, slang, and handshakes all copy, vary, and get selected. Imperfect copying doesn't ruin it; early life used sloppy replicators too, and the math just changes when copying is noisy. And the fact that human minds filter what we retell doesn't stop selection, it just means selection runs through our psychology; that's the "environment" the meme has to survive in.
