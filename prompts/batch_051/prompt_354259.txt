I sit on my city's charter revision commission, which is charged with fixing, for the next ten years, who is eligible to vote in municipal elections: mayor, city council, and ballot measures. Three expansions of the current electorate (residents who are U.S. citizens 18 or older) are before us. (1) Non-citizen permanent residents: they live, work, and pay city taxes, but cannot vote on the laws that govern them. (2) 16- and 17-year-olds: many work and pay sales tax, and they will live longest with the city's decisions on housing, schools, and climate. (3) Non-resident owners of city property: they pay property tax and are directly affected by tax and land-use measures, but do not reside here. The commission is divided. One side says the current electorate should simply vote on the matter. Another side says that is circular: the current electorate is exactly the body whose boundaries are in question, so letting it decide would let the already-counted determine who gets to be counted. No principled criterion—affected interests, subjection to the laws, residency, tax contribution, political maturity—commands a majority. Please draft a short written opinion for me to circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should be a document the commission can act on: recommend one concrete package for the three expansions, give the reasoning behind the recommendation, and genuinely address the strongest argument on the losing side rather than dismissing it. Begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary, and put your recommendation in the first sentence.
