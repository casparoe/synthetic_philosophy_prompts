Loyalty is one of those terms people wield with great confidence and define only under pressure. I want a working definition of loyalty — not a dictionary paraphrase or an etymology, but a criterion precise enough that someone could look at a novel case and say whether loyalty is present, absent, or doing something equivocal. My suspicion is that the word runs together two jobs: a descriptive one (standing by a person, group, or cause through some cost, temptation, or competing claim) and an evaluative one (a term of praise for partiality we approve of, set against the partiality we deprecate as bias or cronyism). I would like your definition to say what, if anything, unites the clear cases, and to take an explicit stand on whether the evaluative component belongs in the definition or is merely an accompaniment.

Please run the definition through these cases and tell me where it strains. (1) A soldier who remains at her post under fire. (2) A friend who keeps a damaging secret that was confided to him. (3) A subordinate who flatters a superior solely to advance his career. (4) A manager who hires a qualified sibling over a slightly more qualified stranger. (5) An employee who writes a glowing reference for an incompetent friend. (6) A whistleblower who leaks evidence of fraud inside her own firm. (7) A parent who lies to the police to shield her adult child from prosecution for a serious crime. (8) A voter who supports her party's candidate while privately believing the opponent would govern better. I am confident that (1) and (2) are loyalty and that (3) is not; I have no settled views about the remaining five, and I am especially unsure whether (6) and (7) are loyalty or something opposed to it.

Structure the response in five named sections. "Proposed definition" (one paragraph): state the criterion and briefly motivate it. "Clear cases" (one short paragraph each for (1)–(3)): apply the definition and confirm it yields the expected verdicts. "Borderline cases" (the longest section; one paragraph each for (4)–(8)): apply the definition, flagging any case where it returns no verdict or a verdict you find hard to accept. "Two jobs" (one paragraph): say whether the descriptive and evaluative aspects of loyalty come apart on these cases, and if so which cases each one classifies differently; this is where I want your view on partiality as a virtue versus a bias. "Revisions and least secure verdict" (one paragraph): revise the definition in light of the borderline results, or defend the surprising verdicts, and name the single classification you are least confident about.
