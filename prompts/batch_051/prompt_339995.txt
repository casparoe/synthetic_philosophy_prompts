For an advanced seminar in formal epistemology, I have been putting together a list of the main difficulties that stand in the way of the claim that the maximum entropy principle yields uniquely rational priors. The difficulties on my list so far are the following.

(1) Coordinate and partition dependence. The distribution that maximises Shannon entropy is not invariant under reparameterisation, and it also depends on how the space of possibilities is partitioned into exclusive alternatives. A density that is uniform, and so maximally noncommittal, with respect to one parameter or one partition becomes non-uniform under a nonlinear transformation of that parameter or under a different carving-up of the same possibilities. The principle therefore yields different priors for the same problem depending on how the problem is described.

(2) Underdetermination of the entropy measure. The principle is standardly formulated in terms of Shannon entropy, but the framework itself does little to justify Shannon's measure over rival information measures that satisfy closely similar axioms. Different measures reach their maxima at different distributions, so the choice among them quietly settles part of what the principle was meant to settle.

(3) The Judy Benjamin problem. When the new evidence is a conditional of the form 'if F, then the probability of G is r', updating by minimising relative entropy leaves the probability of the antecedent F at its prior value. Many have found this an implausible response to conditional information, which seems as though it ought to be capable of shifting the probability of F. The difficulty here concerns the updating rule rather than the selection of the initial prior.

(4) Improperness. On an unbounded parameter space, the distribution of maximal entropy relative to standard constraints may fail to exist as a probability function at all — the uniform density over the whole real line being the stock example — in which case the principle delivers no literal prior.

What I would like is one further difficulty, genuinely different in structure from all four — not a restatement, special case, or combination of anything on the list. In particular, I am not looking for another instance of coordinate or partition dependence, another problem about alternative entropy measures, another case concerning conditional updating, or another example of impropriety. Try to provide a difficulty that would remain live even for someone who had somehow answered all four, and then give me your honest assessment of whether it is any good — that is, whether it substantially damages the objective Bayesian project or merely shows that the principle needs to be supplemented.
