I help maintain moderation tooling for a medium-sized open-source community, and I keep running into a governance question I cannot get a clean answer to. Our community compact contains the following clause: "No member shall be suspended or removed from the community except after notice of the conduct alleged against them and a fair opportunity to be heard in their own defense before the moderators reach a decision." The compact was drafted in 2017, when moderation meant human moderators reading reports and typing replies. We now route most abuse reports through a classifier that flags likely harassment, and a pipeline immediately suspends the account, sends the member a notice listing the flagged messages, and opens an appeal in a web form. Unless the member explicitly requests escalation, the appeal is reviewed by a second model over the archived messages, and no human reads the member's response.

By the numbers, the new system outperforms the old human queue: faster removal of genuinely abusive accounts, fewer missed reports, more consistent enforcement, fewer borderline false positives. But I am not sure it satisfies the clause. On a plain reading of the words, does an automated notice plus a form that a model reviews count as a fair opportunity to be heard? Read by purpose, the clause seems aimed at preventing mistaken expulsions, checking moderator power, and respecting members as people who are owed a hearing before being thrown out; the pipeline may serve the first two, but it is not obvious that it serves the third. And if the clause grants something stronger than a best-effort procedure—the kind of thing people mean when they call a right a trump or a side-constraint rather than an instrument for producing good outcomes—then the improved metrics should not settle the question on their own.

I would like a precise account of how the textual, purposive, original-intent, and reasonable-reader approaches each come down on this case, which one should control and why, and whether reading the clause as a side-constraint rather than an instrument of moderation quality changes the result. What would the people who wrote this clause in 2017 have said if we had asked them about a machine moderator they could not have foreseen, and how much should that answer matter for a community whose document now has to govern systems its authors never imagined?
