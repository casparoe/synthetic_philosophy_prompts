Two friends in my discussion group have spent months arguing about what makes pain bad, and I would like to give them a document they could both sign, with their remaining differences stated precisely. One says the badness is in the sensation itself: if a pain hurts, that hurt is bad for the person who has it, and learning that the person does not mind it does not dissolve the badness. The other says the badness is a further response—the aversive reaction, the wanting it to stop, the way pain monopolises attention—and that this can come apart from the raw sensation. Her favourite piece of evidence is pain asymbolia, where people appear to report a sharp, burning, or stabbing sensation in a particular place but say it does not bother them, they do not find it unpleasant, and they do not flinch or withdraw. She thinks this shows the badness is not in the sensation itself. The first friend replies that either such people are not having pain in the full sense, or they are in a bad state even if they are indifferent to it.

Please draft the adversarial-collaboration statement these two should have produced. It should include: (a) a numbered list of claims both can sign, phrased so neither side could object; (b) a table of the remaining disagreements, with each side's preferred wording accepted by that side; (c) a short list of cruxes, understood as sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, at least one concrete case or observation both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The statement should also separate substantive disagreement from merely verbal disagreement where possible. Please write the entire document in British English.
