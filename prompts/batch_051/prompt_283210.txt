Here is an argument I keep almost believing, and it worries me. (1) In the standard economic view, a person's utility function just is any function that assigns higher numbers to outcomes they prefer; it records their preferences, nothing more. (2) Such a function is defined only up to an arbitrary positive rescaling and shift: if u represents someone's preferences, so do 3u and u + 100. (3) Therefore the only genuine facts about a person's utility are those that stay the same under every such rescaling. (4) But the claim "Ann in outcome A has more utility than Bob in outcome B" does not stay the same when Ann's and Bob's scales are rescaled independently of one another. (5) So comparing utility across people is not just hard but meaningless — no one is, as a matter of fact, better or worse off than anyone else, and any policy resting on such a comparison rests on a pseudo-fact. I suspect the word "utility" is being slid from one sense to another, but I can't locate the exact step where that happens. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and what would a corrected version of that step say?
