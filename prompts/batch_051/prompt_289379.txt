ok so I've fallen into a geometry hole recently — I got curious why squaring the circle is impossible, and now I'm trying to write a small program that animates old geometric constructions. It's made me realize I don't actually know what I count as a legitimate curve, vs a cheat. I've been using three criteria:

1. the curve is given by a finite, unambiguous definition;
2. there's some procedure that actually produces each point of the curve at a finite step or moment, rather than just approximating it arbitrarily well;
3. the procedure doesn't smuggle in the very thing (or number) it's supposed to construct.

Now here's the borderline case I can't settle, and I want you to go through my criteria honestly. It's the quadratrix — the old curve people used to square the circle and trisect angles, before it got pushed out of "proper" geometry.

Construction, in my own words: take a unit square. A ray from the bottom-left corner rotates uniformly, starting by pointing up the left side at t=0 and ending by pointing along the bottom side at t=1. Simultaneously, a horizontal line descends uniformly, starting at the top side at t=0 and ending at the bottom side at t=1. At each time t in between, mark the intersection of the ray and the horizontal line. The set of all those marked points is the quadratrix.

Here's the rub. As t approaches 1, the intersection approaches a specific point on the bottom side, a distance 2/π from the corner (for a side-1 square). But at exactly t=1, the ray and the horizontal line coincide with the bottom side, so there's no unique intersection to mark. The endpoint is never actually produced by the construction — its filled in by taking a limit, and that limit has π baked into it. The curve is useless for squaring the circle unless you accept that limiting endpoint, but accepting it seems to assume exactly the arc-length-to-line ratio you're trying to construct.

So: does the quadratrix qualify under my three criteria? I think (1) passes, (3) might fail on the limit step, and I'm stuck on (2): every interior point is produced in finite time, but the endpoint isn't, so the verdict depends on whether a limit point counts as "produced." Is that the right way to frame it? And more importantly, does this case show that one of my three criteria is badly stated, or is the old suspicion that the curve is a bit of a cheat actually the right answer?
