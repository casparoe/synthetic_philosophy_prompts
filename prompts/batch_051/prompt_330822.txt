I spend a lot of time on alignment forums and keep noticing that discussions of instrumental convergence tend to quote essentially the same list of convergent instrumental goals. I'm compiling material for a piece, and I'd like to push past the standard catalogue. Here is what I already have, stated in my own terms:

1. Self-preservation: an agent with almost any final goal has reason to avoid being destroyed or disabled, because a dead agent cannot pursue its goal.
2. Goal-content integrity: an agent has reason to resist having its final goals rewritten or corrupted, because its future self would then act on altered goals rather than the ones it currently holds.
3. Cognitive enhancement: an agent has reason to improve its own ability to reason and plan, since almost any goal is pursued more successfully by a smarter pursuer.
4. Technological perfection: an agent has reason to improve its tools, hardware, and physical infrastructure, for the same reason.
5. Resource acquisition: an agent has reason to gather matter, energy, money, compute, or other fungible means, since these widen the set of actions it can take in service of whatever it wants.

I'm told this list is essentially the standard one, and I can see why: any candidate I think of collapses into one of these five, or turns out to be a mere strategy for securing one of them, or an application of one rather than a source of further goals. I want one more convergent instrumental goal that is genuinely distinct: not a special case, not a rewording, and not a conjunction or downstream consequence of the five above — and I'd like you to say honestly whether it is any good by that standard. If, on reflection, you think no such sixth goal exists and the standard list is complete, say that and defend the closure claim rather than generating something weak out of politeness.

A few constraints on the answer. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — either name the sixth goal or state that the list is closed — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in British English. And if the request itself rests on a confusion — for example, if 'convergent instrumental goal' is not a well-defined enough kind for the question to have a determinate answer — then say so plainly instead of playing along.
