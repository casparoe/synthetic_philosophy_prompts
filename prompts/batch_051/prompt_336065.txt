In my philosophy of mind course we have just finished a unit on the language of thought hypothesis and on the question whether the formal systems we build, programming languages among them, are candidates for the mind's own representational system, or merely public notations that reflect it. Could you write a villanelle, in the strict nineteen-line form, on whether programming is a language of thought? The two refrains should carry the philosophical work rather than just repeating a phrase. One refrain should keep returning to the genuine parallel: on a computational picture of mind, both a running program and a chain of thought seem to traffic in structured symbols with combinatorial syntax and semantics, so writing code presents itself as making a thought explicit. The other refrain should keep returning to the gap: on the standard language of thought view, the mind's inner code is unlearned and prior to any external notation, whereas a programming language is deliberately designed and can only be written by someone already capable of the very thoughts it is supposed to express. The villanelle's cycle should let the two claims reassert themselves the way they do in the literature, and the final quatrain should say exactly what survives the analogy between code and mentalese and where it loses its grip, so that someone in philosophy of mind would recognize the poem as getting the dialectic right rather than gesturing at it. After the poem, please add a short author's note in continuous prose, in the manner of an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, separating what is settled from what remains genuinely contested: it is uncontroversial that programming languages are formal systems with defined syntax and semantics and that computational theories of mind posit structured mental representations, but it is contested whether an internal language-like code actually exists, whether it is learned or innate, and whether a practiced programmer can be said to think in a public programming language. Please do not present the contested points as consensus.
