In my AP Euro class we covered the Enlightenment and the textbook gave us a summary of Kant's Perpetual Peace. The summary says Kant thought the only way to end war is for every country to become a democracy, since citizens who actually have to fight the wars won't vote for them. His checklist is: abolish standing armies, don't borrow money to fund wars, no secret treaties, no country may interfere in another country's government to 'improve' it, and don't do things in war (assassinations, encouraging treason) that make future trust impossible. The big recommendation, according to the textbook, is that countries should combine into a world government: an international parliament with its own army or police force that can force states to obey international law. Once that exists, war becomes impossible, and Kant supposedly thought nature or providence is pushing history in that direction anyway.

The part that bugs me is the democracy thing, because I thought Kant was more about republics than democracies, and I have no clue whether he treated those as the same. The world-government-with-its-own-army part also sounds off, because I thought the whole point was that there isnt a higher power above states, just states agreeing to cooperate. Is the textbook simplifying or actually wrong, and which parts would a careful Kant defender sign off on? Also, does the "war becomes impossible" conclusion even follow from the parts of the summary that are accurate?
