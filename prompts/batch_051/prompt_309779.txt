I've spent enough years reading rationalist blogs to have internalized the usual stuff about precommitment, the planning fallacy, and not wanting to get dutch-booked, but I keep hitting a wall when I try to actually use Van Fraassen's Reflection principal at work. I do grant recommendations for a small EA-adjacent fund, four or five rounds a year, real money, and each decision gets revisited in later rounds by a future version of me who has (usually) read more and thought longer. The principle, as I understand it, says my current credence in some claim, conditional on learning that my future credence in it will be r, should just be r: future-me is supposed to be treated as an expert about future-me's evidence. But I can think of plenty of cases where that seems wrong — future-me might be sleep-deprived, might have forgotten the strongest argument against a grant, might have been socially captured by a community I currently distrust, or might just have drifted in values. And other times future-me has genuinely read all the reports and thought for months, and deferring seems right.

So: give me a reusable checklist for deciding, at decision time, whether and how much to defer to the credences I predict future-me will hold. It has to be something I can run in about thirty minutes before a grant round deadline, no formal math beyond what a blog-reading autodidact has, and it should keep the actual content of Reflection rather than turning into a generic update-on-the-evidence heuristic. In particular it needs to separate future-me has more evidence from future-me is just causally different in a way that isn't evidence, and it should tell me how to estimate what future-me will believe without that estimate already doing all the deferring I'm trying to decide about. Mark the steps where judgment can't be eliminated, and say where the procedure will misfire.

Apply it to two or three concrete hypotheticals, including at least one where the checklist tells me not to defer to a future-me I really want to defer to, and one where it tells me to defer even though it feels like abandoning my current inside view. And be honest about what the checklist can't do — especially the case where future-me just has different values, and there's no fact of the matter about which of us should be trusted.
