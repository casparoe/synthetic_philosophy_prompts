I sit on the Market Integrity Committee of a mid-sized prediction market platform that runs real-money markets on geopolitical and scientific questions and whose user base includes the sort of people who argue about decision theory for fun. For two years the platform has enforced an automated rule: when the system detects that a user's standing quotes are internally incoherent in a way that would permit a sure-loss arbitrage — for example, posted prices on a mutually exclusive and exhaustive set of outcomes whose implied probabilities sum above one — it freezes those quotes and requires the user to correct them before trading resumes. The rule was introduced after a novice was repeatedly cleaned out by more experienced traders, and the committee at the time justified it as consumer protection rather than as a judgment about how anyone ought to keep probabilities.

We meet Thursday to reconsider the rule in light of two incidents. In the first, a user posted prices summing to roughly 1.09 on a binary market, spotted the arbitrage himself, repriced voluntarily, and later wrote publicly that the freeze had denied him a cheap lesson; his central point was that a Dutch book argument only yields a guaranteed loss for someone who will actually accept each leg of the trade, and that a statically incoherent book is not yet a money pump. In the second, a different user argued that the freeze subsidizes sloppy forecasting by giving incoherent participants a pause to retreat, cancel, or be bailed out, instead of paying a price for a bad book. Our compliance lead answers that a real-money venue cannot run as a bounty system for the most ruthless traders without acquiring duties toward less expert users, and that requiring quotes to be coherent is no more an endorsement of expected-utility orthodoxy than a casino's rule against card counting is an epistemology.

I need a short written memo to circulate before Thursday. It should recommend a concrete course of action — likely one of: keep the auto-freeze; remove it and let all quotes stand whether coherent or not; or remove it but add a prominent warning to anyone whose quote set the system flags, with an opt-in to automatic protection. Give a timeframe for adoption and criteria for revisiting the policy. Engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side, and in particular with the charge that the committee is conflating vulnerability to a Dutch book with actually being money-pumped. I do not need citations or a literature review; I need a document the committee could act on.
