Here is what I'm going to write:

(i) Sovereignty, for Spinoza, just is the power of the multitude. Whatever the constitution says, a state has exactly as much right as it has power, and that power bottoms out in what the multitude will accept, do, or refuse to do.

(ii) I read his claim that democracy is the most absolute form of state as praise rather than warning: in a democracy the sovereign is the whole citizen body acting collectively, so the multitude's power is exercised in the least alienated way possible.

(iii) The more democratic a state is, the less it has to rule through fear, and the more it can rely on people doing voluntarily what the law requires, because the law is, in the right way, their own.

(iv) Universal inclusion is not essential to a Spinozist democracy. A state that excludes women, servants, and resident foreigners from citizenship can still count as a democracy in the relevant sense, so long as the citizen body as a whole governs.

(v) Spinoza's exclusion of women from political rule in the Political Treatise is a local prejudice he inherited, and the underlying theory is better off without it: drop it and nothing structural changes.

(vi) The deepest thing in Spinoza's defense of democracy is not, I think, about equal say at all. It's that a broadly based sovereign is less likely to abuse its power and more likely to preserve freedom of thought and judgment than a narrow one. By that standard, an aristocracy that was genuinely good at preserving freedom of thought would score just as well: output matters, not form.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, whats the cheapest fix for each genuine tension, and which one revision does the most work overall? Please dont give me a vibes-level answer: I want the tensions ordered and discriminated.
