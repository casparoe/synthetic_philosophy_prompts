I teach an upper-level applied ethics seminar at a small college — about 14 students, mix of philosophy majors and pre-meds — and we have one 90-minute session on the ethics of resource allocation in health care. I want an in-class activity where students *feel* the difference between saving an identified person in front of them and saving a larger number of merely statistical lives. I know the conceptual distinction (rule of rescue vs QALY maximization), but I want the students to experience it as a pull, not just discuss it. I want them to run into the problem themselves before anyone names it.

So please design a structured in-class activity for this setting: 14 students, 90 minutes, no real money, no tech beyond paper and index cards. It can be a game with payoffs, a forced-choice triage simulation, a role-play, or something similar. The key is that the rules and incentives should be arranged so participants discover, through their own choices, how differently they treat an identified person (a face, a name, a story) compared to an anonymous person or a statistical reduction in mortality. By the end they should be able to articulate the tension between maximizing quality-adjusted life years and responding to the identifiable victim, and they should have felt the emotional weight that makes the rule of rescue so hard to resist. Include: a clear timeline with phases, what materials I need to prepare, roles if any, how to debrief, and how the debrief should map specific moments in the activity onto the concepts of QALY, value of a statistical life, and the identifiability effect. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — e.g., students gaming the system, one person dominating, the debrief falling flat — and how to handle each. Finally, be honest about which part of the phenomenon the activity can't reproduce and what I'll need to supply in a short lecture afterwards. I'd rather you start by brainstorming two or three possible game structures and then commit to one, explaining why it works better than the others. Please think through the details carefully — I only get one shot at this class.
