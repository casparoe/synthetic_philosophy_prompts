I'm a PhD student working on reasons and rationality, and I keep coming back to the wrong-kind-of-reasons debate because it seems to be stuck in a way participants rarely acknowledge. The standard cases are familiar: a demon offers you a large incentive to desire a saucer of mud, or to admire a person with none of the features that could make admiration appropriate; you are offered a reward to believe a proposition whose truth you have no way of assessing. Everyone agrees these considerations give you some reason to do something — to bring the attitude about, to arrange your incentives, to cultivate it indirectly, to manage your mental life. The fight is over whether they are also reasons for the attitude itself. Some say yes: they are genuine reasons, but of the wrong kind, because they do not bear on the object of the attitude. Others say no: they are only reasons for actions concerning the attitude, while a reason for an attitude must be a consideration that makes the attitude fitting. A third camp effectively treats right-kind reasons as fittingness-grounds and insists the so-called wrong-kind reasons are really practical reasons about how to manage one's mental states.

After reading this literature closely, my sense is that nobody's intuitions about the cases have moved in decades, and the parties are largely arguing past one another. My working hypothesis is that this is not a dispute about the cases at all, but about what "for" imports in "reasons for an attitude" — and beneath that, about whether the rationality of an attitude should be theorized as fittingness to an object or as responsiveness to the full range of reasons bearing on one's mental economy, including practical ones. I would like a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a survey or a verdict. Is it verbal? Is it two different explanatory projects sharing a label? Do the parties start from different bedrock intuitions about what an attitude's rationality consists in? Or is there a hidden substantive claim about the metaphysics of reasons doing the work? And, given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a sharper distinction, a constraint any adequate theory must meet, or an agreement about which phenomenon the phrase "reason for an attitude" is meant to pick out?
