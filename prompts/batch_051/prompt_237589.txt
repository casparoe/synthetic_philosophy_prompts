Here’s the situation. I’ve been at my current church for about six years, and I’m the only person there who didn’t grow up in the same broad evangelical background; I came from a pentecostal-adjacent church before this. Last month I let myself get talked into leading the adult Sunday school class on whether the miraculous gifts — prophecy, healing, tongues, that sort — actually stopped after the apostolic age or are still available. The room will be maybe twenty people, mostly in their 50s, theologically conservative, serious about the authority of Scripture, and quietly cessationist, not because they’ve worked through the arguments but because that’s the default our tradition hands them. They’re not hostile. The pastor once told me, in a perfectly friendly way, that the gifts were “for the apostolic age” and then moved on without pressing it further. I don’t think most of them have read the actual proof texts closely. They just know the slogan, and they care a lot about not turning into the shouty charismatic church across town.

My position is moderate continuationism, and I want to keep it respectable: no chapter-and-verse requirement to babble in the lobby. I think the New Testament never cleanly says the gifts will stop before Christ returns; that the standard cessationist handling of the usual passages rests on reading a word about maturity and completeness as a timetable for the gifts; that church history is messier than the popular story, because sober, non-fanatical reports of healings and prophetic words keep showing up long after the apostles; and that a lot of what actually drives the evangelical default is embarrassment about enthusiasm, not exegesis. My soft spot is the historical evidence — I’m not a historian, and my examples are mostly secondhand.

I need a prep sheet for the question period, not a lecture. Give me the objections in roughly the order this specific crowd will raise them, phrased the way they’d put them, with what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and where my own case is weakest so I can defend it instead of hoping nobody notices. Include the one question I should be most worried about — the one I’ll be praying nobody asks. I’d rather here the hard version now.
