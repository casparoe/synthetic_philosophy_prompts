Smartphone apps and online platforms are routinely designed with features that leverage the way unpredictable rewards shape behaviour: infinite feeds, auto-play, streaks, and near-miss mechanics borrowed from gaming machines. When criticised, the firms behind these products often respond that users reveal what they genuinely want through their behaviour, and that the design simply gives people more of what they freely choose. Yet the same mechanisms are used in slot machines in order to keep a person responding after their considered judgement has told them to stop. Reconstruct the implicit theory of human welfare and preference that this design practice presupposes. Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and assess whether the engagement-maximising design can be coherently combined with the claim that the product is merely satisfying what users want. Finally, explain what would have to change in how such systems are built, measured, and defended if the governing view were that a person's good is determined by their stable, reflectively endorsed preferences rather than by whatever holds their attention. Please answer in British English.
