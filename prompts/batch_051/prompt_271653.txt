Arendt's account of political lying, developed around the Pentagon Papers, rests on a distinction between two kinds of falsehood. The familiar political lie is a specific false claim told against an otherwise intact public record, so it can in principle be found out because the facts remain available as a check. The newer and more dangerous practice she identified is the systematic fabrication of a public image, which does not merely misstate particular facts but erodes the shared factual world in which the distinction between truth and lie can be drawn at all. Now consider the later development of cheap synthetic media: fabricated but compelling audio and video of political events can be produced at scale, and any genuine recording can plausibly be dismissed as fabricated. Does Arendt's argument survive this development in roughly its original form, does it need repair, or does it fail? Identify the premise that deepfakes put pressure on, and state the strongest defensible version of her view under the new conditions. Answer in at most 500 words.
