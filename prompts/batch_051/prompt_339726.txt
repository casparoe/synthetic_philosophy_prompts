My philosophy tutor used to say you could spot an Eleatic — the Parmenides sort — by four tells: reality is one thing, not many; all change and coming-to-be is unreal, or at best a derivative surface; the plurality and flux we seem to see is a trick of the senses; and reason alone, not experience, is what reveals how things actually are. Now take the block universe in contemporary philosophy of time: past, present and future events are all equally real, the apparent flow of time is just an artefact of our persective, and there is no objective becoming — but there are still many events and objects, and a thing can be one way on Tuesday and another way on Wednesday in a perfectly real tenseless sense. Go through the four tells one by one and tell me whether the block universe counts as an Eleatic view. If you end up with a mixed verdict, say whether that means the block universe is a counterexample to the tells, or whether the tells themselves were too blunt to capture what Eleatic monism was after.
