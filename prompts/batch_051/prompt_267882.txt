Habermas's argument for communicative rationality runs roughly like this: under an ideal speech situation — everyone allowed to speak, no pressure except the force of the better argument, participants sincerely trying to reach mutual understanding — whatever survives the conversation has a legitimacy that raw power doesn't. That picture was built for seminars, party meetings, maybe letters pages. Now suppose the relevant forum is an engagement-sorted feed: the ranking algorithm decides whose seen, a big share of the visible posts are generated by LLMs tuned to maximize clicks or outrage, and almost no one is trying to converge on anything — they're performing, holding attention, or farming status. Does Habermas's argument survive that change? Which premise actually absorbs the damage, and is there a repaired weaker version that still does real work for thinking about online discourse, or is the ideal speech situation just the wrong tool now? Take a definite position and defend it — don't just lay out the menu.
