Build me a thought experiment where two standard sides in the value-drift debate swap their usual conclusions. Side one is the lock-in view: we should bind future people to at least a core of our current values, because revisions are as likely to make things worse as better. Side two is the open-ended view: we should let values revise freely down the generations, because later people are better placed to judge. In your scenario, side one should end up defending the revision of one of the group's founding commitments, and side two should end up demanding that one particular value be made permanently unrevisable. Constraints: the case is set in an ordinary human institution (a foundation, a union, a congregation, something like that) with a multi-generation time horizon, no AI or sci-fi tech, and the scenario itself should be under two paragraphs. The scenario also has to generate these verdicts from the positions rather than just having the characters announce them. Then walk through what each side says and why the usual conclusions come out reversed, and identify the single structural feature doing the work. Then say what happens if that feature is removed and how a defender of whichever side the case is unfair to would attack it. Finally, say how confident you are that the case really isolates that feature, and single out the point you're least sure of.
