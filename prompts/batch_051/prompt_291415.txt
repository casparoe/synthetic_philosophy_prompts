I have been working through Part Five of Descartes’s Discourse on Method, the passage where he contrasts parrots and magpies with people born deaf and mute. As I understand the argument, Descartes allows that a magpie or parrot can be trained to produce human sounds, but he denies that those sounds are words in the relevant sense because the animal never uses them as signs to declare its thoughts; it is merely moved by passion or appetite. Deaf and mute human beings, by contrast, lack the ordinary organs of speech yet invent signs through which they make themselves understood to others, and Descartes takes this to show that they possess reason. I want to press that criterion on the case of sign languages. Suppose we ask: is American Sign Language, or another established sign language, a language in Descartes’s sense rather than merely an elaborated system of gestures? How would one test that claim, and what would the test establish? I am not asking for a literature review or citations. I am asking for a diagnostic design.

Please organize the answer into five named sections:

1. **The criterion as an operationalizable claim** — Restate Descartes’s criterion in your own words and say exactly what observable behavior it would distinguish from behavior that merely looks linguistic. Roughly 150 words.

2. **The test design** — Specify the participants, tasks, or comparisons you would use. You may compare deaf children acquiring an established sign language, hearing children acquiring speech, home signers who have not been exposed to a conventional language, and animals that have been trained to use gestures or lexical symbols, if those comparisons help isolate the criterion. Be concrete about what would be measured and why it bears on Descartes rather than on a modern linguistic definition. Roughly 400 words.

3. **What positive and negative results would show** — Lay out the possible outcomes and what each would license. In particular, say which result would support the conclusion that sign languages satisfy Descartes’s criterion, and which would support the conclusion that they are only elaborated gesture systems. Be honest about results that would be ambiguous. Roughly 400 words.

4. **What no test can settle** — Identify the residue that behavior alone cannot reach, especially anything concerning the first-person act of meaning or declaring thought, and whether any test could in principle close that gap or whether the criterion itself must be modified. Roughly 250 words.

5. **Your verdict** — Say whether Descartes’s criterion survives contact with the best available test, or whether it needs repair, replacement, or restriction. Roughly 200 words.
