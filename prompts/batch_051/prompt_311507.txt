I've been in a philosophy-of-science reading group that keeps coming back to an apparent tension in how people talk about dispositions, and I would like a careful treatment. The naive view in the room is that a disposition ascription such as "this glass is fragile" is just a compact way of asserting a conditional: if the glass were struck with moderate force under normal conditions, it would break. Two kinds of counterexample keep getting raised. In a mask case, the manifestation is blocked without the disposition being lost: a fragile glass swathed in foam, or fitted with a vibration-damping coating, will not break when struck, yet no one in the group wants to say it is not fragile. In a fink case, the very event that would trigger the manifestation would also remove the disposition before it could be expressed: imagine a glass engineered so that the first instant of hard contact switches on a micro-heater that anneals the surface and makes it tough by the time the stress wave peaks, or a reagent that is denatured by the very probe used to detect it. The group is now split. Some say fragility is a real feature of an object that is only partly observable; others say the counterexamples show we should stop talking about dispositions altogether and talk only about conditionals that held or failed under fully specified test conditions.

I would like you to design the best test you can of a disposition ascription such as "this object is fragile," where the target is the disposition itself rather than merely the object's behavior in a single trial. Lay out the test concretely; say exactly what a positive or negative result would support; explain how a mask could be distinguished from the absence of the disposition, and how a fink would be detected or ruled out; and then be explicit about what even the best test cannot settle regarding an unmanifested disposition. Conclude by saying whether, in your view, anything in the test results could force a choice between treating dispositions as real features of the world and treating them as a useful but eliminable shorthand. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Answer in American English.
