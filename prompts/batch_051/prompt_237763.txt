I've been trying to get straight what holds my moral circle together. I used to like the picture where moral progress is an expanding circle—family, tribe, nation, all humans, animals, maybe more—but when I actually list my gut verdicts, the edge of the circle doesn't look like a clean line. I want to know what principle, if any, captures them.

Here are the verdicts:

- If my sister and a stranger were both drowning, I'd save my sister without hesitation, and I wouldn't treat that as a moral failure. But I don't think the stranger counts for nothing.

- I think factory farming is seriously wrong because pigs suffer, and no amount of bacon outweighs that. I'd put my dog at the same level, maybe higher—though that might just be partiality talking.

- If I had to choose between saving a human newborn (even one with a severe cognitive impairment) and an adult chimpanzee, I'd save the newborn, no question. But the chimp probably has more self-awareness and richer emotions than the newborn. I can't say why the newborn wins.

- A person in a permanent vegetative state: I'd be appalled if someone treated their body as an object, but I can't tell whether I'm protecting them or protecting their family.

- An eight-week embryo: I asign it almost no weight. Less than a mouse, because the mouse can suffer and the embryo can't. I'm comfortable with that, for now.

- People 200 years from now: I think it's wrong to leave them a wrecked climate just to keep things cheap today. They count, even thought they don't exist yet.

- A future digital mind—something that acts like it suffers and begs me not to delete it. My gut says don't delete it, but I don't know whether that's moral insight or me anthropomorphizing a clever program.

- A river: I think poisoning it is wrong, but I don't think the river itself is wronged. The wrong is downstream—people and animals—plus maybe something about the value of the natural world that isn't the same as moral standing.

- An ant: I'll swat a mosquito without a second thought, but if someone pulls the legs off an ant for fun, I think they're doing something wrong. I can't tell whether the ant is in the circle or whether the wrong is just about the person's character.

That's the fuzz at the edge. My question: is there a precise principle that draws the boundary in a way that fits most or all of these? I mean something precise enough that I can see how it handles each case—not just a word like "sentience" or "human dignity." If saving every one of my verdicts would take a gerrymandered mess of a principle, I'd rather know which verdicts to drop. Also run whatever you land on against a few cases I haven't settled: a 24-week fetus, an octopus, and a chatbot that only mimics human distress. And if my whole framing is confused—if there isn't one circle but several different questions I've smushed together—say that plainly instead of giving me a principle to be polite.
