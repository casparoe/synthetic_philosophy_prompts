You are an expert philosopher of science who works on reduction, emergence, and causal inference, with particular familiarity with simulation-based science. I have been arguing with a collaborator about a cellular automaton we are studying, and I want you to think carefully about how one could actually test a conceptual claim about it, rather than just resolving it by stipulation.

The setup is this. A lattice of binary sites updates in discrete time by a local rule: each site's next state depends on its own state and the states of its nearest neighbors according to a fixed update function. From the full microstructure at any time t, we can compute a global, coarse-grained quantity G(t), for instance the density of domain walls or some similar order parameter. G(t) is simply a function of the microstate, so it carries no energy or information that is not already present in the sites. Yet in our runs, the past and present values of G are highly predictive of individual site transitions, and it looks to us as though G is constraining which local configurations are possible next. My collaborator claims that, in this system, G(t) exerts genuine downward causal influence on the individual sites: it is a real emergent cause, not merely a compressed description or a shadow of the local dynamics. I am not convinced, and I would like to know whether the disagreement is empirically decidable at all.

Please design the best concrete test you can of the claim that G(t) is a genuine downward cause of the site-level updates. Specify the intervention, manipulation, or comparison you would run, what control conditions or null models you would use, and exactly what pattern of results would constitute evidence for the claim and what pattern would tell against it. Then say precisely what such a test would and would not settle. In particular, identify any part of the claim that no computational or observational test could adjudicate even in principle because it is a matter of what we should mean by terms like cause, emergence, or levels. Start by briefly brainstorming possible operationalizations of downward causation in a toy model like this, and flag the respects in which each operationalization might beg the question against or in favor of the claim.
