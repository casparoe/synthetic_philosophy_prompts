When people train LLMs with RLHF, the reward model is usually trained on pairwise comparisons: a human is shown two responses and picks one, and the system fits a single scalar reward to each response using something like a Bradley–Terry model, then optimizes against that number. It seems to me this setup quietly assumes preferences are a particular kind of thing. What theory of preference or value does that design presuppose? Does it presuppose that preferences are complete and transitive, that they're stable across contexts and moods, that everything a person cares about can be put on one scalar, and that choices reveal preferences that were already there rather than constructing them on the spot? I'd want you to separate what the math strictly requires from what is just convenient engineering convention. Then say what would have to change in the pipeline if preferences are actually context-dependent, constructed, or incommensurable. Also, I notice the same companys sometimes use a Likert scale or a written critique as an alternative reward signal in the very same system. Is that inconsistent with the pairwise-comparison setup, or can both fit a single underlying picture of preference?
