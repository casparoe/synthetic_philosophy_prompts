I am a retired natural scientist who has only recently begun reading philosophy seriously. The figure who has most engaged me is Henry Sidgwick, in part because of his involvement in the Society for Psychical Research: he approached reports of telepathy and survival after death as empirical claims to be investigated under controlled conditions, not as matters to be settled by prior metaphysical commitment. I would be interested in your prediction of how Sidgwick would have responded to methodological developments in parapsychology that occurred long after his death — pre-registration, adversarial collaborations between skeptics and proponents, meta-analyses of telepathy experiments (such as the ganzfeld studies), and the broader replication crisis in psychology. Would he regard these developments as vindicating his own approach, as signs that the field had matured scientifically, or as something that would have troubled him? Please reason from his actual commitments, and be explicit about the continuities and tensions.
