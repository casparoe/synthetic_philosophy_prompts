In my philosophy of cognitive science seminar, we have spent the term on the extended mind debate: the question of whether cognitive states and processes can be partly constituted by equipment outside the skull. To push us past slogan-level arguments, our professor has been ruling on a series of cases, and her rulings so far are these:

- On Otto, a man with unreliable biological memory who always carries a notebook in which he writes down useful information and consults it as automatically as most of us consult our memory: she says the notebook is part of his memory.
- On a blind woman navigating by sweeping a white cane and continuously adjusting her steps in response to the feedback it provides: she says the cane is part of her perceptual apparatus.
- On someone working through a long division on paper, writing each intermediate product and reading it back before taking the next step: she says the marks on the page are part of the computational process.
- On a student who keeps a contacts list on his phone, which is frequently out of date, so that he double-checks every number against a mutual friend before dialing: she says the file is not part of his memory; it is merely an external store he interrogates.

She has now asked us to predict her next ruling. The new case is a woman who keeps a meticulously up-to-date digital diary on a phone she always carries and completely trusts. She uses it only a few times a day, at decision points, to retrieve appointments, names, and where she left things. When she consults it, she simply reads the entry and acts on it as she would on an ordinary memory; the device is reliably available whenever a retrieval is needed, but it is not woven into her moment-to-moment activity the way Otto's notebook or the blind woman's cane is.

Before next week, I want to work out what the professor would say. I can see at least two principles that fit all four of her earlier rulings, and they seem to point in different directions on the diary case. What are the leading candidate principles she could be running, what would each of them say about the diary, what is your best prediction of her verdict and why, and what further case would most cleanly separate the leading candidates?
