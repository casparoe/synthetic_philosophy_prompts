I'm assembling a set of prompts for a course on philosophical argument in literary form, and I keep returning to the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the worry that pleasures as different as the relief of a stubbed toe finally easing, the frisson of a choral crescendo, malicious Schadenfreude, and solving a chess problem may share no single felt quality, which would be awkward if welfare really is just the same thing appearing in all of them. I'd like you to take the role of a professional philosopher specializing in value theory and the history of hedonism, who is also an experienced parodist. Think carefully and work through the underlying philosophical positions before drafting; no need to search or cite anything, and please don't fabricate references.

Write a satirical internal memorandum of roughly 650–800 words from a fictional government agency — something like the Office of Hedonic Weights and Measures — whose statutory mandate requires it to reduce welfare to a single commensurable unit (a hedon, a util, or whatever you prefer) for use in cost–benefit analysis. The memo, from the agency's director or chief scientist to staff, should report the agency's failure to establish a common unit of pleasure across heterogeneous categories of experience and issue interim guidance. The humor should come from getting the actual philosophy right rather than from cheap misrepresentation. A knowledgeable reader should recognize the moves genuinely available to a hedonist: the Benthamite insistence that pleasure is homogeneous and differs only in quantity, with the familiar embarrassment that pushpin thereby counts equally with poetry; Mill's distinction between higher and lower pleasures, with the standing suspicion that it smuggles non-hedonic values into the currency; the attitudinal view that what pleasures share is a favorable attitude rather than a felt quality, together with the worry that this collapses hedonism into desire-satisfaction; and the question of whether pleasure is best understood as a sensation, a functional state, or a family-resemblance category. The memo should also let the absurdity emerge from the real difficulty — whether a util of malice and a util of benevolence can be added into the same total, or whether the commuter's tiny relief and the saint's ecstasy belong on one scale — rather than from inventing a strawman hedonist. It should be deadpan throughout, legible to someone who knows none of this, and the target should be the practice of treating pleasure as a single measurable magnitude, not any named individual.

After the memo, append a brief note from you, the author, of about 150–250 words, clearly separated from the parody, stating which elements in the satire are settled or standard in the literature, which are genuinely contested among philosophers working on hedonism, and where the piece leans on a contestable reading of the heterogeneity problem (for example, the assumption that the objection seriously damages modern attitudinal hedonism or that Mill's move is a retreat from hedonism). Please do not present contested positions as consensus; flag them precisely as contested. End there; no citations, bibliography, or further commentary.
