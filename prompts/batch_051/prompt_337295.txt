I chair the review panel of a university life-sciences institute's seed-grant competition. The call says the institute funds projects that explain biological processes, but it does not define explanation. We are deadlocked on one application, and I need a written recommendation to circulate before next Tuesday's meeting.

The applicants propose to train a deep neural network on high-throughput single-cell perturbation data and use it to predict how cells respond to novel combinations of drugs and genetic perturbations. Their evaluation plan is exclusively prospective predictive accuracy on held-out interventions, and they are explicit that the model will not be built around, constrained by, or reported in terms of any mechanistic interpretation: no identified components or interactions, no decomposition of the response into organized sub-processes. The cell is treated as a predictive black box. The applicants add that, at the scale of tens of thousands of interactions, a mechanistic reconstruction would be premature and unreliable.

Half the panel wants to reject. On their view, a biological explanation is the specification of the components, operations, and organization that produce the phenomenon; a predictive surrogate, however accurate, is an instrument for intervening rather than an account of how the system works, and funding it would drift from the institute's stated mission. The other half wants to fund. On their view, a model that accurately predicts the outcomes of thousands of targeted interventions explains those outcomes in the sense that matters for guiding experiment and therapy, and penalizing mechanistic-free prediction would arbitrarily shrink the field's methods.

Please draft the panel's decision memo: recommend fund, reject, or fund with conditions. Put the recommendation up front and give reasons a majority could act on. State the strongest consideration on the losing side in the strongest form its advocates would accept, not as a straw man. Also say explicitly what empirical result or philosophical consideration would most likely reverse your recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
