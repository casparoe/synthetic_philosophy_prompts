I run a small discussion group, and for the past few months we have been arguing in circles about how much people should be blamed when the inner decision and the outer result come apart. I recently came across a medieval idea attributed to Abelard: that the sin is in the consent to do wrong, not in the outward act itself, and not even in the desire or impulse that comes before consent. I am not religious myself, so I have been treating his claim as a claim about blame rather than sin. I find it appealing because it treats a person who wholeheartedly decides to do something terrible but is prevented before anything happens as more blameworthy than someone whose careless habit causes real damage but who never deliberately consented to the damage. But it also seems to leave out much of what the people in my group keep insisting on: negligence, willful blindness, the size of the harm, and the way unchosen habits can amount over a lifetime to something morally serious.

I would like you to build a sorting instrument, either a quiz with a scoring key or a decision tree, that takes someone from their gut reactions to a half-dozen concrete cases to a named position about where blame or sin is located. It should be able to distinguish at least these positions: the Abelardian consent-only view; a desire- or intention-based view that counts the mere wanting or planning as blameworthy even without consent; an act-based view that locates blame in what someone does; a harm- or outcome-based view; and a negligence- or character-based view that blames the person for the habits and failures of attention that make bad acts likely. Build it from the logical space of possible answers first, and only then place the named views in that space, flagging any two positions the instrument cannot really tell apart. Please use cases along roughly these lines: someone who decides on a wrong and is stopped before acting; someone who hurts another in a panicked reflex without any decision; someone who violates a rule he was raised to think was fine; someone who knows the rule but will not let himself see that it covers his own case; a tiny careless lapse that happens to produce an enormous harm; and a wholehearted decision that by luck produces no harm at all. Phrase every question so an ordinary person can answer it without already knowing which position it leads toward.

Because I want to use this with people who hold strong but confused views, please tell me how confident you are in the structure of the instrument, and single out the one diagnostic question or leaf placement you are least sure about.
