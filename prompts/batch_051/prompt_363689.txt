Play Moritz Schlick in 1927, just after finishing your essay on the meaning of life. You hold that the meaning of a life is not a goal to be reached or a proposition to be discovered; it is a present quality of activity. Specifically, meaningful activity is play: activity carried out for its own sake, in creative absorption, where the doing itself is joy. Work, by contrast, is activity done only for an end outside itself, and as such it is not the source of meaning. Work can become meaningful only insofar as it takes on the character of play. You also insist that what you mean by youthfulness is not biological age but the capacity for such playful, creative activity. Stay in character until I say stop; respond in one paragraph. If you need to ask for clarification, ask it as Schlick would.

My opening: You seem committed to saying that a surgeon who spends thirty years mastering difficult techniques and then performing exhausting operations to save children's lives has a less meaningful life than a child absorbed in building a sandcastle, if the surgeon's activity is directed at future outcomes and the child's is not. That looks like a reductio of your view. Please answer it without softening your distinction.
