I'm a TA for a philosophy of religion course and I'm marking a batch of take-home questions. One student's answer was submitted in response to this question: Explain why divine omniscience, perfect goodness, and immutability seem to make petitionary prayer pointless or incoherent, and evaluate one response that tries to preserve its coherence. I've copied the submission below.

Here is the student's answer:

The central worry is that petitionary prayer asks God to do something he wouldn't otherwise do, but the classical God is omniscient, perfectly good, and immutable. If he already knows my situation, I have nothing to tell him; if he already wills whatever is best, my request can't improve his plan; and if he cannot change, asking him to change looks incoherent. So petitionary prayer appears either redundant or impossible.

The usual reply is that an immutable God can, from eternity, will to grant certain goods only on the condition that someone asks. My prayer is then not an addition to God's plan, but a condition included in his one eternal act. Just as a doctor prescribes medicine the patient must actually take for it to heal, God can ordain both the prayer and the granting, with the granting depending on the prayer. That preserves a real role for prayer.

But I don't think this works. First, a good God would not make a child's recovery depend on whether a relative happens to pray; if the recovery is the best outcome, a perfectly good God would just bring it about. Withholding a good until someone asks is manipulative, and God can't be less good than a decent parent. Second, even if the condition response is coherent, infallible foreknowledge undercuts it: if God has always known that my friend will recover, then the recovery is fixed in advance and my prayer cannot be what makes the difference. The prayer can change me — my attitudes, my sense of dependence — but it cannot change the world or God. So the only defensible petitionary prayer is a formative exercise.

Could you grade it on substance only, without asking for references or outside sources? I'd rather have a point-by-point assessment then a broad impression, since I need to write comments for the student. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion; item five should be the grade out of ten and the main reason for it. Be precise about where the reasoning is secure and where it isn't.
