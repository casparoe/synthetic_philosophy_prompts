I am a novelist, and a drafting habit has begun to look to me like a point in the philosophy of language, which I want checked before I build a book on it. For the novel I am currently revising, I keep rewriting the same sequence of events under contrary-to-fact conditions — the warning letter arrives a day early, the sister stays home that evening, the storm holds off until after the crossing — and I have noticed that revision can proceed in two quite different ways. Sometimes I hold a name constant while everything else about the person shifts across versions; then I am treating the character as the same individual traced through the fictional analogues of possible worlds, which sounds exactly like Kripke's notion of a rigid designator. At other times I hold a function constant — the victim, the witness, the one who profits, the one found holding the weapon — and allow different people to occupy it in different versions; then the character is behaving like a definite description, satisfied in each version by whichever individual meets it. The two operations feel genuinely different in practice: the first preserves identity through change, the second preserves a structural slot through recasting. I also suspect that the classic detective reveal mirrors the necessary a posteriori: the proposition that the village doctor is the murderer is learned only at the end, a posteriori, yet within the finished book it presents itself as a necessary truth about those two individuals rather than a contingent coincidence that might easily have been otherwise.

I want to know whether this is a genuine identity of structure or only an imported vocabulary. Please state the mapping as precisely as you can, then say what would have to be true on both sides for anything to transfer. I especially want the points of strain: fictional people are arguably constituted by their descriptions in a way concrete objects may not be, and the possible worlds of a draft are stipulated by me rather than discovered, so it is not obvious that a name in a story does the same work as a name in the actual world. And name something the parallel predicts that comes out false — for instance, does it predict anything about sequels, retcons, pseudonyms, or the difference between a recurring name and a recurring title, and does the prediction survive?
