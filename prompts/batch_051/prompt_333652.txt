I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and after a year of drifting from a permissive subjectivism toward objective Bayesianism, I've settled—uneasily—on the maximum entropy principle as the positive view I'd defend in a dissertation chapter. The position, stated plainly: when evidence can be represented as a set of constraints on a probability assignment (an expected value, a bound, a known marginal), the rationally unique prior is the distribution that maximizes entropy subject to exactly those constraints, because any other choice would import information the constraints do not contain. I find the consistency-axioms line of defense and the broader maximizing-uncertainty-subject-to-what-you-know idea persuasive for quite different reasons, and the fact that they converge is one of the main things keeping me in the view; I worry that I'm rationalizing a principled-looking rule rather than pressure-testing it.

What I want is a self-critical map of what would count against this position, written for someone who already knows the objective Bayesian formalism and doesn't need a survey. Set aside the objections I've already absorbed—parameterization-dependence of continuous entropy, Bertrand-style underdetermination, the charge that one must choose a partition or base measure by hand—or at least only return to them if they matter more than I've been assuming. I care especially about separating pressure on three distinguishable commitments: (i) that the constraint set is a faithful encoding of the evidence rather than a modeling decision, (ii) that entropy is the right measure of the information a distribution flouts beyond the constraints, and (iii) that the procedure really yields a rationally unique prior rather than one workable convention among several. For each, I want to know which results, cases, or arguments would be decisive, which merely awkward, and which a committed MaxEnt defender can concede without abandoning the core view. I also want the map to be honest about the difference between MaxEnt as a rule for setting priors and MaxEnt as a rule for updating when new constraint-shaped information arrives, since I suspect the view is more exposed in the second role.

End by taking a definite position on the question behind the map: is the maximum entropy program well insulated against its defeaters, and if it is, should a defender treat that insulation as the mark of a genuinely normative constraint or as evidence that the view has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability? I'd rather be corrected than confirmed, so write it as an assessment of where the position actually risks the most, not as a defense of where I'd like it to be strongest.
