My friend and I spent the whole evening going back and forth on whether envy is ever just a signal of injustice, and I ended up with a distinction I want pressure-tested. I call it tracking envy versus spite envy. Tracking envy survives the veil of ignorance: if I had to evaluate the situation without knowing which position I'd occupy, I'd still find the inequality objectionable, even when I might turn out to be the one coming out ahead. Spite envy needs the first-person comparison; take away the envier's stake or proximity and the feeling mostly evaporates. My claim is that tracking envy is a fallible but real signal of injustice, and resentment is basically tracking envy plus a demand that the advantage be justified rather than just envied. I know Rawls has his thing about excusable envy, and my friend says I've just relabeled it. Is that right? If not, what does my distinction capture that excusable envy doesnt? And where does it break: what would it flag as an injustice that isn't one?
