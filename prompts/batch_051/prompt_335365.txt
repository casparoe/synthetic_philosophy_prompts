I'm putting together a unit on Sartre's bad faith, and I keep flip-flopping on where bad faith ends and ordinary self-deception begins, so I want to borrow your intuitions. My current line is that Sartrean bad faith is narrower than self-deception: it has to involve a dodge relative to the tension between facticity and transcendence—either you treat yourself as a finished object with no freedom ('I just am this way'), or you treat your freedom as if it erased your facticity ('there's no continuous me, the past doesn't stick'). A friend thinks that's over-engineered and that any motivated refusal to see an obvious truth about yourself counts. To pressure-test my line, I wrote five cases. Could you rate each one 0–10 for how central a case of Sartrean bad faith it is (10 = textbook bad faith, 0 = not bad faith at all), and then rank them from most to least central? For each case give me a sentence or two on the score, and if you think a case is really a different phenomena—coercion, compulsion, akrasia, plain self-deception, whatever—flag that and say what makes it different.

The cases: (1) Anita has missed three deadlines this year and tells herself, 'I'm just not a deadline person, it's how I've always been'; she's never tried any time-management system or asked for accommodations. (2) Ben leaves relationships by vanishing; when a friend points out the pattern, he says, 'You're imposing a story on me. There's no continuous self who keeps doing this—at each moment I simply choose, and the past isn't mine to own.' (3) Chloe is a mortgage underwriter who suspects the bank's criteria are redlining certain neighborhoods. She tells her partner, 'Look, I just execute the algorithm; if I pushed back I'd be replaced.' She has some discretion on edge cases and has never used it. (4) Dan is in a relationship that has gone flat, but he tells himself, 'every couple loses the spark after five years, this is just what commitment looks like,' and changes the subject when his partner brings up counseling. (5) Eve snaps at her kids most evenings and says afterward, 'that wasn't me, I was tired'—but she's said the same thing for months and hasn't changed anything about her evenings.

I know the 0–10 scores are sort of arbitrary; I care more about whether the ranking settles where the line is than about the exact numbers.
