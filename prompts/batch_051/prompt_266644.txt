I'm a novelist, so this is research, but I want something sharper than background color. My next book follows a corrections officer who does fifteen years in a maximum-security prison in the US and then, through a family situation, takes a job at Halden in Norway. I keep running into a puzzle I can't get clear on, and I'd rather you take it seriously than give me the usual "Norway is humane, America is cruel" reflex.

The two places seem to be built on totally different ideas of what a prison is even for. Halden looks like a Scandinavian campus: guards unarmed, prisoners with their own rooms and fridges and flatware, staff and inmates cooking and eating together, job training and a focus on life after release, ordinary prison sentences capped at 21 years. The American side of my character's life is supermax cells, solitary confinement measured in years, mandatory minimums, and sentences that outlast a human life. Neither of these are secrets; I'm bothered by what they presuppose.

Here's what I want: treat each prison system as an established practice and tell me what theory of punishment it presupposes. Does Halden only make sense if you think offenders are broken, excluded, or underdeveloped people who need to be brought back into society — that is, if rehabilitation and reintegration are the point? Or can a retributivist run Halden too, by saying the deprivation of liberty is the punishment and the conditions are just not adding extra cruelty on top? Does the American system presuppose retribution, deterrence, or incapacitation — or does it fail to presuppose any single coherent view, and just accumulate fear, anger, and political incentives? Then tell me whether the two systems are really built on incompatible philosophies, or whether they share a deeper commitment and differ only about how much hardship is justified. Finish by saying what would have to change in each place if the implicit theory were swapped.

And wherever you land, tell me explicitly what would change your mind: which argument, empirical finding about recidivism or deterence, or claim about moral responsibility would make you reverse or seriously redraw your reconstruction, and where you think your reasoning is most likely to fail.
