Suppose Charles Darwin had been presented with the modern units-of-selection debate as it took shape after W. D. Hamilton, George C. Williams, and Richard Dawkins, and had been asked whether natural selection acts fundamentally on genes, on individual organisms, or on groups. Reconstruct the position he would most likely have defended, using as evidence his treatment of the sterile worker castes of social insects, which he regarded as a serious difficulty for his theory, and his appeal to competition between tribes in The Descent of Man. In particular, determine whether Darwin is best read as an early group selectionist, as a proto-gene-selectionist, or as a pluralist who would have treated the choice of unit as a matter of modeling convenience rather than a substantive biological claim, and defend that reading against the most plausible alternative.
