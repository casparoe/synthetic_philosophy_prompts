I keep going back and forth on whether 'the will of the people' is real enough to build an institution around, or whether it's just whatever the measuring device outputs. My prior is that any institution billed as measuring it gets Goodharted, but I want the specifics for a proposal I've been stress-testing:

A country sets up a citizens' assembly with teeth. Every four years, 300 people are drafted by lot for a six-month term. You can decline, but if you decline too many times you pay a fine; if you serve, you're paid twice the median wage for the whole term. The assembly has professional staff, can call witnesses, hold hearings, the normal deliberative stuff. But it has exactly one formal power: it may send one bill to the country, written as a single sentence of under 200 words, which becomes law automatically unless a two-thirds majority of both legislative houses votes it down within sixty days. After that the assembly disbands, and its members cant run for office or register as lobbyists for ten years. The catch: the bill has to concern something the legislature has failed to pass during the previous four years.

I dont want to know whether this is democracy or whether it's legitimate in the abstract. I want to know how it actually plays out. Who bothers to serve, and who opts out? What sort of single sentence survives the internal bargaining: a median pet issue, a huge wealth transfer, something designed to pass but also designed to force the legislature into spending political capital to kill it? How do parties, interest groups, and the legislature itself game the setup before and during the term? Does the two-thirds override make the assembly toothless, or does it turn the assembly into a hostage-taker whose bill is drafted to maximize the cost of voting it down? Can the failure modes be fixed by tweaks—term length, sortition pool, drafting rules, override threshold—without collapsing the thing back into another legislature? And does whatever comes out of this body deserve to be called the will of the people any more than a referendum result does, or is it just a different kind of filter?
