Give me a working definition of "the legitimate government" — who has the right to rule, not just who happens to hold the ministries — that gets these cases right. (1) A disaster kills the president, vice president, and entire cabinet; the constitution has no succession rule past the VP, so a group of senior judges declares itself the interim executive and is widely obeyed. (2) An election is voided for fraud, and under a caretaker clause the outgoing cabinet stays on and governs for a year. (3) After a financial collapse, an emergency council of mayors and civil servants with no basis in the constitution takes over, collects taxes, and runs services for months with broad public support. (4) A general seizes the ministries by force, suspends the old constitution, says a new one will be written, and is obeyed partly out of fear and partly out of relief. (5) After a war, a transitional authority appointed by a foreign coalition runs the country for two years with the old legal order formally suspended. (6) An armed crowd occupies the parliament chamber, declares itself the government, but nobody outside follows its orders. I'm sure (2) clears the bar and (6) doesn't, and I cant decide about the middle four. Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me where it surprises you.
