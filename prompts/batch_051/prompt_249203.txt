I've been messing with a version of the CHSH game and I can't tell if the local bound actually survives. Standard setup: Alice and Bob are separated, each gets fresh random bits x and y, they output bits a and b, and they win the round if a xor b equals x and y (a⊕b = x∧y). With shared randomness they top out at 3/4, and with entanglement they can hit cos²(π/8) ≈ .85. Now suppose the next round's inputs just are the previous outputs: x_{t+1}=a_t and y_{t+1}=b_t. The players know this, can use memory across rounds, still can't communicate. Does the 3/4 bound still apply? I want a concrete answer, not just a wave at superdeterminism: either give me a classical memory-based strategy that beats .85 in the long run (how close to 1 can it get?) or pin down the exact step in the Bell/CHSH derivation that stops working and explain why locality alone doesn't fix it.
