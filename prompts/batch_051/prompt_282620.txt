I have been reading about the modularity of mind, from Fodor's original account to the stronger massive modularity view, and there is a tension I cannot resolve. Modules are supposed to be domain-specific and informationally encapsulated: each one handles its own type of input and does not draw freely on the rest of cognition. But people also solve problems in domains that could not possibly have come pre-wired—novel mathematics, newly invented games, unfamiliar technologies. If every part of cognition is modular, how is this possible? It looks as though the existence of domain-general learning and creativity would be a simple counterexample to modularity. I can see only two escapes, and neither seems satisfactory: either general intelligence does not really exist, or modules are not actually encapsulated and specialized in the strong sense. Which one is the modularity theorist committed to?
