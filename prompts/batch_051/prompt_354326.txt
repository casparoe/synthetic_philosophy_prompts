I serve on my university's community standards board, and we are deadlocked on a case I need to turn into a decision memo before next week's hearing. A student in our immersive-media program used the department's persistent virtual-reality platform to build a detailed, photorealistic replica of a fellow student's off-campus apartment. While the target and several classmates were present as avatars — the target was wearing a full-body haptic suit that translates in-world events into physical sensation — the student set the replica on fire and blocked the target's avatar from leaving for several minutes. The fire also destroyed a virtual artwork the target had spent months building inside the platform, an object that existed nowhere else. The target says they believed themselves to be in genuine danger during the episode, has since been unable to attend the shared virtual workshops the course requires, and describes the artwork's destruction as a real loss. The accused student argues that the episode happened inside a simulation and that any distress came from the platform's standard haptic feedback and the target's own immersion rather than from any act in the world; to sanction it, the student says, would be to police every frightening scene in every virtual environment used on campus. Our code prohibits conduct that causes serious harm to another member of the university community or creates a credible threat of such harm, but it was written before these platforms existed. Half the board thinks sanctioning this would punish a fiction; the other half thinks declining to sanction it confuses what is fictional with what is harmless.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete outcome — whether to sanction and at what level or with what conditions — and it should give the board a principled way to distinguish virtual acts that merely depict harm from virtual acts that constitute or cause it, and to say whether, and when, the destruction of an object that exists only in a virtual world is the destruction of something. Engage the strongest argument for the losing side seriously rather than just restating the code.
