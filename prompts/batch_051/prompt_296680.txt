I need a thought experiment built to spec, for a paper I'm drafting on what invalidates consent.

Construct a case with these features. The consenting person is a competent adult, fully informed, not deceived, and not threatened. The only kind of pressure in the situation comes from an offer. But the case should be one where two accounts of consent come apart. On a threat-based account of coercion, the consent comes out fine: threats can coerce, offers can't, because an offer just adds an option. On an undue-inducement (or unforced-voluntariness) account, the consent comes out invalid, because the offer itself exerts enough pressure that the "yes" isn't the kind of free act that can transfer permission. Constraints: ordinary workplace, not a hospital or research lab, nothing sexual, one paragraph max. Also the offer has to be a genuine offer, not a disguised threat — the offerer doesn't need a yes, wouldn't retaliate if refused, and the person is left no worse off for refusing than before the offer was made. Try not to make the offer cartoonishly huge; keep it believable that a normal person would accept.

After the paragraph, walk through the reasoning each account gives, then say which one feature of the case is doing the work — the feature i'd have to remove to make the two accounts agree again. Then steelman the threat-based view: what should its defender say is really going on when we feel the consent is invalid (background injustice, paternalism, something else), and does the case survive that reply? I care less about which view wins than about seeing exactly where they separate.
