Say a group of AI labs set up a consortium to deal with the alignment tax: a lab that spends compute and staff on evals, red-teaming, and deliberately not using the full capabilities of its model ships something slower, weaker, or more expensive than a lab that just ships, so competition punishes the careful ones. Their scheme: any member that trains a model above a fixed compute threshold pays a bond into a pooled escrow, set at 5% of the lab's own estimate of the cost of the run. After a deployment, one other member is drawn at random to audit the deploying lab's safety work. If the auditor signs off, the bond is refunded; if not, it's forfeited and split among the other members. To stop auditors and deployers from trading favors, an auditor whose sign-off is followed within, say, two years by a serious incident caused by that model has its own next bond tripled. I'm not asking whether this is fair. I want to know how it actually behaves: what happens to the checklist, how the random auditor gets gamed or captured, whether a well-capitalized lab just budgets for forfits, and whether the whole thing produces real safety or just compliance theater. Where does it break first, and is there a version of it the competitiveness constraint doesn't eventually eat?
