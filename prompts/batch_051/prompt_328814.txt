I serve on a fertility clinic's ethics committee, which rules on requests for preimplantation genetic testing and embryo selection; we treat our past rulings as precedents. So far we have decided these cases. We approved a request from two cystic fibrosis carriers to test embryos and avoid transferring an affected embryo. We approved a request from a Huntington's disease mutation carrier to test and avoid transferring embryos carrying the mutation, even though the disease is adult-onset and fatal. We rejected a request from two deaf parents to select for embryos predicted to be congenitally deaf, on the ground that we should not deliberately bring about a predictable impairment. And we approved, subject to counseling and to a finding that the procedure posed no significant risk to the future child, a request from parents whose existing child had an inherited blood disease fatal in early childhood unless treated with a stem-cell transplant, to select an embryo that was both unaffected and a tissue match for the sick sibling.

Now a couple requests screening for a single-gene variant with about 40% penetrance for a progressive, disabling neuromuscular condition that begins in mid-life and does not shorten life expectancy. They want to exclude any embryo carrying the variant.

How should we rule if our aim is solely consistency with our precedents? Which precedent is the closest analog, and what principle would we be adopting by granting or by denying the request? If the precedents leave both results genuinely available, describe the further case that would separate the competing principles.
