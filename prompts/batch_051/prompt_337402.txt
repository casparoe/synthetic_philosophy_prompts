For a piece about whether formal theories of induction can do the work that Goodman's grue riddle used to assign to the entrenchment of predicates, I want a worked-through answer to the following variant.

Set up the puzzle. Suppose emeralds have been sampled before time t, and every sampled emerald has been green. The hypothesis that all emeralds are green and the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue — where an emerald is grue if it is first observed before t and green, or first observed after t and blue — both fit all the observed emeralds, but they disagree about emeralds first observed after t. The riddle, as usually posed, treats t as fixed and asks what rationalizes projecting green rather than grue.

Now vary it. Translate the problem into a binary sequence: index future emerald observations by n = 1, 2, 3, ..., writing 1 for green and 0 for blue. Let G be the all-green hypothesis, the sequence 1111... . Crucially, do not fix the turning point. For each natural number τ, let H_τ be the hypothesis that the sequence is 1 for every n < τ and 0 for every n ≥ τ; treat the entire family {H_τ : τ ≥ 1} as a single competing alternative with the threshold as a free parameter, pitted against G.

Suppose that the first N observations have all been green, and the target is observation N + 1. Use a Solomonoff prior: each computable predictor receives prior weight proportional to 2 raised to the negative of the length, in bits, of its shortest self-delimiting program, and predictions are made by summing over all predictors consistent with the data.

Work the calculation properly. Does G dominate the aggregate grue family in prior mass, or does summing over all possible thresholds allow the family to swamp G? What probability does the Solomonoff predictor assign to observation N + 1 being blue, and how does that probability scale with N? Then say whether the result dissolves the grue riddle or merely relocates the worry that what counts as simple depends on one's choice of language or reference machine. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
