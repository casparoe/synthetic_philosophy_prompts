On a forum I read, proposals to replace or supplement universal suffrage with epistocratic arrangements keep getting met with the same reply: "one person, one vote." The two proposals that draw this response most often are plural voting, where citizens can earn extra votes by passing a basic political-knowledge test, and a sortition-based citizens' council with veto power over the legislature. The slogan is being used to dismiss both at once, as if it named a single principle that rules them out. It seems to me it could instead be making several distinct claims — about equal moral standing, equal influence, equal competence, the distribution of political power, or the bare mechanics of counting ballots — and only some of those claims would actually cut against either proposal. Give me a numbered list of exactly five distinct precise readings of "one person, one vote," ordered from most platitudinous to most substantive; for each, state in a sentence or two whether the reading is defensible and whether it rules out plural voting, the council veto, both, or neither. No introduction or conclusion.
