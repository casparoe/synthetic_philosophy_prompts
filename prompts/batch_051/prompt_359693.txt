I help run a rationality meetup — there's usually 15 to 20 people, a mix of longtime LW readers and new folks, one hour. I want an activity that gets them to feel the pull of updateless decision theory before they hear the name, ideally by putting them in a one-shot version of the counterfactual mugging (Omega flips a coin; in the heads branch you get paid, in the tails branch you're asked to pay, and the heads payment happened only because Omega predicted you'd pay in the tails case — so when you're actually in the tails case, paying has no causal effect on anything you receive) or Parfit's hitchhiker (you're already rescued or already dropped off; paying now can't cause the rescue). But I want the choice to be real — points or candy stakes, a physically flipped coin, something they actually have to do — not just a story we read out loud. The rules should be arranged so people walk into the CDT move on their own: once the coin is tails or once they're rescued, refusing looks strictly better, so they refuse; then they get to see what refusing cost them in the branch that's already settled. It shouldn't collapse into reputation, punishment in later rounds, or an iterated game — one-shot, and the other branch has to be genuinely counterfactual rather than a future round of the same game. Give me concrete rules, materials, a timeline for the hour, and a debrief that maps what happened onto UDT versus CDT and EDT, and that handles the obvious objection: why not just precommit, and if I can't bind myself, why would I ever hand over the money after the fact. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about what a room full of humans can't reproduce about Omega or the setup — the perfect prediction, the literal other branch — and what the facilitator has to supply. If you can keep people from knowing it's a decision-theory night until the debrief, do that; half the fun is someone muttering that this is just a game for suckers right before the reveal.
