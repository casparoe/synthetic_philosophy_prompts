I coordinate mother-tongue literacy projects, so a few times a year I'm the one who has to mediate when we design an alphabet for a language that's never been written, and a bad alphabet can get abandoned or set literacy back years. The phonologist wants one letter per phoneme, the community wants the spelling too look like the national language or to keep word stems spelled the same in every context, and the teachers want as few diacritics as possible, and I have to get a commitee to agree in a few meetings. I need a reusable procedure for deciding how phonemic a script should be: which sound contrasts get their own letter, whether to mark tone, and when to spell the underlying form rather than the surface pronunciation. It should be a set of questions in order, with what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision to the community because it's a values call rather than a linguistics fact. Then run it on two cases: a language with fully contrastive tone where readers tend to skip the tone marks anyway, and a language where two sounds are distinct in careful speech but completely predictable in context so one letter would be phonemically enough. I'm expecting the procedure to sometimes give me an answer the community or my own colleagues won't like, so tell me where that will happen, what the procedure can't settle, and which argument or piece of evidence would change your mind about the main recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. And please write it as continuous prose like an email, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables.
