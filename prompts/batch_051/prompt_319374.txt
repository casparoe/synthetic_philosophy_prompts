I run a small civil-liberties discussion group, and two members have been arguing for the better part of a year about how much citizens should hide from the state. One holds a data-minimization view (individuals should by default keep as much as possible illegible to the state, roughly the German instinct), and the other holds a public-legibility view (we should accept, even help design, visible-to-the-state arrangements when they buy real public goods, along the lines of some social-credit proposals in China). They've agreed to do an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to write the document they'd produce. Structure it as a numbered list of claims both can actually sign -- about state data collection, the costs of legibility, and how much individual hiding genuinely limits state power -- followed by a table where the residual disagreement is stated first in neutral terms and then in each side's preferred wording. Pick what you think the two or three real cruxes are (my guesses: whether defaults do most of the moral work, and whether minimizing your own data just shifts surveillance onto neighbors or third parties), and for each crux give a concrete case or policy tweak both sides would accept as a genuine test, with what each predicts would happen. Flag any part of the fight you think is mostly verbal.
