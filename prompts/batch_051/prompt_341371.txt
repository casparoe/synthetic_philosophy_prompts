I'm a retired biochemist and I used to think priority was simple: the discoverer of a result is whoever got there first, had actual evidence for it, and put it somewhere the field could find it. Lately I've been reading about Stigler's law of eponymy—the claim that no scientific discovery gets named after its original discoverer—and it's got me going around in circles (its a made-up case, so don't worry about the details being real). Jenkins isolates a compound, publishes the structure and some weak antibacterial data in a specialist journal, but he gets the mechanism wrong and nobody follows it up. Ten years later Okafor, unaware of Jenkins, isolates the same compound, works out the actual mechanism, and introduces it as okaformin; the field, the textbooks, and the eponym all credit Okafor. Go through my three criteria one by one and tell me whether Jenkins or Okafor discovered it. Then say whether "discovery" needs community uptake built into the criteria, or whether priority really is just temporal and I should just live with how badly that clashes with the way scientists talk. Please keep clear what's settled among philosophers and historians of science and what's still genuinely contested—don't present a contested view as the consensus.
