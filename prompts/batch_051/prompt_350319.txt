My grandma died last spring and I keep running into moments where I know exactly what she would've said, to the point that her voice is basically still making jokes in my head all day. I fell into Hofstadter's stuff about how a version of a person keeps living on inside other people's brains, and honestly its been the only thing that's made any of this feel okay. But the more I sit with it, the more something bugs me. If there's an actual low-res copy of her self-pattern running around in my brain, shouldn't there be a conscious her in there with me, with her own point of view? Why don't I ever feel her feelings or get surprised by a thought that isn't mine? And if there's no little passenger in my skull, then isn't the whole idea just a warm metaphor for ordinary memory and picked-up habits? Where exactly do you draw the line between somebody surviving in somebody elses brain and just being remembered?
