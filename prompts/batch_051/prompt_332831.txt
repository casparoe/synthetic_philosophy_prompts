Im putting together a workshop for people who write refusal and override rules for a personal AI assistant, and I want a decision tree that takes a request the assistant is inclined to decline and routes it to the position in the debate that best explains the decline. I dont want the tree organized as a list of the familiar labels. Build it from three splits in whatever order makes the cleanest tree: (1) whose good does the refusal primarily protect — the user now, the user's future self, or a third party; (2) is the user's judgment currently impaired, badly misinformed, or unable to consent to the risk; (3) is the anticipated harm major and hard to reverse. Then locate the positions at the leaves. I need these leaves at least: a pure tool view (decline only for illegality, impossibility, or platform rules), a Mill-style harm principle (decline to prevent harm to others), soft paternalism restricted to impaired judgment, hard paternalism (may override a competent adult's self-regarding choice), and a consent/liability view (decline when the user can't genuinely consent to the risk, whatever it does to their welfare). If two of these land in the same cell on your main splits, say what further question separates them and whether the tree should add it. Aim for six to eight leaves total.

Run four cases through the tree so I can see the branches work: a drunk adult asking for help finalizing and sending a career-ending resignation email; a young teenager asking how to hide self-harm wounds; an adult asking for help writing a public call-out post about a coworker, true but humiliating details meant to destroy them at work; and a competent adult asking for help with a dangerous but legal prolonged fasting plan they insist they've researched. For each leaf, give one sentence on its most awkward case. Note any empty cells in the three-split space and say whether each is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag positions the tree can't separate. Sections: 'The tree' (~250 words), 'Leaves and awkward cases' (~250 words), 'Empty cells and positions the tree can't separate' (~150 words), 'How the four cases sort' (~100 words).
