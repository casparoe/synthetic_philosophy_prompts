So my reading group has two people who've been going at it for a year about whether attributing beliefs to LLMs is literal or just a convenient way of talking. One of them (call her the realist) thinks that when a model is correctly described as believing that the pot is on the left, that's the same kind of real, graded, dispositional attribution we make of people — the fact that the model learned from text and wasn't 'trying' to represent the world doesn't matter. The other (call him the instrumentalist) thinks belief-talk about models is a calculating device for predicting outputs, like saying a thermostat wants the room warmer: it can be genuinely useful without picking out any real state of the system. They've both agreed that predictive usefulness alone doesn't settle the question, and they've rejected the idea that you need phenomenal consciousness or biology before you can even ask it.

They're going to produce the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration. Write that document for them. I'd like it to include:

1. A numbered list of claims both of them accept — including what they've already agreed about what the intentional stance is, what it does for them, and what sorts of evidence would be off-limits or question-begging.

2. A table of the remaining disagreements. For each disagreement, give the neutral wording, then the realist's own formulation of her side and the instrumentalist's own formulation of his side, making sure each would sign off on their version as not being a strawman. Include at least: (i) whether the success of belief attributions over a model's behavior is itself evidence that the model has beliefs; (ii) whether finding stable, manipulable internal encoding for proposition-like contents would settle the matter; and (iii) what role a model's own assertions about its beliefs should play.

3. For each crux, specify the concrete case or experiment both would agree bears on it — e.g., a text-trained model that, with no further training, runs a robot arm in a novel kitchen while we intervene on its internal activations; or a model trained to answer 'what do you believe?' whose behavioral predictions and verbal reports systematically diverge. For each one, record in advance what the realist predicts and what the instrumentalist predicts, in terms both would except as a fair statement of their own view.

The thing I care most about is that the agreed-claims section isn't vacuous and the two sides' formulations don't talk past each other — seperate where a genuine crux is from where they were just using the word 'belief' differently.
