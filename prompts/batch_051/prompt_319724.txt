I find that picture beautiful, because it explains the king-minus-man-plus-woman trick without me having to believe in a private dictionary, and it gives a clean answer to "which of these two words is closer in meaning?" But my hands don't believe it. "Odor" and "smell" are basically neighbors in any space I've played with, and near "reek" the model gives me "stink" and "stench"—fine. But on the page they do not behave like neighbors. "A reek of old milk" is doing something to the sentence that "a smell of old milk" is not, and its not just intensity, because "stench" is intense and I still would not swap it in. Each word has a direction, a pressure, an angle of attack on the reader, and I dont know how to read that off a point and its nearest neighbors.
