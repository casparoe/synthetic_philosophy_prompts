Okay, I need help naming a thing, if it even has a name. I spend too much time reading arguments online—a local politics listserv, some national stuff, my family's facebook wars—and there's a pattern I can't stop noticing.

The clearest example was a fight near me over putting a homeless shelter in a church parking lot. The shelter supporters said the neighbors who kept bringing up "traffic and safety" were just using a polite cover for not wanting poor people around; proof was that they hadn't said a peep about traffic when a day care was proposed in the same spot. The neighbors said the shelter people were the ones arguing in bad faith: most of them lived on the other side of town, they were performing compassion for their own social circle, and the proof was that they called the neighbors monsters instead of actually answering the traffic argument. Both sides pointed at the exact same public comments and said "see, they won't engage with what we actually said." Both sides were dead certain.

I've seen the exact same structure in big political fights online. Each side is sure the other side isn't sincere—they're trolling, or performing for their own crowd, or repeating talking points because they've already picked a team, not because they looked at anything. Each side is just as sure that their own side's stated reasons are the real reasons, even if the facts turn out wrong. People wave at echo chambers here, but that can't be the whole story: in the parking lot fight everyone was reading the same comments, and online the two sides are often quoting the same screenshots at each other.

The symmetry is the weird part to me. Each side says "our side is honest and their side is performing," and each side's evidence is the other side's evidence with the names swapped: they ignored our actual argument, they quote us out of context, they never conceded even one small point, so they were never open to being convinced. Each side has a whole story about the other side's motives—they're paid, or brainwashed by their media, or clout-chasing—while their own anger is just the reasonable reaction of decent people being lied about.

I know words like "bad faith," "hypocrisy," and something vague about everyone thinking they're less biased than everyone else. None of those feel exactly right, because they mostly describe one side doing something wrong, not the locked symmetrical version where both sides do the identical thing to each other and both feel completely justified. So: does this symmetrical bad-faith attribution have its own name? And if it does, I want more than the label—I want the carving. Is this one phenomenon or a few different things that happen to look alike (a bias in how we read strangers over text, a structural feature of anonymous arguments, a rational response to actual trolling)? Where are its edges—what if only one side does it, or the two sides aren't equally loud, or one side actually is a coordinated campaign? And the question I keep circling: from inside one of the two groups, is there any non-question-begging way to tell wether your side really is more good-faith than the other, or does the symmetry make that impossible, and what should that do to how we talk about arguing "in good faith"?
