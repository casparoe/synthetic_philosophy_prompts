I've been going back and forth on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I want to stop evaluating proposals one by one until I have a proper statement of what a proposal would actually have to accomplish. The problem, as I understand it, is that pleasant experiences seem to lack any single obvious common ingredient: the sensory pleasure of a hot shower, the intellectual pleasure of seeing a proof come together, the cool relief when a feared outcome fails to materialize, pleasure taken in someone else's deserved misfortune, the fact that even a sensation like pain can under some conditions be experienced as pleasant, and the mild glow of a familiar song are all counted as pleasant, yet they feel different, motivate differently, and sit differently in time. A hedonist about well-being needs these to be instances of one thing — one feature that makes an experience pleasant and that bears the value hedonism assigns to it.

Before I read any more theories, lay out the desiderata: what any adequate account of the common quality of pleasant experience has to be able to do. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable. In particular, say whether any pair of the desiderata are plausibly jointly unsatisfiable, since that would be a result about the problem itself rather than about any particular theory, and say which requirement does the most work in excluding candidate views. I also want the list to respect distinctions I keep seeing collapse: pleasure versus the mere absence of pain, liking versus wanting, felt valence versus a judgment that something is good, and occurrent pleasure versus background mood. Please fix the purpose explicitly, since adequacy is relative to it: I mean a theory good enough to do the work hedonism needs it to do in a theory of well-being — to be the thing whose presence in a life makes that life go better.

Finally, score three families of view briefly against the list — a felt-quality account, an attitudinal account, and a functional or valence account — but keep the weight of your answer on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on the contenders.
