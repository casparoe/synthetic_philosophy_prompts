"George Eliot lost her evangelical faith as a young woman, translated Strauss and Feuerbach, and became a strict atheist. For her, religion was just a projection of human needs onto a nonexistent God, so all religious language is really disguised talk about human feelings. She treated religion as a stage to be outgrown, which is why her novels keep showing devout people as either self-deceiving or hypocrites. Her positive view—the religion of humanity—was an attempt to keep the emotional consolations of faith, the meaning and community and moral seriousness, without the theology. But once you've done that, you have to admit morality has no objective basis; it's just human feeling all the way down."
