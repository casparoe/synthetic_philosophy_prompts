I am a mathematician, and my spouse is a biologist who works on species taxonomy. For the past month our arguments at home keep converging on the same question in different vocabularies, and I want to know whether the convergence is real. I have been teaching the construction of the real numbers and trying to make sense of the claim that the continuum is discovered rather than defined. One wing of my department treats the real numbers as a definitional achievement: complete the rationals, and you have them. Another treats the construction as locating a structure that was already there. A third says that once the continuum hypothesis is independent of the standard axioms, there is no single continuum to describe, only different continua in different models. Meanwhile my spouse has been reading the species debate in biology, which runs on the same tracks: species are out there before any taxonomic criterion; species are artifacts of our sorting conventions; or there is no single species category, because different operational criteria carve different groups.

I suspect this is more than a shared slogan, but I also suspect the word defined is doing different work on each side, and I cannot yet make the correspondence precise. Is this parallel deep, or merely the shared skeleton of any realism/anti-realism dispute with the labels changed? I am not asking for a survey of either debate. Give me the exact correspondence: which position maps to which, where the mapping starts to slip, and whether anything on the biological side corresponds to the independence of the continuum hypothesis. State what would have to be true on both sides for an argument to transfer — for example, if species pluralism is correct, should I become a pluralist about the continuum, and what feature of the mathematical case could block that inference? And give me the sharpest disanalogy, including at least one prediction the parallel seems to license that is false in one of the two domains.
