A historian I know trots out the same analogy whenever anyone gives her guff about doing microhistory instead of "proper" political history. She says the difference between studying one village's burial register and studying the rise of the modern state is just the difference between a microscope and a map: different instruments for different scales, and nobody argues about whether a microscope or a map is "better." She uses it to wave off the charge that history from below just piles up unrepresentative anecdotes. I've started to think the analogy is carrying more weight than it can bear. Can you audit it? Give me the correspondence table first — what's the specimen, the magnification, the map's projection, the terrain being mapped, the scale bar — then the places where the mapping snaps, and then say whether her conclusion, that micro and macro history are just two scales and neither is worse than the other, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If there's a better analogy for what microhistory actually does, I'd take that too.
