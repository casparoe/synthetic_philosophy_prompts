I cover science and ideas for a general audience, and I'm writing a peice on AI companion apps. There's a pattern I keep running into that I can't find a clean name for. The users I interview aren't confused about what they're dealing with: they'll tell me, accurately, that the bot is a stateless language model and every tender message is a statistical guess. Then a beat later the same person will talk about the model update that changed their companion as 'killing' her, or will describe the history they 'built together' as something the bot itself couldn't actually carry. The two stances flip back and forth depending on what I ask, and the grief over an update seems real even while they insist they know no one died. It's not exactly parasocial, because these users hold no illusion that the bot has a mind; and it's not exactly how we relate to a fictional character, because the attachment comes with demands and a sense of betrayal. Does this configuration have a name, and if nothing fits, can you give it one? I don't care much about the label—I need the carving: what the second stance is, what it isn't, whether it's even fair to call it a mistake, and what a company owes someone whose grief is real but aimed at a bot they know is a bot.
