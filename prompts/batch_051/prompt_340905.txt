As I understand Hobbes, geometry is a demonstrable science for a special reason: we are the makers of its objects. We stipulate the definitions and lay down the constructions of the line, circle, and square ourselves, so the truths we derive from them are known with certainty. Natural philosophy, by contrast, deals with bodies whose causes we did not make, so it can only be conjectural. On this criterion, knowledge tracks authorship.

The same line seems to run through formal methods in computer science: a system whose specification and implementation are yours can be reasoned about by proof, while a black-box system — a trained model, a legacy codebase, a vendor's closed component — can only be tested empirically, because you did not construct the thing whose behavior you want to know. Map Hobbes's terms onto the software case and they line up: definitions are the specification, construction is the implementation, and conjectural physics is black-box testing.

But I am suspicious of my own mapping. In software, both terms of the contrast are human artifacts; in Hobbes, the contrast is between our artifacts and nature. And formal verification usually proves things about a model of a system, not the running system itself, so the proof can float free of what was actually built — which sounds more like the gap Hobbes wanted to deny than the one he wanted to explain. Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is genuine, does anything transfer — for example, does the history of verification and false proofs tell us anything about why Hobbes remained so stubbornly confident, in the face of refutation, that he had squared the circle? Please work through the mapping, say how confident you are in your verdict, and single out the point you are least sure of.
