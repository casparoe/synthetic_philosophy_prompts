Draft the joint statement that an aggregationist and an anti-aggregationist about harm could publish together on the torture-versus-dust-specks problem. The aggregationist holds that, in principle, a sufficiently large number of extremely mild harms — each no worse than a dust speck in an eye — can together outweigh one person's fifty years of torture. The anti-aggregationist denies this: harms below some threshold of seriousness cannot be summed across persons, so no number of speck-level harms can outweigh the torture.

The document should contain three parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: what the thought experiment is and is not stipulating; the points both sides accept about the separateness of persons, the difference between aggregating within one life and across many lives, and what each side means by outweighing. Second, a disagreements table (four to six rows) giving the precise residual differences, each side's view in wording that side would accept, and, for each row, the case or objection each side privately finds most troubling for its own view. Third, a list of two to four cruxes — sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side — with each side's prediction recorded and a description of the test or argument both agree would bear on the crux. The cruxes should engage at least some of these variants: repeating the speck many times in one person rather than in many distinct persons; specks just below the threshold of conscious notice; harms of intermediate size; and a forced choice repeated over many iterations.

Close by saying which parts of the dispute you judge to be verbal rather than substantive, and why. Keep the document under 900 words, written in American English.
