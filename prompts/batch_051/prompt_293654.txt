I know the broad shape of Lewis's account from reading around: a convention is a regularity R in a population such that nearly everyone conforms to R, everyone prefers to conform given they expect others to conform, there's at least one alternative R' that could have served roughly as well, and all of that is common knowledge, with precedent usually explaining how the group locked into R rather than R'. What I want you to do is channel Lewis. Imagine he's been brought back and shown a world where LLMs are finetuned by RLHF to obey particular conversational norms, where models from different labs talk to each other and quickly stabilize on new ways of addressing each other, and where a human and a chatbot develop a private shorthand over a long thread. Predict, as specifically as you can, what Lewis would say about each of these cases: does it count as a convention by his criteria or not? Where would he most likely relax his account (the common knowledge requirement? the boundaries of the population? the intentionality?) and where would he dig in and say no, that's just correlated output, not a convention? I'm especially curious what he'd make of the fact that the "common knowledge" in these systems is partly engineered through system prompts and reward models rather than arising from a shared history of precedent. Give me your best Lewis prediction, and be explicit about which parts are straight Lewis and which parts are you stretching him.
