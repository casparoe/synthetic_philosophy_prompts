So I've been going down a panpsychism rabbit hole lately because a friend of mine has gotten really into the idea that consciousness goes "all the way down" — not just in brains but in fundamental particles, like electrons and quarks have some kind of experience. I'm still not sold on the metaphysics, but what's actually been keeping me up is the ethics. If electrons and quarks genuinely have experience, then doesn't every physical interaction become a moral event? Boiling water would mean jostling trillions of tiny subjects; sitting on a wooden chair would mean compressing whatever it is the wood's particles are feeling; even the chemical reactions keeping me alive would be constantly rearranging little experiencers. That starts to look like a moral catastrophe: either you end up paralyzed because every action causes suffering somewhere, or you have to declare that micro-level suffering doesn't count, which feels like it defeats the whole point of taking consciousness seriously in the first place.

So I guess my question is whether panpsychists have some principled way to avoid the "everything we do is a moral disaster" conclusion, or if that's just a bullet they've quietly bitten. I genuinely can't tell if I've spotted a real problem with the view or if I'm misunderstanding what panpsychists even mean by "experience" at the level of an electron — like maybe they're using the word in a way that doesn't include the kind of experience that could hurt.
