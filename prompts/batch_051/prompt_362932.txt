Consider the doing/allowing distinction as provisionally captured by three criteria. Source: in doing, the agent's bodily movement is the proximate source of the lethal process; in allowing, the lethal process has an independent source. Threat: doing involves initiating or redirecting a threat, whereas allowing involves failing to block an existing threat. Withdrawal: deliberately ceasing, or disabling, aid that is holding death back counts as doing rather than allowing.

Now a case. Dr. K is the overnight clinician for a patient kept alive only by a ventilator. Wanting the patient dead, she reprograms the ventilator so that it will not issue the hourly reminder prompting her to renew the ventilation order. She does nothing further; the hour passes, no reminder sounds, no renewal is entered, the ventilator stops by default, and the patient dies of respiratory failure. The question concerns the causal structure of the death, not Dr. K's blameworthiness.

Does Dr. K kill the patient or let the patient die, on these criteria? Work through each criterion, identify where they conflict or underdetermine the answer, and say whether this case shows that some criterion should be revised or that the doing/allowing distinction is not a single natural boundary.
