Below is an argument I keep returning to about whether a vague predicate like "red" can be made to sit comfortably with ordinary classical logic. I would like a rigorous diagnosis. Please tell me whether the argument is valid; if it is not valid, identify the exact step that fails and explain why; if it is valid, say what the argument really forces one to give up, and whether you think that price is worth paying.

Imagine a row of one thousand paint samples. Sample 1 is unambiguously red. Sample 1000 is unambiguously not red. Each adjacent pair of samples is so close in hue that no one can visually tell one sample from its immediate neighbor. Let R(n) stand for "sample n is red." Consider this argument:

(1) R(1).
(2) It is not the case that R(1000).
(3) If "red" is vague, then there is no sharp boundary in the row: there is no n between 1 and 999 such that R(n) and not R(n+1).
(4) "Red" is vague.
(5) Therefore, there is no n between 1 and 999 such that R(n) and not R(n+1).
(6) From (5), for any n between 1 and 999, it is not the case that (R(n) and not R(n+1)). In classical logic, "not (R(n) and not R(n+1))" is equivalent to "if R(n), then R(n+1)." Hence, for every n from 1 to 999, if R(n) then R(n+1).
(7) Starting from R(1), and applying the conditional in (6) successively nine hundred and ninety-nine times, we arrive at R(1000).
(8) This contradicts (2).
(9) Therefore, by reductio, at least one of (1), (2), (3), (4), or the classical equivalence relied on in (6) must be abandoned. Since (1) and (2) are stipulations of the example and (4) is hard to deny, the serious options appear to be these: either reject (3) and, keeping the classical equivalence, accept that there is a sharp dividing line in the row after all, even though no one could ever say where it is; or reject the classical equivalence in (6), and with it abandon the idea that classical logic governs vague discourse unchanged.

Where, if anywhere, does this argument go wrong? Is the pressure in (9) genuine, and if so, which of the two serious options should a defender of vagueness take?
