As part of my doctoral research on intergenerational justice, I am compiling objections to the view that a positive pure rate of time preference—the practice of discounting the wellbeing of future people solely because their lives or experiences occur later in time—should play a role in long-run social decision-making. My list so far contains four objections. First, the temporal impartiality objection: a bare difference in temporal position between otherwise similar lives is not a morally relevant difference, so weighting the nearer life more heavily is arbitrary. Second, the objection associated with Ramsey, that pure discounting is ethically indefensible because it reflects a limitation in our imaginative or sympathetic reach toward distant future persons rather than any consideration that would survive reflection. Third, the conflation objection: much of what is defended in policy as pure time preference is really discounting for expected growth in consumption, the opportunity cost of capital, or the probability that the relevant future never arrives, none of which constitutes pure time preference. Fourth, the revealed-preference objection: observed individual impatience and market interest rates cannot by themselves justify an intergenerational discount rate, since they incorporate private mortality risk, myopia, and coordination failures rather than a considered judgement about the equal standing of future generations.

I would like one further objection that is genuinely distinct from these four, not a rewording, special case, or combination of them. In particular, I am looking for an objection that operates at the level of the decision-theoretic or formal properties of intertemporal discounting, rather than through considerations of impartiality, moral psychology, or the source of the rate. State the objection precisely, and indicate whether it tells against any positive pure rate of time preference or only against particular functional forms of the discount function. Please write your answer in British English.
