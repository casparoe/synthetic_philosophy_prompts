In a seminar on well-being and population ethics, I have settled—tentatively and with some unease—on a view I have been calling lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism, and I want an honest map of how exposed it is rather than a defense of it. The view, stated precisely: intrinsic value attaches only to phenomenal states of pain and pleasure. There is a threshold on the pain-intensity scale such that any outcome containing at least one episode of pain at or above that threshold is worse than any finite outcome containing no such episode, no matter how much below-threshold pain, or how much positive pleasure, the alternative contains. When two outcomes both contain threshold-level pain, the worse one is the one with the more severe, or the more numerous, such episodes. Only where no threshold-level pain is present do pain and pleasure trade off in the ordinary commensurable way, with pain weighted more heavily than pleasure of equal intensity but compensable by enough pleasure.

What I would like is not a bibliography but a map of what would count against this view, sorted by decisiveness. Which cases, arguments, intuitions, or formal results should most unsettle me? Three families seem relevant, though I do not want the answer confined to them: objections to the threshold itself—its placement, its vagueness, and the discontinuity it imposes on an apparently smooth intensity scale; objections to the lexical structure—for example, that it yields absolute-priority verdicts we are unwilling to accept, or distorts reasoning under small probabilities of catastrophe, or violates plausible continuity and separability principles; and objections to the substrate—that pleasure and pain may not lie on a common scale or be commensurable below the threshold in the way the view assumes. More important than those labels is the calibration: which of these would a careful defender concede are genuine costs, which would they dismiss as beside the point, and which would force abandoning the view rather than amending it? I would also like to know whether the position as I have stated it has already been insulated by its qualifications so thoroughly that no case could move it, and whether that insulation is a defect in a view of this kind. The objection I find hardest to set aside is the discontinuity worry, so tell me what a defender's strongest reply to it actually is, and whether accepting that reply means revising the view or simply biting a bullet.
