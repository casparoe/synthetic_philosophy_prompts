I accept, as a working reconstruction of Euclid's arithmetic, these claims: (1) a unit is what makes a thing count as one rather than many; (2) a number is a collection of units; (3) one is not a collection of units, since it is exactly one. From these I infer: (4) one is not a number. I also accept the obvious modern claims: (5) the natural numbers are 1, 2, 3 and so on; (6) one is a natural number. From these I infer: (7) one is a number. So I seem forced to conclude: (8) one is both a number and not a number.

I do not see how to reject any of (1) through (3) without giving up Euclid's arithmetic, and I do not see how to reject (5) or (6) without giving up ordinary arithmetic. So either one of those claims is false on the best reading, or one of the inferences is bad, or the whole argument turns on something subtler.
