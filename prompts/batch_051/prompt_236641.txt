I am a novelist working on a book in which a charismatic tutor trains students in rational decision-making by having them drill a fixed repertoire of cognitive katas: stating one’s prior before updating, listing two alternative hypotheses, performing a pre-mortem, checking for base-rate neglect, and so on. I would like you to argue for the position that this martial-arts model of rationality training is fundamentally mistaken. The strongest form of the opposing view, which you should engage seriously, is that there are isolable reasoning moves that can be practiced in decontextualized form and later applied across domains. In defending the negative position, please address the gap between near transfer on structurally similar problems and far transfer to novel contexts, and take up the objection that the martial-arts analogy misclassifies rational skill as something like procedural memory when it is in fact a domain-sensitive ability to notice when a procedure applies at all. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
