I am a novelist developing the premise for a speculative novel. The divergence is precise, and I want the subsequent world to be governed by mechanisms rather than authorial convenience. On a single identifiable night in the present-day world, worldwide, every mentally competent adult undergoes a change in how their own future well-being matters to them: they cease to discount future pleasures and pains merely because those experiences lie in the future. From then on, a unit of personal well-being twenty years from now carries the same present motivational and emotional weight as the same unit occurring now, except that the person still discounts for the probability that the event will not happen or that death will intervene. The change is purely temporal; it does not strengthen memory, foresight, or self-control, and it does not directly alter how much anyone cares about the future well-being of other people. People notice the change gradually, and for some months most of them distrust their own new reactions.

The novel follows the next thirty years. Before I commit to events, I want a reasoned prediction of where the pressure would fall. Work through mechanisms rather than plot. How would such people's choices about saving and borrowing, health and addiction, education and career timing, marriage, childbearing, and crime move, and in which direction? What would happen to the price of deferred gratification, to the structure of contracts and insurance, and to the willingness of voters to support long-lived public projects such as climate mitigation, infrastructure, and basic research? For each predicted effect, identify the mechanism by which the change produces it, say whether the effect is robust or whether it depends on how quickly people learn to trust the new state and how institutions adapt, and state what would have to be true for the effect to be absent or reversed. Where your confidence is low, say why: which link in the causal chain is weakest, and what must be assumed about human motivation and institutional response for that prediction to hold.
