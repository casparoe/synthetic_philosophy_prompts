Karl Popper argues in the Preface to *The Poverty of Historicism* that human history is strongly influenced by the growth of human knowledge, that we cannot in principle predict the future growth of knowledge, and that therefore we cannot predict the future course of history; consequently, there cannot be laws of historical development in the sense historicists hoped for. I am inclined to accept this as a decisive constraint on what the social sciences can aim at. What would count against it? I want a map of the position's exposure: the observations, conceptual distinctions, formal results, or historical cases that should most move me away from it, sorted by how decisive each would be. In particular, assess whether the premise about the unpredictability of knowledge can fail without simply invoking a trivial or definitional reading of prediction, and say whether the thesis's defenders have qualified it into unfalsifiability—and, if so, whether that is a problem for a claim of this kind.
