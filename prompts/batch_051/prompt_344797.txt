The proposal is a labeling scheme. Every farm that sells salmon, trout, or shrimp in the country has to publish a single "welfare score" from 0 to 100 on the package, updated daily. The score is computed automatically from four cheap seperate measurements: average stocking density over the last 24 hours, minimum dissolved oxygen over the last 24 hours, peak ammonia over the last 24 hours, and whether the slaughter line uses electrical stunning, which is worth a flat 25 points (so a farm that doesnt stun can still reach 75). The sensors are owned and placed by the farms, but the data streams to a government portal, and the portal picks one random 10-minute window from each farm's daily logs and computes the score from just that window. A human inspector visits once a year, with 48 hours notice, to check the sensors are positioned where the rules say and that the stunner is actually used on every animal.
