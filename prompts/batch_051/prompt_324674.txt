I have a rule with animals: whenever a behavior can be explained without saying the animal thinks or feels, I accept that explanation. My dog looks guilty after chewing up the couch, and I tell myself he's just reading my anger, not feeling guilt; a crow works through a puzzle step by step, and I'd rather call it instinct plus trial and error than planning. But I've started to notice the rule has an escape hatch — if the behavior gets too clever I just say we haven't found the mechanical story yet, which means the rule can never be wrong. And I dont hold humans to anything like this. Is this rule defensible, or is it just my dim view of animal minds pretending to be a methodological principle? If it isn't, is there a version that keeps me from seeing minds in every earthworm without automatically ruling out the crow?
