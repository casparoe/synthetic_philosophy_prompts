Write a deadpan, ironic FAQ of roughly 600 words from the fictional Bureau of Confessional Neutrality of a country that has an established national church, collects a separate church tax, funds denominational schools and houses of worship from public revenue, and yet describes itself as the most secular state in Europe. The FAQ should answer questions from puzzled citizens — for instance, why public money maintains churches, why the official faith receives automatic representation in state bodies, and whether the state has, in effect, chosen a religion. Each answer should defend the arrangement with a recognizable argument from the actual repertoire of defenders of establishment and church taxes: historical continuity, national identity, administrative convenience, the claim that a state-supervised church is safer than free religious competition, or the claim that no one is forced to remain a member. A reader familiar with how established churches and church taxes are justified in countries like England or Germany should recognize the logic immediately, but the country itself is invented.

The voice should be serene, bureaucratic, and self-congratulating, with no winking or explicit editorializing; the reader should feel the gap between these defenses and the bureau's claim of perfect neutrality widen until it cannot be ignored. The humor must come from the content rather than the tone, and the parody should be fair rather than cartoonish, exaggerating a real feature of the view instead of inventing one. The target is the proposition that a state can financially privilege one religion while remaining neutral — not any particular country, denomination, or person.
