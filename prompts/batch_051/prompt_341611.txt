Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions on the question of who decides what Scripture means. Call the first the living-interpreter position: the text alone underdetermines its own application, so a stable community requires an authorized living interpreter whose rulings terminate the regress of interpretation, whereas private judgment simply replaces one judge with many. Call the second the perspicuity position: Scripture is sufficiently clear on the matters it treats as essential, so no additional authoritative interpreter is required, and adding one merely relocates the regress to the interpreter rather than resolving it.

The deliverable should open with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering at least the reality of pervasive interpretive disagreement, the limits of appeals to plain meaning, and the distinction between a text's being clear in principle and its being clear to a particular reader under particular conditions. Then state the residual disagreements in a table, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view and of the opponent's. Finally, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions on which at least one side would change its view if answered. For each crux, specify a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, for example a short passage read independently by a hundred sincere, literate readers given the same hermeneutical rules, and record in advance what each side predicts and what pattern of results would count as damage to its own position. If any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, for instance over the terms clarity or authority, say which part and why.
