I have a standing rule in policy discussions: I will not endorse any change unless it is a strict Pareto improvement — at least one person is made better off and no one is made worse off. When someone argues that the winners could compensate the losers, or that the aggregate gain outweighs the aggregate loss, I discount the argument almost to zero unless the compensation is actually written into the policy, because I do not trust my own interpersonal comparisons of well-being. I admit this makes me conservative and status-quo-friendly, but it still feels safer than approving a balance of gains and losses I cannot honestly weigh; I used it recently against a road project that would save thousands of commuters time while a few households lost their quiet. Is this shortcut rationally defensible, and is there a version of it that keeps its discipline without silently enshrining the status quo?
