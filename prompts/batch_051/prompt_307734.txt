I have been trying to get clear about theories of humour, and I keep running into the same frustration. The standard families—superiority, incongruity, relief, and benign violation—each seem to proceed by selecting a joke or two that fits the favoured mechanism and then declaring the competition refuted, while the other families simply select different jokes and do the same. Before I read another theory, I want the specification up front: what exactly must an adequate theory of humour get right to count as a theory of humour at all, rather than a theory of one kind of joke? Please write the desiderata, with the case or consideration behind each one. The list should say what the theory is a theory of—amusement, the judgment that something is funny, laughter, or the social practice of joking—and it must not collapse those. It should cover the phenomena: the gap between laughter and funniness (tickling, nervous laughter, laughing gas, polite or affiliative laughter, and the reverse case of finding something funny without laughing); the sensitivity of the same joke to timing, audience, and whether it feels morally or temporally too close to a real harm; why explaining a joke tends to kill it while repeating one does not; puns and wordplay, schadenfreude and slapstick, children's rough-and-tumble play and being chased in a game, and the fact that laughter-like vocalizations show up in preverbal infants and in nonhuman primates in ways that look like play signals rather than responses to propositions. Then give me your judgment about which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of one theoretical camp, which single requirement is most damaging to the four standard theories and why, and whether any pair of requirements looks as though it cannot be jointly satisfied. I am not looking for a literature survey or anyone's bibliography, just the conceptual specification. Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter or an email: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
