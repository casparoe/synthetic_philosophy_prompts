my friend and I just spent the whole walk home arguing about gradual replacement uploads, and the fight isn’t really about tech. We agreed on the setup: one neuron at a time gets swapped for a chip that mimics its input and output, no behavioral gap, and the person reports being the same throughout. I said obviously you survive, because there’s an unbroken thread of psychological life. My friend said no, the person dies when the last biological neuron goes, because the thing that was you was a living organism, not a pattern. The weird part is we both looked at the same Locke passage and came away with opposite readings. In the Essay Concerning Human Understanding, Book II chapter XXVII, he describes a case where the same soul has two separate non-communicating streams of consciousness, a day-man and a night-man, and says we would count them as two persons even though there is only one soul. My friend thinks that is on his side, because it shows Locke cares about the underlying substance staying the same. I think it shows the opposite, that personhood floats free of whatever substance runs it. We couldn’t even settle what the passage was supposed to settle. So diagnose the actual disagreement here. Is one of us reading Locke wrong, or is the deeper fight about whether personal identity is a matter of psychology, of substance, or of what matters for survival? And if someone granted that gradual replacement preserves psychological continuity but also that the original organismal substance is gone, would the debate be over or would we just find another word to fight about?
