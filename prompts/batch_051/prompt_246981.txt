I have been trying to make sense of what Dennett, following Darwin and Turing, calls the strange inversion of reasoning, and I want a negotiated verdict rather than a lecture. Before Darwin, the natural way to account for any well-designed thing — an eye, a heart, a bird's nest — was to posit a designing mind that comprehended what the parts were for and arranged them accordingly. Darwin inverted the order of explanation: blind variation plus selection can produce craftsmanship-grade competence with no author who understands what it is doing. Turing performed the same inversion for thought: a machine can produce competent, even superhuman, behavior by executing rules it does not understand, which suggests that understanding need not sit in the driver's seat anywhere in an intelligent system. Generalized, this yields the picture of competence without comprehension: comprehension, the felt grasp of reasons and the flash of seeing why, is not the source of competence but a late-arriving product of underlying mindless competences, a kind of user interface evolved and installed by still more mindless processes. I find this picture theoretically beautiful: it is economical, it sweeps away homunculi and miracles, and it explains how understanding could arise in a mindless universe without sneaking understanding in at the start.

But I also have a firm, almost tactile intuition that my own comprehension is not idle machinery. When I work through a proof and see why a step is valid, or notice a contradiction in a plan before anyone else does, my grasping of the reason feels like the very thing that generates the next competent move. If comprehension were an after-the-fact gloss produced by lower-level competences, it should be doing no causal work — the system would run just the same if the gloss were absent — and yet the experience of steering by understanding, of being compelled by a reason rather than merely pushed by a cause, is one of the most solid things I know. I want you to argue this out rather than resolve it by fiat. Give me the strongest case for saying the intuition is a user illusion manufactured by introspection and should be discounted; the strongest case for saying it tracks something the inversion leaves out, so the theory must be amended to give comprehension real causal standing; and tell me which move you would ultimately make, what each move would cost, and whether there is a reading of real, or of comprehension, on which the Darwin–Turing inversion and my sense of understanding-as-cause both survive.
