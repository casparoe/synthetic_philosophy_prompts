I am a software engineer, fluent in the vocabulary of systems design—API contracts, single source of truth, caching and staleness, data leakage, train/serve skew, calibration, feedback loops—but only an amateur in formal epistemology. I have been trying to use the Principal Principle from the philosophy of probability as a design specification for a client that must combine its own local estimate with a probability returned by an external "chance oracle" service. As I understand it, the principle (David Lewis's, roughly stated) says that a rational agent's credence in a proposition, conditional on the proposition's objective chance being x and on any admissible evidence, should be x. The load-bearing notion is admissibility: evidence about the past is typically admissible, while evidence from after the relevant time—or evidence that already reveals how the chance process will turn out—is not. In my idiom, the oracle is authoritative only within a contract, and the client must not smuggle in features that the contract excludes; excluding look-ahead bias and label leakage is how I would naturally express excluding inadmissible evidence.

Please restate the Principal Principle, together with its surrounding apparatus—admissibility, the epistemic status of chance, and the problem of undermining futures, where a possible future, if it occurred, would show that the oracle's present probability assignment could not survive—in my vocabulary. Be faithful: do not quietly turn deference to chance into a merely statistical claim about unbiased estimates. Then give me the residue report. Specifically: what is the analogue, if any, of an undermining future in a system whose downstream actions, taken on the basis of the oracle's output, can change the very process the oracle is modeling? Does the philosophical principle already have an answer to that, or does the engineering picture expose a real gap? Which parts of the position cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all—say, claims about what makes a chance objective rather than merely well-calibrated—and are those parts load-bearing for the principle, or are they metaphysics I can safely ignore? I care at least as much about what the translation drops as about what it carries.
