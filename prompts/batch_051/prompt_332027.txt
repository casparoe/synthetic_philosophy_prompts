I am trying to work out whether I did something wrong, and a philosopher I read has left me more confused rather than less. Here is the situation.

I live on the ground floor of an apartment building. Last week, my upstairs neighbor, a woman I know fairly well, told me she was afraid of a former partner who had been violent toward her and was trying to find her. She said that if he ever came looking for her, she might need to be "not home." Yesterday evening, a man I did not know knocked on my door. He was agitated, and he asked me whether she was in the building. I strongly suspected this was the former partner. She had actually come down to my flat about half an hour earlier, and she was still there, but I told him she was not in and that I had not seen her. He checked the door again and left. She later thanked me profusely.

A friend has told me that I definitely did the right thing, because telling the truth would have exposed her to real danger. Another friend, who is more rigorous about these things, said that my lie was still a lie, and that I cannot simply treat her safety as the only value in play. He pointed me to Immanuel Kant's essay On a Supposed Right to Lie from Benevolent Motives. I read it, or at least I tried to.

As I understand the essay, Kant argues that the duty not to lie is unconditional: you may not lie even to a murderer who asks whether your friend is hidden in your house. His claim is not merely that one should avoid lying in most cases, but that even a lie told for the best possible motive wrongs humanity generally, because it corrupts the practice of truthful declaration on which rights themselves depend. I find this very hard to accept, but I cannot identify the exact point where his reasoning fails, if it does.

Please help me work through this. I want to know whether my reading of Kant is accurate enough, whether his argument actually applies to my situation, and whether the argument is sound. If it is not sound, I want to see the precise step where it breaks. I am less interested in whether I happen to feel bad than in whether I am in fact guilty of a wrong, and why.
