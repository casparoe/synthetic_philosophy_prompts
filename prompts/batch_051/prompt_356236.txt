I spent my career fitting models to data, and now that I've retired I've started treating people's moral verdicts as data. Here's a dataset I can't crack. My daughter-in-law is strikingly consistant about which lies to children she thinks are all right, but I can't state the rule. Her record: fine with Santa, the tooth fairy, and the Easter Bunny; fine with "we can't buy sweets because the shop is closed" when the shop wasn't closed; not fine with "the injection won't hurt"; not fine with "grandad has gone to sleep"; fine with telling a three-year-old that his lost teddy has "gone on an adventure"; not fine with "if you don't come now I'll leave you behind." Now she has to decide about three more: the family dog has died and she's considering "he went to live on a farm"; a grandparent has died and she's considering "people who die go to a better place"; and she's considering telling her son "your drawing is the best in the class" when she knows it isn't. What would she say about each if she stays consistent with herself, what are the candidate rules that fit the record so far, what's your best guess for each and why, and which further case would do the most to show which rule she's actually running? I'm less interested in parenting advice then in whether the rule has any real structure or is just a family resemblance.
