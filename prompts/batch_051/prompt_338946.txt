I've been trying to nail down what I actually mean by 'physical', since I call myself a physicalist but my gut goes different ways on cases. Electrons: physical. The electromagnetic field: physical, no hesitation. The wavefunction: physical, though it feels less concrete than an electron. Spacetime curvature: physical. A rock's center of mass: physical? I'd say yes, but its a point that isn't any particular atom, which feels off. Thermodynamic entropy: physical. Shannon information about a coin flip: not physical. Numbers: not physical. Possible worlds: not physical. Qualia—the way red looks from the inside—not physical, even though I'd also insist the world is entirely physical. My first-pass principle was 'physical = whatever our best physics quantifies over', but that seems to make Hilbert spaces and symmetry groups physical, and maybe numbers too if physics leans on them enough. Is there a cleaner principle that gets most of these, or do I have to drop some verdicts? And is this just Hempel's dilemma again—'physical' meaning current physics, so physicalism is almost certainly false, or some future completed physics, so the claim is empty? I half-suspect the whole question is confused, but what would your principle say about cases I didn't list, like laws of nature or virtual particles, and which of my verdicts does each horn force me to give up?
