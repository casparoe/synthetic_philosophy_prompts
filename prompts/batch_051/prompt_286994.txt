I've been keeping a running list of situations where somebody does the outward thing right and I still feel like something was owed to me that I didn't get — or the reverse, where nobody does anything for me and I still feel treated fine. Wondering if there's a single principle underneath.

A waiter takes my order, brings the food, says "enjoy" without eye contact: totally fine, nothing owed. A doctor gives me an accurate diagnosis and clear instructions, but I'm visibly scared and they talk in the same flat voice the whole time without ever acknowledging the fear: not fine, even though the medicine was correct. My partner after a fight says "I'm sorry you feel that way": I genuinely would have preferred silence, and I'm usually not a "ugh I hate that phrase" person. A coworker thanks me for covering his shift with a quick "thanks, appreciate it" without looking up from his laptop: fine. My brother argues politics with me, says "I see where you're coming from," and can restate my view accurately, including the part that annoys him most, before telling me why he disagrees: I feel more respected than if he'd just said "you're wrong," even though he didn't change his mind and didn't do anything for me. A friend remembers I don't eat meat and picks a restaurant accordingly, but when I'm telling him something that upset me he keeps glancing at his phone and says "uh huh" at the wrong moments: the considerate gesture doesn't cancel the tuning out, and the tuning out doesn't cancel the gesture — I'm grateful and a little hurt at the same time. A funeral director says "my deepest condolences" and "he was a wonderful man" in a smooth, obviously rehearsed way: not fine.

My best attempt at a principle is something like: the attitude you owe is the one the interaction is actually about, so a condolence is about registering the loss and a canned line fails, while a thanks is just small-favor bookkeeping and canned is fine. But I can't tell if that's a real principle or just my verdicts wearing nicer clothes, and I don't know what it says about the pair that bothers me most — the waiter (fine) and the doctor (not fine), who are both just doing thier jobs without attending to me as a person. Is there a single principle that gets all seven of these, or close, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a teacher who grades my kid fairly and gives accurate feedback but clearly thinks of my kid as a pile of skills and has no sense of who the kid is, versus a store clerk who says "have a nice day" on autopilot.
