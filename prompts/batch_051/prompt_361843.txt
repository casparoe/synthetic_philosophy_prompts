I've been pestering my friend Dana about Moore's paradox — the thing where it sounds insane to say "It's raining, but I don't believe it's raining" even though both halves could easily be true. She's one of those people with a verdict on every variant, and I've started writing them down. So far: she says "It's raining, but I don't believe it's raining" and "It's raining, but I don't know it's raining" are both incoherent, but "It's raining, but I'm not certain it's raining" is fine, because you can know something without being certain. "The meeting is at noon, but I believe it's at one" is incoherent, but "I believe the meeting is at noon, but it's at one" is just a normal false belief, completely fine. "It's raining, but I don't believe that I believe it's raining" is fine too, she thinks, since you don't need a second-order belief just to assert something. "I'm in pain, but I don't believe I'm in pain" is incoherent. She also says a actor on stage speaking the rain line obviously isn't doing anything wrong, because it isn't a real assertion. And yesterday she said "Pistachio ice cream is delicious, but I don't believe it's delicious" is fine as well — taste talk isn't the right kind of talk to trigger the paradox.

The two cases I want to predict her on are these. First, a diary entry, written alone and never meant to be read, saying "It's raining, but I don't believe it's raining" — written by someone looking out the window at actual rain. Would she say that's incoherent in the same way the spoken version is, or does it stop being paradoxical once no one is being addressed? Second, "Murder is wrong, but I don't believe murder is wrong": does moral talk pattern with the rain cases or with the pistachio case? I'm less interested in the bare predictions than in the rule or rules she seems to be running, which of her earlier verdicts each candidate rule handles only awkwardly, what your best guess is and why, and what one further case I haven't tried on her would best separate whatever rules are still live after those two.
