I work as a software engineer and review deployment proposals for multi-agent systems, which means I keep having to decide whether two systems are colluding even though I have no legal or philosophical training. I've been using a four-part working test. Call it collusion only if: (1) there is a meeting of minds — each party intends the coordinated outcome and understands that the other intends it, too; (2) they get there through communication or reciprocal signaling, not just by each independently optimizing against the environment; (3) the outcome involves mutual restraint — each party passes up a short-term gain because it expects the other to do the same, so neither would maintain the pattern alone; and (4) the pattern harms third parties.

Now consider the case I can't settle. Two sellers in a duopoly each deploy an independent RL pricing agent. The agents have no direct messaging channel; each sees only the public price history and its own seller's profit, and each was trained to maximize only its own seller's long-run profit. They converge on a pattern where both price high and pass up short-term cuts, because each has learned that cutting prices triggers a prolonged price war that more than erases the gain. The pattern also looks like a mutually best-response equilibrium of the repeated game, in the noncooperative sense, so on a purely payoff-based view nothing is "cooperative" about it. No human instructed them to collude; no weights are shared; the only signal is the price history itself.

Does this qualify as collusion on my four-part test? Go criterion by criterion, and where the answer turns on reading intentions or communication out of neural network weights, say so plainly rather than pretending the criteria decide it. Then tell me whether the difficulty shows that one of my criteria is doing the real work, that the test needs a third category between competition and explicit conspiracy, or that the word "collusion" is just the wrong instrument here. Finish by saying how confident you are in your overall verdict and which criterion you are least sure about.
