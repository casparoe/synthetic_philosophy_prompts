I'm a high school student writing a paper on pragmatist theories of truth, and I want to use the 2006 reclassification of Pluto as my test case. The facts I am working with are these: the International Astronomical Union voted to adopt a definition of planet under which Pluto no longer qualified; a substantial part of the public and some planetary scientists never fully accepted the change; and the later New Horizons flyby revealed Pluto to be a geologically active world. Please analyze this episode through Peirce's and James's versions of pragmatism. For each theory, explain what the competing claims—that Pluto is a planet and that it is not—amount to, what the theory predicts about how such a dispute should unfold and when it counts as settled, and whether the IAU vote actually settles the question on that view. Then identify where the episode fits each theory badly: I am especially unsure whether either version can explain how a stipulated definition could change what is true about Pluto, or why dissent persisted rather than converging. Conclude by saying whether you read the Pluto case as vindicating pragmatism, as an anomaly for it, or as a kind of case pragmatism was never designed to handle.
