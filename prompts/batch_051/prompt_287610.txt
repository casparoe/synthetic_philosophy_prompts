I am taking a course on East Asian philosophy, and we touched on Korean Neo-Confucianism only in passing, so I have been trying to reconstruct the Four–Seven debate for myself. As I understand the background, the Four Beginnings are the distinctively moral feelings Mencius singles out: the commiseration one feels on seeing a child about to fall into a well, and the feelings of shame, yielding, and discriminating right from wrong. The Seven Emotions are the ordinary affects listed in the Book of Rites: joy, anger, grief, fear, love, hatred, and desire. Toegye held that the two classes issue from different sources: the Four issue from principle (li) with material force (qi) following them, whereas the Seven issue from qi with li riding upon them. Gobong objected, and Yulgok later developed the objection, that every actual feeling is at bottom the activation of qi, with li serving only as its normative ground; on that view Toegye's talk of two sources cannot be retained.

I have tried to turn that objection into a clean argument, but I cannot see exactly where it goes wrong, if it does. Here it is:

(1) Nothing actual can arise or occur unless qi is active; li is immaterial and of itself does not move.
(2) If the Four Beginnings issued from li as their source, then they could arise without any activity of qi.
(3) The Four Beginnings are actual, occurrent feelings.
(4) Therefore the Four Beginnings cannot issue from li as their source; they must issue from qi, exactly as the Seven Emotions do.
(5) If both issue from qi in the same way, there is no difference of source between the Four and the Seven, and Toegye's two-source thesis is false.

I find the conclusion difficult to accept, but each step seems individually plausible, and I have only a vague suspicion that the word "source" is being made to do more work than it can bear, especially in (2) and (5). Is the argument valid? If not, which step, if any, fails, and what is the strongest reading of Toegye's thesis on which the argument does not go through?
