I'm writing a paper for a democratic-theory seminar and keep running into a pattern in constitutional law that I can't get straight philosophically. The same documents that open by locating all authority in the people then make large parts of the constitution unavailable to ordinary majorities, and in a few cases unavailable to anyone at all. Constitutions typically entrench their amendment procedures behind supermajorities; some layer several hurdles on top of one another (two-thirds votes in both chambers, ratification by the subunits, a second vote after an intervening election); and a handful mark certain provisions as unamendable even by unanimous vote — the so-called eternity clauses. In effect, a political community binds not only its own future self but later generations that never consented to the arrangement. What I want to know is what this practice presupposes about collective agency, rationality, and legitimacy over time. Is the whole structure committed to the Ulysses-contract picture, according to which the demos is a single enduring agent whose present, sober self can rationally tie down its predictably impulsive future self? Or is that metaphor doing more work than the institutions actually need? Part of my confusion is that different kinds of entrenchment seem to point in different directions: a bill of rights looks like a shield for individuals against the state, while an entrenched amendment procedure looks like a collective agent imposing discipline on itself. I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory as follows: separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with, note any places where different parts of the practice pull toward inconsistent presuppositions, and flag where the practice is underdetermined such that rival justifications — constitutions as insurance, as minority protection, as solutions to coordination problems — would each license the same design. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
