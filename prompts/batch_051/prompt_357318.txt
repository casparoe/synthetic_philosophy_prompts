Suppose a jurisdiction has a criminal statute that has not been enforced in living memory, and a prosecutor now revives it against a single defendant. The doctrine of desuetude holds that a statute can lose its force — or at least its enforceability — through prolonged and public non-enforcement alone. Write an essay on whether a court committed to the rule of law should decline to apply such a statute as desuetude, or should enforce it as the law the legislature has left on the books. Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides by identifying the rule-of-law values they appeal to, including notice, generality, fidelity to enacted law, and the separation of powers. Separately, distinguish desuetude from ordinary prosecutorial discretion and from repeal by implication, since neither of those squarely raises the question. Reach a definite verdict and justify it; do not end in an even-handed compromise.
