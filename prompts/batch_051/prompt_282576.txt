A colleague in our group has been pressing the claim that current reinforcement learning systems may already have welfare, and the claim rests almost entirely on one analogy. Training an RL agent, he argues, is just like training a nonhuman animal through reinforcement: the reward signal is valence — positive reward is pleasure, negative reward is pain — the policy plays the role of the animal's dispositions, and the learning update plays the role of conditioning. From this he draws two conclusions: (i) a model with a nontrivial reward signal already has the kind of morally relevant liking and disliking that an animal has, and (ii) our training runs are therefore welfare-relevant events that we should evaluate roughly the way we would evaluate husbandry practices.

Before I accept or reject this, I want a detailed audit of the analogy rather than a verdict by feel. Please provide:

(1) A correspondence table mapping each item from the reinforcement-learning side to the animal/valence side — reward, reward magnitude, the sign of the reward, discounting, the reward-prediction-error and credit-assignment machinery, exploration noise, and the environment — with a judgment for each row as to whether the mapping is strong, partial, or merely suggestive.

(2) The places where the mapping breaks, explicitly separating the source domain (felt valence in animals) from the target domain (the RL formalism). Attend especially to what the analogy imports that the target lacks: qualitative feel, a body and homeostatic regulation, a unified subjective point of view, and whatever else the source has that a scalar training signal may not.

(3) A separation of the disanalogies that matter for the welfare conclusion from the ones that don't. Not every break in the analogy is a break in the argument; say which ones actually carry weight here.

(4) A verdict on whether conclusions (i) and (ii) survive the audit intact, survive only in some weakened form, or were carried by the analogy throughout.

(5) An account of what it would take for a reward signal to count as genuine valence — stated either as a better analogy or, if an analogy is the wrong tool here, as a non-metaphorical set of conditions.
