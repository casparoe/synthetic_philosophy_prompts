I'm on the board of a small housing co-op. Our bylaws say the board can terminate a tenancy after 60 days of unpaid rent, but they also say that before any termination decision the tenant gets a written notice and a chance to meet with the board. One tenant is 75 days behind after losing her job. She's asked to come talk to us and says she can pay the whole balance in three weeks from a new job she's starting. The board chair wants to skip the meeting and just vote her out: he says the arrears are undisputed, so a hearing would be theater, and 60 days means 60 days. If we hear her out, he says, we're really saying the rules only bind the board when the board feels like it, because a sad story will always move someone to bend them. I get the worry, but I think he's conflating two different things: whether we can act after 60 days and whether we have to give her the hearing the bylaws promise before we act. I can't articulate why that distinction matters, though. Tell me what's actually going on here, which view is right, and what the board should do.
