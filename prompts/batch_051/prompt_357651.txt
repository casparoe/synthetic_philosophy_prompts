I have been trying to get clear about the ethics of blame, and before I present my position to a seminar I would like a careful audit of whether my commitments actually hang together. Here, as precisely as I can state them, is where I have landed.

(1) The aptness of blame—whether the target is blameworthy and the blame fits the deed—depends only on facts about the target and the wrong. Facts about the blamer's own moral record can make their blame inappropriate, but they cannot make the target any less blameworthy.

(2) Hypocrisy removes standing. A person who routinely commits a given wrong lacks the standing to blame others for that same wrong, even when those others are fully blameworthy for it.

(3) Complicity removes standing as well. A getaway driver in an armed robbery is in no position, even years later, to blame the robber for the robbery—not because the robber is not blameworthy, but because the driver is implicated in the very wrong he would condemn.

(4) Meddling can remove standing where there is neither hypocrisy nor complicity. An uninvited stranger who criticises a couple, unbidden, about the distribution of care work in their marriage seems to lack standing, even if the criticism would be fair were it made by someone appropriately placed to make it.

(5) Blame is a form of moral address: to blame is to hold the wrongdoer to a moral demand and to call on them to acknowledge the wrong.

(6) The standing to issue that demand is grounded in self-acceptance. One can hold another to a demand only if one genuinely accepts that very demand as binding on one's own conduct. That is what the hypocrite lacks.

(7) Consequently, a reformed wrongdoer can have standing. Someone who once defrauded investors but has since owned it, made what amends he could, and now treats the norm against fraud as fully binding on himself is entitled to blame a current fraudster.

(8) So, as I see it, standing is a function of the blamer's relationship to the norm she invokes, not of her biographical relationship to the particular wrong or to the parties involved.

I am uneasy that this list may be more comfortable in summary than defensible in detail, and I am not sure where the pressure really is. Please go through it systematically: which pairs are actually in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost. A concrete audit would be more useful to me than a general verdict that the overall package needs rethinking.
