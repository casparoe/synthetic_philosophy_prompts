Exam question: "Explain Wilfrid Sellars's critique of the Myth of the Given. In doing so, (a) state what the Myth of the Given is, (b) identify at least one philosophical view Sellars takes to be committed to it, and (c) explain how Sellars's positive account of perceptual knowledge is meant to avoid the Myth. In part (c), be sure to discuss the role of 'looks' and 'seems' language in his account."

Student's answer: "Sellars's Myth of the Given is the assumption that there is a stratum of non-inferential awareness — typically sensations, sense-data, or the immediate contents of experience — that is simply given to the mind and that supplies the ultimate justification for empirical knowledge. His main target is classical empiricism, especially sense-datum theories, which take acquaintance with private sense-data to be the foundation on which all other empirical beliefs rest. Sellars's objection is that this picture conflates causation with justification: sensations may be caused by the environment and may in turn cause beliefs, but that is a causal story, not an epistemic one. Justification belongs to the logical space of reasons, and only something with propositional content, something that can stand in inferential or evidential relations to other beliefs, can justify a belief. On Sellars's positive account, perceptual knowledge consists in making observational reports such as 'this is red' within a shared linguistic practice in which one has been trained to respond to objects. The crucial point is that this practice includes a foundational, non-inferential layer consisting of 'looks' and 'seems' statements. A statement like 'this looks red' reports only how things appear to the subject and withholds commitment about how they are; for that reason, appearance statements are conceptually prior to 'is' statements and are known directly and non-inferentially. They therefore serve as the self-justifying base of empirical knowledge. In this way Sellars avoids the Myth of the Given while keeping a foundation for empirical knowledge: the foundation is the subject's direct awareness of her own appearances."
