I've been rereading Ramsey's Facts and Propositions for a reading group, and I'm struck by how quickly he dismisses the problem of truth. His move is basically to say that to call a proposition true is just to assert the proposition itself: saying that Caesar was murdered is true comes to no more than saying that Caesar was murdered. That seems too easy, but I also can't quite see where it goes wrong. Could you write a dialogue between two philosophers — a deflationist who thinks Ramsey's remark is basically right and a correspondence theorist who thinks truth is a substantial relation between a proposition and the world — in which they really go at it. The deflationist should press the point that the correspondence theory owes us an account of what this "correspondence" relation is, and of what propositions are. The correspondence theorist should press back that deflationism cannot explain our use of truth in generalisations, truth ascriptions across languages, or the intuition that truth is a norm of inquiry. Make them respond to each other rather than delivering set speeches, and maybe have them consider at the end whether their disagreement is substantive or just a dispute about whether the word "property" is doing any work. Around 700 to 900 words please. Oh, and write the dialogue itself in American English, since I'll be using it for a US-based course handout.
