Novelist here. I keep tripping over the same pattern in my research and I don't have a word for it. A community adopts a rule for a plain practical reason—they stop eating a certain fish because it was making people sick, they demand brutal public truthfulness because it made their trading partners trust them, they keep an odd extra step in tanning hides because the local water used to ruin the hide without it. A couple of generations later the practical reason is gone or forgotten, but the rule has become sacred. People describe it as just right, or pure, or "the way we are," and they treat breaking it as wrong in itself, even where following it now does visible harm. The strange part to me isn't that the rule outlived its usefulness. It's that the rule changed how it feels. It started as a cost-benefit tactic and now presents itself to the people inside it as an end, and they'd be insulted by the idea that it was ever just a tool. I know the rough distinction between valuing a thing for what it gets you and valuing it for its own sake, and I know the word sacralization, but that feels too broad—this is one specific transition, with a specific danger signature: the value keeps its authority after it loses its function, and the authority now feels axiomatic.

Is there an establshed name for this? If the neighboring terms don't quite fit, coin one. I won't be satisfied with a bare label—I want the carving: what the phenomenon is, what it is not, where its edges are.

Format: give me exactly five numered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction, no conclusion. Make the points these: one, your best label, borrowed or coined, with a definition; two, the nearest established concepts and where each misses; three, the boundary between this and ordinary habit, plain inertia, nostalgia, and the thing where people keep a rule only because it might pay off later; four, the edge case that most tests the definition; five, whether the "error" is actually an error or whether making a value feel intrinsic is sometimes the most stable way to transmit it.
