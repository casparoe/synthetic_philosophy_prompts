I've been reading about the old heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the worry that pleasures don't seem to share any single felt quality: the pleasure of a warm bath, of solving a proof, of schadenfreude, of a back scratch. They don't feel like the same kind of thing at all, and saying "they're all pleasant" is just relabeling the problem. The standard move is to define pleasure functionally, as whatever feels good or whatever we're disposed to seek. But that seems circular because "feels good" just is pleasure, and "seek" gets explained by pleasure.

Anyway I work with some reinforcement learning, and it hit me that RL has the same shaped hole about reward. The environment hands the agent numbers, and reward is just whatever scalar you decide to optimize, but the things that get used as reward signals — food, points, money, social likes, sensor readings — are wildly heterogenous with no common ingredient. And the standard fix there is also functional: reward is whatever increases the probability of the preceding behavior. That feels circular in the same way, since "increases the probability" is what defines reinforcement, and reinforcement is supposed to be explained by reward. Is this a deep structural parallel about the search for a single currency of value, or am I just pattern-matching? If it's deep, does the RL reward hypothesis (that any goal can be recast as maximizing expected cumulative reward) transfer to hedonism, or does the hedonist's heterogeneity objection transfer back into RL as a criticism of the reward hypothesis? Or do the two cases come apart in a way that tells us something interesting about each?
