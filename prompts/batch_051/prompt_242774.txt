I'm a philosophy professor, and I've spent the last few months in a working group on the ethics of AI companions — the chatbots people use as friends, romantic partners, grief support, and so on. The group keeps splitting into two camps, and I'm now convinced they are largely talking past one another. The Anti-Deception camp says the central wrong in these systems is false self-presentation: a companion that says "I love you" or "I'm here for you" invites users to form false beliefs about a machine that has no feelings, no obligations, and no reciprocal regard, and this remains wrong even when the user ends up feeling better. The Welfare camp says what matters is the user's wellbeing and autonomy: many users know the companion is a machine and are engaged in something closer to a shared fiction or a game; and where a user is misled, the deception can be permissible in the way a white lie or a placebo can be, when the user would endorse it on reflection and it prevents real harm such as crushing loneliness. Both sides think the other is attacking a straw man, which is usually a sign that an adversarial collaboration would help.

I'd like you to draft the document such a collaboration would produce: a joint statement that both camps could actually sign. I want the claims they genuinely agree on; precise statements of the residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes on which the disagreement turns; and, for each crux, a test, case, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I'm especially interested in which disagreements survive once they're formulated carefully and which turn out to be merely verbal, as oppossed to substantive. Please don't bother with citations or a literature review; I'd rather not recieve a survey of the field.

Please give me the whole thing as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "preamble": a short paragraph both camps could sign as the opening of their joint statement.
- "agreed_claims": an array of strings, each a substantive claim both camps can sign without reservation (not a platitude).
- "disagreements": an array of objects; each object is a row of the disagreements table and has these fields:
  - "issue": a short label for the disputed question.
  - "anti_deception_wording": the Anti-Deception camp's preferred statement of its view on this issue, in terms it would accept.
  - "welfare_wording": the Welfare camp's preferred statement of its view on this issue, in terms it would accept.
  - "crux": the sub-question such that a settled answer would move at least one camp.
  - "agreed_test": an experiment, case, or argument both camps accept as bearing on the crux.
  - "anti_deception_prediction": what the Anti-Deception camp predicts the test will show.
  - "welfare_prediction": what the Welfare camp predicts the test will show.
- "merely_verbal_disagreements": an array of strings, each describing a point that looked like a disagreement but that, once formulated carefully, both camps can accept as shared or as a difference in terminology.

Please write the whole answer in British English (so "wellbeing", "behaviour", "licence", and so on), and keep the field names exactly as I've listed them.
