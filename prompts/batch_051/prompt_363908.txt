Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two metaontologists who have argued for years about one restricted question: whether composite material objects such as tables exist. The first, a quantifier variantist, holds that quantifier expressions like "there is" can vary in meaning across equally good languages, so that "there are tables" is true in some correct languages and false in others, and the dispute is ultimately verbal. The second, an ontological realist, holds that exactly one of those quantifier meanings is metaphysically privileged, carving nature at the joints, so the question whether tables exist has a determinate, non-verbal answer even if it is difficult to discover. Both agree to set aside disputes about numbers, properties, and propositions, and to focus solely on composite material objects.

Produce the document this collaboration should issue, in four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign: claims about the linguistic data (which sentences ordinary speakers and revisionary metaphysicians accept), about what would have to be true for a question to count as merely verbal, and about when a disagreement is genuine rather than a difference in language. Second, a table setting out the precise residual disagreement, with each side's view stated in wording that side would themselves accept. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side toward the other. Fourth, for each crux, the argument, case, or theoretical consideration both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction or assessment recorded in advance. If you judge that part of the residual dispute is itself verbal, identify which part and explain why the statement cannot dissolve it.
