Okay so I'm a junior rep on my school's student-faculty discipline board (we just call it SFB). We're hearing a case about two juniors — I'll call them R and D — who got caught trashing the chemistry lab storage closet after prom. They broke beakers, sprayed a fire extinguisher around, nothing permanently damaged but it was a big mess. The facts aren't really in dispute: R planned the whole thing and D went along with it.

The part that's splitting the board is this. During the hearing R said, kind of bragging, that he'd already decided that if D had chickened out at the last second, R would have threatened to send a humiliating video of D to the entire grade, and D admitted that yeah, that threat would have made him go through with it. D also says he had no idea about the video threat at the time and that he joined in on his own because he thought it would be funny. So now half the SFB is saying D couldn't have done otherwise, so he shouldn't get the same punishment as R, or maybe shouldn't be punished at all. The other half is saying that's crazy: R's backup plan never got used, D made his own choice, so D is just as responsible.

I got assigned to draft the short decision memo the SFB will vote on. It needs to recommend a concrete outcome for D (R is getting a three-day suspension plus a month of helping the custodial staff after school — so should D get the same, a reduced version, or nothing), with the reasoning behind it, and it has to honestly engage the strongest point on the side I'm recommending against.

The thing I keep going back and forth on is whether "couldn't have done otherwise" should actually matter for blame. My philosophy teacher showed us this thought experiment where a guy decides on his own to vote for candidate X, but a neuroscientist secretly planted a chip that would have forced him to vote for X if he'd started to choose candidate Y — and the chip never fired. The guy is still responsible, which is supposed to show that the principle of alternate possibilities is wrong. But I'm not sure that transfers to a real discipline case where we're actually punishing a person, and I don't want the memo to be all abstract. Could you draft the memo for me? Keep it around 600-800 words and make it something I can actually read aloud at the meeting.
