I am a retired geochemist who came to philosophy late, and I keep noticing a pattern in the way certain communities absorb criticism that I cannot name precisely. I would be grateful for help in identifying the phenomenon if it has an established label, or in characterizing it well enough to distinguish it from neighboring concepts if it does not.

Let me offer two instances rather than an abstract description.

The first comes from a research community I used to observe. A group of scientists held a settled empirical view, and when a well-conducted study came out against it, their response was not to ignore the study or to attack the authors' motives. Instead, they processed it in two steps. First, the study was assigned to a category in a familiar catalogue of methodological defects: residual confounding, inappropriate exposure measurement, insufficient control, and so on. Second, an off-the-shelf rebuttal associated with that category was retrieved, a general argument that any study of that shape cannot support the contrary conclusion. Each step looked, in isolation, like ordinary scientific hygiene. But the step doing the real work was the classification itself: whether this particular study actually had the defect, in a form severe enough to void it. That step was never argued, only asserted by a kind of practiced recognition. Studies on the other side that committed the identical sin were called imperfect but informative and were not voided. The result was that members of this community had read more adverse evidence than any outsider would credit, and not one item of it had ever required them to revise their view.

The second instance is philosophical. In a reading group I joined, an unfamiliar or disliked position received the same two-step treatment. Someone would offer, say, a design argument about cosmic fine-tuning. Before its premises were examined, the view would be classified: it is just a god-of-the-gaps argument dressed up with a probability distribution, or it is inference to the best explanation with the competition omitted. Once classified, it was treated as already answered, because each category had an accepted refutation attached to it. Again, the classification, the move on which the whole response rested, was treated as obvious, a matter of seeing the family resemblance. Any part of the position that did not fit the chosen category was simply not discussed.

What strikes me is the common structure. An argument arrives; it is sorted into a fixed taxonomy of objections; each slot in the taxonomy comes pre-supplied with a rebuttal; and the sorting act, which carries all the epistemic weight, is the one step insulated from scrutiny. The taxonomy is rich enough that almost nothing fails to fit somewhere, yet elastic enough that a poor fit is treated as a defect of the object rather than of the taxonomy. The community remains energetically engaged with outside material, indeed seeks it out and welcomes it, but every item is neutralized during intake.

This seems to me different from an epistemic bubble, because the community is not insulated from contrary information; it has a great deal of it. It also seems different from an echo chamber, because outsiders are not discredited in advance; they are trusted precisely long enough to be filed. And it seems different from ordinary confirmation bias, because the weighting is not private and goal-directed but has been delegated to a public, shared filing system that no one any longer feels obliged to defend.

My question is whether this specific mechanism has an established name in the literature, and if not, how it should be carved out so it can be recognized when it appears. I am less interested in the label than in the carving. A satisfying answer would say which existing concepts it most resembles, how exactly it differs from each, whether it requires a community or can occur inside a single mind equipped with the same filing system, what the boundary cases are, and what conditions make the taxonomy itself vulnerable to being noticed and revised. Finally, please say how confident you are in the claims you make, and single out the point you are least sure of.
