Suppose a creative act is defined by four criteria: (1) novelty — the product is original and surprising against the relevant background; (2) agency — the act involves deliberate choice or judgment by an agent; (3) responsiveness — the act is guided by the constraints, materials, or problems of a recognizable domain rather than being random; and (4) value — the product is judged by a reasonably informed audience to have aesthetic, intellectual, or expressive worth. Now consider this case. A team trains an image-generating system for a single purpose: to produce images that a learned model predicts human viewers will find surprising yet competent. The system generates candidates, scores them, and revises them over many iterations. No human selects the final image, and the engineers cannot explain why the system produced it; they chose only the training objective and the data. One output is submitted anonymously to a juried exhibition, where the jurors call it one of the most original works they have seen in years. Is anything in this case creative — the image, the process, or neither? Work through the four criteria one by one, note where they underdetermine the verdict, and then say whether the criteria need revision and which criterion is doing the main work. Please answer in American English.
