My colleague Marta has spent years deciding, case by case, whether particular games count as art, and I've been keeping score. As best I can reconstruct her track record: Braid (a puzzle-platformer built around a time-rewind mechanic, wrapped in a story about regret and a breakup) — art. The Last of Us — art, but she gets annoyed when people say so on the strength of the cutscenes, which she insists are the least artistic thing about the game. Kentucky Route Zero (a magical-realist story about a secret highway, mostly walking and choosing dialogue) — art. Chess at world-championship level — not art; she says the play can be beautiful, but nobody composed the rules to be appreciated, its a contest that happens to have aesthetics around it. A superbly engineered slot machine — not art; she says it manufactures compulsion rather than agency.

The problem is that her stated principle keeps collapsing. She mutters that "the doing has to be the object" and that a game isnt art just because there's a movie hiding inside it. But if that's the rule, Kentucky Route Zero looks like a counterexample to her own view, and she knows it. Next week I'm making her commit on Tetris — falling blocks, clearing lines, speeding up until the board overflows, no characters, no dialogue, no story. I have a bet with a grad student about what she'll say.

Walk me through it. Give me two or three different rules that would rationalize her existing verdicts, say what each predicts about Tetris, and then take a stand: does she call Tetris art or not, and which of her earlier verdicts is doing the most work in your guess? Finally, name the single later case that would cleanest separate whichever two rules are still live.
