i'm on the licensing committee of a small open-source foundation, and we give little grants to people building tools that work with free software. the case in front of us: a developer wants to build an add-on for a GPLv3-licensed parametric CAD program. the add-on runs as a seperate process, talks to the CAD program over a local socket, and never includes any of the CAD program's source; she wrote the whole thing from scratch and wants to sell it under a proprietary licence. one committee member says the add-on is useless except as an extension of that one program, it was built specifically against its API and file format, and therefore it is a work based on the program and has to be GPL. the bit of the Free Software Foundation's GPLv3 we keep going round on is this:

To “modify” a work means to copy from or adapt all or part of the work in a fashion requiring copyright permission, other than the making of an exact copy. The resulting work is called a “modified version” of the earlier work or a work “based on” the earlier work.

we need to produce a short decision memo: fund as proposed, refuse, or fund only on condition that the add-on be released under the GPL (or a compatible free licence). please draft the memo with reasoning we could actually stand behind, and give the strongest point on the losing side its due. and please write it in British English.
