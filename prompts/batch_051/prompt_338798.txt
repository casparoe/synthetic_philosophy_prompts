I'm a CS PhD student who's been reading Kuhn and Popper on the side, and I can't stop seeing the rationality-of-revolutions debate in the question of whether a big-bang rewrite of a legacy codebase can ever be rationally justified. Kuhn's point is that during a paradigm change there are no theory-neutral standards, because the old paradigm supplies the standards for what counts as good evidence; well, a rewrite seems to have the same structure — the old system's test suite and design heuristics encode the old way of doing things, so judging the new architecture by them stacks the deck. Popper would insist you can still falsify the challenger on neutral terms; in software the closest thing would be keeping the old system around as a reference and demanding the rewrite match its input-output behavior on some corpus. Is this a real structural analogy or am I just pattern-matching? If it's real, does the software side show there *is* a paradigm-independent arbiter (the input-output contract), and if so what does the scientific parallel lack? Where does the analogy break down?
