I am a retired ecologist who has recently turned to philosophy of history, and I have just reread Gabriel García Márquez's One Hundred Years of Solitude. I would like a critical reading of the novel through the debate between cyclical and linear accounts of history. On the cyclical side I am thinking of Polybius on the rotation of constitutions, Ibn Khaldun on dynasties rising and decaying as group solidarity waxes and wanes, Spengler on cultures as organisms with a characteristic life course, and Toynbee on the pattern of challenge and response. On the linear side I have in mind the view that history is open-ended and directional, and that what resembles a cycle is often a similarity in how events are narrated rather than a real recurrence in causes.

The novel seems to occupy both positions at once. Macondo is founded, prospers, and decays; the Buendía family repeats names, temperaments, and fates across generations; Melquíades' parchments have contained the whole history from the start. Yet the town is also pulled into a specific and unrepeatable history by the railway, the banana company, and the massacre, and the ending can be read as the closing of a cycle rather than the turn of another one. I would like you to take this seriously as a philosophical reading, not merely to use the theories to label plot points. Where does a cyclical lens genuinely illuminate a scene, a character's choice, or the architecture of the book? Where does the novel resist that lens, and where should an honest critic say so? Does it ultimately dramatize a cyclical view, a linear view, a third position, or a deliberate refusal to choose? And does anything in the novel contribute to the philosophical debate that the theorists themselves do not — for instance about whether repetition within a family or a town counts as genuine historical recurrence or is an artifact of storytelling?

I also want to know the limits of your interpretation. Please say what would change your reading: which alternative interpretation, or which feature of the text, would most threaten it, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Begin with the reading itself, with no preface, no commentary, and no restatement of my question.
