Im giving a talk next month at our departments philosophy of science workshop, and the Q&A is going to be rough. My thesis is roughly this: Solomonoff induction does not give you a formal vindication of Occams razor, and the widespread idea that it does mostly conflates "simpler in the limit" with "simpler now." The argument, in one paragraph: Kolmogorov complexity is only defined relative to a choice of reference machine; the invariance theorem says changing machines changes the complexity by at most an additive constant, which for the prior means at most a multiplicative constant. That's enough for asymptotic agreement, but real inference is finite-data and usually a contest between a small menu of rival hypotheses, and there the machine choice can flip which of two hypotheses the prior treats as simpler. On top of that the prior is uncomputable, so nobody can actually compute it to settle a live dispute. So if Occams razor is supposed to be the thing that tells us, with the data we actually have, which of two theories to prefer, Solomonoff induction does no such thing; it only tells us that in a limit we are never in, most reasonable priors wash out toward the truth. I'll close by pushing the stronger line that the razor is doing the normative work and the formal apparatus is along for the ride.

The audience is mostly philosophers of science and epistemologists, many of them card-carrying Bayesians, plus one statistician who works on consistency of Bayesian procedures, and one machine-learning person who knows algorithmic information theory far better than I do. Theyre broadly sympathetic to formalizing simplicity, and maybe half of them think Solomonoff already did it. The ML person will probably roll her eyes at my machine-dependence point and treat the invariance theorem as the last word on the whole worry.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet. I want the objections in the rough order theyll actually come, phrased the way this audience would put them, with how much damage each one does, a shortish reply for each, and a note on where my reply is weakest. And include the question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks.
