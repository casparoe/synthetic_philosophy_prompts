Write the joint statement that a committed strict conditionalizer and a committed Jeffrey conditionalizer could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on how an ideal agent should update credences when evidence arrives that does not single out any proposition for certainty.

Adopt these working definitions. Strict conditionalization says: when a proposition E is learned with certainty, the agent's new credence function is P_new(H) = P_old(H | E). Jeffrey conditionalization, or probability kinematics, says: when evidence shifts credences over a finite partition {E_i} from P_old(E_i) to new weights q_i while holding fixed all conditional credences given the E_i, the agent's new credence function is P_new(H) = Σ_i q_i P_old(H | E_i). The strict conditionalizer holds that every legitimate rational update can be represented as strict conditionalization on some proposition that becomes certain—paradigmatically a proposition about how things appear or about the evidence itself. The Jeffrey conditionalizer holds that some legitimate rational updates are irreducibly Jeffrey shifts, in which no proposition becomes certain and no reduction to a certain-evidence representation is lossless.

After their collaboration, they are to produce a single document containing:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. These should not be vacuous: include claims about the probability calculus, about the special cases in which the two rules coincide, about what a theory of uncertain evidence would have to explain, and about what constraints any defensible update rule must satisfy.

2. A disagreements table. The rows should give the precise residual disagreements, with each side's position phrased in wording that side would accept. Cover at least (a) whether uncertain-evidence updates reduce without loss to strict conditionalization on a proposition about appearances or about the evidence itself; (b) whether the rigidity condition in probability kinematics is a substantive normative requirement or only one permissible way to complete the shift; and (c) whether the behavior of iterated Jeffrey updates—in particular whether they commute, and whether any order-dependence is a defect that undermines the rule.

3. A section on cruxes: the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one of the two camps. For each crux, state why it bears on the disagreement.

4. For each crux, a concrete case or argument that both parties accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. You may use, but are not limited to, cases such as: a foggy observation that raises several perceptual hypotheses without conferring certainty; an agent who remembers that she updated on uncertain evidence but has forgotten the exact partition of her earlier evidence; and an agent whose evidence consists in a change in her own degrees of belief with no new proposition learned outright. If you conclude that some part of their dispute is merely verbal, indicate which part and justify that verdict.
