Write a villanelle about wu-wei and the paradox of trying not to try. Let the two refrains work as a loop: one voice straining for effortlessness, the other noticing that the straining is exactly the problem, so the poem's repetitiveness actually is the bind rather than just illustrating it. Across the middle tercets I want the Daoist correction to come through properly: wu-wei isn't passivity, isn't laziness, isn't shutting your thoughts off; it's closer to a knack or a cultivated spontaneity you only reach obliquely — you wont get there by trying to be spontaneous head-on, any more than you get to sleep by ordering yourself to fall asleep. The final quatrain should be the turn, where the paradox isn't refuted so much as dissolved or stepped out of. Someone who knows the Zhuangzi or action theory should find it accurate, and someone with no background should still find it a good poem rather than a textbook with line breaks.
