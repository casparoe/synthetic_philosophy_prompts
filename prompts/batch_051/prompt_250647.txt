I keep thinking about that old School of Names argument, the White Horse dialogue, where Gongsun Long says a white horse is not a horse. As I understand it his move is something like: horse names the shape, white names the color, and naming the color isn't naming the shape; plus if you ask me for a horse and I bring a black one, that's fine, but if you ask for a white horse and I bring a black one, it's not, so the two terms don't pick out the same thing. Im a software engineer, so to me this reads like an argument about types — especially nominal vs structural typing and inheritance. If Gongsun Long got droped into a modern design discussion, do you think he'd say class WhiteHorse extends Horse is exactly the mistake he was warning about, or would he say it's fine as long as nobody confuses the type WhiteHorse with the type Horse? Would he accept that a WhiteHorse is usable anywhere a Horse is expected, or would he insist the two are different types because the concepts differ? And which part of his argument does the heavy lifting there — the color-vs-shape move or the bit about asking for one versus asking for the other? Predict as concretely as you can where he'd come down on is-a relationships, and where a modern type theorist would push back on him.
