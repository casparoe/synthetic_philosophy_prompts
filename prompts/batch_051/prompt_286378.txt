I keep going back and forth on hedonism about well-being. The pull, for me, is almost entirely systematic: if one thing makes a life go well—pleasant experience—then you get a clean, unified account plus a tidy decision procedure for a huge range of cases. But I can't get teh category of pleasure to behave. A proof finally clicking, a backrub, the drop in your stomach when you realize the bad thing isn't going to happen, schadenfreude, the first bite when you're actually hungry—these feel different enough that I don't know what single property I'm supposedly pointing at when I call all of them pleasures. I know the standard move is to say pleasure is a propositional attitude, a matter of enjoying that p, rather than a raw feeling, but that seems to just relocate the problem: now I have to say what enjoyment is across those cases. So I'm torn between a theory I like for its simplicity and a fairly firm intuition that the thing the theory is built on is gerrymandered. I don't want a referee's verdict. Give me the strongest case that this intuition is a failure of imagination, or a demand for natural-kind unity that a functional property doesn't need, and the strongest case that the intuition is tracking something that should make me amend or abandon the theory. Then say what it would actually take for each side to give way, and whether there's a version of hedonism on which the disunity is a feature rather than a bug.
