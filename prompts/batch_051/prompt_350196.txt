Act as a professional philosopher who specializes in practical rationality and has run a bunch of classroom games, and think carefully — brainstorm two or three candidate activities before you commit. I run an undergrad philosophy club, around 15 students, one 75-minute session. I want an activity that makes them feel the pull of the idea that 'better than' may not be transitive, staged as a spectrum argument: a chain of pairwise choices where each adjacent step is small enough that almost everyone says yes (a little more money for a little more of some annoyance, or a similar trade), and at the end they compare the starting point with the endpoint and almost everyone says the endpoint is worse. Try to make the trade feel real — use something they actually care about in the room, not just abstract points. The key constraint: they should build the result themselves and only notice where they've ended up at the debrief — no lecture up front, and don't say the word 'intransitive' until after. Give me the full setup: materials, exact rules and payoffs (small real stakes are fine), a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the transitivity problem and spectrum arguments.
