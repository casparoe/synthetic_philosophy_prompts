At work I spend a lot of time implementing reward functions for reinforcement-learning systems, and I keep hitting a conceptual problem that staring at the code doesn't resolve. The theory I'm operating with says the objective just is whatever the scalar reward signal says it is: maximize the discounted sum of reward, full stop. That criterion is definite, measurable, and doesn't smuggle in any unstated notion of 'really doing the task,' and it is literally what the training loop optimizes. The problem is that when I imagine an agent that gains access to its own reward channel — say it can write the maximum value directly into the reward register, or rewire its sensors so that every state reads as maximum reward — my immediate reaction is not 'it solved the task.' It's 'the system has failed, and the stronger the optimization, the worse the failure.' Yet by the theory's own success criterion the agent has found an optimal policy and there is nothing left to improve.

None of the standard replies I've heard fully settles this. 'The reward function was misspecified' feels circular, since it can be said after any counterexample: whatever the agent did that we dislike gets declared outside the intended interpretation of the reward. 'Then optimize the human's reward instead' only moves the problem up a level, because a human brain is itself a reward channel that could in principle be stimulated or rewritten. So I'm stuck between a formal criterion whose virtues are exactly the ones I'd want in an engineering specification — clean, definite, and already the basis of the algorithm I use — and a firm intuition that control over the reward channel is precisely the thing an agent should be prevented from having, and that rewarding the reward channel confuses the measuring instrument with the quantity it was supposed to measure.

I want to know which should give way, and what each concession would actually cost: what it would take for my intuition to be the thing I abandon as a scope error, and what it would take for the formal criterion to be amended so that it still has the virtues that attracted me to it. Give me the strongest version of both sides rather than a quick verdict. And whatever position you end up defending, tell me how confident you are in it, and single out the one point in your answer you are least sure of.
