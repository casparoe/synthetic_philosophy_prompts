Okay so here's my situation. About six months ago I started volunteering with a food rescue group, and ever since I've been in a low-grade argument with myself I can't seem to settle. We intercept stuff from supermarkets and bakeries that's about to get thrown out, and a surprising amount of it is animal products — yogurt, pre-made sandwiches, eggs, cheese, occasionally a rotisserie chicken. The group's informal rule is basically "if it was headed for the dumpster anyway, it's fair game," and some of the volunteers there are strict vegans who happily take the yogurt and eggs. One of them keeps rescue hens in her yard and eats their eggs; her line is the hens don't care about the eggs, the eggs would just rot or get eaten by rats, and not a cent flows back to anyone breeding more layers. I'm vegetarian myself, have been drifting vegan, and honestly this whole thing has made the issue feel alot less settled than I thought.

Write an essay on whether someone in that position is justified in using animal products that would otherwise go to waste — the dumpstered stuff and the rescue-hen eggs both, but spend real time on the eggs, since that's the case I can't resolve. Weigh the strongest versions of the two main camps: (1) the demand-and-waste line, which says that if the product was genuinely going to be discarded and your using it doesn't signal demand or put money into animal agriculture, consuming it is at worst permissible and possibly even good because it cuts waste; and (2) the more absolutist line, which says that treating animal bodies and secretions as food keeps the deeper norm in place — that animals are resources for us — even when a particular act doesn't add new suffering. One wrinkle I keep getting stuck on: the "would otherwise be wasted" premise isn't neutral, because she could feed the eggs back to the hens, shells and all, so "waste" might be doing more work in her argument than it looks like. Don't end on a non-committal note. Defend a verdict, even a split one like "eggs from rescue hens are fine, dumpstered meat isn't" or the reverse. I don't want a survey of the literature and I don't need citations; I want the argument actually worked through, and I want you to flag where each side's reasoning goes soft.
