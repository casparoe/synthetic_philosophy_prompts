Take Goodman's grue but give the predicate two switch points instead of one. Fix two future times t1 and t2 with t1 < t2. Call something double-grue if it's green when first observed before t1, blue when first observed between t1 and t2, and green again when first observed after t2. We're now before t1, and every emerald anyone has examined so far has been green, so 'all emeralds are green' and 'all emeralds are double-grue' fit all the data equally well, same as with the ordinary grue. Ted says the standard anti-grue response — double-grue is disjunctive, gerrymandered, not entrenched, so not projectible — applies unchanged, maybe even more strongly since there are two arbitrary cutoffs. Ned says the second switch actually makes trouble for that response: now there's a whole two-parameter family of double-grue predicates (one for each pair t1 < t2), a continuum of equally well-fitting rivals, and that makes the choice of 'green' look arbitrary in a way the single-grue case didnt.
