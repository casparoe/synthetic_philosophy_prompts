I write about science and ideas for a general but serious audience, and I am preparing a piece about the standoff between defenders of procreative beneficence and critics who regard that view as liberal eugenics. By procreative beneficence I mean the view that prospective parents have at least a defeasible moral reason, and on some formulations a genuine obligation, to select, among the different children they could bring into existence, the child whose life can be expected to go best given the available information, including information from prenatal or embryo screening. By the opposing camp I mean those who grant that reproductive decisions are mostly private but hold that systematic selection against disability or impairment is not merely private: it expresses, and over time entrenches, the judgment that disabled lives are worse in something like the way older eugenic programs did, even without state coercion. Please draft the joint statement that these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, written entirely as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if in an unbroken joint letter in the first person plural. The statement should set out the claims both sides accept, including what they can jointly say about the wrongness of coercive state eugenics, about the value of disabled lives, about parental discretion, and about the non-identity point that the possible child who is not selected is not thereby harmed, since no person suffers that loss. It should then state their residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, and should say explicitly where the word eugenics is doing argumentative work and where the disagreement is substantive rather than verbal. It should then identify the cruxes, meaning sub-questions whose resolution would genuinely move at least one side. At least three cruxes must be addressed: the mirror-image cases of hearing parents who select against deafness and Deaf parents who select for it; the question of what routinized embryo selection does to public attitudes toward disabled people; and one further crux that you judge does more work than the first two. For each crux, state the evidence or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.
