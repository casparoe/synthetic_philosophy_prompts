I am a retired research scientist — my working life was spent in the physical sciences rather than in a seminar room — and over the last two years I have been reading my way through moral and political philosophy. I have assembled a set of commitments about the ethics of voting that I find myself accepting, and I would like to know whether they form a tenable package. Here they are:

1. Considered purely as an attempt to affect who wins an election, turning out to vote is not rationally required, and probably not rationally justified either, because the probability that any single ballot will decide a large election is vanishingly small while the costs of voting are real.
2. Nevertheless I think I should vote, for an expressive reason: casting a ballot is a way of publicly adding my voice to a collective statement about what kind of society we want, and that meaning does not depend on my vote tipping the outcome.
3. I also accept a fair-play account of the duty to vote. I benefit from democratic self-government, and when a cooperative practice produces shared benefits at modest cost, those who accept the benefits should take their turn even if their individual contribution does not make the difference.
4. In a first-past-the-post election contested by two serious parties, I believe a voter who prefers a third-party platform should usually vote for the lesser of the two viable evils rather than for the party they prefer. A ballot is a contribution to an actual collective decision, not a personal testimonial, and scattering it on a candidate with no chance of winning is a poor way to discharge that role when the stakes between the viable candidates are high.
5. But I am not prepared to say that lesser-evil reasoning has no limits. I think some conduct is beyond the pale: if a candidate has engaged in such conduct, I should not vote for that candidate even when the opponent is worse, because casting such a ballot would make me complicit in wrongdoing rather than merely making a responsible choice among imperfect options.

My question is whether these commitments hang together. Please go through them in pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension and which are only apparently in tension. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would be — which commitment would have to be qualified, and how much would that cost. Then say which single revision would do the most to make the whole set coherent.
