Been going down a rabbit hole on Moloch and multipolar traps — the dynamic where each player's individually rational move makes everyone worse off — and races to the bottom, and I've got a concrete mechanism I want you to actually take apart. Not whether it's a good idea in the abstract, but how it would play out if you dropped it into a real shared fishery.

Assume a regional port authority sets a hard annual total catch from stock surveys, but instead of individual quotas it runs a monthly settlement. Every boat has a transponder that logs departures (that counts as fishing, even if the boat lands nothing), and every landing has to go through one of the authority's docks, where buyers report species and weight and a blind auction each morning sets the dockside price (fishermen can't bid up their own catch). The annual total is a hard stop: once cumulative landings hit it, the port closes for the season with no compensation. At the end of each month the authority computes the average landed weight per boat that fished that month. Any boat that landed above the average pays a fee equal to twice that month's average price per kilo on the excess, and the whole pot is split among the below-average boats in proportion to how far below average they were. Payments come automatically out of each boat's escrow account with the authority; if an account goes negative, the boat gets impounded until the owner settles or sells the permit. And this is the part I want stress-tested: there are no individual quotas at all, and entry and exit are free.

Walk me through what this does to fishing behavior — when boats go out, what they target, how hard they push, how many ghost trips show up. Then who survives in the fishery, what happens to the stock and to the dockside market, what collusion appears (between boats, and between boats and buyers), and wheres the first place somebody games it. Finally tell me whether this actually solves the race to the bottom or just relocates it.
