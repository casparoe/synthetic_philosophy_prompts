I've been arguing with a friend about whether risk itself, as distinct from harm, can be a moral wrong, and we've tried to make the disagreement tractable with a stylized case. A delivery driver is late for a guaranteed thirty-minute delivery slot, and if he misses it he loses his job, the only one he can plausibly get this year. To make the deadline he drives 45 mph through a 15 mph residential zone for the final three miles, at a time when about 2,000 pedestrians, cyclists, and other road users are near the street; we stipulate that this imposes a one-in-100,000 risk of death on each of them, none of whom consented. By luck, no collision, no injury, and not even a near miss occurs. I put my own credence at 0.7 that the driver's act is objectively wrong ex ante — wrong in virtue of the risk imposed, independently of how things turn out. My friend puts his at 0.25 and says that where there is no harm there is no wrong to answer for.

Please give me your actual credence, as a single number from 0 to 1, that the driver's act is objectively wrong ex ante in that sense. Decompose it into the terms that do the work for you, including at least: the probability that non-consensual risk imposition can be a wrong in its own right rather than only an ex post matter if the risk eventuates; the probability that a one-in-100,000 risk to a single person falls below any de minimis threshold for personal wronging, and whether such tiny per-person risks can aggregate across 2,000 people; the probability that the benefit of keeping his job is the right sort of thing, and of the right magnitude, to justify this risk if anything can; and the probability that objectively wrong ex ante is a determinate property of this act rather than an indeterminate or ill-formed notion. Give a number and one or two lines of justification for each term, then show how the terms combine into your single credence. Tell me which term you are least confident in, and what argument or evidence would shift it most. Finally, say where my friend's and my disagreement most likely lives. End with \answer{...} enclosing your single credence.
