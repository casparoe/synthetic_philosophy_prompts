Suppose a country amended its constitution so that whenever the ordinary machinery of government simply runs out—no election can be held, no lawful successor, all the standard rules exhausted—a Continuity Council automatically forms. It's made up of the seven longest-serving judges still alive and willing to serve, plus fourteen people drawn by lot from the electoral register; it decides by a majority vote; it's members can't be replaced while it sits; it can't print money or sign treaties; and everything it passes lapses six months after a normal government is back, with the regular courts free to strike it all down retroactively. Don't tell me whether this is a good idea. Work through what it would actually do: who would angle to get on it or keep it from forming, how the majorities would actually play out, what it would try to pass in the first week, and where the whole thing would go wrong.
