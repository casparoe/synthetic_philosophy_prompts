I host a small philosophy podcast and I've got an interview coming up with a philosopher who defends a broadly Parfitian view about personal identity. His line is that identity is a one-one relation, so it can't be what matters in ordinary survival, and what actually matters is psychological continuity and connectedness, with a no-branching clause for cases where a person's psychology splits. He's deffinitely the kind of guest who's heard every shallow objection, so I don't want the usual question about whether you're the same person as your ten-year-old self. Draft me eight to ten questions that work the machinery of the view rather than just restating it. I want at least one question on the teletransporter/fission case and the exact role the uniqueness requirement is doing, one pressing on whether the no-branching clause is ad hoc (if two continuers show up, why should that retroactively cancel the relation that matters?), and one on the Williams-style worry that psychological continuity comes in degrees while self-concern doesn't seem to. For each of these, add the follow-up you'd ask after the reply a standard Parfitian would actually give, and aim the follow-up at the weakest part of that reply. End with a question about whether the view is really offering an account of survival at all, or just quietly trying to change what we care about.
