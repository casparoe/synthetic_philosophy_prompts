Suppose a generalist philosophy journal adopts the following editorial rule for all submitted papers. In addition to the manuscript, authors must file a support ledger: a directed graph whose nodes are either principles (general claims) or case-verdicts (claims about what is intuitively true in a particular described scenario), and whose arrows record every place where the paper treats one node as evidence for another. Three clauses govern the ledger.

First, the registration clause. Any case-verdict offered as evidence for or against a principle must be registered as a datum before any principle is stated in the paper. The registration must include the scenario and the author's pretheoretical verdict on it, written before any general principle is introduced. A case-verdict that is instead derived later from an introduced principle counts as a prediction of that principle, not a datum, and may not be fed back as evidence for the principle that generated it.

Second, the transitivity clause. Every chain of two or more arrows in the ledger must be marked either transitive or nontransitive, and chains left unmarked invalidate the submission. A chain marked transitive treats the support at its starting node as reaching its endpoint; a chain marked nontransitive must identify the link at which support fails and state what destroys it.

Third, the simplicity clause. Simplicity may be invoked to choose among principles that fit the registered case-data equally well only if the registered data include at least one differential case: a scenario on which the simpler principle and its rival issue conflicting verdicts, with the author's pretheoretical verdict matching the simpler principle.

Do not assess whether the rule is a good idea in the abstract. Treat it as a mechanism and work out how it would actually behave: which styles of argument it makes cheap or expensive; how authors, reviewers, and journals would respond to or game the registration, transitivity, and simplicity clauses; where the scheme fails and what second-order effects it has on the shape of the literature and on the relative standing of case-intuitions and principle-intuitions as evidence. For each failure mode, say whether it can be patched without abandoning the rule, and how. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on philosophical methodology, and do not present a contested claim about, for example, whether evidential support is transitive or whether simplicity carries evidential weight, as the consensus.
