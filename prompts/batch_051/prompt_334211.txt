I'm writing an essay on the ontology of musical works and want to use the film Yesterday as a running test case. The film's premise is that a struggling musician wakes up in a world where the Beatles never existed, remembers their songs, and performs them as his own; the cinema audience knows the songs as Beatles works, while the film's characters hear them as original compositions. This sets up the central dispute in the ontology of music: on one view, a musical work is a repeatable sound structure, so producing the right sequence of sounds is performing the same work; on another, a work's identity is partly constituted by its history of creation, authorship, and performance tradition, so it cannot simply be detached from that history. Read the film through this dispute. Which view does the premise most naturally press the film toward, and does the film remain consistent with it? Which scenes or character choices would you build the analysis around? And where does the film resist a clean resolution of the debate? I want the reading to be faithful in both directions — not reducing the film to an illustration of a theory or bending the theory to fit the film — and to end by saying what the film adds to the philosophical debate about types, performances, and recordings that an abstract argument would miss.
