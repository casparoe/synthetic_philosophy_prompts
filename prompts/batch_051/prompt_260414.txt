I cover science and disinformation for a living, so I've ended up reading a lot of hinge epistemology on the side — the idea, roughly from Wittgenstein's On Certainty, that any system of belief rests on 'hinges': commitments that aren't themselves justified by evidence but are the fixed points that let you weigh evidence at all. Things like 'the world has existed for more than five minutes' or 'my senses aren't systematically deceiving me' or, in a newsroom, 'there is a mind-independent world that reporting can get right.' You can't argue for a hinge without already using it, so doubt about a hinge isn't so much refuted as never properly raised. I'm attracted to this because it explains my actual beat: you can throw studies at a committed conspiracy theorist forever and nothing moves, because the disagreement isn't over evidence inside the frame; it's over the frame itself. Fine.

But here's what bothers me, and why I want a committed verdict rather then a menu of options. If hinges sit outside evidence, on what grounds can I say someone's hinge is false, or worse than mine? When I report on people who treat 'the mainstream media is definitionally lying' or 'climate scientists are running a hoax' or 'the other side's voters are not really rational' as bedrock — those look like hinges, not ordinary checkable beliefs. It does not sit right to say they just have a different hinge and the matter's closed. Those hinges feel wrong: formed badly, held badly, the kind of thing that should be criticizable. But the view, as I've been reading it, seems to imply no hinge is more rational than any other, because rational evaluation only happens inside a hinge. So one of us has to give: either I debunk my Enlightenment instinct that every belief is answerable to evidence (treating that instinct as itself just one more local hinge), or I amend hinge epistemology so some hinges can be evaluated from outside — or from some thinner shared ground — or I've misunderstood the view and the two are compatible. Give me your actual position and what it costs you. Please commit: no 'it's complicated and both sides have a point.' I'd rather you be wrong than evasive.
