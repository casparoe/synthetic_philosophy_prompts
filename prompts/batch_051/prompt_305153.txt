I co-run a small philosophy discussion group, and for most of the past year its two most careful members have been deadlocked on one dispute. One, roughly a Scanlonian contractualist, holds that what an agent may do is settled by principles no individual could reasonably reject, and that justifiability to each person is therefore an individual rather than an aggregate matter: a principle that trades one person's serious claim for the summed convenience of many others is exactly the sort of principle the person whose claim is traded away could reasonably reject, no matter how large the total benefit. The other, roughly an aggregator, holds that this is a principled-sounding refusal to let numbers count, and that any view with the following two implications has paid an unacceptable price: it can never say we are required to save five strangers rather than one when only the numbers differ, and it can never trade any number of minor burdens against a single severe injury. Both are steelmanning in good faith, both think the other is quietly assuming the conclusion, and both are tired of having the same argument. I have persuaded them to attempt an adversarial collaboration, and they have agreed provided the joint document is one both could genuinely sign.

Please draft that document. It should have four sections. First, a numbered list of propositions both parties accept as stated, including the case judgments where they already agree. Second, a table of the remaining disagreements, each stated in wording that each side would accept as an accurate rendering of its own view and of the other's, with disagreements that are merely verbal flagged as such. Third, a short list of what you believe the true cruxes are. My own suspicion is that one crux is whether the reasons an individual has for rejecting a principle can be placed on a single scale of urgency or whether some claims have lexical priority over others, and that a second is whether numbers may count within a given severity but not across severities; but I want you to identify the cruxes you actually think the two positions turn on. Fourth, for each crux, propose a concrete case or prediction task both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. As a condition of signing, the parties have asked that the terms aggregation, respect, separateness of persons, and equal consideration not appear unless each is given a working definition in the text.
