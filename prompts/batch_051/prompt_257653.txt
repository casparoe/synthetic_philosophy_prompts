A line of argument I keep meeting in cognitive-science approaches to religion runs roughly as follows. Religious belief needs no special faculty and no supernatural cause: it is a byproduct of ordinary human cognition. People over-detect agents behind ambiguous or threatening events, spontaneously attribute minds to things that may lack them, and remember concepts that are mostly intuitive but violate one or two expectations, which makes those concepts especially transmissible. Religion, on this account, is what those biases produce when they operate across a population. From this causal story, some authors draw an epistemic conclusion: since religious beliefs are produced by mechanisms selected for predator avoidance and social information processing rather than for tracking truth, those beliefs should be heavily discounted or treated as presumptively false.

I want to know whether that debunking conclusion survives a later development in the same field. The debunking inference always seemed to me to lean on the premise that religion is a byproduct rather than something selected for. But the cultural-evolutionary work on prosocial religions and moralizing high gods claims roughly the opposite: certain religious packages spread because they made groups more cohesive, more cooperative, and better at competing with other groups. On that picture, religion is not a side effect but a culturally selected trait with a function. Does moving from the byproduct claim to the culturally-selected-trait claim kill the debunking argument, leave it unchanged, or force it to be rebuilt around a different premise?

- "argument_premises": a compact reconstruction of the debunking argument's main premises as you understand them;
- "development": one or two sentences saying exactly what the cultural-selection research changes or appears to change;
- "premise_assessment": an array of objects, each with "premise", "status" (one of "intact", "weakened", or "defeated"), and "reasoning" explaining how the development bears on that premise;
- "conclusion_status": one of "survives", "survives_with_repair", or "does_not_survive";
- "repaired_argument": the strongest version of the debunking argument that remains after the development, or null if none does;
- "residual_questions": one or two empirical or conceptual questions that would still need to be settled.
