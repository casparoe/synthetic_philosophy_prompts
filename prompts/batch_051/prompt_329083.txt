I've been thinking about moral relativism on my own — the view that what's right and wrong is just relative to a culture or society, so there's no culture-independent moral truth. I keep running into the same handfull of objections to it: (1) if relativism were true, you couldn't say the end of slavery was moral progress, just a change in fashion; (2) it makes real moral disagreement across cultures impossible, since each side ends up "right for them" and they aren't actually contradicting each other; (3) the mere fact of disagreement doesn't support relativism anyway, because people disagree plenty about scientific and historical questions without us concluding those truths are relative; (4) it's never clear whose code counts, since a culture always has subgroups, dissenters, and people fighting to change it from inside; (5) the claim that there are no culture-independent moral truths would itself have to hold in a culture-independent way to do any work, so the view undercuts itself; (6) you can't save it by saying relativism promotes tolerance, because tolerance is just one more local value on this picture, and a culture that praises cruelty would be beyond criticism; (7) the fact that a lot of people in a society accept some practice doesn't make the practice right — that's confusing what is done with what ought to be done. I'm told these are basically the standard objections, so I'm not looking for another version of any of them. Is there another objection to moral relativism I'm missing? It should be genuinly different from these seven — not a rewording, a special case, or a mashup of two of them. And do you think it actually lands, or does a relativist have a decent reply?
