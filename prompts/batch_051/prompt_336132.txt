I've been trying to pin down what actually counts as weakness of will, and here are the criteria I keep using. An action is akratic when: (1) the agent judges, all things considered, that doing X is better than doing Y; (2) she then does Y; (3) at the moment she does Y she hasnt abandoned the judgment in (1) — she is acting against her own current better judgment, not against a judgment she used to hold; (4) she does Y freely enough, and could have done X instead. Now the borderline case. A smoker three days into quitting tells herself at 11 pm that she won't smoke tonight. At 11:30 she gets a craving, walks to the corner store, and smokes. Afterwards she insists that even while lighting it she still thought not smoking was the better option. The wrinkle: a friend in my behavioural econ class said the relapse isn't really akrasia, it's just hyperbolic discounting. His story is that by 11:30 the cigarette is immediate and the health costs are still in the future, so her all-things-considered ranking flips: at that moment she genuinely prefers smoking to not smoking, which means criterion (3) quietly fails. That either feels like a clean dissolution of akrasia or like someone reading self-report sceptically and missing the phenomenon entirely. Is the smoker's relapse akrasia under my criteria? Work through the criteria one by one and say whether the case qualifies or whether the criteria — and the hyperbolic discounting story — need to change. Take a definite position and defend it; dont just lay out both readings.
