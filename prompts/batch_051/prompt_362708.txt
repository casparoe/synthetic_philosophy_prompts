In a seminar on the metaphysics of time, I keep returning to an asymmetry in ordinary feeling. A student who learns they will undergo a painful operation next year feels genuine dread now, though there is nothing they can do to avoid it; the same student, learning with equal confidence that they underwent an identical operation last year and have since forgotten it, feels only mild sympathy for that past self. The two events differ only in temporal direction. If eternalism is true, both are equally real in the block universe and the asymmetry looks like a bias; if presentism is true, the past pain has gone out of existence while the future pain will arrive, and the asymmetry looks like evidence for presentism. I want to know whether anything actually justifies our one-directional dread, or whether it is a brute forward-looking bias we should try to correct. Give me the strongest statement of each position, and then say which one you would keep and why.
