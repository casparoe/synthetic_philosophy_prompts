I'm helping design the amnesty screening process for a truth commission that's being stood up after an authoritarian government fell. We've got maybe eight months and several thousand applications, case officers with uneven legal training, and seven commisioners who each showed up with a different model in their head: South Africa's conditional amnesty (full public disclosure in exchange for immunity), Argentina's route where the early amnesties were later undone and prosecutions resumed, and Rwanda's gacaca-style local hearings with reduced sentences for confession instead of a free pass. I need a reusable procedure I can hand to case officers, not a position paper. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it passes the file up to a commissioner because the leftover question is a values call rather than a factual one, and what the procedure is structurally unable to settle. Then run it on three applications: a mid-level bureaucrat who signed orders but personally harmed no one and offers full disclosure; a low-ranking soldier who killed, says he was following orders, and offers only partial disclosure; and a respected opposition figure who authorized a bombing but refuses to name the people who carried it out. I want to see at least one case where the procedure returns an answer we'll hate, and an honest account of what it can't do.
