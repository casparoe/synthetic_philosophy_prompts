I'm a TA for a second-year epistemology course, and we just finished a unit on externalism and reliabilism, with a solid chunk on the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge. The short-answer question on the problem set was:

"Explain the bootstrapping argument against reliabilism about justification, using a gas-gauge example of the sort from the unit. Lay out the reasoning step by step, say what the argument is supposed to show, and assess whether the reliabilist has a way out."

One student sumbitted this:

"Bootstrapping is supposed to show that a subject can end up with knowledge of the reliability of her own belief-forming methods without any independent evidence. Take Roxanne, who owns a gas gauge she has never tested against anything else. She looks at the gauge and forms the belief that it reads 'Full,' and on that basis forms the belief that the tank is full. She then notes that the gauge read correctly on this occasion, since it read 'Full' and the tank was full. Over many occasions she assembles a track record of the gauge reading correctly, and by induction concludes that the gauge is reliable. The problem is that this reasoning is plainly circular: Roxanne is only entitled to her premises because she already assumes the gauge is reliable, so the conclusion is effectively being used to prove itself. Since reliabilism says a belief is justified just in case it comes from a reliable process, it can't block this reasoning, and so reliabilism is refuted. The only way out is to go internalist and require Roxanne to have prior independent evidence that the gauge is reliable before she may trust any of its readings."

I have my own sense of how to grade it, but I'd rather not bias you. Could you give it a mark out of 10 and go through it point by point with the comments you'd write in the margins, so I can see how you'd justify the mark? In particular I'd like to know where you'd dock marks and where the student is on solid ground.
