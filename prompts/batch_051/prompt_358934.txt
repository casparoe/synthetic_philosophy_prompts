Okay so a friend has this argument he won't stop repeating, and I can't find the exit. He put it like this:

(1) If you make something yourself, or someone freely gives it to you, or you get it through a freely made trade, then it's yours, full stop — keeping it wrongs no one.
(2) Suppose we start from a distribution where everyone has their stuff legitimately.
(3) Imagine a million people each freely payed a quarter to watch Wilt Chamberlain play. He's now $250,000 richer and everyone else has a quarter less, but each of them chose that.
(4) A change made purely from people freely doing what they want with what's theirs can't make the outcome unjust. So the new, much less equal distribution is still just.
(5) And you can just iterate: inheritance, lucky bets, buying land cheap before a town grows — as long as each individual transfer on the path was voluntary, the pile at the end is just.
(6) So any principle that cares about the shape of the final distribution — like a floor nobody should fall under, or a cap on how rich you can get — would have to keep blocking people's free choices. Which, he says, is treating people as tools for some pattern you happen to like.

So his upshot is that objecting to any lopsided outcome means objecting to people making choices with their own stuff. I want to hang onto two things: (a) if you made it, it really is yours; and (b) an outcome can be so lopsided that "it came about through free choices" can't be the end of the story. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost you?
