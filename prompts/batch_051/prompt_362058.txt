I am a novelist, currently drafting a satirical novel about the administrative state and its habit of trying to manage through documentation what cannot, in the nature of things, be documented. In one chapter the protagonist obtains a leaked internal bulletin from a fictional government agency—provisionally the Office of Persistent Information—that has for decades been responsible for managing antimemes: ideas that resist being remembered or spread, typically because the idea itself suppresses its own transmission, so that the harder one tries to record or communicate it, the more reliably it slips from the archive and from memory.

I would like you to write the bulletin itself, which I will insert into the chapter as a found document. It should be a deadpan, formal, genuinely dull piece of government prose—not zany or arch—running roughly 800 words, with a header, a distribution list, and numbered sections along the lines of Purpose, Definitions, Daily Operating Procedure, Containment Annotations, Audit and Reporting.

The piece must work for readers who have never encountered the word antimeme, but it should also be precise enough that someone who knows the concept can tell the jokes come from the real mechanics of the idea rather than from generic bureaucratic satire. Please build the humor out of that content. For example, the bulletin might have to refer to its most serious antimemes only by case number because reading the name is itself a transmission vector for forgetting; it might require staff to re-read the document each morning as a refresh protocol, since the knowledge that one has been briefed will itself decay; it might quietly concede that the office cannot distinguish a successful containment from a failure that has deleted the records of its own occurrence; and it might distinguish antimemes that are merely unmemorable from those that are actively self-concealing. The underlying target should be the fantasy that a sufficiently complete procedure can capture an object whose defining property is to escape representation, and the bulletin should be written as if its author believes in that fantasy without reservation. Keep it administrative, not horror or thriller, and let the document stand alone without commentary.
