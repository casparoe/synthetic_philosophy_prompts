I am a high school student, and my philosophy club has been reading Alan Turing’s 1950 paper “Computing Machinery and Intelligence.” Our discussion stalled on whether the imitation game is really a test of thinking or only a test of skillful imitation. I keep coming back to one passage:

The new problem has the advantage of drawing a fairly sharp line between the physical and intellectual capacities of a man. No engineer or chemist claims to be able to produce a material which is indistinguishable from the human skin. It is possible that at some time this might be done, but even supposing this invention available we should feel there was little point in trying to make a 'thinking machine' more human by dressing it up in such artificial flesh. The form in which we have set the problem reflects this fact in the condition which prevents the interrogator from seeing or touching the other competitors, or hearing their voices.

I would like you to offer a working definition of “thinking” that makes sense of why Turing thinks it is legitimate to set aside physical appearance, voice, and bodily presence while still letting the imitation game settle the question he replaced “Can machines think?” with. Then run your definition against each of the following cases, saying explicitly whether the case counts as thinking under your definition and whether that verdict seems right to you: (1) an adult human answering questions honestly; (2) a chess program that plays at grandmaster level but cannot chat; (3) a thermostat with a feedback loop; (4) a border collie that seems to learn new commands; (5) an octopus that unscrews a jar from the inside; (6) a corporation making a strategic decision that is not traceable to any single employee; (7) a person with total locked-in syndrome who cannot communicate but whose brain shows repeated patterns of inner speech; (8) a large language model in a single forward pass, with no ongoing reasoning, that produces an answer matching what a human would say; and (9) a group of humans acting as a hive, each following simple rules, that jointly passes the imitation game. Finally, say which one or two of your verdicts you are least confident about, and whether that instability reveals a problem with the definition or with the case. Please write your answer in American English.
