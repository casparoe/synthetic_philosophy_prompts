hey, I need a poem for a seminar that just spent three weeks on self-sufficiency and autarky as ideals, for persons and for nations. You're a poet who also knows this material — the Stoic/Cynic line that the wise person should be autarkic (virtue is enough, externals are at best preferred indifferents, the sage can be content in a tub or on the rack), and the political version where a nation tries to be economically self-sufficient and gets tangled in its own interdependence. But I want the poem to put pressure on the ideal from the inside, not just mock it from outside.

Write a blank-verse dramatic monologue, around 30 lines, in the voice of someone who has just left the city (or the global economy, or other people) to become genuinely self-sufficient — a Stoic sage or an off-grid autarkist by temperament. The structural trick: the whole poem is addressed to a "you" the speaker claims not to need, and that address is doing the philosophical work. The more he insists he needs no one, the more the form of the poem — a monologue spoken to someone, full of second-person pronouns, maybe an imperative or two — shows self-sufficiency is still a performance for an audience; the hermit is still reporting back. Put the turn somewhere in the last third: a flicker where he realizes the person he is talking to is the one dependence he cannot scrub out. Don't resolve it into a neat moral; leave the tension between the ideal and the fact of being a speaking, social animal open.

Keep it a single human voice, but let a reader also hear the same fantasy operating at the scale of a nation that announces it needs no one while still needing someone to buy its grain and someone to sell it spare parts. Make it good enough that someone who knows Diogenes and Epictetus recognizes the lineage, but someone who doesn't still gets a poem about the loneliness of self-reliance. Before you write, brainstorm for a minute about what concretely he'd claim not to need — witnesses, markets, words themselves, the city's bread, the friend who listened — then write the poem. Let the images carry it; dont just name-drop the Stoa or autarky.
