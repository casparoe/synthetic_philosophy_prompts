I chair the rules committee for a small housing co-op. We're rewriting our bylaws, and the real fights are about amendment rules: which rules can be changed, by what margin, and whether the amendment section can amend itself. We're about twenty amateurs, we meet monthly, nobody's a lawyer, and the full board has to vote on every change. We keep accidentally proposing rules that lock themselves in or quietly break our own procedure. I want a reusable screening procedure the board can run on any proposed amendment before we debate substance. The backdrop is the Nomic-style point that the rules for changing rules are themselves rules in the same rulebook, so an amendment can in principle change the rules about changing rules, or repeal the clause that gives us the power to amend at all. Turn that into a practical checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand off to a person because it's a judgment call about what the co-op even is. Then run it on three proposals: (1) an amendment making two clauses mutually entrenching, so each can only be amended if the other is amended at the same time; (2) an amendment that raises the threshold for amending the amendment section to unanimity, adopted under that section's old lower threshold; (3) an amendment, moved under the current amendment power, that would repeal the clause granting that power. I personally favor the unanimity threshold, by the way. Make sure at least one case comes out as a no even though I'd like it to pass, and say explicitly what the checklist can't decide.
