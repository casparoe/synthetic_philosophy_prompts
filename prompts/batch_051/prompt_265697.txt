I host a small philosophy podcast and I'm about to record an episode with Plutarch on what biographies are actually for. I want the whole interview to turn on one specific passage instead of the usual line about writing Lives rather than Histories, which he's probably tired of being asked about. Heres the passage, from the opening of his Life of Aemilius Paulus (Loeb translation):

I began the writing of my "Lives" for the sake of others, but I find that I am continuing the work and delighting in it now for my own sake also, using history as a mirror and endeavouring in a manner to fashion and adorn my life in conformity with the virtues therein depicted. For the result is like nothing else than daily living and associating together, when I receive and welcome each subject of my history in turn as my guest, so to speak, and observe carefully "how large he was and of what mien," and select from his career what is most important and most beautiful to know.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that press him on this particular claim. I want at least one question each on: the mirror metaphor (what exactly is the mirror supposed to do to the reader, and why wouldnt a distorted or airbrushed mirror do that job just as well), the guest/host image (what does it mean to welcome a historical character as a guest, and does he really extend that hospitality to the likes of Demetrius or Antony), and the fact that this passage says he's improving himself rather than his readers. For each question include the follow-up you'd ask once he gives the reply he's most likely to give. End with a question that forces him to say what evidence, if any, could show that reading a Life leaves someone morally unchanged or worse. Anticipate his standard deflections and build the sequence so they get harder to use.
