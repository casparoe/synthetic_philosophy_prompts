I've been going around in circles on Sen's liberal paradox, and what I want is a new escape route, not a summary. The moves I already know, crudely: (1) restrict the domain of admissible preference profiles, e.g. exclude meddlesome preferences where someone's ranking of two states depends only on what another person does; (2) weaken Pareto so it only applies to non-meddlesome preferences; (3) reformulate rights as game forms rather than as decisive sets over pairs of outcomes; (4) treat rights as side constraints or constraints on acceptable outcomes, with Pareto applying only after those constraints have done their filtering; (5) give up transitivity or completeness of social preference, so the cycle just sits there and doesn't generate a contradiction; and (6) allow richer information than ordinal non-comparable utilities — e.g. interpersonal comparisons or non-welfarist information about well-being — so the Pareto-superior move needn't win. Please don't give me a variant or a combination of these. Is there a seventh move that differs in kind from all of these? If you think there is, lay it out and tell me honestly whether it works or just relocates the tension. If you think there isn't, make the case that these six cover the logical terrain.
