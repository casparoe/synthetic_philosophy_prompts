I am a graduate student in biology, and last month my brother-in-law dismissed evolution as unfalsifiable, appealing to Popper's early claim that Darwinism belongs to metaphysics rather than to testable science; I know Popper later qualified the charge, but I want to understand what the charge gets wrong in its own terms. In trying to answer him, I constructed the following argument, and it now troubles me because it proves too much.

(1) A system of claims is scientific only if it is falsifiable: it must forbid at least one possible observation, in the sense of entailing that some observation report is false.

(2) Darwinian explanations of particular traits typically take this form: a trait spread or was retained because, in the relevant ancestral circumstances, organisms with it left more descendants than organisms without it.

(3) When a particular explanation of that form meets contrary evidence — the trait turns out not to be heritable, the supposed advantage disappears under measurement, or the assumed ancient environment looks wrong — the explanation can be saved by revising auxiliary assumptions about ancestral environments, the boundaries of the trait, relevant fitness comparisons, or offsetting costs.

(4) An explanatory form that can be saved in this way, no matter what observation comes in, forbids no observation; so by (1), explanations of this form are not scientific.

(5) Therefore Darwinian explanations of particular traits are not scientific.

(6) The same pattern governs central episodes in sciences no one wants to demote: plate tectonics absorbs an anomalous fossil distribution by adjusting assumptions about ancient land connections and sea level; Newtonian mechanics absorbed a mismatch between the predicted and observed orbit of Uranus by postulating a then-unseen planet; radiometric dating absorbs an anomalous date by adjusting assumptions about contamination or alteration of the sample.

(7) So the falsifiability criterion commits us to saying that plate tectonics, Newtonian mechanics, and radiometric dating are unscientific in just the way Darwinism allegedly is.

I cannot accept (7), and I do not want to surrender the claim that Darwinian explanations can be scientific. At the same time, I find (1) hard to give up because it seems to capture what is wrong with astrology or with a creationist framework that can absorb any evidence. I would rather reject (4) or (5), but I cannot see how to do that without weakening (1) as well. Which premise or step should I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost?
