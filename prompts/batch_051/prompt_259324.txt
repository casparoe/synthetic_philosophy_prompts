ok so i've been trying to figure out what i actually think about owning ideas, going by cases, and my gut is all over the place. here are the cases and where i land:

1. a while ago a friend sketched an app idea on a napkin at a bar and never did anything with it. i built the thing and it became my main income. i don't think i stole anything from him, though in hindsight i should have at least told him and maybe cut him in a little as a courtesy.
2. in grad school i read a paper, absorbed the argument, and later wrote the idea into my own work as if i'd come up with it. that one i genuinely feel bad about, even though i didn't take the author's words, just the idea. and it feels worse to me than the napkin case, even though in both cases somebody else's idea ended up benefiting me and not them.
3. a neighbor gave me her "secret" peach pie recipe after making me swear not to share it, and i later put it on my food blog without crediting her. that also sits wrong with me, even though i don't think you can own a list of ingredients.
4. a company files a patent on a molecule from a plant an indigenous community has used medicinally for generations, after learning about that use from the community, and then charges the community for the drug. that one feels like straightfoward theft to me, even though the community never filed anything and the company did the "discovery" in the lab sense.
5. leibniz and newton develop calculus independently. i don't think either took anything from the other. no theft at all, just a weird coincidence.
6. a stand-up comic overhears a stranger's joke on the subway and puts it in her netflix special. i think that's wrong, though not ruin-someone's-life wrong; it feels more like passing off than robbery.

what strikes me is that in a lot of these the originator still has their idea after the other person takes it — taking an idea doesn't seem to leave the person with less of it — and yet i still call some of these wrong. so i don't trust my own verdicts.

thomas jefferson, in his letter to isaac mcpherson (august 13, 1813), says:

"Considering the exclusive right to invention as given not of natural right, but for the benefit of society, I know well the difficulty of drawing a line between the things which are worth to the public the embarrassment of an exclusive patent, and those which are not."

i read him as saying there's no natural ownership of ideas, and that the only real justification for giving anyone an exclusive right to one is that it benefits society — and that the line between what deserves exclusivity and what doesn't is genuinely hard to draw. maybe that's my principle: taking an idea isn't wrong in itself; it's wrong only when the norm that allows the taking (or grants someone an exclusive grip on the idea) leaves society worse off. but i can't tell whether that principle actually gets my verdicts or whether i'm just bending my cases to fit a quote.
