You're a philosopher of psychiatry in the Hacking tradition who's been asked to consult on a borderline diagnosis. Start by spelling out the working criteria you'd use for calling something a mental disorder: roughly, a syndrome (a cluster of co-occurring signs and symptoms) that reflects some underlying psychological or biological dysfunction, isn't just socially disfavored behavior, and causes clinically significant distress or impairment in important areas of life. Now take orthorexia nervosa, which is not in the DSM: a person becomes progressively preoccupied with the purity and healthfulness of their food, eliminates more and more food groups, spends hours planning and checking meals, feels intense guilt or anxiety after eating anything judged impure, and ends up malnourished, socially isolated, or unable to eat outside their own kitchen. Check this case against each criterion, and be honest about where it's genuinely borderline rather than a disguised clear case. Then bring in Hacking's idea of a looping effect: once the label "orthorexia" circulates, people classify themselves under it, their guilt and restriction change in response to the classification, and clinicians start looking for that pattern. Does the looping itself change whether the condition was a disorder, or does it just change the people who fall under the label? Dont just give a yes or no; work through the criteria and the loop.
