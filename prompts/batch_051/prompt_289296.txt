A friend and I just spent an entire evening going in circles over a split in the philosophy of consciousness, and I came away more interested in why we could not make contact than in which of us was right. The split concerns what is often called the meta-problem of consciousness. The hard problem, roughly put, is to explain why there is something it is like to be us at all. The meta-problem is the apparently more tractable task of explaining why creatures like us are so strongly disposed to judge, insist, and report that there is a hard problem — that experience has a qualitative, subjective character that no physical or functional description seems to capture. One side treats the meta-problem as a way of dissolving the hard problem: if we can explain why our cognitive machinery generates the intuition of an explanatory gap, then the gap is exposed as an artifact of that machinery, and what remains is to explain the illusion away. The other side grants that explaining the dispositions and reports is valuable, but insists that this leaves the original datum untouched, because what ultimately needs explaining is not merely the report or the belief that experience resists physical explanation, but experience itself.

What frustrates me about our argument is that both positions seemed willing to grant the same underlying material: the same introspective reports, the same experimental findings about attention and metacognition, and roughly the same evolutionary story about why organisms might model their own states. And yet neither of us moved an inch. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not really empirical, but instead concerns what may legitimately be treated as the thing to be explained — or, more sharply, where the burden of proof sits when someone says that an explanation of their reports about experience still does not capture their experience. But I do not trust that hypothesis yet, and I want it tested. Is the stagnation actually a factual disagreement, for example about what a completed explanation of the reports would have to include, or about what introspection can tell us? Is it a verbal disagreement about what should count as an explanation of consciousness? Or are the two sides simply answering different questions under one heading — one about the etiology of a certain belief, the other about the truth of that belief?
