you're an expert on akrasia, hyperbolic discounting, and the philosophy of action, and i want you to think carefully about something i've been chewing on for a while. i call it "temporal gerrymandering." the idea is this: standard hyperbolic discounting says the present self is overweighted relative to future selves — the near future gets a bonus just for being near. but i think the deeper move in a lot of akratic choices is that we quietly redraw the boundaries of the voting period so that the indulging self gets a majority and the future selves who'll pay the cost get gerrymandered out of the district entirely. "just one more episode" is only a close vote because the relevant constituency has already been redrawn to be the next forty minutes of me; the me at 2 a.m. doesn't get a seat. "i'll start monday" draws the whole weekend as a special district that's exempt from the rule. "it's a special occasion" declares today's self a protected incumbent who can't be unseated.

so my question is: is this a real mechanism or just a vivid metaphor? it feels like it has to already exist under some name — multiple selves, intertemporal bargaining, something of Ainslie's — so tell me what it actually is if so. and then give me the two or three strongest objections. i can allready see one: gerrymandering requires a fair map that's being distorted, but maybe there's no neutral way to draw the boundaries of a self over time at all, so the metaphor presupposes exactly what it's trying to explain. is that fatal, or just uncomfortable? start by steelmanning the idea, then take it apart.
