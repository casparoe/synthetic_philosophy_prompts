I'm putting together a philosophy blog quiz, 'which theory of when an act is sinful do you actually hold?', and I want it to sort people by how they react to cases rather than by which label sounds nice. I keep coming back to Abelard because he draws a distinctions everyone around him kept collapsing: sin isn't in the desire and isn't in the outward act; it's in consent, the moment you say yes to what you take to be forbidden. The usual sequence in him is suggestio (the temptation turns up), delectatio (you feel the pull, maybe even enjoy it), consensus (you consent). On his telling, only the third is sin, and once it's happened the carrying-out adds nothing to the guilt. People routinely run intention, desire, and consent together, and I want the quiz to test whether respondents actually hold them apart.

So: make me the quiz. Eight to ten concrete cases, each with two or three answer options, no jargon in the cases themselves, and no question that just asks what someone believes in the abstract. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: the external-act view (only the deed counts as sin), a consequences view (the sin is in the harm actually done), a desire-defilement view (the urge or the relishing is already sin), Abelard's consent view, and a Kantian-ish view (the wrong is in the maxim or principle you act on). Flag any pairs of positions your questions cannot reliably seperate, and note any answer combinations that come out empty — is nobody there because the position is incoherent, because it's ugly to hold, or just because nobody's thought to occupy it?

And please keep the Abelard exegesis and the philosophy in separate registers in the scoring key: it's settled enough that Abelard says sin is in consent and the act adds nothing to guilt, but whether that's defensible — whether a consented-but-unexecuted murder is really the same sin as a carried-out one — is genuinely contested, and a lot of later moral thought is an argument about exactly that. Don't present the consent view as the consensus, and tell me which of the quiz's diagnostic distinctions would collapse if the consent view were wrong.
