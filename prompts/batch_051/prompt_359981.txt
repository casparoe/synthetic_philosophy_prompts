I'm writing a short statement of my views on fictional names for a seminar and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. I hold all of the following: (a) Proper names are directly referential in the Millian sense—a name's semantic contribution is just its referent, and a proper name with no referent contributes nothing to what is said. (b) "Sherlock Holmes does not exist" is literally true, with "exist" read in the ordinary unrestricted sense, not as shorthand for a claim about descriptions or stories. (c) "Sherlock Holmes was created by Arthur Conan Doyle" is also literally true: in writing the stories Doyle genuinely brought a character into being, in roughly the way a carpenter brings a table into being, and this is not just a figure of speech. (d) Sentences like "Sherlock Holmes smokes a pipe" and "Sherlock Holmes lives at 221B Baker Street" are not literally true; they are true only according to the stories. Please go through these pairwise and distinguish the pairs that are in genuine tension from the pairs that only appear to be in tension. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what it would cost, and then identify the single revision that would restore the most coherence.
