Write a philosophical dialogue of roughly 1,000–1,400 words between two equally sophisticated and good-willed characters, Mara and Dev, on loyalty, whistleblowing, and the ethics of betrayal.

Mara should defend the view that loyalty is a serious moral relation in its own right: whistleblowing against a close colleague or a shared project is often a genuine betrayal even when it is ultimately the right thing to do, leaving a moral remainder, a sense of dirty hands. Dev should defend the view that loyalty is a conditioned and corruptible disposition rather than a fundamental requirement: reporting serious wrongdoing is better understood not as betrayal but as a redirection of fidelity toward the more defensible object — the victims, the public, or the truth.

Set the dialogue in a specific situation: the two are old friends and former collaborators, and one of them has learned that the organization they built together has concealed a safety defect likely to harm people. Do not resolve the dispute; leave each position with real force. Each character must respond directly to the other's strongest point rather than talking past it, and each should make at least one genuine concession.

The dialogue should be in the weeds. At some point, have it touch on most of the following: whether loyalty can coherently be owed to an institution rather than only to persons; whether the wrong of betrayal consists chiefly in deception, in breached trust, or in the wound to a shared self-understanding; whether a loyal person shares in the wrongdoing of those they shield; whether one can be loyal to a person precisely by exposing their wrongdoing; and whether a justified whistleblower still owes apology, repair, or continuing solidarity to the person they expose.
