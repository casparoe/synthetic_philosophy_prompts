Our university's teaching platform has this clause in its terms, quoted exactly: "Users may not upload, post, or share content that promotes hatred, violence, or discrimination against any group."

I teach a course on the visual rhetoric of political movements. Last week I assigned students to reproduce a historical anti-Roma propaganda poster as faithfully as they could — same typeface, same colors, same caricature — then write an analysis of how the composition does its persuading. One student uploaded her copy to the class discussion board, with her analysis attached. The platforms automated filters took it down, and now the dean says she "promoted hatred" by putting the image online. The clause was drafted a few years ago after a spate of neo-Nazi stickers around campus; I doubt anyone drafting it imagined an assigned reproduction for critique. Does a plain reading of "promotes hatred" cover her upload? Does the clause's purpose cover it? What would the drafters have said if asked? Which of those readings should the university apply here, and does it matter that the original is not just offensive but genocide propaganda? Also, say how confident you are in your answer and which part you are least sure of.
