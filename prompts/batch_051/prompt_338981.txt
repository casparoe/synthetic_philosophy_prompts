I'm writing a fantasy novel about the war in heaven and I've backed myself into a theological corner I'd like some help thinking through. The standard way people explain how Lucifer could fall is that God made the angels good, gave them free will, and Lucifer's rebellion was his own free choice made at some point after creation—out of pride, envy, whatever—so the evil is his and not God's. That story obviously leans on there being a before and after: first the angel is good, then something goes wrong, he's tempted or puffed up or bored, and he slides.

In my cosmology, though, the angels aren't temporal like that. Each angel makes exactly one choice, all at once, at the instant of its creation, and it sees with complete clarity everything the choice entails, including it's own final state. There's no process, no temptation, no habit, no ignorance to blame, and no earlier, still-good version of the angel that gradually corrupts. The rebellion, if it happens, is just built into the angel's first and only act of will.

So what I want to know is whether the free will defense can even get off the ground under those assumptions. Could you reconstruct the standard free-will defense for angelic evil, flag the assumptions that do the real work, and then run it on my atemporal one-shot variant and say precisely where it breaks or holds? If it breaks, does that commit me to saying God caused the fall—or only that people had been smuggling in a temporal story without noticing? If it holds, I genuinely don't see what free will could mean for a single, fully-informed, atemporal choice, so tell me what it could mean.
