I was going through my old lecture notes on the two-factor theory of delusions and whether delusions are beliefs, and I’ve tied myself in a knot. Can you pull the argument apart for me?

Here’s the background as I understand it. On the two-factor theory, a delusion needs two things. Factor one is an anomalous experience: in Capgras, the paitent sees their spouse but doesn’t get the usual affective response, so the spouse looks somehow not quite right. Factor two is a deficit in belief evaluation, so instead of rejecting the bizarre thought—‘it’s not my wife, it’s an impostor’—they accept it, even though it is wildly implausible and conflicts with the rest of what they know.

Now here’s the argument I can’t get straight.

(1) On the two-factor theory, the delusional state is formed because of a deficit in belief evaluation, and maintained because that same deficit stops the patient from rejecting it when it clashes with everything else they believe.
(2) A state that is formed and maintained through a deficit that stops the subject from rejecting it in the face of contrary evidence is not responsive to evidence.
(3) Responsiveness to evidence is part of what makes a state a belief. A state that is not responsive to evidence isn’t a belief; it’s something else—an imagining, an obsession, a passing thought.
(4) So the two-factor theory seems forced to say that delusions are not beliefs.
(5) But then the theory hasn’t explained delusional beliefs at all; its only explained why people form a certain kind of imagining, and it gets to call that a delusion only because it quietly treats the imagining as a belief.

I half-suspect the argument is too quick and that the slide is somewhere near (2) or (3), but I can’t pin it down. Is it valid? If it isn’t, exactly which step fails, and on what reading of that step? If it is valid, which premise does a two-factor theorist who wants delusions to come out as beliefs have to reject, and is there a harmless reading of ‘not responsive to evidence’ that lets them accept (3) without losing the claim that delusions are beliefs?
