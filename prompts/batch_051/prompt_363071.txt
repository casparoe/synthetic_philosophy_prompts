so I'm T.A.-ing for an undergrad law-and-tech course and I need a handout that'll actually keep people awake during the unit on contracts of adhesion. Could you write a fake terms-of-service agreement — deadpan legal boilerplate, roughly 650-800 words — for a fictional meditation app called something like MindfulScroll? The piece should be a sustained parody of clickwrap adhesion contracts and especially of the idea that tapping "I agree" on a screen nobody reads amounts to genuine contractual agreement. Target the practice and its doctrines, not any real company or named person.

The jokes have to come from getting the law right and then pushing it maybe ten degrees: a definitions section that quietly expands "you" to include anyone who has ever glanced at the screen; a unilateral amendment clause where continued use counts as acceptance of terms the user never actually saw; a binding arbitration provision that waives class actions and sets the venue somewhere inconvenient; a severability clause explaining that even if one part is unconscionable the rest still gets you; a section about how the user had a full opportunity to read the terms while the app auto-scrolled passed them. In the recitals, include the real legal argument that clicking is a manifestation of assent and that notice plus an opportunity to read equals agreement — but phrased so the reasoning comes out sounding absurd. Keep it flat and official throughout, no winking or joke signs. A law student should recognize each clause as a too-honest version of something real.
