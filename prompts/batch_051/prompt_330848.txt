Two friends of mine, Mara and Jon, spent a long evening arguing about whether the Kelly criterion is the uniquely rational way to size a bet, after someone described a game in which a biased coin paying even money could be bet on repeatedly. Their exchange, compressed:

(1) Mara argued that the rational stake in such a game is the Kelly fraction — with a 60% chance of winning an even-money bet, 20% of one's bankroll each round — because that maximizes expected log wealth and therefore the long-run growth rate of wealth.

(2) Jon replied that maximizing log wealth is just one utility function among many. A risk-neutral agent should bet much more, perhaps the whole bankroll; a very risk-averse agent should bet less. Kelly is rational only if you happen to have logarithmic utility.

(3) Mara answered that betting everything leads to ruin almost surely if the game is repeated, and that the arithmetic expectation of a multiplicative process is dominated by astronomically rare paths. What matters in repeated betting is the typical path, not the average over a tiny set of bonanzas — the right lens is geometric rationality, maximizing the geometric mean (equivalently log wealth), which uniquely tracks the typical path.

(4) Jon said she had just given away the point: "typical path" and "long-run growth" are not the same as expected utility, so at most she had shown Kelly is a good default for an indefinite repeated game, not that it is rationally mandatory. An agent with a finite horizon, or with a different utility function over final wealth, can rationally deviate.

They asked me to settle it and I couldn't. Please adjudicate the exchange claim by claim: say which of each side's specific claims are right, which are wrong or overstated, and who is more right overall.
