Most defenses of the harm principle assume that the harm in harmful speech has to be traceable to some discrete act that follows from the speech: a riot, an incitement, a ruined reputation, a panic. That assumption makes the principle seem clean, but it leaves it poorly equipped for harms that emerge across many speakers, platforms, and years without any single utterance causing the damage: the erosion of shared standards of evidence, the slow corruption of a public's ability to tell signal from noise, or the way repeated falsehoods rearrange what people are able to notice. I want to ask whether the harm principle can be developed so that it counts these cumulative, structural, epistemic harms without collapsing the distinction between harm and mere offense, and without licensing restrictions on speech simply because the speech might change people's beliefs.
