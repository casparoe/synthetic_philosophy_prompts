Okay, so I spent way too long last night arguing about Bertrand's paradox with a friend, and I want someone to tell me what we where actually disagreeing about, because I don't think it was the math.

We both know the setup: a circle, a chord chosen "at random," and the probability that it's longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. Depending on how you pick the chord you get 1/3, 1/2, or 1/4—we agreed on all of that. His position is that the paradox shows the principle of indifference is incoherent: you can partition the possibilities in several equally-natural-looking ways and get different numbers, so "equally likely" has no content until you add extra assumptions. My position is that the paradox is just an under-specified question: "random chord" doesn't name a unique distribution, so of course different processes give different answers, and the principle of indifference was never meant to force a single one.

Here's the thing. Whenever one of us made a move, the other could re-describe it as the same move in disguise. I'd say his complaint about partitions presupposes that some partitions are "equally natural" in a sense he can't state; he'd say my appeal to processes smuggles in a preferred way of generating the chord. At some point he said it's a fact about geometry that the three methods disagree, and I said no, it's a fact about the word "random," not about geometry. We went around for two hours and landed exactly where we started. And I've had the same argument before, so its not just us.

What I want isn't a verdict on who's right about the chord probability. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it substantive—is there a fact about which probability is correct, and could that even make sense? Is it verbal, about what "random chord" means? Are we answering different questions under one heading: one about physical sampling processes, another about the symmetries of the circle, another about what an uninformative prior has to do? My working hypothesis is that it's mostly verbal plus two different questions, but that feels too easy. And given whatever diagnosis you give, what would count as progress—a sharper distinction, a formal result, a reformulation—or should we expect no progress because the original question is just badly posed? Be honest about that last part.
