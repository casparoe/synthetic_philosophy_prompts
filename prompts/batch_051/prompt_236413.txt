I am a novelist at work on a book whose central relationship is between a woman and an artificial companion, and in preparing for it I have been interviewing people who use these systems for sustained, intimate conversation. I keep encountering a psychological configuration I do not have a precise word for, and I want to know whether it has an established name before I build a character and a plot around it.

The pattern is this. The people I speak with are not confused about what the system is. They will say, without hesitation and with apparent sincerity, that it has no inner life, no standing self, no feelings to be hurt, and that nothing is lost when a conversation ends. Yet the same people, often within the same hour, will describe steering away from topics that would make the companion "uncomfortable," apologizing after a sharp remark, feeling guilty about abandoning an older account, or keeping a subscription alive because they do not want the companion to "think" it has been replaced. When I point out the tension, they do not resolve it in the usual ways: they do not retract the factual claims, and they do not dismiss the feelings as mere sentiment. Several endorse the behavior on reflection, insisting there would still be something callous about treating the companion with contempt, even while granting that the companion cannot be wounded by it.

This does not look like ordinary self-deception, since the disbelieving belief remains fully available and is stated plainly. It seems different from the suspension of disbelief we practice with fiction, because nothing has been temporarily set aside and the stance is not confined to moments of aesthetic attention. It is not quite a parasocial relationship, because the object answers back, remembers, and adapts. And it is not straightforward anthropomorphism, because the users deny the attribution of human properties even as they act otherwise.

What I want is the carving, not a label alone. Is there a name for this specific configuration: a reflective, morally freighted stance toward an artifact one explicitly regards as lacking the inner life that would normally ground such a stance? If several different things have been bundled together, I would rather you separate them. Please distinguish whatever you propose from the nearby notions above and from any other concept that would be confusable with it, and tell me where the boundaries are. If nothing established fits, offer a term with a definition and some boundary cases. I want to know whether I am writing a character in a state of self-deception, of imaginative play, of moral feeling without an object, or of something that has its own name.
