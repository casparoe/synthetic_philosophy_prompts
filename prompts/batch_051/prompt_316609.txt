Here's a tension I keep hitting in axiology, and I want someone to walk me through the actual give rather than hand me the labels. I've been building a measure of how good an experience is where value is multiplicative: roughly, consciousness times happiness times duration. Consciousness here means the phenomenal intensity of the state — how awake and present it is, not "information processing"; a drowsy animal's mild pleasure is low on it and a human in a panic is very high. Additive scoring seems obviously wrong because it treats a highly conscious but completely neutral state as having value, and it treats a trillion barely-sentient flickers of mild pleasantness as chips you can sum until they outweigh a few moments of vivid joy. That looks like a units error. Multiplying gives a clean, non-arbitrary rule: neutral or unconscious states contribute zero, happier and more conscious states count more, duration just scales the product, and I don't have to legislate any thresholds.

But I have a rival intuition I can't shake: no number of faint, half-asleep pleasures can justify or cancel one fully conscious episode of acute agony. The weird thing is that the very variable my multiplicative measure is built on — consciousness — seems to be what gives the suffering its authority; the more conscious the pain, the more it matters, and at the top it starts to feel lexically weightier than any aggregate of low-consciousness positive value. One hour of a fully awake person being tortured is not made up for by any quantity of dozing mice feeling cozy. The theory, by contrast, is sign-symmetric: a huge pile of small positive products is allowed to outweigh a deep negative one. I refuse that.

So do these two actually conflict, or is my intuition better captured by keeping multiplicativity and adding an asymmetry term — negative valence gets a steeper exponent, or a floor — and does that stay multiplicative in spirit or give it up? I want you to make each move earn its keep: debunk the intuition as scope insensitivity and show me it's an artefact; amend the theory and show the amendments aren't ad hoc; or find the reading where both survive and say what it costs. Then tell me which you'd actually sign and what it quietly gives up that people won't admit they're giving up.
