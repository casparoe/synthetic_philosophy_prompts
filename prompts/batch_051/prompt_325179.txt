For my metaphysics of time seminar I have to hand in a short "view profile" next week, just a list of the claims about time I'm drawn to, and I've been staring at it long enough that I've convinced myself the whole set is quietly inconsistent somewhere. I can't tell which bits are actually pulling against each other and which ones just *look* like they conflict from the outside, so I'd like a consistency audit before I committ to any of it. Here's the list:

(a) Presentism: only what exists now exists. The past and the future are not real in anything like the way the present is.

(b) But there are perfectly true statements about the past — that dinosaurs once existed, that Caesar crossed the Rubicon — and I won't relativize those to "true for us."

(c) Truth depends on being: if a proposition is true, something in the world has to make it true (truthmaker principle, roughly).

(d) In the ordinary case, past-tensed truths are made true by present stuff — fossils, documents, memories, causal traces, the current state of the universe.

(e) Even if every single trace of the dinosaurs had been destroyed long before any human showed up, "there were dinosaurs" would still be true, not just unverifiable.

(f) I can stand in real relations to things that no longer exist — being the descendant of a particular ancestor, admiring a dead poet — and these feel like relations to the actual people, not just to my ideas of them.

(g) I take special relativity at face value: there is no frame-independent "now", no priviledged cosmic present.

(h) Time genuinely passes; the A-series of past/present/future is objective and not just a projection of how we talk.

Which of these actually hang together and which don't? Please give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, no intro or conclusion. For each item, pick one real or apparent pressure point in the list, say how severe it is, and say what the cheapest fix would be — and be blunt about what that fix costs. I'd rather have the worst tension first and the most minor (or merely apparent) ones last, and don't just flag every pair as a problem; I want to know which ones actually matter.
