I keep turning over a line from Douglas Hofstadter's 2018 Atlantic piece "The Shallowness of Google Translate." About human translation, he writes:

"To my mind, translation is an incredibly subtle art that draws constantly on one’s many years of life experience, and on one’s creative imagination."

I think you can squeeze a much stronger argument out of that line than Hofstadter bothers to make, and the strong version lands somewhere I really don't want to go. Here's the argument:

(1) Translation draws constantly on the translator's many years of life experience and their creative imagination.
(2) If an activity draws constantly on a person's many years of life experience, then a person or system without many years of life experience cannot genuinely do that activity.
(3) A statistical machine-translation system has no life experience at all, never mind many years of it, and no creative imagination; it only has weights trained on a corpus.
(4) So a statistical machine-translation system cannot genuinely translate — it doesn't understand what it's doing. Its output is not a real translation, just something that looks like one.

Fine so far, maybe. But now take a nineteen-year-old exchange student who fluently and correctly turns a Danish bus schedule into English for her host family, or a paralegal who produces a serviceable Spanish lease from mostly memorized boilerplate. Neither one has "many years of life experience" of the relevant sort, and neither act is exactly creative. The very same reasoning in (1)–(4) seems to force the conclusion that they are not translating either — that no novice and no routine rendering is a real translation. But that can't be right, or at least I refuse to accept a view of translation on which beginner translations have literally never happened.

So: which step in (1)–(4) do I give up? I'm sympathetic to Hofstadter's underlying instinct, and I also think the student and the paralegal are translating, so something in the middle has to give. Tell me which step it is, why it looked so solid, and what I'm forced to say about translation if I reject one of the other steps instead. I especialy want to know what view of translation I'm stuck with if I keep step (1) and bite the bullet on the beginners.
