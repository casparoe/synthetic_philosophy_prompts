I keep bumping into a pattern in philosophy of math that I don't have a word for. There are at least two equally standard ways to build the natural numbers inside set theory; in one, 2 comes out as an element of 4, and in the other it doesn't. Both satisfy the usual arithmetic and support all the number theory you'd ever need, so no ordinary mathematical question ever settles it. When I press people on whether 2 is really an element of 4, they say the question is ill-posed, or that it just tracks an arbitrary choice of coding. Fine. But those same people still treat it as an objective fact that 2+2=4, that 2 is prime, and that every natural number has a successor. What exactly is the feature that puts a question on one side of that line rather than the other? Does this situation have an established name, and how does it relate to the idea that numbers are only positions in a structure? I'm not after a one-word label; I want the carving of the boundary, and to know which nearby concepts this is and how it differs from them.
