Recently I re-read the part of Adam Smith's The Wealth of Nations where he says that a worker whose whole life is spent in one or two simple operations loses the habit of exercising his mind and becomes unfit to form any just judgment about the great interests of his country; Smith contrasts this with the varied activity he finds in what he calls the ruder stages of society. I found it disturbing because I manage a manufacturing company, and we have been pushing precisely the kind of minute specialization he describes. I have long thought of myself as a defender of markets, and I am now unsure whether my own beliefs hang together. I would like you to audit a set of commitments I hold. I am not asking you to settle the political question of capitalism; I want to know whether my own position is internally coherent, and if not, where the strain is.

Here are the five commitments. First, commercial society has a broadly civilizing effect on character: it rewards honesty, punctuality, prudence, and the ability to cooperate peacefully with strangers, and these are real moral improvements over a life organized around small-group suspicion. Second, the division of labor is the main source of the prosperity that makes generosity and civility more affordable, and so it tends to improve character through raising living standards. Third, human beings are formed by their daily work; a work life restricted to one simple operation is corrosive of the same judgment, sympathies, and public-spiritedness that commercial society is supposed to cultivate. Fourth, labor market choices are voluntary choices between adults, and it would be paternalistic to interfere with a narrow job that someone freely accepts. Fifth, an employer has an obligation to leave employees no worse as persons than they were when they arrived, even if the employment contract says nothing about their character.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs genuinely conflict, which only seem to conflict, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost someone who wants to defend markets in a fairly ordinary way. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.

Please write in American English, as continuous prose only, like a letter or email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
