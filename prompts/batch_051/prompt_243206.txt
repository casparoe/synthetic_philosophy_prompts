Below is the account of the scapegoat rite from Leviticus 16:20–22 (King James Version):

20 And when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, he shall bring the live goat: 21 And Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins, putting them upon the head of the goat, and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness: 22 And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the goat in the wilderness.

I am trying to understand the conceptual mechanics of this rite, and I would like your help with a specific difficulty. The text says that Aaron "puts" the iniquities upon the goat's head and that the goat then "bears" them away. Taken literally, this seems to treat wrongdoings as things that can be transferred by an act of touch-plus-confession and then carried off, as if they were a load. What conception of wrongdoing and guilt would make that literal transfer intelligible rather than merely figurative? In particular, I would like you to work through: (a) whether the rite presupposes a substance-like or matter-like conception of guilt, and which features of the passage support or resist that reading; (b) how the laying on of hands together with the spoken confession could be thought to effect a real transfer, rather than merely to depict one; and (c) whether the efficacy of the rite depends on the goat's being innocent, or only on its being able to carry the iniquities away. I am especially interested in why the goat is sent away alive into an uninhabited land rather than destroyed, and what that suggests about whether the problem being addressed is moral guilt, ritual pollution, or some fusion of the two.
