I'm drafting a summary of my positions in the philosophy of language for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. My commitments are: (1) Quine was broadly right that the analytic–synthetic distinction is not a principled distinction; there is no clean line between a sentence's being true in virtue of meaning and its being true in virtue of fact. (2) I accept confirmation holism: individual sentences aren't confirmed or disconfirmed by experience one by one; only sizable chunks of theory meet the evidence together. (3) I think the meaning of a logical constant is constituted by its basic inferential rules, so that someone who systematically refused to infer q from p and if p then q should be read as meaning something different by "if," not as holding false logical beliefs. (4) I think translation and interpretation are determinate enough in ordinary and scientific practice that linguistics can be a genuine science, even if there is no fact of the matter about certain Quinean gavagai-style cases. (5) I think the meaning of natural-kind terms runs through the world: water is H2O is an empirical discovery about the stuff our term denotes, not a stipulation. I'd like you to audit this set: which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions rather than presenting them all as equally serious.
