I write about the science of consciousness for a living, and there's a dispute I keep coming back to because I can't tell what kind of dispute it is. The standard demonstration goes like this. A red spot flashes on the left; a few dozen milliseconds later, a green spot flashes on the right. What people report seeing isn't two separate flashes but a single spot gliding across, and it appears to change from red to green somewhere along the way. But the green flash that supplies the new colour hasn't happened yet at the point where observers place the change. It's as if the later event reaches backward and determines how the earlier part of the experience looked.

The trouble is that two camps describe that reaching-back in different ways. One camp says the brain revises the record: the person first really did consciously see the red flash as it was — a stationary red spot, no colour change — and then, when the green flash arrived, the brain went back and edited the memory of it. What the person ends up reporting is the edited version. The other camp says no: there was never a completed conscious experience of that unedited version. The brain held off finishing the experience until both flashes were in, and the first and only conscious experience was already the moving, colour-changing one. These are sometimes called the Orwellian and Stalinesque readings, though I find the labels more vivid than precise. Both accounts seem compatible with the same reports, the same timing measurements, the same physiology, and the debate has been running for decades.

My hunch is that the disagreement is really about what it takes for something to count as part of conscious experience in the first place — a criterion dispute rather than a factual one — but I've also heard researchers claim it's settleable, and I don't trust my own diagnosis. So: why has this dispute not collapsed, and what kind of disagreement is it? Is it substantive, verbal, or two different questions sharing one name? What would actually count as progress — an experiment, a conceptual distinction, a reframing, or just dropping the question as badly posed? And please be explicit about which parts of this are settled and which parts are genuinely contested among specialists; don't present one side's framing as the consensus if it isnt. I'm not after a literature review or a reading list. Answer in British English, no more than 700 words.
