Expected value fanaticism, as the term will be used here, is the view that for any ordinary course of action and any nonzero probability, however small, there is some possible payoff large enough that a gamble on that payoff has higher expected value than the ordinary action. Explain the structure of the argument for this view, identify the premise that the standard bounded-utility response rejects, and discuss whether that response is best understood as a principled constraint on the structure of value or as an ad hoc fix introduced only to avoid fanatical conclusions.
