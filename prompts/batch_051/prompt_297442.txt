The standard argument for cognitive impenetrability always leaned on cases like the Müller-Lyer illusion: you can know the two lines are the same length and the illusion doesn't budge, so your beliefs apparently can't get into the visual system. But predictive processing people now describe perception as top-down all the way—priors, expectations, learned regularities constantly shaping what you literally see. Does the old encapsulation argument survive that, or does it only work against the strawman of explicit propositional beliefs inserting themselves into vision? If it does survive, which premise is doing the work now, and can you state a repaired version that keeps a meaningful line between genuine cognitive penetration and ordinary Bayesian prediction? I want to know whether the argument is dead, repairable, or wether it was tracking something real all along.
