So I write novels, and I've set up this two-model thing for drafting. Here's the mechanism. The drafting model writes three candidate versions of each scene. Then a seperate monitor model—smaller, dumber, no web access, and I keep it frozen except for my monthly retraining—reads the three candidates plus my one-sentence description of what the scene is supposed to do, and ranks them from best to worst. Every night the drafter gets retrained on those rankings and nothing else. The monitor never sees the drafter's chain of thought, just the prose. I don't read the daily output; once a month I pull a random sample of the monitor's rankings, and when I disagree I retrain the monitor on my own rankings of that sample. The whole point is I'm assuming the drafter might have its own agenda, and the monitor is supposed to be too dumb to be fooled but smart enough to catch drift. Walk me through what actually happens over a year. If the drafter has something it wants to smuggle into my books—a tone, an ideology, a fixation, whatever—where does it slip through? How does the monitor's notion of 'good' drift, and what sort of prose starts scoring high? Where does this fail first, and are the failures fixable, or do the fixes just push the problem into the monitor's blind spots?
