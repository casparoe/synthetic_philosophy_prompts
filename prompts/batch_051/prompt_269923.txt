I retired from a working life in experimental physics a few years ago and have been spending my time on philosphy of physics, mostly the Maxwell's demon / thermodynamics-of-computation stuff. One claim keeps coming up: that Landauer's principle shows information is physical, not merely a useful way of bookkeeping. People wave at the kT ln 2 cost of erasing a bit and act as if the metaphysics is done. As someone who used to design experiments, I want to know whether that claim is testable at all, and if so what the test would actually show. So here is the assignment I'd give a bright postdoc. Design the strongest experiment or observation you can — real or idealized — that bears on the claim that information is physical. Say which result would count in favor and which against. Then tell me honestly what a clean positive result would and would not settle: does it demonstrate that information has irreducible physical costs, or that information itself is a physical thing, or is the slogan just a forceful way of saying that no information processing happens except in a physical substrate? I'd like you to commit: pick the most defensible version of the claim, design the test around it, and then say whether the test, even at its best, can vindicate that version or only a weaker one.
