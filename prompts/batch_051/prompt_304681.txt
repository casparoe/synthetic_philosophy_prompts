On the other side is the pluralist view I associate with Isaiah Berlin: there are multiple irreducible goods that can come into genuine conflict, and they cannot be ranked by any common measure without distortion. Restate each position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually work in — scalar reward functions, vector-valued rewards, Pareto dominance and Pareto frontiers, total versus partial orderings, lexicographic preferences, reward shaping, and, where relevant, the aggregation problem from social choice. But I suspect that hypothesis is too deflationary. Tell me which parts of the debate translate cleanly into my vocabulary, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all, and then give a verdict on the residue: is it pointing at something my framework cannot represent, at something the philosophers have left under-specified, or both?
