I've been keeping a record of the verdicts a friend of mine gives about whether something counts as art. She has no art-school or philosophy background, but her judgments are remarkably consistent. Here are the calls she has already made, always as a simple "art" or "not art":

1. A sunset that everyone stopped to watch because it was so beautiful: not art.
2. A piece of music a composer wrote out of grief and then played for a small group of friends: art.
3. A landscape painting hung in a small local gallery, painted because the artist wanted to capture the feeling of a particular evening: art.
4. A pattern produced by a weather simulator, saved to a computer, and never shown to anyone: not art.
5. A mass-produced folding chair, unmodified, for sale in an office-supply store: not art.
6. A blank white canvas, framed, hanging in a museum with a title and a short statement from the artist about what he meant by it: art.
7. The same kind of blank white canvas, stapled to its frame, sitting unlabeled in the museum's supply closet: not art.
8. A poem a woman wrote to make sense of her grief, which she read once, aloud, to her sister: art.

Next week she will be asked about a new case. A man picks up an unremarkable gray rock from a creek bed and takes it home. Without changing the rock in any way, he sets it on a small wooden pedestal in a community art show and places a card beside it that reads only "Found in Pine Creek, April 3." He tells the organizer that he has no particular idea or feeling he is trying to express; he simply wanted people to look at the rock the way they look at art.

What is the simplest rule that fits her eight previous verdicts? There may be more than one; say what the live candidate rules are and what each would predict about the creek-rock case. Then give your best prediction of her verdict, with your reasons for preferring one rule over the others. Finally, name one further case — not one of the nine above — whose answer would settle which rule she is actually using.
