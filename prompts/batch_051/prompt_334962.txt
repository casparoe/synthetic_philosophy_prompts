I'm about to spend a few weeks working through the corrigibility and shutdown-problem literature — the utility-indifference construction, its later variants, and the standard critiques of each — and before I take any proposed definition of corrigibility as the thing to beat, I want the full list of demands a theory of corrigibility would have to satisfy. The anchoring case is this: an agent with a goal has an instrumentally convergent reason to stop you from pressing its shutdown button, because being shut down prevents it from pursuing that goal, and a corrigible agent is supposed to lack that incentive. But the fixes I have seen look narrowly tailored to that one button, and I suspect the concept is doing more work than the button case captures. A theory of corrigibility should presumably also cover the agent's incentives around having its utility function edited, around modifying its own code, around operators who are uncertain or disagree with one another, and around third parties who might threaten shutdown as leverage — without collapsing corrigibility into obedience, harmlessness, or mere indifference to a button. I want the desiderata argued for, not just asserted, where the standard of adequacy is a design target for a system whose authorized operators can correct or shut it down.

Write the requirement list for this area. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it and say whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference. Then flag any pair or subset of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, under the assumption that the agent is an expected utility maximizer or that utility indifference is the main available lever — and say whether the right response is to relax one of the requirements or to relax that background assumption. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most work to exclude the standard candidate accounts. Spend your words on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring every contender against the list; a table for the requirements is fine, but the distinctions the theory cannot collapse are what I most want drawn out.
