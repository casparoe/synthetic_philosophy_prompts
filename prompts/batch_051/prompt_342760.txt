Write the joint statement that a committed "commerce civilizes" person and a committed "commerce corrupts" person could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on what market exchange does to people's characters. By "commerce civilizes" I mean roughly: when people have to buy from and sell to strangers they don't care about, they get in the habit of seeing things from the other side and keeping their word, so over time markets tend to make people more honest, reliable, tolerant, and peaceable. By "commerce corrupts" I mean roughly: markets train people to treat everyone as a counterparty and every good as a price, so over time they tend to make people more calculating, insincere, envious, and servile. In the statement I want: (1) the nontrivial claims both can sign; (2) the residual disagreement stated precisely, with each side's wording acceptable to that side; (3) the cruxes whose resolution would move at least one side, and make sure one of them is the bootstrapping question: can repeated dealing among strangers generate the trust and honesty it needs, or does it mostly run on a stock of trust built up by non-market institutions (family, law, religion, local reputation) that commerce itself tends to erode?; and (4) for each crux, the argument or kind of case both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Also flag where the original dispute was really about words. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
