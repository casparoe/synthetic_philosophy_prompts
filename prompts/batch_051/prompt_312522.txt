For several years I have watched two camps in a discussion group I belong to argue about whether rationality can be trained the way a martial art is trained. The "dojo" camp runs structured exercises—calibration katas, pre-mortems, base-rate drills—and claims the resulting habits transfer broadly, the way footwork transfers between fights. The skeptical camp answers that the evidence for far transfer is weak: improvements are task-specific, decay quickly, and largely reflect who voluntarily walks into the dojo rather than what the training does. Both sides appeal to the same bodies of evidence, and each accuses the other of quietly changing the standard for what counts as improvement whenever the data cut against them. My working hypothesis is that this is not, at bottom, an empirical disagreement; it is a disagreement about whether "rationality" names a trainable skill, a stable trait, or a norm of good reasoning. Diagnose the actual structure of the stalemate, and say what, if anything, would count as progress under your diagnosis.
