Give me a working definition of a person's values, of the sort one would need in order to say what an aligned AI should aggregate, optimize, or respect across a population. The definition should distinguish values from passing preferences, from welfare or interests, and from choices the person would repudiate; it should not presuppose whether all persons' values count equally; and it should not presuppose any particular answer to how conflicts among values or competing conceptions of the good should be adjudicated. Test it against these cases: a stated preference the person disavows on reflection; a second-order desire to have different first-order desires; an adaptive preference formed under severely restricted options; a purely aesthetic taste; a moral conviction about how other people should live; the welfare of a future person who does not yet exist; the distress of a nonhuman animal; and the implicit values inferable from someone's choices but never endorsed. I lean toward counting the disavowed preference as not a value, the moral conviction as a value, and the animal's distress as mattering without being a value, but I am uncertain about the rest. Propose the definition, run it through all eight cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.
