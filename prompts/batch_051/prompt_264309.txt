Write a dialogue between two decision theorists, one a causal decision theorist and one who accepts a form of acausal trade. They're arguing about the following setup. Two agents, A and B, are in causally isolated parts of the universe, so no signal can pass between them and there's no possiblity of retaliation; each is about to play a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against the other, with payoffs of 3/3 for mutual cooperation, 1/1 for mutual defection, and 4/0 for unilateral defection. Each agent has a high-fidelity copy of the other's decision procedure and can simulate it, but cannot causally affect it. The causal theorist maintains that defection dominates and is the rational choice; the acausal theorist replies that, if both agents run procedures which make cooperation conditional on predicting cooperation in the other's simulation, they can each do better than the 1/1 default even without a causal channel. Neither character may be a strawman: the causal theorist should have real answers to evidential moves, and the acausal theorist should answer the charge that he is confusing correlation with causation or engaging in wishful thinking. They have to genuinely respond to one another, and the dialogue should leave the disagreement sharper at the end rather than resolved.
