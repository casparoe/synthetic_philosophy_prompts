I'm a social psych PhD student and I keep running into a pattern I have no word for. People will take one another's word for all kinds of descriptive stuff—what happened at a party, whether a mechanic ripped them off, whether a supplement works—but the moment the question turns moral, a lot of the same people suddenly get individualist: 'whether that joke is offensive isn't something you can just be told; you have to see it for yourself,' or 'your ethical views have to be your own.' Then, sometimes in the next breath, the same person settles a moral question by citing what a trusted friend thinks, or accepts that a film is 'problematic' because a reviewer said so, without having watched it. It doesn't look like plain hypocrisy or motivated reasoning either, since they can often produce a plausible-sounding principle for why some moral questions need firsthand judgment; its just that the scope of the principle shifts with who is doing the telling and how much effort forming their own view would take. Is there an established name for this selective, unstable moral self-reliance? I know philosophers argue over whether you can get moral knowledge from other people's say-so, but I'm after the specific phenomenon: a moral question is sometimes treated as something no one could hand you, and other times as something you can entirely outsource, and the same person can move between these without noticing. I'm not satisfied by a bare label here—if it's covered somewhere, how do the nearest concepts (motivated reasoning, general skepticism about testimony, whatever's in the literature on moral knowledge) each fall short of it, and if it isn't covered, what would you call it and where are the edges?
