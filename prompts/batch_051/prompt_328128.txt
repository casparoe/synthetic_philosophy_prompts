I've been trying to pin down whether deplatforming and similar social sanctions actually count as punishment, and I keep going round in circles. Part of the problem is the motte-and-bailey shape of it: the company retreats to "we're a private platform, we can choose our customers", whilst a lot of the surrounding discourse treats it as if someone had been found guilty of something. I want someone to actually run the criteria rather than gesture at whichever side they happen to like.

I was taught that something counts as punishment, rather than just a bad thing happening to a person, only if it has all of the following:

1. It imposes a harm or deprivation (loss of liberty, money, reputation, access, etc.).
2. It is imposed because of a wrong, or at least an alleged wrong.
3. It is done intentionally by some agent, not merely a side effect.
4. It expresses disapproval or censure (this is meant to mark the difference from quarantining someone with an infectious disease, where blame isn't the point).
5. It is administered by a person or body with authority over the offender, under some set of rules, actual or claimed.

Now the case. A payment processor (think PayPal or Stripe) closes the account of a controversial writer and doesn't give much detail, citing a broad clause of its acceptable-use policy. This happens immediately after a large, well-organised campaign by the writer's critics, many of whom are openly describing the closure as accountability for things the writer has said. After other processors also refuse service, the writer can't practically sell work online and loses most of their income. The company's public statement is a one-liner: it doesn't comment on individual accounts and reserves the right to terminate for any reason.

Go through the five criteria and tell me where this case lands, and where the criteria themselves just don't decide the question. Then say: is the company punishing the writer? Are the campaigners? Does the case show that one of the five criteria is doing less work than it looks? I'm particular interested in whether "authority" is doing all the heavy lifting, wich would be a bit convenient if true.
