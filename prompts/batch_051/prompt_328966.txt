Next week I'm giving a talk to our AI safety reading group, and I want to prep for Q&A because I know exactly who'll be in the room. The crowd is mostly alignment engineers who think low-impact / limited-side-effect agents are one of the more tractable near-term agendas, plus two people who care a lot about embeddedness, and one ex-philosophy type who pounces on any unargued distinction. My thesis: the "just make the agent avoid side effects" program hasn't bypassed value specification, it has moved it into the choice of a state space and a similarity metric. Rough argument: any impact penalty compares the actual world to a do-nothing baseline; that comparison only counts as impact once you've already picked which dimensions of difference matter and how much; those choices encode a partial utility function whether you admit it or not. So you haven't avoided saying what matters, you've just hidden it in the metric. I'm not claiming the work is useless, only that it can't buy corrigibility on the cheap.
