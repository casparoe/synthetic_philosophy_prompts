I teach an upper-level unit on the knowledge argument, and the discussion keeps sliding between two different things Mary might gain when she leaves the room: a new fact, something true she did not know before, and a new perspective on a fact she already knew, the indexical or self-locating component of coming to have an experience. I want a thought experiment built to order that forces these apart, because I suspect much of the intuition that Mary learns something survives even when the qualitative novelty is neutralized and the only residue is indexical.

Construct a Mary-style case meeting these specifications. The subject knows every physical fact in advance, including a complete physical description of her own brain state at the moment of the critical exposure and a mapping from brain states to color experiences, so that before the exposure she knows, third-personally, which state realizes the experience had by the person undergoing the exposure. The exposure must then add something behaviorally testable: at least one forced-choice discrimination task that she performs correctly after the exposure and could not perform before it. The case must be built so that (i) the ability hypothesis is forced to say the subject gains only abilities and no new proposition, (ii) the knowledge argument is forced to say she gains a new, non-physical fact, and (iii) an indexical or perspectival view is forced to say she gains a new proposition she could not have expressed before, but that its worldly content was already contained in her prior description, so it poses no threat to physicalism. After constructing the scenario, show the reasoning each theory must give for its verdict, identify the single structural feature that lets the indexical view peel off from the other two, say what collapses if that feature is removed, and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the ability hypothesis. The case is for seminar discussion, so the verdicts must be generated by the architecture of the scenario rather than stipulated by the setup, and it must use no exotic cognitive powers or science-fictional devices. Please write the response as a numbered list of exactly six items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
