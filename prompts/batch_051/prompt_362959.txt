I keep going back and forth on causation, and I'd like you to help me think it through rather than just settle it.

On reflection, the regularity view seems right to me: when I see a match strike and light, all I actually observe is one event followed by another, over and over. I never observe any "glue" or necessary tie between the striking and the lighting. Any purported necessary connection is something I'm adding, not something I'm shown. The theory is clean, it doesn't multiply entities, and it handles the inference problem neatly. I don't need to perceive a hidden power; I just need observed constant conjunction plus habit. So far so Hume.

But I can't shake the intuition that there is something about the match and the striking surface, the phosphorus and the friction, that makes the flame happen, and that this "making" is real and not just a shadow of my expectations. When I say "the strike caused the flame," I don't think I'm just saying "these always go together." I seem to be pointing at something in the world that explains why they go together. The intuition gets stronger in singular cases: the asteroid that killed off the dinosaurs didn't just happen to precede the extinction. There's a sense in which it did the killing, even though that's a one-off case with no observed constant conjunction to lean on.

I know some readers of Hume have argued he actually kept a belief in real necessary connections in nature, and only denied that we can know or form an adequate idea of them, so the modesty is epistemic rather than metaphysical. That reading would rescue the intuition without pretending we observe hidden powers. But it might also be a comforting compromise that quietly waters down the regularity view.

So which should give, the theory or the intuition? I'm not asking you to pick a side and stop. I want to know: what would it take for the intuition to be the thing that gets debunked, for example a story about why we project our habits and expectations onto the world? What would it take for the regularity view to be amended to include real connections? And is the epistemic-only reading a stable middle position, or is it just equivocating between two senses of "necessary connection"? Give me the strongest version of each and then say which you'd give up, if either, and why.

Oh, and please write the answer in American English.
