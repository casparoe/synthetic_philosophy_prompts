My brother has been offered a standing bet by a wealthy acquaintance, who can comfortably absorb any payout: a long series of fair, independent coin flips. Before each flip, my brother may stake any fraction of whatever pot he currently has, and the unstaked portion simply sits there untouched. If the coin lands heads, the amount staked grows by 50%; if it lands tails, the amount staked shrinks by 40%. He intends to stake the entire pot on every flip. His reasoning is that one flip has an average outcome of a 5% gain — half of +50% and half of −40% — so the more money he puts in and the longer he plays, the better off he should be.

I have been thinking about this on my own, and I suspect he is confusing an average over the many ways the flips could turn out with what actually happens along one person's single path through the flips. After all, one win and one loss, in either order, leave him with only 90% of what he had before those two flips, and an unlucky streak could ruin him long before the lucky streak arrives. My instinct is to tell him to stay out of the game entirely.

Who is right, and what follows for how he should act? If the goal is to grow this one particular pot over many flips in the way a single player actually experiences, rather than in some abstract average over parallel possibilities, is there a strategy better than either betting everything or betting nothing — and if there is, what exactly does it amount to in this case?
