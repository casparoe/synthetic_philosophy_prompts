For the past several years I have covered climate science and the politics of emerging technology, and I am drafting a long essay that states my own position on solar geoengineering. Before I publish, I want a dispassionate audit of whether the position hangs together; I have been close to it for long enough that I can no longer see the fault lines. Here, condensed, is where I have landed.

1. The moral-hazard objection to solar geoengineering research should not by itself justify suppressing or stigmatizing that research. The objection is an empirical claim about how publics and governments will react once the option exists, and as such it needs evidence before it can outweigh the value of better knowledge. Ignorance is a governance liability, not a safeguard.

2. Termination shock is real and distinctive. A world that holds warming down by maintaining a reflective aerosol layer cannot simply stop: an abrupt halt would produce rapid, chaotic warming worse than what was being masked. This makes stratospheric aerosol injection different in kind from both emission cuts and carbon dioxide removal, not merely different in degree.

3. The states and populations most exposed to the regional side effects of aerosol injection — shifting precipitation, weakened monsoons, altered drought patterns — should hold an effective veto over deployment, not merely a consultative role. In practice, I would not endorse a deployment pathway that lacked such a veto.

4. Unilateral or small-coalition deployment is nonetheless the most plausible route to deployment. The entry cost is low compared with the cost of global mitigation, and after a sufficiently severe climate catastrophe the pressure on a capable state to act alone would be overwhelming.

5. Some outdoor experimentation is necessary before anyone can claim to know how much material must be injected, what it would do to precipitation and other regional patterns, and how reversible the intervention is; indoor models, however sophisticated, cannot by themselves close that gap.

Please do not summarize these commitments back to me or write a defense of any of them. I want an audit. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the least costly revision would be. I do not know where the main fault line is, so do not assume I have already chosen which commitment to abandon.
