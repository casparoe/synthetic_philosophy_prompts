Draft the joint statement that a committed modernist and a committed ethnosymbolist about nations would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, and have both sides sign it. The modernist holds that nations are distinctively modern formations, made possible by print capitalism, mass literacy, standardized education, and the administrative state; on this view earlier populations had ethnic, religious, and dynastic loyalties but no national identity in the strict sense, and the apparent antiquity of nations is largely an artifact of invented tradition. The ethnosymbolist accepts that the fully modern nation-form is recent but holds that it is built from and constrained by premodern ethnies: long-standing communities with shared myths, symbols, memories, and boundaries, which would explain why some modern nations crystallized while others failed. The document should contain: (a) a numbered list of claims both can endorse, formulated so that neither side would want to qualify them; (b) a disagreements table in which each row states the two positions in wording each side would accept as fair to itself, with any disagreements that are merely verbal flagged and restated in neutral terms; (c) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that at least one side would revise its view if the evidence went the wrong way; and (d) for each crux, at least one concrete historical or comparative case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Do not cite scholarship; the value lies in the exact formulations.
