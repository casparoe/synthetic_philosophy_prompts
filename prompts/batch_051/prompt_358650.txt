I co-run a small philosophy reading group, and we have been stuck on deflationism about truth for the last month, mostly because the debate seems to have been stuck since Ramsey. In Facts and Propositions, Ramsey more or less says that calling a proposition true is just a way of asserting it again; there is no separate property of truth to be explained. That thought feels right in a lot of ordinary cases, and yet when I read the exchange between deflationists like Horwich or Field and the correspondence and substantive folks, the disagreement somehow never moves. They all seem to agree on the basic data: the T-schema handles ordinary cases, the word true is mainly a device for generalization, and saying that snow is white and saying that it is true that snow is white are normally interchangeable. But the sides talk past each other year after year, and I can't tell whether the dispute is genuine or just a long way of talking about different jobs for the word true.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? My working hypothesis is that the two camps are answering different questions: the deflationists are explaining what the truth predicate does in language, while the inflationists are trying to say what truth consists in, and those projects never directly collide. Is that right? Or is there a hidden methodological dispute about what counts as an explanation of truth, or a substantive disagreement about whether truth plays a causal or explanatory role in belief and assertion? I want a diagnosis, not a survey. If the impasse is real, what would actually count as progress, and if it is not, what question should the field be asking instead?
