I hold a seat on the council of a mid-sized city, and we are currently fighting over a provision in our own municipal code. Section 2-74(a), enacted in 1998 and originally aimed at the public library and the municipal cable-access channel, reads: "All informational services operated or funded by the City shall be strictly neutral among the religious, moral, and philosophical views held by the residents of the City. No such service shall present any particular view of the good life as authoritative, superior, or uniquely reasonable." The city has now launched AskCivic, a generative assistant on the city website that any resident may consult with questions of any kind. The immediate dispute began when a resident who was torn between placing her mother in residential care and caring for her at home asked AskCivic what she ought to do, and received a single, fluently reasoned recommendation that clearly treated one account of filial obligation as the reasonable one. The vendor assures us the assistant is aligned by averaging the stated preferences of a demographically weighted panel of residents, so no one group's outlook is supposedly privileged.

The mayor defends the rollout by saying the clause governs whether the City itself takes an official position, that AskCivic does not promote a doctrine because it is only offering requested information, and that the drafters in 1998 could not have intended to regulate machine-generated text. But I cannot see how a confident, single answer to a question about what to do with one's life can avoid taking some stance on what matters in it; and if AskCivic were instead to refuse such questions, the very choice of which questions it will seriously address seems itself to be a stance. Read Section 2-74(a) for this case: what do the text, the purpose of the clause, and what its authors would have said if asked about a system like AskCivic each require? Which approach should govern here, and is neutrality even a coherent demand when declining to give guidance about a contested matter is itself a kind of guidance?
