Consider Thomson's lamp: a two-state lamp starts off, its switch is operated at times 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, and so on, and the puzzle is that the infinitely many operations before t = 1 leave no determinate answer to whether the lamp is on or off at t = 1. Now replace the switch with a continuous dimmer whose setting can range from 0 (off) to 1 (full brightness). Keep the same operation times and the initial setting 0, but adopt this rule: at each odd-numbered operation, move the dimmer to the midpoint between its current setting and 1; at each even-numbered operation, move it to the midpoint between its current setting and 0. As in the original, the dimmer has a definite setting at every time before t = 1, and the rule says nothing about t = 1.

Work through what the standard analysis of Thomson's lamp says about this modified case, and compare it with the original. In particular: (a) say exactly how the dimmer's sequence of settings behaves, including what its odd-indexed and even-indexed subsequences approach, and state whether there is a determinate brightness at t = 1; (b) say what this shows about whether Thomson's puzzle depends on the state space being two-valued, and identify which feature of the original actually does the work; (c) discuss whether merely stipulating a value at t = 1 would resolve the puzzle, and whether your answer differs between the two-state lamp and the dimmer version.
