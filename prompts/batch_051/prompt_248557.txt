I keep noticing a parallel between conceptual engineering and software maintenance, and I want to know whether it is deep or superficial. In software, there is a distinction between what a component is specified to do and what it actually does; when the two come apart, or when the specification itself is bad, one does not merely document the component's current behavior — one deprecates it, writes a new specification, and migrates the code that depends on it. Ameliorative analysis seems to treat concepts the same way: instead of analysing a concept such as marriage, race, or well-being as it is actually used, the project revises or replaces the concept so that it better serves our purposes, and then updates the practices and inferences that depend on it. On this reading, ordinary conceptual analysis is documentation of existing usage, and conceptual engineering is refactoring against a revised specification. Is this a genuine structural identity, or am I pattern-matching? If it is genuine, what are the conceptual counterparts of a specification, a call site, backward compatibility, and a regression test? And here is a disanalogy that worries me: a codebase can keep an old and a new API running side by side, but a linguistic community may not be able to keep two versions of a concept in circulation without one drifting or the old one leaking back into the new. Does anything on the conceptual side correspond to versioning a concept, or does the parallel break exactly there? Please also say how confident you are in your answer and which point you are least sure of.
