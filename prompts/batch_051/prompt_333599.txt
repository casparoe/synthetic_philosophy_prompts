I've been spending my evenings with Borges and there's a story I can't get out of my head. In 'The Secret Miracle,' a Czech Jewish writer named Hladík is arrested in Nazi-occupied Prague and condemned to die. He prays for one favor: a year to finish a verse drama he has been unable to complete. At the moment the firing squad is about to shoot, the world freezes — the soldiers hang motionless in their aim — and Hladík lives through a full year of inner time, mentally writing and reshaping the play, after which the suspended instant releases and the bullets strike him. What haunts me is the implication that a year of psychological time can occur inside a sliver of physical time, and that the inner year is somehow just as real as any measured year. Separately, I've read that Borges elsewhere argued in 'A New Refutation of Time' that time itself may be unreal: roughly, following Berkeley and Hume, all we ever have is a succession of mental states, so the single continuous, objective time of the physical world is an illusion we construct. I can't tell whether the story is dramatizing that skeptical view, contradicting it, or doing both at once.

Could you act as a critic with a strong command of Borges and the philosophy of time? Please start by brainstorming the different pictures of time the story could be seen to endorse, then settle into a careful reading. Specifically, I want to know whether Hladík's subjective year is meant to show that psychological time is the deepest or only real time, or whether the ending — when firing-squad time snaps back and has the final word — resists that conclusion. And finally, I'd like your honest judgment on whether Borges actually believed his refutation of time or was using it as a thought experiment. I'm a curious reader without academic training, so please keep the philosophical distinctions precise but concrete.
