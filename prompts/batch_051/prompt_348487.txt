I have been trying to isolate a moral concept that I suspect has a name but cannot pin down, and I would be grateful for help. The phenomenon is not valuing something bad, and it is not simply valuing something good too intensely. It is giving a good thing the wrong kind of role in a person's life. Consider three instances. A woman stops letting anyone enter her late mother's bedroom, then refuses to let the room's objects be moved even when her children need the space; the problem is not that she remembers her mother, nor obviously that she remembers her too much, but that a finite room has become the fixed point around which every other good must give way. The supporters of a political cause respond to every failed prediction by saying the failure only confirms how embattled and tested they are; the cause is not worthless, yet it is treated as beyond correction, with the posture one would reserve for a final authority. An intellectual treats a single theory not as one tool among others but as the lens through which a marriage, a child's struggles, and a nation's politics must all be interpreted. In each case the wrongness seems to lie in the place the thing has been given among a person's other values, not in the thing itself or in the amount of concern. I want to know whether this has an established name. If it is something like idolatry, how does that concept work once detached from religion, what distinguishes it from ordinary overvaluation, fanaticism, or simply being mistaken about how good something is, and can the difference be stated without presupposing a correct quantity of love or devotion? If there is no established name, what would you call it, and where exactly is the boundary? Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter; no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
