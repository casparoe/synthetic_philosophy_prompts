I'm a software engineer and I keep seeing people in the AI safety / longtermism crowd talk about "value drift" as if it's obviously something to prevent, but I honestly can't tell where the line is between value drift and just... growing up. When I was 20 I mostly cared about getting good at coding and building a reputation, and now I care way more about my family and not burning out. That change happened gradually and nobody forced it on me. Is that value drift? If it is, why would it be bad? If it isn't, what extra ingredient makes a change count as drift in the worrying sense?

In ML we have a pretty precise idea of drift: concept drift just means the statistical relationship between inputs and outputs shifts over time, and you retrain or fix the pipeline. It's not a moral notion, just a maintenance problem. Does the philosophical use of "value drift" map onto that at all, or is there supposed to be a normative component built in?

I'd like a clear explanation of the conceptual issue: what exactly value drift is supposed to pick out, and how it's different from ordinary preference change, reflectively-endorsed change, or changing what you want becuase you learned new facts. And if we're worried about the values of future systems or future people drifting, what's the ingredient that makes drift dangerous rather than just different? Not looking for citations or a reading list, just want the concepts untangled.
