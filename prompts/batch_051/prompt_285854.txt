Write the joint statement that a committed Epicurean about death and a committed deprivationist could both sign after a good-faith adverserial collaboration. The Epicurean holds that death is never bad for the person who dies, because a thing can be bad for you only if you experience it, and when your dead you don't experience anything. The deprivationist holds that death is bad for the person who dies when it stops them from having the life they otherwise would have had, even though the badness is never felt.

The document should read as the thing they actually produced, not as a report about them. Structure it like this:

1. Agreed claims. A numbered list of the non-vacuous things both actually sign. Include what the question is and isn't about, the plain facts about death they both accept, and the standard moves each side now admits are weak. Don't let them hide in "it depends what you mean by bad."

2. Residual disagreement, stated exactly. Each position in wording that its own side would say yes to, without either side writing a strawman of the other.

3. Cruxes, as a table. For each crux give: the sub-question that would actually move at least one of them; the case, argument, or kind of evidence both accept as bearing on it; and each side's prediction about how it comes out, written down in advance. Make it an actual table.

4. A short note on any part of the dispute they concluded was purely verbal.

Push into the weeds. Make them work through the symmetry argument (if the nonexistence before you were born doesn't trouble you, why should the one after?); the timing question (if death is bad for you, when exactly is it bad — before, at, or after the moment of death?); and the baseline problem for lost goods (lost relative to what, exactly, and do the goods have to be ones you actually wanted or would have wanted?). I suspect the real cruxes will include whether a person has to exist at some time to be harmed, and whether a comparative harm has to be located in experience — but let the two parties arrive at whatever cruxes they'd actually own. If they genuinely can't write a joint wording for one of these, say so and give the closest they got, rather than faking it. Aim for around 800–1200 words.
