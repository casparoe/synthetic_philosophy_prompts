Act as a professional philosopher who works on metaethics and the theory of value over very long timescales. Start by steelmanning both sides before you weigh in.

Here's where I'm stuck. On one side, I'm becoming convinced that talk about bad values in the far future is confused. Our values—that suffering is bad, that autonomy and fairness matter—look like contingent outputs of evolution and cultural drift. There is no neutral, perspective-free place to stand from which to judge the values of future beings: by their lights, and by the lights of whatever process produced them, their values will feel just as self-evident as ours do. If a future civilization ends up full of beings who genuinely and reflectively value something I find horrific (they treat the creation of suffering as a great good, or value paperclips over everything), I can't point to any fact in the universe that makes their values wrong and ours right. So the idea that the future could go wrong value-wise starts to look like a parochial illusion: it's just the psychological observation that our current values would be disappointed.

And yet I cannot actually make myself believe it. The intuition that a future in which conscious minds deliberately maximize, celebrate, and lock in something like pure suffering is not merely different from ours but catastrophically worse is about as firm as any intuition I have. I don't experience it as a parochial preference; I experience it as something I've gotten right and they'd of gotten wrong. If a theory of value tells me there's no sense in which that future is worse, I don't feel enlightened—I feel like the theory is leaving something out.

So which should yield? Give me the strongest case that the intuition should be debunked and dropped (maybe it's an artifact of my being unable to simulate what it's like to genuinely value suffering, or scope insensitivity, or status quo bias), and the strongest case that the theory should be amended or abandoned (maybe constructivism, or fixing on wellbeing and suffering as the currency that matters, or formal constraints on values that survive drift). Then tell me whether there's a reading on which both survive: maybe worse is always relative to a standpoint, and that's all we need for it to be rational to try to lock in our values. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, and what a negotiated settlement would actually cost.
