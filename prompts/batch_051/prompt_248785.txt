But almost nothing I actually believe is all-or-nothing — I'm only fairly sure my car will start, not certain. So I tried to work out what rules a *reasonable* partial belief should obey if its going to be the honest cousin of logic.

The rules I came up with:

1. If P implies Q, then your confidence in Q can never be below your confidence in P.
2. If P and Q have nothing to do with each other, then your confidence in P-and-Q should be the product of the two confidences (or something like a product — this is the rule I'm least sure about).
3. Confidence comes in degrees from complete disbelief to complete certainty, and certainty is just the special case ordinary logic already handles.
4. Evidence should commute: getting evidence A and then B should leave you in the same place as B and then A.
