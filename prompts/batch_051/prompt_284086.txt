I've been chewing on this for awhile because of a falling out with a buddy. He sold me a used car, and when I asked him straight out whether it had ever been in an accident, he said "the previous owner was an old lady who only drove it to church." Turned out that was literally true, but he knew the thing had been rear-ended in a parking lot, and he knew I'd take his answer as a no. What bugs me isn't just that he did it; it's that I can't decide whether what he did is the same wrong as lying or something genuinely less bad, and I keep flip-flopping. So I want you to write a dialogue between two people disagreeing about the general question: is there a real moral difference between lying and misleading? One takes the line that lying is worse because you directly abuse the other person's trust and turn them into your dupe; the other thinks that's a self-serving excuse, and that sneaky half-truths are often worse because they're harder to catch and they leave the victim blaming their own reasoning instead of you. Make both people equally smart and fair—no strawman—and have them actually answer each other, not give alternating mini-lectures.

The dialogue should dig into the parts I keep getting stuck on: whether the harm is in the words or in what the speaker intends the other person to end up believing; whether the liar at least has to own the false sentence while the misleader hides behind "I didn't technically say that"; whether the person who got misled is partly at fault for filling in the gap; and whether we only think there's a difference because we fetishize the spoken sentence. At some point, make each one concede that a particular case cuts the other way—like a small harmless lie to spare someone's feelings versus a technically-true omission that really messes them up—and have them shift their view at least a little on some sub-point rather than just defending their side. It's fine if the dialogue ends unsettled.
