I am a retired planetary scientist who has recently begun reading the philosophical literature on the probability of extraterrestrial life. I keep encountering two mature positions, which I will call the contingency camp and the robustness camp, that tend to talk past each other. The contingency camp holds that the origin of life and the emergence of complex, intelligent organisms are contingent outcomes of improbable transitions, so that life anywhere is rare and complex life rarer still. The robustness camp holds that, given an Earth-like planet and sufficient time, prebiotic chemistry and natural selection make simple life highly probable and complex life a broadly robust outcome. Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Format it as a numbered list of claims both sides accept; then a disagreements table in which each side's position is given in wording that side would accept; then a list of cruxes, with each crux accompanied by a concrete observation, experiment, or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. In particular, separate disagreements about the underlying transition probabilities from disagreements about how strongly a single observed history of life licenses an inference, and do not assume that the second reduces to the first. Avoid bibliographic references, and keep every claim statable without specialist texts.
