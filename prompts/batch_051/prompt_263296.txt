I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, so my working defnition of a privacy violation is probably crude. The four tests I use: (1) the info is about an identifiable person, (2) they didn't deliberately put it out there for anyone to take, (3) another party gets it without consent, and (4) a reasonable person would feel like they'd lost control of it.

Here's the case I can't settle. We built a model for a food-delivery app that infers labels like "likely new parent" or "likely working nights" from stuff users voluntarily gave us: what they order, when they order, delivery address, how often they use promo codes. We don't publish these labels; a model uses them to pick which notification to send, and no human ever sees them. A user who ordered family-size meals for years and then switched to small meals every other day at 2am got labeled "likely going through a divorce." She was, but she never told us that.

Does that count as a privacy violation under my four tests? Go through the tests one by one, tell me where they underdetermine the answer, and whether the problem is with my defnition or with what our model is doing. If my definition is missing a test (like harm, or misuse), say what that test would be and whether this case meets it.
