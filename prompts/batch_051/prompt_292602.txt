I'm the lead moderator of a mid-sized politics forum that deliberately mixes people from very different camps, and I need to turn our one vauge rule -- "no arguing in bad faith" -- into an actual procedure my mod team can run. The stakes are basically the whole point of the site: we get about 200 moderation calls a day, each gets two or three minutes, my mods are smart but have no philosophy background, decisions get appealed to me and then to the site owner, and if we get this wrong the forum either collapses into the usual partisan flamewar or we ban half the userbase for thoughtcrime. The easy heuristics fail in both directions: someone can be perfectly polite while poisoning the well, someone can be sincere while being completely sealed inside their own information bubble, someone can do the just-asking-questions move for a claim they plainly already believe. I want a reusable checklist, grounded in whatever philosophy and social epistemology actually know about good faith, echo chambers, and polarization. It should distinguish good faith from mere civility, from sincerity, and from openness to being persuaded, and it should be honest about how much of each distinction can be read off a single comment versus how much is a judgment call about someone's head. One more constraint: my mods have politics too, and I keep seeing them flag the outgroup while excusing their own side's bad faith. So the procedure should include some kind of self-check against the moderator's own polarization and tell them when their own priors are doing the work.

I want at least one of these to come out in a way my mods will not like, and I want you to flag the specific steps where the procedure is just dressing up a judgment call as a rule. Finally, tell me straight what the procedure cannot catch -- for instance, people who are fluent in the language of good faith but use it instrumentally -- and what I should instruct my mods to do when the checklist runs out.
