Something's been bugging me and I want it straight. The US spends billions walling off and patrolling its southern border, holding people who cross without papers, and chasing visa overstayers for years. But the same government also runs a Diversity Visa lottery that hands out tens of thousands of green cards a year to people chosen essentially at random from qualifying countries—no job offer, no family tie, no asylum claim required. That seems weird together: one set of practices acts like the state has a near-absolute right to keep people out, and the other acts like admission is just... a lottery, not something you earn or need. What view of the right to exclude does this tangle of practices actually presuppose? Be specific about which parts strictly presuppose what—does border enforcement force you to think the state has a moral right to exclude, or just a legal power? Is the lottery compatible with that underlying view, or does it quietly abandon it? And if we swapped the presupposed view for one that says states hold territory as trustees for everyone affected by their admission decisions, what in this set of practices would have to go first and why?
