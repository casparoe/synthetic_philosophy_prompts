Ok so I have this policy about probabilities and I want to know if it's actually defensible or if I'm just being stubborn. My rule is: whenever I know the official probability of something — a fair coin, a die, the weather app, whatever — I set my confidence to exactly that number and I do not let anything else move it. Gut feelings, what my friends think, weird patterns I noticed: all of that is noise unless it changes the stated probability itself. I thought this was just, like, being rational and not trusting vibes.

Last week it got tested. There was a big outdoor thing and the forecast said 30 percent chance of rain. My friend's older brother is into weather stuff, like storm-spotting, and he said the model that made the forecast runs hours behind and a storm cell was forming that would probably hit us. I still said my confidence is 30, because the number says 30. My friend said I was ignoring information. It rained, and now everyone thinks I was being dense, but I don't get why: the model already knows about storm cells in general, right? Or maybe it doesnt know about this one.

After that I got curious and read a little about how you're supposed to handle probabilities. There's this idea, I think it's called the Principal Principle, which says your degree of belief should match the objective chance, but only if your other information is admissable, meaning it doesn't give you a direct line on the actual outcome itself. And I can't tell whether a storm-spotter's live report is admissable or not under that rule, or whether the rule even applies to a weather forecast, since a forecast isn't really an objective chance like a coin.

So what I'm asking: is my policy defensible as it stands? If it goes wrong, where exactly does it go wrong — am I deferring to the wrong kind of probability, or is the problem that sometimes I should move off the stated number when my extra info is about the particular case? And is there a version of the rule that keeps the good part (not letting random hunches push me around) but still lets me update on something like a real live weather report? How would I tell from the inside whether my extra info is actual evidence about the outcome or just more noise?
