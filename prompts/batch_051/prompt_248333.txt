Write the joint statement that a committed defender of the principle of alternative possibilities and a committed Frankfurt-style opponent of it could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. By PAP I mean the claim that someone is morally responsible for what they did only if they could have done otherwise. By Frankfurt-style cases I mean the setups where some hidden mechanism would have forced the person to do exactly that if they'd shown any sign of doing otherwise, but it never has to fire because they go ahead on their own.

Dont just summarize the debate—actually draft the document. Include:
(1) a numbered list of claims both sides genuinely agree on, including which parts of the Frankfurt picture the PAP side now accepts and which parts they still reject;
(2) a disagreements table, with one row per real remaining disagreement. In each row give the two sides' exact formulations, each worded so that side would sign it; then the crux, meaning the sub-question that if settled would move at least one side; and, where one exists, a concrete case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance;
(3) a short closing note on which leftover disagreements each side suspects are merely verbal.

Please commit to specific formulations—pick one version and stand by it instead of listing alternatives. And make it a little in the weeds: don't stop at the usual slogans; for instance, have them actually work through what the hidden intervener's setup does and doesn't rule out about the agent's abilities, or whether little 'flicker' alternatives (like the chance to twitch, or form a different intention just before acting) can really carry the full weight of moral responsibility. Push the two sides to sign at least one agreed claim they'd each be a bit surprised to accept.
