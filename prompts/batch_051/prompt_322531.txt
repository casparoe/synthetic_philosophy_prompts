I'm a philosophy professor, and I've somehow ended up advising a small foundation that funds theory work on AI and society. They've got it in their heads that the next big thing is "safe Pareto improvements." As they use the term, a safe Pareto improvement is a change to a policy or system that counts as a Pareto improvement under every value-and-belief model in the set they're seriously uncertain between — so nobody's expected welfare goes down on any of the rival models, and you can go ahead without first settling which model of people's values is right. They can support about five people for two years, mostly philosophers and decision theorists, and they've asked me what the open conceptual questions actually are. I have my own hunches, but I'd like to here your take before I email them back.

Which open questions about safe Pareto improvements would you prioritize, and in what order? I'm less interested in the list than in the defense of the ordering: for each question, why it's upstream of other work, how tractable and neglected it is for philosophers rather than ML people, and what result over the next couple of years would move it up or down the list. Two or three things I'm already chewing on, just to seed the discussion: whether the right welfare standard is ex ante expected welfare or ex post welfare; whether safe Pareto improvements compose, so that a sequence of them can be jointly counted on not to make someone worse off; and how you even fix the set of value-and-belief models without smuggling in your own values. But don't let those frame the whole answer. If you think "safe Pareto improvement" is a confused category, or that the funder should spend the money on something adjacent instead, that's a legitimate answer too — just defend it with the same care.
