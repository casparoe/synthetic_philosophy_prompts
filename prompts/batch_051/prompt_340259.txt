Moore's paradox is normally introduced as the absurdity of asserting, in a single breath, that it is raining and that one does not believe that it is raining. The proposition thereby expressed can plainly be true, however: it is true whenever it is raining and the speaker fails to believe that it is. Why, then, is this called a paradox at all, and why is it treated as a problem for epistemology rather than as a curiosity of conversational pragmatics? If the sentence is not a contradiction, and if whatever is wrong with uttering it is fully explained by ordinary conversational norms against asserting something that defeats the point of one's own assertion, what does the phenomenon show about belief, self-knowledge, or the norms of assertion?
