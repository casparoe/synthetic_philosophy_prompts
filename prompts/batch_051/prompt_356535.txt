I keep tying myself in knots about what illusionists about phenomenal consciousness could even mean by 'illusion'. The obvious idea is that an illusion is a misleading experience — but then saying phenomenal consciousness is an illusion looks self-refuting, because an illusion would itself have to be an experience. So: give me the best definition of 'illusion' you can such that 'phenomenal consciousness is an illusion' comes out a coherent, substantive claim rather than a contradiction or a restatement of realism. Then run that defintion through these cases and tell me how it sorts them: the Müller-Lyer lines; a mirage on a road; a conjuror's trick where a coin appears to vanish; someone who says they're in agony and then winces; the idea of the self as a 'user illusion'; and the claim that free will is an illusion. Say which of those your definition forces you to call illusions, whether any verdict surprises you, and which is least secure. Finally tell me whether, on your definition, illusionism about phenomenal consciousness comes out true, false, or still an open option — and if no definition works, give me the best near-miss and say where it breaks.
