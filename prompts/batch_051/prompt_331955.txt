I'm an engineering manager and I'm trying to put together a one-page internal guide for my team, because we have a recuring problem I've started thinking of as micro-promises. In standup people say they'll push the PR by end of day, pair with someone on Friday, write up the incident notes over the weekend; then it slips, no one says anything until someone notices, and everyone shrugs because each individual thing is tiny. But over months I've watched it erode trust between teammates and with adjacent teams, and I don't think the usual advice to just underpromise and overdeliver actually touches the ethical part: when is breaking a small commitment genuinely wrong, and when is it just noise that a healthy team should absorb.

So I want a decision procedure an engineer can actually run. It has to fit on a page, take under two minutes to run before making a promise and under five when someone realizes they're about to miss one, and work for someone who will never read any philosophy. It should be grounded in whatever the best philosophical thinking on promising and reliability actually says, but translated, not name-dropped. For each step give me the question to ask, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where the procedure returns a clear 'don't commit' or 'tell them now,' and where it stops because the next step is a value judgment that a page can't make for someone. Be explicit about which steps are values dressed up as process.

Then run the procedure on three cases and show me what it actually outputs: (1) an engineer knows at 4pm she won't finish a bug fix she promised for end of day; (2) a mid-level engineer agreed to help onboard a new hire Friday morning, but a personal conflict came up, and pushing it to Monday would leave the new hire mostly idle for two days; (3) someone has a standing self-imposed commitment to review every teammate's PR within 24 hours and has started missing it roughly once a week. I want at least one case where the procedure tells someone something they won't like — I'd bet it says some of the smallest promises are exactly the ones you shouldn't break. And finally, flag the cases the procedure genuinely can't settle because they turn on what a promise does to the other person: whether the wrong is mostly in the wasted reliance because they planned around you, or in the expectation itself that you created and then pulled out from under them.
