For a comparative government paper, I have to state and defend my own views on group-based preferences, and I want a consistency check before I commit to them. My commitments are these. First, fairness requires judging each applicant or candidate on individual qualifications rather than on group membership. Second, there is no culturally neutral measure of qualifications: every test or interview encodes the background assumptions of the people who design it. Third, universities should be allowed to weigh race as one factor among many in admissions, but should not use fixed numerical quotas. Fourth, Norway's corporate board quota is justified, because it breaks the self-perpetuating appointment networks that keep women out of boardrooms. Fifth, India's reservation policy is justified as long-term redress for caste-based exclusion. I am no longer sure these all fit together. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what the least costly revision of my view would be and what it would sacrifice.
