I have spent the past year making decisions for my mother as Alzheimer's disease has moved her from the early into the late stage, and the experience has forced me to make explicit what I think about personal identity. I have arrived at the following set of commitments, and before I act on them further I want a consistency check.

(1) I think Locke's memory criterion fails as it stands, because the relation of directly remembering cannot by itself constitute identity—as Reid's brave-officer case brings out, it would make personal identity non-transitive. The right repair is neo-Lockean: personal identity over time consists in overlapping chains of psychological continuity, with autobiographical memory as the principal connection.

(2) I hold that an adequate criterion must be non-circular. I therefore understand memory as quasi-memory: a first-personal representation of a past episode, caused through an appropriate causal chain by that episode itself, with no prior assumption that the rememberer is the person who lived through it.

(3) I think the appropriate-causal-chain condition should be understood informationally rather than biologically; a process that preserved the right information about a past episode through a non-neural substrate could in principle preserve a person.

(4) I think ordinary reconstruction and distortion in memory are compatible with personal identity; what matters is the preservation of the right causal-informational relation, not the detailed veridicality of what is remembered.

(5) During the early stage of her illness, when my mother could not form new long-term memories but still recognized us and retained her values and humor, I regarded her as plainly the same person, and I treat that as a fixed point that any account must capture.

(6) Now that she is in the late stage, with almost no autobiographical memory, no recognition of family, and little continuity of values or personality, I find I cannot escape the judgment that the person I grew up with is no longer there, even though I continue to care for her exactly as before.

I would be grateful for an audit rather than a lecture: indicate which commitments are in genuine tension, rank the tensions by severity, explain which pairs only appear to be in tension and why the appearance is misleading, and identify the least costly revision that would restore consistency—and what it costs.
