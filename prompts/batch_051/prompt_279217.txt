I have recently joined the committee of a small political society that is revising its rules about which outside speakers may be invited and which members may be required to share a platform with. Our old rulebook gestures at Mill's harm principle but never defines what counts as harm to others, and our disagreements keep turning on that. I would like a poem to circulate before our next meeting: a Petrarchan sonnet in which the octave gives a careful, accurate statement of the harm principle as a limit on interference with expression, and the sestet turns to the case of a speaker whose words do not threaten violence or legal injury but are expected to cause genuine distress to a minority of members. The poem should leave the question of whether that distress is the relevant sort of harm unresolved, but the turn should enact the move from the principle's apparent clarity to the difficulty that has divided us. After the poem, give a short note on how confident you are in the accuracy of the philosophical content and which point you are least sure of. Write in British English.
