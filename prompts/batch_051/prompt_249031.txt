I'm in a philosophy of mind course, and I keep worrying at a set of tests our instructor gave us for when a mental item—an intention, a judgment, a noticing—genuinely causes a bodily action rather than merely accompanying its neural cause. The tests: (i) counterfactual difference-making: without the mental item the action would have been absent or suitably different; (ii) nonredundancy: the mental item must not be preempted by a distinct, simultaneous sufficient cause; (iii) proportionality: the item must be described at the right grain—not so coarse that it would be there even if the action differed, not so fine that it is tailored to just this one occurrence; and (iv) causal stability: the connection between items of that type and actions of that type must be reliable rather than coincidental.

Now here is the case I can't settle. A skilled accompanist has played a certain modulation so often that it runs automatically; her conscious attention is usually on balance, blend, and the singer's intonation rather than on her hands. One night, in the middle of that modulation, she hears the ensemble drift sharp and consciously registers that the chord is sour. As a result she makes a small, specific adjustment: she half-pedals and eases the pressure of her left index finger. There is a complete neural story available—a pattern in sensory and motor cortex that on its own looks sufficient for exactly those movements—and the conscious registration is realized by that very pattern, not an extra knob sitting beside it. A neuroscientist friend says the neural pattern screens off the conscious judgment, so the judgment does no causal work once the neural facts are fixed. I would like you to press this case through the four tests one by one and say whether the conscious judgment counts as a cause of the adjustment. Where a test does not settle the matter, say what further fact would. Then step back and say whether this case shows that one of the four tests is doing all the real work, or that the list itself needs revising. Please also tell me how confident you are in each of your verdicts and pick the single point you are least sure about.
