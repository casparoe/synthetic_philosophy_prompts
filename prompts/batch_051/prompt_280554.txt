I am a high school student, and I have recently realized that my ordinary judgments about time do not line up neatly with any single position I can state, and I would like your help in working out whether there is a coherent principle behind them. You are a professional philosopher who specializes in the philosophy of time. Please think through this carefully, begin by brainstorming the candidate principles before settling on one, and do not just give me a label; I need to know exactly what the view says, why it handles the cases it handles, and where it forces me to change my mind.

Here are my gut verdicts. (1) When the house I grew up in was demolished, I had no trouble saying, "The house no longer exists." But I also think the sentence "I was born in that house" is made true by something more than present records and photographs; it seems to reach back to the house itself. So I seem to feel that the past is in some sense real, while also feeling that the house is not there anymore. (2) About the future, if someone says "Humanity will land on Mars," I think that is probably true, but I do not think the landing itself exists somewhere, waiting to happen; the future feels open and not yet real. (3) Yet I also accept that a transit of Venus in the twenty-second century is already a closed, predictable fact of astronomy. If that future event is already a fact, then something about it is already true now; but if it is already true, I do not see how the future can be as open as I felt in case (2). (4) A friend of mine insists that only the present moment is real. I find this appealing, but then I remember from physics that there is no absolute cosmic "now": which events count as simultaneous depends on the observer's motion. If only the present is real, then different observers would seem to get different realities, which strikes me as wrong; but if the present is not specially real, then perhaps the future must be just as real as the past, which I cannot bring myself to believe. (5) Finally, I am sure that "George Washington was the first President of the United States" is simply and permanently true. But I would not say Washington himself exists now, even in a shadowy way; the man is gone, not stored somewhere in the past.

What principle about what exists in time could capture all of these verdicts, or most of them, and which of my verdicts would the strongest candidates force me to overturn? To test whatever principle you propose, please also tell me what it implies about a case I have not settled: next Thursday's lunch. When Thursday arrives, has the lunch been real all along, did it become real, or is that the wrong way to talk about it? Please specify which part of the principle handles each of my cases, and point out honestly where my intuitions conflict.
