Right, I've been going down a rabbit hole on this ergodicity economics stuff and I want to check that my position actually hangs together, because I keep feeling like I've got five things I beleive that might be secretly pulling in different directions. My background is software, not philosophy, so I've come at this from probability and expected value rather than any proper theory of rationality.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. For a one-off gamble, you should take it if the expected value is positive. If someone offers me a single bet with +£10 EV, I take it. That's basically being rational about risk over the long run of *different* decisions.

2. But for repeated gambles where your stake compounds, what matters is the time-average growth rate, not the ensemble-average EV. If I keep betting my whole bankroll on a positive-EV coin flip, I eventually go bust; the ergodicity people are right about that. You have to look at what happens to one person over time, not across a million parallel versions of you.

3. Kelly betting is the optimal policy for long-run growth, and it's also just the rational policy when the bet is repeated. I don't see those as two seperate claims, really.

4. I think there's no deep philosophical disagreement between the EV people and the ergodicity people. It just comes down to whether the situation is one-off or repeated, and mixing those up is a modelling mistake, not a disagreement about rationality itself.

5. Log utility is fine as a description of how risk-averse people are, and once you set utility to log wealth, plain expected utility maximisation gives you Kelly anyway. So the 'time average' stuff is kind of redundant if you already have the right utility function.

Are these actually consistent? Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only look like they conflict?
