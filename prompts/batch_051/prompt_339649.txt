Objections to nudging, and to libertarian paternalism in particular, that I already have and do not need repeated: (1) the autonomy objection — even when options are preserved, nudges bypass or exploit rational deliberation rather than engaging it; (2) the manipulation objection — targeting predictable cognitive biases is manipulative even if nothing is forbidden; (3) the epistemic objection — choice architects lack the local knowledge needed to advance each person's ends, so a welfare standard based on the agent's own lights cannot be operationalized; (4) the transparency and legitimacy objection — influence that works only when unnoticed cannot easily be squared with democratic accountability; (5) the welfare and backfire objection — the same fallibility that motivates nudging can make nudges misfire across heterogeneous populations; (6) the slippery-slope objection — normalizing elective manipulation lowers the barriers to harder forms of paternalism; and (7) the public-choice objection — choice architects have their own interests and may nudge toward those rather than toward the interests of the nudged. I believe these exhaust the standard objections. Give me one additional objection that is not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of them — one with a genuinely different normative or empirical basis — and then tell me honestly whether it is any good.
