I'm a software engineer on a consumer app, and after a long-running argument with my team about whether our onboarding flow is manipulative or just normal nudging, I started writing down the principles I actually hold, since I keep getting accused of being inconsistant. I've never done formal philosophy, so it's possible some of my categories are sloppy. Here's the list:

1. There's no neutral default. If the settings screen has a dropdown for data sharing, something has to be pre-selected, and whatever is pre-selected gets more takers. So picking a default isn't itself worse than any other design decision; the main question is whether the user can notice it and change it without much trouble.
2. But I do draw a real line between making one option visually salient and changing how hard the alternatives are to actually take. A filled blue "Accept" button next to a plain "Skip" link is fine. Making the Skip link take three extra screens to find is not.
3. At the same time, I think friction is sometimes justified when someone is about to do something costly and hard to reverse: extra confirmation before deleting an account, a brief cooldown before a big in-app purchase. People genuinely have self-control problems, and the product shouldn't just stand by and watch.
4. I've also come around to the idea that a design choice is okay only if I'd be fine explaining it to the user afterwards in plain words: why this option is first, why that button looks like that. If I'd squirm having to say it out loud, the design is probably wrong.
5. Autonomy, as I understand it, is about the alternative actually existing and being findable by a reasonably attentive user, not about avoiding influence altogether. Every interface influences; that can't be the threshold.
6. Social proof honestly doesn't bother me much: a line that says "82% of users turn on two-factor here" is just information, and the user is free to ignore it.

I can't shake the feeling that a few of these commitments pull against each other, but when I try to pin down which ones, I keep talking myself out of it. Could you work through the list and identify which pairs genuinely conflict and which just look like they do? For anything real, give me a sense of how bad the clash is, what I'd have to change to fix it and roughly what that change would cost me, and then tell me, if I could only change one belief, which change would make the biggest difference to the overall consistency.
