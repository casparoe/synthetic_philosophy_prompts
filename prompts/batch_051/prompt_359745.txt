For a philosophy-of-AI grad seminar I teach, I want a one-page decision tree that sorts the serious positions on this question: can "avoid side effects" be given a specification that doesn't already lean on a full account of what matters? The tree should make the first split whether "side effect" is an evaluative category (you can only pick one out against a background view of what's valuable) or a structural one (you can pick it out with counterfactuals, reachability, power, or the like). Below that, I want it to branch on at least these: does impact consist in divergence from how the world would have gone without the agent, or in a change to what the agent can still do; in the divergence branch, is the do-nothing baseline canonical or undertermined, so that the view needs patching with protected variables or per-task stipulations; and in the can-still-do branch, is the relevant thing the agent's ability to reach its actual goal or its ability to reach many possible goals. Make sure the leaves include at least: the hard evaluative line (no value-free definition is possible; the measures are placeholders), raw baseline divergence, the patched divergence view, reachability-based impact, attainable-utility/conservative agency, and the deflationary line that side effect is a context-sensitive natural-language concept and no single formalization is right, though per-task elicitation can stand in for one. Use a running example—say a home assistant told to "tidy the living room"—and phrase every internal node as a concrete question a student could answer without knowing which leaf they're heading for. Flag any pairs the tree can't cleanly separate, and say which branch-combinations are empty because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it to six to eight leaves, and tell me how confident you are in the result and which part you're least sure of.
