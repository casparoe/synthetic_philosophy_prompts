Over the course of a semester, I've been recording the rulings of a regular in my philosophy of biology reading group. We give her stripped-down, stipulated cases and ask whether a trait has a function and, if so, what it is. Her rulings so far:

- A chambered organ in a salamander lineage, selected over many generations for moving blood through the body and still doing so: its function is circulating blood.
- A display behavior in a bird lineage, selected because it attracted mates and still attracting them: its function is mate attraction.
- A pigment produced as a side product of a metabolic pathway, which binds to nothing and alters nothing further: it has no function.
- A thorn-like outgrowth in a plant lineage, selected to deter herbivores and still deterring them: its function is herbivore defense.
- An enzyme variant that spread by genetic drift and catalyzes nothing: it has no function.

Next week's case will be this. In an isolated lizard population, a color variant arose by mutation and spread to fixation by genetic drift in a very small population. In the current habitat, the variant happens to make its bearers much harder for a local predator to detect, improving their survival, but we are stipulating that this camouflage effect has not contributed to the variant's spread. She will be asked whether the color variant has the function of avoiding predators.

Make a prediction about her verdict. Lead with the prediction in one sentence, then lay out the rule or rules that fit all five of her earlier rulings, what each candidate rule commits her to in the lizard case, which rule you would bet she is actually running and why, and one further case that would settle the matter if the lizard case doesn't. Keep the entire answer to at most 600 words.
