Here is an argument I drafted for a paper, and I have begun to suspect it proves too much, though I cannot locate the false step.

Risk-weighted expected utility theory (REU) allows a rational agent to replace the probability weight p with a decision weight r(p), for some risk function r. For a two-outcome gamble with a better outcome x, a worse outcome y, and probability p assigned to x, REU assigns the gamble the value r(p)u(x) + (1 − r(p))u(y). This is arithmetically identical to an expected utility (EU) valuation in which the agent's subjective probability for x is r(p).

Now consider the following reasoning.

(1) Because subjective probabilities are inferred from choices rather than observed, all that matters behaviourally is the weighting of outcomes that the agent's choices reveal.
(2) In two-outcome gambles, a REU maximiser reveals exactly the weighting that an EU maximiser would reveal if their subjective probability for the better outcome were r(p).
(3) The same point extends to gambles with more than two outcomes, since any REU valuation is a weighted sum of state-utilities, and those weights can be interpreted as the agent's subjective probabilities for the states. Therefore any REU maximiser is behaviourally indistinguishable from an EU maximiser whose subjective probabilities have been fitted to r.
(4) Therefore REU and EU are observationally equivalent: REU is just EU in different notation.
(5) But REU is offered precisely because it can rationalise choice patterns, such as the Allais common-consequence pattern, that violate the independence axiom and so cannot be represented by EU.
(6) If REU and EU are observationally equivalent, then the Allais pattern was compatible with EU all along, once credences are redescribed, and the supposed counterexamples to EU are not counterexamples at all.
(7) Hence the standard motivation for REU rests on forgetting that credences are not fixed by stated probabilities, and REU has no distinctive content.

I should like to keep (5), since it states the motivation for the theory, but I cannot see whether the fault lies in (2), in the re-description at (3), or elsewhere. Identify the step that fails, explain why that step looked valid, and spell out what rejecting it costs a defender of REU. Please write the answer in British English.
