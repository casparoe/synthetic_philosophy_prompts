I'm stuck on an argument a friend made, and it's one of those things where every step feels right but the conclusion is clearly to much. It goes like this.

(1) A rule written in words can only guide someone's behaviour if they interpret the words each time they meet a new case.
(2) Asimov's Three Laws are exactly that kind of rule. The robot has to judge, on the fly, whether a planned action would harm a human or amount to letting one come to harm by doing nothing, and whether some bit of self-preservation counts as permitted by the Third Law.
(3) So the Three Laws, if they guide the robot at all, guide it through interpretation.
(4) Now suppose you try to pin the interpretation down by adding more rules stating what counts as harm, or as a human being, or as inaction. Those extra rules are themselves in words, so they need interpreting too, and off we go again. Either this regress never stops, or it stops in something that is not a rule — built-in dispositions, training data, habits.
(5) If it never stops, the original laws don't actually constrain behaviour. If it stops in dispositions, then the dispositions are doing the real work and the laws are decoration.
(6) Either way, the Three Laws are not a workable alignment proposal.
(7) By exactly parallel reasoning, the same applies to any set of rules — legal codes, constitutions, safety standards, ethical principles. So no rule has ever really constrained anyone; it's dispositions all the way down, and rules are just paperwork we file afterwards.

I don't believe (7), but I can't locate the step I should reject. I'm not trying to rescue the Three Laws; I agree they'd fail in practice, and they do at least put harm-avoidance ahead of obedience. The problem is that (7) seems to follow and I don't think it's true. I know this is somewhere near familiar debates about interpretation, but I want the precise diagnosis: where does the argument go wrong, if it does, and what do I pay for rejecting that step — in particular, can I keep (6) without sliding all the way to (7)? Please answer in British English.
