I am a high-school student, and I have been asked to give a five-minute address at the opening of our school's new history archive. The audience will be mixed: several members of the history faculty know Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, while the principal, parents, and students from the younger grades do not, and they should not feel as though they have wandered into a lecture. I would like the speech organized around the three modes of history Nietzsche distinguishes there — monumental, antiquarian, and critical — but in the speech itself you should never name Nietzsche or these terms; the structure should do the work quietly, so that the history faculty catch it and everyone else simply hears a well-shaped speech. Each mode should have a clear job: a monumental movement that celebrates the archive's past greatness without flattering the present, an antiquarian movement that honors careful preservation without turning into a catalog, and a critical movement that explains why a school should be willing to sit in judgment on its own past without souring a celebratory day. Please write the speech in my voice, addressing the room directly, and make it land warmly for both halves of the audience. After the speech, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: 'section' (a short label for each major section of the speech in order), 'mode' (the Nietzschean mode governing that section: monumental, antiquarian, critical, or none), 'plain_hearing' (what a listener who knows nothing of Nietzsche would take from the section), 'expert_hearing' (what a history teacher who knows the essay would likely notice), and 'main_risk' (the main way that section could misfire with this mixed audience, and how the speech avoids it). Please write the speech and the JSON summary in British English.
