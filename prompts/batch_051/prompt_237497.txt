You are a metascientist with expertise in the philosophy of science, incentive design, and the replication crisis. Think carefully, and start by brainstorming the incentives the proposed rule creates for each group before committing to a judgment.

An empirical discipline proposes the following rule. Before collecting data, every study must be pre-registered with two items: (i) one precise, falsifiable prediction about its main result, and (ii) an epistemic bond, a fixed percentage of the grant deducted from the authors' own funds and held in escrow. Within two years of publication, a replication lab chosen by lottery runs the registered test. If the prediction replicates, the authors recover the bond plus a multiple paid from a common replication fund; if it fails to replicate, the bond is forfeited to that fund. Ambiguous outcomes are decided by an arbitration panel under a procedure fixed in advance.

Treat the proposal as a mechanism, not as a moral position. What does it incentivize for authors, for the lottery-chosen replicators, for journals, and for funders? Where does it get gamed first, and what does the gaming look like? What second-order effects follow for what gets studied, for scientific risk, for who can afford to publish, and for the difference between a result's being true and its being easy to confirm? Finally, does the rule actually tighten the feedback loop in a way that would speed up the field's progress, or does it select for something else, and can its failure modes be fixed by changing the rule without gutting the mechanism?
