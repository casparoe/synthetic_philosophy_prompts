I’ve been trying to sort out the different kinds of arguments Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz gives for women’s right to education, and I can make out four so far. First, women have the same rational soul as men and so the same capacity for understanding. Second, Scripture itself contains learned women, whose example seems to license women’s study. Third, secular learning is not a distraction from theology but a help to it, because the Bible and the Church Fathers themselves draw on history, languages, and natural knowledge. Fourth, the exclusion of women rests on custom, or on men’s concern for reputation, rather than on any divine command. I want a fifth argument from her that is not just a restatement or special case of these four—something with a different logical shape. What would that argument be, and what does it show that the other four leave out?
