There's a debate I keep running into about loss aversion and the endowment effect. One side says they're biases: a loss shouldn't be treated differently from a foregone gain of the same size, and the endowment effect is just the gap between what I'd need to be paid to give up a mug and what I'd pay to get one if I didn't own it, which should wash out when transaction costs are trivial. The other side says no, they're legitimate preferences: people really do care about changes measured from a reference point, and calling that irrational just because it offends a final-wealth view is question-begging.

I'd like you to restate that whole debate in the vocabulary I actually have. For instance: restate the endowment effect as a kink or slope discontinuity in the value function at current holdings, maybe state-dependent utility, and restate loss aversion as the value function being steeper below the reference point than above it. Tell me how "bias" shows up in that translation, if it does at all.
