Working through some stuff for a chapter on comparitive cognition, and I keep coming back to Morgan's canon. The way it usually gets stated, it tells us to prefer the lower psychological explanation of a behaviour over the higher one when both fit the data. But that instruction leans pretty heavily on a scala naturae picture: a single ladder of mental faculties with each species sitting somewhere on it. We don't really believe in the ladder anymore, partly because the same clever-looking capacities keep showing up on distant branches. A crow and an octopus both solve novel problems, and their last common ancestor was probably some simple wormy thing with barely a brain. So my question is whether the canon collapses once the scala naturae goes, or whether it can be repaired without the ladder. If it can, what is the lower explanation now meant to be lower relative to? If it can't, is there any residual caution worth keeping under a different name?
