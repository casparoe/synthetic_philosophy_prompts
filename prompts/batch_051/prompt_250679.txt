I keep going back and forth on what it is to literally see something through a picture or screen, as opposed to just looking at a depiction of it, and I want a principle that fits my gut verdicts. Looking at my reflection in a mirror: I'm seeing my face. Through a window or binoculars: seeing the tree, the moon. A live security-camera feed of the parking lot: seeing the parking lot. An old printed photo of my mum as a kid: seeing her as a kid. A digital photo someone texts me of their new flat: seeing the flat. A video of the wedding: seeing the wedding. But a hyperrealistic charcoal drawing of that same wedding, however accurate, is a depiction, not seeing the wedding; same for a painting. Yet the photo cases get shaky: a heavily Photoshopped picture (not just fixed lighting, but moved or removed things, swapped faces) starts to feel like I'm no longer seeing the actual scene, and a deepfake of a politician saying something they never said is definitely not seeing them say it. What principle, if any, captures this? Then tell me what your principle says about two I keep waffling on: a pinhole-camera projection of a solar eclipse (do I see the eclipse?), and a phone photo that's been automatically enhanced by AI—brightened, sharpened, and with a few missing pixels filled in. I'd rather you commit to one principle and defend it than give me a menu; and where your principle clashes with one of my verdicts, just say so and tell me which one to drop.
