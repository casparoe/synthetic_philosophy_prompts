Please give me your credence that assertion is governed by a single constitutive norm — a rule that determines, for any assertion, whether it is correct as an assertion — whose standard is knowledge: an assertion of p is correct if and only if the speaker knows p. I want a number, but more importantly I want the decomposition behind it. Break the question into sub-questions: (1) whether assertion has any such constitutive correctness norm at all, rather than being answerable only to a shifting mix of conversational, moral, and epistemic pressures; (2) if it has one, whether the norm concerns the speaker's epistemic position, as opposed to the truth of p or the audience's entitlement to rely on the assertion; (3) whether that epistemic standard is knowledge specifically, rather than justified belief, certainty, or having better evidence than one's audience; and (4) how much weight standard cases should carry — the felt defectiveness of asserting p while disclaiming knowledge of p, the apparent acceptability of asserting outright that a lottery ticket will lose, and blameless false assertions. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question, show how they combine into your final credence, and say which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to and what evidence would shift it most.
