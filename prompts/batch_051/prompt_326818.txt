My thesis, roughly: the Browning–Goldhagen debate keeps treating 'situation' and 'culture/character' as competing explanations, but that's the wrong cut. Situations in the police battalions weren't just given; they were built and kept in place by cultural scripts, institutional routines, and lots of small acts of looking away, so the interesting unit is what I'm calling the maintained situation, and it dissolves the person-vs-situation framing instead of splitting the difference. The room will be mostly Holocaust historians who think Browning allready settled this, a couple of social psychologists who think Goldhagen was never a serious causal explanation, and one cultural historian who thinks both sides treat ideology as a black box. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those groups would actually put them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft, plus the weakest point in my paper I should defend rather than hope they miss, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
