Suppose a colleague defends a version of the Weber thesis: she claims that the spread of a Protestant ethic of methodical work and deferred gratification was among the causes of early modern commercial expansion in Northern Europe, not merely a post hoc vocabulary for describing it. I suspect that when she says the ethic caused the behavior she is running together two separable claims: that certain values produced measurable differences in conduct, and that agents acted from those values rather than merely in ways that later observers can describe in value-laden terms. Design the strongest feasible test of the first claim, specifying the comparison cases, the likely confounders, and what result would count for or against a cultural cause. Then say what a positive result would and would not settle about the second claim, and whether the residue marks an empirical limitation or a defect in the idea that culture can be a cause.
