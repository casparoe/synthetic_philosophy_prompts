hi, my high school astronomy club is doing an "are we alone?" night and i got assigned the great-filter table. i want to make one of those sorting quizzes, like a buzzfeed quiz, except instead of "which star wars character are you" it's "which great filter do you actually believe in?" i'd like eight to ten questions, and i'd like it to be genuinely diagnostic rather than just "do you think the origin of life is hard?"

the problem is that people in the club who have read about the fermi paradox will just pick whichever term they've heard sounds deepest, and people who havent will answer based on nothing. so i want the quiz to be built from the actual ladder of stages from dead chemistry to a galaxy with visible civilizations: abiogenesis (dead chemistry into something that can evolve), simple cells into complex cells with nuclei and mitochondria (the step that seemed to take forever on earth), complex cells into intelligent tool-users, tool-users into a technological civilization, and a technological civilization into a durable, expanding, detectable one. each question should be a concrete case or bet that forces someone to choose between two stages without naming the stage. so for example, if a lab manages self-replicating protocells in plausible early-earth conditions next year, which stage does that let you off the hook, and which stage does it make look worse? or if we find independently evolved microbes under the ice on europa, does that push the filter later, and toward what? the scoring key should map answer patterns onto leaves like "the hard step is getting life started," "the hard step is complex cells," "the hard step is intelligence," "the hard step is surviving our own technology," along with "several modest filters with no single choke point" and "no real filter, we're just early or bad at looking." please don't start from the names of filters; start from the answer-patterns and then see where the positions land.

and please be honest about the limits: flag leaves the quiz can't actually seperate (i suspect intelligence and civilization are hard to split), mark combinations of answers nobody seems to hold and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or unexplored. the audience is highschoolers, some of whom know the debate and some of whom just like aliens, so the questions should define their moving parts without lecturing.
