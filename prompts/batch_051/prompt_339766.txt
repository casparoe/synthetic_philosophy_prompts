At work we've been arguing about Copilot and the copyright lawsuits, and it's got me stuck on the old Lockean argument about property. The idea is that you come to own something because you've mixed your labour into it — the apples you picked, the field you cleared, the code you wrote. I've never studied philosophy, and the argument always felt intuative to me, but as I understand it it assumes there's some unowned raw material sitting around and one identifiable person doing the mixing. Large language models seem to blow that assumption up: they're trained on millions of people's writing and code, and then the model sort of remixes all that labour into new output that nobody can trace back to a single author. Does the labour-mixing justification for intellectual property survive this? Which premise is actually under pressure, and is there a repaired version that still says something worth keeping, or is it basically dead once the mixing is done by a model over everyone's work all at once?
