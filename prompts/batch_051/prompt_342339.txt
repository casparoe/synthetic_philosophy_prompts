I help run a small sanctuary for ex-battery hens. The birds, once they have recovered, lay a steady stream of eggs. We have no use for them, and I have been composting the eggs, but our vet warns that they attract rats and points out that the protein could be fed back to the hens. Meanwhile, several neighbours have offered to buy the eggs, and the money would cover feed and veterinary bills. My co-founder argues that selling them is the only sensible policy: the eggs will exist whatever we do, the hens are indifferent to what becomes of them, and the income does more good than composting does. I am uneasy about letting the sanctuary's finances depend on the hens' output, and about turning the sanctuary into an egg business, but I cannot articulate a principled objection.

Some of our volunteers are stricter: they say any sale treats the hens as resources, while others reply that eating or selling eggs imposes no suffering at all, since the eggs are already there and would otherwise be wasted. Am I being irrational to resist selling? What should we do, and why?
