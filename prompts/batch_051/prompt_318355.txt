An adversarial collaboration in the philosophy of biology has reached the write-up stage. The two parties are a defender of the physiological-integration view of biological individuality — on which an organism is a tightly coupled functional whole with shared boundaries and homeostatic coordination, even if its parts are genetically heterogeneous and reproduce separately — and a defender of the evolutionary-individuality view — on which an organism is a unit of selection whose parts have aligned fitness interests and reproduce together through a reproductive bottleneck, with germline–soma separation where relevant. Draft the joint statement they could both sign, written as the document itself rather than as an outside commentary. Include: (a) a numbered list of substantive claims both accept about holobionts (e.g., aphids and their obligate bacterial endosymbionts, corals and their algal endosymbionts), eusocial insect colonies (the superorganism cases), and boundary cases such as lichens or the Portuguese man o' war; (b) a disagreements table stating, in wording each side would accept for its own position, exactly where the two criteria diverge on those cases; (c) the cruxes — the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, at least one case or kind of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The agreed claims should be substantive, not merely verbal, and the table should separate residual disagreements from disagreements that were only about words.
