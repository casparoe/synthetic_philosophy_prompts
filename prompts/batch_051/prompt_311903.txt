Suppose that Jean-Jacques Rousseau could observe a present-day populist movement that claims to give effect to the will of the people through online plebiscites and through the personal authority of a leader who bypasses ordinary legislative procedures. By his lights, would these mechanisms be legitimate ways of ascertaining the general will, or would he classify them among the partial wills, factions, and usurpations of sovereignty that his political theory warns against? In your answer, work through his distinction between the general will and the will of all, and consider what conditions he imposes on votes and assemblies if their outcomes are to be genuinely general rather than a mere aggregation of private interests.
