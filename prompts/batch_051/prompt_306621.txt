Descartes's cogito is usually taken to move from the activity of thinking to the existence of a subject who thinks: even if an omnipotent deceiver is controlling the contents of my mental life, there must still be an I that performs or undergoes the thinking, and so the sceptic cannot coherently doubt that I exist. A later clinical and philosophical literature describes thought insertion, in which subjects with schizophrenia report that a thought has been placed into their minds by an external agent and is not their own; some philosophers of psychiatry argue that these reports should be taken literally, as evidence that a thought can occur within a person's mental life without being that person's thought. Suppose we accept that reading. Does the cogito still prove the existence of a subject, or does it now show only that thinking is occurring? Identify precisely which premise of the Cartesian argument thought insertion puts under pressure, and say whether a weakened Cartesian conclusion survives the case, or whether the argument should be abandoned.
