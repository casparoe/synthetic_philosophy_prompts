For my school's end-of-year philosophy magazine, I would like a villanelle about what makes every lower-case 'a' the same letter. The two refrains should pull in opposite directions: one voice insists that an 'a' is a single fixed form which every printed or handwritten instance merely approximates; the other insists that a scrawled 'a', a schoolbook 'a' and a serif 'a' share no common feature, only overlapping likenesses. The repeating refrains should themselves do the philosophical work: each time the same line returns, it should sit differently in its new context, so that the form enacts the puzzle of sameness and variation the poem is about. Bring the two refrains together in the closing lines without pretending the puzzle dissolves. Commit the poem to whichever side you think is the stronger account, and let the other refrain keep resisting rather than offering a balanced summary. Afterwards, add a short note saying where the poem takes a definite side or leans on a contested reading of what makes a category one category even as it is stretched across styles.
