I am a retired geochemist who spent most of my career working on Quaternary paleoclimate records, and I have recently begun reading philosophy of science and historical theory. I co-run a small reading group for advanced undergraduates and graduate students, and our next meeting is on the Anthropocene. The group keeps getting stuck on two questions that turn out to be inseparable: when did the Anthropocene begin, and who has the standing to decide that?

I would like you to produce a decision tree for the session that sorts a participant into a view about the Anthropocene's start without beginning from a date or a label. Treat the "when did it start" question as downstream of a prior question about what kind of thing the Anthropocene is: a formal chronostratigraphic unit in the geological time scale (one requiring a global boundary stratotype section and point, a "golden spike"), an informal but real episode in Earth-system behavior, a historical period in the ordinary human sense, or a political and normative designation. Subsequent branch nodes should separate positions by what they regard as decisive: whether a single globally synchronous marker is required or a diachronous onset is acceptable; whether the relevant signal is a detectable early-agricultural disturbance several thousand years ago, a sharp CO2 minimum around 1610 associated with depopulation of the Americas and forest regrowth (the "Orbis spike" proposal), the fossil-fuel signal of the Industrial Revolution around 1800, or the mid-twentieth-century "Great Acceleration" with its radionuclides, plastics, and technofossils; and whether the authority to fix the start belongs to stratigraphers, to the broader Earth-system science community, to historians, or to the communities whose histories are being periodized. The leaves should include at least the following named or unnamed positions: the proposal to formalize the Anthropocene at the mid-twentieth century, the 1610 Orbis-spike proposal, an Industrial Revolution boundary, the early-anthropogenic hypothesis, an informal Great Acceleration Anthropocene, a capitalism- or colonialism-centered view (often called the Capitalocene or Plantationocene), a "reflexive" Anthropocene that begins only once humanity can recognize itself as a planetary agent, the position that there is no Anthropocene and the Holocene should simply continue, and a pluralist "Anthropocenes" view. Write each branch node as a question someone can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to, and do not simply ask which date they prefer. Flag any pairs of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate — I suspect the mid-twentieth-century and 1610 candidates come apart on whether onset must be globally synchronous and on what a boundary marker must be, but check whether a simple quiz can actually separate them. Also mark unoccupied combinations of answers and briefly say whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Before building the tree, please take the role of a philosopher of science who also knows the stratigraphic and historical literature on the Anthropocene. Start by brainstorming the candidate cutting questions in one pass, then construct the tree from the resulting logical space rather than from a list of famous positions, then criticize your own tree and revise it. Please aim for roughly eight to twelve leaves and no more than ten branch questions, written for people who are new to this debate but willing to do careful reading.
