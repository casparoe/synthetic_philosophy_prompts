I've been going back and forth with my cousin about this thing I saw online: that the more egalitarian a country is, the more men and women pick different kinds of jobs. Like in the Nordic countries you supposedly get more women becoming nurses and more men becoming engineers than in places with stronger traditional gender roles, which feels completely backwards. So I made up a thought experiment to keep the argument honest. Here's the setup. Pick a rich country, say the Netherlands. Starting tomorrow, every social driver of gender difference we can name gets set to zero and stays there for thirty years: no hiring or pay discrimination, parents and schools treat boys and girls exactly the same, housework and childcare get split 50/50, media and advertising stop showing men and women in different roles, hiring committees are blind to gender, and the law treats everybody identically. Nothing about biology changes, and the economy just keeps doing what a rich economy does. The only thing that changes overnight is the social environment.

Now I want you to predict what the job market looks like in thirty years. Does the share of women in engineering climb to exactly 50% and the share of men in nursing climb to exactly 50%, or does it settle somewhere else, and why? I care less about the exact number than about the chain of reasoning. Walk me through which parts of your prediction your confident in, and which parts rest on your assumptions about how much of the gap is biology versus culture, and about whether equalizing how we treat people would also equalize what they actually want. Then the part I really care about: if the gap mostly closes, does that show the current pattern was unfair; if it barely moves, does that show it's just natural and fine; and are both of those conclusions to quick? Also, my cousin says thirty years isn't long enough because parents are already set in their ways, so tell me whether your answer changes if the change lasted two hundred years instead.
