I'm running a small aesthetics podcast, and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends the strong transparency view about photography—the kind of position Kendall Walton lays out in "Transparent Pictures: On the Nature of Photographic Realism," where photographs aren't just pictures we imagine into, but are literally aids to seeing the way mirrors, telescopes, and closed-circuit television setups are. I want the interview to get past the slogan. Near the end of that essay Walton discusses seeing an object through a prosthetic optical system and asks whether we see the object itself or merely an image of it, and he wants to say we genuinely see the object through the system. I need eight to ten questions built around that thought. At least one question should force her to say what happens when the photograph outlives the object—do we still see the object, or has the transparency relation broken? Another should press on whether heavy editing or generative fill destroys the relation and where exactly she draws the line. And another should ask whether the causal chain has to be purely optical and chemical, or whether a digital sensor and an algorithm still count as transparent. For each question, add the follow-up you'd expect if she retreats to saying photographs are merely counterfactually dependent on the scene rather than genuinely seen through.
