I'm a novelist and this has been nagging me, so I want someone to read The Prestige through the identity of indiscernibles—specifically Black's two-sphere thought experiment. In the film, every performance with Tesla's machine creates a second Angier, identical in body and memory at the instant of duplication, except one ends up in the water tank and the other takes the bow. Leibniz says two things sharing all their properties can't really be two, but Black's pair of exactly similar iron spheres in an otherwise empty universe is meant to show they can be. Where does the film actually land? A Leibnizian might say the tank and the balcony give the two Angiers different relational properties, so they aren't indiscernibles after all; but Black's point is that location only does that work if there's already something else to anchor it. And the film's real horror—that Angier can't know which one he'll be—is stranger than Black's case, because he's one of them from the inside. I'm writing a craft essay and I'd like a reading that takes both the metaphysics and the movie seriously, and says where the doubling in The Prestige complicates Black's thought experiment rather than just illustrating it.
