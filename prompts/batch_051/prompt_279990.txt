The historians split hard. Some think that contrastive question is the central one, and others think its a trap that reads history backwards and treats Britain as the outcome that had to happen. I kept trying to translate the argument into my own vocabulary during the talk — units, outcomes, identifying variation, multiple equilibria, threshold effects, path dependence — and I could get maybe a third of the way.

Could you do it properly? Take the debate over whether the why-Britain-and-not-China question is well posed, and restate it in the vocabulary of growth economics and applied econometrics as faithfully as you can: what are the units, what exactly is being explained, what would count as identification, which positions translate into which kind of model, etc. Then give me the residue report. Which claims from the historians translate cleanly, which only fit if you distort them, and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary?
