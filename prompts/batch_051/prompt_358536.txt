An influential argument for the singleton hypothesis runs as follows: once one agent or coalition acquires a decisive strategic advantage—plausibly by first developing machine superintelligence—it will be able to anticipate and suppress rival projects before they mature, so a world with many independent centers of power is not a stable equilibrium. Now suppose the relevant capabilities arrive through open-weight models: frontier-scale systems are published, copied, and locally adapted by many actors at roughly the same time, before anyone holds a decisive lead. Does the singleton argument survive this development? In answering, identify which premise of the argument is under pressure, say whether the open-weight scenario merely weakens it or removes it altogether, and state the strongest repaired version of the singleton claim that remains defensible.
