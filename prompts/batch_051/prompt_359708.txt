I'm a reporter working on a long magazine piece about whether a finite mind—human or machine—could ever reach a point where it has no genuinely new experiences left. Not the jadedness of having seen too much, but a structural sense in which that mind's space of possible experience is exhaustible. I keep running into the same snag: people slide between 'new to me' in a biographical sense, 'new in content' in a combinatorial sense, and 'new in what it feels like' in a phenominal sense, as if these were one question.

So here's what I need: the desiderata that any adequate theory of the exhaustibility of experience has to meet before I'd trust it in the piece. Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Items one through four should each be a desideratum—for each, give the case or consideration that motivates it and say whether it's non-negotiable or just one camp's preference. The fifth item should say whether any subset of the first four is jointly unsatisfiable, and if so which one and why; that's the part I most want to see. Skip scoring existing theories, I just want the standard itself. Star the desideratum that does the most work excluding candidate views.
