Ok so in my current events class we were talking about blackmail and I can't get past this. Apparently philosophers even have a name for it, the paradox of blackmail, which makes me feel less crazy. Think about a kid at my school who has a video of a classmate having a full meltdown in the parking lot. Posting the video is embarassing but, as far as I can tell, legal — and asking someone for money is obviously legal too. But the second you combine them — "give me fifty bucks or I post the video" — it's blackmail, an actual crime. Both ingrediants are legal on their own, but the sandwich is illegal. That seems like a weird asymmetry.

And it gets weirder if you swap the order. If the kid just asks for the fifty bucks first, gets told no, and then posts the video, most people would say he's a jerk but not a criminal — even though it's the exact same two actions, just done one after another instead of bundled as an if-then. So is there a principle that makes the threat version uniquely wrong, or are we just punishing him for how he worded it?

I'm supposed to write an essay on this, so: write an essay on whether anything actually justifies the blackmail asymmetry. Find the strongest justification you can, test it against the order-swap case and any other cases you think put pressure on it, and end with a real verdict — is the law tracking something important here, or is it just inconsistent?
