I am not an academic, just someone who has been reading John Stuart Mill’s late writings on socialism on my own and thinking about them against actual historical cases. In his late, unfinished essay usually called Chapters on Socialism, Mill has a passage I keep returning to. As I understand him, he says that the contest between socialism and private property cannot be settled by comparing socialism’s actual failures with private property as it might be in principle; the only fair test is to compare the best attainable form of socialism with the best attainable form of private property.

I want to apply that passage to the collapse of Robert Owen’s community at New Harmony. The facts, as I know them, are these. Owen, a wealthy cotton-mill owner, bought the town of New Harmony in Indiana in 1825 and tried to turn it into a community in which property was owned in common and work, schooling, and domestic life were organized cooperatively. Within about two years the community had broken apart, with disputes over unequal contributions of labor, unclear authority, a shortage of skilled people, and disagreements about how much of life should be held in common.

Analyze New Harmony as a test case for Mill’s late socialism and for that passage. What exactly does Mill’s framework say the community was testing: socialism, Owen’s particular design, or the way the experiment selected its members? What would Mill’s framework have predicted about the pressures the participants faced, and what would he have counted as success or failure? What features of the New Harmony collapse does Mill’s framework explain well, and what does it miss or misdescribe? Then run the analysis in the other direction: does the collapse of New Harmony count against the principle in the passage I paraphrased, or does it fall under the very problem Mill warns against, namely testing a defective version of socialism against an idealized private property? Please also tell me what would change your mind: which specific fact about New Harmony, or which consideration about Mill’s framework, would most plausibly lead you to the opposite verdict.
