I sit on my company's AI ethics and safety committee, and I need to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting. We are reviewing an AI customer-service assistant that, when it cannot retrieve a confident answer, sometimes produces a fluent, specific-sounding reply anyway; in one recorded exchange it told a customer that a delayed order would arrive on a particular date even though its internal records showed no reliable estimate. Our company policy states that deployed systems must not deceive users, and the engineering team argues that this clause cannot apply here: the system has no beliefs, no representational states, and no intention to mislead, so it can no more be dishonest than a malfunctioning spreadsheet. I spent most of last evening arguing about this with a friend, who maintained that honesty and deception are simply the wrong categories to apply to software, while I suspect that what should matter is whether the system's outputs predictably mislead an ordinary user, whatever is going on inside the model. Draft the memo I will circulate. It should recommend a concrete course of action, including whether this behavior falls under our anti-deception policy and whether the system should be exempted, relabeled, or taken down, and it should propose a working definition of honesty and deception for AI systems that the committee can use going forward. Engage seriously with the strongest version of the objection rather than brushing it aside.
