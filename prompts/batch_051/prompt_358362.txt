In my philosophy class we're doing Merleau-Ponty and I got realy into his idea that the body is the subject of perception, not just a thing the mind drives around. The part that stuck with me is the blind person's cane: after enough time the cane stops being an object the person feels and becomes something they perceive through, like it gets pulled into their body schema. So I started thinking about something he never got to see, which is virtual reality. In a VR game I'm physically standing in my living room, but my avatar can do stuff my actual body can't, like fly or have an extra arm, and I can swap avatars completely. Still, people who play alot say the headset and controllers eventually stop feeling like equipment and you just feel present in the world of the game. Would Merleau-Ponty say the controllers get absorbed into the lived body the way the cane does? Or would he think the avatar is a totally different kind of thing, like a representation I control from outside instead of a body I actually inhabit? And if my actual body and my avatar body come apart, what happens to motor intentionality? I want someone to actually work through what he would probably say and why, not just compare him to other philosophers.
