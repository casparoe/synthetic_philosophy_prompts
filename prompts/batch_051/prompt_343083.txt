A friend walked me into this at a local EA meetup and I honestly can't find the exit. He claims it's a reductio against strong longtermism, and the conclusion feels way too strong, but each step looked fine as he said it.

(1) If there is any nontrivial chance humanity has a long future — say even 1% of surviving for millions of years — then the number of possible future lives dwarfs the number of lives any near-term program can save now.
(2) So in expected-value terms, a small intervention that nudges the probability of an existential catastrophe down by even 0.0001% beats bednet distributions; it could be worth millions of current lives.
(3) By the same logic, any plan with any nonzero chance of touching the far future can be made to look astronomically valuable, as long as you are willing to assign it a very small probability of a very large payoff.
(4) So you can justify nearly anything: cryonics, a one-person AI-safety startup, writing letters to future generations, a campaign to make the far future 0.1% less religious — each can be made to beat AMF in expectation, depending on the numbers you pick.
(5) But a decision procedure that lets you make anything win by choosing a small enough probability and a large enough payoff is not a procedure; it's a rationalization engine.
(6) So expected-value reasoning about large futures is either vacuous or self-defeating, and strong longtermism leans entirely on that kind of reasoning.
(7) Therefore strong longtermism, as a practical guide to action, collapses.

I'd rather keep expected-value reasoning, since its the only game in town for comparing interventions, but I don't see how to keep it without also licensing the made-up-numbers version. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If a specific step is the culprit, say which one and why it looked legitimate; if the argument is valid, tell me which premise you would deny and what you give up. Is the trouble in the arithmetic, in how probabilities get assigned, or in the slide from extinction risk to far-future influence generally?
