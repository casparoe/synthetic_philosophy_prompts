For most of my career I worked as a plant physiologist, and since retiring I have been reading in the philosophy of biology. I have become absorbed by the question of what is being claimed when one says a biological trait has a function, and I would value your help comparing the main families of answer. As I understand them: an etiological (selected-effects) account holds that a trait's function is what it was selected for in the past; a causal-role account holds that a trait's function is the contribution it now makes to the workings of some larger system, irrespective of its history; a hybrid account requires both a selection history and a present contribution; and a forward-looking (propensity) account identifies a trait's function with what it does that tends to promote future survival or reproduction. I am not asking who defended which view; please treat these families simply as I have characterized them.

I would like you to sort these four families into tiers according to how well they handle the following five cases, and to justify the placement of each family in a sentence or two. The cases are: (a) a newly arisen viral mutation that has not yet been through a relevant selective episode, but biochemically increases the rate at which infected cells shed new virions; (b) a mole's vestigial, skin-covered eye, which now makes no measurable contribution to the animal's capacities; (c) a crop trait that breeders selected a century ago for drought tolerance, but which under current irrigated cultivation no longer contributes to yield or survival; (d) a trait that contributes to an organism's fitness now, but arose as an unselected side effect of selection for something else and has never itself been selected; and (e) a cellular process inside a tumor that contributes strongly to the tumor's growth while harming the host organism.

Please state your tiers explicitly, identify which cases do the most work in separating the accounts, and indicate where the ranking would be fragile under a slightly different specification of one of the views.
