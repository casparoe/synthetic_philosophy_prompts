Okay here's one for you. I keep seeing historians claim that the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution were all basically invented after the fact, but they can't all be equally invented. I want you to rate each one from 0 to 1, where 0 means "purely a later construction" and 1 means "picks out a real, naturally bounded chunk of history." Then rank the three from most real to most constructed.

To keep this from being pure vibes, score each one on three sub-criteria and average them:

- self-description: did people living through it think they were in a distinct period with roughly that name, or somthing close?
- boundary sharpness: are the start and end points clearly tighter than the messy changes they're supposed to mark?
- synchrony: did the features we associate with the period (art, politics, science, religion, whatever) actually shift together across the places the label covers?

After you've scored and ranked them, take the one you ranked most real and give me the strongest revisionist argument that it should be merged into a neighboring period or dissolved entirely. Then take the one you ranked most constructed and give it one serious defense — a reason a careful historian might keep it around as more than just a teaching convenience. No need to cite anyone; I just want the reasoning.
