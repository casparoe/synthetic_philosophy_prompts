Act as a professional philosopher specialising in collective responsibility and the ethics of collective action, and think through the structure carefully before writing: first brainstorm the full logical space, then build the tree. I'm an undergraduate in a seminar on collective action preparing a revision handout on the inefficacy objection. Please write a decision tree that sorts the main positions on whether an individual is required to perform a small act—abstaining from one factory-farmed chicken, skipping one flight, casting one vote—when the act by itself makes no perceptible difference to a collectively produced harm.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from labels. Branch nodes should ask, in whatever order is most revealing (add at most two if needed): whether the act makes an actual causal difference in this particular pathway, separating threshold/triggering cases from merely aggregative ones; whether evaluation should track expected rather than actual difference; whether complicity in a harmful collective practice can be wrong without marginal efficacy; whether a duty attaches first to a group and is then distributed to members; and whether the act matters through integrity, hypocrisy, or expression. Use at least these diagnostic cases, phrased so a student can answer without already knowing the positions: a single vote in a large electorate; one purchase from a supply chain in which a single purchase does not change production volume; a threshold campaign that reaches its target or fails regardless of any individual pledge; and joining a mob that will proceed regardless of any one member.

Leaves should include, descriptively named: a strict actual-consequence view that accepts the inefficacy objection, an expected-value consequentialist view, a threshold/trigger-sensitive view, a complicity/participation view, a joint-obligation view, and an integrity/expressive view. Flag any unoccupied branch combinations and say whether each is incoherent, merely unattractive, or under-explored. Flag any pairs of leaves the tree cannot separate on the listed cases, and specify one further case that would separate them.
