"Any problem in geometry can easily be reduced to such terms that a knowledge of the lengths of certain straight lines is sufficient for its construction."

I made up a word for what I think he's doing there. I call it "straight-edge legitimacy." The idea is: a mathematical object is legit only if there's a finite recipe for it that bottoms out in the simple strokes—straight lines and circles—and anything that can't be reduced that way is what I've been calling a "shadow object." We talk about it, we manipulate it, but we don't actually have it. Under this, compass-and-straightedge constructions are legit, but transcendental curves like the sine curve or the catenary are shadows. Which would make a lot of modern math shadows, so I know something is off, but I can't tell where.
