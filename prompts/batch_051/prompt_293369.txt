I have settled into a habit in disputes about new technologies, and I want to know whether it is a defensible epistemic policy or a bias wearing the costume of caution. My habit is this: when a proposed intervention is described as having a plausible failure mode that would be catastrophic and effectively irreversible, I treat that possibility as decisive from the moment it is credibly described. I do not ask how likely the failure mode is; I ask only whether the mechanism is coherent and whether anyone serious has proposed it. If the answer to both is yes, I conclude that the intervention should be rejected, and I hold that conclusion until the risk has been ruled out to my satisfaction, which is a high bar. So, for example, if someone proposes altering rainfall patterns and one physical mechanism by which the intervention could disrupt a monsoon is put forward, I move directly from the existence of that mechanism to opposition.

I have noticed two features of my own rule that trouble me. The first is that I apply it only to risks produced by human action, and not to the risks of doing nothing, even when inaction has its own plausible catastrophic failure modes. The second is that I have never specified a probability below which a grave risk may be set aside; in practice, no nonzero probability has ever been low enough. Worse, I can see in myself a tendency to search for such a mechanism until I find one and then to stop looking for base rates. My question is whether this rule can be stated in a principled form, or whether it is just caution systematized beyond the point of usefulness. Is the action/inaction asymmetry defensible, or is it a status-quo bias in epistemic clothing? Does the precautionary principle have a coherent reading with an explicit threshold, or does it only appear to be a principle because the threshold is left vague? And from the inside, how could I tell whether my habit is tracking something real, such as the special burden of imposing irreversible harms on others, rather than simply equating plausibility with menace?
