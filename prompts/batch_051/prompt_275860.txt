Back when Kant, and later Hare and Singer, were making versions of the what-if-everyone-did-that argument, you basically had to reason about the rule, because nobody could credibly calculate the marginal effect of one extra person's contribution to a collective harm. Now we're drowning in randomized trials, per-flight emissions calculators, and voter-power models that estimate the marginal effect of a single token act—and it often comes out tiny or effectively zero. Does the universalizability test survive this shift from type-level to marginal reasoning? If it does, say exactly which premise is doing the work now and what the repaired statement should be; if it doesn't, say what should replace it, because the obvious fallback—"what if everyone reasoned like me about their marginal contribution?"—seems circular rather then decisive.
