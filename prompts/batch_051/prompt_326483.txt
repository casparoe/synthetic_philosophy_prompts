I spent my career as a research physicist, and in retirement I have been reading the philosophical literature on whether physics could have a final theory and whether we would be in a position to recognize one if we had it. I find myself automatically restating the arguments in the vocabulary I actually worked in: effective field theories, renormalization-group flow, the fact that every theory I ever used came with a domain of validity, and the practice of treating a theory as a fixed mathematical structure plus parameters that are fit to data and run with scale. I am fluent in that vocabulary; it is the philosophical one I am learning.

Please restate the main positions in the end-of-physics debate in my native vocabulary, and then tell me what does not survive the translation. The positions I have in mind are, roughly: (1) the realist view that a final theory is a coherent aim and that the historical arc of physics is convergence toward it; (2) the instrumentalist or empiricist view that a theory is always a theory at a scale, so that the demand for a final theory is something physics has never actually needed; and (3) the structural-realist view that what may be final is a mathematical or structural framework while its particular physical instantiations remain approximate. Restate each as faithfully as you can in terms of effective field theories, cutoffs, renormalization-group fixed points, the underdetermination of ultraviolet completions by low-energy data, and the distinction between being approximate at a scale and being the whole truth. Do not simplify by deciding in advance that my vocabulary already contains everything worth saying; I want the translation kept honest, with strain marked where the original claim is genuinely foreign.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only under distortion, and which have no expression at all in the vocabulary of effective field theory? In particular: does the realist's claim that there is a fact of the matter about the final theory have a genuine counterpart — for example, a unique ultraviolet fixed point or parent theory whose renormalization-group flow lands on every effective theory we observe — or is that a distortion? Does the question of how we would know we had found the final theory have a natural counterpart in model selection or parameter inference, or does it get mangled because effective-theory reasoning only licenses confidence within a regime? And when something fails to translate, say where the failure lies: is my vocabulary missing a distinction the philosophical claim needs, or is the philosophical claim less substantial than it looks once it is forced into terms that respect how physics is actually done?
