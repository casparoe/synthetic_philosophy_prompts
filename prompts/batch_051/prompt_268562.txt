just got back from a long argument with my friend about frege. her line was that he didn't refute syllogistic so much as bulldoze it, and that the subject-predicate sentence was a perfectly good tool for its little corner. mine was that he showed the old logic couldn't even say "brutus killed caesar" without pretending a killing-of-caesar is a property brutus has. i want a poem about it. a sonnet, italian-ish: the octave should sit inside the old logic, where every sentence looks like a subject wearing a predicate and the barbara syllogism feels like the whole art. the volta should be the sentence that won't fit that shape — a relation or nested quantifier like "everyone loves someone" — landing as the thing that breaks the toy. the sestet should do frege's rebuild: predicates as unsaturated functions with argument-places, quantifiers binding variables, the noun dethroned from the center of the sentence. and close the last two lines on what actually got lost in that trade, because i half-believe her that something did — not a shrug about progress. after the poem, a short note: which step or image is the most historically simplified or contested, and what consideration would make you restructure the volta or the trade-off.
