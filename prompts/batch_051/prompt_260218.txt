I spent my career in condensed matter physics, and in retirement I have been reading philosophy of science. For a discussion group of working physicists and philosophers of physics, please write a decision tree that sorts respondents into positions on what the success of effective field theories shows about the autonomy of scales. Begin the tree from a commitment every member of the group accepts: effective field theories are predictively successful because irrelevant operators are suppressed by powers of the ratio of the low-energy scale to the cutoff, so low-energy predictions can be computed without specifying an ultraviolet completion. From there, the branch nodes should be yes/no questions answerable by a physicist with no philosophical training, and the leaves should be named positions with one sentence each on the commitment that gives that position its shape.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. Use two screening questions, asked through the cases rather than in philosophical jargon: first, whether the decoupling of scales is a fact about our knowledge and computational limitations or a fact about how the world is organized; second, whether any level of description is metaphysically privileged. At minimum, the leaves must include: a view that treats the decoupling as merely pragmatic and takes only the most fundamental theory to be complete; a view that grants the low-energy theory epistemic autonomy while remaining agnostic about higher-level entities; a view that treats stable large-scale patterns as real and explanatory in their own right; and a view that denies any privileged fundamental level and treats physics as a tower of effective descriptions with no bottom or top. Keep the finished tree to between six and eight leaves. If the cross of the two screening questions leaves empty combinations, mark each empty cell and say in one sentence whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely underexplored.

At least four of the following must be used as branch questions or worked examples: phonons in a crystal; the universality of critical exponents across liquid-gas and magnetic systems; two distinct ultraviolet completions that give identical low-energy physics; the description of superconductivity in terms of Cooper pairs as against a derivation from quantum electrodynamics; and the computation of a transport coefficient such as the viscosity of water directly from the Standard Model. Phrase each so that it tests a specific philosophical fault line, for example whether a derivation from the fundamental theory would explain the lower-level phenomenon rather than merely retrodict it. If two leaves cannot be separated by any question you can fairly put to a non-philosopher, flag them explicitly and say what additional evidence would separate them.

Do not include citations or reading lists; the instrument must stand on the cases and questions alone. Begin with the decision tree itself, with no preface, and include no commentary after it.
