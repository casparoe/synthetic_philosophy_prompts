I'm writing a novel about a volcanologist who, after badly botching an eruption forecast, falls into a rabbit hole where he convinces himself every prediction from past experience is as good as any other. The argument he writes on his whiteboard has to be one a smart non-philosopher could genuinely talk himself into, and I want to understand where it comes apart before I let another character try to talk him down.

Here's the argument.

(1) Every emerald anybody has ever examined has been green.
(2) Every emerald anybody has ever examined has also been grue, where "grue" means: examined before the year 2100 and green, or not examined before 2100 and blue. Every emerald examined so far was examined before 2100 and was green, so it fits the first half.
(3) Induction says: expect the unexamined to resemble the examined. So from (1), we should predict that emeralds first examined after 2100 will be green.
(4) By the exact same rule, from (2), we should predict that emeralds first examined after 2100 will be grue. And since an emerald first examined after 2100 is not examined before 2100, for it to be grue it has to be blue. So induction also predicts that emeralds first examined after 2100 will be blue.
(5) So induction predicts, about the very same emeralds, both that they will be green and that they will be blue.
(6) A rule that predicts flat-out contradictions about the same event cant be a trustworthy guide to how things are.
(7) Therefore induction is worthless. And so are volcano forecasting, climate modeling, and every other science that extrapolates from past regularities.

I know (7) is absurd, and my protagonist knows it too, but he can't find the step that's doing the damage. I'd personally rather give up (4) than (3): I refuse to believe "green" and "grue" are on a par. But I can't say why the second predicate is rigged without just insisting that it is, and "insisting" isnt a satisfying chapter. So: where does the argument go wrong, and what exactly do I have to buy if I reject (4)? Or is the real weak point somewhere upstream?
