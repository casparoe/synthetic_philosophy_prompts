I keep coming back to this bit from Samuel Butler's 1863 letter Darwin among the Machines: "we are ourselves creating our own successors; we are daily adding to the beauty and delicacy of their physical organisation; we are daily giving them greater power and supplying by all sorts of ingenious contrivances that self-regulating, self-acting power which will be to them what intellect has been to the human race. In the course of ages we shall find ourselves the inferior race." I'm tryign to figure out whether there's a serious research agenda hidden in this old teleological way of talking, and which parts are basically Victorian rhetorical dressing. If you had one year to work on "humans as a stepping stone to AI"-type claims, which specific questions or implications would you put at the top? Rank maybe three or four, and defend the ranking. I want the criteria used—tractability, whether it would actually change what a person or lab does, how many other questions depend on it—and what would bump something up or down. I'm especially interested in whether the really useful content isn't about AI literally replacing us, but about selection pressures shaping what systems get built, or about whether values survive when the thing doing the improving isn't trying to preserve them. Don't just survey the topic; I want an agenda with a defended order.
