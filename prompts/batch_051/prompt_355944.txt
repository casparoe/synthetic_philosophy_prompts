An acquaintance in movement psychology argues that the Daoist idea of wu-wei is not merely a culturally local ideal but a real and unified psychological state. On her account, it is skilled performance that proceeds without conscious striving or self-monitoring; it is distinct from ordinary automaticity, such as driving a familiar route, and from flow, which she says one can deliberately set up; and it can be reached only when the agent stops trying to reach it. I suspect wu-wei is being used as a name for a family of related states, and that the paradox of trying not to try is an artifact of lumping them together. How could one test her claim that effortless action is a single distinct state with that specific self-undermining profile? Propose the strongest experimental or observational design you can, say which pattern of results would count for each side, and be explicit about what the test could never settle, including whether what gets measured deserves the name wu-wei or is only an operationalized stand-in for it.
