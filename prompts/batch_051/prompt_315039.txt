I've been trying to sort out what I actually think about putting prices on things, going off my gut on specific cases rather than whatever theory I half-remember from a college ethics class. I dont need a reading list, I need the principle, if there is one.

Here's where I've landed.

Selling a kidney to a stranger for $40,000 so you can pay off debts: this feels wrong to me and I think it should stay illegal, even though I can't point to exactly who it hurts. I know someone who did it and she says it was fine, and I don't have a good answer beyond "it just feels dirty," which embarrasses me.

Paying a woman $35,000 plus medical costs to carry a baby as a surrogate: fine. The money is for the work and the risk, not for the baby.

Selling blood plasma for $50: fine, I did it in college.

A store charging $50 for a case of water that's normally $8 the day after a hurricane: not fine, they should be prosecuted. But a guy who buys up generators before the storm and drives them in afterwards to sell at triple the price: also not fine to me, maybe only a little better because he took a real risk and actually increased supply.

A broker reselling a concert ticket for $400 that was $90 at face value: I'm annoyed, but I don't think it should be illegal.

Buying someone's vote for $50: absolutely not, one of the only things I'm certain about. But I don't blink at a campaign paying people $20 an hour to gather signatures or knock on doors, even though that's also paying for political activity.

A city selling a $50 fast-pass that lets you skip the DMV line: not fine, it's a government service and I hate that its officially two-tier. But I pay for TSA PreCheck and Clear and skip the airport lines without a second thought.

Selling naming rights to a public park or a school gym to a corporation: not fine.

A country selling passports to investors for a million dollars: not fine.

Paying kids $10 for every A on a report card: I don't find this fine at all, though I can't say why when I'm fine paying the same kid to mow my lawn.

My working theory is something like: markets are fine when what's being sold is a normal commodity and everybody's roughly equal going in, but they stop being fine when the thing shouldn't have a price because pricing it changes its meaning, or when the buyer is exploiting someone's desperation. But that theory dies immediately on plasma versus kidney—both body parts, both sold mostly by poor people, one fine and one not—and it doesn't explain why a fast-pass is fine at the airport but not at the DMV.

So: is there an actually coherent principle, or a small set of them, that gets most or all of these verdicts? Be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, and whether the ones it saves can be kept without turning the whole thing into a patchwork. Then tell me what the principle says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a private company paying homeless people $50 each to join a low-risk medical trial for a new allergy drug.
