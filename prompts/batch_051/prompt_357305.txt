A friend of mine works in fundraising for a global health charity, and she shown me a script they use on calls and at events. The person hasn't said or done anything yet; the point is to cast them as a generous, caring person so they feel pressure to live up to the role. I later learned this move has a name, altercasting: you assign someone a role or identity so the role does the persuading for you, and saying no afterwards feels like betraying the person they've just been told they are.

Here's the proposition I want your actual credence on: that this use of altercasting in a fundraising pitch is manipulative in the objectionable sense—the sense where it wrongs the listener or undermines their autonomy—rather than just a skillful bit of legitimate persuasion.
