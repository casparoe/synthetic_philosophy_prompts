Last month I ran into my old roommate at a tiny train station in Croatia, and then on the flight home I was seated next to someone with my same fairly unusual last name. I've been telling it like it means something. My wife keeps reminding me I have a rule against exactly this. Its from Littlewood's A Mathematician's Miscellany, in the chapter Large Numbers, where he asks: "what is the most remarkable coincidence you have experienced, and is it, for the most remarkable one, remarkable?" His point being that over a lifetime you have millions of events, so one-in-a-million things happen all the time. My actual policy is: when a friend reports a coincidence and starts treating it as significance, I discount it to nearly zero, since they're selecting one weird moment from a huge stream. But I clearly didn't apply that to my own. Is my policy defensible if I only apply it to other people? Is there a better version where I apply it to myself too? And how would I know when a coincidence should actually update me rather than being dismissed as selection?
