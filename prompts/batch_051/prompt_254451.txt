I'm secretary of a small philosophy discussion group, and after we started issuing official position statements based on internal votes, I've found myself with a set of views about how group judgment should work, none of which I'd easily give up, but I suspect they can't all be true at once. I'd like an audit of the whole set.

My commitments are roughly these:

1. For a flat, non-hierarchical club like ours, majority rule is the right default way to turn individual judgments into the group's judgment.
2. The group's official position should be logically coherent. If our position commits us to P and also commits us to Q, then we shouldn't simultaneously deny P-and-Q or some other obvious consequence of the pair.
3. When a question has several connected parts (say whether a proposal is workable, whether it's fair, and whether we should adopt it overall), each part should be voted on and decided by majority on its own. Any other approach lets a bottom-line outcome steamroll what a majority of members actually think about the parts.
4. As long as every individual member's own views are internally consistent, majority voting can't manufacture a group-level inconsistency; that kind of incoherence has to come from something else.
5. We shouldn't respond to worries about coherence by handing a chairperson or outside expert the power to tidy up the final position, because that's just one person overruling the majority by stealth.

I once read something about courts where each judge's reasoning is consistent, yet the court's aggregated reasoning seems to produce a conclusion that most of the judges reject, and it's made me suspect the fault line is somewhere in my list. But I can't pin down which commitments are doing the damage, and I don't want to abandon more than I have to.

Could you go through the set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other, ordered by how severe the tension is, and which pairs only appear to conflict (and why they don't)? For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix is. Then tell me which single revision would restore consistency at the lowest overall cost.

Oh, and the group is UK-based, so please write the answer in British English.
