Two friends of mine got into it at an EA meetup the other night about whether destroying a habitat is bad or good for wild animals, and I honestly couldn't referee it. Maya said bulldozing a forest is obviously bad: it directly kills a lot of animals, and the survivors starve or get picked off. Dan said that's too focused on the dramatic part. If the forest contains large numbers of animals whose lives are net suffering, then destroying it also prevents huge numbers of future miserable births, so on total welfare the destruction could be neutral or even good. Maya replied that he's letting nonexistent future animals cancel out the actual killings of animals that actualy exist, and those have to be counted differently. Dan said every policy choice is about comparing possible futures; a death today and a birth that doesn't happen tomorrow are both just differences between futures, so there's no deep line between them. Maya came back that his logic would make us obligated to destroy every habitat as fast as possible, which is absurd, so the reasoning must be broken somewhere. Dan said she's pointing at a repugnant-conclusion-style result but hasn't actually shown the result is wrong here, since maybe wild animal welfare really is that bad. They asked me what I thought, and I froze. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about which specific point, and what both of them are missing?
