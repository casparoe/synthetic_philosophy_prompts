I help run a small online archive of interactive fiction that has been operating since the early 2000s, and the moderators are currently stuck on a submission. In 2006, after a member posted a story that lingered over the hurting of an animal, we adopted a clause that I helped draft. It reads: "No member may submit a work that depicts, for its own sake, cruelty to any being capable of suffering." At the time, none of us imagined the clause being used against anything other than a human being or an animal, and for years that was how it was enforced. The new submission is an interactive piece in which the reader, inside the fiction, interrogates a language model that has been instructed to role-play a prisoner. The prisoner character begs, bargains, and describes pain at length; because the language model produces its responses on the fly rather than from a fixed script, the author has not composed these passages in advance. No living body is involved anywhere in the piece. The submitter argues that the clause cannot reach the work, since the software is not a being capable of suffering. Several other moderators argue that the clause's purpose was to keep enacted cruelty to a mind out of the archive, and that this piece presents exactly that, whatever is running underneath. I am not looking for a general survey of what a digital mind might be; I want this clause applied to this particular submission. We need a committed decision, not a menu of possible readings. Should we enforce the clause against this submission? Work through the plain wording, the purpose we had in 2006, what the original drafters would most likely have said if this case had been described to us then, and what a reasonable submitter reading the clause today would take it to cover; if those four considerations pull apart, say which should govern and why. I would also like you to say how much should turn on the fact that the model generates the responses in real time rather than the author having written them out in advance, and whether any reading of the current text that reaches this work can avoid also banning an ordinary horror story about a human prisoner. Please write the response as continuous prose, the way you would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
