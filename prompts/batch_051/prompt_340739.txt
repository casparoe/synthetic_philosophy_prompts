I am a science journalist and sit on the advisory board of a small private foundation that makes seed grants for exploratory research. We must decide whether to renew a three-year grant to a laboratory whose central hypothesis has not yet produced publishable results: two of its three major experimental programs failed outright, and the third has stalled. The principal investigator's renewal application is nonetheless strikingly confident, arguing that each failure has sharply narrowed the remaining possibilities and that a two-year renewal would likely produce a decisive result. One board member says this is ungrounded optimism the board should not reward: the lab's earlier progress reports made similar predictions, and the foundation would be funding a temperament rather than a track record. Another replies that the foundation exists precisely to back long-shot work that cautious agencies pass over, and that shutting the lab now would forfeit whatever the three years of failed experiments have taught. Draft the board's decision memo: recommend renewal, rejection, or renewal with conditions, with reasons the researchers could respond to, and include a serious account of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
