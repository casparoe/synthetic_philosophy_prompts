im a cog sci PhD student and I've been going in circles on the computationalism debate. It feels like a huge chunk of the argument is really a fight over what counts as a computer in the first place, and people slide between definitions when it's convenient. So I want a working definition of computer (or computation, if you think the verb is the better place to start) that does actual classifcatory work.

My gut, for whatever it's worth: a pocket calculator, a chess engine, and a brain are computers; a rock and a hurricane are not. But I can't say what principle backs that up, and I keep getting stuck on edge cases.

Run your definition through all of these and tell me where it lands:
- a human following a recipe step by step
- a thermostat
- a sunflower tracking the sun across the sky
- an abacus, with a person actually using it
- Conway's Game of Life running a universal Turing machine
- a GPU doing one forward pass through a transformer
- a market setting prices through lots of local trades

The definition also needs to not immediately fall to the old triviality worry where you can interpret any physical system as implementing any finite-state automaton if you're allowed to gerrymander the mapping. If your definition can't avoid that, I'd rather you say so and tell me where you'd bite the bullet.

Which of my gut verdicts does the best definition force me to give up, and what's the weirdest concession it drags you into?
