I've got a rule I apply when I read cognitive science, and I want to know if it's defensible or if I'm just smuggling in the language of thought hypothesis. I basically refuse to count a model of some psychological ability as an explanation unless it tells me what the system's representations are and how they combine and get manipulated. So when a connectionist or neural-network paper says the network learns the systematicity, or that the distributed patterns encode the structure, I treat that as a redescription of the data with extra steps rather than a theory of mind. My labmate says I'm holding competitors to a classical, compositional standard of explanation that they never agreed to, so my rule settles the argument by stipulation. Is this rule a defensible epistemic policy, or is it circular? And if it is circular, is there a restated version that keeps whatever the rule is getting at without begging the question—or should I just drop it and evaluate these models on their own terms?
