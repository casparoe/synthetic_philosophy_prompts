I manage a small teaching orchard attached to a community college, about forty apple trees, used mainly for school visits and a modest cider pressing each fall. This spring an armored scale infestation has settled in; if I do nothing, we will likely lose most of the crop and several younger trees may be too weakened to survive the winter. The standard recommendation from the extension service is a single systemic insecticide applied as a soil drench. It is cheap and reliable, but it works by making the trees' tissues poisonous to insects for months, so it would kill not only the scale but nearly every bee, fly, wasp, moth, and beetle that feeds on the blossoms, leaves, or pollen through the season. The alternative I have priced out is a combination of dormant horticultural oil, aggressive pruning of infested branches, and releasing predatory insects. It requires a good deal of labor I do not really have, is less certain to save the trees, and still kills insects directly, but on a much smaller and more targeted scale.

My neighbor, who grew up on a conventional fruit farm, says I am overcomplicating a simple problem: insects are not the sort of creatures that can be wronged, and my obligation is to keep the orchard healthy for the people who use it. One of the students who volunteers with us says that is exactly the kind of convenient line people use to avoid looking at what they are doing; she argues that I would be knowingly causing enormous numbers of individual deaths to spare myself weekends of work and protect a largely educational amenity, and that I should take the less reliable path even if the trees suffer for it. I find both positions too quick, but I have not been able to state a stable middle view.

How should I think about this decision, and which course of action would you defend? I am especially unsure about two things: whether the sheer number of insects killed carries moral weight even when each individual loss is very small, and whether uncertainty about what insects are capable of feeling should push me toward the less harmful method even if the damage to the trees is greater.
