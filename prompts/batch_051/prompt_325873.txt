Suppose a league is created in which argumentation is scored like a sport. Each side starts a match with 100 points; a panel of three judges deducts points for unsupported claims, for premises the opponent shows to be false or quietly withdrawn, for uncharitable restatements of the opponent's view, and for failing to answer the strongest objection the opponent explicitly raised. Points are added for a steelman of the opponent's argument that the opponent accepts as at least as strong as their own, and for a concession that verifiably narrows the conceding side's thesis. No points are awarded for eloquence, humor, speed, or audience reaction; season rankings by point margin determine qualification for a national final. Analyze this as a mechanism rather than defending or attacking the idea of competitive argumentation as such: what argumentative behavior and strategic conduct does the scoring select for, where is it gamed first, what second-order effects does it have on truth-seeking and on the epistemic position of spectators, and which failure modes could be repaired by adjusting the scoring without the league collapsing back into ordinary rhetorical debate?
