Construct a thought experiment in which epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment deliver opposite verdicts about a single knowledge ascription, in the direction that is harder to engineer: the subject's practical stakes are high, but the attributor's conversational context is ordinary and would not raise the standards for "knows." Constraints: one everyday scenario, no bank cases, no fake barns, no lotteries, no deliberate deception or misleading evidence, and the proposition in question must actually be true. After the scenario, run the diagnostics. Identify the subject, the proposition, the method by which the subject formed the belief, what is practically at stake for the subject, and why the attributor's conversational context remains low-standards. Then state, for each theory, whether the ascription "S knows that p" comes out true or false, and explain why. Finish by pinpointing which feature of the case is doing the divergence-producing work, and give the strongest reply a contextualist could make to try to keep the case from counting.
