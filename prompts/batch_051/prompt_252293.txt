I run a 90-minute session for 10–12 technically minded rationalists, mostly longtime readers of LessWrong and adjacent decision-theory discussions. Design a runnable in-person game or activity with small real stakes that makes the difference between counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail felt before it is named. Participants should make actual choices in both setups, and the rules or incentives should be arranged so that paying in the counterfactual-mugging case and refusing in the XOR-blackmail case become live options rather than a reproduction of standard blog arguments. Include exact rules, materials, a payoff scheme that does not require real money but does create real stakes or rankings, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened to the relevant decision-theory distinctions without introducing the labels until after the choices. Also indicate what will predictably go wrong on a first run and which features of the original thought experiments the activity cannot reproduce. The structural point should emerge from the choices themselves, not from the facilitator.
