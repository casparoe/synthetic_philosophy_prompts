Draft the joint statement that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between two positions on pure time preference and the discounting of future welfare. Position A holds that a positive rate of pure time preference — valuing a future benefit less than a present benefit of the same kind solely because it lies further in the future, after correcting for uncertainty about whether it will occur — is rationally permissible, not merely a bias or a policy error. Position B holds that pure time preference is rationally and ethically indefensible, and that the only legitimate grounds for counting a future benefit as worth less are uncertainty, opportunity cost, and, where relevant, expected changes in circumstances or resources.

The deliverable is the document the two parties would sign. It should contain: (i) a numbered list of non-vacuous agreed claims, making clear what the dispute is not about — for instance the distinction between discounting future welfare and discounting future goods or money, the descriptive claim that people and institutions commonly display positive time preference, and the uncontroversial roles of uncertainty and growth in practical valuation; (ii) a table of the remaining disagreements, in which each side's view is stated in wording that side would accept; (iii) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one side, for example whether pure time preference is more like a primitive rational attitude, as risk aversion is usually taken to be, or more like a bias, as present-bias is; and (iv) for each crux, the arguments, thought experiments or empirical findings both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. State which parts of the disagreement, if any, you judge to be merely verbal.

Throughout, indicate how confident you are that your formulations would be accepted by each party, and identify the single point about which you are least sure.
