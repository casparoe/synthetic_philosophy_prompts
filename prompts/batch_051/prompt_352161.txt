Im trying to get a handle on whether Malthus's argument is actually falsifiable, or whether its defenders have just kept moving the goalposts. In An Essay on the Principle of Population, Malthus writes:

"It has been said, that the great question is now at issue, whether man shall henceforth start forwards with accelerated velocity towards illimitable, and hitherto unconceived improvement; or be condemned to a perpetual oscillation between happiness and misery, and after every effort remain still at an immeasurable distance from the wished-for goal."

That "perpetual oscillation" is his real claim, not just that food grows slower than population for a while. Write an essay on whether Malthusian population theory should be treated as falsified by what has happened since, or whether "right but early" is a legitimate way to rescue a long-run prediction. Weigh both sides and then actually defend a verdict—dont end with "well it depends." In particular, think about what evidence would have to be enough to count against a theory like this, and whether the theory has been given so many escape hatches that it no longer predicts anything definite.
