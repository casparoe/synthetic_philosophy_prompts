Compose the document that a committed higher-order thought theorist and a committed self-representational theorist of phenomenal consciousness could both sign after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration. The higher-order thought theorist holds that a mental state is phenomenally conscious only when the subject is aware of it in virtue of a numerically distinct, occurrent, assertoric higher-order thought to the effect that they are in that state. The self-representational theorist holds that a conscious state represents itself, so the awareness of it is intrinsic and no numerically distinct higher-order state is needed. Neither side should be a strawman; each claim about a position should be wording its own advocate would accept.

The document should have: (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign about state consciousness and about what any adequate theory must explain; (2) a disagreements table stating the remaining disagreement precisely, with each side's entry in wording that side would accept; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one of them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. Begin with the document itself, not with any preface or commentary.
