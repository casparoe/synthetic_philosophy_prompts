Just had a long argument with a friend about collective memory vs. the historical record. I ended up defending something like Nietzsche's line—that a community's need for a usable, life-oriented past should often outweigh the obligation to preserve every inconvenient detail of what happened. The passage I kept coming back to is from Nietzsche's "On the Use and Abuse of History for Life":

We do need history, but quite differently from the jaded idlers in the garden of knowledge, however grandly they may look down on our rude and unpicturesque requirements. In other words, we need it for life and action, not as a convenient way to avoid life and action, or to excuse a selfish life and a cowardly or base action.

My friend kept saying this is just a license for communities to ignore inconvenient facts about themselves. I want you to steelman that objection but also go further: what would actually count against the position? I mean observations, cases, arguments, or consequences that should make someone retreat from it, not just the standard "memory distorts" stuff. Sort them by how decisive they'd be. And be honest about whether the position as stated is even falsifiable, or whether it just absorbs every counterexample by saying "well, that isn't serving life." Tell me what would change your mind, or where my reasoning is most likely to fail.
