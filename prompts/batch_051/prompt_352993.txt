I sit on the animal welfare and ethical review body at a British university. We are considering a proposal from a psychology research group to study whether domestic dogs will incur a cost to spare a familiar dog from pain. The protocol involves training each subject dog to press either of two levers; after training, one lever begins to deliver a food reward to the subject while simultaneously delivering a brief, low-intensity electric shock to a dog in an adjacent enclosure, whereas the other lever delivers the same reward without the shock. The shocks are expected to be transient and below any threshold for tissue damage, and the protocol includes early stopping criteria. There is no dispute that the work is methodologically competent, is not required by regulation to be replaced with non-animal methods, and has plausible scientific merit. The committee is nonetheless split down the middle. Some members, on broadly Kantian grounds, hold that the dogs are not ends in themselves and that our duties regarding them are only indirect: cruelty or callousness toward animals is objectionable because it erodes dispositions we owe to human beings, and on that view the proposal is acceptable provided the human researchers are properly safeguarded against moral coarsening. Others reply that the experiment cannot intelligibly be assessed without granting that the second dog's pain matters directly, because the entire hypothesis is about the subject dog's sensitivity to another dog's suffering; they argue that if the shocks were merely harms to our own character, the study would be about the researchers, not the dogs, and approval would be incoherent. Both sides accept that the disagreement is philosophical rather than technical. Please draft the written opinion I shall circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- set out the reasoning for it, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than merely refuting a caricature of it.
