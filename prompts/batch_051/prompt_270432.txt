I am a retired neurophysiologist who came to philosophy of mind late and has now been reading it seriously for a few years. I have agreed to lead a single ninety-minute session on epiphenomenalism for a discussion group of retired scientists and physicians, none of whom have formal philosophical training. I do not want a general introduction to epiphenomenalism; I want a plan for teaching the specific difficulty usually called the paradox of phenomenal judgment—namely, that if conscious experience is real but causally inert, then the judgments we make about our own experience are themselves neural events with neural causes, and the experiences those judgments report never enter the causal story; a subject's report that they are in pain would occur even if the pain were absent but the underlying neural state were unchanged. Please do not supply the explanation itself, but the teaching plan. The plan should specify: (1) the order in which the component ideas must be introduced and what depends on what at each step, including where the distinction between phenomenal consciousness and psychological or access consciousness needs to enter so the audience does not conclude that experience merely has no function; (2) a single carrying example or thought experiment—I have been considering the contrast between a normal subject and a physically identical subject in whom the relevant neural state occurs without any experience—and what that example must have, and lack, not to mislead this particular audience; (3) the misconceptions to expect, the exact point in the sequence at which each should be headed off, and which ones will keep resurfacing—I anticipate at least the conflation of epiphenomenalism with illusionism, a drift into the evolutionary question of why consciousness was selected for, and the slide into treating the paradox as global skepticism about reasoning; (4) a final question or short check that shows whether they have grasped the paradox as distinct from merely finding epiphenomenalism implausible; and (5) what I should openly set aside as beyond the session's scope.
