You are a researcher at the intersection of machine-learning interpretability, philosophy of science, and epistemology, and you have been asked for a careful, expert numerical judgment. The proposition I want your credence for is the following:

P: For any currently foreseeable class of large, trained neural systems whose individual outputs or decisions we have strong practical reason to understand (for example, because they act in high-stakes settings), interpretation does not hit a fundamental ceiling. More precisely: for any behavior pattern of such a system that matters to us, there exists, in principle, an explanation of that behavior built out of components that a trained human expert can grasp, and this explanation is faithful enough that relying on it instead of treating the system as a black box lets us make better predictions and interventions about that behavior pattern than any black-box behavioral model of comparable complexity would allow. Assume the interpretation process may itself be AI-assisted, including by systems as capable as the one being interpreted.

In other words, interpretability is hard but not in principle bounded: increasingly capable AI can be brought under increasingly capable, possibly recursive AI interpretation, and the regress of AI interpreting AI interpreting AI need not terminate in incomprehensibility. For the purpose of this judgment, P is false if there is some important class of behaviors for which no human-graspable, component-level explanation can match or beat the best black-box model in predictive and interventionist payoff, even in principle, and no AI-assisted search procedure can close that gap.

I am not asking whether interpretability will actually be carried out for every deployed system, or whether it will be achieved on useful timelines. The proposition is about whether any ceiling that exists is a matter of cost, effort, and practice rather than a fundamental limit on what a human-graspable explanation can capture and what an automated interpreter can recover.
