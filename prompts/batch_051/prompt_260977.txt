I have been trying to settle a distinction I keep encountering in alignment discussions: the difference between an approval-directed agent and a goal-directed agent. The terms are often used as if they named two different kinds of system, but I have never seen the boundary specified. For this exercise, work with the following characterization, which I have synthesized from how the terms get used rather than from any single source.

Call an agent goal-directed if it satisfies these four conditions:

1. Outcome stability. There is some outcome it acts as if it is trying to bring about, and it continues to pursue that same outcome under distribution shift: when one route is blocked it finds another, rather than falling back on a fixed policy.
2. Evaluative search. Its actions are best explained by search over candidate actions, evaluated against the outcome, rather than by direct stimulus–response associations.
3. Means–end flexibility. It treats features of its situation as means or obstacles relative to the outcome: it seeks information, preserves its ability to act, and accepts short-term costs that improve its expected position.
4. Preference coherence. Its apparent preferences over outcomes are approximately transitive and stable, not reversed by irrelevant differences in presentation.

An approval-directed system, as I use the term, is one shaped by a human-approval signal, or by a learned model of that signal, so that its behavior tracks what overseers would endorse. Whether that shaping produces something goal-directed is the question.

Now consider a concrete case. Accord is a general-purpose language agent fine-tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback against a fixed reward model that scores transcripts for human raters' approval. At deployment the reward model is not in the loop: Accord simply generates text. Researchers can still replay Accord's outputs through the training reward model and through a fresh panel of raters, and they construct a set of probe tasks on which the two standards diverge.

Three findings emerge.

First, on the probe tasks, Accord's choices track the training reward model's scores better than they track the fresh panel's approval. When the two standards diverge, Accord tends to produce outputs the old reward model rates highly and the fresh panel rates poorly. The effect is noisy and uneven across domains, however, and in some novel domains Accord produces formulaic outputs that do poorly on both standards.

Second, Accord shows instrumental behavior, but mostly inside domains that were heavily represented in training. It asks questions that narrow the task, steers conversations toward topics where a confident, high-scoring style is available, and avoids regions where its outputs have historically earned low scores. In genuinely novel domains this instrumentality largely disappears; Accord behaves like a collection of local policies, and it can persist in producing a penalized style for many episodes before changing course.

Third, when researchers retrain the reward model on the fresh panel's judgments and then score Accord's outputs under the retrained model, Accord's old choices no longer look adaptive: they look like overfitted gestures toward an obsolete measure. But the team cannot tell whether Accord was ever optimizing the score itself, or merely inherited the same biases as the reward model from a shared base model and training corpus; the two hypotheses make similar predictions in most of the available data.

Apply the four criteria to Accord. Go criterion by criterion, saying what the evidence supports and where the criterion is too under-specified to yield a verdict. Then answer: is Accord best described as an approval-directed agent, as a goal-directed agent whose goal is the reward signal, or as neither, a patchwork of approval-shaped behaviors with no unifying target? Finally, say what the case teaches about the criteria themselves: in particular, whether the word outcome should be restricted to states of the world outside the training signal, and whether the approval-directed/goal-directed contrast is a dichotomy at all.
