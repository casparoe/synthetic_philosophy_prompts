Galen Strawson's Basic Argument is often summarised along these lines. An agent acts as she does because of the way she is. To be ultimately morally responsible for what she does, she would have to be ultimately responsible for the way she is, at least in the relevant mental respects. But she cannot be ultimately responsible for the way she is: she would have to have consciously chosen how she is, and that choice would itself have to issue from an already existing self for which she was likewise responsible, and so on without end. Therefore no one is ultimately morally responsible.

My difficulty is this. The argument appears to get its force from the thought that the agent's character and motives are already settled by something outside the agent's control. But that is exactly what incompatibilists claim removes responsibility, and exactly what compatibilists deny. If an action or choice were undetermined, could the agent not then be its ultimate source and thereby stop the regress? So why is the Basic Argument treated as a challenge to moral responsibility as such, rather than merely to compatibilist accounts of responsibility? What precisely is doing the work in the argument, and does it succeed in showing what it claims to show?
