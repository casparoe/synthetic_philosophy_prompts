I am a retired natural scientist who has recently begun reading Plato’s Republic closely. In Book II, Glaucon describes the ring of Gyges and argues that anyone who could render himself invisible would behave unjustly: the shepherd uses the ring to enter the palace, seduce the queen, and kill the king, and Glaucon treats this as what any man would do once detection is impossible. I want to alter one feature of the case. Suppose the ring makes the act visible while it is happening, but erases every witness’s memory of it within an hour and prevents any lasting record of it from being formed. The wearer knows there will be immediate reactions—distress, perhaps attempts to resist or obstruct him—but the act will afterward be unmemorable and unattributable. Does Glaucon’s conclusion survive this change? Or does his argument depend on there being no witness at all, no resistance, and no moment of confrontation? State which standard diagnosis of the original puzzle is most affected—the idea that justice is only a compromise for mutual protection, the idea that people seek reputation rather than justice, or the Socratic reply that justice is a condition of the soul—and say whether the variation strengthens or weakens that diagnosis. Be explicit about exactly what the variation removes from the original.
