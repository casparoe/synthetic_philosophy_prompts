I am easing back into epistemology after some years away and have been trying to get clear on what the bank cases are supposed to show about the relation between knowledge and practical stakes. A piece in a popular philosophy magazine summarized the debate as follows: "Both contextualists and pragmatic encroachers are responding to the same puzzle. In the familiar bank case, a person believes the bank will be open on Saturday on the strength of a fairly recent visit, and his evidence does not change between a low-stakes situation and a high-stakes one in which being wrong would cost him a great deal. Nearly everyone agrees that we will readily say he knows in the first situation and are reluctant to say so in the second. The contextualist explains this by saying that, once more is at stake, the person's evidence no longer meets the higher evidential bar: the higher stakes make his justification insufficient. The pragmatic encroacher explains it by saying that knowledge is constituted partly by how much a person has to lose, or equivalently by what he can safely rely on in practical reasoning. What matters is that both positions end up claiming that knowledge itself—not merely our willingness to use the word 'knows'—rises or falls with practical stakes. Once this is granted, the two labels look like two ways of marking the same point, and the dispute is largely verbal: should we describe the change as raising standards or as raising the threshold of justification?" That paragraph unsettles me. I had filed away the thought that contextualism is a semantic thesis about the word "knows" while pragmatic encroachment is a metaphysical thesis about knowledge itself, but the article makes them sound nearly interchangeable. Since the author is a professional and I am returning to this material, I do not trust my own suspicion. Please give me an accuracy audit of the summary. Separate what is accurate, what is a defensible popular simplification, and what a contextualist or a pragmatic encroacher would reject. In particular, trace where the summary moves from genuine data points about the bank cases to the claim that both views make knowledge itself depend on stakes, and say whether the final claim that the dispute is largely verbal follows from the accurate parts or hangs on the errors. Finally, state how the bank cases should be described under each view if one is being careful.
