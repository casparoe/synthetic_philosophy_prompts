I write about science and ideas for a living, and there's a reccuring move I keep seeing in stories about calendar reform that I don't have a word for.

The move goes like this. Someone points out that the calendar most of the world uses is anchored to a religious event — the traditional date for the birth of Jesus — and that this is arbitrary, or at least not universal. So far so good. Then they propose a fix that's supposed to be neutral. The astronomers' version quietly inserts a year zero and negative years so the arithmetic comes out cleaner; only, the new year 0 sits exactly where 1 BC sat, so the 'neutral' system has just inherited the same sectarian pivot with smoother arithmetic. The Holocene calendar people add 10,000 years and say the era now starts near the beginning of the Holocene and the first farming villages, as if that were a fact about nature; but the actual system is just the AD year with 10,000 added, same months, same underlying year count, minus the candor. The French revolutionary calendar pulled the same trick earlier: it replaced the Christian era with Year One of the Republic while claiming to be based on nature and reason, but its year was anchored to an equinox observed in Paris and to a political founding. Every time, the arbitrary bit doesn't go away; it just gets pushed up a level, and the person proposing the fix usually seems sincere about not having noticed.

I know there's plenty of writing on conventions and on value-laden choices hiding inside supposedly objective systems. What I'm after is this specific maneuver: denouncing an epoch as parochial, then replacing it with one presented as natural or universal or merely technical, when the replacement either inherits the old pivot or tucks a new arbitrary choice out of sight. Does that maneuver have a name? If it doesn't, what would you call it, and how does it differ from a plain arbitrary convention (where nobody pretends), from a persuasive definition (where you redefine a term to win a point), and from ordinary motivated reasoning? The label matters less to me than the carving: where does this pattern start and end, and are there cases where a supposedly neutral epoch actually succeeds at being neutral?
