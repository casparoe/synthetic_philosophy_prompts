I've been going in circles on forgiveness lately, mostly because a friend and I spent a whole evening arguing about whether you can forgive someone who isn't sorry, and about whether saying "I forgive you" through gritted teeth is a lie, a promise, or just noise. The more I read the worse it gets: people seem to mean completely different things by the word — the resentment fading, a speech act, giving up your claim to payback, going back to normal as if nothing happened — and every definition I try keeps sliding into a seperate question.

Before I read any more theories I want the checklist a view has to clear. Write the desiderata for an adequate account of interpersonal forgiveness — forgiveness between ordinary adults in personal life, not legal pardon or divine absolution, though you can use those as contrast cases. For each requirement, give the concrete situation or consideration that forces it, not just a label. I especially want to know which contrasts a theory isn't allowed to blur (forgiving vs. excusing, vs. condoning, vs. forgetting, vs. reconciling, and any others you'd add), which situations it has to get right (unrepentant wrongdoers, repeat offenders, forgiving the dead, forgiving yourself, forgiving on someone else's behalf, small slights vs. grave harms), and what it commits one to about whether forgiveness can be a duty and whether it can be unconditional. Flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be satisfied together — for instance, the requirement that forgiving not turn into shrugging off wrongdoing, and the requirement that forgiving someone who isn't sorry be possible. Then briefly run three broad families of view — feeling-based, speech-act, and relationship/commitment — against the list, but keep that part short; the criteria are what I want, not the scorekeeping.

Use exactly these sections, in order: "The requirements, with the case behind each" (about 55% of the response), "Non-negotiable vs. negotiable" (about 15%), "Where requirements conflict" (about 15%), "How the three families do" (about 10%), "The single most demanding requirement" (about 5%), and "Bottom line" (a sentence or two). A table is fine for the first section.
