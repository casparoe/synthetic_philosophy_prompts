In my course on the Nordic model we keep coming back to the idea that high taxes and high trust reinforce each other: people pay their taxes because they trust that everyone else is paying too, and the welfare state makes people less scared of being taken advantage of, which supposedly makes them even more willing to pay. I was trying to turn this into a variant of the one-shot prisoner's dilemma we did earlier in the term, and I think I've confused myself.

Take the standard numbers before any tax: temptation T=5, reward R=3, punishment P=1, sucker S=0, so it's a proper prisoner's dilemma (mutual cooperation is socially best, but defecting is strictly dominant). Now add a redistributive stage after every play: each player pays 50% of whatever they just earned into a common pot, and the pot is split equally between the two players. In the asymmetric case where one defects and the other cooperates, the defector keeps 2.5 of his 5 and then gets half the pot (1.25), so 3.75 total, while the cooperator walks away with 1.25. When I work through the best responses I get something strange: if I expect you to cooperate I still want to defect (3.75 > 3), but if I expect you to defect I now want to cooperate (1.25 > 1). So mutual defection stops being an equilibrium, but mutual cooperation doesn't become one either. Am I right that the only pure equilibria are the two asymmetric ones were one person ends up the sucker and the other the free-rider?

Run through the 50% case for me to make sure I've got it. Then I want to know what happens as the tax rate increases: at what rate, if any, does mutual cooperation become the unique equilibrium? And the conceptual part: is that because high taxes genuinely make people trust one another more, or just because a high enough tax makes it rational to be the exploited one? Does the toy model say anything about whether the Nordic model can be exported, or is it too stripped-down to tell us anything about the real thing?
