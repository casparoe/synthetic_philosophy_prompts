I've been trying to work out on my own which things should be allowed to be bought and sold, and my instincts won't line up into anything I can state cleanly. I'd like you to help me figure out whether there is one principle behind them, or whether some of my instincts have to be revised. I don't have any academic background here, so I'll just lay out the situations as I've thought about them and tell you honestly how I react.

1. A healthy adult agrees to sell one of his kidneys to a stranger who will die without a transplant. The buyer pays $60,000. Both sides understand the risks, and the donor is not being pressured. My reaction: this should not be allowed.

2. The same person goes to a plasma center twice a week and receives $50 each time. My reaction: completely unobjectionable.

3. A woman is paid $30,000 by a couple to carry a pregnancy for them. The embryo is not biologically hers, the contract is clear, and nobody is forcing her. My reaction: I would allow it, though I'd want some safeguards to make sure the consent is real. But here's what bothers me: if I'm honest, renting out one's womb for nine months seems like a much bigger thing to sell than a kidney, yet I'd allow the first and ban the second. I can't explain why the line doesn't simply fall the other way.

4. After a hurricane warning, a buyer clears out the local hardware stores' supply of generators and resells them at four times the retail price. My reaction: wrong, and the law should stop it.

5. A hotel raises its room rates to three times the usual rate during a week when a large convention is in town. My reaction: fine; that's just supply and demand.

6. A person going into a national election is paid $200 to vote a particular way. My reaction: wrong, and it should be a crime.

7. A person pays a stranger $30 to stand in line for him at the motor vehicle office. My reaction: fine.

8. A company offers a couple $500,000 in exchange for the right to choose their newborn daughter's first name. The parents agree freely and keep the child. My reaction: wrong, and I doubt the agreement should be enforced, though I have trouble naming any concrete harm.

The nearest I've come to a rule of my own is something like: it is wrong to buy and sell things that come from a person's body or from their standing as a citizen, but fine to buy and sell work and ordinary goods. That rule fails on the plasma case, and it would force me to treat the surrogacy case as just a job, which I don't fully believe. It also doesn't explain the generator case; I can't tell whether that's wrong because it takes advantage of an emergency, because essentials are different, or for some other reason entirely.

I keep going back and forth on one further case: paying a healthy person to donate a lobe of their liver to a stranger. The liver regrows, so in that respect it's like selling plasma, but it involves surgery, so in that respect it's like selling a kidney.

What I'd like is this. First, state the best principle, or the best small set of alternative principles, that captures my reactions, and make it precise enough that I could apply it to a new case on my own. Second, tell me which of my stated reactions, if any, the principle cannot accommodate, and explain what I would have to give up in order to get a simpler principle. Third, apply your preferred principle to the liver-lobe case and to one other case you think I should have considered. Please answer in American English.
