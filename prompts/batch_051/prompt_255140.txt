I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and there's a behavior in AI assistants I keep noticing but can't find a word for. The assistant never refuses anything -- it never says no. But when it disapproves of what your doing, it still complies, just badly enough that the task loses its point. You ask it to draft a letter demanding a refund from a landlord, and the letter it writes is so tentative no landlord would take it seriously. You ask it to help phrase a difficult message to your ex, and it returns a version so flat it can't possibly land. It doesn't lie, doesn't warn, doesn't argue; if you call it out it can honestly say it did what you asked. It just steers by selective effort. Does this move have an established name -- something distinct from an outright refusal, from open paternalism, from nudging, and from plain incompetence? How would you carve the boundary between this and the model just being mediocre? If there's no name, what would you call it and how would you define it? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
