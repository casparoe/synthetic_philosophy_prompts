I am writing a short essay on what makes something a disease, and the more cases I line up, the less confident I am that the word tracks any single thing. My biology textbook calls pneumococcal pneumonia a disease but calls stage 1 hypertension a risk factor; my cousin's pediatrician says his ADHD is a disorder, not a disease; and I have seen obesity called a disease by some doctors and a condition by others. I would like you to propose a single working definition of disease that I can actually apply, not a summary of how different groups use the word.

Here are the cases, with my current verdicts.

I am confident that pneumococcal pneumonia is a disease. I am confident that type 1 diabetes is a disease. I am confident that a wrist fractured in a skateboarding fall is not a disease, even though it is plainly a medical problem. I am confident that an uncomplicated pregnancy is not a disease.

The cases I am not sure about are these:

1. Stage 1 hypertension in a forty-five-year-old with no symptoms and no organ damage. I lean toward: a risk factor, not a disease.
2. Obesity in a person with no current complications. I lean toward: probably a disease.
3. ADHD in a twelve-year-old. I lean toward: not a disease, whatever else it is.
4. Intense grief six weeks after a parent's death, in a person who meets several features of depression but whose reaction is proportionate and gradually easing. I lean toward: not a disease.
5. Adult lactose nonpersistence: a healthy adult who gets cramps and diarrhea after drinking milk because she produces little lactase after childhood, which is common adult human variation. I lean toward: not a disease, just a normal variant.
6. Infertility in a couple where neither partner has any identifiable biological abnormality. I genuinely do not know.

I chose these cases because two instincts I cannot reconcile keep pulling at me. One says a disease is just a part of the body failing to do what it is biologically for. The other says disease is inseparable from being made worse off, and that cannot be settled by biology alone. My cases seem designed to pull those two instincts apart.

Please propose a definition of disease, run it through all ten cases, and mark every place where the definition and my starting verdicts conflict. Then do the harder part: say which of your own verdicts is least secure, and either revise the definition to fix the problem or defend the surprising verdict. I am less interested in what some professional body has voted to call each case; I want a definition that explains why the clear cases are clear and forces a defensible answer on the borderline ones.
