A colleague in an ethics discussion group defended the view that mortality is a condition of a meaningful human life, and in reconstructing his reasoning I seem to have arrived at an argument that proves too much. I still cannot see exactly which step should be surrendered, and I would like a diagnosis.

(1) A human life in the biographical sense — the life of a particular person considered as a whole — is meaningful only if it has the structure of a narrative rather than a mere chronological sequence of events.

(2) A narrative gets its structure from the way it ends: the ending is what gathers earlier events into a whole, allows the whole to be assessed for coherence and point, and distinguishes a shaped story from an arbitrary stretch of time.

(3) Death is the only ending a human life can have. If death were indefinitely postponable, a life would not have a fixed end from which it could be assessed as a whole, and so it would lack the structure required by (1).

(4) Thus mortality is not merely an unfortunate fact but a condition of meaning; radical life extension is in tension with the very structure that makes a life meaningful.

(5) But a death at thirty supplies an ending just as surely as a death at ninety. On the criterion in (2) and (3), both lives are closed by death, so both have the feature said to confer narrative structure.

(6) Consequently, the view cannot distinguish a life that is completed from one that is cut short. It seems committed to saying that dying young is not, in the relevant sense, a defect of shape.

(7) That conclusion is unacceptable: whatever else we say, a life that ends at thirty is precisely one whose story has been left unfinished. So the argument in (1)-(6) must be wrong somewhere.

Where does the argument go wrong, if it does? If the reasoning is valid, identify the premise that is least costly to abandon and explain what abandoning it costs for the rest of the view. I am most reluctant to give up (1) and (2); before I accept that the asymmetry between dying at thirty and dying at ninety has to go, I want to see exactly what the alternatives are.
