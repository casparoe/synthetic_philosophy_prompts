I keep noticing a conversational pattern that I don't have a name for, and I'm trying to understand where its boundaries are. A speaker asks a question or makes a remark that only makes sense if some background claim is true, even though that claim has never been introduced, let alone accepted, in the conversation. Often the other person simply answers or responds, and afterward both of them treat the background claim as established, though neither ever asserted or argued for it. For example, if someone asks me which of two books I found more useful, when I never said I found either of them useful, and I answer "the first," the claim that I found them useful seems to slip into the shared record along with my answer. I also notice an asymmetry: answering such a question in the negative sometimes rejects only the explicit content while leaving the embedded assumption standing, whereas objecting to the question itself blocks the assumption from entering at all. Is there an established name for this mechanism—and for the hearer's act of silently allowing the unargued assumption into what both parties may henceforth rely on? If the territory is covered by several neighboring notions, I'm less interested in a bare label than in the carving: which notion covers which part of what I have described, and which cases fall outside all of them.
