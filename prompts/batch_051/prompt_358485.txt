In my philosophy club we were arguing about whether praying for stuff makes any sense if God is all-knowing and never changes. I think the anti-prayer side basically wins, and I wrote the argument out like this:

(1) If God is omniscient, then before I pray, God already knows what I'm going to ask and exactly how He's going to respond.
(2) If God is immutable, then His will can't be changed by anything I do.
(3) So my praying can't make God do anything He wouldn't have done anyway.
(4) If my praying can't make God do anything He wouldn't have done anyway, then petitionary prayer is pointless.
(5) So petitionary prayer is pointless.

Everyone in the club says the argument is to quick, but I think its sound. The only way out I can see is rejecting (2), and that's just giving up on God being immutable. Am I right, or does a specific step (or the move from one step to the next) fail? If something fails, point to the exact step and give the strongest reading of the premise or word that causes the trouble.
