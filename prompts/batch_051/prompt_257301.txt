I'm a novelist, deep into a multi-strand historical novel set between roughly 1470 and 1570. The book is supposed to be polycentric: chapters in Antwerp, Hormuz, Mbanza Kongo, Tenochtitlan, and Nanjing, with no single city treated as the place the story is really happening from. I've done the research, so the facts aren't the problem. The problem is that my narration keeps defaulting to a European center anyway. A chapter that's mostly about Gujarati and Acehnese shipping starts on a Portuguese deck; my Kongo material keeps introducing local politics through the arrival of Portuguese clergy; background narration keeps saying "Europe had not yet..." as if the future were already a European fact. A historian friend read three chapters and said the thing is structural, not local, and that I should stop fixing scenes one by one and run a procedure on every scene.

So here's what I want from you. Write me a checklist/decision procedure for auditing each scene of a polycentric historical novel for Eurocentrism. It has to be usable by me at a desk, not by a room of specialists, and it can't take an afternoon per scene or the draft will die. I want the questions in the order I should ask them, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision back to me because its an artistic call and not a detectable bias. Ground it in what's really at stake in the global-history debate: the difference between writing about European actors and writing from a European point of view, between showing connections and making Europe the cause of them, between setting a scene in one place and treating that place as a periphery waiting for something. Then run the procedure on two or three short scenes (use my examples or invent your own), and make one of them a scene I'll probably want to keep even though the procedure flags it. Be honest about what the procedure can't do. I suspect the underlying question — whether the past can be told from everywhere at once, or whether that's an impossible ideal the book can only fail at — isn't going to be settled by a checklist, so tell me where the checklist ends and that question starts.
