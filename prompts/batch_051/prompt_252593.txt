(2) But if she says "You're fired" while we're both laughing at a party, I'm not fired—same words, same person.
(3) A stranger on a bus saying "I now pronounce you married" hasn't married anyone.
(4) An actor on stage saying "I swear to avenge my father" hasn't actually sworn anything.
(5) If I say "I bet you ten dollars it rains tomorrow" and you stay silent, no bet has happened. If you say "you're on," there's a bet.
(6) If I say "I promise to send you the file by Friday" while planning not to, I still promised. I broke it, but the promise existed.
(7) But if someone says "I'm sorry" with an eye roll and zero regret, I want to say they didn't actually apologize. And that feels in tension with (6), since both are the standard words with the required feeling missing.
(8) A waiter who tells me "I order you to leave" isn't ordering me; he can ask me to leave, but it isn't an order.

I can't tell whether there's one principle behind all this or whether I'm tracking several different things and calling them all "doing something with words."
