I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, working on temporal experience and postdictive effects. I'd like you to work through a modification of a classic case.

The classic case is the color phi display: a red spot is flashed at one location, and a green spot is flashed shortly afterward at a nearby location. Observers report a single moving spot that changes from red to green roughly mid-path, before the green flash physically appears. Dennett contrasts an Orwellian account, on which the subject first experiences a red moving spot and the record is edited after the green flash arrives, with a Stalinesque account, on which conscious experience is delayed long enough for the green to be spliced into the moving spot before it reaches awareness. On Dennett's treatment these accounts are behaviorally indistinguishable, and asking which is true is meant to be an unanswerable question rather than a live empirical dispute.

Now modify the case in one way: add an online speeded response. Participants are told to press a key the instant the moving spot appears to turn green, and reaction times are measured relative to the physical onset of the green flash. After each trial they also give the usual retrospective judgment about where along the path the color change occurred.

Work through what the Orwellian and Stalinesque accounts should each predict about (a) the distribution of reaction times relative to the green flash, and (b) the relationship between those reaction times and the retrospective judgments. Then say whether this variation breaks the behavioral equivalence, or whether each account can absorb the new data without changing its core commitment. If they reconverge, specify exactly where the remaining choice now lives.

After your analysis, include a JSON object with the following fields:
- "variant": a one-sentence restatement of the modification.
- "orwellian_predictions": the reaction-time and report pattern the Orwellian account must commit to under the speeded task.
- "stalinesque_predictions": the corresponding commitments for the Stalinesque account.
- "verdict": whether the variation breaks the equivalence or merely relocates it, with a one- or two-sentence justification.
- "residual_locus": where the two accounts still differ, if anywhere, once the new data are accommodated.
