I read decision letters and policy documents for a living, though I am not a philosopher by training, and I have started to notice something I cannot name. A grant committee rejects a proposal, and its letter gives a careful, substantive rationale. But when I talk with the reviewers individually, none of them actually holds the rationale in the letter: one thought the methodology was weak, another thought the topic sat outside the funding line, a third simply found the proposal unconvincing — and yet all signed the letter. It was not a lie in the ordinary sense, because each genuinely endorsed the decision; nevertheless, the reasons the committee gave were, quite literally, nobody's reasons. I have since seen the same shape elsewhere. A loan algorithm rejects an applicant and produces a clean explanation of the rejection, but the explanation was generated after the decision, and neither the engineers nor the system actually used it; still, everyone treats it as the reason the applicant was denied. And in public debate, a policy is defended by a rationale that, on inspection, no participant in the original decision ever weighed, though people now speak as if that had been the reasoning all along.

I am less interested in criticizing these cases than in understanding what kind of thing such a rationale is. Is there an established concept for reasons or justifications that do real work — they persuade, justify, and shape later decisions — while being no particular agent's reasons? The closest notions I can think of are post hoc rationalization, group or corporate intention, and the loose way people talk about markets or institutions as if they knew things; but none of those fits the cases where the rationale floats free of any mind without anyone having lied. A bare label will not satisfy me; I want the concept carved at the joints. Please answer as a professional philosopher whose work deals with reasons and collective agency. Take the time to reason through the alternatives. Begin by brainstorming three or four candidate concepts I might be reaching for, explain how each fits and where each falls short, and then give your considered verdict: does this phenomenon have an established name, and if not, how should it be defined, named, and marked off from the neighboring ideas?
