Hey, I'm writing up a one-page "these are my epistemological committments" thing for a reading group and I'd like a consistency check before I show it to anyone. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Knowledge is closed under known entailment: if I know P, and I know that P entails Q, then I can come to know Q just by putting those two together.

(2) If I genuinely know P, I can rationally shrug off evidence that seems to go against P: since P is true, any evidence against P has to be misleading in some way, and I know that, so I'm not being dogmatic — I'm just respecting what I already know.

(3) It can happen that I know P at one time and then, after taking in new evidence, I rationally come to not know P anymore (or at least rationally suspend judgment or lower my confidence a lot).

(4) New evidence against P always counts for something against P, in the sense that it gives me a reason, maybe outweighed and maybe not, to lower my confidence.

(5) Knowledge is factive: if I know P, then P is true.

(6) None of this depends on the order in which the evidence arrives or on how confident I was at the start; these are meant to be fully general principles.

I have the nagging sense that something in here is the source of that paradox everyone used to talk about, but I honestly can't see which pair is doing the damage myself. Could you work through these for me and say which ones are in genuine tension and how severe each tension is? I'd especially like you to distinguish the pairs that only look inconsistent from the ones where something actually has to give, and for the genuine tensions, tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost. Please rank them rather than giving me a flat list. And because I'll be distributing this to people who know the topic, please mark which parts are pretty much settled among epistemologists and which are live, contested stuff — I don't want to write as if one side of a live debate is the consensus, but I also don't want to hedge about things nobody actually disagrees on.
