A product reviewer at my magazine keeps insisting that a high eBay feedback score or Stack Overflow reputation score measures trustworthiness. I don't see why its any more than a running tally of how many strangers clicked a button, but I worry I'm being to cynical. So treat the strong version as testable: the number tracks something real about the person — like whether they'd act decently when no one's watching — rather than just visibility, habit, or knowing how to farm the system. Design the best test you can, and tell me exactly what result would count as evidence the score carries genuine information about trustworthiness and what would count as evidence it's mostly a popularity counter. Then do the part people skip: say what even a clean positive result would leave unsettled — whether trustworthiness is one stable trait, whether it transfers to other settings. And tell me what would change your mind: which finding would flip your verdict, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail.
