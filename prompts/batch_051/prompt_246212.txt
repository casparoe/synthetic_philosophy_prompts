I sit on the collections and conservation committee of a mid-sized art museum, and I am also a practicing sculptor, so the case before us next month touches my own sense of what makes a sculpture the same work. A ceramic sculpture in our permanent collection, made in the 1970s, was badly fired and is now actively crumbling. The curator proposes to grind down the original, mix the resulting dust with new clay and a stabilizing binder, and refire the work from a 3D scan of its current form, then exhibit the result as the original sculpture, conserved. The artist's estate objects that destroying the original matter destroys the work: what comes out of the kiln would be a replica, not the artist's sculpture. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before next month's meeting: recommend whether to approve the conservation, reject it, or approve it with conditions; state the bottom line plainly enough for a vote; and engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
