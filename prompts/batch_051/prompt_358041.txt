I've been trying to become more intellectually self-sufficient, and I've settled on a rule: in any disagreement, I don't let myself lean on a claim unless I can reconstruct at least the rough argument for it from premises I've actually checked myself. Last week a friend quoted some economist about rent control, and I started to do the same thing back—cite smart people who agreed with me—and I stopped myself, because i couldn't have rebuilt their models if you'd asked me to. He laughed and said I'm trying to run a person-sized autarky, and that it's as doomed for an individual mind as it is for a national economy: nobody has time to produce all their own epistemic goods, so the rational move is to borrow most of them and only check a few. I see the force of that, but I also know the borrowed-argument route is how I've ended up confidently defending things I didn't actually understand. Is my rule defensible, or is it a bias against testimony dressed up as an ideal? If there's a principled version of it, where should the line be between rebuilding arguments myself and wisely deferring—and how would I tell, from the inside, that I've crossed it?
