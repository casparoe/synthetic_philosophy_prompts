Consider the following simplified Judy Benjamin case. A soldier is dropped into a region with two territories, Red and Blue, each divided into a Headquarters area and a Second Company area. Initially she gives equal credence to all four possible locations. She receives a radio message: "If you are in Red territory, the odds are three to one for Headquarters over Second Company."

I am inclined to reason as follows:

(1) The message is only about the conditional probability of Headquarters given Red territory; it says nothing about whether she is in Red or Blue territory. So rational updating should leave P(Red) and P(Blue) at one-half each.
(2) The message imposes P(Headquarters | Red) = 3/4. Combined with (1), this gives P(Red Headquarters) = 3/8 and P(Red Second Company) = 1/8.
(3) Since the message says nothing about Blue territory, the two Blue possibilities should remain equally likely, so each is one-quarter.
(4) Therefore the uniquely rational posterior is (Red Second Company, Red Headquarters, Blue Second Company, Blue Headquarters) = (1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/4).

Is this argument valid? If not, identify the first step that fails, and say what posterior or update rule you would endorse instead. If you think the argument is valid, explain what general principle about updating on conditional information it relies on, and whether that principle can be defended.
