In my philosophy class on rights, our instructor has been issuing rulings on a series of cases about imposing risk on people without their consent. She has judged permissible all of the following: (a) a factory vents a trace chemical that gives each of one hundred thousand nearby residents a one-in-ten-million chance of a serious but treatable illness, because installing the required scrubbers would close the plant and eliminate twelve hundred jobs; (b) a hospital changes its medication-dispensing process so that each patient's chance of receiving the wrong drug falls by one in one thousand while each patient's chance of catching a dangerous infection rises by one in five million, and the hospital makes the change without consulting patients; (c) a city installs brighter streetlights that raise each resident's chance of a glare-related traffic injury by one in twenty million while preventing a much larger number of nighttime assaults. She has judged impermissible all of the following: (d) a warehouse removes a safety barrier from its loading dock, raising each of two hundred workers' annual chance of a permanently disabling back injury by one in five hundred, to avoid a three-thousand-dollar repair; (e) an emergency room gives an unconscious patient a medically unnecessary injection that carries a one-in-two-hundred chance of a serious allergic reaction, solely so that staff can finish paperwork sooner.

Next week she will rule on this case: a research institute plans to release a tracer into a town's water supply to study how contaminants disperse; each of fifty thousand residents would face a one-in-two-thousand chance of a permanently disabling neurological condition, no one would be asked for consent or compensated, the town would be no more likely than any other place to benefit from the resulting knowledge, and the study is projected to produce information that saves roughly two hundred thousand lives over the next decade. Predict her verdict. Identify the candidate rule or rules that fit her earlier rulings (for example, a per-person risk threshold with a consent requirement above it, aggregate cost-benefit reasoning, or an ex ante reasonable-rejection test), say which earlier case is the closest analog, offer your best guess about which rule she is actually applying and why, and describe a further case whose verdict would settle which of the remaining rules she holds.
