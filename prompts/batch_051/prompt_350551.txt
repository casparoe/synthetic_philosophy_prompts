Homework question:

Some researchers describe the following paradigm. A corvid has separately learned several component behaviors in earlier tasks: picking up sticks, probing and poking at boxes with sticks, and pulling food closer with a long stick. In a new task, the bird sees food that can be reached only with a long stick, while the long stick itself is inside a box that can be opened only by poking a short stick through a hole; the short stick is too short to reach the food. On the bird's first exposure to this arrangement, it pokes the short stick into the box, retrieves the long stick, and then uses the long stick to pull in the food. The researchers claim this supports attributing means-end reasoning or planning to the bird. State what Morgan's canon requires in a case like this, and assess whether the first-trial success supports an attribution of means-end reasoning rather than an explanation in terms of associative learning or other lower capacities. Be explicit about what would count as discriminating evidence.

Student answer:

Morgan's canon says that a behavior should not be interpreted as the outcome of a higher psychological faculty if it can be interpreted as the outcome of one lower in the psychological scale. In this case, each component in the sequence is already present in the bird's repertoire from earlier tasks, so associative chaining can interpret the whole sequence: each action was previously reinforced in similar stimulus conditions, and the new arrangement simply releases those components in order. Because a lower faculty can account for the behavior, the canon requires us to reject the planning interpretation. First-trial success is not decisive, since it can be produced by generalization from previously learned components. To count as planning, the animal would need to show that it represents the food as a goal while selecting among the tools, for example through verbal report or some comparable demonstration; until then, parsimony favors the noncognitive account, since that account posits fewer unobservable mental entities.
