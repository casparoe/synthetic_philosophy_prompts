I'm a distributed systems engineer, so my brain is basically formatted in terms of processes, nodes, local clocks, message passing, leases and failover. A philosopher friend has been trying to convince me there's something irreducibly special about words like 'I', 'here' and 'now', and that a language without pronouns or indexicals would be missing something, not just be a bit clunky. The strong version of the claim is that you can't swap 'I' for a name or definite description without changing the thought itself: someone can belief 'John is in danger' and just sit there, but the moment they think 'I am in danger' they run, even though the proposition looks the same. The other side says indexicals are basically shorthand or hidden quantifiers, and in principal a logically perfect language could get rid of them and still express whatever matters.

I'd like you to restate both positions in the vocabulary I actually use. Treat an utterance as a process running on a node, with its own process ID, local state and local clock; treat 'I' as something like self-reference or a this-pointer, 'here' and 'now' as the node's address and a timestamp, 'you' as the counterparty in a message exchange, and so on. Try to make the translation faithful rather than convenient: don't just assume that a global, position-independent description is always available and equivalent to the local one. Where the philosophers' point doesn't fit my toolkit, say so plainly.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the argument map cleanly onto things like self-locating information, or a node needing to know its own identity to route or to decide whether a write is quorum-safe? Which parts did you have to fudge? Which parts have no counterpart in distributed systems at all? And finally: is the essential-indexical business just a roundabout way of noticing that a replica's local view affects what it can safely do, or is there a genuine leftover that my vocabulary can't express — and if there is one, is that because the philosophy is unclear or because my toolkit is limited?
