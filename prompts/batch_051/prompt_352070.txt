Take Mary's room, but give Mary a computational budget instead of letting her be an idealized knower. Standard setup: Mary knows every physical fact about color vision before she's ever seen red, and the question is whether she learns something when she finally does. Now suppose the physical facts really do entail facts about what color experiences are like — that's all in there — but getting from the full physical description to a claim about the subjective character of the experience is computationally intractable. Concretely, say working out what a given pattern of neural activity is like from the inside is NP-hard, or even PSPACE-hard, so with any feasible amount of time and memory she cant finish the derivation; the shortest proof exists but is astronomically long. Run the knowledge argument with this bounded Mary. Does the anti-physicalist get a stronger version of the argument, or does the physicalist now say her surprise is just a complexity limitation and nothing about physicalism follows? Does it matter that the original thought experiment never specified her computational powers? And at what point, if any, does 'entailed by the physical facts' come apart from 'Mary can actually get there from inside the room'?
