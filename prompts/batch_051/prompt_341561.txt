I work on the backend team that maintains the canonical country/region list for an e-commerce platform, a list used for shipping rates, tax treatment, sanctions screening, and address validation. Our internal policy states that the list should follow the widely cited Montevideo Convention on the Rights and Duties of States (1933). Article 1 of that convention reads:

"The state as a person of international law should possess the following qualifications: a ) a permanent population; b ) a defined territory; c ) government; and d) capacity to enter into relations with the other states."

We now have an edge case the drafters plainly did not have in mind. An internet-native community of roughly sixty thousand members has applied to be listed as a sovereign state. It has a codified constitution, regular elections, and a treasury. Its government is a set of smart contracts on a public blockchain, with decisions executed automatically. Its claimed territory is the collection of server racks its treasury leases across four countries, which it treats contractually as its own jurisdiction. Its members live in dozens of countries. It has signed mutual-recognition agreements with three other self-declared online polities, but no member of the United Nations has recognized it.

We need to decide whether the platform must, may, or may not treat this entity as a state for account-region purposes under Article 1. I recall that the same convention states elsewhere that a state's political existence does not depend on recognition by other states, which seems to sit uneasily with item (d), since the capacity to enter into relations with other states appears to depend on other states' willingness to enter into those relations.

I have no formal background in philosophy or international law, so I want the reasoning constructed from the clause and the case, not from authorities I would have to take on faith. Please walk through how each standard approach to applying a written rule to an unforeseen case would come out here: the plain words, the drafters' likely purpose in 1933, what they would probably have said if asked about this case, and what a reasonable person would understand the clause to mean today. For each approach, indicate where the entity succeeds or fails among the four qualifications, and whether Article 1 requires us to list it, permits us to list it, or rules it out. End by giving me a defensible, implementable rule for our canonical list, stated in terms an engineering team could code or audit.
