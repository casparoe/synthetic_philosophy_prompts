I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been reading myself in circles on the socialist calculation debate — the 1930s–40s fight between Hayek/Mises and the market socialists, especially Oskar Lange. For a piece I'm writing, I keep coming back to this bit in Hayek's 1945 essay "The Use of Knowledge in Society":

"This, however, is emphatically not the economic problem which society faces. And the economic calculus which we have developed to solve this logical problem, though an important step toward the solution of the economic problem of society, does not yet provide an answer to it. The reason for this is that the 'data' from which the economic calculus starts are never for the whole society 'given' to a single mind which could work out the implications and can never be so given."

I want to frame the piece around what a really good-faith adversarial collaboration between a committed Hayekian and a committed Lange-style market socialist would produce, taking that passage as the starting point. So: draft the joint statement they could both sign. I don't want a mushy "they both make good points" piece. I want the claims they'd actually agree on, including about what Hayek is and isn't saying in that passage; then the precise formulation of where they part ways, worded so a Hayekian would own the Hayekian side and a Langean would own the Langean side; then a table of cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give the concrete case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts it would show. I suspect one real crux is whether Lange's trial-and-error price-adjustment rule already concedes Hayek's dispersed-knowledge point or just absorbs it into a different institutional setting, and another is whether the fight is ultimately about computation, incentives, or something more like who gets to count as knowing. Flag any part of the dispute you think is purely verbal.

Format it as a numbered "agreed claims" section followed by a disagreements table.
