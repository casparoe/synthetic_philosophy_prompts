I am drafting a paper on human enhancement, and the therapy/enhancement distinction is doing argumentative work that I have not yet earned. I want a working definition of 'enhancement'—or of the therapy/enhancement distinction as a whole, if you think the line is best drawn jointly—that classifies the following cases correctly. I am confident that (1) is a therapy and that (2) and (10) are enhancements; I have no settled view about (3) through (9), and I want a defensible definition to decide them rather than intuitions I do not have.

1. A course of antibiotics for bacterial meningitis.
2. Anabolic steroids taken by a healthy bodybuilder to gain muscle mass beyond what training could produce.
3. A measles vaccination given to a healthy child.
4. A stimulant prescribed to a child diagnosed with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder.
5. The same stimulant, taken without a prescription by a graduate student after four hours of sleep, to concentrate on an exam.
6. A cochlear implant fitted in a child born without hearing.
7. Human growth hormone given to a child with no growth hormone deficiency whose predicted adult height is at the first percentile.
8. A beta-blocker taken by a professional violinist with no anxiety disorder to keep her hands from trembling during auditions.
9. An SSRI taken by a person who is persistently unhappy but falls below the clinical threshold for depression.
10. A hypothetical intervention that changes a person with 20/20 vision to 20/10 vision.

Propose the definition, run it through all ten cases, and say where it delivers the wrong answer, no answer, or a verdict I should find surprising. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdicts. If you conclude that no single definition captures the distinction, give me the best candidate, identify which cases it forces me to reclassify, and—if you think the distinction is scalar rather than binary—say what the degrees are degrees of.
