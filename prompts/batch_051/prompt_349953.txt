I have been rereading Eddington’s The Nature of the Physical World, and a line of thought in his discussion of the running-down of the universe keeps returning to me while I work on causal modeling. As I understand him, Eddington’s point is that the microscopic laws are reversible and carry no direction at the particle level. The direction we call time’s arrow appears only when we aggregate configurations into macrostates and compare their probabilities. The forward direction is the direction in which the mixed, less organized, more numerous states lie; the reverse direction would be equally lawful if that happened to be the slope of the distribution. The arrow is in the statistical gradient, not in the laws.

Here is the parallel I cannot decide about. In causal Bayes nets, the joint probability distribution over a set of variables carries no edge direction by itself. One can factor the same joint distribution as P(X)P(Y|X) or as P(Y)P(X|Y). The directed arrow from X to Y is licensed only when one introduces interventions: setting X independently changes Y, but setting Y does not change X in the same way. That intervention asymmetry is supplied by an extra modeling layer, not discovered in the bare distribution. This looks structurally similar to Eddington’s claim: the base object, whether the reversible dynamics or the undirected joint distribution, is direction-free, and the arrow appears only after adding a layer that is itself defined in temporally asymmetric terms, whether the entropy gradient across macrostates or independent exogenous interventions. Both look like cases where time is imported by the descriptive apparatus rather than found in the ground-level facts.

Is this a real structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it is real, does the causal-modeling literature on whether interventions already presuppose a time order transfer to the older debate about whether the thermodynamic arrow is objective or a projection of our coarse-graining? Or does the analogy break because interventions are agent-relative and goal-directed while Eddington’s entropy gradient is supposed to be independent of any observer?
