In Book II, Chapter 30 of Augustine’s Harmony of the Gospels, he addresses the apparent conflict between Mark, who reports Jesus telling the disciples to take nothing for their journey except a staff, and Matthew and Luke, who report Jesus telling them not to take a staff. Augustine writes:

“Now this question admits of being solved on the principle of understanding that the staff which, according to Mark, was to be taken, bears one sense, and that the staff which, according to Matthew and Luke, was not to be taken with them, is to be interpreted in a different sense; just in the same way as we find the term temptation used in one meaning, when it is said, God tempts no man, and in a different meaning where it is said, The Lord your God tempts [proves] you, to know whether ye love Him.”

My question is about the method on display here. Augustine dissolves the apparent contradiction by assigning the word “staff” two different senses, just as he does with “temptation.” But if this kind of move is available whenever the evangelists appear to conflict, how does the doctrine of inerrancy remain a claim about the text rather than a policy of never admitting defeat? What is supposed to distinguish a genuine case of polysemy from an ad hoc reinterpretation offered only to preserve consistency, and does Augustine himself give any criterion for telling them apart, or is his harmonization here open to the charge of being unfalsifiable?
