I am cataloguing families of response to the sorites paradox. My working list contains four: epistemicism, which keeps classical logic and bivalence and says there is a sharp but unknowable cutoff; supervaluationism, which treats truth as truth on every admissible sharpening of the vague predicate and allows borderline sentences to lack a determinate truth value; degree-theoretic or many-valued approaches, which assign intermediate truth values to borderline claims; and contextualism, which says the extension of the vague predicate shifts with conversational context. I want a fifth family that is not on this list and is not a recombination or relabeling of any of them. It should locate the flaw in the sorites reasoning at a different point from those four, and it should explain why the inductive premise feels compelling without simply conceding that it is true. Give me the strongest such account you can construct, state precisely where it intervenes in the chain of conditionals, and then say honestly whether it solves the paradox or only relocates it.
