So me and my friend where arguing for like an hour last night about my aunt, who told everyone at a family thing that she’s “on the committee” for the neighborhood flood relief fund. Turns out she misspoke—she’s on the waiting list for the committee, but she honestly thought she’d been appointed. My friend kept saying that by Augustine’s definition she didn’t lie, because lying requires a kind of duplicity, not just saying something false. I think that’s way too lenient; saying a false thing to someone who relies on it is lying, period. Then we got into the weeds of Augustine’s actual text, On Lying. He has this passage where he says:

“Therefore he who utters a false thing for a true, which however he opines to be true, may be called erring and rash: but he is not rightly said to lie; because he has not a double heart when he utters it, neither does he wish to deceive, but is deceived. But the fault of him who lies, is, the desire of deceiving in the uttering of his mind.”

I want you to build me a thought experiment that would settle the structural issue between us, not the aunt case itself. Construct a scenario to my specs:

- Realistic: a workplace or a family, no weird philosophical machines, nobody religious.
- One paragraph.
- It must contain two people who each say the same false sentence, where according to Augustine’s definition exactly one of them lies and the other merely says something false.
- The hinge must be the difference between belief and opinion that Augustine draws in the passage: one person actually believes the false thing, the other only opines it but decides to present it as something he knows. Keep it close to the passage’s terms, especially “double heart” and “desire to deceive.”
- Don’t let the liar be a cartoon villain; make it a normal, socially plausible moment.

Then walk through the case: for each person, say what is in his mind when he speaks, and why Augustine’s test counts one as lying and the other as erring/rash. Finally tell me which feature of the case is doing the work—what if that feature were removed, would it collapse? And how would my friend, who thinks my aunt didn’t lie, most likely attack the case you built?
