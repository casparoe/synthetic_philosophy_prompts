After retiring from a career in biology, I have been reading Aristotle’s De Anima slowly, and I keep returning to a passage early in Book III, Part 2, where Aristotle considers whether the awareness that we are seeing or hearing belongs to sight itself or to some other sense. In the translation available at the MIT Classics Archive, the passage runs:

“Since it is through sense that we are aware that we are seeing or hearing, it must be either by sight that we are aware of seeing, or by some sense other than sight. But the sense that gives us this new sensation must perceive both sight and its object, viz. colour: so that either (1) there will be two senses both percipient of the same sensible object, or (2) the sense must be percipient of itself. Further, even if the sense which perceives sight were different from sight, we must either fall into an infinite regress, or we must somewhere assume a sense which is aware of itself. If so, we ought to do this in the first case.”

I find this passage puzzling because later in Book III, Part 3, Aristotle introduces phantasia and says that it is neither perception nor thought, yet it seems to have a similar kind of reflexive or self-involving character. I would like you to write a short dialogue between two equally competent interpreters of Aristotle on phantasia and its role in thought. One holds that phantasia is essentially a sensory afterimage-like capacity, the mechanism by which perceptions persist and become available for memory, dreaming, and action; the other holds that phantasia is best understood as a cognitive intermediary, a kind of quasi-perceptual representation that makes abstract thought possible by presenting images to the intellect. The dialogue should start from the quoted passage: one character might argue that the self-aware sense Aristotle finds necessary in Book III, Part 2 is really a first step toward phantasia, while the other might reply that this reading conflates phantasia with perception. The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth, with each character responding to the other’s actual claims rather than talking past one another, and neither side should be a strawman. Please write the dialogue in British English.
