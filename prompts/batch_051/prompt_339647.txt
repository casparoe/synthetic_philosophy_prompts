I'm writing a paper for a bioethics seminar on the relationship between reproductive technology and the value of disability, and I keep finding that the actual practice of embryo selection looks philosophically strange in a way I want someone to reconstruct for me. In many U.S. clinics, a couple using in vitro fertilization can have embryos screened before implantation, and providers will readily help them avoid implanting an embryo with genes for conditions like Tay-Sachs, cystic fibrosis, or Huntington's, on the ground that the resulting child would otherwise have a life of serious suffering. Those same providers generally will not help a couple select for non-disease traits such as height, eye color, or musical ability, and sex selection is tightly limited unless it is tied to a sex-linked disorder. There is also an awkward gap between this clinic-side sorting and the broader culture's commitments: outside the clinic, the same legal culture treats disability-based discrimination as wrongful and professes to value disabled lives, yet support services for children with the very conditions screening aims to prevent are often inadequate, and parents who decline screening and raise a disabled child are frequently left with far less help than the screening regime seems to assume they need.

What theory of value and of parental obligation does this patchwork presuppose? Does it presuppose that the conditions being screened against are intrinsically bad for a life to include, or merely that parents may legitimately try to avoid expected suffering? Does it presuppose a stable moral line between preventing disease and shaping a child's capacities, and can that line hold once we notice that many disease traits are compatible with lives their bearers value? Is the practice even compatible with a child's-best-interest standard, given that the alternative child would never exist at all? And how would the practice have to change if it instead adopted the principle that parents ought to select the embryo, among those they could implant, that is expected to have the best life? Please reconstruct the implicit theory that makes the best sense of the practice, separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and flag any point where rival theories would license the same behavior and the practice is genuinely underdetermined. End with a one-sentence summary of the implicit theory you attribute to the practice, beginning 'Summary:'.
