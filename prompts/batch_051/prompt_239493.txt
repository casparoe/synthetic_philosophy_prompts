Before I accept that appeals to human dignity can settle moral and political disputes, or that dignity is a redundant concept doing no work beyond autonomy or respect for persons, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of dignity would have to do. Write the desiderata for a theory of human dignity: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results it must be consistent with, giving the motivating case or consideration behind each requirement. Treat the aim of the exercise as producing the test that candidate theories would have to pass, rather than defending one of those theories up front. Assume the theory is meant to be adequate for practical reasoning about torture, degrading prison conditions, humiliation, the commodification of bodies (organ sale, sex work), assisted dying, and whether dignity grounds human rights or is derivable from something more basic.

Address at least the following candidate requirements, though you may reject any of them if you can show it is a prejudice of one camp rather than a genuine constraint, and you may add others:

- distinguishing dignity from autonomy, welfare, honour, and social status;
- distinguishing having dignity from having one's dignity violated, and both from the felt experience of being degraded;
- accounting for equal dignity across beings with radically different capacities, such as an infant, a person with advanced dementia, and a person in a permanent vegetative state;
- saying whether dignity is a status, a threshold value, or a scalar magnitude, and whether it can be forfeited through one's own acts;
- saying whether dignity operates as a constraint on how a person may be treated or as a value to be promoted, and not allowing these two roles to be conflated;
- handling degradation the victim never learns of, such as mockery of the dead or ridiculing an anaesthetised patient, and saying whether this is best explained by dignity or by something else;
- handling self-directed degradation, including acts the agent avows, such as selling an organ under economic pressure or consenting to public humiliation, and whether autonomous choice silences a dignity objection;
- specifying the relation between dignity and rights: whether dignity grounds rights, summarises them, or is in principle separable from them, and whether there can be a dignity violation where no right is violated.

For each requirement you keep, give the case or consideration that forces it and say whether it is negotiable. Then briefly score the main contenders against the list: at least a Kantian-style view on which dignity is absolute, equal, and non-forfeitable, and a deflationary view on which dignity is redundant once autonomy and respect for persons are on the table. Identify which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates. If some subset of your requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so and state which one must give. Write in British English throughout, and near the end state how confident you are in the list overall and which single requirement or verdict you are least sure of.
