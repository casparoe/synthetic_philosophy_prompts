I cover science and ideas, and for a while now i've been in and out of the metaphysics-of-time rabbit hole, mostly because the presentists and the eternalists seem to be having the same fight they've been having for a century without either side losing ground. Presentism is the view that only the present exists: the past is gone, the future isn't here yet, and a true sentence like "there were dinosaurs" has to be made true by something that exists now, which is harder than it sounds. Eternalism is the view that all times are equally real—dinosaurs, the heat death of the universe, next Tuesday—and that "now" just marks the time you're at, the way "here" marks the place you're at. Both sides more or less agree on the physics (special relativity complicates things but doesn't hand the win to either camp) and on the raw feel of time passing, and yet nobody moves.

The passage I keep coming back to is McTaggart's setup in "The Unreality of Time" (Mind, 1908):

"Positions in time, as time appears to us prima facie, are distinguished in two ways. Each position is Earlier than some, and Later than some, of the other positions. And each position is either Past, Present, or Future. The distinctions of the former class are permanent, while those of the latter are not. If M is ever earlier than N, it is always earlier. But an event, which is now present, was future and will be past.

Since distinctions of the first class are permanent, they might be held to be more objective, and to be more essential to the nature of time. I believe, however, that this would be a mistake, and that the distinction of past, present and future is as essential to time as the distinction of earlier and later, while in a certain sense, as we shall see, it may be regarded as more fundamental than the distinction of earlier and later."

McTaggart himself took the first option: he thought the A-series—the shifting past, present, future ordering—was essential to time and also contradictory, so time was unreal. Presentists and eternalists look to me like two different ways of refusing that conclusion: the presentist keeps the moving now as the whole of reality, the eternalist says the permanent earlier/later order (his B-series) is the whole of reality and the moving now is just a perspective. My hunch, offered as a hunch, is that the standoff is really about wether the felt character of the passing present counts as evidence, or about what the word "exists" is supposed to be doing in a theory, more than about any question of fact the two camps actually disagree on. What I want is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a scoreboard for the two views: is this substantive, verbal, a clash of starting intuitions neither side can argue for, or an ill-posed question in disguise? And given your diagnosis, what would count as actual progress—a distinction, a theorem, a new result, a change of question—or is the treadmill just the nature of the beast?
