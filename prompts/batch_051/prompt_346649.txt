Take the standard Nomic self-amendment puzzle, but change one thing: let the proposed amendment be explicitly retroactive. In a simplified Nomic, Rule R says any mutable rule may be changed by majority vote, except Rule R itself, which needs a unanimous vote. A player proposes P, which says: (1) strike the unanimity exception, so R itself becomes changeable by majority; and (2) P takes effect retroactively to the moment the vote on P began. P gets seven yes votes out of ten, so it has a majority but not unanimity. Under the old R it seems to fail; under the new R it would pass, and its retroactivity clause appears to reach back and validate the very vote that enacted it. Work out wether P was validly adopted and which rule governed the vote. In particular: does the usual "proposals are judged under the rules in force when voted on" answer survive this variant, or is there a bootstrapping problem with no non-arbitrary resolution? Take a definite position and defend it. Max 700 words.
