Write the document that a good-faith adversarial collaboration would produce between the following two opposed positions in the theory of instrumental rationality.

The first position, call it the exploitability-as-irrationality view, holds that vulnerability to a money pump is sufficient to show that an agent's pattern of preferences and choices across time is rationally defective, even when each local choice follows from the preferences the agent has at the time. On this view, diachronic coherence is a genuine rational norm: a sequence of choices can reveal that the agent's underlying evaluations fail to constitute a single consistent outlook, and the money pump is a vivid way of exposing that inconsistency.

The second position, call it the local-rationality view, holds that money-pump arguments do not by themselves establish irrationality. On this view, rationality attaches first to the agent's choices at each time given the preferences the agent has then, and an agent can rationally take each leg of a pump while jointly losing money. Diachronic coherence has no independent normative force unless the agent already has reasons to care about their future selves; otherwise the pump merely assembles a set of individually rational acts whose aggregate consequence is unfortunate.

Produce the joint statement these two parties could both sign after a year of arguing past each other. Use this structure:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. The claims should be substantive and non-empty, and they should cover what actually happens in canonical pumps before either side reaches for a general principle. Address at least the following fine-grained points explicitly: whether a strict preference of A over B must carry a willingness to pay at least some small positive amount to exchange B for A; whether merely possible exploitation has the same import as actual exploitation; and whether an agent who foresees the whole sequence and could adopt a resolute intention or precommitment is best treated as avoiding the pump or as changing the norm that would otherwise be violated.

2. A disagreements table. Give the neutral wording of each residual disagreement, then the statement of each side's position phrased so that its own advocates would accept the wording.

3. A cruxes section with three to five cruxes. Each crux should be a sub-question such that settling it would force at least one side to revise; for each, specify a concrete case, argument, or experimental test that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts or claims about that case.

Throughout, separate disagreements that are genuine from ones that are merely about the semantics of "preference," "choice," or "irrational." Do not adjudicate; the document should model constructive precision about where the two sides still differ rather than trying to resolve the dispute.
