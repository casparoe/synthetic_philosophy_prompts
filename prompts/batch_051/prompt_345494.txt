In my phil of law class we've been playing a simplified Nomic variant all semester, and last week I think we stumbled into a real self-amendment paradox. Our rules include these three, quoted word for word:

Rule 4: "A player may not trade any game item unless two other members approve the trade before it happens."
Rule 7: "Any rule may be amended at a meeting by a two-thirds vote of the members present."
Rule 8: "An adopted amendment takes effect at the close of the meeting at which it was adopted."

At last week's meeting someone moved to amend Rule 8 to read: "An adopted amendment takes effect immediately upon the vote that adopts it." It passed by exactly two-thirds. The moment the chair announced the result, the same person moved to repeal Rule 4, and that also passed. Then a bunch of us traded without advance approval. Now half the class says the Rule 4 repeal couldn't have taken effect immediately, because at the time of the second vote Rule 8 still said amendments take effect at close—the Rule 8 amendment can't pull itself into force by its own bootstraps. The other half says that once the Rule 8 amendment was adopted, it made itself immediate, so the Rule 4 repeal was valid on the spot and the trades stand. Our prof claims the drafters never foresaw an amendment to the timing rule itself.

I dont need a recap of Nomic. I need the best argument for what the actual state of the rules was (a) right after the first vote, and (b) right after the second vote. Walk through the exact words, the purpose of Rule 8, and what the original drafters would most likely have said if you asked them about this exact case, then tell me which interpretive approach should decide it and why.
