Take the original position behind the veil of ignorance. Rawls's parties don't know who they'll end up as, and Rawls thinks that kind of one-shot, high-stakes ignorance pushes them toward maximin-type reasoning (aim at the society whose worst-off position is best), while Harsanyi thinks it pushes them toward assigning equal probability to being each person and maximizing expected utility, which lands at average utilitarianism. Now change one thing: the parties are told behind the veil that the position assignment won't be one-shot. Every ten years, everyone's social position (income, job, status, authority) is randomly reassigned, and people keep their memories across the reassignments, so over a normal lifespan each person lives through several different positions rather than just one. Does Rawls's argument for maximin survive this change, or does Harsanyi's expected-utility route now just go through? Does it matter that the reassignments are finite rather than continuous, and does the variant quietly smuggle in a single continuing self who can average utility across positions—which is exactly the kind of thing Rawls's separateness-of-persons point is meant to rule out?
