Since God's dignity is infinite, wronging God is infinitely serious, and infinite punishment fits.

I want an audit of how far this analogy actualy carries. Spell out the mapping first (what corresponds to the shove, the victim's status, the harm, the sentence, the legal system), then pick apart the disanalogies, separating the ones that matter for the conclusion from ones that don't. I'm especially suspicious about two moves: treating dignity as a scalar you can multiply by a harm to get a deserved sentence, when maybe it changes the kind of wrong rather than just its magnitude; and treating eternity as just a very, very long finite sentence, when it might be a different kind of quantity altogether. Does the conclusion—eternal torment is proportionate—survive once the disanalogies are on the table? If the president-shove analogy fails, is there a better analogy that does the same work for the defender, or does the case for infinite punishment collapse? Please answer in American English.
