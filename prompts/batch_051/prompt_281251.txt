I am an undergraduate currently taking a philosophy of mind module, and I have been finding eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires easier to state than to test. I would like you to construct a thought experiment to a precise specification, so that I can see whether the view can be made to earn its keep rather than merely issue a promissory note about future cognitive science.

Construct a single realistic scenario in which an eliminative materialist about beliefs and desires does better than a folk-psychological explanation of the very same action: better in prediction or in the interventions it recommends, not merely in rhetoric. The scenario must satisfy all of the following constraints.

1. The agent is an ordinary adult with no relevant pathology, and the action is mundane rather than contrived (a consumer choice, a routine professional judgement, or something similar).
2. The action is one that folk psychology would naturally explain by attributing a specific belief–desire pair: the belief and the desire should be individually identifiable and jointly sufficient in the usual folk-psychological way.
3. Afterwards, the agent is able to give a sincere, articulate, and accurate-sounding report of exactly that belief–desire pair. The eliminativist must not handle this report simply by calling it confabulation; it has to be treated as further behaviour to be explained and predicted.
4. The eliminativist's alternative explanation is a stipulated but internally coherent cognitive-scientific model of the kind eliminative materialists gesture towards, for example a trained dynamical or vector-based model that predicts choices and verbal reports without positing any states with propositional content. The model may be stipulated, but it must be specified enough that its predictions diverge from the folk-psychological account at at least one point, and the divergence must matter: it should lead to a different expectation or a different intervention.

After presenting the scenario, complete the following checks. State what the folk-psychological explanation says and why it seems appropriate to the case. State what the eliminative materialist says, and identify the precise point at which the two accounts issue different expectations or recommend different interventions, explaining why the eliminativist's expectations are to be preferred in the case as stipulated. Then say which single feature of the case is doing the work, what would happen to the case if that feature were removed, and how a defender of the view that beliefs and desires are real, causally explanatory states would attack the case. Please write the answer in British English.
