I'm a software engineer on a trust and safety team, and I've hit a conceptual problem I can't code around. Our policies refer to punishing repeat offenders, our logging schema has a punishment_type field, and we treat an account suspension as a more severe response than a post removal without being able to say exactly what separates the two. I'd like a working definition of punishment that handles the following cases:

1. A court sentences someone to two years in prison for fraud.
2. A city issues a parking fine.
3. A parent grounds a teenager for a month.
4. A school suspends a student for three days for cheating.
5. A platform permanently bans a high-follower account for targeted harassment.
6. A subreddit moderator deletes an off-topic post.
7. A recommendation system quietly downranks a user's content so it reaches far fewer people, with no notice to the user.
8. A company fires an engineer after a coordinated online backlash over old tweets.
9. A neighborhood group stops inviting one household to events after a dispute.
10. Thousands of customers boycott a brand after it donates to a political cause.

I'm confident that (1) through (4) are punishment and that (6) is not. I genuinely don't know how to sort (5), (7), (8), (9), and (10). In particular, I need to know whether calling something punishment already says it was deserved, or whether the description can come apart from the moral charge. This matters because I want to know whether our enforcement actions inherit the justifications and constraints that normally attach to punishment.

Please take a definite position rather than laying out the options. Propose a definition, apply it to each case, and flag any case where your definition gives a verdict you would not want to defend. Then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. If you think the word does two distinct jobs, such as an institutional description and a condemnatory judgment, say so explicitly, give me the definitions you need, and tell me which cases move between them.
