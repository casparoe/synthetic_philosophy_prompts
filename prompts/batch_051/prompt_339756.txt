I spent most of my career as an immunologist, working on the mechanisms by which the body learns to distinguish self from non-self, keeps responses against its own tissues suppressed without leaving itself open to infection, and fails in characteristic ways when that balance breaks down. Recently I have been reading political philosophy, and the paradox of tolerance strikes me as the political version of a problem my field has grappled with since the mid-twentieth century. I would like a careful translation, not an analogy drawn in passing, and a candid report of what does not survive the translation.

Take the main positions in the debate over tolerating the intolerant: the claim that unlimited tolerance permits the intolerant to destroy tolerance itself; the further claim that a tolerant society may therefore refuse tolerance to movements that would use it to abolish tolerance, first by argument and, if necessary, by suppression; the liberal qualification that the intolerant are still owed toleration so long as they do not credibly threaten the constitutional order; and the civil-libertarian view that the proper response to abhorrent speech is more speech rather than suppression. Restate each position in the vocabulary of immunology as I know it: central and peripheral tolerance, clonal deletion, anergy, regulatory T cells, immune privilege, self/non-self discrimination and its rivals such as the danger model, commensals and pathogens, molecular mimicry, tumor immune evasion and checkpoint blockade, and the canonical failure modes of autoimmunity, immune deficiency, and cytokine storm. I am fluent in both vocabularies, so please do not spend time explaining the immunology or summarizing the philosophy.

For each position, say what translates cleanly, what translates only under distortion, and what has no expression at all. Then give the residue report. Specifically, I want to know whether the immunology mapping exposes assumptions the political debate has not had to state — for example, that tolerance is an active, costly, ongoing achievement rather than a default posture of non-interference, or that the line between the tolerable and the intolerable is learned, revisable, and error-prone rather than fixed by a principle — or whether the inexpressible residue shows instead that the political concepts, such as rights, legitimacy, reciprocity, and the treatment of persons as ends, are doing work the biological vocabulary cannot reach. Keep the translation honest: do not import the immunology's commitments and present them as discoveries, and do not smooth over the failures. The failures are the part I am most interested in.
