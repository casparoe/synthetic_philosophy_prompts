My colleague runs a small seminar on war guilt, and over the years I've kept a list of her bottom lines on particular wars. She's a historian with a philosopher's taste for principles, not a lawyer. Here's what I have:

- Second World War: Germany bears primary responsibility, full stop — it launched a deliberate war of aggression.
- 2003 Iraq War: the United States bears primary responsibility — it launched a preventive war on an intelligence pretext that fell apart.
- 1982 Falklands War: Argentina bears primary responsibility — it invaded the islands and turned a long sovereignty dispute into an armed conflict.
- 1950 Korean War: North Korea bears primary responsibility — it launched a full-scale invasion across the 38th parallel.

I've allways thought she was applying one clean principle, but now I'm not sure which one, because these cases all point the same way. I want to know what she'd say about the First World War, where the plausible principles come apart. In July 1914 Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia first, after the Sarajevo assassination and a deliberately harsh ultimatum; Russia then mobilized; Germany had already given Austria-Hungary the blank check, then declared war on Russia and France and invaded Belgium. The old Fischer controversy is exactly about this: did Germany deliberately seek a European war, or did the powers slide into one?

So here's what I need. Work out the candidate principles that each fit her four earlier verdicts (I can think of at least three, something like: blame the state that first launched full-scale war; blame the state that turned a local crisis into a general war; blame the state whose long-running aims made the war predictable). Then say what each principle says about 1914, tell me which of her four earlier cases is the closest analog to 1914, and give me your best guess about her verdict and which principle she's really using — including whether she'd land closer to Fischer or to the slide-into-war view. Also flag which of the four earlier verdicts is doing the least work for the induction, and name one further case (not the First World War) that would most cleanly force her to choose between the principles.

This is prep for an arguement with her, so keep it casual and don't give me a bibliography or citations; I need the reasoning, not the reading list.
