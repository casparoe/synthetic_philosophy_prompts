I'm preaching the Good Friday service at a church that's the product of a recent merger between an old mainline congregation and an evangelical church plant. The room is split, and everybody knows it. The evangelical side grew up on penal substitution: on the cross Christ bore the punishment our sins deserved, took the wrath we had coming, propitiation, the whole vocabulary. The mainline side tends to find that picture morally repellent—a father demanding payment in blood—and gravitates toward the cross as a demonstration of God's love that changes us, plus a deep sense of God suffering with the oppressed. There's also a smaller, older group that still thinks in Anselm's terms: the cross as satisfaction of a debt of honor, which they find more dignified then either of the other two. I'm the young associate pastor and I've been given twelve minutes. I don't want to split the difference with poetic fog about 'mystery,' and I don't want three mini-sermons. I want one thread that takes the rivalry itself as the subject: something that lets each group hear its own commitments taken seriously without the others being told, from the pulpit, that they're wrong. The hard part: it has to be an actual Good Friday sermon, not a lecture about atonement theories, and the theology needs to do the structural work—no sentence like 'however you understand it, the important thing is...'. Can you write it?
