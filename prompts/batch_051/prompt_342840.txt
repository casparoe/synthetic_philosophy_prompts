I spent most of my career in a geochemistry lab and only got into philosophy after retiring, so the chances are high this thing I keep noticing already has a clunky German name and I just don't know it.

Here is the pattern I mean. A colleague says, almost as a way of ending a conversation, 'I'll have a look at your draft over the weekend.' You don't hear from him. Or at a conference someone says 'I'll phone you next week about the collaboration', and then never phones; or 'see you at two' turns into quarter past, and the person left waiting is expected to shrug it off. If you complain, people look at you as though you've lost all sense of proportion: it wasn't a signed contract, it was just a friendly remark. And it's true that the speaker can always say 'I only said I'd try' or 'I meant if I had the time.' Yet the person on the other end often did take it as a commitment, rearranged things, and feels a small but real let-down — but a let-down they aren't allowed to voice, because each individual lapse is so slight.

The odd thing is the asymmetry. The same people who brush off their own 'I'll send you the data by Tuesday' are often quietly furious when someone else does it to them. Meanwhile the person who treats every one of these passing remarks as a firm promise — who actually phones when he said he would, and takes offence when others don't — gets seen as rigid and slightly unbearable, not as virtuous. We had a postdoc like that. He was the most reliable person I ever worked with, and somehow he was the one people thought was socially difficult, while the charming collegue who never delivered was still liked even after you'd stopped trusting a word he said.

So I suppose my question is in two parts. First: is there an established term for this middle category of commitments, the things that are clearly more than small talk but less than a formal promise, and maybe also for that asymmetry between what the speaker can get away with and what the hearer is allowed to feel? Second, and more important to me than the name: how do you draw the line? Is a passing 'I'll do it' binding because of the words themselves, because the other person reasonably relied on it, or because of the relationship? And does the fact that nobody can complain about any single instance matter, or does it only become a moral failing when there's a pattern? If there are several nearby concepts, I'd rather you say which ones my description sits between and where it stops matching each of them, than just give me a label.

One small request: British English, please, spelling included.
