Sometimes people say things like “the market wanted interest rates to rise” or “the practice stuck around because it solved a coordination problem,” even when no single participant consciously reasoned that way. I have been trying to think through whether this is just a loose metaphor or whether it points to something real. Please argue for the view that there are genuine free-floating rationales—explanatory reasons that belong to a process, system, or pattern rather than to any individual reasoner—and that they cannot always be reduced without loss to the intentions and reasoning of the people involved. In making the case, be sure to say what would have to be true for such a rationale to be more than an after-the-fact rationalization.
