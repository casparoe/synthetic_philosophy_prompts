I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been pulled into a persistence debate through a work dispute. Our team spent two years replacing every module of an aging backend service, one subsystem per quarter, until no line of the original implementation remained; the new system keeps the old name, database, and API contract. We genuinely couldn't agree on whether the thing we run today is 'the same service' we started with. One side argued that a single, self-same thing persists through each commit and is wholly present at each point in its history. The other side argued that the service just is its version history — a temporally extended sequence of states — and that 'the service' names the whole series rather than something wholly present in any one of them. I later learned that philosophers have names for roughly these positions: endurantism and perdurantism.

I'd like you to act as a professional metaphysician with a working knowledge of the endurantism/perdurantism debate, and to draft the document a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a committed endurantist and a committed perdurantist should produce. Begin by brainstorming in notes: what claims can both sides sign about persistence, change, and the problem of temporary intrinsics (the puzzle of how one and the same thing can be straight on Monday and bent on Tuesday without being both straight and bent full stop); what each position says; and what the dispute is not about. Then write the document. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table whose columns are: the disputed question; the endurantist's formulation, in wording an endurantist would accept as fair; the perdurantist's formulation, in wording a perdurantist would accept as fair; and your judgment whether that particular disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions whose answers would actually move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. Use my software case as a running test case: both camps should say what, on their view, persists when a service is rewritten module-by-module until nothing of the original code remains, and what 'the same service' means under that description.

Please write carefully and avoid strawmanning either side: if a formulation would be rejected by one side as slanted, reword it until it can be signed, or move it to the disagreements table. Focus the document on making the disagreement precise rather than resolving it, and if you conclude that some part of the dispute is verbal, identify that part and say why. Keep the whole thing comprehensible to an engineer without formal philosophical training, but precise enough that a specialist in the debate would recognize both positions as fairly stated.
