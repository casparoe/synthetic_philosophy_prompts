A colleague and I recently spent a long evening arguing about what "meaningful human control" over autonomous weapons actually requires, and we realized we had been talking past each other for months. She defends what I will call the engagement-specific view: human control is meaningful only if a human authorizes each particular attack, or at least each particular target, before it is engaged, because the judgment that lethal force is necessary and proportionate here and now is not the sort of judgment that can be delegated. I defend a systemic view: meaningful human control is a property of the whole human–machine system across its lifecycle — the setting of objectives and rules of engagement, target categories, geographic and temporal bounds of the operation, real-time supervision, and the capacity to interrupt or abort — so a system can remain under meaningful human control even when it selects and engages individual targets faster than any human could sensibly approve. We have agreed to stop arguing and draft an adversarial-collaboration statement instead.

Write the document we should jointly sign. It should include: (a) a numbered list of claims about autonomous weapon systems, human judgment, and control that both of us can accept, without being vacuous; (b) the exact points where we still differ, each stated in wording the relevant side would accept for its own position; and (c) a disagreements table whose rows are the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us — and whose columns record, for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence we both agree bears on it, plus each side's prediction of what it would show. Use at least three concrete cases; good candidates include a loitering munition operating within a human-approved engagement box against a mobile and time-sensitive target, a fully automatic close-in weapon system defending a ship against incoming missiles, and a lethal strike list assembled by human analysts in which the machine chooses the timing and method of engagement. Lead with the document itself, with no preface or commentary.
