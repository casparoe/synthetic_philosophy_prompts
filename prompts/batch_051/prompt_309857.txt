I'm a novelist. My agent just called to say that a debut writer in another country published a book last month with almost the same premise as the novel I've been working on for four years: same central relationship, same mid-book reversal, even a couple of near-identical images I thought were private to me — a woman folding a map into a paper boat while her father watches from a pier, that kind of thing. She definitely couldn't have seen my work. The manuscript has never been online, and the only people who've read it are my partner and my agent, so I don't think it's theft. It just seems to have happened.

What's bothering me isn't really the publishing side, though my agent is worried about that. It's that I grew up believing a novel is a singular act of imagination, that it's yours because you made it. But this has happened to me once before with a short story, and I keep noticing the same pattern everywhere — two people patent the same gizmo, two labs find the same enzyme, two screenwriters pitch the same logline. It makes me wonder whether stories are less like things we make and more like things that are already theere, waiting to be caught, and somebody was going to write this book whether I did or not.

If that's true, then I don't know what to do with the idea of priority. Does it matter that she got there first? Does her book change what my manuscript is, or what my claim to it is? Part of me wants to say no — the book will stand or fall on its sentences, and a coincidence in premise and imagery can't reach as deep as the actual prose. But another part of me feels scooped in a way I can't argue myself out of.

I'm not asking for publishing advice. I want help thinking through what the coincidence actualy shows: is the "ideas are just in the air" thought a real insight or a consolation story, and can I hold onto both that thought and the belief that the work is mine because I made it?
