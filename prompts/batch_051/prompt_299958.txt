i've been turning over a distinction for a while and i want it checked before i lean on it. i call it grade-knowing and pull-knowing. a psychopath can breeze through any test of right and wrong -- they can list the rules, rank how wrong each action is, even predict what a judge or a vicar would say. but the rules don't pull on them. they know the rules the way i know the French word for umbrella, not the way i know my dog is in pain when he whines. my theory is that the whole 'do psychopaths know what they're doing is wrong' argument is two people using 'know' for two different things and then calling each other confused. in the grade sense the psychopath knows better than most of us, because they aren't fogged up by feelings; in the pull sense they know nothing at all. the real argument should be about which sense deserves to be called moral knowledge, but nobody says that because they're too busy disagreeing.

my worry is this. maybe pull-knowing is just caring, and caring isnt knowledge. then i've only renamed motivation, and the psychopath does know right from wrong, full stop. is this distinction already out there under a cleverer name (it feels too tidy not to be)? is it actually coherent enough to do any work? and where does it break -- e.g. does a severely depressed person who's lost the pull but remembers all the rules count as a psychopath on my account, and if that's absurd does it mean the pull is the wrong thing to focus on?
