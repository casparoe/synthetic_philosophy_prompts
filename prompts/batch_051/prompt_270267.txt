I'm doing Hume on miracles next week and I keep failing to get students past "Hume was a skeptic, he didn't like miracles." So I want a poem that makes the argument land. Write a villanelle where the two refrains do the actual philosophical work. One refrain should state the definition-and-evidence move: a miracle is a violation of a law of nature, and a law of nature is established by exceptionless uniform experience, so testimony for a miracle starts out opposed by a full proof from experience. The other refrain should state the condition under which testimony could win: the falsehood of the testimony would have to be a greater miracle—a bigger violation of uniform experience—than the event reported. Structure it so each new stanza brings in a fresh alleged wonder (a healing, a prophecy, a resurrection) and the returning refrains tip the scale back each time. Work in two more of Hume's moves before the end: people's appetite for the marvelous makes them credulous, and competing religions report incompatible miracles, so their testimonies cancel out. The closing quatrain should put the two refrains together so the reader sees why the outcome is to make belief match the evidence and withold assent. It should be accurate enough that a Hume specialist would nod rather than wince, and there should be no name-dropping—the refrains have to carry the argument.
