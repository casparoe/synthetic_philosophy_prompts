I am a novelist at work on a book that deliberately refuses to sort its beings into persons and objects ahead of time. The narrator lives with a colony of bees in the wall, an old thermostat that always clicks on too late, a newer domestic system that has learned to warm the kitchen before her migraines, and a moth that keeps appearing in the same corner as if it kept a schedule. The prose already treats these as different intensities of something rather than different kinds of thing, and I would like my explicit conceptual vocabulary to keep up with what the book is doing. I want a working definition of 'sentient' that is precise enough to keep me honest on a second reading and graded enough to compare, or at least order, the cases that actually appear in the novel.

Could you propose such a definition and run it through the following cases: a dog with a broken leg; a honeybee defending its hive; a Pacific oyster, an animal with no central brain and a simple nervous system; an ant colony taken as a whole; a bimetallic-strip thermostat, in which two bonded metals bend with temperature and mechanically trip a switch; a smart thermostat that builds a predictive model of its occupants and pre-warms a room ahead of a headache; a large language model producing the sentence 'that hurts' in a single forward pass, with no memory between outputs; and a person in deep, dreamless sleep under anesthesia. I am fairly confident about the dog and the simple thermostat at opposite ends, and I lose my grip somewhere around the honeybee and the smart thermostat. I want the definition to reveal whether felt experience and moral considerability can come apart in the middle range, and whether the middle cases differ in degree or merely in kind. Please run the definition through all eight cases, say where it yields an awkward verdict or no verdict at all, and then either revise the definition or defend the surprising answer; I am not looking for a literature review, only a definition that survives its test.
