I just spent two hours arguing with a freind about whether America is actually exceptional, and I think half the argument was just us using the phrase "American exceptionalism" to mean different things. He meant it as a kind of compliment: America is uniquely good, has a unique mission, and gets to play by different rules. I meant the boring historical claim that the US developed differently from Europe — no feudal aristocracy, lots of cheap land, a weaker labor movement, a frontier culture — so its politics came out weird by European standards. Then someone else jumped in and said the whole term is just an ideology for waving away slavery or bad wars as not-the-real-America. So now I want a working definition of the term that would settle whether we were even talking about the same thing, and I have a pile of cases I'd like it to handle:

- A sociologist points out that among rich democracies the US is an outlier on a bunch of measures: much more religious, much higher gun ownership, no national health service, lower taxes, more imprisonment. Does this count as exceptionalism, or is it just "the US is different on some variables"?
- A president says the US has a unique duty to defend liberty and can act alone without waiting for allies. I'm confident this is a normative claim, but the definition needs to say exactly what makes it normative rather than merely comparative.
- A French leader says France too is an exception with a universal mission. If a definition only fits America, I want to know whether thats anything more than America being the case we happened to care about.
- A historian argues the US really does differ from European nations because it skipped feudalism, and that explains its weaker socialism and its weird individualism. This is the use I'm most comfortable with, but I'm no longer sure it should share a word with the president's use.
- A critic says the whole idea is unfalsifiable cover: every time the US does something ugly, people file it under not-the-real-America, so the myth survives any evidence. I think this is a third use, but I need the definition to say whether the critic is using the same concept or a different one.
- An American says: "we're not better than anyone, we're just different, and being this powerful makes everyone else treat our differences as a moral claim." I keep going back and forth on whether this person has stripped the concept down to its least interesting core or stopped using the concept at all.

Propose the definition (or, if you think the term is doing two or three jobs, give me the separate definitions), run it through all six, tell me where it misclassifies, and then say which of the six verdicts you'd be least willing to defend.
