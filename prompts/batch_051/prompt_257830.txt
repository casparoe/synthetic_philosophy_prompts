Been trying to formalise Gödel's modal ontological argument and I keep ending up with a conclusion I don't know how to refuse. Here's my reconstruction:

(1) God is by definition a necessary being: necessarily, if God exists then God necessarily exists.
(2) It's possible that God exists.
(3) In S5, whatever is possibly necessary is actual: ◇□p → p.
(4) From (1) and (2), it's possibly necessary that God exists.
(5) So God exists.

Step (3) is just S5, so I'd have to ditch S5 to block it. Step (1) looks like an unpacking of the concept. And (2) feels far weaker than (5). But the argument seems to let me define a necessarily existing lost sock into existence, which can't be right. Is the argument valid? If it is, which premise is doing the smuggling, and what exactly does rejecting it cost? If it isn't valid, say which step fails and under what reading.
