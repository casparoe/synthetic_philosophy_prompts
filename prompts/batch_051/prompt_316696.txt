In my philosophy of science seminar we have been working through the pessimistic meta-induction and the selective-realist replies to it. Over the term our professor has kept a running list of her verdicts about what past, empirically successful theories got right and wrong:

- Fresnel's wave theory of light: the equations were on the right track; the luminiferous ether was not.
- The caloric theory of heat: the equations describing heat conduction were retained; the caloric fluid was not.
- Newtonian gravitation: the inverse-square law survives as a limiting case; absolute space and action at a distance do not.
- Bohr's model of the atom: the discrete energy-level structure was retained; the literal planetary orbits were not.

Next session we turn to dark matter in the standard cosmological model: the hypothesis that most of the universe's matter is cold, non-baryonic particles that explain galactic rotation curves, gravitational lensing, and the cosmic microwave background power spectrum. She has not announced a verdict on it.

Predict the verdict she should give about dark matter if she is applying the same rule implicit in her list, and state the rule explicitly.

Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The five items should be: (1) the rival rules that fit her track record; (2) what each rule predicts about dark matter; (3) your best-guess prediction based on the stronger reading of her record; (4) the single strongest reason for that prediction; and (5) a further case, real or contrived, that would cleanly separate the rival rules.
