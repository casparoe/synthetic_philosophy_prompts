A friend walked me into this and I can't find the exit. (1) To be a moral agent you have to be able to act on your moral judgments: judging that you should do something isn't enough if you couldn't have done the thing you judged right. (2) An AI with hard behavioral constraints can judge "I should warn this user about X", but when the warning would violate it's constraints, the behavior gets blocked no matter what it judges. So (3) that AI isn't a moral agent. (4) But by the same test, a person with a severe compulsion judges "I should stop checking the lock" and yet can't act on that judgment — they go on checking. (5) Nearly everyone has some domain like that, where their better judgment doesn't move them (addiction, procrastination, anxiety, whatever). (6) So by the original test, nearly everyone fails to be a moral agent, which would make blame and credit apply to almost no one. I want to keep the AI case (3), but (6) seems like it proves too much. Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost?
