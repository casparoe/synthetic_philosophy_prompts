Suppose a well-funded NGO announces tomorrow that it has a working CRISPR gene drive for the main malaria-carrying mosquito, and that it plans to release it in one country in sub-Saharan Africa in five years. The drive is designed so thier edited allele is inherited by about 95% of offspring, females with two copies are sterile, and females with one copy stay fertile so the drive keeps spreading for a while. Assume the lab data hold up, and assume no international agreement clearly governs this and the local regulator has never approved anything remotely like it. Give me your best reasoned prediction of how the next ten years play out: which actors try to block or accelerate the release, which arguments actually move them (as opposed to the ones people put in press releases), what happens to mosquito populations and malaria cases in the first few seasons after release, and where the first irreversible-looking surprise is most likely to come from. I care less about the story than about the causal chain: for each step, tell me whether it is robust or whether it flips under small changes in the setup.
