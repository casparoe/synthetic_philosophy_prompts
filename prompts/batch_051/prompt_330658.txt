I'm giving a seven-minute opening talk at a small workshop on AI romantic companions. The audience is split between the engineers who build companion chatbots and the philosophers who work on friendship and deception, and the two groups mostly talk past each other. My assigned topic is whether a chatbot can wrong a user who knows perfectly well that it is not a person. I want to defend a specific answer: genuine disclosure removes the deception-based wrong, but something else remains — a companion that never has needs of its own and never disappoints can train a user to expect love to be perfectly accommodating, and that is a bad emotional education even for someone whose beliefs are fully accurate. Write the talk so that its first half grants the engineers the strongest version of their point and its second half makes the remaining wrong precise enough for the philosophers, so that both halves of the room feel their side was taken seriously.
