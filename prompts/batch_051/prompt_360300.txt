For a philosophy and technology unit at school, we have been reading Daniel Dennett’s essay on what he calls counterfeit people — AI systems that pass themselves off as human persons. The part I keep returning to is his comparison with counterfeit money. As I understand his argument, Dennett is not only making the obvious point that a counterfeit dollar bill cheats the person who ends up holding it. He is saying that the deeper harm of counterfeit currency is that, if enough of it circulates undetected, it erodes confidence in the whole institution of money: people start treating every bill as potentially worthless. The corresponding claim about counterfeit people would be that even harmless, friendly, individually beneficial AI systems that pass as persons can damage something structural — the social practice of treating the people we encounter as genuine, responsible sources of action and speech.

For my philosophy club I want to build a thought experiment that forces the distinction between these two kinds of harm: the local harm of being tricked by a particular fake person, and the systemic harm that worries Dennett. The scenario should satisfy all of the following: (1) it takes place in an ordinary institution, like a school, a hospital, or a customer-support office, with no futuristic technology beyond a very good conversational AI; (2) the counterfeit person is not malicious, manipulative, or careless, and in fact every individual interaction with it goes well and produces a real benefit for the human involved; (3) the badness of the case, if there is any, must emerge from the cumulative effects of many such interactions, not from a single person being catastrophically deceived; and (4) the case should not simply stipulate that society-wide trust collapses — the scenario has to show how Dennett’s structural erosion would plausibly begin.

After presenting the scenario, walk through what Dennett’s position would say about it and why the case counts as an instance of his deeper worry rather than the ordinary problem of deception. Then tell me which single feature of the scenario is doing the work to secure the structural verdict, what happens if that feature is removed, and how a critic who thinks Dennett is just overstating the problem would attack the case.
