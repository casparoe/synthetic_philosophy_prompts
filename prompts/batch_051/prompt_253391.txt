Write a short story set in a community whose language contains no indexical or demonstrative vocabulary: no words for "I," "you," "now," "here," "this," or "today," and no tense system that anchors a sentence to the moment at which it is uttered. The language is otherwise maximally precise — every person has a unique proper name, every place can be identified by name or coordinate, and every moment can be identified by calendar date and clock time. The story should follow one speaker of this language through a situation in which self-location suddenly matters: waking up in an unfamiliar room, needing to report where and when they are, or trying to make someone else understand that the person speaking is the one who needs help. The conflict should turn on whether complete non-indexical information (a name, a room number, an exact time) can take the place of the missing first-person, here-and-now content, or whether something essential is lost. Write it as fiction rather than a disguised essay: let the philosophical point emerge from character, situation, and dialogue, and make the story work both for a reader who has never studied philosophy of language and for one who knows the essential-indexicality debate.
