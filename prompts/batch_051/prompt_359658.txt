I run a small sanctuary for rescued farmed animals. Every few weeks someone proposes that we use or sell something the animals produce anyway: eggs from rescued hens, wool that has to be shorn from sheep for their own comfort, manure, shed feathers. The team is divided. Some say these byproducts are true waste unless we use them, and that selling a few eggs or fleeces would fund more rescues without causing new suffering. Others say the sanctuary exists to stop animals from being treated as resources, and that the moment we sell a hen's eggs or a sheep's wool we have started treating the animal as farm property, whatever the welfare facts are. I want a reusable procedure the animal-care manager and I can actually run in about twenty minutes, with no ethics background, every time this comes up.

Please write exactly five numbered steps or decision rules, in the order they should be applied, with no introduction or conclusion and each step one or two sentences. For each step, make clear which answers let the proposal proceed and which stop it. Illustrate the steps along the way by applying them to two recurring cases—using or selling eggs from rescued hens, and using or selling wool from sheep that must be shorn—and include at least one illustration where the procedure gives an answer my staff will not like. Make the fifth item a statement of what the procedure cannot settle and must therefore be decided by a person acting for the sanctuary.
