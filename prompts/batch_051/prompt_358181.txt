Yesterday evening I had a long argument with a friend about whether DNA is a code. He kept returning to the slogan "DNA is a code" — sometimes in its more scientific-sounding form, "the genetic code" — as though it settled the matter. His move was this: the genetic code is an unproblematic fact of molecular biology, and therefore genes literally carry instructions, and therefore talk of the genome as a program or blueprint for building an organism is not merely a figure of speech. I pushed back that a molecule cannot carry instructions the way a sentence or a computer program does, because instructions require a reader or interpreter, and the cell is just chemistry; I suggested that "code" was doing metaphorical work and that he had helped himself to the metaphor's force without establishing that it applies. He replied that I was being perversely literal about a term working biologists use without embarrassment.

I want to get clear about what "DNA is a code" could actually amount to before we resume. Please lay out the distinct precise claims that the sentence might be used to assert, as a menu with verdicts. The candidate readings I can half-see but cannot hold apart include: a merely empirical claim about the triplet mapping between codons and amino acids; a claim that this mapping is arbitrary or conventional rather than physically forced; a claim that DNA carries semantic information — content, aboutness — rather than merely causal information; a claim that the genome is a program or blueprint in something close to the literal sense; and a claim that DNA's being a code requires some kind of sender and reader, which the cell may or may not supply. I also suspect the slide between "DNA is a code" and "there is a genetic code" matters, but I cannot make the difference precise. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and state which reading or readings would actually support my friend's inference from the existence of the genetic code to the conclusion that genes literally carry instructions. Finally, tell me which reading is typically doing the work when "DNA is a code" is used to shut down this argument the way he used it.
