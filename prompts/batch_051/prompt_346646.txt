I am running an advanced reading group on Lakatos's Proofs and Refutations, and after working through the Euler polyhedron episode I find that participants can name the local moves—monster-barring, exception-barring, lemma-incorporation—but cannot say what a finished account of informal mathematical development would have to achieve. Write the list of desiderata that a theory of how informal mathematics grows must meet in order to count as a genuinely quasi-empirical account of mathematics in Lakatos's sense, as against a formalist or Euclidean picture of mathematics as a finished deductive system. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration from the dialogue that forces it: the original vertex-edge-face conjecture, the nested cubes, the picture frame, the distinction between a counterexample that refutes only a lemma and one that refutes the main conjecture, and the way the triangulation proof exposes a hidden assumption. Do not recap the dialogue; turn its episodes into criteria.

Divide the desiderata into two tiers. First give the non-negotiable ones, and state what each rules out—for example, a non-negotiable criterion might be that the account explain how a proof can change the meaning of the theorem it proved, or that it treat counterexamples as the engine of concept formation rather than as mere error. Second give the camp-specific preferences—for example, how far the account should be formalizable, or how much of the later Lakatosian talk of research programmes should be imported—and indicate for each who would insist on it and why a rival could drop it without losing the quasi-empirical core.

The part I want most is the interaction between criteria. Identify any subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable. I suspect one tension is between the demand for a fully rational reconstruction of the dialogue and the demand for fidelity to how working mathematicians actually behave; another is between the demand that the concept of polyhedron be allowed to shift after each refutation and the demand that there be something stable that the original conjecture was about. For each suspicious pair, say whether it is genuinely unsatisfiable, or only hard to satisfy simultaneously, and state which requirement a working account may relax first and what it costs. Keep the response on the criteria and their motivation; do not compile a survey of Lakatos scholarship or a readings list.
