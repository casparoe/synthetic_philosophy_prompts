I've spent most of my career defending some version of the standing view of blame: the claim that whether you're entitled to blame someone isn't just a question of whether your accusations are true, or whether what they did was wrong. A hypocrite can be completely accurate about what you did and still, we think, there's something defective about *their* blaming you — and I've wanted to say the defect is a genuine normative one, not a merely pragmatic or rhetorical one. Their blame fails not because it's false, but because they lack standing; past wrongdoing of the relevant sort is a seperate condition on blame, over and above blame's fittingness.

Lately I've been trying to figure out how exposed this view actually is, and I'd like you to press on it honestly rather than defend it. What would have to be true for the standing view to be wrong? I want a real inventory of defeaters, sorted by how decisive they are — from the ones that would make me abandon the view to the ones that would only make me qualify it. Include the cases a critic would reach for: the parent who smoked at seventeen and now blames his kid for vaping; the recovering alcoholic who blames drunk drivers; the person who blames themself for the very wrong they committed. Are these pressure on the view, and do they all press in the same direction? Say whether a committed defender should concede that any of these count against them, or whether they'd reasonably re-describe them. I'm also worried that the view has a built-in escape clause — that whenever a counterexample seems to show someone both lacks standing and blames okay, we just discover they had standing all along because of repentence, or temporal distance, or the fact that their wrong wasn't *exactly* the same. Tell me whether the view can be stated in a way that risks something: what pattern of judgments, what possible case, what finding elsewhere in moral psychology would actually falsify it? And if it turns out the view is well insulated, I want to know whether that's a problem in this domain or just the normal condition of a view about a practice like blame. Play it straight: which of these challenges are live, and which are merely logical possibilities.
