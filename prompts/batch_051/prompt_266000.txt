I'm a PhD student in political theory, and my officemate Dana keeps making speech-regulation calls that I cant quite turn into a rule. She says she's a Millian about speech — only harms count, never the offensiveness of an idea — but I want to know what the rule actually is and what it would say about a case we're fighting over now.

Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it:

- Our university rescinded an invitation to a speaker who argues for ethnic segregation. Dana said that was wrong and the talk should have gone ahead. The harm on offer was offense to students, and she won't restrict for that.
- But she supported our local parenting forum taking down anti-vaccine posts during a measles outbreak in our town. Her reasoning was that there was a short, real causal route from those posts to unvaccinated kids and then to hospitalizations.
- She said a climate-change-denial documentary shouldn't be banned, even though she thinks it's full of falsehoods, because she couldn't see an identifiable harm you could trace back to it. But she thought an oil company should be fined for an ad campaign that understated how its own products contribute to warming — commercial speech, she says, does not get the same latitude.
- She defended a general manual on protest and sabotage tactics as protected, but said the same instructions handed out at a specific blockade to people about to act on them was aiding the illegal act, not mere publishing.

The new case: state legislators are considering a law that would prohibit fossil-fuel companies from publishing op-eds that dispute the science on climate change in ordinary newspapers — not ads, but op-eds under the company's own name arguing the science is unsettled or overblown.

Half our reading group thinks Dana has to oppose this: it's general public advocacy, and the harms are too diffuse. The other half thinks her anti-vax call and her commercial-speech call together commit her to supporting it.

Forget the actual vote for a second — what principle would make her earlier calls come out coherent, what would it say about this law, and what futher case would best separate that principle from the obvious rivals?
