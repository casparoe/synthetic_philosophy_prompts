I am a novelist, and I am drafting a novel whose protagonist—a retired notary in a small town—has come to serve as the person to whom neighbors bring their privacy complaints. I have already drafted four episodes in which she rules on such complaints. I want her rulings to be principled and consistent, and I am now drafting a fifth episode. Here is what she has ruled in the existing chapters.

A pharmacist, meaning to be helpful, telephoned a customer's husband to say that his wife's prescription had arrived, without checking whether the husband knew about it. My protagonist called this a serious breach, holding that the decisive wrong was that a private fact had passed to another person without the customer's leave.

A company that sold fitness trackers had sold its users' location histories, including their late-night movements, to insurance companies. She called this an invasion, saying that a person should be able to decide who learns where she has been and at what hour.

A school had installed cameras inside its changing rooms, and the head teacher had shown the footage to several colleagues. She condemned the school, and her harshest words were reserved for the circulation of the footage.

A local blogger had intercepted a neighbor's voicemails and printed excerpts on his website. She ruled that the publication supplied the full measure of the wrong.

The new episode concerns a man who has, for months, stood each evening at his unlit window and watched a woman in the building opposite as she ate her dinner and read in her armchair. Her curtains are usually open, and passers-by on the street could glance in; he, however, does not merely glance. He never photographs or films her, never records what he sees, never speaks of it, and never approaches her. She learns of the habit when she looks up one evening and catches his eye. Nothing he saw was hidden from view in any ordinary sense; she was always clothed and going about her evening routine. She is nonetheless disturbed, and she brings the matter to my protagonist.

Before I write the scene, I need to know how this character would rule if she is being faithful to the pattern of her earlier rulings. Which of the four earlier rulings is the closest analog? If her track record genuinely underdetermines the answer, spell out the principle she would be adopting under each ruling, and say which principle you think the character, as I have drawn her, is more likely to hold. Please also say how confident you are in the prediction and identify the single point about which you are least sure.
