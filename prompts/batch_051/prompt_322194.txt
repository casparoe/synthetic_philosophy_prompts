I am compiling a taxonomy of proposed norms of assertion, and I want to move past the familiar options. The list so far: the knowledge norm (assert p only if you know p); the truth norm (assert p only if p is true); the belief norm (assert p only if you believe p); the justification or reasonable-to-believe norm (assert p only if it is reasonable for you to believe p); and the certainty norm (assert p only if you are certain that p). Please propose one more candidate norm that is not on this list and is not a mere rewording, special case, or combination of any of them. In particular, I am interested in a norm whose force does not derive solely from the asserter's standing epistemic state. State the norm precisely enough that it separates cases, give one case that the listed norms handle badly or divide over and that your candidate handles cleanly, and then say honestly whether this is a genuine structural alternative or merely the justification norm relabeled with a social veneer.
