At dinner last night two friends got into an argument about a third friend, Leo. Leo had promised weeks ago to help Marcus move on Saturday. Then Leo's girlfriend, who he hasnt seen in four months, got a one-night layover in our city that same Saturday, and Leo wants to cancel on Marcus to see her. Ava said he has to keep the promise; Jae said he should obviously see his girlfriend. They went back and forth for a while and I couldnt referee it. Here's roughly what they said.

Ava: 'He gave Marcus his word. Once you promise, you don't get to reopen the question just because something better came along. If you do, your word means nothing and no one can count on you.'

Jae: 'That can't be absolute. If keeping the promise meant missing a parent's funeral, clearly he should break it. So promises can be outweighed. The only question is how much the promise weighs here, and one afternoon of carrying boxes is less than one night with someone you haven't seen in months. The move can be rescheduled; the layover can't.'

Ava: 'I never said promises are absolute. I said the promise itself is a reason, and not one that just melts into "how much harm will breaking it cause." He told Marcus he'd be there, so he shouldn't be deciding Saturday morning whether it still suits him. Otherwise it wasn't a commitment, it was a forecast.'

Jae: 'But if the promise itself has weight all on its own, it should still have weight even when Marcus doesn't care. Suppose Marcus had found another helper and would be glad Leo bailed. Would Leo still be doing something wrong by going to the airport? That seems crazy. What's wrong with breaking a promise is that you disappoint someone and damage trust. Without that, there's nothing left to be wrong.'

Ava: 'If Marcus is genuinely fine with it, then Marcus has released him — and that's Marcus's call, not Leo's. The whole point of promising is to hand the other person the right to hold you to it or let you off. If a promise only had the force the current cost-benefit total gives it, that right was never really handed over. Why say "I promise" instead of "I currently expect I'll help"?'

Jae: 'Because "I promise" changes the costs — it makes Marcus rely on him more, so bailing is more damaging. That's a real difference, but it doesn't remove him from ordinary weighing. And here the weighing should be easy: any decent friend would release him for this.'

Ava: 'Then he should ask Marcus, not just bail. If Marcus wouldn't release him, that tells you something. And Leo didn't promise a hypothetical decent friend; he promised Marcus.'
