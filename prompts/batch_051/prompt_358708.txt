Consider the idea that a writing system is an implicit theory of the language it writes: a script must decide which levels of linguistic structure are represented on the page and which are left to the reader's competence. Rank the following five levels of structure from most to least plausibly treated as what an alphabet encodes: contrastive phonemes, surface phones, morphophonemic alternations, syllable structure, and morpheme identity. Allow ties where two levels seem equally well encoded, and give a one-sentence justification for each placement. Then state, in light of your ranking, what exactly is being asserted when a script is called phonemic, and what a script would have to encode for that description to be literally true rather than an approximation.
