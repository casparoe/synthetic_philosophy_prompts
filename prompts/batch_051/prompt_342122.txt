Suppose the strategic positions had been exactly reversed: Sony licensed Betamax cheaply and widely to other major electronics manufacturers from the start, while JVC kept VHS mostly in-house for the first five years. Keep all technical facts as they actually were—Betamax with its shorter recording time and smaller cassette, VHS with its longer recording time and larger cassette. Give me your best argued prediction of which format would have dominated the U.S. home video market by 1985, and make your reasoning visible. In particular, separate the effects of the licensing reversal from the independent effects of recording time and cassette size; identify which links in your causal chain are robust and which are more speculative; and tell me what would have to be different about consumer preferences, retailer incentives, or the movie rental market for your prediction to fail. The underlying question I am trying to answer is whether the real-world outcome was locked in by technical and economic fundamentals, or whether a small change in early licensing strategy could have put a different format on top.
