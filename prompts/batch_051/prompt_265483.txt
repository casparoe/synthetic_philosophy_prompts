I'm trying to work out whether Mill's critique of appeals to nature in The Subjection of Women can be made consistent with his own reformist conclusions. His argument, as I understand it, is that we can't infer anything about what women are 'naturally' like from how they behave under a system of subordination, because that system is itself a huge distorting influence, so the appeal to nature is just custom dressed up as nature. Fine. But then he seems to think that once you strip away those distortions—equal laws, open professions, no more legal subordination—you'll get a much better view of what women actually are like, and that this is the experiment that will reveal their real capacities. My question is wether that's just the same appeal to nature in a forward-looking disguise. Like, he's still assuming there's a 'real' nature sitting underneath the conventions, waiting to come out when the conventions are removed. If the earlier argument says the social world is always shaping people, why think there's any layer of unconstituted nature left to reveal at all?
