I have been working through the White Horse Dialogue, the text associated with Gongsun Long and the School of Names, in which the conclusion reached is that a white horse is not a horse. As I understand the dialogue, it gets there by at least three distinguishable moves: first, that horse names a shape while white names a color, and naming a color is not the same as naming a shape; second, that the request for a horse and the request for a white horse have different satisfaction conditions, since a bay or black horse answers the first request but not the second; and third, that white horse is a compound formed by adding a color qualifier to a kind term, so the compound term is not interchangeable with the bare kind term. The standard interpretive dispute is whether this is a point about the semantics of the copula, about sortals and how restrictive qualifiers compose, or about the intensionality of compound terms.

I want to stress-test how much of the argument is being carried by the particular qualifier white. Keep the structure fixed but replace white with female (so the compound is a female horse), with dead (a dead horse), and with toy (a toy horse). Choose two of these three substitutions, and for each one run each of the three moves above through it individually. For each move in each substituted case, say whether it transfers intact, transfers only after being repaired, or has no genuine analogue. Then use the resulting pattern to say what the original dialogue was really relying on: does the seemingly paradoxical conclusion arise only when the added qualifier picks out a separable quality such as color, or does a more general point about compound sortals survive the substitution? Given that claims like a dead horse is not a horse or a toy horse is not a horse can, on some readings, come out as fairly ordinary truths, what does that suggest about how much of the original conclusion was sophistical and how much was a real observation about how qualifiers restrict or change a kind? Finally, please say how confident you are in the overall assessment and identify the single step in your reconstruction that you are least sure about.
