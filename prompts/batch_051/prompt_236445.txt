I'm on the policy team for a video-sharing site, and we're stuck on our synthetic-media rule. The rule reads:

"We remove manipulated or synthetically generated media that depicts a real person saying or doing something they did not actually say or do, when a reasonable viewer would be likely to mistake the media for authentic."

The case: a researcher posted a deepfake that a state-run propaganda outlet has been circulating, showing a candidate "admitting" on camera that he paid voters. The researcher's caption says plainly that the clip is fake, names the outlet, and points out the visual tells. A local news page later reposted the same clip stripped of that context, captioned "This is circulating online." The rule was written years ago, with impersonation and cheapfakes in mind, not people trying to inoculate others against propaganda.

Walk me through what the rule requires for the researcher's post and for the news page's repost under four readings: the plain words, the underlying purpose, what the drafters would have said if someone had asked at the time, and what a reasonable user would take the rule to mean. Then tell me which reading I should adopt, and whether we need an explicit exception for reproducing propaganda in order to debunk it, or whether the rule as written already handles this. Flag the ugly part for each option, like a fact-checker getting taken down or a stripped-of-context repost sliding through.
