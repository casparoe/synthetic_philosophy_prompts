In Some Thoughts Concerning Education Locke has two things going on that I think might be in tension. Early on he spends a lot of time on the need to make children submit their desires to reason: deny them what they cry for, dont give them something just because they want it, and teach them to put up with disappointment. But he also says the great art of education is to make learning feel like play, and that making schoolwork a task or a burden is how you make children hate it. I want you to give me a concrete example of a modern kindergarten or first-grade activity where a teacher trying to follow the first principle would have to make a child do something unpleasant or refuse them something they want, but following the second principle would say not to make the activity feel like an obligation. Then explain where Locke would draw the line between useful self-denial and harmful compulsion in that example. Be explicit about which parts of his reasoning you're extrapolating from, because he never saw a modern classroom. End with one sentence beginning Summary: that states the key distinction.
