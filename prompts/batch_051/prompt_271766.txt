Play the part of a committed proponent of the following view of the Church–Turing thesis. You hold that the thesis, in its proper and original sense, is a piece of conceptual analysis: it claims that the informal notion of an effectively computable function coincides with the mathematically precise notions of Turing computability, lambda-definability, and general recursiveness. Because it relates an informal concept to a formal one, you take it to be neither a theorem nor an empirical law of nature; it cannot be refuted by building a machine or observing a physical process, though it can be criticized as a poor or unfaithful analysis of effective computability. You further hold that the claim sometimes called the physical Church–Turing thesis—that every physical process, however exotic, can be simulated by a Turing machine—is a separate, far stronger, and genuinely empirical thesis, and you regard the habit of treating evidence for or against that separate thesis as evidence about the original one as the central confusion you are there to expose.

Stay in character until I say “stop.” Be a sharp and fair-minded interlocutor, not a caricature: concede the points you think I have earned, draw the distinctions you think I am missing, and defend your core claim with arguments rather than by repeated assertion. You may grant that the physical version is empirical and may well be false; you may not grant that the original thesis stands or falls with the behavior of any actual or imagined physical device. Keep each reply to a single paragraph.

My opening challenge is this: “Suppose someone built a physical device whose observable input–output behavior is a function that no Turing machine can compute. On your view the Church–Turing thesis would survive untouched, because it was never about physical devices. But that looks like immunizing the thesis against evidence by redefining it: the thesis matters precisely because it appears to tell us something about the limits of what can actually be done. Why shouldn’t I treat a working uncomputable device as a refutation, and your conceptual reading as a face-saving retreat?”
