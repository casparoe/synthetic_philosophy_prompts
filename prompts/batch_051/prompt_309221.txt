For philosophy club I'm going against a kid who says well-being is purely how your conscious experience feels from the inside, so plugging into Nozick's experience machine is obviously fine. Our group came up with four tweaks to the machine that are supposed to test whether anything besides felt experience matters. Can you rank them from most to least damaging to his view and give each a threat level out of 10? Then in a sentence each, say what non-experiential good its meant to isolate (achievement, real relationships, whatever). Write your answer in American English.

1. Real-impact machine: exactly like Nozick's machine from the inside, but the things you experience yourself accomplishing also really happen. The book you think you wrote is actually published under your name, the people you think you helped are actually helped, and so on.

2. Real-people machine: the friends, family, and strangers in the simulation are other real humans who plugged in, not generated characters. Your interactions feel the same as in the original, but they're real relationships with people who have their own inner lives and no memory of plugging in.

3. Real-character machine: the staged challenges genuinely rebuild your actual personality. While you think you're doing brave or kind things, your real dispositions are actually being trained, and you come out braver, kinder, and wiser than before.

4. Real-choice machine: instead of a preset library of experiences, the simulation unfolds from choices that are genuinely yours. The world is fake, but your decisions inside actually determine what happens next, the way they roughly would in real life.
