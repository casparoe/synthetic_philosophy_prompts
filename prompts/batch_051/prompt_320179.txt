i'm trying to get my views on divine simplicity into a shape where I can defend them as a package, and I'd like an audit of the set rather than a lecture on the doctrine. Here's where I've ended up:

1. God is absolutely simple: no parts of any kind, no composition of form and matter or essence and existence, no real distinction between God and God's nature.
2. God is pure act, with no passive potency or unrealized capacities.
3. God's attributes—omnipotence, omniscience, justice, mercy—are really identical with one another and with God.
4. God's act of knowing is itself really identical with God; divine knowledge isn't a state God is in.
5. God creates freely; God could have not created, and nothing external forced the decision.
6. God knows contingent particulars, like what I spilled on the counter this morning.
7. God genuinely loves particular creatures, and this isn't merely metaphorical.
8. Predication about God is analogical, so I don't think (3) commits me to saying justice and mercy are synonyms.

I'm sure some of these pull against others, but I can't tell which are real problems and which only look bad until you make a distinction. Go through the pairs that matter, say which tensions are genuine and which dissolve, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each say what would have to give. Don't treat every pair as equally problematic.
