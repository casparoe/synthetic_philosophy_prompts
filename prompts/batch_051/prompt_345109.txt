A city is piloting a "transferable place in line" system at its central permit office. Instead of forming a physical queue, each arrival draws a sequentially numbered token, and the token entitles its holder to be served in that order. Tokens may be sold or given away freely, and the office maintains a public bulletin board where holders can list tokens for sale or buyers can post bids. The city caps resale prices at twice the local hourly minimum wage, requires no registration for small trades, and staff call numbers strictly in order with no exceptions. The stated rationale is that queueing misallocates time: people who urgently need the permit can pay to go earlier, while people with time but little money can earn something by waiting.

Analyze how this scheme would actually behave, not how it is intended to behave. Specifically address: who buys, who sells, and how the composition of the line changes over the course of a day; what happens to the informal norm of first-come-first-served, including whether participants continue to treat queueing as a moral obligation and whether visitors who do not share the local norm now face a clearer or murkier rule; the first ways it gets gamed; and which failure modes are fixable by small rule changes and which are intrinsic to putting a price on sequence.

After your analysis, give a brief structured summary as a JSON object with this schema:

- parties_affected: array of {party, likely_outcome, confidence}
- gaming_vectors: array of {vector, who_exploits_it_first, severity}
- second_order_effects: array of {effect, mechanism, direction}
- failure_modes: array of {failure, proposed_fix, fixable, residual_problem}
