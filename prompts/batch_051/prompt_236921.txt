I've ended up on my town's flood-planning commitee, and we're trying to decide whether to spend a big chunk of the budget raising the river wall. We got ten different climate models to project peak river height in 2050, and they're all over the place—from basically today's levels up to nearly two meters higher. The committee keeps asking me for 'the number,' since I'm the only one who's read the technical reports. The consultant we hired says just average the ten outputs, because the errors will cancel out.

But I started reading the fine print and realized six of the ten models share a lot of the same underlying code and were tuned on the same historical floods, so it isn't really ten independent votes; when those six agree it feels more like one opinion copied six times. Meanwhile the two models giving the scariest numbers come from groups that did the most recent field measurements. A contractor friend says I'm overcomplicating it: build for the worst case, since the cost of being wrong is mostly one-sided. I have to give the town a recommendation next week. Is there a principled way to combine these models, or at least to think about how much to trust their agreement and how to decide under this kind of uncertainty without just picking a scary number or a comfortable average?
