At my high school math club we have been working through the incompleteness theorems, and a debate came up that none of us could settle. Most of us had absorbed the familiar conclusion that no consistent formal system strong enough to express arithmetic can prove its own consistency. One member pushed us further, though: that result still leaves open the possibility that the basic axioms of arithmetic are actually inconsistent, so that a contradiction is derivable even though no one has found one. I had always treated arithmetic as the firmest thing there is, and now I do not know how much this possibility should matter.

Please describe the most important implications of the hypothesis that the basic axioms of arithmetic are inconsistent, not merely incomplete or unable to prove their own consistency. I would like you to separate the purely logical consequences (under the standard logic used in ordinary mathematics) from the consequences for mathematical practice, for the sciences that depend on arithmetic, and for how we should regard our own reasoning. In particular, should the bare possibility make us substantially less confident in ordinary arithmetic and in arguments built on it, or are there principled reasons to think the situation would be much less catastrophic than it sounds? Please keep your response to no more than 500 words.
