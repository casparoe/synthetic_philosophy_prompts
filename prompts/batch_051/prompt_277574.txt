Take Pascal's mugging—some guy asks you for $5 to avert an astronomically unlikely but astronomically large harm—and turn it into a threshold public-goods game. A mugger seperately tells each of 100 people the same thing: pay me $5 now; if at least 90 of you pay, nothing happens, and if fewer than 90 pay I cause 10^30 utils of harm. The setup is common knowledge. Work through the expected value of one person's $5 as a function of their beliefs about the other 99; where exactly is the pivotality? Does expected-value reasoning still say pay? And do the standard replies (complexity priors, bounded utility, rounding tiny probabilities to zero) still bite in the same way, or does the coordination structure change which part of the argument you should attack?
