I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions on the proportionality of final punishment: the doctrine of eternal conscious torment and annihilationism, sometimes called conditional immortality. Assume both parties are philosophically sophisticated, work in a broadly Christian theological idiom, and are attempting to produce a document they can both sign before their genuine disagreements are set down. Assume both accept that some people ultimately face a severe, divinely sanctioned final judgment; set universalism aside except where one side needs it to state an objection.

Structure the document in four parts. First, a numbered list of substantive claims both sides can sign about punishment, proportionality, divine justice, and the relevant biblical imagery, with each claim informative enough that neither side would sign it if the other side were obviously correct. Second, a precise statement of the residual disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position and would not regard the other side as a caricature. Third, a short list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would force at least one side to revise its view. Fourth, for each crux, the argument, evidence, or case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Give the two positions the strongest available forms. A defender of eternal conscious torment may hold that the gravity of a sin against God is not measured by the lifespan in which it was committed, or that the endlessness of the final state reflects a fixed post-mortem orientation rather than a discrete sentence prolonged for the sake of maximizing suffering. An annihilationist may hold that no finite offense committed in a finite life can warrant unending conscious suffering, and that the biblical language of destruction is best read as the irreversible extinction of the person rather than as ruin that goes on forever. Let each side use the best retributive and corrective-justice considerations available to it, and let them contest directly whether permanent loss of existence can count as punishment at all. Do not quote any source from memory; paraphrase biblical imagery such as fire, destruction, exclusion, and punishment that lasts into the age to come without quotation marks, and do not make the document depend on a particular translation or verse citation.

The document should also be candid about its own status. Where the two camps disagree only over which word to use, say so plainly instead of inventing a substantive disagreement; for example, they might accept the same facts about the afterlife but divide over whether permanent non-existence is properly called a punishment, or whether a conscious state from which repentance is impossible is usefully described as a sentence. Conversely, do not dissolve a real disagreement into truce language that neither side would actually sign. Since this is a text both parties would put their names to, be self-critical about whether each claim would in fact be signed by a reasonably careful defender of the stated position rather than by a convenient fictional version of one.

If the task as I have posed it is confused, or the two positions cannot carry the adversarial-collaboration format as stated, say so plainly and either repair the task or explain why it cannot be repaired, rather than handing me a document that is polite but vacuous. Write the answer in American English.
