So if you march down through mice, bees, nematodes, and eventually a thermostat, the one-neuron rule forces you to say the thermostat is sentient too. And if you don't say that, you have to point to an exact stage in the sequence where the moral light blinked out, which sounds insane. I know the standard sorites responses (sharp cutoffs, degrees of truth, supervaluationism, contextualism, epistemicism), but swapping 'sentient' in for 'heap' feels like it does something weird to each of them. Saying there's a sharp but unknowable cutoff of sentience seems much stranger than saying there's a sharp but unknowable cutoff for 'heap,' while saying sentience comes in degrees basically hands the argument to my side.
