I volunteer on the appeals board for a mid-sized video platform—the kind of site that has a trust and safety team but no actual policy department. The entire rule we're supposed to enforce is one sentence: videos that demean or attack a person or group on the basis of race, religion, national origin, sex, disability, or sexual orientation get removed. Every appeal has to be resolved by a single reviewer within 24 hours, and the interface only offers two buttons: remove or restore. There's no way to mark something borderline, no option to say the rule itself needs clarification, and the decision is final. Last week we had an appeal over a video that was right at the edge: it didn't use any obvious slur, but it danced around one pretty transparently, and the user appealing argued that swapping one mild word for a harsher one can't be the thing that flips the video from allowed to banned. One of my co-reviewers says the system must already assume there's a fact of the matter about where the line is, even if no one can find it; otherwise we're just flipping coins. A friend in the philosophy department says we've rebuilt the sorites paradox as a software product, but he wouldn't say exactly what that means for how I should vote.

I'm not asking for a ruling on the specific video. I want to understand what philosophical commitments our appeals process quietly buys into. Does a forced binary ruling on a vague term like hate speech presuppose that the term has a sharp, though maybe unknowable, boundary? Is the system also compatible with the view that the boundary is just underdetermined by our linguistic rules, or with the view that hatefulness comes in degrees? Reconstruct the implicit theory of vagueness, seperate what the two-button system strictly presupposes from what it's merely compatible with, and then tell me: if we accepted one of the rival theories, what would actually have to change about how the platform words its rules, trains reviewers, or handles borderline appeals—and would we just end up with the same problem one level up?
