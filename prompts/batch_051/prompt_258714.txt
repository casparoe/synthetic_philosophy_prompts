I want a sorting instrument—a "which deontological position do you actually hold?" decision tree—for a rationalist reading group working through the paradox of deontology. The paradox, in the form I care about, is that an agent may not kill one innocent person to stop a murderer from killing ten, even though the rule that forbids the killing seems to be about how bad it is that innocents get killed; the rule therefore appears to forbid the very thing it treats as mattering most. Please build the tree from logical space rather than from a list of named positions: each branch should be a concrete case or claim that someone can answer without knowing the labels, and the leaves should be named views. Include splitters for at least these four questions: (a) whether the constraint is explained by the state of the world (fewer violations overall) or by a feature of the agent's own action (agency, integrity, doing rather than allowing, what I owe the victim); (b) whether the constraint aggregates—if killing one to save five is wrong, is killing one to save five million also wrong, or is there a threshold; (c) whether it matters that other agents would be making the same exception, so that an exception harmless when one person makes it is still condemned when everyone like you makes it; and (d) whether the constraint controls which acts I may perform or which outcomes I may bring about, so that redirecting an existing threat to a different victim can come out differently. Use at least these probes: a surgeon harvesting one healthy patient to save five dying patients; a detective torturing a detainee to get the location of a bomb; and a bystander who can kill one person to stop a gunman about to kill ten. Leaves must include at least plain act-consequentialism, rule consequentialism, threshold deontology, strict agent-relative deontology, and a directed-obligation view. Explicitly mark combinations that nobody occupies and say briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag positions the tree cannot separate (a doing/allowing refinement versus a double-effect refinement, for instance) rather than papering over the difference. The point is to sort people by the cases they are willing to act on, not by the words they would use for themselves.
