I'm on the grants committee of a foundation that funds long-range modelling of population and resource pressure. In 1999 we gave a team a ten-year grant to build a neo-Malthusian model of global grain markets; their headline prediction was that, under business-as-usual policy, real food prices would rise for at least ten consecutive years between 2010 and 2020 in three major import-dependent regions, and calorie intake per head in those regions would fall for at least five consecutive years over the same period. It didn't materialise. They're now applying for a sizeable renewal, arguing that the underlying mechanism is sound and the 2020 end-point was never essential—the pressure is just building more slowly than the baseline assumed, so the prediction was 'right but early' rather than falsified. Our external reviewers are split: some think funding them again rewards a programme that can never be shown to be wrong and has simply moved the goalposts; others think cutting them now punishes exactly the long-horizon work we exist to support, and that a model can be wrong about timing yet still be the best guide we have. The chair has asked me to draft the decision memo for next week's vote. Draft it: reccommend whether to renew, reject, or renew with conditions, and give the strongest case for the losing side its due.
