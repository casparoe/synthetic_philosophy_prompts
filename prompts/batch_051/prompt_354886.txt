i'm the student rep on our university's AI in Student Services committee, currently taking a module on risk and AI safety, and honestly it's made me worry we're framing the decision badly. We meet on Tuesday to decide whether the counselling service can pilot a triage chatbot: a student types a few sentences about how they're doing, and the system either sends them self-help resources, books a counselling slot, or flags them as urgent for a human. The vendor's evaluation shows it beats the existing service on the average case (quicker responses, fewer missed moderate-risk cases), but our standing committee rule says no student-facing AI can be approved unless the provider can show 'that no plausible use of the system could produce serious harm'. In testing, the bot sometimes reads 'im fine actually lol' or an argumentative reply as low urgency and doesn't escalate, and the vendor says a worst-case guarantee is impossible for any open-ended language system — including the human staff who currently do triage. Half the committee wants to reject it under the rule; half says that if we applied the rule literally we'd never approve anything and we'd be trading rare AI failures for the known, everyday harm of students waiting weeks to be seen. I need to circulate a written opinion before the meeting: a concrete recommendation (approve, reject, or pilot with conditions and a time frame), plus reasoning that engages the strongest point on the losing side honestly. If I'm setting the problem up wrongly, or the rule can't be applied the way we're trying to apply it, say that plainly instead of just giving me something to read out.
