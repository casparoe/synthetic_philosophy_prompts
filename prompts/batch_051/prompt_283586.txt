So I just had this long argument with a friend about the tautology objection to natural selection. His line was that the objection is dead because philosophers of biology settled on the propensity interpretation: fitness is a probabilistic disposition to leave offspring, not the actual number an organism happens to leave. What tripped me up, and what I couldnt get him to answer, is that in practice biologists seem to go right back to measuring fitness as actual realized reproductive output — you count the surviving offspring and call the ones with more fit. Doesnt that just reintroduce the tautology one level up: the fittest are the ones who leave more offspring, and leaving more offspring is how we know theyre fit? I want to know whether the propensity interpretation actually survives the way fitness gets measured in real research, or whether it just relocates the problem. Walk me through which premise is under pressure, and if theres a weaker but honest version of the defense that still works.
