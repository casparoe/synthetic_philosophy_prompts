Adorno and Horkheimer think the culture industry runs on standardization: every supposedly unique product is the same template with different cosmetic bits, and the cosmetic bits are what keep people reconciled to the system. Build me a case where a piece of culture that is fully inside that system — a streaming show, a radio hit, a reality franchise, your call — makes at least some normal viewers more critical of the social order rather than more reconciled to it, and where this effect is produced by the cosmetic/template features themselves rather than by anything smuggled in from outside. Rules: no avant-garde art as the exception, no audience that already has the Frankfurt School critique and is just reading against the grain, and the thing has to be the sort Adorno and Horkheimer would file under culture industry rather than art. The point is to test whether the theory can make room for this possibility at all, or whether it is built to reinterpret every such effect as pseudo-individuality. Then walk me through: which feature of the product is doing the work, why they would say it is still just pseudo-individuality rather than a real difference, what happens to the case if you remove that feature, and how a defender of the theory would swat the example away.
