I cover science and ideas for a living, and I keep noticing a move in popular science writing that I don't have a precise term for. A piece begins with a careful claim — that some high-level phenomenon can be explained by, or is tightly correlated with, an underlying physical or biological process — and then slides into the much stronger claim that the phenomenon just is that lower-level process: love is nothing but oxytocin and pair-bonding circuitry; consciousness is nothing but synchronized cortical activity; a political conviction is nothing but a pattern of neural firing. I know the general term reductionism, but it seems too broad, since it also covers the modest view that higher-level facts can be accounted for by lower-level ones without thereby being declared unreal. I also know eliminativism, but that usually names an explicit thesis that some category is empty, not this quiet slide inside an otherwise respectable explanation. Is there an established name for this specific overreach — the move from "can be explained in terms of" to "is nothing but" — and for the accompanying assumption that the lower-level description is not merely useful but exhaustive? If the closest named view only partly matches, say exactly where the boundaries are; I want the carve, not just a label. Please keep the answer to at most 400 words.
