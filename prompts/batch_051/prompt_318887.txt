I've been trying to salvage the idea that memes genuinely evolve, not just as a loose metaphor, and I think I've found the line everyone else keeps missing. I distinguish what I call script memes from gist memes. A script meme travels as a fixed sequence of words or steps: a recipe, a joke retold word for word, a chord chart, a dance taught move by move. A gist meme travels as a point or an attitude: the claim that breakfast is the most important meal of the day, the idea that the customer is always right, the vague sense that bureaucracy always grows. The person who picks it up rebuilds the content from the gist, and details can drift a lot between tellings.

My theory is that only script memes actually replicate in the way Darwinian selection needs, with high enough copying fidelity for small changes to be preserved and selected. Gist memes don't really replicate at all; they get reconstructed each time, so saying they evolve by natural selection is where the meme analogy breaks down. They are more like themes that each person recomposes from their own background.

I know this sounds close to the standard objection that cultural transmission is reconstruction, not copying. What I think is new is that the two kinds can be pulled apart empirically: if you ask people to pass something along after a delay, script memes should change mostly by dropping or swapping chunks, while gist memes should change toward whatever the reteller already believed. Ive been calling this the copy-versus-rebuild boundary.

Is this distinction actually sharp, or are almost all cultural items somewhere in the middle? Is it already out there under a name I would recognize? And what is the meanest counterexample you can think of that would make the whole thing collapse rather than just need patching?
