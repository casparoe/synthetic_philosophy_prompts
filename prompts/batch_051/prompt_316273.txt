I spent the afternoon arguing with a friend about whether LLMs understand anything, and we kept circling back to the stochastic parrots argument. Her line was that the training objective is just next-token prediction over surface form, so it doesn't matter how large the model gets: it's all mimicry, no semantics. But that argument was built for the era when models were trained mostly by imitating human text. The newer systems get pushed past that—reinforcement learning on verifiable outcomes like math graded right or wrong or code that has to run, tool use, long chains of reasoning before they answer. The reward is now being right about the answer, not just sounding plausible. Does the stochastic-parrots argument survive this shift from form-imitation to outcome-based training? Which exact premise does the new training regime put pressure on, and is there a repaired version that still says something important about these models? I'm not after a balanced overview—take a definite side and defend it.
