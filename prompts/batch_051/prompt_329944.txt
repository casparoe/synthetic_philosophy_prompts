I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a philosopher friend about whether "responsibility gaps" for autonomous systems are a genuine problem or the product of too rigid a picture of agency. By the end we had circled from self-driving cars to clinical decision support and back without settling which parts of the debate would survive contact with law reform. I came away unclear, not because I lack a view, but because I could not tell which disagreements are load-bearing once someone has to draft rules rather than defend a position.

I direct a small policy research unit at a UK law-reform body. Over the next three years we are to advise on how liability, insurance, and professional regulation should change for two uses of autonomy: automated driving systems and machine-learning tools that influence clinical decisions. We have three researchers, none with formal philosophical training, and our work must feed into consultation responses and draft statutory language. I want to spend our time where the philosophy actually changes a legal or regulatory choice.

Within the responsibility-gap debate, which open questions or distinctions should I prioritise for that three-year agenda, and which familiar lines of argument are overrated or do not translate into legal consequences? Please give a defended ranking of the two or three questions you would put at the top, and explain the criteria you are using — for example, importance for downstream law, tractability for non-philosophers, and how many other decisions depend on the answer. Then identify at least two commonly drawn implications of responsibility gaps (such as moving toward strict manufacturer liability, creating a new category of legal person for autonomous systems, or pausing certain deployments) that you think matter less than the literature suggests, or do not follow at all. Where the ranking rests on a judgement call, say what would change your mind. Please do not present the deliverable as a survey or reading list; engage the ideas directly. Please write your answer in British English.
