Now that I've started reading philosophy of mind, I keep running into the debate over whether introspection is reliable — whether my reports about my own conscious experience (what I'm seeing, feeling, or thinking right now) are decent evidence about what's actually going on in my mind. The skeptics say introspection is badly unreliable: people get their own experience wrong, confabulate, and their reports get shaped by what they're asked and where their attention is aimed. The defenders say introspection is the only access we have to conscious experience, it's decent if you're careful, and any outside check that claimed to correct it would have to rely on introspection anyway. To me the dispute reads like technicians arguing over an instrument they're not allowed to calibrate against a reference standard but whose readings they keep using. Restate the main positions and the structure of the debate in actual measurement-science vocabulary, as precisely as you can without cheating and without quietly assuming instrument talk is the right frame. I especially want your honest verdict on whether the untranslateable residue is just a defect of my instrument mindset or whether it's where the philosophers were doing real work that a measurement framework can't see.
