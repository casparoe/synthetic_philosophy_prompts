Ive talked myself into an argument for a paper on whether games are art, and it now looks like it proves too much. The view I want to defend is that what makes a game artistic isn't its story or its graphics but the felt quality of the choices it offers — the shape of the decision space, the way rules turn your own agency into something you can notice and appreciate. Not what you choose, but the texture of choosing. So far I love that view. Then I started wondering why that feature has to stay inside games, and the argument came out like this. (1) If a game is art in virtue of the designed texture of its choices, then anything that presents a designed texture of choices is art in the same way. (2) A lot of obviously non-art things do exactly that: TurboTax is a branching decision tree with its own pressures and pacing; a hospital's wayfinding signs and waiting-room flow are deliberately designed to structure what you do and when; Instagram's interface is engineered to make choices feel free while steering them. (3) In those cases the texture of choosing is genuinely designed and genuinely felt, not merely a metaphor. (4) So TurboTax, hospital wayfinding, and Instagram are art in exactly the same way a game is. (5) That can't be right, or at least it drains the category of art until it covers every piece of commercial choice architecture. (6) So the premise that the art of games lives in the aesthetics of agency is either false or badly incomplete. I'd rather keep (1) than (5), but I'm not sure what I'd be paying.
