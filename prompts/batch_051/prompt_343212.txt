Please argue that in the centipede game, the standard backward-induction conclusion—that the first player should take at the first decision node—does not follow from common knowledge of rationality alone. Focus your argument on the fact that the backward-induction calculation requires each player to determine what she would do at later nodes if they were reached, even though, by the calculation's own lights, those later nodes could only be reached through moves that a rational player would not have made.
