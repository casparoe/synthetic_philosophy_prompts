An ethics reader I was assigned summarizes Mill's relation to empire approximately as follows. Mill spent most of his working life in the East India Company's service and defended British rule in India on utilitarian grounds. The reader's key claim is that Mill judged the value of any government solely by whether it promoted the general happiness; since he believed British administration was modernizing India, Mill regarded despotism over peoples he called backward as legitimate. On Liberty's harm principle is then said to be restricted to societies Mill considered mature, so once the principle is read as applying only to already-civilized peoples, there is no genuine conflict between Mill's liberalism and his colonialism. The only remaining problem, the reader suggests, is that today's students find this position offensive. I find the neatness suspicious but lack the background to say where it goes wrong. How fair is this as an account of Mill's actual views on India and colonial rule? Please separate what it gets right from what it overstates or conflates, and assess whether the claim that his liberalism and his imperialism were perfectly consistent holds up. If the summary is too misleading to correct piecemeal, I would rather be told that outright.
