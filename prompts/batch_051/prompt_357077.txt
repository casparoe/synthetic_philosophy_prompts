A colleague of mine has been reading a comparative psychologist's published verdicts on which animals are conscious. The psychologist has said yes for chimpanzees, dogs, parrots, octopuses, cuttlefish, zebrafish and honeybees, though she described the bee's consciousness as minimal; and no for fruit flies, nematode worms, jellyfish, sea anemones and oysters. Her next case is the lamprey, described as a jawless vertebrate with a small, simply organised brain, limited behavioural flexibility, and little evidence of pain beyond simple nociceptive withdrawal. Predict how she should rule on the lamprey if she is to stay consistent with her earlier verdicts. Identify the main principles her record could be read as implementing, say what each principle predicts about the lamprey, give a best guess with your reasons, and name one further case that would cleanly separate the leading two principles.
