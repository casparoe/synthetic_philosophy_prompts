hey — i'm a gameplay engineer on a strategy game with a social simulation layer. npcs keep a model of whether other npcs "count" them as a real agent: one npc can treat another as an equal, as a subordinate, or refuse to acknowledge them at all, and that changes which actions are available. i have zero philosophy background. a coworker gave me this passage from Hegel's Phenomenology of Mind (the J.B. Baillie translation, 1910, which is up on marxists.org):

This process of self-consciousness in relation to another self-consciousness has in this manner been represented as the action of one alone. But this action on the part of the one has itself the double significance of being at once its own action and the action of that other as well. For the other is likewise independent, shut up within itself, and there is nothing in it which is not there through itself. The first does not have the object before it only in the passive form characteristic primarily of the object of desire, but as an object existing independently for itself, over which therefore it has no power to do anything for its own behalf, if that object does not per se do what the first does to it. The process then is absolutely the double process of both self-consciousnesses. Each sees the other do the same as itself; each itself does what it demands on the part of the other, and for that reason does what it does, only so far as the other does the same. Action from one side only would be useless, because what is to happen can only be brought about by means of both.

i think this is saying recognition only works as a two-way loop — each side does its part only because the other side does the same, and one-sided action is pointless. but i dont know how to turn that from a paragraph into logic our agents can run. can you write a decision procedure, or really a reusable checklist/state machine, that our ai team could follow for deciding when an npc should attempt to recognize another, what counts as the other side reciprocating, when the loop should close, and when it should stay open or fail? the procedure has to run for hundreds of agents at once and designers (not philosophers) have to tune it, so it needs to be simple enough to implement and honest about which parts are design calls rather than something hegel settles. then apply it to two or three cases: (1) two equal-strength npcs meeting for the first time, (2) an npc with all the power trying to "recognize" a much weaker npc while the weaker one plays along out of fear, and (3) the case i actually care about for our game: a faction that treats another faction as an equal while the other only ever treats them as a tool. i specifically want the procedure to tell me whether case 3 is real recognition or just a mislabeled dependency, because if the state machine says it isnt, our designers will fight it. also flag where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a human.
