I've been arguing with a friend who is writing something about technological standards, and I've started keeping a list of her verdicts because she is weirdly consistent but never states the rule she's using. On QWERTY she says the 'locked in by a bad initial accident' story is overblown—Dvorak's advantages were never big enough that a re-run of history would plausibly end any other way, so she won't call it path-dependent. On VHS versus Betamax she does call it locked in: Betamax got there first with a better picture and a smaller cassette, but VHS won through longer recording time and then a cascade of video stores stocking rentals for it; by the time the quality difference could have mattered, the network had tipped and Betamax owners couldn't find tapes for their machine. She says Brunel's broad gauge losing to standard gauge was genuine lock-in too, because the wider gauge was faster and more stable but standard gauge already had the mileage through the coal districts, and a government commission essentially froze the choice before the engineering got a fair test. Then on AC versus DC she says no lock-in: Edison's DC lost becuase transformers made AC so much cheaper over long distances that any re-run which keeps the physics gets AC, or something near it. And she won't call left- versus right-hand traffic path dependence even though switching would be appallingly expensive—she calls it an arbitrary coordination convention where neither side is better, so for her 'locked into an inferior standard' isn't the same thing as mere contingency.

The case I want a prediction on is the early automobile. From what I've read, around 1900 electric cars were cleaner, quieter, and easier to start than gasoline cars, and by some accounts outsold them in cities early on, while gasoline cars were noisy, smelly, and had to be hand-cranked. Gas won anyway after the electric starter and cheap fuel fixed the worst gas-car problems and batteries couldn't match the range. If I ask my friend whether the gasoline car counts as locked in, what should I expect her to say? More useful: what rule fits all of her verdicts so far, what other rules fit them just as well, which rule you'd actually bet on, and what one further case I could put to her would cleanly separate the best two? I'm not that interested in whether her history in each case is right; I want to know whether her pattern actually forces a verdict on the car.
