I've been rereading the first Critique alongside Kant's logic lectures and I keep thinking I understand the intuition/concept distinction right up until I try to sort actual representations, at which point it falls apart. So I'd like a working definition of intuition in Kant's technical sense (Anschauung) — one that would let me sort some given representation as intuition, concept, or neither, without leaning on the ordinary English word "intuitive." Then run the definition through the following cases, which are the ones I keep tripping over.

1. A particular afterimage of a red circle, the kind that floats in the visual field. I assume this is an intuition, or at least the sensory matter for one.

2. The empirical concept red. Definitely a concept, no trouble here.

3. The pure form of space itself, taken apart from any actual shaped thing. Kant treats this as a pure intuition, so my definition needs to place it there rather than with the concepts.

4. A mental image of a particular triangle, say the one I summon up while reading a proof. Is an image an intuition, or is an image something the imagination produces that isn't itself an intuition?

5. The triangle drawn in thought in a Euclidean proof — one particular triangle, but it's supposed to stand for all triangles. Is that an intuition, a concept, or a third thing?

6. The schema of a triangle, the rule for producing an image of a triangle. Schemata always feel like neither concept nor intuition to me, but then what are they?

7. A demonstrative thought like "this red patch here." There's obviously an intuition involved, but is the whole thought a concept plus an intuition, or is the "this" itself the intuition?

8. The pure form of time, taken as a whole rather than as some particular duration or succession.

9. The transcendental unity of apperception, the bare self-consciousness that Kant says must be able to accompany all my representations. I'm fairly sure this is neither an intuition nor a concept, but I'd like the definition to say why — otherwise I worry my definition is just a vibe.

10. A sensation of sweetness, taken strictly as a feeling. Kant tends to treat sensations as matter for intuition rather than as intuitions themselves, so I'm tempted to call this neither, but I want to see whether the definition gives that verdict on principle.

A few of these I have settled verdicts on (1, 2, 3, 8, 10), and a few I genuinely don't (4, 5, 6, 7, 9). Give me a definition — or two candidate definitions if no single one works — stated precisely, run it through all ten cases, and tell me which of my settled verdicts it overturns. Also answer whether Kant actually operates with an exhausitve twofold division of representations into intuitions and concepts, or whether schemata, images, and sensations are forms that break the dichotomy.
