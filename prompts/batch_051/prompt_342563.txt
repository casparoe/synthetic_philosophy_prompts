The standard thing you hear is that an alphabet encodes phonemes — letters map to sounds. But that seems backwards to me, at least for english. The cases that bug me are things like the silent g in sign that shows back up in signature, or the t in act vs action — the spelling is tracking something that stays the same under the alternation, not the surface sound itself. Can you make the strongest argument you can for that position: that english orthography isnt really phonemic, its morphophonemic, and the letters-equal-sounds view is a category mistake?
