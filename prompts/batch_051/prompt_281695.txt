So this has been bugging me and I want to know if I'm actually being irrational or not. Back in March I bought two tickets to a show I really wanted to see, $90 each. The show sold out fast and now a kid in my grade is offering me $250 per ticket. And I don't want to sell. Which feels weird, because if I didn't have the tickets and someone offered me those same seats for $250, I would absolutely not pay that, I'd think they were insane. But when the tickets are mine, $250 doesn't feel like its enough.

My econ teacher says this is classic irrational behavior. She says the tickets are the same physical objects either way, so the only thing that's different is whether I happen to own them, and ownership shouldn't change what they're worth to me. She says either the tickets are worth at least $250 to me or they aren't, and refusing to buy them at $250 but also refusing to sell them at $250 is just inconsistent.

But it really doesn't feel like a math error. Selling them feels like I'm losing something I already have, and the bad feeling from that seems bigger than the excitement of getting $500. Whereas not buying them in the first place just feels like... not getting something extra. Those feel like genuinely different situations to me. Its not that I'm worried the price will go up or that I don't want to deal with this specific kid, I honestly think I'd say no to anyone.

So who's right here? Is my teacher right that I'm violating some basic rule of rationality and these should feel the same? Or is there a legit reason that giving up what you have and choosing not to acquire something should feel different? And should I force myself to take the $500 anyway?
