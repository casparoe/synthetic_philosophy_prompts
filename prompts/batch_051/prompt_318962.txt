I am writing a piece on the coherence of the traditional doctrine of divine omnipotence, and I would like an audit of the following set of claims, all of which I am currently inclined to accept. I have numbered the claims for ease of reference, but I am not signalling which, if any, I would be willing to give up.

(1) God is both essentially omnipotent and essentially morally perfect.

(2) Omnipotence should be analyzed as the power to actualize any state of affairs that is metaphysically possible, where metaphysical possibility is a matter of the intrinsic consistency of the state of affairs itself and is not relativized to any agent's nature, character, or powers.

(3) God cannot create a stone too heavy for God to lift. This is no limitation on God's power, because the state of affairs in question is impossible: it includes an omnipotent being's failing to lift a stone, which contradicts that being's omnipotence.

(4) God cannot sin. This, too, is no limitation on God's power, because it is an inability to fall short of the good, and it follows from God's essential moral perfection rather than from any defect of power.

(5) God is free in the libertarian sense. When God chose to create this world, God had alternatives available — other worlds, or no world at all — and God could have chosen otherwise.

(6) An omnipotent being cannot exercise its power so as to bring about its own ceasing to be omnipotent, because omnipotence is an essential property of that being.

Please work through this set and tell me whether the full package is jointly tenable. I would like to know which pairs or larger subsets are genuinely in tension, ranked by severity, and which claim or claims would have to be revised to remove each tension. I would also like to know which apparent tensions are not genuine and dissolve once the relevant distinctions are drawn. Finally, I would like the cheapest overall repair: the smallest revision that leaves the package internally consistent, together with an account of what the revised view would commit me to and what accepting that revision would cost. Treat this as an audit of internal consistency rather than an evaluation of whether the doctrine itself is true.
