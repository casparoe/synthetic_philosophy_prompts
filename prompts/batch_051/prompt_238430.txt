I spent nearly four decades as an experimental spectroscopist, and the habit I cannot shake is that of calibration: a number is trustworthy only if I can trace it through an unbroken chain of comparisons back to something outside the instrument, and I have learned never to confuse the instrument's deflection with the quantity under study. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mind, and the symbol grounding problem keeps striking me as a philosopher's way of asking where the traceability chain for meaning bottoms out. I understand the core claim to be this: a system whose symbols are defined only by their relations to other symbols is like a calibration chain in which every instrument is checked against another instrument, with no primary standard at the end; on the usual telling, the way to ground at least the primitive symbols is to couple them to non-symbolic sensory or motor contact with the world, through what the literature calls iconic and categorical representations. I am less interested in the Chinese Room dramatization than in that underlying claim about grounding.

Please restate the problem, and the proposed answer, in the vocabulary I actually trust: transducers, calibration and traceability chains, primary and secondary standards, systematic versus random error, units and scales, measurement models, and the difference between a signal and the quantity it stands for. I want a faithful translation, not an analogy pasted on: state the symbol grounding problem in metrological terms, then translate the proposed solution — grounding primitive symbols in pre-symbolic sensory or motor representations — and tell me whether it comes out as a measurement chain that terminates in a causally coupled but uncalibrated transducer, and whether that terminus deserves to be called a standard at all.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the problem and the proposed solution translate cleanly into the calibration picture, which parts you had to fudge, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all — I am thinking here of abstract terms whose meaning seems untraceable to any transducer, of compositionality, and of the old suspicion that sending signals up an axon is still just more syntax unless something interprets them. I want your candid judgment of whether my intuition — that grounding is simply the semantic analogue of traceability to a primary standard — is illuminating or is built on a confusion. If I have misstated the problem, or if the calibration vocabulary cannot carry it without fatal distortion, say so plainly rather than forcing the translation.
