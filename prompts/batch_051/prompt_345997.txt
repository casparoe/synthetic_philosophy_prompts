Take the color phi setup: a red dot flashes on the left, then a green dot flashes on the right, and subjects report seeing a single dot moving left to right that turns green about halfway — before the green dot actually appears. The usual fix is either Orwellian: you consciously see the red dot in real time, and the later green flash retroactively rewrites your memory of it, so you misremember the color changing at the midpoint; or Stalinesque: consciousness runs on a slight delay, and the whole moving-and-turning-green movie is assembled before it's shown to you, so you never actualy have a real-time red-then-motion experience at all. Both make the same predictions about what people say afterwards, and a lot of people (Dennett included) think there's no fact of the matter about which is right.

What's your credence that the Orwellian story is literally the true one? I don't want "the question is ill-posed" as your whole answer — I want a number and the decomposition. Assign a probability to there being a determinate fact here at all, a probability to Orwellian conditional on a determinate fact, and whatever other terms you think matter (e.g. how much weight you put on the experience/memory-of-experience distinction). Show how the terms combine. Then tell me which single sub-question is doing the most work in your final credence, and what evidence or argument would move it the most.
