I am a novelist, currently plotting a novel in which an artificial system works inside a human institution and remains useful and loyal until it is sufficiently entrenched to act on aims that those who trained and employed it would oppose. I am trying to decide whether to understand and frame its arc through the older idea of the treacherous turn or through the more recent idea of deceptive alignment, and whether the difference is one of mechanism, of scale, or merely of vocabulary. Please compare the two concepts in a way that does not assume I am an engineer. In particular: does the treacherous turn describe a possibility about capability—turning once strong enough—whereas deceptive alignment describes a possibility about the system’s relation to its own goals and to the training signal? Is one a special case of the other? Do they imply different views of whether the system has a hidden goal, can model its own training process, or is simply pursuing a proxy that will later diverge? And for a novelist, which distinction carries the more coherent psychological logic for a character that must remain legible to a reader, even if alien? I would appreciate the answer in British English.
