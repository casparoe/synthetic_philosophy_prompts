Last evening a friend and I argued for hours about expressivism, and by the end he had compressed the Frege–Geach problem into a numbered argument that, he said, shows the view is false unless I can identify the exact step at which it goes wrong. I resisted the conclusion more than I could resist any particular premise, which worries me, and I would like a precise diagnosis rather than a general survey of the issue.

Here is the argument as he gave it. (1) If expressivism is true, then the meaning of a moral sentence such as "Breaking a promise is wrong" is the non-cognitive attitude it expresses — roughly, disapproval of promise-breaking. (2) A sentence's semantic contribution inside a complex sentence is the same as its contribution when asserted; otherwise an inference like the following would rest on an equivocation: "Breaking a promise is wrong; if breaking a promise is wrong, then asking someone to break a promise is wrong; therefore asking someone to break a promise is wrong." (3) The moral sentence occurs embedded in the antecedent of that conditional, but one can assert the whole conditional without expressing disapproval of promise-breaking — for example, because one thinks promise-breaking is actually permissible while wanting to mark that were it wrong, then soliciting it would inherit that wrongness. (4) So, on expressivism, an embedded occurrence of "Breaking a promise is wrong" does not express the attitude that gives the sentence its meaning, and therefore it contributes a different meaning inside the conditional than it does on its own. (5) By step (2), the inference therefore does equivocate, and moral arguments of this modus ponens form are not valid. (6) But that cannot be right: ordinary speakers and competent reasoners treat such inferences as straightforwardly logically valid, not as puns or fallacies. (7) So expressivism is false.

I half-suspect the bad step is (4), and that the word "express" is being made to do two different jobs, but I cannot put the diagnosis cleanly. Is the argument valid? If not, which step fails, and under exactly what reading?
