I have a standing policy for how I handle waking up disoriented, which I adopted after a series of brief medical procedures in which I was woken for a cognitive test, sedated again, and woken once more, and could not tell from the inside how many rounds had already occurred. The policy is this: when I come to without knowing which of several indistinguishable episodes I am in, I keep my degree of belief in the non-indexical hypotheses (the hypotheses about how the world is, rather than about which episode I occupy) exactly where it was before I lost my bearings. I refuse to shift that degree of belief merely because one hypothesis says my current kind of experience would occur more often. Thus, if a fair coin determines that I will be woken once under Heads and twice under Tails, with my memory reset between wakings, I would wake still one half confident in Heads. And in the reverse protocol, where Heads produces two wakings and Tails produces one, I would likewise wake one half confident in Heads. A colleague tells me this is not a neutral shortcut but a substantive stance on the Sleeping Beauty problem: it commits me to treating the number of possible awakenings a hypothesis generates as never being evidence. Is my policy defensible as a general epistemic habit? Under what conditions does it track the truth, and under what conditions does it go wrong? Can it be restated so as to keep its simplicity without simply importing one side of the Sleeping Beauty debate?
