I run product at a mid-sized community platform—few hundred thousand monthly users, mostly lurkers, a few thousand heavy posters. We get pitched karma and reputation features nonstop: downvote-to-hide, trust levels, reputation thresholds for editing or moderation powers, karma decay for dormant accounts, badges that unlock real privileges. Every quarter we green-light or kill a batch of these and right now the process is basically vibes. I want a reusable decision procedure my team can run in a couple of hours per proposal, written so a smart PM with no economics or game-theory background can follow it. Turn whatever is actually known about reputation systems and incentive design into an ordered checklist: what to ask first, what counts as a sufficient answer, where an answer just kills the proposal, which steps are irreducibly judgment calls, and where the whole thing has to punt to a human. Be honest about which parts are genuinely established and which are fashionable guesses, and tell me plainly what this procedure won't catch. Then run it on three proposals currently in front of me: (a) hiding posts below −3 karma, (b) requiring 50 reputation to post more than once an hour, and (c) decaying karma 1% a month for accounts inactive over 90 days. At least one should come out as a no; if its the one our community team is most attached to, say so and explain why the procedure disagrees with them.
