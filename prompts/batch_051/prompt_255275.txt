I run a graduate reading group on vagueness and the sorites paradox, and a recurring problem is that members identify with a label—usually epistemicist or contextualist—but answer concrete cases in a way that fits some other position. I want a sorting instrument that catches exactly that. Please write a 'which position do you actually hold?' instrument (a decision tree or a short scored quiz, whichever gives cleaner separations) over the main positions on vagueness. The audience is philosophy graduate students who can already use the technical labels, but the quiz itself should be answerable by someone who has never heard them.

Build the instrument from the logical space rather than from a pre-given list of names. First choose diagnostic questions whose options track what the respondent believes about concrete cases, not which tradition they identify with. Use at least these cases: a heap sequence from one grain to ten thousand; a continuous color spectrum from red to orange; a lineup ordered by height from clearly short to clearly tall; and a legal boundary, such as reaching the age of majority. Label each branch with the separating claim—for example whether a sharp boundary exists whose location we are merely ignorant of, whether borderline predications are true, false, neither, both, or true to a degree, whether classical logic survives the loss of bivalence, whether vagueness is in our representation or in the world, and whether 'borderline' and 'definitely' talk can itself go vague at higher orders. Make sure at least these positions appear as leaves and are separated wherever separation is possible: epistemicism, supervaluationism (truth-value gaps with classical logic preserved), degree/continuum-valued theories, contextualism, and subvaluationism (truth-value gluts). Where two positions cannot be told apart by any diagnostic question you can devise, say so explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. Also mark the empty cells of the logical space: for each combination of answers no known position occupies, say briefly whether the combination is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

End with a scoring key (or, if you chose a tree, a brief summary of how each leaf is reached) and a short note stating how confident you are in the taxonomy and singling out the one separation or brute factual point you are least sure of.
