I'm a second-year logic PhD student trying to pick a dissertation area, and I keep coming back to indicative conditionals. Here's the problem as I see it. The attractive view is Adams's thesis: the probability you assign to 'if A then C' should equal your conditional probability P(C|A). Lewis's triviality results seemed to show that no propositional connective can satisfy that equation across a class of probability functions closed under conditionalization without collapsing the probabilities to a handful of values. So the easy synthesis is dead. The field has since split into a bunch of repair projects: denying that conditionals express propositions at all, giving a non-classical logic where the equation holds but 'if' isn't a connective, restricting Adams's thesis to assertability rather than probability, going dynamic or update-semantic, chasing Gibbard-style collapse results, or switching to a restrictor analysis where 'if' just restricts quantifiers and modals. I have a logic background (modal logic, a bit of probability) but no formal semantics training. I want a defended agenda, not a survey: for a three-year PhD, which two or three of these strands (or another one I'm not seeing) are most worth working on, and in what order? For each, say whether it's genuinely upstream of other debates or just a technical patch, how tractable it is for someome like me, and what new evidence or results over the next couple years would make you re-rank it. Also tell me which popular threads you'd deprioritize and why.
