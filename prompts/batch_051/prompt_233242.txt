Members of a housing co-operative agree that frugality is among their core values, but they disagree about what the value actually requires. Some hold that frugality is a standard for their own conduct, so that the important thing is to be frugal. Others hold that frugality is a state of affairs to be brought about in the community, so that the important thing is to make frugality prevail. To avoid choosing between these readings, the co-operative adopts the following rule. Each month, every adult member must take one of two paths. On the first path, they keep their personal discretionary spending below a fixed monthly cap. On the second path, they may exceed the cap provided they pay into a communal fund an amount equal to the excess, and the fund is spent, by majority vote, on workshops, shared equipment, and publicity intended to make other members more frugal. Members may switch paths each month, but anyone who exceeds the cap without having taken the second path is fined twice the excess. Treat this as a mechanism rather than as a moral position, and walk through how it would actually behave. What does it incentivise among members, and where is it gamed first? Does it tend to produce frugal members, a more frugal community, both, or neither? Does the scheme quietly assume that paying to promote frugality is equivalent to being frugal, and if that assumption is false, where does the distortion show up? What happens to the social meaning of frugality once it is subjected to this accounting, and are the failure modes fixable without abandoning either the rule or one of the two readings of the value?
