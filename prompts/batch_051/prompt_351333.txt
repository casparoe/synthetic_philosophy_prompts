You will construct a take-home final examination question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, for an upper-level undergraduate seminar titled Effective Altruism and Its Critics. The course has covered three critical positions: the institutional critique of effective altruism, the critique of earning to give, and the measurability critique.

The assessment constraints are fixed: take-home format, 48 hours, 1,200–1,500 words, and 25 marks total.

First, write the exam question. It must be a single essay question built around an original scenario of roughly 250–350 words, invented by you, in which a person faces a career choice between a high-earning, donation-oriented path and a lower-earning advocacy or policy path with diffuse, hard-to-measure outcomes. The question must require students to do all three of the following: (i) explain how each of the three critiques bears specifically on the scenario; (ii) identify and develop at least one substantive tension between two of the critiques as they apply to the case; and (iii) take a reasoned position on which critique, or which combination of them, should carry most weight in the decision, defending that weighting against the strongest objection to it. The question must be unanswerable by merely restating the positions; a student who has memorized the labels without being able to apply them should not be able to earn more than a low pass.

Inside the exam question, include a short paragraph that glosses the three critiques without quotations or citations, so that the exercise is self-contained. You may refine but not distort these glosses:
- Institutional critique: effective altruism concentrates on individual donor choices and discrete interventions while neglecting the background institutions, laws, and distributions of power that shape outcomes, so improving or transforming those structures may matter more than optimizing within them.
- Earning-to-give critique: the strategy of taking a high-paying job in order to donate large sums can be challenged because the position would likely be filled anyway (replaceability), because working in the industry implicates the earner in its harms, and because high-earning careers risk value drift or moral licensing.
- Measurability critique: a preference for interventions with strong quantified evidence can produce a streetlight effect, systematically favoring projects that are easy to measure over structural or advocacy work whose effects are diffuse, slow, or resistant to randomized evaluation, without showing that the measurable projects actually do more good.

Then write a model answer that a strong student could produce within the stated constraints. The model answer must show the reasoning rather than merely state conclusions; it must apply each critique to the particulars of the scenario; it must identify a genuine tension between the critiques and explain why that tension matters; and it must reach a defeasible verdict while addressing the strongest counterargument. Do not include a bibliography, named authors, or citations; the answer must stand on its own.

Then produce a marking scheme for the 25 marks, divided into five named criteria whose marks sum to 25. For each criterion, state the number of marks, the specific moves a top answer must make, and the specific mistakes that should lose marks, naming the confusion behind each mistake where relevant (for example, conflating the institutional critique with the measurability critique, or reducing the earning-to-give critique to a single psychological worry). The scheme should be detailed enough that two independent graders applying it would likely assign the same mark.

Return everything as a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- course_level: a string stating the course level.
- assessment_parameters: a string giving the take-home format, time allowed, word limit, and total marks.
- exam_question: a string containing the full question, including the scenario and the gloss paragraph.
- model_answer: a string containing the model answer.
- marking_scheme: an array of five objects, each with the following fields: criterion (string), marks (number), moves_top_answers_make (string), and errors_that_lose_marks (string).

No other top-level fields should appear.
