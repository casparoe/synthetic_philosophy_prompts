For a unit I'm teaching on determinism and predictability, I've been keeping a running list of my physicist friend Dana's offhand rulings. She tends to answer "is this system predictable?" in one word, so the file is cleaner than it should be. Here's what she's said:

- a pendulum making small swings: predictable.
- a double pendulum, asked where it'll be ten seconds after release: not predictable.
- a coin toss, called while the coin is still in the air: not predictable.
- the weather, asked for the high temperature in a particular city three weeks out: not predictable.
- the orbits of the inner planets, asked a couple hundred million years out: not predictable.
- next year's tide tables: predictable.

Now she's been asked about a made-up but well-defined case: a gravitational three-body system that is definitely chaotic, but in which a tiny error in the initial state doubles only every 30,000 years. The question is whether you can predict the position of one of the bodies 200 years out. I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she's implicitly running. What are the plausible rules that fit her track record so far, what would each of them say about the new case, what's your best guess about her actual verdict, and what further case would best tell the candidate rules apart? I suspect her old rulings don't settle it, but I'd like a careful prediction rather than a shrug.
