i've been trying to work out what i actually believe about deciding under ignorance (you know the possible outcomes but have no probabilities at all) and about fairness, because to me they feel like versions of the same problem. ive ended up with a list of commitments and i want someone to check whether the whole set hangs together, because i genuinely cant see where the weak spot is. here they are:

1. when there are no probabilities, i go maximin: pick the option whose worst possible outcome is best.

2. but for big one-off life choices i catch myself thinking in terms of regret instead. i compare how much i'd kick myself under each possible outcome, and minimax regret (pick the option whose worst-case regret is smallest) feels just as right.

3. i think regret should be measured against the best outcome you could have gotten in the same state of the world, not against some other state.

4. adding an option i would never pick anyway shouldnt flip my ranking of the options i already had.

5. for fairness across people, i hold the difference principle: the best system is the one where the worst-off group ends up as well-off as possible.

6. but if there is a literal equal-odds lottery over positions in a society, then i think the right system is the one with the highest average welfare.

what i want is an audit, not a lecture. go through and tell me which pairs (or triples) are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by how severe they are, and for each say what would have to give and roughly what it would cost me. then tell me which single revision would leave the most coherent overall view.
