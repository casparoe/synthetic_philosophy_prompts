Please explain a conceptual difficulty internal to the doctrine of sola scriptura when it is combined with the standard hermeneutical rule that unclear passages should be interpreted in light of clear ones. Walk through the following worry: in deciding which passages are clear and which clarify which, the interpreter must already apply some criterion of fit, relevance, or theological coherence, and an appeal to the text itself cannot settle what that criterion should be without presupposing the very interpretive judgment at issue. Distinguish, as clearly as you can, the claim that a text has a determinate meaning from the claim that there is a non-circular procedure for resolving disputes about that meaning. Then assess whether the appeal to the perspicuity of Scripture—the doctrine that Scripture is clear at least in what is necessary for salvation—genuinely halts the regress, or whether it merely relocates it from particular passages to the criterion by which perspicuity itself is recognized.
