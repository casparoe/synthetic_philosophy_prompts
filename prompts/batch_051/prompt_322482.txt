Before I read another proposal about how to assign responsibility for harms caused by autonomous systems, I want the specification that any adequate proposal must satisfy. Write the desiderata: what a theory of responsibility for autonomous systems has to deliver if it is to ground legal liability and deployment policy. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and state whether the requirement is negotiable or non-negotiable. Your list should cover at least the following ground, and you should add anything I have omitted.

First, classification of core cases. The theory must classify each of these without absurdity, and it must say what each case is a test of: a wholly manual system in which a human operator makes the decision that causes the harm; a narrow-domain autonomous system such as a high-frequency trading algorithm; a continuously learning system whose post-deployment behavior departs from what its testers observed; a system whose specific failure no designer could have foreseen; a failure that emerges from interactions among components built by different teams; and a case in which no human is in the loop at the time of the harm.

Second, distinctions the theory cannot collapse: causal responsibility, moral culpability, legal liability, and the obligation to compensate or repair; ex ante questions about what may be deployed and ex post questions about who answers after a harm; and the system's unpredictability versus any human being's culpability for that unpredictability.

Third, constraints from engineering and from legal practice. The theory must be consistent with the fact that learned systems can act on their training data and reward signal in ways no one enumerated in advance, and that retracing a specific decision can be computationally infeasible or even undefined. It must also say how it handles legal tools such as strict liability and vicarious liability, which do not require a guilty mind, even if its answer is that one of them should be abandoned.

Then do three things briefly. Flag the pair or subset of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and state the tension. Score the main families of views — those that locate responsibility in the human overseer, in the manufacturer or deployer, or in the system itself as a responsible agent — against the list. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most work separating adequate from inadequate views, and say why.
