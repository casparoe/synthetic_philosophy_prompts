I'm a PhD student in political theory working on the ethics of armed intervention, and I'd like you to analyze the 2011 NATO intervention in Libya through the lens of the Responsibility to Protect doctrine. I already know the chronology and the standard commentary: the Security Council's Resolution 1973, its authorization of all necessary measures to protect civilians while excluding a foreign occupation force, the movement from a no-fly zone to direct air support for the rebellion, and Gaddafi's fall in October 2011. What I want is a disciplined conceptual mapping rather than a recap. Treat the doctrine as having three pillars: the state's primary responsibility to protect its own population; the international community's responsibility to assist states in meeting that responsibility; and the responsibility to take collective action through the Security Council when a state manifestly fails to protect its population. Ask what each pillar actually licensed in the Libyan case and identify the point at which the operation outran the doctrine's own categories. Finally, run the analysis in the other direction: tell me what the case shows about the doctrine. Should Libya count as a misapplication of a sound doctrine, a case the doctrine never anticipated, or evidence that the line between protection and regime change was never as clean as its framers claimed?
