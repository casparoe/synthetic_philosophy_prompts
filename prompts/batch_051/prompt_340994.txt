I'm compiling a list of the standard ways in which an infinite regress can be vicious, for a paper I'm writing on what gives regress arguments their force. So far I have three entries: (1) the regress, together with the other premises, entails a contradiction; (2) the regress merely postpones the explanation, because each step appeals to an item of the very kind that was supposed to be explained, so the original item is never accounted for; and (3) the regress conflicts with an independent ban on infinite descending chains of the relevant relation, such as dependence, priority, causation, or justification. I would like you to give me one more criterion for viciousness that is not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of these three, and to say how it differs from them. If you think the standard list is already complete and the request for a further criterion rests on a mistake, please say so plainly rather than inventing a pseudo-criterion, and explain what that would tell us about the way regress arguments actually do their work.
