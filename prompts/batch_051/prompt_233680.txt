I serve on the governing board of a national museum of the history of mass violence, which is preparing its first permanent exhibition. The curators want the galleries organized comparatively: paired rooms on, for instance, the Armenian genocide, the Holodomor, the Holocaust, Cambodia under the Khmer Rouge, the Rwandan genocide, and the Srebrenica genocide, with wall text comparing perpetrator ideology, methods, scale, and bystander response across cases. Organizations representing survivors and descendants on our advisory council are urging us to reject the framework outright, on the ground that setting these events side by side — let alone ranking them — turns the dead into a data set and erases what is singular in each destruction. Other members answer that visitors already compare these events informally, and that the museum's responsibility is to make such comparisons exact and explicit rather than leave them to prejudice. Please prepare the decision memo for the board. It should lead with a one-sentence recommendation we could vote on verbatim — adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions — then give the reasoning, engage the strongest argument on the losing side honestly, and state any conditions the curators would have to meet. Limit it to 800 words; no footnotes or references.
