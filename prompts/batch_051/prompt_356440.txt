Ive been teaching Leviathan for the first time and got into a fight with a colleague about what Hobbes is actually doing in the social contract argument. We both read the bit in chapter 14 where he says a covenant made out of fear is still binding, because if fear voided covenants the original compact that sets up the commonwealth would be void too, since it is made from mutual fear. Then we stalled. The secondary stuff seems to have been stuck on this for decades too: one camp treats the state of nature as a factual claim about how people actually would behave, another treats it as a rational reconstruction of authorization, and a third says it is not an argument at all but a bit of rhetoric. Everyone accepts the same text and the same examples. My working theory is that the debate is not about Hobbes at all but about what kind of thing a political obligation is supposed to be. Diagnose this impasse: is it a verbal disagreement, a difference in background assumptions, or an ill-posed question? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a formal model, a change of question — or is there no reason to expect any? I want a diagnosis, not a survey.
