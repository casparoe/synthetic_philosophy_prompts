Last night a friend and I argued at length about Arendt on political lying, and he ended by leaving me with an argument I cannot get out of. As I understand her, Arendt holds three things: (1) politics is the realm of opinion and plurality, in which claims are advanced and contested by persuasion and nothing simply compels assent; (2) political action requires a shared factual world as its stage; (3) the modern political lie — the image-making lie that constructs a coherent false account of events rather than merely hiding one — is uniquely dangerous because it dissolves that shared factual world. My friend reasoned as follows: (4) if (2) and (3) are right, then some factual claims must stand outside the realm of opinion, fixed and not open to being spun, since otherwise there would be no shared factual world for the lie to dissolve; (5) but then politics is not in fact the realm of opinion stated in (1), and on (1)'s own terms the image-maker is not corrupting politics but doing it supremely well; (6) so Arendt cannot keep (1), (2), and (3) together, and whichever she gives up costs her most of the position. If forced, I would surrender the strong reading of (1) before losing (3), but I cannot see what that move would cost. Which step do you reject, and what does it cost?
