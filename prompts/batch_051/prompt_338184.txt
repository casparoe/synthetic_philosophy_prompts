My study group was going over an epistemology handout and one of them summarized Nozick's tracking theory like this, and I'm not sure whether to trust it:

"Nozick's idea is that knowledge is true belief that tracks the truth. You know p only if you'd believe p if p were true, and you wouldn't believe p if p were false. That's all there is to his analysis — no separate justification condition. This leads him to deny closure under known entailment. Take 'I have hands.' If you didn't have hands, your experience would be different, so you wouldn't believe you had hands; so you know it. But take 'I'm not a brain in a vat.' If you were a brain in a vat, everything would seem exactly the same and you'd still believe you weren't one; so you don't know it. Since 'I have hands' entails 'I'm not a brain in a vat,' and you know the first but not the second, Nozick has to say you can know something and know that it entails something else without knowing the thing it entails. Since knowledge is just obviously closed under logical consequence, this is generally regarded as a refutation of the whole account."

I'm inclined to think the last clause is doing alot of work, but I can't tell whether the rest is a fair rendering. Go sentence by sentence and tell me what a Nozickian would accept, what they'd wince at, and whether the 'refutation' conclusion actually follows.
