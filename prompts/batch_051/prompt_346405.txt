I've been arguing with two friends about whether you can literally buy your way out of a wrong you've already committed — not just pay compensation to the victim, but buy a "credit" for the wrongness itself, the way you'd buy a carbon offset. We've been half-jokingly calling it "moral trade," and we ended up stuck on the question of whether duties are the kind of thing that can be traded at all. I write for a small philosophy humor zine, and the next issue is on markets in everything, so this seems like the moment.

I'd like you to write a deadpan mock terms-of-service agreement for a startup that lets users buy and sell moral obligations: pay someone to keep a promise you made, sell the right to break a minor promise, buy "atonement offsets," bundle small duties into portfolios, that sort of thing. The company can have some plausible fintech name, like Dutable or Atonement.ly. The humor has to come from getting the actual ethics right, not from being silly — a specialist should recognize that you actually understand the arguments. Work in these worries without naming or explaining them in the piece: whether all obligations share a common denominator; that some duties may only be owed by a particular person to a particular person; the risk that one good deed gets counted twice; the difference between perfect and imperfect duties and how the platform treats each; moral licensing, where buying an offset makes people behave worse afterward; and an information problem about whether a seller genuinely intends to perform the duty. Let them surface in how the clauses operate, not as abstract talk.

Make it read like a real terms-of-service: definitions, eligibility requirements, account tiers, a valuation or exchange-rate mechanism, dispute resolution, a refund policy for "tainted credits," an arbitration clause, and something on what happens when a duty defaults. Around 700–800 words. Keep it deadpan the whole way — no winking, and a reader should be able to tell whats being ironized without you ever saying it outright. The target is the practice of moral offsetting and the idea that duties are fungible commodities, not the customers. Please start with the piece itself, no preface or commentary.
