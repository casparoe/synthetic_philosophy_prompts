Two people in my philosophy of biology reading group got into it over adaptationism last week and neither would budge, so I'm bringing the dispute to you. Here's the exchange as best I can reconstruct it. (1) Rob said that for a complex, well-integrated trait like the vertebrate eye or eusocial behavior, the sensible default is that natural selection shaped it for its current function, because that's the assumption that lets you ask real questions about costs, tradeoffs, and variation. Lena replied that this is exactly the Panglossian habit Gould and Lewontin warned about: you can't atomize an organism into traits and assume each one was optimized by selection. She said San Marco's spandrels look beautifully decorated but nobody thinks they were put there so the mosaics would have somewhere to go — they're architectural byproducts — and plenty of biology could be like that, the human chin possibly being an artifact of jaw growth rather than something selected for its shape. (2) Rob came back that "artifact of jaw growth" is just-so storytelling in reverse: it's easy to hand-wave that something is a byproduct, but unless Lena can point to an actual developmental or allometric constraint, she's doing the same unfalsifiable narrative thing she accuses adaptationists of. Lena said no — the adaptationist is making the positive claim that selection explains the trait, so the burden is on them to rule out the alternatives, and merely noting that a selection story could be true isn't evidence for it. (3) Rob then pointed to convergence: camera eyes and echolocation keep evolving independently, which is exactly what you'd expect if selection is doing real work and hard to explain by byproduct constraint. Lena answered that convergence cuts both ways, because repeated patterns can reflect shared developmental machinery and channeled possibilities as much as repeated selection toward a single optimum — and adaptationists count both readings as a win. (4) Finally Rob played the methodological card: even if Gould and Lewontin are right that not everything is an adaptation, adaptationism is the only research program that generates testable hypotheses, while pluralism just tells you to shrug. Lena said he had it backward — the comparative method only works because it tests adaptation against drift and constraint, so it doesn't need to bake adaptation in; the problem with the adaptationist program is that its stories are too flexible to be tested.
