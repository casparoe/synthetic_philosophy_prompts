I have been trying to give a precise statement of a deflationary view about what is lost when a community dissolves — say, a neighborhood, a congregation, or a long-standing voluntary association. The view is that such a dissolution is not a further loss over and above the losses borne by the individual members and by the particular pairs of people whose relations to one another change. A colleague has said that I am smuggling in a reductionist step, so I have set the argument out as carefully as I can:

(1) A community is constituted by its members and by the relationships among those members.
(2) If a community is constituted by its members and their relationships, then every fact about the community — including facts about its value and about what would be lost if it dissolved — is fixed by facts about its members and their relationships.
(3) Facts about the members and their relationships are just facts about individual persons and facts about the ties between pairs of persons.
(4) Therefore, every fact about the value of a community, and about what is lost when one dissolves, is fixed by facts about individual persons and about pairwise ties.
(5) Consequently, the dissolution of a community is a loss only insofar as it changes facts about individual persons or about pairwise ties.
(6) Therefore, when a community dissolves, nothing is lost over and above what is lost by individual members and what is lost within particular pairwise relationships.

I suspect the argument turns on a slide in the phrase "the relationships among the members," but I cannot say exactly where the slide occurs or whether the argument is invalid rather than merely unsound. Is the argument valid? If it is invalid, identify the first step at which the inference fails and specify the two readings of the relevant phrase. If you think the argument can be made valid by holding one reading fixed, say which premise then becomes the weak one, give the strongest version of the objection to it, and say what the argument would still fail to show.
