Me and a friend spent last night's walk home arguing about consent, and he backed me into this argument. I'm pretty sure it proves too much, but I can't find the step that fails.

(1) Consent is valid only if it's informed.
(2) You're not informed if the other person has left you with a false belief about something material to what you're agreeing to.
(3) A fact is material just when the person wouldn't have consented if they'd known it.
(4) By that test, almost nobody ever validly consents to anything serious. If I tell someone I want a relationship but I only want a hookup, then on this test they didn't consent to the sex. My sister would not have bought the car if she'd known the previous owner smoked in it, but the seller didn't mention it. The guy I hired wouldn't have taken the job if he'd known I was about to quit, and I let him believe I'd be there. A patient who would refuse a filling if she knew the dentist was on probation hasn't consented either, even though nobody lied and the work was fine.
(5) So either almost all sex, jobs, purchases, and medical care happen without valid consent, or one of (1)–(3) has to go.
(6) He says he can live with the sexual conclusion but not the rest, which seems like he's drawing a line he hasn't stated.

Where does the argument go wrong, if it does? My instinct is to keep (1) and (2) and say something has to be wrong with the dealbreaker test in (3), but my friend says that's just special pleading for whichever lies we've decided are normal. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
