I am at work on a novel about a small, isolated community whose entire political order consists of a written rulebook of the Nomic type: the rulebook contains, among its ordinary rules, a rule specifying how the rulebook's rules may be amended, and this change-procedure rule is itself listed among the changeable rules rather than being fixed. One of my characters, a jurist, contends that this arrangement is subtly incoherent, and before I let him persuade the assembly I want to know whether his reasoning is sound. He argues as follows.

(1) The rule of the rulebook that specifies the procedure for changing rules is itself a rule of the rulebook.
(2) No rule of the rulebook is marked as immune to alteration; hence the change-procedure rule may itself be amended.
(3) Every valid amendment to any rule in the rulebook is valid only if it is made in accordance with the change-procedure rule.
(4) Therefore, an amendment to the change-procedure rule would be valid only if it is made in accordance with the change-procedure rule itself.
(5) But a rule cannot supply the standard of validity for an act that alters that very rule; to suppose otherwise is circular, because the act would owe its authority to the rule even while the rule's altered content would owe its existence to the act.
(6) Therefore the change-procedure rule cannot be validly amended.
(7) Since step (2) says it can be amended, the rulebook is inconsistent.

I suspect the trouble lies somewhere around step (5), but I cannot articulate exactly what it is: whether it equivocates between the rule as it stands before the amendment and the rule as it is afterward, or whether it smuggles in the unwarranted assumption that a standard must be external to what it measures. Is the jurist's argument sound? If not, at which numbered step does it fail, and under what exact reading of that step? I need the flaw, if there is one, specified precisely enough that another character could refute him in dialogue rather than merely gesture at self-reference. If the argument is sound, then what should the community's rulebook say instead to avoid the inconsistency?
