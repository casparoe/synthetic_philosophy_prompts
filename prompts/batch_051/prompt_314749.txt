I keep reconstructing the following argument and cannot see which step fails, though the conclusion is plainly absurd. (1) The map n ↦ 2n puts the natural numbers into one-to-one correspondence with the even numbers, so the two sets have the same cardinality: |ℕ| = |E|. (2) The natural numbers are the disjoint union of the evens and the odds, so |ℕ| = |E| + |O|. (3) Substituting (1) into (2) gives |E| + |O| = |E|; ordinary algebra then permits cancelling |E|, yielding |O| = 0. (4) But there are odd numbers. (5) Because (1) is merely Cantor's correspondence and (2) merely records a partition, the misstep must be (3). Yet rejecting (3) appears to mean that, when dealing with infinite cardinals, an elementary algebraic move like cancellation is no longer valid. That looks less like saving Cantor than like a reductio of actual infinities, and the only stable alternative seems to be finitism, which I find too costly. If I must choose, I would rather keep (1) and abandon whatever in (3) is illicit, but I want to know exactly what the loss amounts to. Which step do you reject, and why did that step look so innocuous?
