A teacher announces to her class on a Friday afternoon that next week there will be exactly one exam, on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday at 9:00 a.m., and that on the evening before the exam the students will not be in a position to know that the exam will occur the next morning. A student accepts the announcement and, on Sunday evening, reasons as follows.

(1) The exam cannot be on Friday. Suppose it were. Then, if no exam had occurred by Thursday evening, Friday would be the only remaining day, and the student would be able to infer from the announcement that the exam is on Friday. The student would then be in a position to know on Thursday evening that the exam is the next morning, contrary to the announcement. Friday is therefore ruled out.

(2) The exam cannot be on Thursday. With Friday ruled out, suppose it were Thursday. Then, if no exam had occurred by Wednesday evening, Thursday would be the only remaining day among those not yet eliminated, and the student would be able to infer from the announcement that the exam is on Thursday. The student would again be in a position to know on the preceding evening that the exam is the next morning, contrary to the announcement. Thursday is therefore ruled out.

(3) The same form of reasoning eliminates Wednesday, Tuesday, and Monday in turn: on each evening, every later day has already been eliminated by an earlier application of the same argument, so the next morning is the only remaining candidate, and the announcement would allow the student to identify it as the day of the exam in advance.

(4) Therefore no day next week can satisfy the announcement, and the announcement cannot be true.

In the event, the teacher gives the exam on Wednesday morning. The student, having concluded that no exam could occur, is genuinely taken by surprise: on Tuesday evening she is not in a position to know that the exam will be the next morning. The announcement turns out to have been exactly correct.

Is the reasoning in (1) through (4) valid? If it is valid, explain how a true announcement can force the conclusion that it is false. If it is not valid, identify the first step that fails, state the suppressed premise on which that step depends, and explain why that premise is unavailable to the student in the counterfactual case the step considers. Please answer in American English.
