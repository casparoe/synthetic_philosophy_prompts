Me and a friend just spent an hour arguing about akrasia, and I'm left with a bunch of case-verdicts I can't fit together. He was pushing that weakness of will is exaclty steep hyperbolic discounting — that the January dieter and the snooze-button hitter are the same as someone who'd take $90 today over $100 in a year. But my judgments feel messier. Deciding in January to quit sugar, then eating cake at a friend's February birthday: weak-willed, even though the birthday gives me a reason I didn't have in January. Pre-committing to a non-refundable trip because I know future-me will chicken out: not weak-willed, maybe the opposite, but it only makes sense because future-me can't be trusted. Taking the $90 today when I'd have said $100-in-a-year from any distance: weak-willed, and the delay seems to be doing real work. Snoozing the alarm three times: weak-willed but kind of automatic, no real moment of decision. Deciding at 25 I don't want kids and at 35 wanting them: not weak-willed, the person just changed. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or at least most, and what does it say about precommitment and about changing my mind mid-temptation? Which of my verdicts should I be most willing to drop?
