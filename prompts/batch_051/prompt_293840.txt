Okay, so my friend and I just spent an hour arguing about a review site we both read, and I need someone to actually work through the question instead of just picking sides. The site's review policy has said, pretty much since it launched back around 2011: "Reviews must reflect the reviewer's own first-hand experience of the work. We remove reviews based on trailers, excerpts, or the opinions of others." Last month a user posted a one-star review of a branching narrative game after watching a streamer play through every branch and ending in real time, over about forty hours. They never touched the controls. I say that's still a second-hand experience of the work, because the game is about the choices and the reviewer only saw someone else make them; my friend says at that point you've seen the whole aesthetic object, so the review is as first-hand as anyone's. The mods are split on weather "own first-hand experience" covers watching the entire thing. The policy was obviously written before full playthrough streams were a thing, so its authors never had this case in mind. Take it from the wording, the point of the rule, what the authors would probably say if you asked them now, and what a reasonable user reading the policy would expect — and tell me which of those should decide whether the review stays up.
