I teach introductory university physics, and every semester I see the same wrong answers: "a constant net force is needed to keep a cart moving at constant velocity," "heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones in a vacuum," and "a book resting on a table has no forces acting on it." I understand there is a genuine research dispute about what such errors are: some treat them as stable, theory-like misconceptions that must be elicited and replaced; others treat them as fine-grained intuitions (sometimes called p-prims) that work in some contexts and get cued in the wrong one; still others treat them as artifacts of framing, since the same student answers differently when the problem is posed differently. I want a diagnostic procedure I can actually use while grading or in office hours — a few minutes per answer, written work plus at most one or two follow-up questions, no clinical interview — that classifies an error as a coherent alternative theory, a miscued primitive, a framing effect, or a one-off slip, because my choice of remediation depends on which it is. Write it as an ordered checklist: for each step, state the question I ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to continue or stop, and where the step is a judgment call rather than an observation; keep the research distinctions that change what an instructor would do and drop those that do not. Then run the procedure on the three errors above, including at least one case where it returns a classification I am likely to resist, and end by stating what the procedure cannot discriminate.
