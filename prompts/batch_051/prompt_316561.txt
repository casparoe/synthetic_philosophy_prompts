A foundation that funds controversial research will decide its hardest cases by a procedure modeled on Thomson's lamp. A ball starts in a box marked FUND. Thirty seconds before noon, the first reviewer may move the ball to a box marked REJECT if she has an unresolved objection, and otherwise leaves it. Fifteen seconds before noon, the second reviewer may move it to the other box under the same condition; at 7.5 seconds, the third; then at 3.75 seconds, and so on, halving the interval each time, so that infinitely many reviewers have acted by noon. The box containing the ball at noon is recorded as the final decision. Analyze how this mechanism would behave in practice: under what conditions is the noon decision determinate; how could an applicant or the committee chair exploit the ordering of reviewers and the classification of objections to force FUND, force REJECT, or make the decision indeterminate; and which failure modes, if any, could be fixed without dropping the infinite structure?
