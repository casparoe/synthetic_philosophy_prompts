I mostly cover risk and decision-making for a science-and-ideas desk, so when my city passed a new downtown safety ordinance after a street-racing crash killed two people, I started following it less as a crime story than as a live case study in how much risk a society is willing to trade away before ordinary life becomes the threat. The council members were explicit about the aim: the racers would stop, they argued, if the crowds that gather to watch them disappeared, so the city made it an offense to be on the sidewalk in the Entertainment District between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. unless you were patronizing a business there. The clause I keep coming back to reads: "No person shall stand, sit, loiter, or assemble on any public sidewalk, plaza, or other pedestrian way between the hours of 11:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m. within the Downtown Entertainment District, except while actively entering or exiting a lawfully open business."

Last week I was out on Garnett Street reporting on how the law was actually being enforced, and I watched a hospital custodian get cited at a bus stop. Her shift ends at 11:30; the 11:40 bus is the only one that gets her home; and the stop sits on a sidewalk plainly inside the district. The officer's line afterwards was that waiting for a bus is not "actively entering or exiting a lawfully open business," so the ordinance covers her. I'm not looking for a legal prediction. I want you to work through what the clause would have to mean for her to be in violation on each approach—the plain text, the council's stated purpose, what members would have said if someone had raised night-shift workers and bus stops, and what a reasonable officer on the street should understand it to mean—and to say which of those readings also makes the law coherent as an instrument for preventing street racing. The bigger question I'm circling: when a city starts treating ordinary nighttime activity as the thing to be secured against, has it drawn a line between tolerable risk and sacrificed freedom, or has it just confused risk with presence?
