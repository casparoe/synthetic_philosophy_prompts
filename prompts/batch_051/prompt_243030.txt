Consider how public memory and biography treat three figures. Thomas Jefferson is widely remembered as a defender of liberty and equality who nonetheless held people in slavery; in most serious assessments that fact is treated as a grave defect, but one softened by the observation that he was a man of his time. Winston Churchill's expressions of racial hierarchy and his attachment to empire tend to be handled in much the same way: noted, regretted, and then set to one side as typical of his age rather than the heart of his achievement. Yet when it comes to Christopher Columbus, the same move carries far less weight: the enslavement and violence that followed his voyages are more likely to be judged as straightforwardly condemnable, and the remark that he too was shaped by fifteenth-century norms is often felt to be an evasion.

Take this uneven pattern as an existing practice: a way public judgement distributes exculpation across historical figures. What theory of moral responsibility does this practice presuppose? In particular, does the phrase "a man of his time" function as an excuse, a mitigation, or a denial that the agent is answerable to our standards at all? Does the practice commit us to holding that moral requirements are indexed to an era, or only to holding that blame should be tempered by non-culpable ignorance of moral truths the agent could not reasonably have reached? And what should the practice say about the presence, in each case, of contemporaries who did not share the supposed blindness — abolitionists in Jefferson's America, critics of empire in Churchill's Britain, Spanish critics of the conquest in the generation after Columbus such as Bartolomé de las Casas? Does their existence show that the moral facts were available, so the defence should collapse, or can a consistent version of the practice allow that some people are ahead of their time without thereby indicting everyone else? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with, and say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined.
