"People who say history bends toward justice mean that, over the long run, human societies become more just, and that this is more or less inevitable. On this view history has a built-in direction: slavery, empire, and oppression are not just wrong but historically doomed, because the universe is on the side of justice. The job of activists is therefore not to create justice but to speed up an outcome that is already guaranteed. Critics point out that the twentieth century alone refutes the whole idea — if history had a moral direction, the Holocaust would have been impossible — so the view is just wishful thinking dressed up as history."

I suspect this is a bit of a caricature, but I can't tell how much of it is a fair reading of a real position and how much is the podcaster attacking a strawman. My hunch is that he's running together three different claims: an empirical claim about moral progress, a teleological or providential claim that history is guaranteed to end justly, and a practical slogan about what activism is for. Am I right? Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me which parts a serious defender would accept, which are subtle distortions, and whether the twentieth-century objection refutes anything except the strongest version?
