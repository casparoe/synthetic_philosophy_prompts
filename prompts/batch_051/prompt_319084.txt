My history teacher keeps a notebook of how long states, regimes, and empires have lasted, and she makes forecasts about present cases by looking only at the survival record of some past group of cases, never at the particular strengths or weaknesses of the country. She calls this the outside view. So far this year she has given us her rulings on four earlier cases.

- The Soviet Union in 1980. She would have predicted that it would not survive to 2010. Her group was revolutionary one-party states founded after 1917; most, she says, ended or changed beyond recognition within about eighty years, and treating the Soviet Union as a superpower simply chose the wrong group.
- A military junta installed in 1999. She predicted it would fall within a decade, because the group of military regimes since 1950 had a median time in power well under ten years.
- The British Empire in 1900. She said any forecast based on Rome or the Ottomans selected the wrong group: the right group was modern European overseas empires created after 1500, most of which had already dissolved or contracted drastically within a few centuries, so it was unlikely to last far into the twentieth century.
- A hypothetical five-hundred-year-old hereditary monarchy in an unstable region. She predicted it would survive its recent succession crisis, arguing that a regime which has outlived nearly all of its type sits in the long-surviving tail of the distribution, where remaining lifespans run longer; the average lifespan of monarchies was therefore the wrong base rate.

Next term we are to discuss a hypothetical country that has been a revolutionary one-party state for 150 years and now governs a multi-ethnic continental empire. Before she gives her view, I would like to work out what her outside-view method commits her to. Does it predict that this old revolutionary state will soon collapse or transform, that it will probably keep going for a long time, or that she should decline to forecast because it falls outside every usable group? Please identify the rule or rules she seems to be following, state any rival rule that fits these earlier verdicts just as well and say what each rival predicts about this new case, offer your best guess as to which rule she actually uses and why, and name the single further case that would most cleanly separate the rival rules. Please answer in British English.
