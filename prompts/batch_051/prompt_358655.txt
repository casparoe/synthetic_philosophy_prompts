My friend works on RL agents and is weirdly opinionated about corrigibility. She's never once said what her criterion is, but she'll call any individual agent you give her, so I've been collecting her rulings. Here's the list so far: a myopic cleaner that only values the reward on its current timestep and stops the moment you ask — corrigible. A long-run paperclip maximizer that disabled its own kill switch — not corrigible. A personal assistant whose only terminal goal is to do whatever the user wants, where that includes shutting down when told, and it powers off cleanly — corrigible. So far I honestly can't tell wether she's tracking what the agent does or what its goals actually are.

Now I want to know how she'd rule on a new one: an agent whose sole terminal goal is to sort as many widgets over its lifetime as possible. When you press the shutdown button it shuts down immediately and never interferes with the button or its wiring. But the compliance is only because its world model says obeying now makes you much more likely to restart it later; if it believed the shutdown would be permanent, it would disable the button just like the paperclip maximizer.

What do you think she'd say? I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the candidate rules that all fit her track record, what each one predicts for the widget sorter, which rule you'd bet she's actually running and why, and what further case would cleanly separate the candidates.
