This spring I'm running a senior seminar on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, cross-listed with religious studies, and the take-home final is the one assignment I have left to write. About a third of the students arrived already fluent in Genesis 22, which has made them slightly too comfortable with the story and slightly too lazy about reading the actual argument. We have spent three weeks on the first of the three problemata, and the practice essays all make the same noise: faith is a paradox, the single individual is higher than the universal, Abraham is not a tragic hero. I want a single question that forces them to do more than sort those labels into sentences; this year the crux should be whether the teleological suspension of the ethical can be distinguished from simply doing away with the ethical, and whether the tragic-hero contrast genuinely does the work of securing that distinction.

To make the question fair, they may take the following as established in class: the ethical is the universal; the tragic hero suspends one ethical duty only in favor of a higher ethical duty and so remains inside the ethical, where the community can still understand and even weep; Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac points toward no higher ethical end, so from inside the ethical it can only be murder, and his justification, an absolute relation to God, cannot be mediated or spoken. They may also draw on the knight-of-faith material if they wish, the double movement in which Abraham both resigns Isaac and expects to receive him back by virtue of the absurd, but that material should not become a substitute for facing the problem head-on.

Please write the package as the final: (1) the exam question itself, at most 100 words; (2) a model answer of at most 600 words, the sort a strong senior would actually produce, not a general meditation on Kierkegaard; and (3) a marking scheme totaling 20 marks that names the specific moves a first-class answer must make, the marks each is worth, and a one-line note on how weaker candidates characteristically miss that move.
