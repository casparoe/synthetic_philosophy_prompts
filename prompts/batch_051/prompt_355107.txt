I am a philosophy professor chairing a Q&A session at a philosophy of technology seminar. The speaker has been defending the thesis that Isaac Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics, read retrospectively as an unsystematic early proposal for what we now call AI alignment, fail for instructive reasons but nonetheless get one thing importantly right: safety is better implemented as a lexicographic hierarchy of constraints (one in which lower-ranked considerations are simply irrelevant unless higher-ranked ones are satisfied) than as a single objective to be traded off, with harm-avoidance ranked above obedience and self-maintenance. She is therefore committed to the view that the problem with the Laws is one of specification, not of structure.

I want you to draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate that thesis. At least one question should address each of the following: (i) whether the purported structural insight is substantive once harm and human being are left unspecified; (ii) the First Law's inaction clause, and how she prevents it from turning every robot into a totalising harm-preventer; (iii) the Second Law's exposure to malicious, negligent, or merely unwise orders, and the circularity involved in telling a robot not to obey harmful orders; (iv) Asimov's later Zeroth Law, and whether shifting protection from the individual human being to humanity as a whole solves the specification problem or merely relocates it; and (v) the fact that, in the stories themselves, conflicts are often resolved by the robot's interpreting, extending, or effectively revising the laws, which suggests the real work is done by judgement rather than by the rule hierarchy. For each question, include the follow-up you would ask once she has given the reply she is most likely to give, and ensure that at least one follow-up presses her on the difference between a hard constraint and an objective with a large penalty. Conclude with a question about what evidence would show that her structural reading of the Laws is untenable rather than merely incomplete. Please write your answer in British English.
