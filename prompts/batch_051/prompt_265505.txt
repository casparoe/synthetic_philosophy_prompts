I would like you to draft the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between two metaphysicians of music could both sign. The first position is a Platonism about musical works: musical works are independently existing abstract sound structures, composers discover or select them rather than bring them into being, performances are sound events that exemplify the structure, and the identity of a work is fixed entirely by its full sonic profile, with instrumentation and historical provenance mattering only insofar as they make an audible difference. The second position is a creationism about musical works: musical works are created abstract artefacts, brought into being by a composer's act of indicating a sound structure in a particular musical-historical context, and the identity of the work includes that context together with the intended means of sound production, even where a different means would have produced the same audible result.

The document should contain, in order, the following elements. First, a numbered list of substantive claims both parties accept: claims about the type-token structure of performance, about scores as specifications rather than the works themselves, about defective performances versus sound events that do not count as instances, and about the status of recordings. Second, a disagreements table in which each row states a single point of difference in neutral language and then gives, side by side, the wording each party prefers for its own view. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side, each formulated so that both parties agree it is well posed. Fourth, for each crux, the argument or concrete case both parties agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

The document should not rest at the usual slogans. Press toward precision using the following test cases: a pop recording assembled entirely in the studio, which has never existed as a live performance; a note-for-note transcription of a piano work for harpsichord that is auditorily indistinguishable from the original; a faithful performance taken at a radically slower tempo; a notationally identical score composed independently in a different era and tradition; and a generative work whose authorized realizations differ on every run. End by stating whether any part of the residual disagreement is merely verbal, and if so, which terminological stipulation would dissolve that part and which portion of the dispute would remain live afterward.
