I teach a philosophy seminar on minds and machines, and this year the students are mostly ex-systems people — they think natively in terms of hypervisors, VMs, snapshots, live migration, quiescing, fork/clone, failover, process identity versus image identity. I want to run the whole-brain emulation debate through their vocabulary, and I want the translation to be honest rather than a sales pitch for either side.

Here's the territory. Whole-brain emulation, as I'll use the term, is the project of scanning a person's brain finely enough, reconstructing its causal machinery (neurons, synapses, ion channels, whatever turns out to carry the signal), and running that reconstruction on some other substrate. The philosophical fight splits into two questions. First, fidelity: what does finely enough amount to, and can you ever know you achieved it? Second, identity: is a running emulation the same mind as the scanned person — a continuation, a copy, a different person, or just a model of one?

Please restate each of the following positions in systems vocabulary, one by one: substrate independence (the mind is the pattern of causal organization, so a faithful emulation preserves it), biological chauvinism (the wetware matters and computation is only a simulation), Chalmers-style fading qualia with piecemeal neuron replacement, psychological-continuity views of personal identity, and the branching problem (the original person still alive while the copy runs, or two copies booted from one scan). For each, say what carries over cleanly — say, a live migration that preserves a virtual machine's causal continuity, an emulator versus a simulator, a snapshot restore, a clone/fork, two processes with the same image but diverging state — and what had to be bent or silently dropped.

Then give me the residue report. I especially want to know: which parts of the philosophical debate have no expression at all in the systems vocabulary, and is that because the vocabulary is missing a primitive (something like this running instance is me) or because the philosopher's question is partly an artifact of a framework that never had to ask it? Do the people who say the emulation obviously isn't you have any genuine purchase on someone who's watched a clean failover, or do their arguments only sound sharp to people who've never administered a host? And does the systems intuition — same entity only if there's the right kind of causal continuity — quietly fudge anything about the first-person side, and do the philosophers quietly fudge anything about latency and fidelity?

My intution, for what it's worth, is that the engineers are closer to right about identity and the philosophers are closer to right about what could still be lost, but I want the residue report to make me work for that conclusion. Be precise about what the translation cannot carry — that's the part I'm actually after.
