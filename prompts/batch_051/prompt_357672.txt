I work in the country-risk unit of a sovereign wealth fund. Several times a year I have to put a number on the chance that a country we lend to will still be around in basically its current form in fifty years—we hold their 50-year bonds and the board wants a survivability estimate, not just a credit memo. I'm tired of writing the usual inside-view story (this president is popular, this coalition is fragile, this region is unstable) and want a repeatable procedure built on the outside view instead: start with how long polities of the relevant kind have actually lasted in the past, then adjust from there only with facts that are strong enough to justify departing from the base rate.

Write me the procedure. It needs to be runnable by a junior analyst in about one working day per country, with no access to realtime data and no specialist history training beyond a one-page cheat sheet you write for them. It should tell the analyst:

- how to pick the reference class from a small fixed menu (all states since 1815, empires, post-colonial states, one-party states, oil states, land-based autocracies, etc.) and what to do when a country belongs to several classes that give wildly different survival curves;
- how to turn the class's historical survival record into a base rate for the next 50 years, including a correction for survivorship bias in the record (the empires and states we remember are disproportionately the ones that lasted long enough to get wrote about);
- which inside-view facts are allowed to move the number, and by roughly how much (so they can't just add ten points because the current leader looks competent);
- where the genuinely judgement calls are, and how to flag them so the board doesn't mistake them for arithmetic.

Then apply the procedure to three cases: one where it produces an alarmingly high collapse probability for a state everyone treats as stable; one where it produces a reassuringly low number for a country the newspapers keep calling a failed state; and one where two defensible reference classes give very different numbers for the same country (make it a country where this actually matters, like the US classed as a rich democracy versus as a hegemon past its peak). For each, show the calculation, and tell me when I would be right to override the procedure and when I'd just be fooling myself.
