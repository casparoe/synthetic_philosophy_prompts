In my work on democratic theory I keep returning to the fact that the demos is not pre-political: every franchise rule draws a boundary around "the people," and that boundary is one of the most consequential decisions a polity makes, yet it is also the decision for which the standard justificatory resources seem thinnest. The 2016 United Kingdom referendum on EU membership looks to me like an unusually clean test case for the boundary problem, because the legal definition of the electorate produced a set of inclusions and exclusions that were hard to map onto any single underlying principle. For the purposes of the analysis, I want to hold fixed the following features of the franchise. Under the European Union Referendum Act 2015, the electorate was defined by reference to the parliamentary franchise, with the addition of certain peers and of Commonwealth and Irish citizens in Gibraltar. In practice this meant that citizens of other EU member states resident in the UK, including long-term residents, workers, and taxpayers who were neither Irish nor qualifying Commonwealth citizens, could not vote; UK citizens living abroad in other EU member states for more than fifteen years could not vote; while Irish and qualifying Commonwealth citizens resident in the UK could vote.

Please analyze this episode through the lens of the boundary problem. Specifically, give a disciplined mapping: take the leading accounts of who belongs to the demos, including the all-affected interests principle, the all-subjected-to-coercion principle, the stakeholder principle, and a membership or civic-republican account, and say for each which of these inclusions and exclusions it would license and which it would condemn. Then assess what the actual franchise reveals about the adequacy of those accounts, and whether it is best read as the application of one principle, an ad hoc political compromise, or something with theoretical commitments of its own. I am less interested in whether the decision to leave was right than in what the decision about who got to decide shows about the tenability of the standard principles for drawing the demos.
