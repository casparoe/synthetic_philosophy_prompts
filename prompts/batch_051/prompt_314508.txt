The symbol grounding problem, in its contemporary form, is the problem of how symbols in a computational system come to have meaning rather than merely standing in syntactic or statistical relations to other symbols. The classical worry is that a system can manipulate symbols impeccably while they remain empty unless they are anchored in something outside the symbol system—perception, action, reference, or affect. The current version of the worry targets large language models: trained only on text, they may produce fluent, true, and useful sentences while, on the skeptical view, the tokens mean nothing because the model has never seen, acted on, or referred to the world. The opposing view is that no such extra-symbolic anchor is required, or that rich distributional structure over a sufficiently large corpus already constitutes a kind of grounding.
