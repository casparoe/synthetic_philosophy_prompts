I am a psychometrician, and my working vocabulary is the measurement model: classical test theory, item response theory, generalizability theory, inter-rater reliability, construct and criterion validity, latent traits, and expert-panel aggregation. I have read philosophy only at a survey level, and I remember Hume's essay Of the Standard of Taste only in outline. I want you to translate that essay into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

As I remember it, Hume's argument runs roughly as follows. Beauty and deformity are not properties in objects but sentiments in the mind. Yet taste is not merely arbitrary: some judgments are better than others, some works survive across time and place while others fall away, and there is, in principle, a standard by which disputes of taste can be settled. That standard is the joint verdict of ideal critics—critics possessing delicacy of sentiment, practice, comparison, and freedom from prejudice. My hunch is that this is really a psychometric thesis: aesthetic judgment is measurement of a latent construct; critics are raters whose judgments are noisy and biased; delicacy, practice, and comparison are just what one does to improve reliability and calibration; and the joint verdict of ideal critics is a well-selected expert panel. On this reading, the objectivity of taste is a construct-validity claim, with individual judgments treated as fallible indicators of an underlying construct.

Do this as a disciplined methodological translation, not an analogy. Map each component of Hume's argument onto my vocabulary where it fits cleanly. Where you have to stretch a term or reinterpret a claim to force a fit, say so. Where a claim has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all, say so. Then give the residue report. I want two questions addressed in particular. First: in my framework, how does one validate an instrument against a construct that is itself defined by the agreement of the ideal raters? Hume's way of identifying the true critics appears to appeal to the very standard those critics are supposed to establish; is that just the familiar bootstrapping problem in construct validation, or is it worse? Second: does the normativity of aesthetic judgment survive—the claim that one ought to prefer one work to another, not merely that qualified judges tend to—or does my vocabulary treat that as external to the model?

Start with a one-paragraph bottom line. Then give the translation, then the residue report, and end with a single sentence on whether my psychometric reduction captures Hume's position or loses something essential. Hard cap: 850 words.
