Here's one I keep going in circles on. I want a working definition of 'a life worth living' that I can actually use when I'm doing population ethics, especially the repugnant conclusion stuff, where you're supposed to compare a small population of great lives to an astronomically huge population of lives that are only just barely worth living. Define the term so it gets these cases right:

- an 80-year life of ordinary ups and downs, clearly worth living
- a life that lasts two minutes and consists of one faintly pleasant sensation, then nothing
- 50 years of low-grade malaise capped by a single afternoon of intense joy
- a life of constant pleasurable contentment but no relationships, achievements, or knowledge, like an opiate dream
- a life spent in dreamless coma from birth
- a life that is exactly neutral at every moment, never up, never down
- 50 years of acute suffering followed by 50 years of mild comfort
- the classic barely-positive life from the repugnant conclusion, the one that nets out at +1

I suspect the phrase is doing two jobs at once: a threshold job (is the life above the zero line?) and a summing job (does the whole life come out positive on balance?). And I keep hearing people distinguish 'worth starting' from 'worth continuing', which feels like a third thing. Give me the definition or definitions, run each through all eight cases, tell me where it gives no answer or surprises you, and then say which of the three jobs the repugnent conclusion actually needs. I care more about clean distinctions than one tidy sentence.
