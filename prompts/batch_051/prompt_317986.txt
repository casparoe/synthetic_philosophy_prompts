I'm a novelist, and for years I've been jotting down moments of bluntness and white lies the way other people collect beach glass. I lived in Amsterdam for a while and then in Tokyo, and the two places left me with opposite reflexes about whether saying the plain true thing is a virtue or a failure of care. The problem is my own verdicts on cases keep coming out inconsistent, and I'd like to know if there's a principle underneath them or if I'm just a hypocrite with a notebook.

Here's the list:

- A friend in Amsterdam serves me herring and asks if I like it. I say no, to salty. Fine, almost obligatory.
- The same friend gives me a hand-knitted scarf for my birthday and asks if I like it. I say I love it, though I'll never wear it. Fine.
- A Tokyo colleague asks me to take over a project I don't want. I say 'that's a little difficult' and change the subject; he reads it as a no. Fine, better than a blunt no.
- A writer friend hands me a draft and asks what I really think. I tell her the truth: the structure collapses in chapter three and the main character is a void. She's hurt, then revises, and the book improves. Not just fine, I'd be failing her to tone it down.
- An acquaintance corners me at a party about a screenplay he's poured his savings into and asks if it's any good. I think it's unsalvageable but say 'it has potential.' Not fine; I felt gross after.
- My editor asks if the pages are basically done and I say 'basically, yes,' though I havent started the last third. Not fine.
- In a writers' workshop someone reads a section that's genuinely terrible and I say, in the room, 'this doesn't work; the prose is dead on arrival.' Not fine, even though every word was true, because I said it in front of others.
- A friend's husband died in an accident and she asks me whether he suffered. He did, badly. I say no, it was quick. Fine.

The herring and the scarf are the pair that really bother me: same friend, same table, both direct questions about my taste, and I give opposite answers. Is there one principle — or a small set — that gets most of these right, stated precisely? What would it say about the case I'm actually losing sleep over: a fellow novelist asks whether she should keep writing, I'm fairly sure she has no talent and should quit, but she's asking me as a friend and probably won't ask twice. And which of my verdicts should I be willing to drop if no clean principle handles them all? Please keep it under 500 words.
