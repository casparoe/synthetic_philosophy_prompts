I've been chewing on a parallel and I can't tell if it's real or just pattern-matching. Term limits strike me as early stopping, or regularization, for electoral politics: a career politician is a model that keeps training on the voters, donors, and media cycles of its district, and incumbency advantage is the overfitting—it fits the current environment so well that it can't generalize when the world shifts. Sortition, picking legislators by lot, would be the radical version: reinitialize the weights every term so you never overfit, at the price of throwing away whatever expertise the polity had managed to learn. This felt deep for about a week, and now I suspect its just the idea of preventing overfitting doing all the work. What would have to be true about political selection, on one side, and model training, on the other, for the ML-side results—say, the bias-variance tradeoff or why early stopping helps—to actually transfer? And can you run the analogy backwards to get a political prediction that is clearly false, which would be the tell of a merely loose analogy? Deep structure, or just a good metaphor?
