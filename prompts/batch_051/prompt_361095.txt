I'm taking a philosophy of religion course and have been asked to write out my current commitments on the cosmological arguments so that others can check the set for coherence. I would be grateful for an audit of the whole package. Here is roughly where I have landed:

(1) I find the kalam argument compelling in its basic structure: whatever begins to exist has a cause; the universe began to exist; therefore the universe has a cause; and the usual further move from that cause to something timeless without the universe, immaterial, enormously powerful, and personal seems broadly right to me.

(2) I also find the contingency argument compelling, and in one respect superior, since it would work even if the universe turned out to be past-eternal. I take the universe to be contingent because I can clearly conceive of its not having existed, and conceiving of its non-existence seems to me a genuine guide to its possible non-existence.

(3) I reject the unrestricted principle of sufficient reason. I think some contingent facts are brute; for example, I accept that there is no sufficient reason why a particular radioactive atom decayed at one moment rather than another.

(4) I am drawn to a broadly Humean view of causation: causation is a matter of regular succession, and we never observe any necessary connection between events.

(5) I have grown suspicious of conceivability arguments in general. I think, for instance, that the conceivability of philosophical zombies tells us very little about whether zombies are possible.

(6) I am troubled by how both arguments end. Saying that God is a necessary being strikes me as stipulating an exemption from the very demand for explanation that set the arguments going, rather than actually satisfying that demand.

I would like to know whether this set hangs together. Which commitments are in genuine tension, ranked by how serious the tension is? Which pairs seem to be in tension but in fact are not? If I were permitted to give up at most one of these commitments, which single revision would restore the most coherence, and what would that revision cost me?
