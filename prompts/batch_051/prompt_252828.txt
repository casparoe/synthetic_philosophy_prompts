Two people in my philosophy reading group got into it over panpsychism, and I honestly couldn't referee it. Ben said the combination problem is just obviously fatal to constitutive panpsychism: if every electron is a subject, then my consciousness would have to be built up out of billions of separate electron-level subjectivities, and summing subjects doesn't give you a subject; it gives you a plurality. Lena replied that this is too quick, because every known large thing is composed of smaller parts, and we don't reject physicalism just because we don't yet see how integrated biological wholes get there from their parts. Ben said physical wholes are not subjects: you can combine spatial parts into a larger object, but the unity of consciousness is not a spatial aggregation, so the analogy breaks down at the point that matters. Lena said that assumes our introspective sense of being one subject is reliable; maybe it's an illusion, and the only real subjects are the micro-level ones, with macro "subjects" being useful fictions. But then Ben said if there's no macro subject, there's no one around to be wrong about being one, which is incoherent. Lena switched track: maybe macro subjects are real but strongly emergent, so combination isn't needed in the first place. Ben replied that strong emergence just relabels the problem rather than giving a mechanism. Please adjudicate move by move, rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and say who is more right overall.
