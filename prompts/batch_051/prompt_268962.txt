I run a 90-minute reading group for twelve to sixteen adults who have spent years reading LessWrong and adjacent forums; most have hobbyist programming experience or some quantitative training, but I cannot assume formal computer science. I want to use one session to make the group feel the force of Solomonoff induction, specifically the claim that a prior over programs that decays with program length turns Occam's razor from an aesthetic preference into a betting strategy, and that a rational predictor is a mixture over many programs rather than one chosen hypothesis. I do not want this taught by lecture, and the names Solomonoff, Kolmogorov, and the phrase Occam's razor should be withheld until after the activity.

Please design an induction game in which participants see the first n terms of a sequence, binary is fine, and must commit to a rule, or a portfolio of rules, predicting the next k terms. There should be a constrained rule-language so that every proposed rule has a definite cost measured by its length, and the scoring should make shorter rules cheaper or higher-prior in a way participants experience as a trade rather than a stated preference. The scoring should also reward holding several rules at once when the data do not yet separate them, and it should visibly punish long rules that merely memorize the observed prefix once further terms arrive. If the structure permits, include a round in which two rules fit the data so far, one short and one long, and the group must allocate points before the next terms break the tie, so that overfitting is felt as a loss. No real money: use points, with at most a token prize. Please give the exact rules, the materials needed, a timing breakdown for the 90 minutes, and the sequences to use in early rounds.

Then supply a debrief that maps events in the game onto the formalism: a hypothesis is a program; the prior is proportional to 2 to the negative length of the program under a prefix-free universal machine; observing the first n terms is a likelihood update; the posterior over the next term is the mixture over all computable hypotheses, dominated by short ones because their priors are exponentially larger. The debrief should also be honest that the game has already smuggled in a particular rule-language and a finite pool, whereas full Solomonoff induction ranges over all computable programs and its prior depends on the choice of reference machine only up to a constant. Tell me what will predictably go wrong on a first run, for example the room locking onto a simple but false rule, or the scoring accidentally rewarding verbose rules that look sophisticated, and how to steer each failure back to the intended point. Finally, say plainly which feature of real Solomonoff induction the activity cannot reproduce and whether that omission materially distorts the takeaway.
