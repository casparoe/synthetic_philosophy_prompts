I am taking an upper-level course on the evolution of cooperation and am drafting a short statement of my working commitments for a term paper. Before I commit to these views, I would like a consistency check. I hold:

(1) In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with standard self-interested payoffs, defection is the unique rational choice.
(2) In a finitely repeated prisoner's dilemma with a known final round and common knowledge of rationality, backward induction implies defection in every round.
(3) Cooperation among unrelated individuals, including at non-trivial rates in one-shot anonymous experiments, is a robust phenomenon that requires an explanation.
(4) Kin selection, direct reciprocity, and indirect reciprocity are, together, the best available explanations of human cooperation.
(5) A satisfactory explanation of cooperation must show that cooperating is an equilibrium or evolutionarily stable strategy; an appeal to a prior preference for cooperation simply redescribes what needs to be explained.

Please tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost the position as a whole.
