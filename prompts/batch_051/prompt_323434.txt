Here's one from a church constitution written in 1908: "The miraculous gifts of the Spirit, such as tongues, prophecy, and healing, were given to confirm the apostles and ceased when the canon of Scripture was closed; therefore no member shall speak in tongues, prophesy, or lay hands on the sick for healing in any meeting of the church." Fast forward to a church that now meets partly online. A member records a "prophecy" at home on Tuesday and the pastor plays the video during the Sunday livestream. A second member, typing in the live chat during that same service, writes out a message she says is a tongue and then gives the interpretation underneath. Has either of them broken the clause? Take each approach -- the plain words, the purpose, what the 1908 drafters would likely have said if asked, and what a reasonable member now would understand -- and tell me which approach should win and wether the three actually come apart here. Do it in British English.
