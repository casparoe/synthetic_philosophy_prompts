Compare two rival tests for determining whether a command in scripture is culturally limited or transculturally binding. The ground test says a command is transcultural only if it is explicitly anchored in something independent of the original culture—such as the created order, the character of God, or a pattern the text itself traces back to creation—and its application does not depend on a passing social institution. The trajectory test says a command's status is fixed by the direction of movement across the canon: a practice is cultural if the developmental arc of the text moves away from it, and binding if that arc reaffirms or intensifies it. Use at least two of the following as test cases: the head-covering instruction in 1 Corinthians 11; the regulation of slavery; and the dietary laws. Where do the two tests agree, where do they diverge, and what does that tell us about whether each is treating revelation as a static deposit or an unfolding process? Answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; wherever you judge one test superior or a verdict contestable, state what consideration would most likely change your mind.
