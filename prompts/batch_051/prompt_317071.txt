A friend and I spent the evening arguing about whether process-based supervision of language models is genuinely different from outcome-based supervision, or merely outcome-based supervision with finer-grained bookkeeping. Her line was that a step is worth rewarding only because it makes a correct final answer more likely, so every process reward collapses into an expected-future-outcome reward in disguise. I spent the rest of the night trying to articulate why I do not believe that, and I landed on a parallel I would like an outside verdict on.

The distinction looks to me like the difference in epistemology between internalist and externalist accounts of justification. Outcome-based supervision scores only the final answer's correctness, the way reliabilism makes a belief's justification depend on whether the process that produced it reliably yields truth. Process-based supervision scores each inferential transition as it is made, the way access internalism requires that each step be endorsable from the reasoner's own perspective on the basis of what she can access at that moment, whether or not the chain happens to land on the truth. The cases even seem to align point for point: a chain that reaches the right answer through an invalid step earns a positive outcome reward but negative process rewards, just as a true belief produced through a bad inference is justified for the reliabilist only if the process type is reliable overall; and a chain of locally valid steps ending in a wrong answer earns process reward but no outcome reward, just as a justified false belief is possible for the internalist.

Is this a genuine identity of structure, a loose analogy, or pattern-matching on the word process? If it is deep, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer? And can you identify something the parallel predicts that turns out false on one of the two sides? I keep noticing two disanalogies I cannot dismiss: truth in epistemology is not a target a system designer chose, while a correct final answer is; and internalist justification is ordinarily taken to matter because it is tied to agency, reason, and knowledge, none of which obviously applies to a language model. Are those real breaks, or only surface differences an analogy of this kind is allowed to abstract away from?
