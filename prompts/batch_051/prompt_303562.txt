I'm putting together a list of the main proposed solutions to the Born-rule problem in many-worlds, and I can't shake the feeling I'm missing at least one more. So far I've got:

(1) the decision-theoretic line — a rational agent who knows she'll have a successor in each branch should nonetheless assign betting weights to outcomes, and the only weights that leave her preferences coherent across branches are the squared amplitudes;
(2) self-locating uncertainty — before a splitting measurement you should be genuinely unsure which branch you'll wake up in, with your credence proportional to branch weight;
(3) envariance — the symmetries of an entangled system-plus-environment state force the only consistent probability measure to be amplitude-squared;
(4) the brute-measure view — just take amplitude-squared as a primitive 'amount of world' and say rational agents should match their expectations to it;
(5) the many-minds variant — add a continuous infinity of minds distributed over branches according to the Born weights, so the probability of finding yourself in a branch is just the proportion of minds there.

Give me one more strategy that is genuinely different from these five — not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of them. Then tell me straight whether the new one is any good. Lead with your bottom line: name the new strategy in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
