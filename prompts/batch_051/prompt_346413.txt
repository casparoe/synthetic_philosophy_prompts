I want you to engage with a claim from the scalable oversight literature. In "AI Safety via Debate" (2018), Geoffrey Irving, Paul Christiano, and Dario Amodei describe a debate game in which two agents alternate in making short statements about a proposed answer or action, and a human judge then selects the side that gave the truer, more useful information. They write: "Our hypothesis is that optimal play in this game produces honest, aligned information far beyond the capabilities of the human judge." I would like you to treat that sentence as a substantive claim about what debate can do, not as a slogan.

Your task is to design the strongest feasible empirical test of that claim. By "test" I mean a concrete experimental setup: specify the population of judges, the domain of questions, the comparison conditions, the measure of success, and what would count as a positive, negative, or ambiguous result. Then tell me exactly what a positive result would settle and what it would not settle.

I want you especially to confront a dilemma that seems built into the claim. If the judge in your experiment can eventually verify the truth of the contested answer—by consulting a reliable source, by checking a proof, by waiting for events—then your test may not be testing a capability "far beyond" the judge, but only a temporary or local limitation. If the judge cannot verify the truth even afterward, then you face the question of how you know the debate tracked truth at all rather than merely producing statements that persuaded a fallible human. Say how your design addresses this dilemma, or explain why the dilemma cannot be fully resolved and what residual uncertainty remains even after your best test.

Finally, distinguish two things your test might show: that debate improves judge accuracy relative to some baseline, and that the information produced by the debaters is correctly described as "honest" and "aligned" in the way the quoted sentence suggests. What would count as evidence for the second claim, and what part of it would no behavioral test be able to reach?
