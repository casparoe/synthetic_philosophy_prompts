I keep going back and forth with a friend about whether a finite mind's experience is, in principle, exhaustible. His claim is: take any mind realized in a finite system — a brain with finitely many neurons at finite precision, a computer with finite memory, whatever. There are only finitely many total states such a system can occupy, so there are only finitely many distinct "maximal" experiences available to it. Live long enough (or keep the machine running long enough) and you run out of genuinely new experiences; everything after that is just a permutation or recombination of ingredients you've already had. He admits the number might be astronomically large, but insists it's finite, so "used up" is true in principle.

I want to say this sounds empirical but isn't, because we can't get at the experiences themselves directly. He disagrees: he says you could build a novelty probe. For instance, expose a subject to an enormously long, systematically varied sequence of stimuli and ask them to report whenever an experience feels categorically new rather than a reshuffled version of earlier ones; or train a predictor on their reports (or on their neural patterns, if you're working with a biological subject) and look at whether novelty scores asymptote toward zero or never bottom out. I don't think that fully seperates the issue, but I can't make the line precise.

So here's what I want: treat "a finite mind's space of possible experiences is finite (exhaustible)" as a would-be empirical claim. Design the strongest concrete test of it you can, with a clear operationalization of "novel experience." Say exactly what pattern of results would favor the "finite/exhaustible" side and what pattern would favor the "open-ended/inexhaustible" side. Then be honest about what the test — even a clean positive or negative result — would and would not settle. Where does the empirically tractable part stop and the conceptual residue start? And after all that, is the original claim best read as empirical, conceptual, or some strange mix?
