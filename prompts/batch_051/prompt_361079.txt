I'm a grad student and last fall a friend and I started a small "argument league" in our department to make our reading group less passive. We assign two people a disputed claim (they get sides by coin flip, so you might have to defend something you actually disagree with), give them a few days to prepare, and then the rest of the room scores the rounds on a ten point rubric: clarity, responsiveness, steelmanning the other side, and so on. We keep a running leaderboard and the winner each month gets a cheap trophy. Its been weirdly popular.

But now I'm not sure whether we've made the seminar better or just more competitive. Everyone preps harder and the objections are much sharper than they used to be, but I've also noticed people quietly angling for the more defensible side of a question, refusing to concede a point even when the other person landed something obvious, and a general vibe that the goal has shifted from "what should we believe" to "who won." My friend says this is fine—sports like tennis or chess have clear rules and they clearly make their players better, so a point system should do the same for arguing. I get that, but something about it still bothers me. If everyone joined voluntarily and the rules are transparent, is there actually a problem? Should I lean into the gamification, change the rules, or scrap the leaderboard? Or am I just being squeamish about competition?
