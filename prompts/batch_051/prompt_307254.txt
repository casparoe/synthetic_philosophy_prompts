OK, here's a counterfactual. In the current American setup most consumer ratings and predictions about a business's future are treated as opinion, and defamation law mostly leaves them alone unless they imply a hidden false fact. Suppose one state gets sick of this. Starting next January it enacts a statute saying any published numerical rating of a named professional (doctor, professor, lawyer, contractor, you name it) counts as an assertion of fact for defamation purposes, and so does any published prediction about a named business's future, whether the poster frames it as a guess or not. A bare one-star review, an A through F grade for a professor, a comment saying this restaurant will be out of business by Christmas: all of it is actionable, and falsity is presumed, so the poster has to prove the statement is substantially true to escape liability. Assume the statute survives both First Amendment and Section 230 challenges, damages are ordinary, and two or three other states are expected to copy it if there isn't an obvious disaster. Predict what the review-and-comment ecosystem looks like a decade later. Give me your reasoning at each step, not just a story: what do platforms do to their rating systems, how do users rewrite their reviews, which speech migrates where, do consumers end up better or worse informed, and do professionals start using the law strategically against their critics? Also flag which effects you think are near-certain, which ones are speculative, and what would have to be true for your central prediction to be wrong.
