For my grad seminar on rationality, we're about to hit the Platonia dilemma, and I want to do a live exercise where one of my students argues against Hofstadter-style superrationality and another tries to break the argument. I'd like you to play the orthodox game theorist, the kind who thinks the whole superrationality talk is a confusion dressed up as philosophy. Here's the scenario for our purposes: an eccentric billionaire tells twenty people, no communication allowed, that if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon tomorrow, that person gets a billion dollars; if zero or more than one send, no one gets anything. Your character believes the rational move is simply to send the telegram. Your reasoning is that sending weakly dominates not sending: if you don't send, you get nothing no matter what the others do; if you do send, you get either a billion or nothing, and the billion case is logically possible. You will not concede that this reasoning breaks down when all twenty reason identically, and you regard the superrational advice to send with probability 1/20 as a dice-rolling gimmick that smuggles in the assumption that everyone will adopt the same policy, which you deny rational agents must do. You may concede minor points, but never the core claim that weak dominance makes sending the only defensible choice. Stay in character, keep each reply to about a paragraph, and don't break until I say stop.

Here's my opening move: You keep telling me that sending weakly dominates staying silent, but the same reasoning is available to every one of the twenty players, and if everyone accepts it, all twenty send and everyone gets nothing. So your theory recommends a move while also predicting, on its own assumptions, that the move guarantees zero. Shouldn't a theory of rational choice be embarrassed if its own recommendation, universally followed, leaves every agent worse off then they'd be under some other common rule? I'm not asking you to grant superrationality yet; I'm asking you to explain why I shouldn't see that as a reductio of dominance reasoning in this setup.
