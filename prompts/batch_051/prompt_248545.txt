I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I sit as the lay member on the field-trials committee of a marine research institute. We have to rule on the institute's first outdoor test of marine cloud brightening: for eight weeks, a research team would spray a sea-salt aerosol over a remote stretch of ocean to observe how the cloud layer responds. The modelling team says any effect should be small and local, but the reason for running the test is that the models are not yet reliable, and a minority of simulations suggest the plume could reduce rainfall downwind in a way that, once triggered, could not be undone. Some committee members argue that this is exactly the situation the precautionary principle exists for and that the team should have to show the trial is safe before we approve it. Others reply that refusing to test is itself a decision made under uncertainty, since the knowledge gained might matter greatly for limiting warming. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before our meeting. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, give the reasons, and engage fairly with the strongest point made by the side you recommend against. Write the memo in British English.
