I've been reading my way through the Korean Four–Seven debate for a seminar and I cant shake the feeling that the thing stalled for structural reasons, not because anyone was being dense. Both sides agree the Four Beginnings (commiseration, shame and dislike, deference, the sense of right and wrong) are always good, and both sides agree the Seven Emotions (joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, desire) can be good or bad depending on whether they hit the mean. The fight is over whether the Four and the Seven are the same kind of thing. The Toegye line says they differ in origin: the Four come from principle (li) and the Seven come from material force (ki). By the final version of that line, the Four are li issuing with ki following, while the Seven are ki issuing with li riding along to keep them straight. The Gobong/Yulgok line says this multiplying of origins is a mistake: the Four just are the Seven in their well-ordered aspect; everything arises through ki, and li is what makes the good instances good rather than a separate source.

What gets me is that every argument that looks like it should settle the thing turns out to hang on how you read one Mencius remark about the Four already being in us, or a Doctrine of the Mean passage about the emotions before and after they are aroused, and on what it even means to say an emotion issues from an immaterial principle. My working theory is that this is not really a disagreement about moral psychology or about the metaphysics of li and ki, but about what the whole enterprise is for: are they trying to classify emotions, prescribe a course of self-cultivation, solve an exegetical puzzle in the classics, or defend a reading of Mencius against a reading of the Doctrine of the Mean? So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is my diagnosis right, or is there a real question underneath it, about the unity of the emotions or about whether a normative standard can itself be a source, that the debate really is about and that could in principle be answered? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the debate, and is there part of it that cant be moved because the two sides need the idea of issuing to do different jobs? I want the structure of the stalemate diagnosed, not a recap of who said what.
