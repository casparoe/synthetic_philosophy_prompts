I'm a PhD student in a cognitive science lab, and I spent an awkward hour while two labmates argued about whether we should rename LLM hallucinations "bullshit" in the paper we're drafting; I'm stuck refereeing. (1) Ana said we should call them bullshit in Frankfurt's sense: the model doesn't care how things actually are, it just emits text that looks like a competent answer, and that's exactly the indifference to truth Frankfurt sets against the liar's concern with truth. (2) Sam replied that Frankfurt's bullshitter is still an agent who performs concern for the truth while secretly being indifferent to it; an LLM doesn't perform anything and isn't trying to get away with anything, so it can't bullshit—it just outputs falsehoods. (3) Ana shot back that Sam is smuggling in more agency than the distinction needs: what matters is whether the process that produces the text is truth-indifferent by design, and next-token prediction is. Error would mean the system was aiming at truth and missed, but the system isn't aiming at anything, so between "bullshit" and "error," bullshit is the less wrong label. (4) Sam said that's going purely functional, and on that standard a hurricane "lies" when it knocks a house down. Ana answered that a hurricane isn't built to produce speech acts, while an LLM is, and design-level indifference to truth is exactly the thing Frankfurt was tracking. Sam then said: fine, then it's neither lying nor bullshit nor error—it's a new category we don't have a name for, and "hallucination" is no worse than the borrowed terms.
