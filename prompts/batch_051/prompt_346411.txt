Take the usual St. Petersburg game: you flip a fair coin until it comes up heads, and if the first heads is on flip n you win 2^n dollars. The puzzle is that the expected payout is infinite, yet actual people will only pay a few dollars to play. Now vary it. Instead of a one-shot additive dollar payout, consider a multiplicative version: each round, before the toss, you pay a fraction c of your current wealth (0<c<1) as an entry fee, and if the first heads is on flip n your remaining wealth is multiplied by 2^n, so W_{t+1} = (1−c) 2^n W_t. The ensemble expected wealth after one round still diverges, because the expected multiplicative factor is infinite. But the ergodicity-economics criterion says to look at the time-average growth rate, E[ln(W_{t+1}/W_t)]. Work out that growth rate, find the cutoff c where it crosses zero, and use it to say how the St. Petersburg analysis changes when the payoffs are multiplicative rather than additive. Does this version just reproduce Bernoulli's log-utility resolution in different clothes, or does the time-average versus ensemble-average distinction do extra work here? Finally, briefly state what the time-average criterion gives for the original additive version at fixed current wealth W.
