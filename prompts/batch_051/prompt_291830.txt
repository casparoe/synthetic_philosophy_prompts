ok so I'm in a medieval Islamic philosophy course and we've been going over Avicenna's flying man (sometimes called the floating man), and I want to write my paper on a version of it that I don't think we covered. The setup as we read it: Avicenna tells you to imagine a person created all at once and suspended in a void, eyes veiled, limbs not touching, no memory of a body, and nothing arriving through any sense. A complete sensory blank, but with a normal adult intellect. Avicenna says this person will still know that he exists — he'll affirm his own existence — even though he can't perceive any body part; and from that he concludes the 'I' or soul isn't any body part, so the soul is distinct from the body (or at least not identical to the body in the way we usually think of it).

Instead of just arguing about whether the flying man would actually have interoception, I want to run the argument across four levels of body-input, with everything else held fixed:
S0 — no body input at all (Avicenna's setting)
S1 — interoception only: heartbeat, breathing, temperature, hunger, pain, but nothing localized to organs
S2 — S1 plus propioception: a felt sense of limb position and movement
S3 — S2 plus perceiving his own limbs from the outside, by touch and sight (body parts as objects)

Question: at which setting does the move from 'he knows he exists without being aware of any body part' to 'the self is not the body' stop working? I'm trying to see wether it breaks as early as S1 (is feeling a heartbeat already awareness of a body part?), or whether a defender can just say those are states of the body-as-subject rather than parts of the body-as-object, so the argument only really fails at S3. And if it only fails at S3, does that show Avicenna is leaning on an ambiguity about what 'knowing the body' means?

Also, for each setting, say which of the two standard readings survives: the minimal one (all the flying man shows is self-awareness, a subject of experience) and the maximal one (it shows an immaterial soul distinct from the body). I want to know where the load-bearing premise really is once you look at all four settings.
