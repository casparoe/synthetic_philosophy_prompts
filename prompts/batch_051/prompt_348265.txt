I've just come out of a long argument with a friend, and I've ended up committed to a qualified Cartesian theater view of consciousness. I don't think the theater needs to be an anatomical place in the brain, but I do think there must be a single, functionally unified stage at which the contents of an experience are assembled, given their apparent temporal order, and made available to the subject; and I don't see how Dennett's multiple drafts model can account for the apparent unity and temporal order of experience without surreptitiously reintroducing something equivalent to that stage. Tell me what would count against this position. I want a map of its exposure, not just the stock objections: for example, that there is no place in the brain where it all comes together, and that the picture leads to a homunculus regress. Which empirical phenomena (the color-phi phenomenon, metacontrast masking, backward referral, Libet-style timing experiments, split-brain dissociations), which conceptual arguments, and which commitments from neighboring debates about representation and time consciousness would have to turn out one way rather than another for the position to stand or fall? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed defender of the functional Cartesian theater would themselves concede as counting against the view rather than dismiss as question-begging, and conclude with whether the position is well insulated and whether that insulation is a problem.
