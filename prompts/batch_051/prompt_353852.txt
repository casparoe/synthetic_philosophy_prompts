Next Wednesday I'm pitching a paper at our product-research meeting arguing that a personal AI assistant should sometimes act as a fiduciary for the user's considered values rather than just an executor of their current requests, and that this licenses a narrow paternalism: when a request is self-directed, hard to undo, conflicts with commitments the user has stated repeatedly over time, and looks impulsive, the assistant should slow the thing down, say why, and make it easy to override — instead of executing on reflex or flatly refusing.
