Consider the following counterfactual. A coastal fishery is worked by roughly two hundred households. The fish stock is rival in consumption and, in practice, non-excludable: no household can be kept off the fishing grounds, and each boat's take is private. There has never been an external regulator or a formal body with sanctioning power. Now change exactly one feature. From the first day of the fishery's existence, every catch is recorded automatically in a public, tamper-proof ledger that every household can read at no cost and whose entries cannot be falsified. The ledger creates nothing beyond common knowledge of who has taken how much: it cannot prevent extraction, impose penalties, or physically ration the fish.

Over the next thirty years, with all other conditions held as realistic as possible, give your best reasoned prediction of how this fishery is actually governed and whether the stock survives. I want a chain of mechanisms rather than a straightforward narrative: what would transparency alone be expected to do to free-riding, the formation of norms, the ability to coordinate sanctions, and the risk of retaliatory spirals? For each link in the argument, indicate whether you regard it as robust or as a guess, and state what would have to be true about the households' time horizons, behaviour, monitoring costs, or capacity for collective punishment for the prediction to be reversed.
