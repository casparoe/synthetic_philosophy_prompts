Robert Nozick's discussion of animals in chapter 3 of *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* contains what looks like a tension, and I want to know whether it is genuine or an artifact of the way I am reading it.

On one side, Nozick treats human beings as inviolable in a broadly Kantian way: they may not be used merely as means, and moral side constraints protect them even when violating those constraints would produce better overall results. But when he turns to animals, he seems to lower the standard. He suggests, if I am reading him correctly, that animals are not owed the same side constraints; instead, their pains and pleasures enter into something like a utilitarian calculus. Their welfare counts, but an animal may be sacrificed when the aggregate benefit justifies the sacrifice.

On the other side, when he takes up meat eating, he does not simply say that the aggregate consequences decide. He appears to allow that animals may have some kind of moral status strong enough that actually existing factory farming would be objectionable, while also suggesting that raising animals under pleasant conditions and killing them painlessly might be a different matter. That sounds like a rights-like or constraint-like objection to at least some treatment of animals, not merely a complaint about a bad utilitarian balance sheet.

So the apparent conflict is this: if animals are utilitarian patients rather than bearers of side constraints, why would factory farming be singled out as morally problematic in a way that depends on how they are treated, rather than on the total welfare produced? And if animals do have protections akin to side constraints, what grounds the permissiveness about painlessly killing them for food? Can these two moves be reconciled without attributing to Nozick an ad hoc hybrid he never actually argues for? Is the most honest reconstruction that he is genuinely ambivalent, or that he is sketching several positions without committing to one? If you think I have misdescribed either move, or that the tension dissolves once a distinction Nozick draws is made explicit, say so plainly rather than offering a strained reconciliation.
