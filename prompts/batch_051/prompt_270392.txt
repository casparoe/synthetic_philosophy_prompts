I've been reading about the language of thought hypothesis in my spare time, without any academic background in the area, and I've been trying to work an idea up into a short essay. The hypothesis, as I understand it, says that our thinking does not happen in English or any other spoken language but in a structured mental code, and it is usually defended by appeal to productivity and systematicity: we can entertain indefinitely many thoughts we have never had before, and whoever can think that a cat is on a mat can normally also think that a mat is on a cat, so our thoughts must have recombinable parts arranged by something like a syntax. My worry began while I was practicing a short phrase on the guitar. I realized that musical competence has the same feature: a player who can produce a five-note phrase can usually play it backwards, transpose it, or swap the middle two notes, and can produce indefinitely many phrases never played before; the same seems true of rearranging the parts of a floor plan, a dance step, or a diagram. If recombinability is what forces us to posit a language-like code, the argument would seem to force us to posit a language of music, a language of maps, and a language of dance.

My essay would then press the question of where the line is. A defender might say thought is special because thoughts can be true or false while a melody or a gesture cannot. But maps and diagrams can be accurate or inaccurate, and they have parts that combine to make a whole without being sentences. So I do not see why truth-evaluability should specifically require sentence-like syntactic structure rather than some more general part-whole structure. My tentative conclusion is that the hypothesis may be right that thought is compositional, but that the word language is doing more work than the argument entitles it to. I would be grateful for comments on whether this is a fair challenge to the strongest version of the hypothesis, whether the comparisons to music, maps, and dance are misleading in a way I have overlooked, and whether granting that thought has recombinable parts while denying that it is literally a language is a real concession on my part or only a change of labels. If there is an obvious reply a defender would give, I would appreciate hearing it stated and then judged, since I would like to know whether the essay should answer it directly.
