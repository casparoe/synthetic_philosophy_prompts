You are a moral and political philosopher specializing in the ethics of social norms, comfortable translating theory into rules ordinary practitioners can apply. I run the moderation team for a large online community, and our hardest cases are disputes over insults, public call-outs, and harassment. We process several hundred reports a week; junior moderators make most calls in under fifteen minutes; I review only what they escalate. A misclassified dispute can turn into a platform-wide pile-on, so the stakes are high.

I want our decisions organized around the contrast among honor cultures, dignity cultures, and victimhood cultures. By honor culture I mean a norm system in which a slight must be answered directly and publicly, or one loses standing. By dignity culture I mean a norm system in which slights are absorbed, or quietly referred to an impartial authority, and are not taken to diminish one's standing. By victimhood culture I mean a norm system in which a wrong is brought before an audience that can shame the offender and confer on the victim the status of the injured party.

Please write me a reusable procedure a moderator can follow for a single complaint: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops because the framework does not fit, and where it must escalate because a moral judgment, not a classification, is being made. Begin by listing the distinguishing features of the three cultures as you will use them, and say which feature does the work at each step. Keep the distinctions sharp while making the procedure usable by someone who has never read the academic literature.

Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) a user answers a public insult with a threat to retaliate in kind; (2) a user reports an insulting comment and asks only that it be removed, saying they have no interest in a fight; (3) a user, after reporting an offense and getting a slow response, posts screenshots on another platform and urges followers to mass-report the offender and demand a public apology. Make at least one case return an outcome I will not welcome, and explain why the procedure gives it anyway.

Finally, tell me what the procedure cannot do: in particular, whether it can rank the three cultures as morally better or worse, and which step in your procedure is really a values judgment wearing the clothes of a classification.
