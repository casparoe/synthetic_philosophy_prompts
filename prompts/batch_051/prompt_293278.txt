A few of us in my school philosophy club got into a long argument about whether dreams should count as experiences, and we could not resolve it. The dispute began when Maya defended a broadly Malcolm-style position: she said it is a conceptual mistake to treat a dream as a stream of conscious experience that occurs while you are asleep. Her reason was that any dream report is a waking report, produced after the fact, and there is no independent way to check the report against the sleep itself. She thought the word experience is doing illicit work here, because it imports the very thing the sleep state seems to rule out.

Eli replied that this is a form of verificationism, not an argument. He brought up Dennett’s cassette-style scenario: imagine that the brain composes a dream narrative during sleep and then inserts it into consciousness only at the moment of waking, like a cassette being played only after the fact. That scenario would produce the same waking reports as the ordinary view. But Eli said the ordinary view is still a substantive empirical hypothesis, because we can in principle look for real-time neural correlates of imagery during REM sleep and test whether something like a stream of imagery is unfolding. Maya answered that if the cassette scenario predicts the same reports, then saying dreams are experiences adds no extra predictive content. Eli responded that science posits unobservable causes all the time, such as electrons. Maya countered that electron theory earns its keep by predicting new measurable effects, whereas the experience claim, without a live dream-reader, does not. At that point Jonas added a hypothetical: suppose we build a device that can decode and display visual imagery during sleep in real time, so an observer can say that the sleeper is now processing the image of a red corridor. Maya said this would still only show that the brain is processing images, not that there is something it is like for the sleeping person. Eli said that if the decoded imagery lined up with later waking reports, it would be almost perverse to deny that the experience happened during sleep.

Please adjudicate the disagreement move by move: identify who is right about which specific point, where each side overreaches or underargues, and what considerations both are missing. Then evaluate the Jonas hypothetical on its own terms.
