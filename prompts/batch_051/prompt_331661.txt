I have been trying to sort out, in my graduate seminar on collective responsibility, whether individual purchases of factory-farmed meat can be written off as morally inert because the supply chain does not respond to any single consumer's choice. A colleague keeps pressing the following argument on me, and I suspect its conclusion is too quick, but I have not been able to identify the precise step at which it collapses. I want a committed diagnosis: does the argument go through as stated? If not, which step fails, and under which exact reading of its central terms does it fail?

The argument runs as follows. (1) An act is wrong only if it makes a causal difference to the amount of suffering in the world. (2) My buying this one factory-farmed chicken does not change how many birds any store, distributor, or producer orders or rears: suppose the grocer orders in cases of fifty and reorders when stock reaches a threshold set by aggregate weekly sales; a single purchase is only a small fraction of a case, and weekly sales fluctuate by far more than one chicken, so one purchase is never the factor on which the ordering of another case turns. (3) So my buying this chicken makes no causal difference to the number of chickens raised and killed. (4) Since the amount of suffering in chicken production is fixed by how many chickens are raised and killed, my buying this chicken makes no causal difference to the amount of suffering. (5) Therefore, buying this chicken is not wrong. (6) But every individual purchase of factory-farmed meat is relevantly like this one. So no individual purchase of factory-farmed meat is wrong.

My own suspicion is that the defect lies in the move from (2) to (3), or perhaps from (3) to (4): a purchase can make an expected causal difference by changing the probability that some threshold in the supply chain is crossed, even when it does not alter any particular order in a way one could point to afterward. But I want you to tell me, with reasons, whether that is really where the argument fails, whether (1) is the questionable premise instead, or whether the argument is in fact sound and the unwelcome conclusion should simply be accepted.
