I'm a first-time TA for an early modern political thought course, and the instructor has me grading a stack of midterm essays without a detailed key. The essay question the students answered (I didn't write it) was roughly:

In Leviathan, Hobbes claims that covenants entered into out of fear are still obligatory, but he also claims that the sovereign is authorized by, and is not a party to, the covenant that institutes a commonwealth. Explain how these two pieces of his account of the social contract fit together. More specifically: does fear create the commonwealth, keep it in being, both, or neither? Be precise about the difference between what binds a subject and what moves a subject.

One student wrote the following answer:

Hobbes thinks the state of nature is a condition of universal war, because there is no shared power that everyone fears enough to keep them in line. The way out is a covenant where each person hands over their right to govern themselves to one sovereign, on the condition that everyone else does the same. Hobbes says a covenant made under fright or coercion is still considered binding, so the fact that people are driven by fear of violent death does not invalidate the original agreement. The sovereign then makes everyone actually perform by threatening punishment, and because the sovereign never entered a covenant with anyone, he cannot be said to wrong his subjects. So fear is what both creates the commonwealth and keeps it stable.

I have to assign this a letter grade and write the comments that justify it. The instructor said a strong answer has to keep seperate the question of what makes a covenant binding from the question of what makes it rational to perform, and also has to explain how authorization differs from merely hiring an enforcer. Please grade the answer as if you were an expert on Hobbes and an experienced grader, thinking carefully about what a high-quality answer would need before assigning the grade. Start your response with the grade in the first sentence, then give point-by-point comments that justify it; no preamble and don't restate the essay question.
