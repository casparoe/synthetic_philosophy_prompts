I am a PhD student in epistemology, and I have been developing a view I call the two-tier warrant thesis. The core idea is that there are two distinct epistemic statuses, which I call *probationary warrant* and *standing warrant*. A belief has probationary warrant when it is produced by a source that appears reliable from the subject's own perspective, the belief is not in conflict with anything else the subject currently accepts, and the subject has no specific reason to distrust the source on that occasion. Probationary warrant entitles the subject to act on the belief and to use it in ordinary inference, but not to deploy it in an explicit track-record argument for the reliability of the very source that produced it. Standing warrant, by contrast, is what a belief acquires when its probationary warrant survives repeated exposure to the subject's other sources over time: outputs from distinct enough channels increasingly fail to conflict, and the subject may then use those outputs in assessing the reliability of the source that produced them.

My motivation is the bootstrapping problem. I think the problem arises because we allow probationary warrant to do the work that only standing warrant can do. A source cannot immediately licence itself by its own track record, but a track record built up under cross-checking by independent sources can, over time and in the absence of conflict, upgrade the source's outputs to standing warrant. The licence is not earned by a single circular inference; it is earned by accumulated, uneventful coherence across channels. I know this sounds close to coherentism, and also to views in the neighbourhood of Sosa-style epistemic circularity or Wright-style entitlement. My hope is that the probationary/standing distinction captures what is right in those views while identifying exactly which phase of the track-record reasoning is illegitimate.

I want an honest assessment rather than encouragement. What are the three strongest objections to the distinction itself, and are any of them fatal rather than merely uncomfortable? In particular, how would you handle the case of a globally deceived but internally coherent subject who accumulates exactly the same absence of conflict as a normal subject, and the case of a single modality, such as vision alone, that is in fact reliable but cannot check itself?
