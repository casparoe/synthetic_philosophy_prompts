I work as a policy analyst for the competence-review commission of a federation whose constitution commits it to subsidiarity — the principle that public questions should be handled at the smallest level of government that can deal with them effectively. In practice the commission has stopped trying to read that formula directly and instead works from its own precedents. The four the office treats as binding are these. It moved ambient air-quality standards up to the federal level because pollutants do not respect cantonal borders. It kept primary-school curriculum at the cantonal level, rejecting the federal government's argument that a national curriculum would make transfers and testing easier, on the ground that running a school system is a form of local political life. It assigned nurse licensing to the federal level because nurses move among cantons and a patchwork of credentials fragments the labor market. And it upheld municipal power over zoning and building heights, even where a neighboring town complained that shadows and traffic crossed the boundary, treating the municipality's land-use plan as an exercise of self-government.

The case now before us is close to home in both senses. The city where I grew up, Merztal, has passed a municipal minimum wage set above the federal floor, covering all work performed within city limits. Suburban towns and logistics firms are challenging it: they say employers will simply shift jobs across the municipal line, workers will commute in, and a patchwork of local wages will fragment what is functionally a single metropolitan labor market. The mayor answers that a living wage is exactly the kind of distributive choice a self-governing city is entitled to make, and that the commission's zoning precedent already protects municipal authority even when some effects spill over.

If the commission wants to be consistent with its own track record, what should it decide about the Merztal ordinance? I would like you to lay out the distinct principles that fit the four precedents, say what each implies for this case, identify which precedent is the closest analog and why, and then give a best guess about the decision with the reasons for preferring one principle over the others. Where the precedents genuinely underdetermine the result, say so, and propose one further case that would best separate the remaining candidate principles. Throughout, please distinguish what the precedents settle from what is genuinely contested among people who work on subsidiarity, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
