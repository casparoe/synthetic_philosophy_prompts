I'm giving a workshop on Aristotle's account of money and exchange to a finance department: mostly people who model interest as an intertemporal price plus a risk premium, one historian of economic thought who knows the texts far better than me, and two economic sociologists who think calling the practice unnatural is just folk moralizing. I'm defending a deliberately modest neo-Aristotelian claim. Aristotle said money is naturally barren and that usury is the most unnatural way to get wealth because it lets money beget money; my version is narrower — being paid for the mere passage of time and the borrower's risk is morally different from being paid for the use of a productive asset, and the difference doesn't reduce to a confusion between money as cash and money as capital.
