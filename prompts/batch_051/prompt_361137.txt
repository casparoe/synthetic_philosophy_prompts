I've been playing with a term I call "counterfactual residue." The idea is that omissions don't cause outcomes the way positive events do: if I don't water your plant, the plant dies of dehydration, full stop — the dryness kills it, not my not-watering. But when we blame someone for an omission, we're pointing to a nearby world where they did the thing, and we let the causal story from that world leak back into ours; that leaked bit is the residue. So omission causation would always be partly normative — we're charging the person for not bringing about the better world, not for a mechanism in the actual one. Is this a real distinction or just a metaphor? Where does it break, like for omissions with no clear positive act behind them, or when the person couldn't have brought about the better world? And is there a version of this already worked out under another name?
