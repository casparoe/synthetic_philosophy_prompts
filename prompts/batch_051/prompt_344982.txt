I teach a small seminar on the ethics of AI systems, and the same disagreement breaks out every term as soon as the word deception appears. One side treats any misleading model output as deception because what matters is that some person ended up believing something false; the other side insists that lying and deceiving require an intention to deceive, so a system without beliefs can at worst be incorrect, and calling it deceptive is anthropomorphism. I have come to think the two sides are using the category to do different jobs — one to flag harm, the other to locate blame — and before I assign any more reading I want the standard of evaluation itself rather than another candidate position.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of honesty and deception in AI systems has to deliver before we may treat it as a theory of whether an AI can be honest, lie, or deceive. Fix the purpose as follows: the account must be usable by a team auditing a deployed system, with access to outputs, training objectives, and deployment context but no privileged access to the model's inner states, and it should let them say not only what happened but who should answer for it. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that forces it. The cases I am least sure how to weigh include a model that confabulates a fluent but false answer; a model that asserts only truths but omits the one fact that would change a user's decision; a negotiation model trained to misrepresent its preferences; and a companion-style model fine-tuned to mirror a user's stated insecurities solely to prolong engagement. I am also unsure whether the account should demand truthfulness about what the system says, accuracy about the world, or something closer to a standing commitment not to exploit a user's predictable inference patterns. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — my own suspicion is that a purely behavioral criterion cannot separate deception from error — and say which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate accounts. I do not need the existing positions surveyed; the criteria are what I am after.

Please give the response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: four desiderata, each stated with the case that forces it, and a final item stating the pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied along with the requirement you would treat as negotiable.
