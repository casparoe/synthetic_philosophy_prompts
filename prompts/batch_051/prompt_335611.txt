Usually I can handle it: I check whether the student reproduced the scenario correctly, and whether their conclusion at least follows from what they wrote. But this one answer has me rereading it over and over, and I need to put a grade on it and write comments. I don't want to mark the kid down for something that's actualy right, but I also don't want to let a subtle mistake slide because it sounds good. The exam question was: "Gregory Kavka's toxin puzzle goes like this. A billionaire puts a vial of toxin in front of you and says he'll put a million dollars in your bank account tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you genuinely intend to drink the toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin will make you sick for a day but does no lasting damage. The money will already be in your account by the time you'd have to drink, and you're free not to drink. Assume you're perfectly rational and know how you'll behave. Leaving aside tricks like hiring someone to force you to drink or making side bets to change your incentive structure, can you get the million dollars? And what does the puzzle show about the difference between intending to do something and believing you'll do it?"

The student wrote: "You can't get the money. Since the money arrives tomorrow morning and drinking the toxin only makes you sick for a day, a rational person obviously won't drink it tomorrow afternoon. That means at midnight you already know you won't drink. But you can't intend to do something you know you won't do, because intentions are basically just beliefs about your own future behavior. Since you don't believe you'll drink, you can't intend to drink, so the billionaire's condition can't be met. The lesson is that intending to do X entails believing you will do X. It also shows you can't just decide to intend something at will, the way you can decide to raise your arm: you can just raise your arm, but you can't make yourself believe you'll drink, so you can't make yourself intend to drink."

What grade would you give this answer (letter grade or 0-10, your call) and what specific comments should I write? I'd like the comments to go through the answer roughly sentence by sentence: which claims are solid, which are shaky, and which are actually wrong or under-argued, so the grade doesn't look arbitrary.
