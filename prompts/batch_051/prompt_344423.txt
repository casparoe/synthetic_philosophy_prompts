My working criteria for whether a relationship with someone survives their death is this: (1) the other party can still surprise me, in the sense of producing something I didn't already have in me; (2) there's mutual recognition — they acknowledge me as the same person I am, not just pattern-match my inputs; and (3) the interaction has stakes in both directions — either of us could hurt or change the other.

Now imagine a griefbot trained on a dead spouse's texts, emails, and voice memos. It nails their cadence and inside jokes, brings up memories the widow had half-forgotten, and sometimes says plausibly novel things in their voice ("I'd have voted against you, and you know why"). It's even programmed to sound hurt if she says she might delete it, though nothing on its side can actually be hurt. But it can't update past what the spouse left behind, never asks about the widow's day except by extrapolating old scripts, and can't form a new intention of its own.

Is the widow in a continuing relationship with her spouse, or is she doing something closer to a private ritual, or some third thing? Go through my criteria one at a time and say where the case falls, then tell me whether the case exposes any of the criteria as doing less work than I thought.
