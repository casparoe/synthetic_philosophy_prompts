I'm a history grad student and I've got to write a term paper on the early Cold War, and my seminar has now spent eight weeks on the question of who started it without anyone changing their mind about anything. It's not that we're missing facts. Everyone knows the standard chronology: the bomb, Eastern Europe, the Long Telegram and Novikov, the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, the Berlin blockade. But the discussion just loops through the same positions. One group says the USSR is clearly responsible, because imposing puppet governments in Eastern Europe is what turned a wartime alliance into a bipolar order. Another says the US set the terms: atomic diplomacy and the Truman Doctrine turned a messy settlement into a global rivalry, and the whole question downplays how much of Soviet behavior was defensive. A third says both were responsible, but then people can't agree whether the proportions matter or whether that's just a cop-out. And one person keeps insisting the question has no answer at all, because a decades-long structural conflict isn't the kind of thing a single actor can "start" the way you start a fire, or a war with a declaration. The weird part is we're all reading the same documents, and I don't think anybody actually believes more archives would change their answer.

So before I write this up I want your diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is there a factual question hiding inside it? Is it a dispute about how to weigh security motives against ideological aims? Is it really a disagreement about what blame means in history—legal, moral, causal, or something else? Is the "who started it" framing smuggling in a counterfactual that different people run differently, or is it several different questions sitting under one name? I have a hunch it's a meta-disagreement about blame talk in historiography, but I keep flip-flopping on wether that's right. And once the shape of the impasse is clear: what would progress look like—a distinction that dissolves it, a better-posed question, or a reason to think this just isn't the kind of question that settles?
