The standard way people frame the meta-problem treats our 'problem intuitions' about consciousness — the explanatory gap, the conceivability of zombies, the feeling that Mary learns something new when she leaves the black-and-white room — as three symptoms of one underlying thing, roughly 'consciousness is puzzling in a special way.' I suspect that's wrong. The explanatory-gap intuition might come from a failure to integrate first-person and third-person information, zombie conceivability from the way our modal reasoning runs on gappy descriptions, and the Mary intuition from a seperate fact about knowledge-by-acquaintance versus knowledge-by-description. If they're different cognitive phenomena with different etiologies, there might be no single explanation of the meta-problem — and, more interesting to me, the hard problem might look like one big mystery only because we've been lumping several smaller, more tractable puzzles together. Chalmers's own paper throws out a bunch of candidate explanations, but it mostly treats them as rival accounts of one phenomenon; what I havent seen is anyone giving real criteria for deciding when two of these intuitions are two expressions of the same underlying thing versus two different things. That's the gap I want to press on.
