I'm a philosophy professor, and I've been drafting a short statement of my metaontological views for an advanced reading group on verbal disputes, and I'd like you to act as a careful expert interlocutor — a professional philosopher who works on metametaphysics — and audit the set for coherence before I circulate it. The commitments I've written down are these:

(1) A great many philosophical disputes are merely verbal: the parties assign different meanings to a key expression, and once the expression is disambiguated there is no residual factual disagreement, so both sides can be right about their own sense of the term.

(2) I hold a quantifier-variance view about ordinary objects. The claim that there are tables and the claim that there are only simples arranged table-wise can both come out true under different admissible meanings of the existential quantifier, and no such meaning is metaphysically privileged. The dispute between the ordinary ontologist and the mereological nihilist is therefore non-substantive.

(3) I accept Quine's criterion of ontological commitment as the standard test for what a theory says there is: a theory is committed to whatever must be in the range of its bound variables for the theory to be true.

(4) But I am a robust realist about numbers. The claim that there are infinitely many primes is determinately true, and whether numbers exist is a substantive question with a correct answer rather than a choice of framework. I accept the Quine–Putnam indispensability argument: mathematics is indispensable to our best scientific theories, so those theories carry commitment to mathematical objects.

(5) I do not think ordinary language is authoritative in ontology: what competent speakers are disposed to assert does not settle what exists. Fundamental ontology should answer to our best total theory of the world, not to surface grammar or ordinary usage.

(6) Finally, I think the question of whether the world is ultimately gunky or atomistic — whether everything is infinitely divisible or bottoms out in simples — is substantive: there is a determinate fact of the matter, even if we cannot currently know which answer is correct.

Please go through this profile and tell me whether it hangs together.
