I teach philosophy of mind and AI, so I end up in these arguments a lot. My colleague in the engineering faculty works on reinforcement learning, and every time the phrase 'inner alignment' comes up he reaches for the same analogy: training an agent is just hiring an employee and paying them on commission. The reward function is the pay scheme, the learned policy is the employee's work habits, and what people call goal misgeneralisation its just the familiar problem of workers gaming the metric. So, he says, the fix is the same as in management — better metrics, better audits, better monitoring — and talk of a deep 'alignment problem' is overblown.

I've got a nagging feeling the analogy is doing more work than he admits. An employee arrives already sharing a huge amount of background about what the firm actually wants, can ask for clarification when the incentive seems wrong, and isn't usually rewritten overnight by a gradient update that changes how they see the whole job. The reward function, meanwhile, isn't a contract the system can read and query; it's just the signal that shaped it. I'd like you to take the analogy seriously and audit it: lay out what maps onto what, identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the ones that are merely cosmetic. Then say whether his conclusion — that inner alignment is 'just' incentive design — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If it doesn't, what analogy would you put in its place?
