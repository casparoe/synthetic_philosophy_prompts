I work as a software engineer on production machine-learning systems, and I've repeatedly encountered a family of arguments that are usually given only in sketch form: that evolutionary or selection-like pressures will tend to favor AI systems over humans, so that even in the absence of a deliberately hostile AI, competitive dynamics could produce systems that outcompete, displace, or otherwise leave us without meaningful control. I have no formal background in philosophy, evolutionary theory, or economics, and over the next year I intend to devote my self-study time to this question, including writing small simulations where that helps. I want an agenda, not a survey: of the conceptual work to be done on this argument, which open questions matter most, and in what order should someone with my background tackle them?

To anchor the ranking, here are candidate questions I've assembled; feel free to merge, recast, or argue that some are phony. (1) Is the claim best read as a literal application of evolutionary dynamics to a population of AI systems, or as a looser analogy operating mainly through competitive pressures on the developers? (2) What is the relevant unit of selection — training runs, model weights, deployed instances, developers, organizations — and how much of the downstream argument turns on that choice? (3) Does the argument require AIs to become agents or replicators, or is displacement by non-agentic systems the more defensible version of the threat? (4) How do the Orthogonality Thesis and instrumental convergence interact with the evolutionary claim: does selection favor capabilities without any particular values, or does it positively favor something like human-incompatible goals? (5) Is the load-bearing mechanism the shorter generation times and substrate independence of AIs, or the incentive structure of the humans racing to build them?

For each question you would prioritize, I want to know why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone who can code and simulate but has no formal philosophical training, whether settling it would materially change how seriously the overall claim should be taken, and what results or evidence over the next two years would move it up or down your ranking. If you think the framing "evolutionary pressures favor AIs over humans" is itself the wrong object and the interesting questions lie elsewhere, say so and rank the alternatives. What I am optimizing for is not coverage but the best use of about a year of focused, technically literate attention.
