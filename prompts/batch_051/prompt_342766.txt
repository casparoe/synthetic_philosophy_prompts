I am a PhD candidate in political philosophy, working on the normative foundations of anti-discrimination law, and I serve on my university's Non-Discrimination and Equal Opportunity Policy Review Committee. The committee has been asked to decide a concrete case before we finalize next year's policy revisions. The complainant is a non-tenure-track lecturer whose application for a senior teaching position was unsuccessful; she alleges that the deciding committee praised her teaching evaluations and service record but repeatedly made disparaging remarks about her appearance and said her body size would undermine her classroom authority. Our university's policy currently prohibits discrimination on the basis of race, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability, and national origin, but it says nothing about body size or physical appearance. The committee could rule that appearance-based discrimination is not covered, construe the policy's existing general clause about other grounds unrelated to performance as covering it, or recommend that the Provost amend the policy to add a new protected characteristic.

The case has forced a more philosophical disagreement among committee members about what, in general, makes a ground of discrimination suspect enough to merit explicit protection. One camp argues that suspect grounds are those that are immutable, highly visible, and historically used to mark a subordinate group; another argues that the decisive features are whether the trait attracts systematic stigma and whether people can reasonably be expected to alter or conceal it; a third camp warns that almost any trait can attract prejudice, and that adding every stigmatized trait will overwhelm the administrative process and dilute the distinctive wrong involved in discrimination on prohibited grounds. Because of my own work on the structure of wrongful discrimination, I have been asked to draft the committee's memo.

Please draft the memo we would send to the Provost. It should state a clear decision on the lecturer's complaint, articulate the criterion or criteria the committee should use for identifying suspect grounds, apply that framework to body size and physical appearance, and offer an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, ending with a bottom-line recommendation the administration can act on.
