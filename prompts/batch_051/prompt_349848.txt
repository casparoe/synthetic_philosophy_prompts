I have been reading about Douglas Hofstadter's model of creativity, which treats much of creative thought as subtle variation on an existing theme: you take a familiar pattern and slowly turn its parameters, which he calls knobs, while watching which changes keep the pattern recognizable and which ones cause it to collapse. The interesting claim is that discovering which knobs actually matter and which ones are only decorative is a way of discovering what the original pattern was really about. My philosophy club has been trying this method on classic thought experiments, and I would like to work through one case carefully because I am not sure I trust my own reasoning.

Consider the ordinary Sleeping Beauty puzzle. On Sunday, Beauty is put to sleep and a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, she is woken once on Monday and interviewed. If it lands tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, but between those awakenings she is given a memory-erasing drug, so when she wakes she cannot tell Monday from Tuesday, and she knew the whole setup in advance. Each time she wakes she is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. As I understand it, one standard answer is one half, because the coin was fair and waking up does not seem to teach her which way it landed, while the rival answer is one third, because if the experiment were repeated many times, only one of every three awakenings, on average, would be a heads-awakening.

Now change only a single knob. Suppose everything remains the same except that if the coin lands tails, Beauty is woken and interviewed one hundred times instead of twice, with her memory erased between consecutive awakenings, and she is told this in advance; the coin itself is still fair. On a particular awakening, what should her credence in heads be under each of the two standard styles of reasoning? Does the style that gave one third in the original puzzle now give one over one hundred and one, forcing roughly ninety-nine percent credence in tails, and does the one-half style stay put? In Hofstadter's terms, which knobs in this puzzle are load-bearing - the fairness of the coin, the number of awakenings, the memory erasure, the single heads-awakening - and what does turning the number-of-awakenings knob up this high reveal about what the disagreement between the one-half and one-third camps was really about?
