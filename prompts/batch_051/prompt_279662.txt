Ok so I'm writing a one-pager on where I stand with constructivism for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency check before I show people. Here are my committments:

(1) I buy the Brouwer-Heyting-Kolmogorov interpretation: the meaning of a mathematical statement just is what counts as a constructive proof of it, so I'm some kind of intuitionist.
(2) I don't accept excluded middle in general. For instance, I won't assert "either there's an odd perfect number or there isn't" until someone actually finds one or proves none exists; until then the disjunction isn't estbalished.
(3) But I do think arithmetic has a determinate subject matter: there's one standard model of the naturals, it's a completed infinite structure, and every sentence of Peano arithmetic is determinately true or false in it, even when we can't tell which.
(4) I also accept that ¬¬(P ∨ ¬P) is an intuitionistic theorem for any P. I don't see how this gives me excluded middle back, but I won't deny the theorem.
(5) I think intuitionistic logic is just a restriction of classical logic: every constructive proof is classically valid, not conversely. Classical mathematics is coherent, just too permissive for my taste.

Does this hang together? My main worry is (2) and (4): if I don't accept P ∨ ¬P, how can I accept its double negation — doesn't that sneak excluded middle in through a back door? I also can't tell whether (3) sits oddly with the rest or is fine, since truth and provability are just different things. Please rank any tensions by severity, say which pairs are only apparently in tension, and tell me what the cheapest fix is if something has to go.
