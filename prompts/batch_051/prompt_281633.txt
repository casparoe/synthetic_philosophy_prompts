In my scientific career I internalized the rule that belief should not outrun evidence: a colleague who announced a result before the data justified it lost standing, and we taught students that suspending judgment under uncertainty was a virtue. But I notice that in personal life we condemn the very disposition we cultivate in science. A father who waits for adequate evidence before believing his son is telling the truth, or a husband who suspends judgment about his wife's fidelity until he has proof, is not regarded as scrupulous; he is regarded as lacking something essential to the relation. So we seem to demand that belief wait on evidence in one sphere and that evidence wait on belief, or trust, in another. Is there a principled way to hold both norms, or is one of them a mistake? If they cannot be reconciled, tell me which one should be given up, and defend that verdict against the obvious objection that it licenses either gullibility or coldness.
