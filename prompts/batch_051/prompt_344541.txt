I'm a software engineer who works on computational photography, and I have only an informal background in philosophy. I've run into a distinction I can't make precise, and I'd like a working definition.

When people say that a photograph is transparent — that in looking at a photo of a dog you are, in some sense, seeing the dog itself, and not merely seeing a marked surface — I think I understand the claim. But my own tools have made it harder for me to say exactly what the claim amounts to. I'd like you to define the relevant relation: something like subject S sees through image I to scene O. A definition I can run mechanically against cases.

Here are the cases I want the definition to handle:

1. A single well-focused color photograph of a dog, taken with an ordinary lens and viewed normally.
2. A careful pencil drawing of the same dog, copied from that photograph.
3. A live video feed from a security camera in the next room, displayed on a monitor.
4. An HDR image made by merging thirty bracketed exposures so that shadow and highlight detail are visible that no individual exposure recorded.
5. A thermal image of a house, showing heat leaking through a wall.
6. A deepfake video of a real politician appearing to say something the politician never said.
7. A photogrammetric reconstruction of a sculpture, rendered from a novel viewpoint that no actual camera occupied.
8. A long-exposure photograph of star trails, whose arcs correspond to no moment anyone could have seen.

I'm confident that (1) and (3) are cases of seeing through, and confident that (2) and (6) are not. I genuinely do not know what to say about (4), (5), (7), and (8).

Propose a definition of seeing through an image, run it through all eight cases, and tell me where it conflicts with my verdicts or changes them. If your definition gives counterintuitive answers somewhere, either defend those answers or revise the definition. Because I work in digital imaging, the definition should not quietly assume that a picture is a single optical exposure from one lens and sensor; it should keep working for computational and synthesized images.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the definition you end with, and single out the point you are least sure of.
