Ok, I'm putting together a little "where I stand" statement for a seminar on moral psychology, and I want someone to kick the tires before I read it out. The seminar is about whether emotions like envy and resentment should be treated as vices to be expunged, or as signals that a social arrangement is unjust. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) Envy is a vice, full stop. It aims at another person's good—wanting it diminished or wanting them brought down—so it's never the right response to a situation, even a deeply unjust one.
(b) Resentment is different in kind: it responds to a wrong done to me or to someone I'm tied to, and it can be a fitting expression of self-respect, a way of demanding to be treated as an equal.
(c) Yet occurent emotions aren't under direct voluntary control, so it seems wrong to say anyone is morally required to feel or not feel one at the moment it arises.
(d) At the same time, the Rawlsian thought that envy becomes excusable when inequalities get so severe that they damage the less advantaged person's self-respect strikes me as right—where excusable means understandable and not something to pile extra blame on, not that the envy itself is good.
(e) But in the serious cases I actually care about, I'm tempted to say that what gets called envy in the face of steep inequality is often really a confused form of indignation at an unfair advantage: the person wants the hierarchy gone, not the rich person's goods to be destroyed, even if they report it as wanting what the other person has.
(f) And a decent society should be working to remove the conditions that generate these feelings—status competition, winner-take-all outcomes—rather than mainly asking the worse-off to manage their emotional lives better.

So: which of these genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each real tension? Rank them by severity, say for each what would have to give, and don't fall back on saying the whole thing is fine or that it's all just a mess; I need to know which revision buys me the most coherence.
