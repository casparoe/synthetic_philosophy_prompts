I've been turning something over in my head and I cant tell if it has a name or if I'm just describing being bad with money. My brother is the example. Every January, when his work does one of those payout choices for a cash prize, he picks the later, bigger check—say $55 in April instead of $40 in March. Both feel far away in January, so he just takes the bigger number. But when March gets here, he wants the $40 that day; waiting another month for $55 suddenly feels like forever. So he pays a small fee to switch to the earlier, smaller check. The guy who runs the pool is happy to make that switch every single time. In April my brother is mad at himself and says next year he will really hold out.

He does the same thing with a card he owns that spikes in price before a local convention every year. In October he swears he is holding until January, he wouldnt take a penny under the post-convention price. Then the week before the convention somebody flashes cash in front of him and he sells early, and the price jumps without him. Every year.

To be clear, I havent done this to him and I dont want to, but I also can't stop noticing that a person who knows him well could just stand next to him and collect a fee every time his mind changes. At no point would he say he was tricked—each choice is the one he'd defend in that exact moment.

So I'm not asking for life advice about my brother. I want to know if there's a name for this setup: a person gets drained across time by his own shifting preferences, even though at any single moment his preferences seem perfectly consistent. Is that the same thing as the thing where somebody's preferences go in a circle all at once—they'd trade A for B, B for C, C for A—or is it a different animal? If its different, what do you call it, and where exactly is the line between the two? Also, is my brother making a mistake in any real sense, or is this just what having changing priorities looks like? A one-word label won't satisfy me; I want the carving, the boundaries, the part where it stops being the nearby thing and becomes this thing.
