Suppose a country replaces its existing paid parental leave arrangements with the following individual scheme. Every new parent is eligible for a benefit equal to 80 percent of their own average earnings over the previous two years, up to a cap, for a maximum of fourteen weeks. The benefit is paid by the state, but the full amount is recorded as a personal, interest-free debt. Repayment is collected as a flat 2 percent surcharge on that parent's taxable income above a low exemption for twenty years from the child's birth, after which any outstanding balance is forgiven; the debt also lapses at the parent's death. The surcharge is paused in any year in which the parent has no taxable income or is the primary carer of a child under three. Both parents can qualify on the basis of their own earnings, and either may transfer up to half of their weeks to the other, but the cash benefit and the associated debt always follow the parent who actually takes the leave. Treat the scheme as a mechanism rather than a policy to endorse or reject. Analyze how it would actually behave: who would take leave and for how long, how couples would use the transfer option, what it would do to labor supply after leave and to the distribution between higher and lower earners, how it would interact with employers' own leave policies, and what gaming or avoidance it would invite, especially around the pause and forgiveness rules. Also consider second-order effects on childbearing, family structure, and the normalization of leave-taking. Finally, say which of the scheme's failure modes could be fixed without gutting it and which could not. Please answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
