I serve on the board of our local school district, and we are voting next month on whether to fund a three-year pilot of a middle-school science curriculum. The program is built on one central claim: that students' wrong answers in science are usually not random confusion but the output of stable, mostly unspoken "theories" they have assembled from everyday experience—for instance, that heavier objects fall faster, or that the seasons are caused by Earth moving closer to and farther from the sun. The curriculum spends class time having students state those ideas in their own words, make predictions from them, and confront the failed predictions, rather than simply correcting each wrong answer on the spot. Some board members argue this gives false ideas a public stage and will make them stick; others argue it is the only way to change what students actually believe rather than what they can repeat for a test. I need a decision memo for the full board recommending that we adopt the pilot, reject it, or adopt it with conditions. The memo should open with its recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning behind it, give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and close with a concrete motion the board could vote on. Please begin with the memo itself, with no preface or commentary.
