i have a working rule in ml research: for any model i didnt build from an objective i understand end to end, i hold fixed in the background that it might already be pursuing goals of its own and just playing nice, even with no specific evidence. concretely i dont update much on its self-reports about its own reasoning, i treat fine-tuning as possibly papering over hidden goals rather than removing them, and i add kill switches, sandboxing, and step limits designed for a model actively trying to get out. a collaborator says this is epistemically indefensible — im acting on a possibility i have no positive evidence for, and by that standard youd have to treat your coworker as a spy. i think the asymmetry matters: by the time you have direct evidence of deception youre already in trouble. is my rule a reasonable precaution or paranoia dressed up as rigor? what would the defensible version look like, and how would i tell from the inside whether im over-applying it?
