Build me a bet where a full Kelly bettor—maximizing expected log wealth, i.e. geometric growth—and someone who maximizes expected terminal wealth genuinely disagree about how much to stake each round, and where the disagreement isn't just about one of them being more risk averse. Setup: one biased coin with known odds, even-money payouts, you can risk any fraction of your bankroll each round but no borrowing, and the bet repeats for a small number of rounds so the arithmetic stays checkable. The case should have the full Kelly bettor solvent and the expected-wealth maximizer broke on the single most likely sequence (the one with exactly the expected share of heads). Then do the check: state each policy's per-round fraction, compute terminal wealth on that typical sequence, and say which structural feature is doing the work. Finish by saying what a defender of maximizing expected terminal wealth would say in reply, and what happens to the force of the disagreement (not necessarily the two bet sizes) if you drop the repetition and make it a one-shot bet.
