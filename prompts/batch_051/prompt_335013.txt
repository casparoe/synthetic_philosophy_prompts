I have a rule for stuff people tell me, and I honestly can't tell if it's sensible or just me being paranoid. If someone tells me something surprising, or something that would make me think worse of another person, I don't count it as true unless I get it from a second person who didn't just hear it from the first person, or unless I can check it myself. Boring everyday stuff I accept straight away. Last month a boy in my year told me a quiet kid in our class had been caught shoplifting, and I basically said okay and then didn't use the information. A few days later a girl who doesn't hang around with him at all mentioned the same thing, and only then did I start treating it as probably true. It turned out the main fact was right but alot of the details were wrong, which made me feel justified and also a bit sneaky, like I was waiting to convict someone in my head only once there were enough witnesses. My friend says the rule is unfair because it treats everyone like a liar until proven otherwise, and she says it's inconsistent too: I'll accept all sorts of things from teachers and textbooks without a second source, and I can't actually check most of those myself. I think she has a point, but the rule has saved me from believing rubbish more than once. So: is my rule defensible, or is it just mistrust dressed up as a policy? Put differently, do I always need a positive reason to believe what I'm told, or is it okay to take people as reliable until I have a specific reason not to? Is there a better version of the rule that keeps the good parts without treating my friends as unreliable by default? And how would I tell from the inside whether the line I've drawn is the right one, rather than me just being arbitrary? Please answer in British English.
