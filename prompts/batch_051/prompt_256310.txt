I run a small philosophy of language blog and there's a recurring argument in the comments about the difference between conditionals like If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did and the matching If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have. I want to turn this into a flowchart-style quiz for readers — "which semantics of conditionals do you actually hold?" — but I'm wary of the usual format where people just tick the label they fancy. I want it to sort people by what they say about cases first, so someone who's never heard of Stalnaker or Adams still comes out holding, say, a no-truth-conditions probability view without knowing its name.

Could you build me a decision tree with eight to ten diagnostic nodes, each one a concrete forced-choice case or inference phrased without jargon? The leaves should at least cover: material implication, a strict conditional view, a similarity/possible-worlds view that accepts conditional excluded middle, a similarity view that rejects it, a probabilistic no-truth-conditions view (at least for indicatives), and a dualist mixed view where indicatives and counterfactuals work differently. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: set out the underlying questions first (does the sentence have a truth value? does strengthening the antecedent preserve truth? must there be a unique closest way for the antecedent to come out true? do indicative and subjunctive use the same machinery?), map the possible answer combinations, then locate the named positions inside that space and say which combinations have no live position and briefly why — internally unstable, merely unattractive, or nobody's bothered to defend it. Include the Oswald pair and a future indicative against a weak subjunctive as the indicative/counterfactual diagnostic, a dry-match/wet-match pair to test antecedent strengthening, the Bizet and Verdi compatriots case to test conditional excluded middle, and at least one case with a conditional embedded under negation or in the antecedent of another conditional. At the end flag which positions the tree can't seperate with only these cases — I suspect a restrictor/dynamic view and a strict conditional only come apart with quantified or modalised cases — and tell me the extra case that would finally pull them apart.

It's for an audience that's had roughly one term of formal semantics, so the leaf names and notes can use technical language but the nodes themselves have to be answerable by someone with no training at all. Give a short scoring key so people who prefer a quiz can add up their path instead of following the flowchart. No citations needed; I'll add a short reading list on the blog myself.
