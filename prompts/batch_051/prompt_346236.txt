The question was worth 20 points, with the rubric as follows: 8 points for stating the asymmetry accurately, 6 points for explaining its role in the comparison between existence and non-existence, and 6 points for identifying what the asymmetry does and does not establish on its own. Here are the question and the answer.

Question: State Benatar's asymmetry between pleasure and pain across the two states of a person's existing and that person's never existing. Then explain what Benatar takes the asymmetry to show about coming into existence, and identify what further claims beyond the asymmetry are needed to reach his conclusion about the ethics of procreation.

Student answer: Benatar's asymmetry contrasts pleasure and pain across two states — the state in which a person exists and the state in which that person never exists. In existence, pain is bad and pleasure is good; that half is uncontroversial. In non-existence, the absence of pain is good, a benefit to the would-be child, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad, because there is no one there to be deprived of it. The asymmetry is that the absence of a bad thing counts in favor of non-existence, while the absence of a good thing does not count against it. Non-existence therefore gets credit for all the pain an existing person would have suffered, but it is not penalized for the pleasures an existing person would have enjoyed. Since every life contains at least some pain, non-existence comes out ahead in every case, which is why Benatar takes coming into existence to be a harm in every case. It follows directly from the asymmetry that any life containing any pain is not worth living — avoided pain counts in favor of non-existence while forgone pleasure does not count against it, so no amount of pleasure can compensate. To get from this to his conclusion about procreation, Benatar needs only the modest premise that it is wrong to inflict a harm on someone when one could refrain at no comparable cost; from that he concludes that procreation is always a harm and always morally wrong.
