I keep running into a dispute about the status of the washing out of priors. One side says it is an empirical claim about finite learners, not merely an asymptotic theorem about ideal Bayesians: if two learners start from different priors but receive the same finite sequence of observations in the same order, their predictions should measurably move toward one another, and the remaining predictive disagreement should be traceable mostly to how they weight early versus recent evidence, not to the initial priors themselves. How would one go about testing that claim? Design the strongest concrete test you can, whether computational, experimental, or both. Say what pattern of results would support the claim, what pattern would count against it, and what a favourable result would still leave open. I suspect the claim runs together the convergence of forecasts with the unrecoverability of the prior, so make explicit which component your test operationalizes and which component has to remain a modelling decision. Please also say how confident you are in your proposed design and single out the point you are least sure about.
