I am a retired physical chemist who has only recently turned to philosophy, and I have been reading the free-will literature with an amateur's mixture of fascination and impatience at how often a single line is made to do too much argumentative work. In a discussion group, a participant repeatedly responded to compatibilist proposals by invoking the one-liner that one cannot be ultimately responsible for who one is, and that this settles the question of moral responsibility. The line appears to be a loose paraphrase of the conclusion of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, though I have also seen it used much more crudely. I would like to know exactly what claims that line could be making.

Separate the distinct precise theses this slogan might express, from the trivially true to the radical and possibly false, and state for each how plausible it is as a reading of Strawson's argument and whether it actually supports denying ordinary moral responsibility. In particular, I want to know which readings depend on the impossibility of being causa sui, which depend on determinism, which depend on luck, and which depend on a specific definition of moral responsibility. I would be grateful if you would write the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each item one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
