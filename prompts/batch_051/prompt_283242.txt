Political scientists and commentators now routinely call Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States cases of "democratic backsliding," but critics answer that the term is applied selectively and has become little more than a partisan label. Before I use it in my own research, I want the standard itself. Write the desiderata that an adequate account—or definition—of democratic backsliding must satisfy, where "adequate" means usable in comparative research and in public monitoring without collapsing into partisanship. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it: the paradigm and borderline cases the account must classify correctly (including the four countries above), the distinctions it cannot collapse—for example, backsliding versus ordinary policy disagreement, a decline in liberal rights versus a decline in electoral democracy, and recent backsliding versus long-standing democratic deficits—and the institutional facts a measurement of it must respect. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect the priorities of one school or camp. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude popular accounts, and tell me which requirement you are least confident belongs on the list and what consideration would lead you to revise or drop it. Write in American English.
