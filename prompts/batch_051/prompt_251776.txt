A friend in my behavioral econ seminar walked me through this and I can't find the exit. It starts from the standard picture of weakness of will plus the standard picture of hyperbolic discounting, and ends up concluding that weakness of will is basically an illusion. The conclusion feels to strong to accept, but I can't see where the chain bends.

(1) Weakness of will is acting against your all-things-considered judgment at the time you act. Merely breaking an earlier resolution isn't akrasia; at most you changed your mind.

(2) Suppose I resolve at 10pm to go to sleep early, and at 1am I'm still scrolling. If I'm a hyperbolic discounter, this is a preference reversal: from 10pm's perspective the delayed reward (being rested tomorrow) ranks higher, but from 1am's perspective the immediate reward (one more video) ranks higher, because a hyperbolic discount function weights near things much more heavily. Nothing changed except which option is near in time; I didn't learn anything new.

(3) Decision theory's notion of rational choice for an agent at a time is: choose the available option that maximizes that agent's expected value at that time.

(4) So at 1am, scrolling maximizes my current expected value; it's what my own discount function ranks first for me at 1am.

(5) If scrolling is what my own current value function ranks first, then—so long as my all-things-considered judgment is in touch with my values—I either judge, or coherently would judge, that scrolling is the best thing to do at 1am.

(6) So at the very moment I seem to be weak-willed, I'm actually going with my current all-things-considered ranking. That isn't akrasia. It's just two different momentary selves, each maximizing its own discount function.

(7) Therefore: if hyperbolic discounting is the right model, ordinary weakness of will doesn't exist—what we call akrasia is an artifact of comparing the rankings of two different time-selves. If you want to keep saying the 1am me is genuinely weak-willed, you must say the 1am self's values are not the ones that settle what I have reason to do, and then hyperbolic discounting stops being a theory of what I should do and becomes only a mechanical description of temptation.

So you can't have both—not without a patch I can't find. I want to keep both: im pretty sure people are sometimes genuinely weak-willed, and I also think hyperbolic discounting is capturing something real. Which numbered step do I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it force me to give up? Or is the conclusion just right, and "weakness of will" is a confabulated label for a shift between selves?
