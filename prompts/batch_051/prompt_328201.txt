I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and I am applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship. I need to submit a two-page research proposal, and I would like you to draft it for me. My working area is the interpretation of gauge theories, and I have a half-formed idea that I want turned into a defensible proposal.

The default view in much of the philosophy of physics is that the gauge freedom of classical electromagnetism and Yang-Mills theory is merely redundant description: any two configurations related by a gauge transformation represent the same physical possibility, and the gauge-variant degrees of freedom are surplus structure with no physical content. The main alternative has been to treat some gauge-invariant but nonlocal quantities, such as holonomies or Wilson loops, as the real content of the theory, largely because of the Aharonov-Bohm effect.

I suspect both positions are using too blunt an instrument. Whether a degree of freedom counts as surplus or physical seems to depend on two things that are usually left implicit: the boundary conditions and the domain on which the field is defined, and the algebra of observables one is willing to treat as measurable. Two phenomena make this vivid. First, the Gribov problem in non-Abelian gauge theories, meaning the failure of continuous gauge-fixing conditions to work globally, shows that the configuration space is not cleanly partitioned into gauge equivalence classes in the way the simple redundancy picture assumes. Second, on manifolds with boundaries, degrees of freedom that are pure gauge in the bulk can become physical at the boundary, as with edge modes. I want to defend a deflationary but precise thesis: the claim that gauge freedom is surplus structure is not made true by the formalism alone; it holds only relative to a stipulated space of kinematically possible states and a stipulated class of observables, and it can fail at a boundary or in the presence of a Gribov obstruction even within a single theory.

Please write the proposal so that it contains all of the following:

1. A precise statement of the research question, formulated so that it has a determinate answer rather than collapsing into a vague "it depends." Something like: under what conditions are we justified in identifying gauge-related configurations as the same physical possibility, and does the correct answer force us to replace a single global notion of surplus structure with a context-relative one?

2. An explanation of why the existing positions leave the question open. The redundancy view and the holonomy-based view both seem to assign each theory a single verdict about what is surplus and what is physical, and neither view explains what changes at a boundary or when gauge fixing fails globally.

3. A method section. The project should be a philosophical analysis driven by two concrete case studies, classical electromagnetism and non-Abelian Yang-Mills theory, rather than an original physics computation. I want it to say what I would actually do, month by month, over the fellowship year.

4. A clear statement of what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure, stated honestly.

5. A realistic scope section saying what can be completed in a year and what will be explicitly set aside.

Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will add references myself. The proposal should be written for a committee of philosophers who are not necessarily specialists in gauge theory, so any technical notion that appears should be briefly explained the first time it is used.
