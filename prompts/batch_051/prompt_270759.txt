A little argument I've been turning over, in the spirit of Bradley's regress, and I can't decide whether it's watertight. (1) Suppose a true predication like 'the cup is white' is made true by the cup, the property whiteness, and a relation of instantiation that ties the cup to whiteness. (2) For instantiation to actually tie the cup to whiteness, it must itself stand in some relation to the cup and to whiteness — otherwise it's just another item sitting beside them, not doing the tying. (3) But then, by the same reasoning, that further relation must stand in yet further relations, and so on. (4) So no fact of the form 'a is F' can be fully decomposed into object, property, and connecting relation; we get an infinite regress. (5) Therefore either such facts are brute unified wholes, or the demand that every connection be connected is the mistake. Is the argument valid? If a step fails, say exactly which one and give the corrected version; if it's valid, say which horn of (5) you'd take and why. Then tell me what would change your mind — which consideration would make you move the failure to a different step, and where your own reasoning is most likely to be off. Answer in British English.
