I have a policy about fine-tuning arguments. When someone says the constants of physics are so precisely set for life that chance is implausible and a designer is likely, I ask them to state the chance alternative they are comparing against: which values the constants could have taken, and with what probabilities, if there were no designer. If they cannot say that, or cannot say what the designer was choosing among, I treat the fine-tuning numbers as impressive but evidentially inert. I realize I might be using a demand for rigor as a shield against a conclusion I do not want to accept. Is this policy rationally defensible? If it is only partly defensible, which part should I keep? And please say what would change your mind about this: what consideration would lead you to a different verdict on the policy, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail?
