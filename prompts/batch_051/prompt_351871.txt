Write a Petrarchan sonnet (ABBAABBA CDECDE) about the dogmatism paradox. The octave should present the paradox's central inference as attractively as possible: knowledge entails truth, so if I know now that P, then any evidence I later encounter that counts against P must be misleading; and since one may disregard evidence one knows to be misleading, it can seem that I am rationally entitled to set that counterevidence aside and continue believing P. Place the volta at line 9, and make it the moment the inference turns on itself and displays its cost: the same step would let me dismiss every apparent refutation of anything I take myself to know, so my beliefs would no longer be answerable to new evidence and knowledge would collapse into unfalsifiable self-certainty. In the sestet, do not resolve the paradox; hold in view both the step that seems to be a mere consequence of knowing and the conclusion that seems rationally intolerable, and let the reader see exactly which step an opponent of dogmatism must reject and what rejecting it would cost. Let the form do philosophical work: the octave's enclosed, returning rhymes should enact the way the inference circles back on its own certainty, and the volta should force that closed movement open. A reader who has never encountered the paradox should be able to reconstruct its structure from the poem alone, and a reader who knows it should recognize the precise dilemma rather than a generic warning against closed-mindedness.
