Two members of my Spinoza reading group have been arguing for months about what Spinoza’s ontology actually commits us to, and I am trying to get them to produce something constructive instead of another round of rebuttals. One is a priority monist: she accepts Spinoza’s conclusion that there is exactly one substance and that finite things—tables, persons, stones—are modes whose reality is entirely dependent on that one substance. The other is a pluralist: he thinks ordinary finite things are substances in their own right, or at least that any view that treats them as mere modes must explain away real difference, causal independence among finite things, and the possibility of incompatibility or conflict. They have agreed to anchor their collaboration in this passage from Spinoza’s Ethics, Part I, Definition III and Axiom I:

“By substance, I mean that which is in itself, and is conceived through itself: in other words, that of which a conception can be formed independently of any other conception.”

And Axiom I:

“Everything which exists, exists either in itself or in something else.”

Please draft the joint statement these two people should produce, under the constraint that both could sign it. The document should include: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps accept, formulated so that neither side feels the wording is slanted; (2) a precise statement of the residual disagreements in neutral terms, with each side’s own position stated separately in a way the other side can acknowledge as fair; and (3) the cruxes—the specific sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—with, for each crux, a concrete case or test both agree bears on it and each side’s prediction recorded in advance. Do not adjudicate between them. The deliverable is the document itself, with the disagreements made exact rather than resolved.
