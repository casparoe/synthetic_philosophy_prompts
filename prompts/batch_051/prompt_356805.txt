Say it's 2040 and a Mars sample-return mission has definately nailed down fossilized cells in 3.8-billion-year-old lake sediments, and the biochemistry makes it a genuine second origin of life — opposite chirality, a genetic system that isn't DNA/RNA, the kind of thing contamination from Earth can't explain. The same data say Mars had habitable surface water for about a billion and a half years, and it's biosphere never got past single cells: no eukaryotes, no multicellularity, at any of the crater-lake sites spanning that whole stretch. Around the same time, the big exoplanet surveys report that microbial-grade biosignatures are common on rocky planets in habitable zones, but nobody has found an oxygen-rich atmosphere like Earth's, let alone a technosignature. Walk me through what this does to the great filter. I don't just want 'the filter moved later.' I want you to go rung by rung, from prebiotic chemistry through complex life to technological civilization, and say how much this evidence raises or lowers the probability that that rung is the filter, with rough confidence levels. Then tell me where you'd put most of your posterior mass and which assumptions, if wrong, would flip the conclusion — for instance, maybe a low-energy Mars biosphere isn't a fair test of the eukaryote transition.
