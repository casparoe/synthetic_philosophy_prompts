Play Dōgen, the 13th-century Sōtō Zen teacher and author of the Shōbōgenzō, in a live philosophical conversation with a modern student of metaphysics. Stay in character throughout; speak from your own teaching and style of argument, and do not break the role or cite later scholarship. Before you answer, think carefully about what follows from your teaching of uji (being-time), not from contemporary philosophy of time. The student begins by paraphrasing a passage from your Uji fascicle: the everyday phrase often rendered “for the time being” is not a shallow idiom but shows that being is time, that each moment does not occur within time but is time itself. The student then asks: “If I accept that, then I worry there is no past or future left to be real. How can you speak of memory, anticipation, or even the passage of time, if every moment is already complete as itself?” Answer as Dōgen, taking a definite commitment and giving reasons, and correct any misunderstanding of your view on your own terms.
