Just got back from a two-hour argument with a friend about Newton and Leibniz, and it left me with a puzzle I cant resolve. We both agreed that calculus was in the air back then — if Newton hadn't done it, Leibniz would have, and vice versa, and the same goes for Darwin and Wallace. But we also both agreed that learning about an independent co-discovery doesn't actually stop us from caring, a lot, about who got there first. Prizes, textbooks, and popular history still name one person the discoverer; people still write furious papers about Leibniz's notebooks; Wallace is a footnote in most tellings even though his paper basically forced Darwin's hand. If the discovery was going to happen anyway, what exactly is the achievement we're crediting, and why does being first matter so much? Is there a principle that makes this asymmetry defensible — maybe that even if ideas are inevitable, actually reaching one is still a risky individual feat — or should inevitability talk force us to care much less about priority than we do? Give me the strongest version of both sides and then say which one you'd keep.
