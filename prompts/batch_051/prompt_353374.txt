Lately I have been trying to get clear on a taxonomy of minds I first met in passing — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures — where the difference is supposed to lie in where and when the trial-and-error that builds a creature's competence takes place. Darwinian creatures get their design only from variation and selection across generations; Skinnerian creatures add selection of behavior within a lifetime by reinforcement, though still without foresight; Popperian creatures can run candidate moves through an inner modeling space and let bad hypotheses perish there rather than in the world; and Gregorian creatures can inherit cognitive tools — words, notations, diagrams, procedures — built by other minds, so that design improvements are transmitted culturally rather than re-derived by each individual. Before I read yet another paper that uses this ladder, I want the specification of what a theory in this area must deliver to count as adequate. Give me the desiderata: the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the comparative-cognition results it cannot flatly contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of particular camps.

The cases I most want to see addressed are the ones that make the ladder look too clean: a rat that learns a lever-press by chancy reinforcement; a honeybee that tracks changing flower rewards with modest flexibility; a songbird that shapes its song by hearing its own attempts; a scrub jay that behaves as if it is weighing where it might want breakfast tomorrow; a chimpanzee that fashions a tool but seems unable to pass the procedure to others; and a human child who masters parity and place-value notation, not by inventing them, but by catching them as public technologies. For each, say what a theory would have to be right about, and where in the Darwinian/Skinnerian/Popperian/Gregorian picture the case puts pressure. Also tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular versions of the taxonomy, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied without making the framework untestable or too coarse. Keep the criteria, and the case behind each, the center of the answer; a brief scorecard of existing views is fine, but only after the list is built.
