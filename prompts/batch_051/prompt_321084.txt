ive been going down a rabbit hole reading about moloch and multipolar traps and races to the bottom (the idea where everyone doing what makes sense for them individually makes things worse for everyone, like an arms race or doping in sports). and i think ive spotted a distinction that ppl dont really make, or at least ive never seen it spelled out.

i call them loud traps and quiet traps.

a loud trap is where its obvious your in a competition. theres a visible rival or at least a visible mechanism, everyone knows the situation is bad, and you can point to the moment you had to choose to enter. arms races, doping, price wars, maybe the obvious version of social media making everyone perform.

a quiet trap is where you never actually feel like you entered. each step is locally reasonable, often even kind or responsible. nobody makes a big visible move. the trap builds up through defaults, deadlines, small frictions, people copying people copying people. by the time you notice its a trap, opting out costs way more than staying in, but you cant name the step where you "chose" to get in. examples: grade inflation (a teacher who refuses to fail hardworking students is being decent, but the whole system drifts), the college admissions extracurricular arms race, schools where everyone is sleep deprived because the homework load just sort of ratcheted up, beauty standards where each person is just doing normal grooming but it aggregates into something insane.

my actual claim isnt just that there are two kinds. its that quiet traps are harder to escape than loud ones, but not because the incentives are stronger. its because there is no "start" to point to, so theres nothing to coordinate around. in a loud trap you can at least call a ceasefire or negotiate rules. in a quiet trap you cant coordinate against a choice nobody admits theyre making, and anyone who tries to stop it ends up looking unreasonable or preachy because every individual step was defensible. i also think its a mistake to talk about quiet traps as moloch in the usual sense, because moloch usually implies somebody (or something) is being greedy or power-hungry, whereas a quiet trap can be made entirely of people trying to be decent.

so my questions. is this a real distinction or just moloch with extra steps? has someone already said this under a better name (if so just tell me the name and i'll go read it myself, no bibliography)? and where does it break — give me the hardest counterexample you can: either something that looks quiet but my theory misclassifies, or a loud trap that is basically impossible to escape (which would kill my claim that quiet ones are harder). i need the three strongest objections and an honest verdict on whether any of them is fatal and not just uncomfortable. dont strawman me — steelman the distinction first so i know you actually got it. oh and please write in british english.
