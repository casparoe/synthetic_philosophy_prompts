I'm suppose to present our neighborhood's new housing plan to the Westfield Neighborhood Council next month, and I'm honestly more nervous about the Q&A than the talk itself. The plan would allow two-, three-, and four-unit buildings by right on any residential lot within half a mile of a light rail station, with a 15% inclusionary set-aside in buildings of three or more units, and a rule that demolishing an occupied rental unit triggers a right of first refusal for the existing tenants if the new building has rental units. My pitch is: we have a genuinely low vacancy rate regionally, missing-middle housing near transit is the cheapest way to add units without public subsidy, the set-aside and tenant protections should blunt the usual displacement story, and if this neighborhood refuses, the shortage just moves somewhere poorer and less equiped to handle it.

The room is what worries me. Most of the council are longtime homeowners who fought a 2016 six-story apartment building on the corner, and they'll talk about tree canopy, parking, shadows, and the character of the street. The chair of the infrastructure subcommittee is a retired civil engineer who will want to know about stormwater and sewer capacity and will catch me if I wave my hands. There are two renters from the citywide tenants' union who are generally pro-development but think market-rate units just accelerate gentrification, so they'll come at me from the left. And there's a county planner who actually likes the concept but will nitpick the by-right part and the design standards. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this exact room, not the ones from a generic online YIMBY-versus-NIMBY fight. Order them by how likely and how damaging they are, phrase each one the way that person would actually say it at a council meeting, and give me a short reply for each plus a note on where that reply is weakest. Tell me what to concede up front, what not to give away, the one reply that has to land for the plan to survive, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
