I have been puzzling over an asymmetry in the way people judge the creation of money, and I want to know whether it can be defended rather than just being a social convention.

Consider two acts. In the first, a private person runs a hidden press, produces paper notes that closely imitate official currency, and spends them. Almost everyone treats this as counterfeiting, fraud, and theft. In the second, a central bank creates new money on a large scale, for instance by buying government bonds and crediting banks with new reserves, and this new money eventually enters the wider economy. Most people treat that as normal monetary policy, perhaps unwise in some circumstances, but not as theft and not as a crime.

Yet in both cases the total amount of claims on real goods and services seems to increase, and the purchasing power of existing money can fall. So I am wondering: what exactly is supposed to justify the enormous difference in ordinary judgment and legal treatment between these two ways of expanding the money supply? Is there a principled distinction, or is the asymmetry mostly the result of state authority, habit, scale, or the fact that one is done openly by experts?

I am not asking for a policy recommendation about any particular central bank action. I want to know whether the ordinary asymmetry is intellectually defensible. Please answer as a structured JSON object with the following fields:

- asymmetry: a precise statement of the asymmetry in ordinary judgment and legal practice that I am describing.
- candidate_justifications: a list of the most serious principles or distinctions that might justify treating the central bank case differently from the counterfeiting case.
- tests: for each candidate justification, a nearby case that tests whether it really tracks the distinction people rely on, such as a local community creating its own currency, a central bank directly financing government spending, or a private bank creating deposits through lending.
- verdict: whether the asymmetry should be kept, reversed, or given up, and a brief explanation of why.
