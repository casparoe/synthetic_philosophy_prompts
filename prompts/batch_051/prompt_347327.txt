I'm giving a seminar next month to a religious studies dept—mostly historians of early Christianity and ethnographers of West African religion, with two philosophers of religion who are Hume-friendly but quiet. My pitch is a cognitive natural history of religion along Hume's lines. The opening move is a close reading of that passage in The Natural History of Religion where he says, roughly, that polytheism didn't start from someone contemplating the regular order of nature and inferring a designer; it started with the uneasy mind populating unknown causes with invisible agents. I'm using that to argue the recurring pattern across cultures—spirits for weather, illness, sudden death—is overdetermined by ordinary human cognition, without needing revelation or sui generis religious experience. I'm not saying theism is false, just that the basic forms of religion don't need any special story.

Prep me for the Q&A. What objections will this specific audience raise, in what order, phrased the way they'd actually put them? For each: is it fatal, what should I concede, what's my two-minute reply, and where is my reply softest? And what's the question I'm hoping nobody asks?
