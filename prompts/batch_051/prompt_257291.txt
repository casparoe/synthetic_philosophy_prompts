I'm a PhD student in philosophy, working on the epistemology of aesthetic judgment, and I keep returning to the aesthetic testimony debate without a sense that it has moved much at all. As I understand it, the pessimist holds that a knowledgeable critic's report that a work is graceful cannot by itself give the hearer knowledge of its gracefulness, because judgment of that kind requires first-hand acquaintance with the work; the optimist replies that testimony transmits knowledge here just as it does elsewhere, and that pessimism merely converts a sensible preference for seeing things for oneself into a spurious epistemic barrier. My working hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions under one heading: the optimist is defending the transmission of a true proposition, while the pessimist is defending something about the acquisition of a sensibility, or about the authority to judge for oneself. Is the impasse really this kind of conflation, or is there a substantive disagreement that survives once the questions are separated? Diagnose the disagreement from what each side actually says, and say what would count as progress given the diagnosis.
