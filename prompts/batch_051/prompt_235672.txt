I'm making a "which theory of intentionality do you actually hold?" sorting quiz for the rationalist-adjacent part of my blog, and I want it to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they already like. The angle I want: build the whole thing around Dennett's wandering two-bitser as the forcing case. For reference, the two-bitser is a soft-drink vending machine designed to detect US quarters; it ends up in Panama, where the local quarter-balboa is mechanically indistinguishable from a US quarter, so the machine happily accepts balboas as if they were quarters. Dennett uses it to argue that the original/derived intentionality distinction does no real work, and that the machine's "aboutness" is no more and no less derived than ours; Searle-ish people want to say the machine's intentionality is derived/as-if while people have original intentionality.

Build it as an 8-10 question quiz. Required cases (at least these should appear, wrote as self-contained vignettes with no jargon): the wandering two-bitser, the Chinese room, a thermostat or a similarly minimal gadget, a lightning-created molecular duplicate of a human with no history, and a nonconscious subpersonal brain state (say, edge detection or language parsing going on below awareness). Each question should have two or three answer options, and no option may say something like "I agree with Searle." The answer options should be the kind of thing a smart person could pick without knowing any of the literature.

The scoring key should map answer patterns to at least these positions, each defined by a claim about what (if anything) has original intentionality and where it comes from, not by a biography:
- Searle-style biological original intentionality: only brains, or things with the same causal powers, have it.
- Fodor-ish language-of-thought view: original intentionality lives in inner mental representations; words and machines get theirs secondhand.
- Millikan/Papineau-style teleosemantics: original intentionality is fixed by selection history, so a swamp duplicate lacks it, at least initially.
- Phenomenal intentionality: only conscious experience is originally intentional; nonconscious representation is derived.
- Dennett-style stance view: the original/derived distinction dissolves into more and less complex patterns, and the two-bitser is intentional in the same sense you are.
- Whatever "intentionality is flat-out unreal" error-theory view falls out of the scoring, even if you have to call it unnamed.

After the scoring key, include a short section on answer patterns that map to no position, and for each say whether it's incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any two positions the quiz ends up unable to separate. I can already see that Searle-style and Fodor-ish answers may come out nearly identical; figure out whether a silicon-vs-wetware question actually separates them, or whether you should admit the quiz can't.

Audience: people who read LessWrong/ACX-type stuff, so you can assume they know the Chinese room and the swampman but not "two-bitser" or "teleosemantics." Write the questions like that.

And please start with the quiz itself (title, instructions, first question), with no preface or commentary before it.
