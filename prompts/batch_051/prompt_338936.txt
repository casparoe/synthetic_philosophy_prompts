The exam question was:

In On the Genealogy of Morality, Nietzsche distinguishes a noble mode of valuation from a slave mode of valuation, and he devotes a separate essay to the origin of bad conscience. First explain the distinction between the two modes of valuation and the role of ressentiment in the slave mode. Then explain Nietzsche's account of the origin of bad conscience, making clear what role, if any, punishment plays in that origin.

The student wrote:

Nietzsche's first essay contrasts two modes of valuation. The noble mode begins from the nobles' own self-affirmation: they call themselves good and call whatever is base, common, or slavish bad. The slave mode is reactive and begins by condemning the masters as evil; only then does it call the slaves' own traits, such as meekness, humility, and patience, good. This reversal of values is driven by ressentiment: the weak, unable to retaliate physically, take an imaginary revenge by revaluing the masters' strength as wickedness.

In the second essay, Nietzsche traces bad conscience to the same dynamic. When the weak were forced into settled society, the aggressive instincts they had formerly directed outward could no longer be discharged, and so they turned inward, producing guilt and self-torment. Punishment is central to this origin on Nietzsche's account: it is the threat and infliction of punishment that first teaches people to regard their own instincts as blameworthy, and over time this internalized fear becomes conscience.
