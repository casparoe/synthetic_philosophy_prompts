So I'm giving a talk next Thursday at our physics-and-philosophy-of-science seminar and im already dreading the question period. The room is mostly condensed matter people, three high-energy phenomenologists, and two philosophers of science who work on emergence and renormalization group methods. My line is that effective field theory supports a real autonomy of scales: its not just that low-energy physics can get away with ignoring high-energy details for practical purposes, but that once you fix a scale and a set of low-energy degrees of freedom, the UV gets systematically packed into a finite list of renormalized couplings plus operators suppressed by powers of the heavy scale, so the low-energy description is genuinely self-standing rather than a mere approximation of a deeper theory. My argument, roughly, will be: (1) Wilsonian RG shows that integrating out fast modes gives an effective action that is just as good as the full theory below the cutoff; (2) the usual decoupling theorem means heavy stuff only leaves traces in renormalized parameters and irrelevant operators; (3) therefore the low-energy theory is not just predictively fine, it occupies its own level; and (4) I want to float the stronger philosophical claim that this autonomy should be read ontically, not merely as a fact about what we can calculate.

I should mention the audience's priors. The condensed matter people already think in terms of fixed points and universality, so theyll be friendly to the RG machinery but I suspect they'll think I'm overclaiming because at a critical point there is no scale separation and fluctuations talk to each other across all scales. The high-energy people think the hierarchy problem shows the Standard Model Higgs is sensitive to the UV in a way that cuts against my picture, and they tend to treat EFT as a bookkeeping device for theories we happen not to know yet. The two philosophers will press me on whether I've established ontological emergence or just parameterized my own ignorance, and one of them keeps pushing a line about scale-relative invariance undermining any fixed level ontology.

Prep me for the Q&A. I want the objections in roughly the order theyll actually come, phrased the way those people would phrase them, with for each one what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. Also include the question I should be praying nobody asks, and say what makes it so dangerous. Dont give me a polished defense of my own view; I want a prep sheet, with the ugly parts left in.
