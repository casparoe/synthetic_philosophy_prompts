I am treating the following proposal as a mechanism to analyze rather than a reform to endorse. A country proposes replacing its upper chamber with a Citizens' Repeal Jury: two hundred and fifty residents selected by lot for a single, non-renewable two-year term. The Jury may repeal, but neither amend nor initiate, any statute more than twenty years old, by a two-thirds vote. Each repeal is suspended for two years, during which the lower house may re-enact the statute verbatim by simple majority; if it does so, the statute is protected from the Jury for another twenty years from that re-enactment. Members are paid the median national wage, and no one may meet with a member to lobby a pending vote; instead, any organization may submit a written brief of at most one thousand words on any eligible statute, and every brief is distributed to all members with its source kept unidentified until after the vote. Walk through the incentives this creates for lower-house legislators, for interest groups, and for the citizens selected; say where the institution would be gamed first; trace its second-order effects on the age and drafting of legislation; and assess whether its likely failure modes can be fixed without emptying the scheme of its point.
