Two colleagues in my department got into an argument about doxastic self-deference after one of them endorsed van Fraassen's Reflection principle. I remember the exchange as follows.

(1) She stated the principle roughly: your current degree of belief in a proposition should equal the degree of belief you expect to have in it at any designated later time. When pressed, she argued that violations are diachronically incoherent. Suppose you now assign credence 0.5 to a claim but expect that in an hour you will assign it 0.8. Then a bookie could buy a bet on the claim from you now at your low price and sell it back to you in an hour at your high price, thereby guaranteeing a loss for you whether the claim is true or false.

(2) He replied that this cannot hold in full generality. A pill could be administered in an hour that will make you certain of a proposition you now regard as obviously false. If you know the pill's effect, Reflection requires you to be certain of that proposition now, which is plainly irrational, and no bookkeeping argument can make it rational.

(3) She answered that the Dutch book argument never assumed the later self is reliable. It shows only that present and future credences must cohere, whatever causes the later ones. If you know a pill will make you certain of a falsehood, the right response is not to revise now but to refuse the bets now, thereby blocking the bookie; the defect the bookie exploits remains.

(4) He said refusing the bets is ad hoc. Anyone can dodge any Dutch book by refusing to bet, so that reply would make diachronic coherence vacuous. The deeper point, he said, is that a later credence matters only as evidence about the proposition, and a credence you know will be produced by a pill has no evidential value. Reflection holds at most toward later selves you have reason to regard as at least as reasonable as you are now.

(5) She replied that this restriction gives up what made Reflection useful. We defer to our later selves in ordinary cases because we expect them to have completed a line of reasoning, not because we first certified them as reliable beyond question. If Reflection had to wait on that certification, it could never guide someone who is uncertain of their own future competence.

They asked me to referee, and I could not. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the five moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and then say what, if anything, both parties are missing about the proper form and limits of the principle. Keep your response under 800 words.
