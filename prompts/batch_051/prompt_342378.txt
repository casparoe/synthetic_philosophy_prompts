I keep going back and forth on a parallel I noticed. There's a live-ish view in value theory, the Temkin-style spectrum arguments, that 'better than' can be nontransitive: compare lives along two dimensions like longevity and quality, and a chain of small tradeoffs can leave you with A better than B, B better than C, but C better than A. Structurally that looks just like nontransitive dice, where each die beats the next pairwise but the whole set cycles with no best die. Both seem to say that pairwise comparisons can be perfectly fine and still refuse to add up to a global ranking. Is this a deep point about local pariwise comparison across rival dimensions, or is it just the word "intransitive" doing the work? What would have to be true of the value case for the dice analogy to really transfer, and what does the analogy predict about value that turns out false?
