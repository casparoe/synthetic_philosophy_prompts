I am a control engineer who thinks natively in state spaces, transition maps, trajectories, and reachable sets; I have no formal philosophical training beyond the usual sketch of the free-will problem. Restate the debate between compatibilism and incompatibilism about free will and moral responsibility in that vocabulary, as systematically as you can.

Cover at least these five load-bearing notions, giving each a formal counterpart or explaining why none exists: determinism; an agent's being able to act otherwise; an action's being up to the agent; moral responsibility and desert; and the incompatibilist charge that under determinism an agent is merely a component executing a pre-fixed trajectory. Use whatever control-theoretic machinery you need — states, inputs, disturbances, transition functions, initial-condition counterfactuals, controlled and uncontrolled degrees of freedom, controllability, invariant sets, assignment of credit or blame over a run. Where a philosophical notion is ambiguous, say which engineering disambiguation you chose and why.

Then provide the residue report. For each notion, say whether it translated cleanly, translated only under distortion, and where the distortion entered, or had no counterpart at all in your formalism. Identify what in the philosophical debate your vocabulary cannot express, and give your best reconstruction of the residual disagreement in engineering terms — for example, whether it concerns which counterfactual runs are relevant, or whether a property belongs to the trajectory itself rather than to the way a controller selects among trajectories.

Finally, tell me: if a compatibilist and an incompatibilist were each implemented as a scoring function over one and the same simulated agent trace, on which properties of the trace would their verdicts actually diverge, and what does that divergence suggest about whether the original debate is a factual disagreement about the world or a practical disagreement about how to describe and respond to agents? Keep the translation honest: do not round off the incompatibilist position to make it fit the formalism, and do not let the engineering picture quietly settle the question.
