Suppose a novel respiratory virus appears and within three weeks a government has closed borders, shut schools, and told people to stay home. It issued the orders under a dusty 1940s public health act that, everyone agrees, was never written to cover anything like this. Two months later parliament votes to ratify the measures, and the courts then say the measures are lawful from the date of ratification but remain pointedly silent about the pre-ratification period. Here's the proposition I want your credence on: the lockdown and border closure were legitimate during those pre-ratification weeks, in the sense that the government was entitled to impose them even though they weren't yet legal. I know the bare number will partly track which theory of legitimacy you reach for—that's exactly what I want unpacked. Give me a number and then decompose it. What are the main sub-questions your credence rests on? At least these seem relevant: (i) whether legitimacy requires lawful authorization or can arise directly from the obligation to protect people from serious harm; (ii) whether the measures being widely accepted and probably saving lives matters to legitimacy on its own; (iii) whether later ratification can make an earlier unauthorized act legitimate, or only excuse it after the fact; (iv) whether "legitimate" even has a determinate answer here, or whether applying it is partly a decision about what we want the concept to do. Give a rough probability for each branch, say how they combine into the final number, and tell me which sub-question would shift your credence the most if you got a definitive answer.
