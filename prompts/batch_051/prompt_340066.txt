I've been mulling over a notion I keep calling 'axiological arbitrage'—an economist friend says 'moral gains from trade' is the honest name for it. The thought is this. When two people, or two communities, or two political factions disagree about fundamental values, we tend to assume a compromise has to find shared ground or require someone to capitulate. But I suspect there's usually a third option: each side can trade away something that, by its own lights, it rates as comparatively minor, in exchange for something it rates as comparatively major, while the other side makes the mirror trade. The result is that both can end up judging the package better than the no-deal default by their own standards—even though they share no common yardstick for the goods in question. It's a kind of Pareto improvement across incommensurable value systems: compromise as arbitrage rather than consensus. Neither party has to revise its first principles or even respect the other's; they just need asymmetries in how strongly they weight different things, plus a way of bundling the trades so neither can cherry-pick. My go-to example is two factions, one that cares mostly about keeping a local school religious in character, the other mostly about expanding free childcare: each can accept the other's priority as the cost of securing its own, because each cares about winning its own priority much more than it cares about blocking the other's. I know this sounds a bit amoral. Tell me straight: is the notion coherant, does it already exist under another name (surely welfare economics has something like it), what does it get right, and where exactly does it break—especially the cases it wrongly counts as good compromises. Give me the three strongest objections and say whether any is fatal rather than just uncomfortable. And don't soften it; if it's just bargaining dressed up in moral language, say so. Please write in British English, and end with a single last line beginning 'Summary:' that says in one sentence what the notion, charitably reconstructed, really amounts to and whether it survives.
