Right, so a mate and I went round in circles about this most of yesterday evening and I still can't decide which of us was being dense. He'd been reading the Fearon-style rationalist stuff about war as a bargaining failure, and he built an argument out of it that I couldn't find a hole in at the time, but the conclusion is so strong it has to be wrong somewhere. He was convinced its airtight and that I was smuggling in irrationality. The argument went roughly like this.

(1) Fighting is costly and risky, so if two actors end up fighting there must have been some bargain available — a deal about who gets what, what each side is allowed to build, where the red lines are — that both would prefer to the fight.
(2) Rational actors take the option they prefer, so two rational actors should always find that bargain and avoid fighting.
(3) An AI race is just a contest over a prize: leadership in advanced AI, or at least not ending up defenceless against the other side's systems.
(4) So by the same logic, two rational actors in an AI race should be able to find a bargain — some mix of compute caps, testing moratoria, no-deployment-past-a-certain-capability, whatever — and stick to it.
(5) Therefore serious AI-driven conflict, or even a ruinous race to the bottom, shouldn't happen between rational players. At worst you get a normal standoff with bluster and spying, not actual war.

But that's exactly what it can't establish, because the race clearly isn't stalling: both sides are hoarding compute, hiding what their models can actually do, and treating pre-emption by the other side as a live risk. So either everyone involved is irrational, or the argument is broken somewhere. Which step or move goes wrong, if any? Is it the same move that goes wrong in the original bargaining model of war, or something specific to AI? The bit I'd most like to keep is the thought that some kind of deal is genuinely available — I don't want to be left with the conclusion that the race is unstoppable and attempts at agreement are pointless. What do I have to give up, and what does it cost?
