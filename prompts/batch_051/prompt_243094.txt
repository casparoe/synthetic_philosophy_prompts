I've been turning over an idea I call moral arbitrage. The thought is that two people with very different value systems can each end up acting more in line with their own values by trading concessions on things they care about unequally. Take an animal advocate and a hunter. She cares far more about cutting long-run deer suffering than about opposing this one cull; he cares far more about keeping the cull than about a wildlife corridor he'd otherwise vote down. They agree: he backs the corridor, she stops fighting the cull, and both judge the package better than the status quo by their own lights. My stronger claim is that this isnt just compromise or logrolling, because neither side gives up anything it treats as a core principle—theyre trading on different marginal intensities, not meeting in the middle. Is this a real category, does it already exist under a name I'm missing, and where does it break? I want the strongest objection, not reassurance.
