Compare two ways of assigning probability in the many-worlds interpretation: branch-counting, which assigns equal probability to each future copy, and amplitude-weighting, which lets credence follow the squared magnitude of each branch's amplitude. Work through this version: an ideal agent knows that a measurement will split her into two future copies whose squared amplitudes are in a 9 to 1 ratio. Explain why branch-counting cannot recover the Born rule here, and state what exactly must be added for the agent to assign 0.9 credence to the larger-amplitude branch before the split. Then take a definite position on whether that added ingredient is best understood as a new physical law, a constraint of rationality for agents facing branching, or a convention, and defend it against the other two readings.
