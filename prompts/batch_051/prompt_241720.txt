A welfare-aggregating polity proposes the following safeguard against utility monsters. Each citizen may report utility in arbitrary magnitudes, but when the state computes total welfare it counts any individual's contribution only up to the median citizen's full-year utility; all reported utility above that cap is discarded, and the cap is recomputed from the distribution every five years. Analyze how this mechanism would actually behave. What does it incentivize in what people report, in what goods and policies get supplied, and in how the state treats cases of genuinely extreme suffering or bliss? Where does it fail—through proxy reporting, threshold gaming just below the cap, or definitional moves about whose utility is being capped—and can repairs such as emergency appeals, need-scaled caps, or auditing claims rather than truncating them fix the failures without reopening the vulnerability the cap was designed to close?
