Draft the joint statement that a committed compatibilist about determinism and moral responsibility and a committed incompatibilist about determinism and moral responsibility could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should concern whether determinism is compatible with the kind of control or freedom required for moral responsibility; set aside the separate questions of whether determinism is actually true and whether anyone is in fact morally responsible.

Format the document in two parts. Part 1 should be a numbered list of agreed claims. These should be substantive rather than merely diplomatic: include the formulation of determinism both accept; the shared logical structure of the consequence argument and the precise point at which the two sides read its central premise differently; the shared criteria for an adequate analysis of the phrase "could have done otherwise," or a shared explanation of why no such analysis is expected; and any facts about manipulation cases, causation, and the phenomenology of choice that both sides accept as data to be explained.

Part 2 should be a disagreements table. Each row should state a crux: a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side. For each row, provide (a) the compatibilist's position, in wording the compatibilist would accept; (b) the incompatibilist's position, in wording the incompatibilist would accept; (c) what each side would count as evidence or argument against their own view on that crux; and (d) the case, distinction, or argument both sides agree would bear on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

The table should include, but need not be limited to, cruxes about the following issues:

1. Whether the ability to do otherwise is best understood as a conditional ability—for example, the agent would have done otherwise if they had chosen or wanted otherwise—or as a categorical ability whose possession is ruled out if the past and the laws entail the actual choice.

2. Whether manipulation arguments—in which an agent satisfies every condition the compatibilist offers while the agent's preferences and deliberations are in a specified sense engineered by an outside manipulator—show that the compatibilist's conditions are insufficient, or merely show that manipulation is a different kind of case to which those conditions were never meant to apply.

3. Whether moral responsibility requires the agent to be the ultimate source of the action, and whether a deterministic history can supply the kind of sourcehood that is missing in a manipulated agent.

Before the table, identify any portion of the dispute that both sides conclude is merely verbal—for example, a disagreement that disappears once the two sides fix a single semantics for "ability" or "free will"—and say precisely which wording each side would replace with what.

Write the statement in American English.
