I have spent the past few weeks on a curriculum committee deciding whether to keep a certain poet on a first-year syllabus after we learned of something serious he did in private life. I will not rehearse the details; what matters is that the wrongdoing does not appear in the poems themselves, either in their subject matter or in their making, so far as any of us can tell. Half the committee says that, once we know, we cannot read the poems as we did before; the other half says the poems are exactly as good as they always were and only the practical question of whether to teach them should change.

The argument keeps reminding me of a contrast. In mathematics and the sciences there is no real analogue of our impasse: people routinely use statistical procedures, proofs, and experimental techniques developed by figures now known to have held abhorrent views or to have done terrible things, and almost no one argues that the validity, the importance, or even the intellectual beauty of those results is diminished by their origins. At most there is a dispute about how to memorialize the person, not about whether to trust the product. So the parallel I cannot decide about is this: both aesthetics and mathematics confront the question of whether a product inherits the moral character of its producer, yet aesthetics treats this as a live and unresolved problem about the value of the work while mathematics mostly treats origin as evaluatively inert.

My inclination is that the asymmetry is principled, because an artwork is an expressive act of a particular person in a way that a theorem or a statistical method is not; the artist can enter into the work's content, point of view, and material history, while a proof's content does not depend on who first moved through it. But I can also see the asymmetry as a mere double standard: perhaps origin always shapes how we properly value the things people make, and mathematicians have simply not had to confront it. I want you to tell me whether this parallel is deep or superficial, exactly where the two cases match and where they come apart, what would have to be true of both sides for a conclusion from one field to transfer to the other, and at least one thing the parallel seems to predict that turns out false.
