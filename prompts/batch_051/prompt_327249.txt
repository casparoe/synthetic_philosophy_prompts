I'm on the prioritisation committee of a regional health authority. We've got about £8m to allocate next round, and the two shortlisted bids are: a screening programme that the modellers estimate would save around 40 statistical lives a year, mostly among older people, at a very good cost per QALY; and an expansion of a small specialist unit for a rare and very nasty paediatric condition, where the same money would keep perhaps three named children alive over the next year but scores terribly on cost-per-QALY and value-of-a-statistical-life grounds. The clinicians keep saying a child's life can't be reduced to a number and that statistical lives are a spreadsheet fiction, and several of us on the committee find that harder to dismiss than we'd like, even though we know it isn't a policy argument. Our framework commits us to rank by cost per QALY, but it also has an 'equity' override that nobody has ever defined. Draft the short memo I'll circulate before next Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision, give the strongest version of the argument for the option you don't pick rather than a straw man, and say what the equity override should actually mean in cases like this and whether it changes the ranking. Write it in British English, please.
