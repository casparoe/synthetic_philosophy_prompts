Could you write something on this? Thucydides has Cleon open the Mytilenean debate (History of the Peloponnesian War 3.37, Crawley translation) with: "I have often before now been convinced that a democracy is incapable of empire, and never more so than by your present change of mind in the matter of Mitylene." The background: the assembly had voted the day before to kill all adult male Mytileneans and enslave the women and children, then repented and reopened the question. I want an essay on whether that about-face is evidence for Cleon's claim that democracy is too inconstant for long-run policy, or whether its better read as the institution working—correcting a rash decision rather than flailing. Weigh both readings but reach a real verdict (qualified verdicts are fine, like Cleon's right about one thing but not another). Dont just recount the episode; engage the underlying disagreement about what a collective change of mind means.
