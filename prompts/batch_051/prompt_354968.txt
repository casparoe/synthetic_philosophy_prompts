On the other hand he couldn't stand the zodiac signs, the houses, and most of the predictive judicial astrology of his day. In Tertius Interveniens he says something like: astrology is a foolish daughter, but her wise mother astronomy would starve without her, because no one will pay for pure astronomy. I'm not asking for a survey of his views. I want you to help me prioritize the open interpretive questions about what Kepler actually thought astrology could and couldn't do, for someone planning about three years of PhD work. Give me two or three questions, ordered, and defend the ordering: which questions are upstream of which, which are actually answerable with the available sources, and which ones I should avoid because they're mostly about late-Renaissance theology or they don't change much downstream. I'm especially interested in the tension between his physical realism about aspects and his dismissal of signs and houses—what's the question there that would do the most work if I could settle it? Also say what kind of evidence would move each question up or down.
