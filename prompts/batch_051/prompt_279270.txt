I spent most of yesterday evening arguing with a friend about whether psychiatric classification is a discovery or a form of intervention. My friend kept insisting on something like Ian Hacking's idea of the looping effect: naming a category of person — dissociative identity disorder, say, or social anxiety disorder, or the current broadened construal of autism — does not simply record a pre-existing condition but helps to constitute it, because people take up the label, reinterpret their past and their present in its terms, describe their suffering differently, and present to clinicians accordingly; those altered presentations then feed back into the manuals, which revise the category, which changes people again, and so on. I pushed back that this is an unfalsifiable story dressed up as social science, and that every historical example can be explained more cheaply by greater awareness, shifting diagnostic thresholds, and the incentives of clinics and pharmaceutical markets. We went round in circles because neither of us could say what evidence would actually tell the two stories apart.

Suppose one wanted to test the distinctive claim — that a psychiatric classification can causally reshape the people classified, and that this reshaping loops back into the classification itself — as rigorously as the claim permits. Design the strongest feasible empirical strategy you can think of: an observational, comparative, quasi-experimental, or longitudinal design, or some combination, since one cannot randomise an entire classification system. Specify exactly what would be measured, what pattern of results would favour the looping account over the mere-discovery-plus-increased-detection account, and what pattern would count against it. Then say precisely what a positive result would and would not establish: would it show that the category is an interactive kind in Hacking's sense, or only that labels have causal effects on individual people? More generally, where does the operationalisation run out?

I am not after a catalogue of existing studies; I want a proposal one could run in principle and a clear-eyed account of its limits. In particular, tell me which component of the looping claim is the narrowest testable core, and which part, if any, simply is not the sort of thing empirical evidence can settle.
