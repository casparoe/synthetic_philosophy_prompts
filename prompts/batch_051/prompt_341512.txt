I keep coming back to the evolutionary debunking debate in metaethics, and I'm starting to suspect the reason it hasn't moved in nearly twenty years is that the two sides aren't actually having one disagreement. I'm a PhD student in moral psychology, so I read this stuff mostly for the empirical load-bearing parts, and its striking how little the empirical side ends up doing in the argument. Everyone agrees evolution shaped our moral dispositions; everyone agrees those dispositions were selected for their fitness effects, not for their tendency to latch onto the moral facts. Then everything turns on how to describe that fact. The debunker says: if realism is true, the match between your moral judgments and the moral facts would be an enormous coincidence. The realist says: not if there's a third-factor story—the very facts that made, say, parental care good also made it useful for us to judge it good, so the correlation isn't a coincidence. The debunker says those third-factor stories get reverse-engineered ad hoc. The realist says the debunker's standard would debunk every evolved belief, including belief in natural selection itself. And round it goes. People have been stuck here since Street first put the Darwinian dilemma on the table, and I honestly can't tell whether there's a real epistemic question underneath or just different uses of "undermining," "tracking," "safety," and "defeat."

What I want is a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, not a survey of positions. Is it substantive—and if so, about what exactly: which counterfactuals hold, the correct theory of epistemic defeat, how much coincidence is too much, or what realists are allowed to assume at the outset? Is it two different questions (one about reliability, one about semantics) under one heading? Is it maybe a verbal dispute about what "coincidence" demands? And given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result about sensitivity or safety, a distinction, a psychological experiment, or a reason to think none should be expected? Also say what would change your mind about the diagnosis—which consideration would push you to a different read of the debate, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
