I am applying for a one-semester research fellowship and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea concerns the received way of framing the dispute between Peirce and James over truth. The dispute is usually presented as a head-on metaphysical disagreement: Peirce held that truth is the opinion on which inquiry would settle in the long run, while James treated truth as one kind of good, marked by the way a belief guides action and fits our wider commitments. I suspect this framing flattens the disagreement. My hunch is that the two are frequently answering different questions. Peirce is giving an account of the property of truth: what it is for a proposition to be true, stated in terms of what indefinitely continued inquiry would not overturn. James is giving an account of the epistemology of belief under uncertainty: what entitles a specific, finite agent, facing a forced and momentous decision, to take a proposition as true now and act on it. If that is right, much of the supposed clash dissolves into a difference in project. But I do not think the whole of it dissolves. There may remain a substantive disagreement about whether the practical good of believing is internal to truth itself or only to our warrant for taking something as true; and this residual disagreement, if it exists, would need to be stated in a way that does not collapse back into the original caricature. Please turn this into a short proposal. I want it to include: a precise statement of the question; why the standard readings, both those that defend one side against the other and those that dismiss the dispute as merely terminological, leave it open; the method I would actually use, roughly reconstructing each philosopher's originating problem before comparing their answers rather than matching slogans, and why that method rather than a straight compare-and-contrast of definitions; what the possible conclusions are and what considerations would decide among them; what would count as the project failing; and a realistic scope for one semester. I will add citations myself, so please do not include a bibliography or ask me to locate sources.
