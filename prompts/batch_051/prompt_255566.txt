In my philosophy of physics class we've been doing special relativity and last week the professor sketched the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. Me and my friend tried to write it out with actual numbered steps, because the usual presentation felt kind of hand-wavey. Here's what we ended up with:

(1) In special relativity, simultaneity is frame-relative: for any two spacelike separated events, there's an inertial frame where they're simultaneous, and other frames where either one comes first.
(2) An event is happening "now" for me just in case it's simultaneous with my here-now in my own rest frame.
(3) But no inertial frame is physically privileged. So if two events are simultaneous in some frame, and one of them is real now, then the other one is real now too.
(4) Now take any event E in my future, even one inside my future light cone. Pick an event D far enough away that D is simultaneous with my here-now in my rest frame, and D and E are spacelike separated. Then there's some other frame where D and E are simultaneous.
(5) By (3), D is real now. Then, applying (3) again to the pair D and E, E is real now.
(6) Since E was arbitrary, every event in my future is already real now. So the future is fixed, presentism is false, and we're stuck with the block universe.

My friend thinks this is basically airtight and that the only escape is to deny special relativity. I keep feeling like the phrase "real now" is doing something different in (3) than it was in (2), like we've stopped relativizing it to a frame but are still using frame-relative simultaneity to spread it around. But when I try to say that, she says I'm just insisting on a privileged "now" that relativity already ruled out. Is the argument valid as written? If not, which step fails, and what exactly is the reading of that step that makes it fail? And if the right move is to reject (3), what does that commit me to about what special relativity does and doesn't show?
