"The absurdity doctrine is an exception to the plain meaning rule. Normally judges must apply a statute according to what its words plainly say, but when the plain meaning leads to an absurd result the court can set the text aside and read in an exception, because the legislature could not have intended an absurd result. Absurd here means a result that is unreasonable or causes serious injustice; the court does not need to find that the legislature considered the exact case, only that the outcome is bad enough on balance. In the ambulance case, the plain meaning of 'vehicles' covers ambulances, so the prosecution is textually correct. The court should therefore find an implied exception for emergency vehicles. Because the absurdity doctrine is grounded in legislative intent, a judge may depart whenever the text defeats the statute's obvious purpose.
