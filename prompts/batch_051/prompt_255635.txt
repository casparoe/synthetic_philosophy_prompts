I run a philosophy of physics discussion group, and for the first meeting on Bell's theorem I want to make a "which position on Bell's theorem do you actually hold?" decision tree. The group is mixed—some physicists, some philosophers, some neither—so the questions cant rely on knowing the labels or the formalism. The point is to sort people by what they say about a concrete case, not by which interpretation they like the sound of.

Use one running Bell scenario: Alice and Bob share a singlet pair, each picks a detector setting at the last moment (say from a distant quasar to close the "they coordinated ahead of time" loophole), and they get correlations that violate the CHSH inequality. The tree should branch on yes/no commitments about that scenario. The questions should seperate people on things like: are the outcomes definite before measurement, or does measurement produce them; does Alice's setting choice affect Bob's outcome, and if so through what channel; is the quantum state the whole story or are there hidden variables; does measurement have a special role that other interactions lack; and could the hidden variables be correlated with the settings from way back.

The leaves have to include at least: Bohmian mechanics/pilot-wave, spontaneous collapse (GRW), many-worlds/Everettian, QBism, and superdeterminism. Add retrocausal views only if they fall out of the questions naturally.

Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, and only then place the named positions in it. If some combination of answers has no known defender, say so and say whether it looks incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag positions your tree cant tell apart rather than pretending the questions separate them. I suspect many-worlds and Bohm will both say "deterministic, no collapse," so you'll need a later question to split them, like whether the wavefunction is all there is.

If the whole fixed-tree format is the wrong instrument here—maybe these positions differ along dimensions that dont cut cleanly into yes/no questions—tell me that plainly and give me the closest thing that would actually sort people, instead of forcing it. Keep the whole response under 800 words, tree and all.
