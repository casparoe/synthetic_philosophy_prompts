Before I read any more proposals for revising concepts—redefining "marriage" or "fish," giving a social-kind analysis of "race" or "gender," replacing "addiction" or "mental disorder" with something normatively better—I want the specification of what an adequate account of concept revision must do. The work I have seen either takes the target extension for granted and argues only about the best definition, or assumes that a normatively attractive revision is automatically legitimate. Those two moves are exactly what I want the desiderata themselves to rule in or out.

Write the requirements list for an adequate theory of conceptual engineering (ameliorative analysis, concept revision, concept replacement—use whichever label you prefer). For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. At minimum, address:

- the distinction between discovering what a concept is and deciding what it should be, and whether a theory that collapses the two can still count as adequate;
- the distinction between revising a concept, replacing it, and eliminating it, and what would count as an unacceptable change of subject;
- continuity of subject matter: the revision must still be about what the original debate was about, even though the old analysis is the very thing being discarded—so say what could secure that continuity;
- expressibility of the target: the defect in the old concept must be statable without already possessing the new one, since otherwise the proposed revision cannot even be specified from where we now stand;
- uptake without tyranny: a revision no community can actually adopt and transmit is wishful thinking, but it is not obvious why facts about what a term means should bow to facts about what will catch on;
- epistemic adequacy: the revised concept must do real work in explanation and inference, not only in politics or policy.

Then flag any pairs or subsets of these requirements that you doubt can be jointly satisfied—I expect continuity and normative improvement to pull apart, and uptake to be in tension with at least one of them—and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude popular proposals. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of existing views.
