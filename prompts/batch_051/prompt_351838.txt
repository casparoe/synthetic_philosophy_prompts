Okay so I'm in a philosophy of language class and we've been doing Putnam's Twin Earth thing and Burge's arthritis case, and I keep coming back to a timing problem I don't think either of them actually settles. Here's the homemade theory I've been kicking around: I call it semantic settling. The idea is that what a word means for a speaker isn't fixed at the moment they say it; it settles over time. Like, take an Earthling who gets teleported to Twin Earth and, before anyone tells her the stuff in the lake is XYZ, points at it and says 'water.' On my view her word still means H2O at that moment, because her causal history is still almost entirely earthing. But after weeks of showering in XYZ, drinking it, and using 'water' to buy it and talk about it, her word gradually comes to mean XYZ. And in the middle stretch I want to say the extension of her 'water' is genuinely indeterminate — not just that she doesn't know which one she means, but that there isn't yet a fact about which one she means until enough new history accumulates to outweigh the old. So externalism is true but slow: meaning is a function of causal history, and causal histories have something like a half-life. I think this explains why someone who moves countries doesn't instantly switch concepts, while keeping Putnam's point that the stuff in the environment does the anchoring once the settling is done.

I know Burge has that bit about slow switching where a person can be switched without being in a position to know it, but I dont think he ever bites the bullet on a genuinely indeterminate middle. So two honest questions: is this already a position with a name I've just missed, and does the middle-stretch indeterminacy actually follow, or am I just confusing how fast someone finds out what they meant with what they meant all along? Give me the strongest version of that objection and tell me whether I can wriggle out or whether the whole thing collapses into plain old Putnam.
