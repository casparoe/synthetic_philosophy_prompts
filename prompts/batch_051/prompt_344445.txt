I’m a science writer working on a piece about whether the vocabulary of subjective experience can survive contact with cognitive science, and I keep circling back to a thought experiment from Daniel Dennett about two coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn. Both end up reporting that the same house blend no longer tastes good to them, but they explain the change differently. One says the experienced taste itself is unchanged and he has simply come to dislike that very taste; the other says his liking and standards are unchanged and the experienced taste itself has altered. Dennett’s suggestion is that no amount of third-person investigation—even ideal neural measurement—could settle who is right, and that this reflects badly on the idea that “qualia” pick out determinate, private, intrinsic qualitative properties of conscious experience.

I want a number rather than a survey of positions. What is your actual credence that qualia, as standardly glossed—private, intrinsic, directly apprehended qualitative features of experience—are an incoherent posit, in the sense that the notion cannot be made to do stable explanatory or descriptive work once cases like Chase and Sanborn are taken seriously? I’d like the reasoning decomposed into the sub-questions your credence depends on, with a rough probability for each and a note on how they combine. Also tell me which single sub-question would move your final number most, and what new empirical result or conceptual argument would move it. Please structure the response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
