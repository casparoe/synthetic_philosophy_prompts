Here's a debate I keep seeing and I want you to write the document that would force both sides to be precise. One side (call them the legibility-first people) says we should train models so their reasoning is itself understandable: for example, have the model produce a deliberation trace a human can actually audit, and accept some loss of accuracy or competence to get that. The other side (call them the post-hoc people) says legibility is not a property of the surface outputs; it's something we achieve by studying the trained model from the outside — probes, mechanistic interpretability, attribution — and training a model to "think legibly" just makes it worse and makes us overconfident because it's now telling us a story we like. Draft the adversarial-collaboration joint statement these two camps could both sign. In it: (1) a numbered list of claims they both actually agree on, phrased so neither side winces; (2) a disagreements table where each row gives the residual dispute, with each side's view worded the way that side would word it themselves; (3) the cruxes — the subquestions such that resolving them would move at least one side; and (4) for each crux, a test or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Finish by saying what would actually change each side's mind and where the statement is most likely to be papering over a real difference.
