I am a PhD student in statistics working on causal inference, and I have been asked to run a 90-minute guest session for a graduate seminar on Pearl's causal hierarchy and the do-calculus. The group will be about fourteen students from statistics, computer science, and philosophy of science; they can read a DAG and know basic probability, but most have not worked with structural causal models in any detail. I would like the session to be a playable classroom game, not a lecture, and I would like the students to feel the force of two ideas before either is named: first, that unlimited passive observation does not determine what happens under an intervention; and second, that the do-calculus works only because structural causal models assume interventions are surgical, setting a variable's value while leaving every other mechanism intact, and that this is a substantive assumption that can fail in recognizable ways. The game should be self-contained: cards, dice, and printed score sheets only, with no code, no projector, and no reliance on pre-assigned readings.

Please design the full activity for me: the materials and setup; the roles and their rules; a timed sequence of phases that fits within 90 minutes; and a payoff structure arranged so that participants who act on observational regularities are reliably punished in at least one condition involving an unmeasured confounder and at least one condition involving a fat-handed intervention that changes more than its target equation. The technical vocabulary, including the do-operator, modularity, causal sufficiency, identifiability, and the observational-interventional gap, should appear only in the debrief, after the failures have happened. The debrief should map specific moments in the game onto those parts of Pearl's framework. Then tell me what will predictably go wrong when the game is run, for example participants treating an intervention as a conditional probability or the confounding being too easy to spot, and how a facilitator should handle each failure. Finally, be candid about which feature of the framework this game cannot reproduce and which I will have to supply afterward myself. Please write the activity and the debrief in British English.
