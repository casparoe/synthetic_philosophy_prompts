In a seminar on cultural tightness and looseness, we have been arguing about how much conformity to unwritten social rules a community may legitimately demand of the people who live in it. One side says a "tight" culture can demand a great deal—that it can expect people to follow local customs about dress, deference, eating, even mourning, without first giving them a reason, precisely because the customs are local. The other side says this is just etiquette dressed up as authority. A classmate has begun treating the proverb "When in Rome, do as the Romans do" as if it settles the matter: if you live inside such a culture, you follow its rules, full stop.

I am sure that move is too quick, but I cannot pin down why, because the proverb seems to slide between several different ideas.
