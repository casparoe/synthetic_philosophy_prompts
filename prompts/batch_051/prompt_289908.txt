I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been trying to think through Thomson’s lamp on my own, and one passage from James F. Thomson, “Tasks and Super-Tasks,” Analysis 15 (1954), p. 6, keeps blocking my attempts to sort out the positions:

Then the question whether the lamp is on or off… is the question: What is the sum of the infinite divergent sequence  
+1, −1, +1, ...?  
Now mathematicians do say that this sequence has a sum; they say that its sum is 1⁄2. And this answer does not help us, since we attach no sense here to saying that the lamp is half-on. I take this to mean that there is no established method for deciding what is done when a super-task is done. … We cannot be expected to pick up this idea, just because we have the idea of a task or tasks having been performed and because we are acquainted with transfinite numbers.

I want to map the space of live answers to the question “what is the lamp’s state at two minutes?” without assuming from the start that Thomson is right. Please write a decision tree that sorts people by what they believe about the setup, not by which label they prefer. Build it from the logical space first: at each branch, put a concrete diagnostic question about the lamp or a slight variation on it, something someone can answer without already knowing the names of positions. Use at least these distinctions: whether the associated alternating series is relevant; whether a completed supertask must settle the lamp’s state; whether “on” and “off” are exhaustive for an underspecified or limit-state lamp; and whether changing the switch’s path through intermediate positions changes the answer. The leaves should include at least Thomson’s contradiction/impossibility view, the underdetermination view that says the description fails to fix an answer, and the ill-posed view that says the lamp has no state at the limit. Mark leaves that are logically possible but undefended, and say whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where two positions cannot be separated by the tree, flag them rather than papering over. End with a compact scoring key or path summary so the tree can be used as a self-quiz. Keep it to at most seven branch nodes and at most nine leaves.
