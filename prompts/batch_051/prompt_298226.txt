I keep coming back to the old story about the digger wasp (Sphex, I think): it drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to check, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, it comes out, repositions it, and goes back in to check the burrow all over again. It never "gets" that the burrow was already fine. I taught animal behavior for years, and this was my stock warning against reading cleverness into complicated-looking routines.

Lately I've been trying to turn that story into an argument about free will, and I can't find the fault, although the conclusion feels off. Here's the argument.

(1) The wasp's rigidity is a specific kind of thing: its next move is set by a local trigger, and it can't use a higher-level fact (the burrow was just checked) to skip a step.
(2) The only evidence we can ever have that a creature could have done otherwise is that, across some range of counterfactual circumstances, it would have done otherwise.
(3) A deterministic control system could be built to pass exactly that test: its next state is a function of its current state plus input, and across a very wide range of counterfactual inputs it would produce different outputs.
(4) So counterfactual flexibility can't be the mark that separates the wasp from us; at best, we are higher-dimensional versions of the same trigger-and-response scheme.
(5) If free will just is counterfactual flexibility, then a sufficiently complex deterministic machine has free will.
(6) But a deterministic machine does not have free will in the sense worth wanting.
(7) So free will can't be counterfactual flexibility, and our difference from the wasp is only one of degree, not a difference in kind that could ground free will.

Where does this go right and where does it go wrong? I half-suspect the weak spot is step 2, but compatibilists might say the weak spot is step 6. Or maybe the whole thing is valid and I should stop fighting the conclusion. If it's invalid, I want the exact step, and what the equivocation or suppressed premise is.
