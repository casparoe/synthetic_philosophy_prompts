Philosophical accounts of universalizability differ about what the ordinary challenge "what if everyone did that?" is really doing, and how much of its force the arguer is entitled to keep. Three canonical answers stake out the main positions. Kant's Formula of Universal Law tests whether I can will my maxim as a universal law: if universalizing the maxim yields a contradiction in conception, I have a perfect duty to refrain; if it yields only a contradiction in the will, I have an imperfect duty. Hare treats universalizability as a logical feature of the language of morals rather than a substantive test: anyone who prescribes a certain act for himself in the circumstances is committed, on pain of logical inconsistency, to prescribing the same act for anyone in relevantly similar circumstances. Singer, in "Famine, Affluence, and Morality," treats the move as a substantive principle of impartiality — roughly, that we ought to prevent something bad when we can do so without sacrificing anything of comparable moral importance — and presses it toward strong duties of assistance.

Sort these three accounts into tiers — top, middle, bottom — according to how well each captures the legitimate force of the ordinary "what if everyone did that?" argument and how honestly each diagnoses the ways that argument misfires. In particular, weigh how each account handles three failure modes: (i) the slide into a prediction about aggregate consequences, as in the thought that if everyone stole, society would collapse; (ii) the confusion between everyone's performing an act at once and everyone's adopting the maxim or policy behind it; and (iii) the cases in which universalization looks beside the point — where an act seems wrong even though I can coherently will its generalization, or seems right even though I cannot. Give a sentence explaining why each account lands in its tier. Then say which account you would defend as the best explanation of the argument's force, and what the other two would need added or reinterpreted to match it.

Throughout, separate what is settled about how these positions work from what is genuinely contested among people who study them, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
