For this exercise I want you to work with a specific passage in Frank Jackson's knowledge argument, not merely with the often-anthologized thought experiment by itself. In "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (Philosophical Quarterly, 1982), near the end of the paper, Jackson considers the objection that when Mary leaves her black-and-white room, her new knowledge is only ability knowledge or an acquaintance with color, not factual knowledge. As I paraphrase the passage: he rejects that deflation; he insists that Mary comes to know something about what it is like for someone to see a colored object, something expressible as a truth about another person's experience, and that this truth is not entailed by the complete physical information she already possessed. Treat that rejoinder as your target.

Please write your answer in American English.

Give me your credence that Jackson's rejoinder in the passage just characterized is sound. That is, give me your credence that Mary's post-release epistemic gain includes a factual component the complete physical truth does not entail, and that this suffices to show physicalism leaves something out. Do not give me a bare number. Decompose the probability into at least the following terms:

(a) your credence that Mary's post-release epistemic state contains knowledge-that, i.e., factual knowledge, rather than only abilities or acquaintance;

(b) your credence that, conditional on (a), the relevant knowledge-that is not deducible from the complete physical truth about Mary and her environment;

(c) your credence that, conditional on (a) and (b), this result refutes physicalism or establishes that there are nonphysical facts;

(d) any further term you think belongs, including a term for the possibility that Mary merely gains a new way of representing physical facts she already knew under a different guises or guise; or a term for phenomenal concepts.

Provide a probability and one line of justification for each term, then show how you combine them, and give your final credence in the proposition that Jackson's rejoinder is sound.

Finally, tell me which term in your decomposition you are least confident in, and describe what evidence, argument, or consideration, whether philosophical or empirical, would do the most to move it.
