I have been trying to make my views about the aggregation of very small harms precise, and I have settled, tentatively, on the following five commitments. I would like you to audit the set for consistency rather than to argue for or against any one of them.

1. A speck of dust in the eye is a genuine harm. If two possible outcomes are exactly alike except that, in one of them, one additional person briefly gets a speck of dust in the eye, that outcome is worse.

2. The relation of being worse than is transitive: if outcome A is worse than B, and B is worse than C, then A is worse than C.

3. There is no number of people, each receiving a single speck of dust in the eye, such that this outcome is worse than one person being tortured for a year. I intend this for every finite number, however astronomically large.

4. Within a single life, by contrast, tiny harms do aggregate: there is some number of dust specks delivered to one person over time such that undergoing that series is worse for that person than being tortured for a year. I would say the same about enough pinpricks or enough hours of mild nausea.

5. The badness of an outcome is exhausted by how it goes for the individuals in it, together with how many such individuals there are; there is no further impersonal or cosmic badness over and above the individual harms.

Are these five jointly tenable? Go through them and identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which are only apparently in tension. For each genuine tension, ordered by severity, say how severe it is, what would have to be given up to resolve it, and what the cheapest single revision of the set would be. I am not attached to any one of the five, and I cannot yet see where the problem lies.
