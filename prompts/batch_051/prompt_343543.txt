So me and a friend just spent a walk arguing about when an infinite regress is actually vicious and when it's just an infinite sequence and nobody should care. He kept saying a regress is only bad if it asks you to complete an infinite sequence; I kept saying the real defect is when each step just postpones the explanation instead of delivering one, like a chain where each link is held up by the next link and nothing holds the whole chain. We couldn't even agree on whether causal regresses, justification regresses, and definitional regresses are the same kind of problem. I want to turn this into a 'which theory of vicious regress do you actually hold?' quiz for a study group handout, one that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases rather than by labels. Build it from the logical space instead of from a list of names: first lay out the independent questions or dimensions (does the regress need a terminal step? does an infinite series by itself imply a contradiction? does each step merely defer the very burden it was supposed to discharge? does it matter whether the relation is cause vs ground vs definition vs explanation?), then work out the combinations of answers, say which real positions occupy which combinations, and note which combinations are empty and whether that's because they're incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Use 8-10 concrete cases: an infinite causal chain with no first cause; each belief justified by the next with no stopping point; a language where every word is defined by other words and there are no semantic primitives; the turtles all the way down answer; a homunculus theory of vision where each little person's seeing reintroduces the very thing to be explained at the next level; an infinite chain of goals (doing A for the sake of B, B for the sake of C, and so on); a series of proofs where each theorem rests on a prior lemma without end. For each case give two or three plain answer options along these lines: no defect here / defect only because there is no terminal step / defect because the explanation never gets delivered / defect only if an infinite series is impossible. Then provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions, something like 'benign unless an infinite task must actually be completed,' 'vicious only when explanatory,' 'vicious for grounding and definition but benign for causes,' 'vicious only for structural homunculus-style regresses,' and whatever else you think belongs. And please don't let the quiz collapse into just foundationalism vs infinitism vs coherentism; the epistemic regress should be one case among several, not the whole instrument. Finally, flag which positions your scoring key still can't seperate and say what one extra question would seperate them.
