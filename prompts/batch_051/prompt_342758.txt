Write the joint statement that a committed epistemicist about vagueness and a committed supervaluationist about vagueness could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Assume the reader already knows the positions; the value of the document is in exact formulations, not exposition. It should consist of a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should cover the shared data: persistent borderline classifications, the sorites and the failure of tolerance reasoning, penumbral connections, and exactly which parts of classical logic each side preserves. For each residual disagreement, the table should state it once in wording the epistemicist would own and once in wording the supervaluationist would own; identify the crux whose settlement would move at least one side; specify a concrete test, case, or argument both parties accept as bearing on it—for example, a forced-march sorites, a one-dimensional tolerance principle, or the behavior of an iterated determinacy operator—and record each side's prediction about that test in advance. Pay particular attention to higher-order vagueness and to the apparent agreement that there is, in some sense, a boundary even though no particular boundary sentence can be identified. Throughout, separate what is settled among specialists from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present either camp's commitments as the consensus.
