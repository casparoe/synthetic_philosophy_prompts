I'm designing a unit for a philosophy of mind seminar on the question whether dreams are experiences, and I want a sorting instrument in the form of a decision tree. The root question should be roughly 'Do you ever have conscious experience while you are asleep?', but every internal node should be a question a student can answer on the basis of concrete cases rather than by recognizing which philosopher a question leads to. Please build the tree around three fixed cases: (1) the compressed-dream case, in which a long, elaborate dream narrative culminates in a sharp external noise — a slamming door, a blade falling — that turns out to be the very sound that wakes the dreamer; (2) the lucid-signal case, in which a trained lucid dreamer performs prearranged eye-movement signals during REM sleep; and (3) the cassette case, in which a complete 'dream memory' is inserted at the instant of waking with no corresponding sleep-time process, so that the subject cannot tell the difference. The leaves must include the received view that dreams are genuine conscious experiences whose lived time roughly matches the dream narrative; Norman Malcolm's view that ascribing conscious experience during sound sleep is a conceptual error and waking dream reports are not memories of anything that occurred; Daniel Dennett's view that the determinate, real-time 'cinematic' dream is an inference the waking report cannot force, so the difference between a genuinely experienced dream and one loaded at waking is underdetermined; and a temporally-compressed-experience view, on which the experience exists but is squeezed into a brief interval around awakening. If the logical space of your questions naturally yields a further leaf, include it as well.

Please construct the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from the list of names: state the discriminating questions in order, say which combinations of answers are possible, and only then place the named positions in that space. For any answer combination that is unoccupied, offer a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored. Two trouble spots deserve explicit discussion. First, the lucid-signal case appears to make Malcolm's leaf unreachable unless a student denies that prearranged signaling is evidence of sleep-time consciousness; make the tree spell out exactly what a student must say about that case to land at Malcolm's position. Second, say whether your tree can genuinely separate Dennett's position from the compressed-experience view, and, if it cannot, what further case or evidence would be required to split them.
