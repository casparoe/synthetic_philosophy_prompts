I'm a philosophy of mind PhD student and there's a bind I keep noticing in how people define 'physical' when they're trying to make physicalism come out true, and I don't know whether it has a name. If 'physical' means whatever current physics talks about, physicalism is hostage to physics changing and probably false as stated. If it means whatever a final, completed physics would posit, whatever that turns out to be, then 'everything is physical' stops saying anything—it just says everything is whatever science eventually describes. The move I actually want to understand is a third one I've seen a few times: people define the physical as whatever completed physics would posit, except that consciousness (or mental vocabulary) can't be among its fundamental posits. That blocks the triviality, but it looks blatantly ad hoc, like vitalism with extra steps. Does the original two-horned bind have an established name, and does that third move have it's own name, or does it just collapse back into one of the horns? I'm not after a bare label—I want the carving, so say exactly what the phenomenon is, what the nearest concepts are, and where the edges are. Reply as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction or conclusion.
