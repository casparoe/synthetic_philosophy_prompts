I work as a data engineer on master data management and entity resolution, so my everyday problems are about identity: deciding whether two records refer to the same real-world person, organisation, or object, and designing the keys and matching rules that enforce those decisions. My working vocabulary for all of this is attribute vectors, natural versus surrogate keys, deduplication, matching thresholds, and the distinction between a record and the entity it represents. I've been reading a philosophical debate that looks at first glance like it should be exactly my subject, and I want to know how much of it actually translates.

On one side is the identity of indiscernibles, the Leibnizian principle that two things cannot differ in number alone: if x and y share every property, then x simply is y. On the other side is Black's two-sphere universe, a thought experiment in which the entire world contains nothing but two iron spheres of precisely the same radius, composition, temperature, and colour, separated by some fixed distance. The claim is that they are plainly two spheres rather than one, even though nothing qualitative distinguishes one from the other. Defenders of the principle reply that relational facts such as being some distance from another iron sphere, or belonging to a world containing two spheres, are themselves distinguishing properties, and the debate then turns on whether such relational or global facts count, and on whether numerical distinctness could be brute rather than grounded in any difference at all.

Restate this debate in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can — in terms of entities, relation instances, key structures, record matching, and deduplication — and then give me the residue report: which claims map cleanly, which map only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in the way I work. I suspect, for instance, that talk of possible worlds and of what could exist may have no analogue in a system that only ever sees actual records, but I want to know whether that is a real limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that that part of the dispute was doing less work than it appeared to do. I am fluent on the engineering side, so do not explain primary keys or matching thresholds, but please be precise about every philosophical term you rely on, and do not smuggle in assumptions from data modelling and call them translation.
