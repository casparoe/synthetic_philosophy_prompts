Longtime lesswrong/ACX reader, so I've already absorbed the standard debunking arsenal: scope insensitivity, absurdity heuristics, expected value versus expected utility under model uncertainty, the works. Here's my problem. The scale argument for caring about insect welfare seems structurally airtight and I can't find an honest dial to turn. The setup is: I don't need a defensible number for P(a given insect has valenced experience). I just need it to not be literally zero, and there are something like 10^18 insects alive at any moment, with way more insect-lives turning over each season, so the insect term swamps every other animal-welfare calculation by orders of magnitude. If I set the probability at one in a million, that's still a trillion expected-sentient insects. To stop that from dominating, I have to say the probability is zero, or the capacity to suffer is zero, or that my credence is too imprecise to do arithmetic with at all. I don't have a principled reason for the first two, since my uncertainty about insect cognition is exactly that — uncertainty, not evidence of absence.

But when I actually picture a wasp, the whole calculation stops feeling like moral reasoning. I'm not sure I even have a coherent handle on what a wasp's welfare would be such that I could trade it against, say, a pig's. It feels like multiplying a number I can't ground by a probability I can't constrain and then letting the product boss my ethics around. I know that feeling is exactly what a scope-insensitivity debunking would predict, so I can't just trust it; I've also seen people on the forum try to defuse the argument with Knightian uncertainty, and others say thats just refusing to play the expected-value game. Neither response lands for me.

So I want the negotiated version, not a winner. Walk me through what it would actually take for each thing to go. (1) What would have to be true for the intuition to be the thing I drop: is there a non-ad-hoc prior over insect sentience that makes the insect term small, or is any prior that avoids bug dominance just reverse-engineered to avoid it? (2) What would have to be true for the scale argument to be the thing I amend or abandon: is the right fix that maximizing expected value is undefined when the inputs are unmeasurable, or that my utility function should be bounded or uncertain about itself, and what other domains does that break? (3) Is there a stable reading where both survive, like wasp welfare is real but counts as a different kind of value that doesn't simply sum with pig welfare? I'm less interested in which side you'd pick than in knowing the exact dial the whole dispute turns on.
