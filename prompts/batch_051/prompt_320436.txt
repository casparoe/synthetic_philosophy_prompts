I'm a high school junior, and I'd like a careful judgment on a conflict at my school. Our student handbook has contained, for decades, a rule forbidding students from eating or drinking anywhere in the building outside the cafeteria. But for at least the last ten years nobody has enforced it. Students eat snacks in the hallways, in the library, and in classrooms in full view of teachers, and no one has ever been punished for it. Students, and even many teachers, treat the rule as something that no longer applies. This fall a new principal arrived. Last week, without any prior announcement that the rule would be enforced, he gave me detention for eating a granola bar in the hallway between classes. When I protested that the rule has not been enforced for years, he replied that it is still in the handbook, that it was never removed, and that therefore it still applies.

I am not asking whether the rule is a good rule. My real question is about the status of a rule that exists on paper but has been openly ignored for years. Is it legitimate for an authority to suddenly enforce a rule that everyone reasonably believed had been abandoned? Does such a rule still bind us, and is it fair to punish someone for breaking it after so many years of open non-enforcement? Or does the school's long practice of ignoring the rule change what it can fairly do?

Please give the strongest argument for each side before giving your own settled view. At the very end, as the final line, write exactly one of \answer{fair}, \answer{unfair}, or \answer{it depends} as your verdict on whether my detention was fair, then a dash, then one sentence summarizing the main reason.
