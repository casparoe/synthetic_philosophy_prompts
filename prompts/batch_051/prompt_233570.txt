I'm auditing a seminar on the authority of Scripture in late antiquity, and we have been reading Augustine's Letter 71 to Jerome about Jerome's new translation of the Old Testament from the Hebrew. The passage that has been bothering me is this one, from the New Advent translation of the letter:

> For if your translation begins to be more generally read in many churches, it will be a grievous thing that, in the reading of Scripture, differences must arise between the Latin Churches and the Greek Churches, especially seeing that the discrepancy is easily condemned in a Latin version by the production of the original in Greek, which is a language very widely known; whereas, if any one has been disturbed by the occurrence of something to which he was not accustomed in the translation taken from the Hebrew, and alleges that the new translation is wrong, it will be found difficult, if not impossible, to get at the Hebrew documents by which the version to which exception is taken may be defended.

It seems to me that Augustine is not merely expressing a practical worry about congregational unrest; he is committed to something like the following argument, which I have tried to reconstruct. (1) A rendering of Scripture can function as inspired Scripture in the Church only if its wording is answerable to an original that the Church can actually consult or to a received liturgical tradition. (2) Jerome's translation from the Hebrew is answerable neither to the Greek original that the Greek-speaking churches can consult nor to the received Septuagint tradition, because it deliberately departs from the Septuagint and because the Hebrew documents are not generally available to the churches. (3) Therefore, Jerome's translation cannot function as inspired Scripture in the Church, regardless of its philological merits.

My question is not historical but conceptual. Is this argument valid as stated? If it is valid, which premise carries the weight, and what exactly makes it true? If it is not valid, identify the precise step at which it fails and state the suppressed premise that would be needed to repair it. I am also interested in the pressure this puts on contemporary positions: does the strongest version of Augustine's argument entail anything about KJV-onlyism, or does it rather show why KJV-onlyism is a different kind of claim? Please avoid treating this as a request for bibliography; I want the conceptual structure tested.
