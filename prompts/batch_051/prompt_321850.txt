I'm preparing a short statement of my position on utility and welfare for a seminar on the foundations of welfare economics, and before I present it I'd like a consistency audit of the position as a whole. My working commitments, each of which has come to seem right to me for its own reasons, are these. (1) In positive economics I am a revealed-preference theorist: attributing a preference to an agent is just a compact way of summarizing her choice behavior, and the utility function is nothing over and above a numerical representation of those choices; talk of utility as an inner magnitude of satisfaction strikes me as unscientific. (2) I am also persuaded by the behavioral findings — framing effects, default effects, and preference reversals among them — and I take them to show that people's actual choices are often not a trustworthy guide to their real interests. (3) For normative purposes I think welfare should be understood as the satisfaction of a person's informed preferences: what she would want for herself under full information and full attention, reflectively endorsed rather than merely displayed in choice. (4) I think utility over sure outcomes is ordinal: only the ordering carries content, and neither intensity of preference nor interpersonal comparison of utility is meaningful. (5) At the same time, I accept von Neumann–Morgenstern expected-utility theory as the right way to model choice under risk in positive economics, including its recovery of a cardinal utility function over sure outcomes from preferences over lotteries. (6) Finally, on the strength of (2) and (3), I have come to defend a mild, preference-respecting paternalism in policy design: set defaults and frames so as to steer people toward their informed preferences, while preserving the ability to opt out.

I want a committed audit rather than a survey of possible readings. Go through the six commitments and identify the pairings or larger groupings that genuinely pull against one another; rank them by severity and explain why each tension is real and not merely terminological. Then identify at least one pairing that appears to be in tension but dissolves on closer inspection, and say exactly why it dissolves. Finally, name the single revision that would leave the most coherent overall position at the lowest cost.
