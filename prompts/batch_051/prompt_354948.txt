I keep going back and forth on hedonism about value. On paper its got everything I want from a theory: one thing that's good in itself, one thing bad, and everything else gets its value by tending toward those. But every time I try to actually belive it, I hit the same snag. The states I'd have to file under 'pleasure' don't seem to share any felt core. There's the diffuse animal warmth of lying in the sun after a swim, the sharp little relief of scratching an itch, the cool click of realizing a proof goes through, the glee of a joke landing in a crowded room, the pleasant ache of a sad song in a minor key. What exactly do these have in common, beyond my being inclined in some loose way to call them pleasant or to seek or keep them? It feels like the theory needs pleasure to be a single kind of state so it can be the single currency of value, but introspection keeps handing me a disjunction.

I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes—what would a hedonist have to show about pleasure (that it's an attitude rather than a sensation? that the heterogeneity is real but doesn't matter?) for me to stop trusting the introspection. And what it would take for the theory to be the thing that goes, or to get amended into something more pluralist. Is there a reading of 'pleasure'—or of 'good in itself'—on which hedonism survives the heterogeneity point without collapsing into 'whatever people happen to like'? Where should I be looking for the pressure point: in the mental states, or in the value theory?
