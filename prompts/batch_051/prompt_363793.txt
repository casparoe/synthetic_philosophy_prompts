I keep noticing a pattern in how people analyze claims like "this thing is fragle," "this rail is live," "this stuff is rustable" in terms of what would happen if some condition occurred, and I can't tell whether what I'm seeing is one phenomenon or several, or whether it has established names. Three cases, all with the same general shape.

One: a bare electrified rail wired to a safety cutoff that senses contact a few milliseconds before a hand lands and grounds the rail. So the rail is live — if you touched it, it would shock you — but if you actually touch it, it doesn't shock, because the contact itself trips the cutoff before your skin meets the metal. The claim "it would shock if touched" seems true when things are idle, and false at the exact moment of contact.

Two: a wine glass that's genuinly fragile, but it sits inside a custom foam cradle strapped to it. If someone drops it, it doesn't break — not because it stopped being fragile, but because the cradle absorbs the impact. The trigger happened, the fragility stuck around, and still no breaking.

Three: a ceramic tile that is not fragile at all; its internal structure is tough. But it's mounted in a rig that, upon detecting an incoming hammer a split second before it lands, sprays a solvent that embrittles the tile, so when the hammer hits, it shatters. With the rig on, "if struck, it would break" is true, but I'd still deny the tile is fragile.

My question: do these three configurations have names, and are they treated as distinct problems? More specifically, how does each sit with the simple conditional analysis of dispositions — roughly, an object has a tendency T just in case it would display behavior B under circumstances C? I've heard people say cases like one and two just refute that analysis, while others think it can be patched by qualifying the circumstances. I want the carving, not just a label: which case threatens which direction of the biconditional, where the line falls between "the trigger destroys the tendency" and "the trigger blocks the display" and "the trigger manufactures the tendency," and whether case three even belongs in the same file as one and two.
