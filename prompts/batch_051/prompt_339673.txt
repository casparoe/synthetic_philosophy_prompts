I've been trying to use Dennett's ladder of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures as a way of classifying minds, and I understand the rungs like this. Darwinian creatures only get new designs through selection across generations. Skinnerian creatures also vary their own behavior within a lifetime and keep whatever gets reinforced. Popperian creatures can test candidate actions in an inner model of the world, so bad candidates die inside the model instead of out in the world. Gregorian creatures import cultural tools—words, diagrams, procedures—and use them to build better inner models and better candidates in the first place.

Here's the borderline case I want a verdict on. Say an RLHF language model at inference time uses best-of-fifty: it samples fifty candidate completions, scores each with its learned reward model, and returns only the highest-scoring one; the other forty-nine are discarded before anyone sees them. The reward model was trained on human preferences, so it's a kind of imported summary of what people would approve of. But the system never acts in the world between candidates, and its only inner model is whatever is distributed in its weights. Is this thing Popperian (the rejected candidates die in its stead), Skinnerian (variation plus an external reinforcer), or Gregorian (the reward model is an imported mind-tool)? If it lands between rungs, tell me whether thats the case's fault or the ladder's.
