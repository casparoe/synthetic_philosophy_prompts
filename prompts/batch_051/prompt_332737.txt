I have no background in philosophy or law, so I have been trying to work this out with plain cases and would welcome a second opinion. My friend has very settled views about who owns information about a person. Over the years she has made the following judgments:

1. Her search history is hers, and the search company has no right to build a profile from it without her say.
2. The fact that she attended a particular street fair on a particular afternoon is not owned by anyone.
3. A photograph a tourist takes of her on a public street is the photographer's, not hers.
4. An MRI scan of her knee is hers, and the clinic may not sell the scan or anything derived from it without her permission.
5. A report stating that 43 percent of households in her neighborhood walk to work is not hers, not even in part.
6. A grocery chain's inference, drawn from her loyalty-card purchases, that she probably has a toddler is hers; the chain may not sell that inference even though it did the computing.

Now I have a new case. A data company scrapes the selfies she posted publicly on her own social media account and sells them, labeled with her name and face, to a facial-recognition company. What should I expect her to say: that the scraped images are hers, that they are the data company's, or that no one owns them? More importantly, I want to know what rule or rules she is actually running. Which distinct rules would fit all six of her earlier judgments, what would each of those rules say about the scraped selfies, which rule is your best guess about her and why, and what single further case would most cleanly force a choice among the rules that fit?
