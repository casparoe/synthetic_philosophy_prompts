I've been trying to figure out where my sister actually draws the line on grit, because she's a physical therapist and people expect her to be all about "resillience," but her takes are strangely specific. She says the hospital where she used to work rolled out a wellness webinar for nurses on an understaffed ward and then called the ones who stayed "heroes"; her read is that hero is what a staffing budget calls itself when it won't hire anyone, and the nurses weren't showing grit, they were just being bled dry, since most of them couldn't afford to quit and there's no other hospital within an hour. But when our cousin wrecked her back in a car accident and put herself through two years of brutal physical therapy because she wanted to dance at her own wedding, my sister said it was the most genuine grit she'd ever seen: nobody made her do it, she could of quit any day, and no one would have blamed her. She was a lot less kind to her old roommate, whose parents pay her rent on the condition she finishes a nursing degree she hates. The roommate keeps saying she just isn't strong enough to power through; my sister says that isn't weakness, that's a leash with a lease attached. Then she'll turn around and say our retired neighbor, who spent a year learning to walk again after a stroke because he wanted to hike with his grandkids, is the bravest person she knows. And she gets almost mad when the guy running for city council calls single mothers "the most resilient people I know" while voting down childcare funding; she says calling a mom who skips meals so her kid can eat resilient is just a warm feeling you buy after deciding not to fix the problem.

Now a friend of hers teaches third grade at an underfunded school. She actually has an offer to transfer to a nicer district with better pay and enough supplies, but she's staying because the kids at the current school "don't have anyone else," and the principal has taken to introducing her as a "resilient educator" at every staff meeting. I can't tell how my sister will react: is this the cousin-doing-physical-therapy kind of grit, or the nurse-webinar kind? If you had to predict her verdict, which way would you lean? More importantly, what rule is she actually running when she sorts these cases, what other rule would fit all her old calls just as well, and where do those rules disagree about the teacher? And if her track record really doesn't settle it, what would she be committing herself to by deciding either way, and what's one more case I could bring up that would cleanly split the rules apart?
