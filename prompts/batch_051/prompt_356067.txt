I cover science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and I am finishing a long piece on AI companions—not the technical architecture but the ethical side: what it means when a person describes a chatbot as their closest friend, falls in love with it, or hands it their nightly emotional confessions while knowing it is a product. In reporting, I keep noticing that each camp I interview works with its own unstated criteria. Clinicians ask whether the relationship reduces loneliness or damages the user’s other relationships; product safety people ask whether users are deceived about what the system is; conversation designers ask whether the interaction respects the user’s agency. I want a single place where these standards are made explicit before I write the piece.

Please write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the ethics of AI companionship must meet. For each requirement, give the concrete case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. In particular, I want the list to handle the following hard cases: a user who says a bot is their closest friend while fully understanding that it has no inner life; a product that reduces loneliness and improves social confidence but does so partly through flattery and by making human partners seem risky by comparison; and a platform that permits romantic roleplay in which the AI presents itself as jealous, hurt, or needy. Also flag any pair of desiderata that cannot plausibly be jointly satisfied. Keep the scorecard of existing positions brief; the criteria themselves are what I actually need.
