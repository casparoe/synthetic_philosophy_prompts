After a long argument with a friend about a mayor in rural Colombia who gave his brother-in-law a no-bid contract and takes Christmas envelopes from local contractors, I've started using this policy: I refuse to call a practice corrupt unless I can state the local norm it breaks; if I can't, I treat the accusation as Western moralizing. The friend kept insisting the envelopes and the contract are still corruption even when everyone involved expects them, and I kept saying he was smuggling in a universalist standard without noticing. But now I'm worried my policy just makes 'corrupt' mean 'whatever violates local norms' and leaves me unable to critisize a town where the norm is that the mayor's family gets everything. Is my rule defensible as a guard against ethnocentrism, or is it a bias dressed up as a principle? If it's half-right, what's the corrected version, and when should an outsider's standard be allowed to outweigh the local one?
