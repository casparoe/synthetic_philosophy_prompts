Here's a career decision I keep going back and forth on. I work in climate adaptation policy, and a friend in the effective altruism community has been pushing me to move into AI governance instead. His argument, stripped down: because future people vastly outnumber present people, the expected value of a decision is dominated by its effects on the long-run trajectory; and because we are plausibly entering the transition to transformative AI, the next few decades have an unusually high chance of being when that trajectory gets set. He grants that any specific intervention is uncertain, but concludes that even a small probability of steering the trajectory swamps ordinary near-term impact.

My unease is that a thoughtful person in 1850 could have made the same formal argument about industrialization, or in 1950 about nuclear weapons, and we now look back on those claims less as accurate forecasting than as predictable overconfidence in one's own era. But I know that a general induction on every generation believing it is special does not, by itself, show that ours is not the exception. How should I reason about his argument before making a costly commitment, and does my unease track a real weakness in it or just personal status quo bias? When you answer, please say how confident you are in your overall judgment and single out the step you are least sure about.
