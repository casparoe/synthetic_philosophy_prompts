Two people in my reading group have spent a year fighting about moral responsibility, and at this point there not even really hearing each other. One is a Strawsonian: responsibility lives in the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation, guilt — and what those attitudes track is the quality of someone's will and the role they play in the ordinary practice of holding one another to account, not some metaphysically heavy power to have done otherwise. So determinism isn't a threat to responsibility. The other is a responsibility skeptic in the hard incompatibilist vein: backward-looking desert — the "you deserve my anger" thing — requires both the ability to do otherwise and being the ultimate source of what you are, and we don't have either whether or not determinism is true, so the desert-shaped reactive attitudes are unjustified; forward-looking stuff like deterrence, quarantine, and reform survives, but not genuine desert. I'm running an adversarial collaboration between them, and I need the document they should have produced.

Write that document. It should start with a numbered section of claims both sides can sign — not anodyne ones, ones with actual bite. Then a disagreements table where each side's view is phrased so that side would accept the wording as the strongest fair version of it. Then the cruxes: the subquestions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them, with the merely verbal disagreements flagged and set aside. For each crux, give a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it — a manipulation case where a person's bad act is engineered by neuroscientists but he acts on desires he endorses and is reasons-responsive, a friend's betrayal reconsidered after you learn determinism is true, the question of whether we could live the objective stance toward everyone, whatever does the real work — and record each side's prediction about that case in advance. They're not asking for a verdict on who wins, and no citations or reading lists; they want a statement they could both actually put their names to.

Start with the statement itself. No preamble, no "here is the document," and no commentary after.
