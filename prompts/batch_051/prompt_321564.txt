I am designing a unit on global justice for an upper-level applied philosophy course, and I need a thought experiment built to order. The unit is organized around one question — "What does a rich country owe a poor one as a matter of justice?" — and I want the opening case to isolate a single variable: whether the rich country's wealth is causally downstream of wrongs done to the poor country. The point is to show students that the three views in the course are separated not only by their conclusions but by what they count as a reason. The case is for an exam-style exercise in which students must reconstruct each view's reasoning from the scenario alone, so the verdicts must be generated from the theories; a scenario that simply stipulates them is useless to me.

Design a one-paragraph, ordinary-world scenario about two fictional countries — call them Verdantia and Alluvium — Verdantia wealthy and Alluvium poor in the way the unit takes for granted. Let them be matched in every other respect that could plausibly matter to the theories: population size, institutional competence, absence of corruption, and the ability of Verdantia's citizens to know the facts and to help. The single additional stipulated difference is historical: Verdantia's wealth has no causal connection to Alluvium's poverty — no colonial extraction, no coercive loans, no terms imposed by force, no unfulfilled commitments, no other historical wrongdoing linking the two. The scenario must make the following three verdicts fall out of the views:

- View F (forward-looking cosmopolitan consequentialism): Verdantia owes Alluvium substantial, ongoing transfers as a matter of justice. Marginal resources do far more good in Alluvium, so the current distribution is unjust, and any fact about the historical provenance of Verdantia's wealth is inert.
- View R (reparative-relational): Verdantia owes Alluvium nothing as a matter of justice. Duties of justice arise only from a specific history of wrongdoing or unfulfilled commitments between the two parties, and the stipulated isolation extinguishes all such grounds; at most Verdantia owes humanitarian charity.
- View I (institutional basic-structure): Verdantia owes Alluvium substantial transfers as a matter of justice. The two countries are already embedded in one global structure of trade, finance, and taxation, and that structure is unjust in a way that benefits Verdantia; no bilateral history is needed.

Then produce, in this order and with roughly the indicated lengths:

1. The scenario (150–250 words): the case itself, written so the stipulated isolation feels natural rather than didactic, and so View R's verdict lands with its full harsh cost without editorializing.
2. Verdicts and reasoning (350–500 words): for each of View F, View R, and View I, show the inference by which the specified verdict follows from the scenario, making clear where the views disagree about what counts as a reason and why F and I can agree on the verdict while diverging on the ground.
3. The load-bearing feature (100–150 words): identify the single feature doing the work — presumably the stipulated absence of historical wrongdoing — and state exactly why the three verdicts pattern themselves around it.
4. Damage control (150–250 words): imagine a defender of View R attacking the scenario as rigged, question-begging, or so idealized as to be irrelevant; then show whether a defender of View I or View F can answer that objection without changing the stipulated facts.
5. Removal test (150–200 words): say what each view would conclude if the load-bearing feature were removed — that is, if Verdantia's wealth were made continuously traceable to past extraction from Alluvium — and explain whether the case would still isolate the intended variable or would collapse into a more familiar colonial-reparations example. End by stating exactly what the thought experiment would then fail to test.

Constraints: no ideal observers, veils of ignorance, moral experts, or science-fictional devices; the only permitted idealization is the stipulated historical isolation itself. Do not introduce real countries, historical cases, or named authors; everything needed is in the prompt. If you judge that any of the three specified verdicts cannot legitimately be generated from the stated views, say so explicitly and repair the setup by adjusting the scenario, not by weakening the views.
