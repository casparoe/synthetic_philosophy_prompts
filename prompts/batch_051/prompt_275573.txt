I'm a science/environment reporter working on a piece pegged to the Wilderness Act turning 60, and I keep getting hung up on what looks like a direct contradiction inside the statute itself. Section 2(c) has the famous line that wilderness is an area "where the earth and its community of life are untrammeled by man, where man himself is a visitor who does not remain." But Section 2(a) says the designated areas "shall be administered for the use and enjoyment of the American people in such manner as will leave them unimpaired for future use and enjoyment as wilderness." And later the Act says those uses include recreation, scenery, science, education, conservation, and history—which sure sounds like a lot of people doing a lot of things in the place where man is only supposed to be a visitor who does not remain.

Can you reconcile those two? Lay out the distinct readings: maybe "man" means different things in different clauses, maybe "visitor who does not remain" is about permanent habitation not recreational presence, maybe "untrammeled" means unregulated by human design rather than untouched by human feet, maybe the recreation allowed is primitive and non-mechanized so it doesn't imprint the same way. For each candidate, say what it saves and what it costs, and then give a verdict: which reading is the honest reading of the statute and which ones are just agency PR or environmentalist wishful thinking.

Hard cap of 700 words, and lead with your bottom line before you lay out the options.
