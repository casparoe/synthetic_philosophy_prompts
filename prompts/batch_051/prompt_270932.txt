Act as an expert in philosophical semantics who also understands how to design practical organizational processes. Think carefully, and before you write the procedure, briefly work through the core commitments of an inferential-role approach to meaning and the main risks of translating it for a team with no philosophical training.

I run the terminology group at a clinical-research software company. Every quarter we revise the controlled vocabulary and API data dictionary for our trial-management platform; research sites, sponsors, and monitors rely on these definitions when the software routes events, generates reports, and decides who must be notified. A bad definition can lead to missed regulatory filings or audit findings. The group has no training in philosophy or formal semantics, and we typically get about two hours of discussion per term. Every change must be approved by both our engineering lead and a regulatory lawyer.

Our leadership has decided, on a consultant's advice, that we should stop writing dictionary-style glosses and instead define each term by its inferential role: what classifying something under the term commits a user to, what it entitles them to do or say, what it is incompatible with, and what downstream system behavior it licenses. The consultant left before explaining how to make this operational, and I have been told to turn it into our standard process.

Please write a reusable procedure my group can follow each quarter. It should give, in order: the questions to ask about any candidate definition; what counts as an adequate answer to each; where the procedure should stop with a no and send the definition back for rework; and where a human must make a value or business judgment rather than a factual one. Be explicit about which distinctions in the underlying idea we must preserve and which we can safely drop in our context, and about what this procedure cannot do or where it will misfire. Then run the procedure on three terms we are revising this quarter: "serious adverse event", "protocol deviation", and "data custodian". Use only our ordinary working understanding of these terms—do not reconstruct actual regulatory definitions—and make sure at least one worked example reaches an answer the group will not like.
