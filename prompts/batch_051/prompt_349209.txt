Ok so im a highschool student and I got super into the language acquisition debate for a class paper, but now im stuck. We read this passage from Chomsky's 1959 review of B.F. Skinner's *Verbal Behavior*, and I dont get why its supposed to be such a big deal:

"The problem to which this book is addressed is that of giving a “functional analysis” of verbal behavior. By functional analysis, Skinner means identification of the variables that control this behavior and specification of how they interact to determine a particular verbal response. Furthermore, the controlling variables are to be described completely in terms of such notions as stimulus, reinforcement, deprivation, which have been given a reasonably clear meaning in animal experimentation. In other words, the goal of the book is to provide a way to predict and control verbal behavior by observing and manipulating the physical environment of the speaker."

My teacher says this passage is part of the poverty of the stimulus argument, but when I try to read actual back-and-forth between nativists and empiricists it feels like they're not even arguing about the same thing. One side says children don't get enough data to learn grammar; the other side says you don't need rich innate knowledge, just general learning mechanisms and indirect negative evidence. Then someone says "but what counts as an explanation?" and everyone nods and then goes back to their corners.

So my actual question: why has this debate stalled for like 60 years? Is it a hidden factual disagreement about what kids actually hear, a disagreement about what counts as an explanation in psychology, a disagreement about the word "innate," or just two groups answering different questions under the same heading?
