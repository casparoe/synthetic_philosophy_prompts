I help run a small farm sanctuary — we take in spent hens, sheep, and goats from animal agriculture, and we're a mix of vegans and freegans. Our freegan members are fine using animal products when no demand is created and the thing would otherwise be wasted, like dumpstered cheese or roadkill. That principle is clear for what we pull out of a dumpster, but we keep going in circles about the byproducts of the animals we've rescued: the hens lay eggs, the sheep need shearing so we have wool, there's manure, and occasionally an animal dies of old age. Some of us think using or selling the eggs and wool to cover feed and vet bills is just freeganism applied to sanctuary life, since the goods exist either way and someone might as well use them. Others think selling them turns the animals into workers and tells visitors that eggs and wool are here to be used, which undercuts the whole point of rescue.

I'd like you to act as an expert on the ethics of animal sanctuaries and on freeganism, think carefully, and write us a decision procedure we can actually run each time one of these byproduct questions comes up. Start by brainstorming all the ways a byproduct could be wrong even when it involves no new killing or demand. Then give us a ordered list of questions to work through, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because it's a values call rather than a facts call. The constraints are: whatever we do can't create demand for animal products, can't treat the rescued animals as means, and can't blur the sanctuary's message; the procedure also has to be usable by a small staff with no philosophy background in under an hour per decision. These decisions come up every season, they eat whole meetings, and the board has to sign off on anything we sell. Then run the procedure on three cases: the hens' eggs, the wool after shearing, and the manure. Make it give definite answers when it can, flag which of those answers you think we won't like, and say what the procedure can't settle and will keep punting to a person.
