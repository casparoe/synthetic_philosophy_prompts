I am a novelist at work on a story whose crisis turns on the decisions of a hospital triage committee during a severe epidemic. I have drafted the committee's written policy myself, and two of its clauses seem to me to demand incompatible things. The first reads: "No patient shall be denied care or assigned lower priority by reason of age, disability, or diminished baseline life expectancy." The second reads: "Where resources are insufficient to treat every patient, priority shall be assigned so as to maximize the total number of quality-adjusted life-years gained." A physician who follows the second clause will favor the young and the previously healthy, which the first clause appears to forbid. Can these two clauses be reconciled at the level of principle, rather than by adding vague saving phrases? What are the strongest readings on which they are consistent—for example, one clause as an individual right and the other as a tie-breaker between otherwise equal claims, or one as a rule of exclusion and the other as a rule of ranking—and which of those readings could an honest drafter actually have intended? If no reconciliation is genuine, I would like to know which clause must give way, and at what cost.
