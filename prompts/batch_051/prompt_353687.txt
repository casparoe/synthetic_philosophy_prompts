Write a deadpan, bureaucratic FAQ page of roughly 600 words from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of an invented state that has governed a defined territory and population for decades but has never received a single act of diplomatic recognition. The entries should answer ordinary, practical citizen questions — whether their passports will be stamped, why the flag is absent from atlases, whether the trade agreements the state signs bind anyone, what observer status actually observes. The humor should come from the underlying philosophy: the ministry must insist, straight-faced, that recognition merely declares a statehood that already exists, and gesture at the four standard criteria of a permanent population, a defined territory, a government, and a capacity to enter into relations with other states, while every concrete answer quietly concedes that without others' acknowledgment the state cannot vote, trade, sue, borrow, or otherwise exercise the sovereignty it claims recognition cannot confer. Do not name the declaratory or constitutive theories of recognition; a specialist in international relations should recognize both positions being pushed to their mutually undermining conclusions, while a general reader can still follow the jokes. Keep the bureaucratese unbroken and the irony deadpan throughout.
