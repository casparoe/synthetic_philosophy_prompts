Give me a working definition of a moral trade. The phenomenon I have in mind is roughly this: an agent accepts something she regards as a moral wrong or cost, in return for another agent's doing something morally better than that other agent would otherwise have done. But that gloss is too loose, and I find that it sorts the following seven cases inconsistently.

1. Two legislators trade votes. Each votes for a bill she regards as mildly unjust, on the condition that the other votes for a bill she regards as preventing a much larger injustice. Neither would cast the traded vote without the deal. I think this is clearly a moral trade.

2. A person pays a neighbor forty dollars to mow his lawn while he is away. I think this is clearly not a moral trade.

3. A traveler buys a carbon offset for a transatlantic flight, paying a firm to plant trees that will, by the firm's estimate, absorb roughly the emissions the flight will release. She buys the offset because she regards the emissions as a real harm. I do not know whether this is a moral trade.

4. A hospital director takes a bribe to move a wealthy patient ahead of others on a surgery list, and then uses the bribe to fund operations for three uninsured patients who would otherwise not receive them. I do not know whether this is a moral trade.

5. A vegan eats a small plate of meat at a wedding, on the understanding that her sister—who would otherwise not do so—will eat vegan for the following month. I lean toward calling this a moral trade, but I cannot say why.

6. After shoplifting an item, a person privately donates twice its value to a food bank. I think this is not a moral trade, but I want the definition to explain why it is not.

7. A government abstains on a U.N. resolution condemning an atrocity, after the offending regime privately agrees to release several political prisoners it had jailed. I lean toward calling this a moral trade.

Propose the definition, run it through all seven cases, and say explicitly where it surprises you—that is, where it returns a verdict you would reject, or where it gives no verdict at all. If it misclassifies one of the cases, either revise the definition or defend the resulting verdict.
