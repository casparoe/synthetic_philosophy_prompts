I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I've been going down a rabbit hole reading about Han Feizi and the old Legalism-versus-Confucianism debate, because I help run a mid-sized open source community and we can't agree about how to govern it. My messy understanding is this. The Legalist side, Han Feizi roughly, says you shouldn't try to make people good; you set up clear public rules with reliable rewards and punishments and you assume everyone is looking out for their own interest, because if you rely on people being virtuous you just get flattery and gaming. The rule-by-virtue side says that's exactly how you hollow a community out, since people become whatever the system measures, so instead you should have leaders who model good conduct and slowly build a culture where people do the right thing because they'd be ashamed not to.

I've been using a rough four-part test for whether a governance setup is Legalist in that sense. One, are the rules explicit and legible to the people they govern. Two, are rewards and punishments tied to observable outputs rather than someone's private judgment of character. Three, is the system designed so it would still basically work if everyone in it were purely self-interested. Four, do appeals to virtue or shame do any actual governing work, or are they just stories people tell about the system afterwards.

Here's the borderline case. The project I help moderate has a moderation system that looks Legalist on paper. We have a public list of specific infractions, automatic penalties that apply the same to everyone, and a helpfulness-point ledger where reviewing other people's code earns priviledges. But the way we actually justify it is different: we say the rules and points are just the community's shared values written down, we only recruit moderators we think are genuinely decent and will apply the rules with forgiveness rather than mechanically, and we deliberately keep the points coarse so people can't min-max them. Half the team says we're rule-by-incentives with a virtue story on top; the other half says we're rule-by-virtue with a little legible scaffolding so we don't have to trust any single mod's judgment.

So my question is: applying my own four-part test, should this community be classified as Legalist, as rule-by-virtue, or as something that breaks the test? Go through the four criteria seperately and sort out where the answer is ambiguous, and say whether the ambiguity lives in my criteria or in the system itself. And do you think the setup is a stable middle ground, or is it exactly the unstable halfway house the Legalist side warns about, where the virtue-talk is just there to make the incentives look less crass?

When you write back, just do it as plain continuous prose like an email, no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. I want your actual judgment, not a survey or a reading list.
