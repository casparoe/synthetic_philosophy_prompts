His summary of the essay went something like this:

"Moloch is the god of destructive competition. The argument is that most of the genuinely awful things in the world—arms races, climate change, companies doing the polluting equivalent of cutting corners, the way beauty standards and work hours ratchet up—happen because competition forces people to act worse than they actually want to. Anyone who unilaterally opts out just gets outcompeted, so in the long run everyone is dragged toward the bottom. It's basically the tragedy of the commons on a civilizational scale. Since every player is being individually rational at each step, the only real solution is a strong central authority that can force everyone to stop racing; that's more or less where the essay lands."
