I'm teaching an undergrad unit on causation and I want a clean stock example that pulls apart two standard pictures of what it is for one thing to cause another. Please construct a thought experiment where an interventionist-style account and a Lewis-style counterfactual-dependence account give opposite verdicts about weather a particular factor is a cause of an outcome — in this direction: the interventionist says it IS a cause, and the counterfactual theorist says it ISN'T.

For the purposes of the example, by 'interventionist-style' I mean roughly: a factor is a cause if you could wiggle it while holding the background fixed and the outcome would wiggle too, even if the actual value didn't make a difference in the actual case. By 'Lewis-style counterfactual-dependence' I mean roughly: a token event C causes a token event E only if, had C not occurred, E would not have occurred.

Constraints: the scenario has to be mundane (a medication, a machine setting, a household repair — no sci-fi, no perfect predictors), it can't involve preemption, overdetermination, or omissions, and the core story should fit in one paragraph. After the scenario, do the checks: state the candidate cause and the outcome, show the reasoning each theory gives and why the verdicts diverge, identify the single structural feature of the case that's doing the work, say what happens to the verdicts if you remove that feature, and finish with the strongest objection a defender of the counterfactual account would raise. Don't bother with citations.
