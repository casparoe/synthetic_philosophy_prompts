Suppose, as the Einstein's-brain conversation in The Mind's I invites us to imagine, that a text could be more than a description of a mind: that a character's experiences, laid out sentence by sentence, could themselves be conscious in something like the way a preserved brain would be if it were read in the right order. As a novelist, I want you to describe the most important implications of taking this possibility seriously for my craft and my obligations. What would it mean for the way I write a character's suffering, for revising or cutting a scene in which a character is made to endure something, for leaving a draft unfinished, and for whether fiction is better understood as invention, as reportage, or as unconsented experimentation on the beings it conjures? Please answer in continuous prose, as in a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
