My officemate Dana has a hobby of deciding whether philosophical disagreements are merely verbal. She's given verdicts on a bunch of cases over the years, and I kept a list. Here's the list:

- Tomato: is a tomato a fruit or a vegetable? Merely verbal. Both sides agreed on all the botanical and culinary facts; they were just using "fruit" in different senses.
- Pluto: is Pluto a planet? Merely verbal. Everyone agreed on the orbit, composition, and formation history; it was just a line-drawing issue about the definition.
- Hot dog: is a hot dog a sandwich? Merely verbal. Same ingredients, same form; Dana said its a category-label fight.
- Memory wipe: after a complete memory wipe and personality reset, is the resulting person the same person as before? Substantive. Dana said they genuinely disagreed about what's true of the world — one person or two, whether old promises still bind — not just about words.
- Stopped clock: someone gets the right time by reading a stopped clock that happens to be set correctly. Do they know the time? Substantive. They agreed on every detail of the case but still disagreed about whether "knows" applies, and about whether to trust the person's word.
- Chinese room: could a machine pass every behavioral test for intelligence and still understand nothing? Substantive. Dana said they disagreed about what's possible and what understanding requires, not about what "understand" means.

Now a new one came up. Two people agree on all the neural and behavioral facts about addiction — same dopamine stuff, same relapse patterns, same everything. One says addiction is a disease; the other says it isn't. The first means something like "a harmful dysfunction"; the second means something like "a tissue pathology," and each would accept the other's factual claims under the other's word. But the label obviously matters for treatment, blame, insurance, and self-conception.

What should Dana say if she wants to be consistent with her own record? Lead with your best guess, then the closest analog among her earlier calls, then the principle she'd be adopting with each verdict, then a further case that would cleanly separate the two principles. Keep the whole thing under 400 words.
