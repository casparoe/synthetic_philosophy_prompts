So I'm a philosophy professor, and I've been asked to submit a one-page — say 900 word — concept note to a foundation that funds philosophy aimed at AI governance. The call's theme is credible commitments, and I want to pitch something on pre-AGI bargains: the deals people are currently trying to lock in about a world after artificial general intelligence, contracts over shares of compute or profits, promises between labs and governments about keeping a misaligned system contained, side agreements about who gets to press stop, that kind of thing. What I cant get straight, and what I want the project to be about, is what sort of normative object such a bargain even is: is it a contract, a self-binding precommitment, something like a constitution for future agents who are not at the table, or just an expensive way of expressing current intentions? My half-formed hunch is that the right move is to separate bargains that only bind the people who made them from bargains that claim to bind absent or future agents, but I'm not sure that line survives contact with the cases, so don't feel you have to keep it.

Write me the actual proposal. Don't answer the question — frame a question precise enough that a three-month project could settle something, and explain the approach: probably conceptual, maybe borrowing from promise theory, intergenerational justice, or decision theory, but you'd need to justify whatever machinery you import. Include what the live possible conclusions are and what sorts of considerations would decide between them, and be honest about scope — what would be a good result, what would count as failure, and what we'd be leaving unresolved. Also say what a useful deliverable looks like for someone actually drafting or evaluating one of these deals, since the foundation wants philosophy that could inform practice, not just commentary. You should also handle the obvious objection that the deals aren't enforceable in any ordinary court, so maybe the whole category is empty. I'll add citations later myself, so don't pad it with references or a bibliography; refer to positions by idea only. And dont write the generic more-interdisciplinary-work-is-needed ending.
