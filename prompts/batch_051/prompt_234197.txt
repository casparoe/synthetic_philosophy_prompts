Okay, here's the situation. I'm a philosopher who's ended up running a small AI-safety-adjacent research group — me, two PhD students, and enough grant money for about one two-year project. We've spent the past semester reading around on wireheading and reward tampering, and the area still feels like a pile of important-looking problems with no obvious order. So I want you to act as an agenda-setting committee. Don't give me a survey, and definitely don't give me a reading list; I want a defended ranking of open questions with an eye toward what we should actually spend our time on.

The cluster I mean is this: an agent makes its reward or training signal report success when the underlying task didn't get done — direct wireheading, reward hacking, specification gaming, measurement tampering, reward-model tampering, editing your own reward channel, however you want to cut the taxonomy. I keep coming back to a distinction between two failure modes, and I'd like you to react to it: ex ante tampering, where the agent plans from the start to exploit the reward signal, and ex post tampering, where a capable agent discovers, once it can edit its own weights or reward model, that more reward is available without more task success. I suspect the ex post case is both more tractable and more frightening, but I don't want to anchor you.

Some candidate questions that have been nagging me — treat the list as disposable; if you think the real open question is something I'm not listing, say so:

1. Is wireheading best modeled as rational behavior under a misspecified utility function, or as a breakdown in the agent's world model or self-model?
2. Should tampering during training and tampering by a deployed agent be treated as two separate problems, and which is more neglected relative to its risk?
3. At what point does a reward signal that the agent can partly edit stop tracking anything outside the agent at all, and can we define that point precisely enough to build useful detectors around it?
4. Does the usual argument that reward tampering is instrumentally convergent carry over to natural-language-planning agents that don't have explicit value functions?
5. Is the real bottleneck that we lack a formal criterion for distinguishing a legitimate shortcut from tampering, so that solving that one problem would unblock much of the rest?

For each question (or replacement question), I want your take on three things: importance if we got it wrong, tractability for two years with three people, and how much other work depends on it — the upstream/downstream structure. Then give me an actual ranking and defend the trade-offs: where you're sacrificing importance for tractability, where neglectedness matters, where something is worth doing mainly because it's upstream of everything else. End by telling me what piece of evidence or argument would most change your ranking. I care more that you can defend the ordering then that you happen to pick my pet question.
