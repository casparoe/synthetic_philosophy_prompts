I've started using the outside view as a blanket rule for personal decisions, and lately Ive started to worry the rule has eaten me. I'm about to quit my job to start a small cabinet-making shop, and every time I catch myself thinking my case is different because of X, I force myself to go with the base rate instead. Now the policy is: whenever my inside story conflicts with a base rate I happen to know, I follow the base rate, full stop, even when I can point to concrete features of my situation that the base rate doesn't separate out. Is that actually defensible, or is it epistemic modesty turned into a refusal to think? Part of what bothers me is the reference class problem: am I "a first-time small-business owner" or "a cabinetmaker with eight years of experience and some regular clients already lined up"? Should I keep the rule, fix it, or drop it before I hand in my notice?
