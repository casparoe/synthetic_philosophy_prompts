I'm preparing a section of a paper on Aristotle's treatment of money and exchange, and I want a consistency check before I present it. My anchor text is from the Ross translation of *Nicomachean Ethics* V.1: "The just, then, is the lawful and the fair, the unjust the unlawful and the unfair." Against that background, I hold the following about the discussion of money in V.5:

(1) Money exists by convention (nomos), not by nature (physis).
(2) Its function is to represent chreia, the reciprocal need that holds a political community together.
(3) A fair exchange is therefore one that equalizes the parties with respect to mutual need, not merely one that conforms to whatever terms the law uses to denominate prices.
(4) Nevertheless, because money is legally instituted, the lawful side of exchange cannot be reduced to the fair side: the community must authorize the measure before it can do its work.
(5) Aristotle's condemnation of interest-bearing loans follows from (1)–(3), since interest treats a representative of need as if it could generate an increase from itself.
(6) Since money is conventional through and through, changing or devaluing a community's currency is a purely technical administrative act, not a substantive injustice.
(7) Despite that, Aristotle can treat money as a store of value over time, because holding money stores the community's continued recognition of a claim, not an intrinsic productive power.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension? Which merely appear to be in tension and dissolve on a better reading? Please rank the tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix would be for each, and identify which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent.
