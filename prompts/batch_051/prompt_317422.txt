I am a PhD student in moral psychology, and for the past year I have been running an adversarial collaboration between two researchers in my group who disagree about whether purity and sanctity belong in ethics. One side is pluralist about moral content: it holds that purity/sanctity tracks a distinct class of violations — contamination, degradation, desecration, and the sacred — and that disgust and sacralization can reveal or constitute genuine moral reasons not reducible to harm, fairness, or autonomy. The other side is critical: it holds that purity-talk is credible only as a heuristic for downstream harms, and that where purity judgments outrun harm or autonomy they reflect moralized disgust rather than independent moral reasons. The two sides have accepted one diagnostic case: a municipality proposes to compost unclaimed cremated remains that it legally holds and to plant a public garden in the resulting soil, over the objection of residents who call the use desecration, though no surviving kin can be identified. Draft the joint statement this collaboration should produce. It should begin with a numbered list of agreed, non-vacuous claims; follow with a disagreements table stating each residual disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own view; then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — and, for each crux, specify a concrete case, measurement, or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and, finally, state which parts of their dispute were, in your view, purely verbal, if any, and why.
