I recently signed an employment contract whose dispute-resolution section requires binding arbitration before a private provider, with the arbitrator’s award stated to be final and reviewable only on very narrow grounds. Around the same time, I was reading Locke’s *Second Treatise of Government*, where he explains why people form civil society, and I kept thinking about that contract. In Chapter IX, “Of the Ends of Political Society and Government,” he writes:

First, There wants an established, settled, known law, received and allowed by common consent to be the standard of right and wrong, and the common measure to decide all controversies between them: for though the law of nature be plain and intelligible to all rational creatures; yet men being biassed by their interest, as well as ignorant for want of study of it, are not apt to allow of it as a law binding to them in the application of it to their particular cases.

Secondly, In the state of nature there wants a known and indifferent judge, with authority to determine all differences according to the established law: for every one in that state being both judge and executioner of the law of nature, men being partial to themselves, passion and revenge is very apt to carry them too far, and with too much heat, in their own cases; as well as negligence, and unconcernedness, to make them too remiss in other men’s.

Thirdly, In the state of nature there often wants power to back and support the sentence when right, and to give it due execution, They who by any injustice offended, will seldom fail, where they are able, by force to make good their injustice; such resistance many times makes the punishment dangerous, and frequently destructive, to those who attempt it.

I want to use this passage as a framework for evaluating privatized dispute resolution as I am now encountering it. Please give me a tier list of the following mechanisms for resolving ordinary disputes between private parties, ranked by how completely each supplies the three things Locke says are wanting in the state of nature: a known settled law, an indifferent judge with authority, and power to back and execute the sentence.

The mechanisms are: (a) conventional adversarial litigation in a state court with appellate review; (b) standard commercial arbitration under a major provider’s rules, with discovery, motion practice, and a reasoned award; (c) expedited employment arbitration with minimal discovery, no written opinion, and no appeal; (d) private mediation; (e) a platform’s internal adjudication system for disputes between users, such as a marketplace or social media site; and (f) private judging in a jurisdiction where parties may hire a retired judge under a statute.

Please assign each mechanism a tier from A to F, and in a sentence or two for each say which of Locke’s three wants it supplies well and which it supplies poorly. Then tell me whether the exercise reveals a fourth want that Locke’s list omits when evaluating privatized justice in particular, and, if you added that criterion, whether any tier would change.
