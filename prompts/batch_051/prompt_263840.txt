I would like a working definition of corruption. I keep reaching for the standard gloss that corruption is the abuse of an entrusted position or role for private gain, but I suspect it will not survive all the cases below, so do not adopt it unless it does. The context is an essay on whether corruption is a parochially Western category that misdescribes clientelism and gift exchange in other societies, and I want a definition that does not settle that question in advance. Test your definition against these cases: a border official who demands an unofficial payment before stamping a passport and keeps the money; a voter who gives a friend on the city council a bottle of wine, with no council matter pending; a legislator who secures a new clinic for the district that re-elected her and advertises the achievement in her campaign; a customs agent who accepts a modest New Year's gift from a long-standing importer whose files are under review, on the grounds that refusing it would be rude; a village chief who, in a community that expects chiefs to look after kin, gives scarce food aid to his struggling relatives first; and a major campaign donor later appointed to a government advisory board. Propose the definition, run it through all six cases, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and revise it or defend the surprising verdicts. I am especially interested in whether the definition ends up treating the legislator, the customs agent, and the chief as corrupt. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the definition and its verdicts, and single out the point you are least sure of. Please write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
