I'm taking a module on the limits of computation and a freind gave me an argument I can't shake. He says its just Gödel applied to AI, but the conclusion seems far too strong, so I suspect something slipped somewhere, yet I can't locate the move. The argument goes like this:

(1) Gödel's first incompleteness theorem says that for any consistent formal system F rich enough for arithmetic, there is a sentence G_F that is true but not provable in F. (2) G_F says, in effect, of itself that it is not provable in F; so whenever a mathematician understands F and can see that F is consistent, she can see that G_F is true. (3) Hence, for any consistent formal system, a human can know an arithmetic truth that the system cannot prove. (4) Now suppose an AI does mathematics. By the Church–Turing thesis, the set of sentences it could in principle output is captured by some formal system F_AI. (5) If that AI is consistent, then humans can know its Gödel sentence G_F_AI, but the AI cannot prove it. (6) So no consistent AI can output everything a human mathematician can know. (7) If the human mind were itself a computer program, there would be such a system F_mind capturing everything a human mathematician can know. (8) But then, substituting the mind for the AI in (4)–(6), there would be an arithmetic truth — G_F_mind — that the human can know but the mind-as-program cannot output, contradicting (7). (9) Therefore the human mind is not a computer program, and computationalism about the mind is false.

He says any reply either denies that we can see a system is consistent, which he thinks is obviously false because mathematicians prove consistency claims in practice, or gives up the Church–Turing thesis, which nobody wants to do. I most want to keep the thought that mathematical truth is objective and that we genuinely know some of it; I would give up the rest if I had to.

Could you roleplay as a professional philosopher of logic, think carefully, and start by brainstorming two or three candidate diagnoses before settling on the one you think is actually correct? Then tell me which step is false (if any), why it looked convincing, and what somebody gives up by rejecting it. Please write your answer in British English.
