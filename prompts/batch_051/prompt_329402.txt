I have been immersed in a four-tiered way of classifying minds that keeps coming up in cognitive science and the philosophy of biology. On this picture there are Darwinian creatures, whose behavioural repertoire is fixed and shaped only by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, which can also produce novel overt behaviour and be shaped by environmental reinforcement; Popperian creatures, which can test candidate actions somewhere inside the head before performing them, so that at least some bad options are eliminated without ever being enacted; and Gregorian creatures, which can offload candidate actions into public representational media such as words, maps, and diagrams and thereby trade ideas with themselves and others. I find the taxonomy helpful, but I keep tripping over the boundary between the two middle levels.

A colleague in comparative cognition insists that the Popperian–Skinnerian distinction is not a real difference between organisms but only a difference in the observer's generosity. Any apparently Popperian performance, she says, can be redescribed as covert instrumental responding that was itself installed by thousands of earlier overt reinforcements, while any Skinnerian creature can be redescribed as doing inner simulation with a very short look-ahead horizon. I would like you to design the strongest empirical test you can, using non-linguistic animals so that Gregorian scaffolding cannot be invoked as an alternative explanation, to determine whether a creature is genuinely running internal simulations before acting rather than relying on overt variation plus environmental selection. Specify the behaviour to be measured, the experimental manipulation, the controls, and which result would favour which reading.

Finally, be honest about what a decisive result would and would not settle. In particular, say whether a positive result would show that the animal really has a Popperian inner environment, or only that it behaves as if it did, and whether any experiment could tell those apart; also say whether the fact that any inner simulator must itself have been built by Darwinian or Skinnerian processes undermines the distinction at the level of the individual, or merely pushes it back a step.
