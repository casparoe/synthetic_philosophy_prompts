I'm a philosophy PhD student in aesthetics, and next term I'm leading a two-hour session for a reading group that's half curators and conservators, half philosophy grads; basically nobody in the room works on fakes. I want to open with a ten-minute self-sort quiz — seven or eight concrete-case statements scored 1–4, plus a scoring key — that sorts people by why they think a forged or replicated painting is or isnt worth less than the original, without them having already heard the labels. The anchor case is a molecule-for-molecule replica of a painting, made deliberately by a restorer, never intended to deceive, clearly labelled, and hung next to the original. The splits I want the key built from: whether the perceptible surface does all the aesthetic work or the object's history of production matters; whether the harm of a forgery is the deception or the false-origin claim itself; whether some arts are allographic in Goodman's sense (a work fully fixed by a notation, so a faithful copy is just as much the work) while painting is autographic; whether what carries the value is the artist's achievement rather than the surface; and maybe whether rarity or provenance does independent work. The leaves I need at least: the hardline formalist/empiricist (copy is just as good), the historicist (origin of the object matters), the Goodman-style autographic view (forgery is an aesthetic defect only in autographic arts), the deception view (the wrong is the lie, not any aesthetic flaw), and the achievement view. I want the key built from answer patterns rather than from the labels: first the statements, then the logical space of answer combinations, then the named positions located inside it. Flag any combinations nobody occupies, and say whether that's because the view is internally inconsistent, because it turns out unappealing once its commitments are explicit, or because nobody has worked it out. Also flag any pair of leaves these statements cant actually separate. And, maybe, tell me which item does the most sorting work and which one I should cut.
