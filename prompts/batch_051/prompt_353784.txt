I'm drafting my second novel and it's ground me to a halt. I write from a very detailed outline — every scene planned, each character's background and temperament mapped out. Once the outline's fixed I basically just run the story forward from it. But what actually happens every time is that some tiny thing I changed early on, a line of dialgoue on page 30 or a train being delayed, means that by page 300 the ending I planned no longer fits and I have to redraft the whole last third. I see the logic, but it feels false to how writing actually goes. The other odd thing is that the endings I keep getting pushed towards aren't random: they're always one of three or four recurring shapes, even when the details are wildly different. So my question is this — is my friend right that a detailed outline plus fixed characters makes the ending predictable in principle? And if it isn't, what kind of 'unpredictable' is it?
