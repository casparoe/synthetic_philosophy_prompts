Question: Explain Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument. What exactly is it meant to show about patterned principles of distributive justice, which principles does it actually target, and does it succeed?

Student answer: The argument starts from a distribution D1 that is just according to a patterned principle, such as equality of resources. Wilt Chamberlain signs a contract under which a small surcharge on each ticket sold goes directly to him, and many fans freely buy tickets. Since each exchange is voluntary and violates no one's entitlements, the principle of justice in transfer says the resulting distribution D2 is also just. But D2 is highly unequal, so the original pattern has been destroyed. To maintain the pattern over time, the state would have to continuously forbid these exchanges or confiscate the resulting extra holdings. This shows that liberty is incompatible with any patterned principle of distributive justice. Since every egalitarian principle is a patterned principle, it follows that liberty and equality are incompatible. The argument also vindicates Nozick's claim that redistributive taxation is on a par with forced labor, because both take the fruits of voluntary exchanges. Only the entitlement theory, which assesses a distribution by how it arose through justice in acquisition and justice in transfer, can respect freedom of contract. The argument therefore succeeds.
