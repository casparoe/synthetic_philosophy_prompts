i write about consciousness research for a living, so i get shown motion illusions and afterimages all the time, but one demo has been chewing on me for years, and the argument i've built out of it feels like it proves way too much. the demo is this: a red dot flashes briefly on the left, then after a short gap a green dot flashes briefly on the right. nobody sees two separate flashes. people see one dot drifting smoothly across the gap, and the wierd part is that it turns from red to green in the middle of its path, before the green dot actually occurs in the world.

here's the argument:

1. call the moment the dot seems to turn from red to green "t". at t, the visual system hasn't received any green signal yet; the green flash is physically later.
2. but the experience at t includes green. a subject would insist they saw green at that point, not that they worked it out later.
3. an experience at t can't have its content supplied by information that doesn't arrive in the brain until after t. that would be backwards causation, and the brain doesn't do that.
4. so one of two things must be true. either the whole experience is delayed until the green signal lands, and then staged in the right order — so you're living in a slightly delayed broadcast and never notice (stalinesque) — or you really did have a red experience at t and it got silently rewritten into a green one afterward, with the edit invisible from the inside (orwellian).
5. both options say introspection is systematically wrong about the timing and content of your own experience. on one story, your "now" is always out of date; on the other, you can't tell a live experience from an after-the-fact memory edit.
6. but that can't be right. the one thing a person is supposed to be incorrigable about is what their own experience was like at the time they had it.
7. so at least one of 1, 2, or 3 has to go. but each one looks solid on its own.

which premise do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? if i keep 6, what am i giving up somewhere else, and if i ditch 6, what do i have to say about the authority of introspection? i don't need you to pick between orwell and stalin; i need to know whether the argument even earns the right to force that choice.
