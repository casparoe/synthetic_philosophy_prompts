A friend and I have been going back and forth for weeks about whether Sweden or Norway is more liberal, and it's become obvious we're not even using "liberal" to mean the same thing. He means something like drug policy and sex-work laws, I keep drifting toward economic regulation and how much the state nudges people around, and a third person in the group chat keeps saying we're both ignoring migration and how the countries treat religious minorities. So I've been trying to build a definition that doesn't just smuggle in whichever country I already like.

Here's the thing I came up with, and I want an honest assessment of it. I've been calling it "annoyance liberalism," or sometimes "liberalism as tolerated aggravation." The claim is that a country is liberal to the extent that it keeps protecting activities, speech, and ways of life that its own median voter finds genuinely annoying, distasteful, or mildly offensive, without turning each of them into a permanent culture-war emergency. Any country will protect things most people are fine with; the real test of liberalism is what happens when a majority would rather not have to put up with something but the thing stays protected anyway. I genuinely cant tell whether Sweden or Norway comes out ahead on this. On some axes Norway feels more traditional and Sweden more eager to engineer behavior, on other axes it seems like the reverse, and I don't trust my memory of the specifics — which is part of why I want the concept checked before I go cherry-picking examples.

So: is this a coherent notion of liberalism at all, or is it just a vibe with a definition bolted on? Does it already exist under a better name somewhere? What's the strongest objection to it, and where does it break — for instance, does it confuse being liberal with being permissive, or with being indifferent to harm? And if you're willing to run with it for a minute, does it actually settle the Sweden-vs-Norway question, or does it just relocate the argument to what counts as "genuinely annoying" to the median voter?
