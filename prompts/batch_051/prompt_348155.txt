A physics-enthusiast friend of mine takes Maxwell's demon to demonstrate that information is a kind of fuel. His favored way of putting the thought experiment is the club-doorman analogy: the demon is a bouncer at a trapdoor who inspects each molecule as it arrives, routes the fast ones one way and the slow ones the other, and thereby builds up a temperature difference that can drive an engine. Since the bouncer, so the argument runs, spends only information and never energy, the second law looks violable by information alone, and information therefore has to be physical in the strongest sense. Audit how far this doorman picture actually holds. Give the correspondence table (the door, the inspection, the fast and slow molecules, the accumulating gradient, the engine that runs off it), identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the conclusion from the ones that do not. In particular, address what the analogy omits about the bouncer's memory between inspections and about what it costs thermodynamically to erase a record, and say whether the claim that information is a fuel survives once those costs are in view. If the doorman is the wrong picture, what is the right one?
