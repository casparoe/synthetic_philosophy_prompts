I work with someone on a small commission that sets policy for a private METI fund, and I'm trying to reverse-engineer the rule they're implicitly running. They talk a lot of dark-forest logic but their not pure never-transmit types. Their case rulings so far:

- Approved full-sky passive listening in every band.
- Rejected an omnidirectional beacon that would have broadcast a bitmap of a stick figure, a double helix, and the solar system, pointed nowhere in particular.
- Approved using a powerful planetary radar to map an asteroid, even though the pulse train would be detectable from around a hundred light-years away.
- Rejected a reply to a candidate narrowband signal at the hydrogen line that never got confirmed and probably was local interference.
- Approved a one-shot, very low-power, content-free acknowledgement pulse to a repeating signal from forty light-years away, after it was independently confirmed as artificial and looked aimed at us.
- Rejected putting an engraved plaque with the same kind of information as the beacon on an interstellar probe.

Now the proposal in front of them is a high-power, information-dense reply—basically a primer on Earth biology and technology—to a recently confirmed artificial signal from Alpha Centauri that appears to be aimed at us. The argument is that whoever's there already knows we exist, so staying quiet doesn't buy much anymore, and a richer message maximizes the chance of a useful exchange. Predict their vote, say which earlier ruling is the closest analog, and if the track record actually underdetermines the new case, spell out what principle each possible vote would commit them to. Also tell me one further transmission proposal you'd put on the agenda to split whatever candidate principles you identify.
