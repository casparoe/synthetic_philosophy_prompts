I keep running into a move in arguments about word usage, and I want to be able to answer it precisely. In a recent discussion about whether English is being corrupted, someone dismissed the use of decimate to mean "devastate" and of literally as an intensifier by repeating the slogan that the original meaning of a word is its real meaning. His conclusion was that decimate, from the Latin for "tenth," can properly mean only "reduce by a tenth," and that since literal originally meant "by the letter," literally cannot properly mean "figuratively." I could tell the slogan was doing all the work, but I couldn't say exactly what it was claiming. Lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making: about what a word's meaning consists in, about which of a word's several meanings is the correct one, about what makes a use a misuse, and about whether and how word meanings may change. For each reading, say whether it is trivially true, defensible but contentious, or simply false, and tell me which reading, if any, would actually justify his verdicts about decimate and literally. I want the readings as a menu I can take back to the argument.
