For the past year I’ve been trying to dissolve the bootstrapping problem, and I’ve arrived at a distinction I call tracer use versus background use of a cognitive source. I’d like an honest assessment of whether it holds up.

In background use, a source’s deliverances are accepted in order to answer some object-level question: I look at the wall and form the belief that it is red. In tracer use, the deliverances are treated as data about the source’s own reliability: I notice that vision represented the wall as red and that the wall is red, and I treat the match as evidence that vision works. My central claim is that tracer use is parasitic on background use in a way that makes global reliability conclusions viciously circular. To assemble a record of source said p and p as evidence for the source’s reliability, I must already rely on the source in background mode to identify that p; so the would-be metasource is really just the source wearing a different hat. The bootstrapping problem, on my view, is the mistake of treating a pile of background successes as if it had been generated by an independent check on the source.

I want to block only global conclusions, not ordinary calibration, so I add a calibration asymmetry clause: conversion from background to tracer use is permissible when the source’s failures would be locally detectable in background mode—for example, when a false deliverance would tend to conflict with other deliverances or produce a downstream failure I could recognize using the same source in its ordinary object-level way. This is meant to let me calibrate a thermometer against yesterday’s readings without letting the red-wall reasoner conclude that vision is generally reliable. My worry is that the line between global and local is doing all the work, and that the tracer/background distinction itself is little more than a relabeling of the object-level/meta-level split. Maybe every tracer use is just background use with a reflexive question in view, and maybe some ordinary background uses—like taking vision to settle that the wall is red—already smuggle in the very reliability claim the bootstrapper wanted to earn.

So: is this distinction coherent as stated? Is it a rediscovery of something already in the literature under one of the usual headings—rule-circularity, dogmatism, entitlement—and if so, what does my version get right and what does it obscure? Be charitable but candid: steelman it where it is weak, then give me the strongest counterexample you can—either a background use that seems to bootstrap reliability illegitimately, or a tracer use that seems to justify reliability without any independent check. Finally, tell me what would change your mind about your overall verdict, and where in your own reasoning you think the failure most likely sits.
