Nietzsche has this wonderfully unfair section in Ecce Homo, Why I Am So Clever, where he says the practical question of nourishment matters more for human salvation than anything theology worries about, and he frames it as how to eat to reach your maximum strength, virtù in the Renaissance sense, virtue without moralizing. Most of what follows is his specific rules: no alcohol, light meals, no heavy German cooking. But I dont care about the rules; I want the concept underneath them. Give me a working definition of health, in the sense Nietzsche seems to be using when he talks about diet and vitality, that correctly classifies these cases: a professional athlete with excellent bloodwork who lives on processed food and energy drinks; a chronically ill person who follows Nietzsche's diet strictly but is always exhausted; a person who eats a huge traditional German meal and feels great afterward; a person who fasts regularly and says it makes their mind sharper; a person with normal metabolic markers who is miserable and listless; and a person who eats by modern dietary standards perfectly but spends all day obsessing over food. Run your definition through all six, say which verdicts are least secure, and separate what's settled about Nietzsche's actual views from what's contested among scholars—don't make a contested reading sound like the consensus.
