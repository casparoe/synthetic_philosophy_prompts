I'm preparing a senior seminar on cliodynamics, and before my students evaluate any particular theory I want the full specification of what a theory in this area actually has to get right. The seminar asks whether macrohistory can be made quantitative and weakly predictive, and our central test case is the structural-demographic theory of 'secular cycles' — the Turchin-school claim that agrarian states oscillate over a few centuries through population growth, popular immiseration, elite overproduction, fiscal strain on the state, intra-elite competition, and a crisis phase of rebellion, civil war, or collapse that resets the demographic and social conditions. The theory is meant to be adequate for two jobs: explaining recurrent instability in the cases it claims to cover, and supporting weak probabilistic forecasts a few decades out, not point predictions and not mere after-the-fact retrodiction.

Write me the desiderata an adequate theory of macrohistorical cycles must meet: the phenomena it has to explain, the historical cases it has to get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the constraints from historical demography, economics, and the quality of the surviving record that it cannot contradict. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a camp preference, and mark it as non-negotiable or negotiable. A table for the requirements is fine.

Then do two further things. First, tell me which single requirement does the most work excluding the main families of contenders — traditional narrative historiography of the kind that treats each crisis as a singular product of contingency, monocausal economic or environmental explanations, and the structural-demographic package itself. Second, and this is the part I most want: say whether some plausible subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. I suspect there are two tensions — one between demanding predictive discipline and respecting the small-sample, nonstationary, proxy-heavy character of the historical record, and another between treating cycles as endogenously generated while still having to absorb large exogenous shocks such as pandemics, climate shifts, and conquest. If those tensions are real, state them precisely and say what has to be weakened to dissolve them, and at what cost; if they are not, say exactly why. Keep the scorecard brief: I want the criteria worked out, not a ranking.
