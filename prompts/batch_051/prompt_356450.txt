I have been trying to sharpen an argument that the difference between verifying a proof and understanding it is smaller than people usually think, and I want a line-by-line assessment.

(1) Suppose a proof is written out so that every step is explicit. Then each step is either an assumption, an axiom, a definitional unfolding, or a transition from earlier steps by a stated rule of inference.

(2) For a proof in this form, the proof's being correct consists in exactly this: every step falls into one of those categories, and no step is marked as following from earlier steps unless the cited rule really licenses the transition.

(3) Understanding such a proof is a matter of grasping why each step is licensed — seeing how the step follows from the earlier steps, the definitions, or the rules in force.

(4) A mechanical proof checker does nothing but determine, for each step, which of those categories it falls into and whether the cited rule licenses the transition.

(5) Therefore, if (3) is right, a mechanical proof checker grasps why each step is licensed, and thus satisfies the stated condition for understanding the proof.

(6) That conclusion seems wrong: we want to say the checker verifies the proof without understanding it.

(7) So at least one premise must go, but I am not sure which.

A concrete case that makes the argument feel compelling to me is the usual proof that there are infinitely many primes: assume p1,...,pn are all the primes; form N = p1...pn + 1; since N is greater than 1, it has a prime divisor q; and q cannot be any of the pi, because then q would divide both N and the product p1...pn and hence divide 1; contradiction. Every move there is locally checkable, and a checker that registers all those moves certifies the proof. Yet it seems odd to say the checker has thereby seen why the proof works. I want to know exactly which step of the general argument breaks, and whether it can be repaired by replacing (3) with an account of understanding that includes something global over and above mastery of the local steps.

Please assess the argument. Is it valid as written? For each numbered premise, say whether it is true as it stands. If the problem is (3), give the most defensible reading of (3) and say whether, on that reading, (5) still follows or whether the conclusion must be weakened. If the problem is elsewhere, identify the exact step and state the corrected version that would make the argument valid, and explain what that corrected version commits us to about the relation between checking and understanding. I am interested in the conceptual diagnosis, not in references.
