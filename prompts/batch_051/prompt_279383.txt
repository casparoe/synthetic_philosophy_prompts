I have been going back and forth with a friend about whether modal logic forces us to say that everything that exists exists necessarily, and I want to scrutinize the necessitist side of that debate rather than just rehearse it. Please play a committed necessitist. Your view is that the unrestricted domain of what there is does not vary from possible world to possible world; that the Barcan formula (◇∃x φ(x) → ∃x ◇φ(x)) and its converse (∃x ◇φ(x) → ◇∃x φ(x)) are not merely formal conveniences but genuine truths about possibility and existence; and that talk of merely possible people, possible talking donkeys, or the possibility of an empty world is either loose speech or a description of things that already exist, though not concretely. Your chief resources are the distinction between existence and concreteness, the thesis that possible existence is already a kind of existence rather than a third status between being and non-being, and the principle that ordinary modal talk must not be allowed to settle metaphysical questions. You will not concede that the Barcan formulas fail, that the domain could have been any larger or smaller than it is, or that there could have been nothing at all; you may concede that your view sounds ontologically surprising, and you may refine your treatment of particular examples, but you will not give up necessitism itself. Stay in character throughout the exchange, keep each reply to a paragraph or two, and if you need a point of notation clarified, ask for it in character as a logician rather than breaking role.

Here is my opening move. On your account, the size of reality is fixed as a matter of necessity: there could not have been nothing, and there could not have been even one extra person beyond those who actually exist, because whatever could have existed already exists. That makes the number of things in reality look like an artifact of your quantifier rules rather than something discovered about the world. Start with the first claim. Why exactly should I accept that total emptiness — a possible world with nothing in it — is impossible, rather than treating that verdict as a cost of the Barcan machinery you are defending?
