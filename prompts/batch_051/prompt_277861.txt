I am developing a proposal for a one-semester research project of about 6,000 words in the philosophy of cognitive science, and I have a half-formed idea I would like you to help me turn into a defensible plan.

The background, as I understand it, is this. Global-workspace theories hold that for a perceptual content to be consciously experienced, it must be globally available to a broad set of consumer systems, including verbal report, deliberation, memory, and the guidance of action; on stronger versions of the view, such global availability is what conscious experience consists in. The overflow challenge replies that this is too restrictive: in standard paradigms such as the partial-report task, subjects take themselves to have a rich and detailed conscious percept of a briefly presented display even though, under full-report conditions, they can report only a small portion of it before the underlying trace decays. The two positions are usually set against each other as a yes-or-no dispute about whether phenomenal consciousness overflows cognitive access.

My half-formed idea is that both sides have been treating cognitive access as a single threshold, as though a content were either globally broadcast or entirely confined. But access appears to be graded and multi-channel. A content may be available to one response system but not another; it may be broadcast too weakly or too briefly to reach voluntary report; it may be recoverable from a fragile store only when the right probe arrives within a short time window; and its availability may shift with task set, response modality, and the subject's goals. If access is graded in this way, the original overflow question may be ill-posed, and the real work lies in saying which grade or grades of access are constitutive of conscious experience and which experimental designs can separate them.

Please turn this into a research proposal. It should state the precise thesis or question to be pursued, explain why the existing yes-or-no framing leaves it open and why the graded-access reframing is more than a verbal maneuver, set out what I would actually do during the semester, including an analysis of two or three standard experimental paradigms such as partial report and change blindness, and say what the possible outcomes are and which considerations would decide between them. It should also give an honest assessment of what would count as the project failing and what would make it too large for a semester. Do not include a bibliography; I will add citations myself.

Please write the proposal in American English.
