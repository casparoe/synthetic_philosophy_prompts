I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about quotas rather than just gesturing at them, and my gut verdicts don't obviously add up. Norway forcing big companies to have 40% women on their boards: for it. India's reservations for scheduled castes in universities and government jobs, including hard numerical quotas: for it, no discomfort. US-style race-conscious admissions where race is one soft factor among several but never a fixed quota: for it, though I end up defending it as a correction for bias more than as diversity, and that distinction feels load-bearing in a way I can't articulate. Gender parity on party candidate lists: for it. A quota for first-generation college students at selective universities: for it, but I get queasier the more explicitly it's run as a quota. A quota for people with disabilities at large firms: for it, no discomfort. A quota telling a think tank that half its senior hires must be political conservatives: against—viscerally against. A quota for men in nursing: against. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? If not, which of my verdicts should I overturn? And what does the principle say about a quota for religious minorities in the senior civil service of a deeply divided country—that's the case I keep flipping on.
