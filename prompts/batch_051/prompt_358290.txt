A friend and I recently had a long argument about mathematical knowledge, and he kept coming back to Benacerraf's dilemma as if it settled the matter. Afterward I wrote down his summary of it as best I could:

"Benacerraf's dilemma is really just the problem that Platonism makes mathematical knowledge impossible. Numbers, on the Platonist view, are abstract objects that exist outside space and time, so they have no causal powers and can't affect us. But knowledge requires a causal connection between the knower and the thing known — you can't know an object that can't impinge on you in any way. So the Platonist has two options: either claim we have some mysterious non-natural faculty for grasping numbers, which no naturalist can accept, or accept that mathematical knowledge is impossible. That is why Benacerraf rejected Platonism, and why most philosophers after him abandoned it."
