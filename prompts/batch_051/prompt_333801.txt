I've got a talk next month at an econ department that's mostly empirical micro people — field experiments, development, a couple of structural folks — plus one decision theorist and a philosopher of science who already thinks 'utility' is a smuggling operation. These are people whose papers live and die by clean identification, but whose policy sections still say 'welfare improved' without blinking. My claim is that when choice experiments keep finding people who pick the thing they'd themselves rate as worse, the standard fix is to keep the utility function alive by layering on noise, present bias, or time-inconsistency. I think that's backwards: a utility function is a summary of a choice pattern, not a property of the chooser, so 'choosing against your own utility' is a category mistake, like asking which frame a shadow belongs to. Welfare conclusions built on top of it inherit the arbitrariness of the bookkeeping, and I say we should own that instead of pretending the bookkeeping measures something inside the person. This has gone over fine with philosophers, but this crowd will not be friendly.
