I run a monthly philosophy discussion group at a bar, about eight of us, mostly non-academics but everyone's read enough to argue for hours. For the last few months we've been stuck on one question: does philosophy make progress. The weird thing is that all of us agree on the obvious data points. Everyone grants that logic and probability theory have accumulated real results since Frege; everyone grants that Gettier killed the JTB analysis and no one seriously defends it anymore; everyone grants that the compatibilism/incompatibilism fight has been more or less frozen for decades. The disagreement is about what those examples show. One faction says the list itself settles it: some subfields converge, some don't, so "does philosophy make progress" is just too coarse a question to have a single answer. Another faction says the list proves the opposite: the non-converging subfields are where the genuinely philosophical problems are, and if ethics and metaphysics haven't converged after a couple thousand years, then philosophy as a whole doesn't progress in the sense that matters. A third faction thinks the whole fight is about the word "progress," since we'd all agree philosophy progresses if progress just means better tools, more distinctions, richer theories than before, and we'd all deny it if progress means a moderately smart beginner can look up the answer. Its been months and no one has moved. My own hunch is that the third faction is closest to right, but I don't want my hunch massaged.

I'm asking for a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict on whether philosophy makes progress.
