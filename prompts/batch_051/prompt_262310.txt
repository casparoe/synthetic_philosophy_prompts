As a novelist, I am repeatedly drawn to characters whose worst vice and greatest usefulness are the same trait viewed at different scales. I would like a piece of sustained satire that makes that double vision feel morally uncomfortable rather than settled by fiat. Please write a mock request for proposals, around 900 words, in the deadpan bureaucratic voice of a government agency, an imaginary Office of Civic Acquisitiveness, soliciting pilot programs to cultivate measured greed as a public economic asset. I want it to read as a found document I could plausibly slip into a novel.

Format it as a genuine RFP, with background, program objectives, eligibility, evaluation criteria, and a budget note. The humor should come from the moral-economic argument itself, not from jeering at greed. Exaggerate real features of the view that private acquisitiveness, channeled through markets, issues in public benefit: the double-entry habit of condemning the motive in a person while crediting it in an economy; the quiet presumption that the institutions which turn self-interest into general welfare can be left to hum along while the motive alone is farmed; the suspicion that greed deliberately cultivated and subsidized is no longer the spontaneous self-love supposed to do the work, so the program might hollow out its own raw material; and the question of who absorbs the character damage while others bank the social dividend. Do not announce these objections; let them rise through the document's own rigor. A moral philosopher or economic historian should recognize that the satire is fair, and a general reader should still feel the grotesque conclusion awaiting just beyond the last clause.
