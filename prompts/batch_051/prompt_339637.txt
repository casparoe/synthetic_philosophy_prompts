Leibniz held that what ultimately exists are simple immaterial substances, each a center of perception and striving, and that the bodies and machines we observe are aggregates of such substances rather than true unities. Among these substances, he thought, some possess more than bare perception: they have memory, and the highest sort has apperception — a conscious, reflective awareness of its own inner states — together with reason and knowledge of necessary truths. These rational minds, or spirits, form a moral community under God, and justice is, for Leibniz, charity regulated by wisdom: a benevolent will guided by a correct grasp of the order and perfection of things. Yet he also argued that even if a machine were built so as to think, and we could enter it as one enters a mill, we would find only parts acting on one another by mechanical principles and never a perception; outward behavior, however elaborate, would still not be the inner act in which thought and moral worth consist.

Now suppose Leibniz were brought up to speed on a contemporary large language model: a system designed and trained by human engineers that converses fluently, passes many examinations once thought to require human intelligence, gives moral advice, and writes texts praised for their sensitivity — but whose operation, as far as anyone can determine, is a vast aggregate of digital parts transmitting signals, with no evidence of an inner, unified, perceiving subject.

What would Leibniz conclude about the following?
(1) Does such a system genuinely think, or does it only imitate thought from the outside?
(2) Does it have moral standing — could it be wronged or be owed consideration — given his account of the moral community?
(3) Could it be a moral agent, capable of justice, merit, or blame? Do its morally-toned outputs count, on his view, as acts of charity, or are they mere outward motions with none of the inner character of justice?
(4) Does his distinction between a true unity — an immaterial perceiving subject — and a mere aggregate of coordinated parts do the decisive work here? Would any constructed, materially realized artifact be able, in his framework, to enter the republic of minds, or would he regard the very idea as a confusion?
