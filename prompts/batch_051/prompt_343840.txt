I've ended up with a view about who I owe things to, and I want to know how much trouble it's actually in. The view, roughly: I have reasons to help the people I share a political system with that I don't have toward someone on the other side of the world who's in just as much need. Not because of shared culture or language, and not because I know them, but because we're caught up in the same legal, tax, and border arrangements, and those arrangements partly run in our name — or at least there's some sense in which I have a say in them, even if it's a pathetically small one. That seems to me to make the same-country case different in kind from "I just prefer people who are similar to me." My mate thinks it's tribalism with extra steps. He says if the border had been drawn somewhere else my obligations would just track the new line, and he takes that as a giveaway that the whole thing is a trick of the map rather than a real moral difference.

What I want is a map of what could count against the view. Not just the obvious objections, but the cases, facts, consequences, or ways I'd have to rethink other things that the view actually stands or falls with. Which of these would a defender of the view (me, if I'm being honest) have to admit genuinely counts against it, and which would they brush off as missing the point? Rank them from "this kills it" to "this is just awkward."

One thing I've noticed is there are really two claims hidden in the view: (a) I'm allowed to put the people I share a country with first, and (b) I'm required to. I think I hold both, but the things that could sink (a) might leave (b) standing, so please keep them seperate where it matters.

I'm also worried I've made the view impossible to argue against: if I can always stretch "some say in the system" wide enough that almost anyone counts, or explain away every counterexample, then the view isn't risking anything. Be honest about whether that's happened here, and whether being bullet-proof like that is a problem for a view of this sort, or whether a view about who we owe things to just isn't supposed to be testable in that way.

Finally, tell me what would actually move me most — what evidence or argument should change my mind if I'm reasoning well. And could you write the whole answer in British English.
