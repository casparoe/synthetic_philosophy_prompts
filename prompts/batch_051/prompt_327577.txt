I am a policy analyst for a state child-welfare body that is reviewing a proposal to license parents before they may raise children. The proposal's rationale is not that the state should hand-pick parents, but that every child has a right to an open future: a right to reach adulthood with a reasonable range of life options still available. Please turn that principle into a screening procedure my office could actually run on applicants for a parental license. It should be a sequence of questions or tests, in the order they should be asked, with enough specificity that two trained staff members would usually reach the same decision. For each step, state what counts as a pass, what counts as a fail, and what falls into a gray zone requiring review by a senior official. The procedure must be usable in a one-hour interview plus document review by staff with no philosophical training. Mark the points where the procedure makes a moral or political choice rather than applying the principle neutrally. Then run the procedure on three applicants: (1) parents who would raise the child in an insular religious community with no schooling outside the community and no access to secular media; (2) parents who are poor, competent, and affectionate, whose child would grow up with materially fewer options than a middle-class child; (3) a single parent who plans to dedicate nearly all the family's resources to making the child an elite athlete from age four. Please be committed: make definite choices about the procedure and its results, and defend them rather than just laying out alternatives.
