I keep seeing infinite regress arguments used as though the mere existence of a regress refutes a view, but the grounds for calling a regress vicious are rarely made explicit. Here are five candidate diagnostics for viciousness. Rank them from best to worst as a general test, and give a one-sentence justification for each ranking.

1. A regress is vicious when it would require completing an infinite series of steps before some specified outcome, and that outcome could never be reached.
2. A regress is vicious when the same explanatory burden reappears at each stage, so the original phenomenon is never explained.
3. A regress is vicious when it entails a contradiction under the assumption that the initial item exists or the initial task is completable.
4. A regress is vicious when it shows that a chain of dependence lacks a ground, even if no formal contradiction follows.
5. A regress is vicious when a finite agent could not in practice run through the steps, so the regress undermines the relevant belief or action.

After ranking, apply your top criterion to the justification regress for an empirical belief and to the benign truth regress: p is true, it is true that p is true, it is true that it is true that p is true, and so on. If the top criterion fails to separate them, say whether that is a problem for the criterion or for the assumption that the truth regress is benign.
