I've gone down a rabbit hole lately after a friend insisted Newton got robbed in the calculus thing, and I couldn't figure out why I wasn't sure. Now I'm reading about old scientific priority fights -- Newton vs Leibniz, and the three-way thing over who discovered oxygen in the 1770s -- and what bugs me is that these disputes never actually settle, even centuries later, even though everyone is looking at the same letters, notebooks, and publication dates. In the Newton-Leibniz case the facts are mostly agreed: the timeline of letters and papers isn't really in dispute anymore, but people still fight about whether Leibniz saw Newton's work, whether that even matters, and what it even means to "have" the calculus when two people built it differently. The oxygen one is even stranger. Priestley made the gas but explained it with phlogiston, which turned out to be wrong; Scheele made it earlier but didn't publish in time; Lavoisier was last but actually understood what the stuff was and named it. Textbooks mostly hand the discovery to Lavoisier, which looks like they just picked the guy who won the chemistry revolution, not the guy who actually had the thing first.

My working theory is that the whole debate is stuck because the word "discovered" is doing too many jobs at once, and each side quietly picks the meaning that favors its guy: being first to make it, first to publish, first to understand it, first to convince everyone. If that's right, the disagreement isn't empirical anymore -- it's verbal, and that's why no newly found notebook would ever end it. But I'm not sure. Maybe there's a real factual or conceptual question hiding in there about what a discovery has to be. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, not a history lesson and not a verdict on who should get credit. And given that diagnosis, what would it take to unstick it: a agreed-on definition, a different question, or is the whole fight just badly posed? Put it as a numbered list of exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no introduction or conclusion.
