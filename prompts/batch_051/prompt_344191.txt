You are a legal philosopher who works on the rule of law, especially the demand that rules be public, prospective, and knowable in advance. You have been asked for a written opinion on the case below. Think the problem through systematically, and begin by briefly canvassing the considerations on each side before committing yourself.

A university's disciplinary regulations contain the following clause:

"Section 9. No student shall be subjected to any academic penalty except in accordance with a regulation published and in force at the time of the conduct complained of, and that regulation shall specify with reasonable clarity both the conduct prohibited and the penalty that may follow."

The regulations were drafted long before automated decision-making existed. The university has recently adopted a commercial tool that flags suspected contract cheating. The published regulations already prohibit "submitting work produced wholly or partly by another person as one's own." The tool analyzes a semester of the student's keystroke timing, cursor movements, editing pauses, and login activity from the university's learning-management system, and it assigns a risk score. When the score crosses a threshold, the Academic Integrity Office charges the student under the contract-cheating regulation. The vendor treats the model and the threshold as trade secrets, and the model is updated frequently without notice. A student was penalized on this basis even though she wrote her own essay; she was flagged because she worked in short bursts at unusual hours. She cannot learn which features triggered the score. The university's sole answer is that the tool is not itself a regulation but only an evidence-gathering instrument that "operationalizes" the published offense, much as an examiner's suspicion might.

Write an essay on whether this use of the tool violates Section 9 as applied to her case. Address the question from each of these standpoints: (a) the plain words of the clause; (b) the purpose the clause serves; (c) what the drafters of the clause would most plausibly have said if asked; and (d) what a reasonable student reading the clause would understand it to promise. Then state which standpoint should be decisive and why, and reach a clear verdict rather than ending in equipoise.

After your verdict, say how confident you are in it and identify the single point of your argument about which you are least sure.
