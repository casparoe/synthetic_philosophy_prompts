In my former life as a research scientist I learned to ask whether an empirical trend survives a change in how the relevant quantity is defined and measured. That habit now strikes me as applying directly to a thesis I keep encountering in my philosophical reading: the claim, argued most forcefully by Steven Pinker, that human violence has declined dramatically over the longue durée. The claim rests heavily on per capita rates and on a fairly narrow operationalization of violence as direct bodily harm—homicide, war deaths, torture, and the like. Now suppose we make two changes. First, we score the trend in absolute numbers of people harmed rather than per capita. Second, we admit into 'violence' forms of harm that the standard account largely excludes or undercounts because they were only recently measured or classified as such—structural violence, coercive control, slavery-like labor conditions. Which premise of the decline argument does each change bear on, and does the argument survive in a repaired but still meaningful form, or does it collapse into a merely verbal stipulation about what should count as violence, with no fact of the matter left over? I am interested in the structure of the argument, not in a survey of the empirical literature.
