I have been assembling a catalogue of the standard responses to the problem posed in Lewis Carroll's dialogue 'What the Tortoise Said to Achilles', where Achilles cannot get the Tortoise to take the step from premises to conclusion, and every attempt to add the missing step as a further premise merely opens up another step in the regress. The responses I have collected so far are these. (1) The rule/premise distinction: modus ponens is a rule of inference rather than a further premise, and the regress arises only because the Tortoise insists on treating a rule as if it were a proposition among the premises. (2) The dispositional response: accepting a rule of inference is not believing an additional proposition but being disposed to draw conclusions in accordance with it, so the Tortoise has merely given verbal assent and has not accepted the rule at all. (3) The constitutive response: the disputed rules are partly constitutive of the relevant concepts, or of reasoned judgement in general, so a person who withholds them while continuing to use those concepts has not adopted a coherent position. (4) The quietist response: deduction needs no external justification, because deduction is itself the standard by which justifications are assessed, and the demand for a non-circular vindication rests on a mistake. (5) The semantic response: one can show by truth-table or model-theoretic reasoning that the disputed rules preserve truth, and the fact that this demonstration itself uses deduction is harmless, since the demonstration is not meant to persuade someone who has withdrawn from rational argument altogether.

I suspect this list is close to exhaustive among the standard moves, and that is precisely my difficulty: I want one more response to the Tortoise that is genuinely different from these five. By genuinely different I mean not a rewording of the rule/premise distinction, not a special case or variant of the dispositional or constitutive responses, not the quietist answer in different dress, and not a mere combination of items already on the list. State the new response precisely, explain its structure so that I can see how it differs from each of the five, and then give an honest assessment of whether it is any good as a way of either blocking the regress or dissolving the original problem.

Please write the answer in British English. End with a single line of the form \answer{...}, where the braces contain a short label naming the new response you have proposed.
