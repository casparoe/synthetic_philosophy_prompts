So I fell down a hole reading about this old Korean Neo-Confucian debate (the Four–Seven debate, I think its called) where they argued about whether the four beginnings — compassion when you see a kid about to fall into a well, shame, deference, a sense of right and wrong — are a fundamentally different kind of thing from the seven emotions (joy, anger, sorrow, fear, love, hate, desire). One camp said the four come from principle (li) and the seven come from your physical makeup (qi); the other camp said no, both are the same kind of emotional stuff, and the only real difference is whether a feeling happens to line up with what principle wants. Reading about it made me notice we run the same split in everyday life. We treat moral feelings as something a person can be judged for: if someone cheats and feels zero guilt, that seems to tell us something important about who they are. But we dont treat plain feelings that way: if someone hates mornings or never gets excited about anything, we call it personality, and we wouldnt say they are morally on the hook for it. Both are just feelings that show up, mostly without being chosen. Is there any real justification for treating moral emotions as a special class you can be evaluated for, or is the asymmetry just a habit? If you defend the asymmetry, defend it against the case where guilt and boredom are both equally involuntary automatic responses to a situation.
