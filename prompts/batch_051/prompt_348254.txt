I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I keep meeting the principle that every continuous symmetry of a physical system has a corresponding conserved quantity, often loosened to the claim that symmetry explains conservation. Recently I watched someone use it to end a debate: they said the conservation of energy needs no further explanation because it follows from time-translation symmetry, and that momentum conservation is likewise explained by spatial-translation symmetry. I want to know what that principle could actually be asserting. Please separate the distinct precise claims it might express, and for each say: what the symmetry is a symmetry of (the laws, the equations, the action, a particular solution, our way of describing the system), what it means to say a conservation law follows or results from a symmetry, and whether that reading is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false. Then say which of these readings, if any, would actually do the work it was used for in that debate, and which reading someone who repeats the slogan is most likely to have in mind.
