I'm a PhD student in political theory working on a dissertation chapter about how different institutional settlements shape religious accommodation, and I'd like you to help me stress-test my framework by reasoning through a counterfactual. Please treat this not as a request for a summary of laïcité or of multiculturalism, but as a request to work through mechanisms, incentives, and constraints, with your reasoning made visible at each step.

The scenario. France, 2026. Over the course of a single legislative term, a governing coalition enacts a "civic accommodation package." The package (1) repeals the 2004 statute barring conspicuous religious symbols in public schools and the 2010 statute barring full-face coverings in public space; (2) replaces the public sector's duty of strict religious neutrality with a Canadian-style duty of reasonable accommodation up to the point of undue hardship; and (3) amends the anti-discrimination framework so that the administrative courts, school authorities, municipal governments, hospitals, and the public broadcaster must treat religion-linked indirect disadvantage as a form of actionable discrimination. The 1905 separation law and the constitutional principle of laïcité remain on the books, so courts must reconcile those older commitments with the new statutory duties. The surrounding society changes only through the reform: the same parties, unions, religious organizations, school head teachers, and judicial institutions remain in place.

I would like you to predict how this regime looks twenty years later, at the level of structure rather than particular elections or personalities. Draw the prediction from the mechanisms the reform sets in motion, and be explicit about which effects you regard as robust and which are guesses.

Please structure your answer under the following five headings, in this order, with roughly the indicated lengths:

1. Baseline mechanics (~250 words). Explain how the pre-2026 neutrality regime actually functioned in three settings — public schools, public employment, and ordinary state services — so that the prediction is anchored in institutions rather than slogans.

2. First-order effects, 2026–2031 (~300 words). Work through how courts, school authorities, public-sector employers, and political actors respond in the first five years. Include both the accommodation claims that immediately become available and the likely counter-mobilization.

3. Medium-term equilibrium, 2031–2046 (~400 words). State which accommodations become routine, which generate durable conflict, and what changes in school enrollment, the composition of public employment, anti-discrimination doctrine, and the party system.

4. Robust and fragile links (~300 words). Separate the predictions driven by strong, repeated mechanisms from the predictions that are genuinely speculative, and state the assumptions each fragile link depends on.

5. What would change my mind (~250 words). Identify the specific observations — judicial decisions, school enrollment patterns, cycles of protest, labor-market outcomes, or particular accommodations that get reversed — that would most change your overall assessment, and say where your chain of reasoning is most likely to fail.
