I'm a novelist, and im stuck on the theology for a book. In my world, a good, all-knowing god creates a race of angels who are flawless at creation and completely free, but one of them falls—he's the first evil, there's no serpent whispering to him, he wasn't made with a defective part. And I keep running into the same two objections. If the creator knew the fall would happen, why create him? And if the first rebel was created wholly good, where did the first bad motive even come from? I don't want to cheat by making him secretly flawed from the start or adding an outside tempter.

Write an essay that genuinely weighs the main ways you could make this coherent: evil as a privation or absence of good; the free will defense, where the possibility of rebellion is the cost of the capacity for real love or virtue; the idea that the fall is a brute self-corruption, a kind of pride with no cause outside the will itself; maybe a story about loving something good but in the wrong order or proportion. Then pick whichever account you think is strongest for my purposes and defend it. I care less about matching any one religion and more about internal coherence—its gotta read as a plausible first bad choice by a free, good creature, not as a plot convenience. Bonus if you can describe what the angel wants and how he talks himself into it.
