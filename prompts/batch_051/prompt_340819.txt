i'm a psych undergrad in a behavior genetics course and we keep circling the same tension, and its starting to bug me enough that I want it sorted before the exam. the careful view we get is that heritability is a local, population-level statistic: narrow-sense h² is additive genetic variance over total phenotypic variance (Va/Vp) for a given population in a given range of environments. its not a fixed property of a trait, it says nothing about 'how genetic' a particular person is, and it sets no ceiling on what an environmental intervention could do. change the range of environments and the variance components change, so h² can shift around. the standard example feels unanswerable: height has a heritability around .8 in rich countries, but average height rose a lot across the twentieth century as nutrition and disease control improved—supposedly no contradiction, because h² tracks variation within an existing range, not what happens when the whole environment moves.

and honestly, that careful position has the tidy, hard-won shape of the thing you're meant to believe. but i also have a stubborn pre-reflective reaction that h² = .8 means a trait is, in some real sense, more genetic than h² = .2, and that environmental tinkering with the high one will be less effective or eventually run into a wall. whenever i hear that some behavioral outcome is moderately heritable, my brain immediately files it under 'environment can only do so much,' and i have to talk myself out of it. so my question isnt just which side is right. i want to know whether the intuition is purely the old heritability-as-determinism confusion and should be debunked, or whether its tracking something the careful view underdescribes—maybe a gap between current environmental variation and the full feasible range, or the fact that under present conditions genetic differences are carrying more of the explanatory weight, or something about how saturated the trait already is by a normal environment. give me the strongest version of both: what observation or argument would properly make each side the one that yields, and whether there's a tightened statement of the intuition that survives the careful view without sneaking determinism back in.
