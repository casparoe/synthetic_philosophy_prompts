I've been reading the defamation cases that try to separate protected opinion from actionable fact, and the argument about credit ratings and analyst forecasts seems permanently stuck. Take Compuware Corp. v. Moody's Investors Services, from the Second Circuit in 2007. As I read it, the court treats a credit rating as an inherently predictive judgment—something like a forward-looking assessment of creditworthiness, so it can't be verified the way a statement about whether a company missed a loan payment can be. It can be wrong without being false in the defamatory sense.

That move feels clean, but then Milkovich says labeling something an opinion doesn't help if it implies an undisclosed factual basis, and every later case just re-fights the same line. Some courts say a rating is just an evaluative judgment; others say if a rating agency gave a company an investment-grade rating while privately believing otherwise, or if the rating model relied on bad data, then there's an implied factual assertion buried inside the opinion. The public factual record is often the same in these cases: the rating existed, the company later collapsed. Yet the outcomes keep splitting.

I've started to suspect this isn't a doctrinal disagreement at all—or at least not one that more doctrine can fix. It might be a philosophical disagreement about whether forward-looking evaluations have truth conditions in the first place, or a First Amendment policy disagreement about the chilling effect on financial speech, or a semantic disagreement about what 'fact' and 'opinion' even mean under Milkovich. But I'm not confident, and I want a diagnosis rather than a survey of the cases. What kind of disagreement is this actually? Do the sides disagree about a factual question—how readers actually interpret ratings and forecasts—or about a definitional question, or about underlying values? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a different legal test, empirical evidence about investor interpretation, a philosophical distinction, or just admitting the line is blurry and living with it?
