The last-person thought experiment is usually run with a pocket of wilderness: the last human alive wrecks an old-growth forest for the hell of it, knowing no one will ever miss it, and we're meant to feel this is still wrong because the forest matters over and above its usefulness to people. Now update the case for a world where there's basically no pristine wilderness left—nearly everything wild has already been shaped by human action, through fire regimes, introduced species, shifting climates, conservation management. The 'forest' in the thought experiment is now something like a rewilded moor or a novel ecosystem made of species humans have moved around. Does the argument survive this update? I'm interested in which premise is taking the strain: the claim that nature has value independant of human purposes, or just the availability of examples that look untouched by us. If it survives, say what weaker or different claim is left (for example, does it become relational?); if it dosen't, say what the thought experiment should be redescribed as being about.
