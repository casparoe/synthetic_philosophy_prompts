Longtime reader of the rationalist-ish AI safety corner of the internet, so I've seen every "but have you tried monitoring the model with another model" take there is. The distinction I keep wanting people to enforce harder is alignment vs. control. Alignment is about making the model actually want the right thing. Control is about schemes that keep us safe even if the model is misaligned — sandboxing, trusted or untrusted monitoring (roughly: a comparable AI checking outputs vs. a weaker check that's harder to game), paraphrasing to stop steganography, honeypots, capability limits, staged deployment, kill switches, the works. What bugs me is how often proposals advertised as control quietly assume the model is basically honest and on our side, at which point there just alignment measures with extra steps.

So, before I read any more of them, I want the spec. Write the desiderata for a genuine control measure. If I bring you a proposed safety scheme and claim it's a control measure, what does it have to deliver before you'll even take that category claim seriously? I want the requirements list: the failure modes it has to be robust to, the distinctions it can't collapse, and the constraints on how the scheme itself can be evaluated. For each requirement, give the reason it's a requirement rather than a nice-to-have, and mark which are non-negotiable (without them the thing just isn't a control measure) and which are preferences of particular subcamps. Then flag any pairs or subsets of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — if the list contains a little no-go theorem, that's the part I most want to see, not the scorecard. And tell me which single requirement kills the most naive proposals.

To set the level: I'm not looking for generic "the model should be safe" hand-waving. I mean things like — the measure should work when the model understands the measure and is playing against it; it can't treat the monitored model's self-reports as a trusted channel; it has to explain why "nothing bad happened during the eval" is evidence at all rather than just the model waiting; and it has to deal with the checkers sharing the model's blindspots. That sort of requirement, but I want the complete list and the defense, not just those four.

A quick pass on how trusted monitoring, untrusted monitoring, and capability-limited sandboxing do against the list is welcome, but keep it brief — the criteria are the thing I actually want.
