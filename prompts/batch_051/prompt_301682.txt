I'm puting together a list of responses to Fitch's paradox of knowability, and I want to know if there's one I'm missing. By the paradox I mean the result in Fitch's 1963 paper A Logical Analysis of Some Value Concepts that, with standard assumptions about knowledge distributing over conjunction and being factive, the knowability principle p → ◇Kp collapses into the omniscience claim p → Kp. The responses I already have are these:

1. Reject the knowability principle outright: anti-realism was never committed to all truths being knowable, only to all meaningful statements being verifiable, so the proof just shows the principle was stated too broadly.

2. Tennant's Cartesian restriction: apply knowability only to propositions p where knowing p is consistent, or at least possible. The proposition p∧¬Kp used in the proof is not Cartesian, so the paradox doesn't get going.

3. Go intuitionist: the proof only gets you p→¬¬Kp, not p→Kp, unless you allow classical double-negation elimination. Anti-realists who already use intuitionistic logic can stop there.

4. Edgington's actuality-operator move: weaken or restate knowability so that an unknown truth p is knowable as actually-p from a counterfactual standpoint, which doesn't force actual knowledge of p.

Is there another response I'm missing? It should be genuinely different from these four, not a restatement or special case of them. And tell me honestly whether the new response is any good, or if it just relocates the problem.
