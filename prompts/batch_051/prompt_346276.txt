I am a novelist, and for the better part of a year I have been drafting a book set in a pre-agricultural community that left no writing. In the course of researching it, I have spent a lot of time with the problem of how archaeologists move from objects — pits, postholes, broken figurines, ochre stains, the arrangement of hearths and middens, the placement of bodies and grave goods — to claims about what past people believed and how their institutions were organized. The more I read, the more I have drifted toward a position I did not start with. It is roughly this: below the threshold of writing or a continuous descendant tradition, the material record underdetermines reconstructions of past beliefs and institutions. A pit can be refuse or the trace of a closing ritual; a figurine can be a toy or an ancestor; a room without domestic debris can be a meeting house or a shrine. The same objects, because of equifinality — different activities leaving similar material traces — can usually be made to fit several incompatible stories, and the choice among those stories is driven more by the analyst's background assumptions than by the finds themselves. So most confident claims about prehistoric cosmology, kinship, or ritual are closer to projection than to inference.

I want to know how exposed this position really is. What would count against it? Please give me a structured map of the evidence, arguments, methods, or cases that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be. I would be especially interested in what the position's defenders would themselves have to concede as a genuine vulnerability, not just what its critics say. I also want an honest assessment of whether the position can be qualified into unfalsifiability — for example, by always saying that a given inference is underdetermined, no matter how many independent lines of evidence converge — and if it can be, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Personally, I would be most persuaded by cases where a reconstruction made a novel prediction that later excavation or analysis confirmed, but I worry those cases are rare or selectively reported.

Please lead with a one-sentence verdict on how exposed the position is, and keep the entire answer to no more than 900 words.
