I'm a novelist and keep coming back to what a phootograph actually does, because it feels different from a paragraph or even a painting. One view I half-remember holds that a photograph is transparent: because the image is caused mechanically by light from the scene, if some detail had been different—a hand moved, a cloud shifted—the photo would have differed too, so in a real sense we see the thing through the photo, not just a representation of it. The rival view says that's too fast: a photo is shot, cropped, lit, selected, printed; it is an artifact made by choices, so we never literally see through it—we interpret a picture. Compare these two ways of thinking about photography. Is "transparent" a fair word for what a photo does, and which view should a novelist trust when they use a photograph as a metaphor for showing rather than telling?
