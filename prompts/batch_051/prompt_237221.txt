Write a short dialogue between an epistemic structural realist and an ontic structural realist who are trying to determine whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal. Set the scene briefly, but make the argument the center of the piece. The epistemic structural realist maintains that mature science can know the relational structure of the world—the causal, functional, and mathematical relations expressed in its theories—but must remain agnostic about the intrinsic nature of whatever stands in those relations. The ontic structural realist holds the stronger view that, at the fundamental level, there is nothing to the entities over and above their place in the web of relations; talk of unknowable intrinsic natures is a ladder we should kick away once it has served its purpose.

Give the dialogue genuine back-and-forth. The epistemic structural realist should press a version of the Newman problem: if structure is characterized purely formally, the claim that the world has such-and-such structure threatens to become too easily satisfied, so structure must be understood in terms of genuinely physical, concrete relations—but that looks like an admission that quietly gives up the epistemic modesty that motivated the position. The ontic structural realist should reply that this is a reason to abandon the hidden relata rather than to reintroduce them, and should press the question of what explanatory or theoretical work those relata could possibly do once all their relational, causal, and structural work has been fully accounted for. Let each character respond to the other’s actual arguments, concede when a point lands, and explore whether their dispute turns on epistemology, metaphysics, or the semantics of terms for objects. Do not settle it cheaply; a partial convergence or a clearly stated impasse is fine.

Limit the dialogue to 700 words.
