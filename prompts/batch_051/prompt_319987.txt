I keep encountering the claim that it is illegitimate to judge Aristotle's physics by modern standards, because he was not doing the same thing we do when we do physics. I have never understood what this claim amounts to. When Aristotle explains projectile motion by saying that the surrounding air, set in motion by the thrower, rushes around behind the projectile and pushes it along, he is plainly offering a causal explanation of a physical process. By modern physics, that explanation is false: a projectile continues after release because of inertia, not because a medium keeps pushing it from behind. So why is it not simply correct to say that Aristotle was wrong about projectile motion? Are phrases like he was doing something else or his standards were different anything more than polite ways of avoiding that verdict? What exactly do people mean when they say that calling him wrong is anachronistic, and what would a non-anachronistic assessment of his claim look like without giving up the idea that some physical claims are just false?
