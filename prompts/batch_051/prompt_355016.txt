I sit on my company's technical design review board, and we are deadlocked over a proposal to replace our data ingestion pipeline. The proposing team ran a two-week shadow deployment with strong results, but when the board pressed them on the design itself — why this queueing model rather than the alternatives, why this retry policy, why this schema — every answer invited a further 'why.' Followed far enough, their justifications either looped back to claims made earlier in the same design document or stopped at something like 'it is the sensible engineering choice' or 'our lead architect has done this for twenty years and trusts it.' Half the board considers this unremarkable: reasoning has to stop somewhere, and we cannot demand infinite documentation. The other half thinks the discussion exposed a genuine defect. Their view is that if every design rationale needs a further rationale, then approval must rest on one of three unsound bases — an infinite regress of reasons, a circle, or an arbitrary stopping point — and none of those is a legitimate ground for signing off on a change that could take down payments for several hours if it fails. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend a concrete standard for what counts as a sufficient justification in design review going forward; apply that standard to the pipeline proposal, with a clear bottom line of approve, reject, or approve with conditions; and fairly engage the strongest version of the opposing position rather than caricaturing it.
