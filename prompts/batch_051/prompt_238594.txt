In my previous life as a microbiologist I worked for many years on quorum sensing in Vibrio fischeri, and since retiring into philosophy I keep coming back to a structural parallel that may be either illuminating or seductive. The bacterium has a two-tier architecture. Each cell runs its own local metabolism and gene-expression machinery, while secreting and detecting a small autoinducer molecule whose ambient concentration tracks cell density. Below the quorum threshold the signal stays local: the regulon is effectively off, and each cell goes about its own business. When the autoinducer crosses the threshold it binds the LuxR activator and triggers a coordinated, colony-wide program, classically bioluminescence. Information that was distributed across many separate cells is suddenly made available to, and acted on by, the whole population. Lately I have been reading about global workspace theory, and the architectural parallel strikes me as remarkably tight. The theory posits specialized, parallel, local processors; a limited-capacity workspace; a threshold or competitive process that determines which contents enter it; and a global broadcast of the winning content to the rest of the system. That broadcast, on the theory, is access consciousness, as distinct from phenomenal consciousness. The Vibrio colony looks to me like a limiting case of the same functional diagram: local processing, a chemical signal that accumulates until a threshold is passed, and then a population-wide broadcast. My question is whether this parallel is deep or superficial. Would the colony, at the moment of quorum-sensing broadcast, satisfy the functional conditions for access consciousness while lacking phenomenal consciousness? If we rightly refuse to say the colony is conscious in any sense, is that because global workspace theory contains further requirements—a serial winner-take-all workspace, competition among candidate contents, or executive systems that consume and act on the broadcast—that the bacterial case lacks? Or does the example instead show that the broadcast-and-availability criterion is too easy to satisfy, and therefore under-describes what consciousness is supposed to be? I would like the answer to state the mapping precisely, mark the points where the two structures come apart, and say whether the bacterial case is a genuine test case for the theory or only a loose metaphor.
