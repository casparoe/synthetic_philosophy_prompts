After a political theory class, a friend tried to explain the whole positive/negative/republican liberty thing to me like this: "There are basically three kinds. Negative liberty is just not being stopped from doing what you want, especially by the state. Positive liberty says you're only free when you're actually in charge of yourself, so addictions and ignorance make you unfree and the state can legitimately step in to make you 'really' free. Republican liberty is positive liberty in Roman dress: it says you're free only if you participate in self-government and aren't under a king. The upshot is that Berlin showed negative liberty is the only coherent one, and the other two both end up as excuses for forcing people to be free." I'm suspicous that he's flattening things, but I don't know the literature well enough to push back. Which parts are accurate, which are unfair, and does his upshot actually follow once the accurate parts are cleaned up?
