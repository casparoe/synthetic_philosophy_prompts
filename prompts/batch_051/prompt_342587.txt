Two camps have formed about the bearing of Gottlob Frege's late political diary on the assessment of his philosophical work. Camp A holds that the diary's antisemitic, nationalist, and anti-democratic entries are philosophically irrelevant: Frege's contributions to logic, the philosophy of language, and the foundations of mathematics stand or fall on their internal merits, and biographical or political commitments are external to their evaluation. Camp B holds that the diary bears on his philosophy in at least one of several ways: it reveals the motivations behind his anti-psychologism, it exposes a conception of objectivity that treats certain kinds of minds as illegitimate, or it affects how his work should be taught and credited.

Draft the joint statement that these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should contain the following, in order:

1. A numbered section of claims both camps can accept. These claims should cover, at a minimum: what the diary is and when it was written; what Frege's core philosophical and logical doctrines claim, stated without slanting; and what standards of evaluation are in play. The claims should be substantive but not vacuous.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should state an issue, then give Camp A's precise formulation and Camp B's precise formulation, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. Avoid formulations that one side would reject as a caricature.

3. A section identifying the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, state a concrete test, argument, or case that both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that test would show.

4. If you conclude that part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why, and state the agreed reformulation under which the verbal element disappears.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The document should be self-contained and evaluable on its own reasoning.
