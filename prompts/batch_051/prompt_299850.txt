For biology extra credit I've been trying to nail down what makes something one organism, and I've got a bunch of verdicts that don't all seem to go together. Can you find the principle behind them, or tell me if there just isn't one?

- A dog is obviously one organism even though it's full of gut bacteria. I want to say the dog is the organism and the bacteria are passengers. But a coral is also an animal with algae living inside it, and I find myself calling the coral-plus-algae thing one organism (I think someone called it a holobiont?). That seems inconsistent with what I said about the dog, but I don't know which judgment to drop.

- A lichen (fungus + algae) I'd also call one organism, not two organisms working together.

- A Portuguese man o' war is technically a bunch of little zooids stuck together, but it looks and acts like one jellyfish, so I say one organism.

- An ant colony gets called a superorganism, but I don't buy it: I say each ant is the organism and the colony is more like a family. Except then I remember the worker ants can't reproduce on there own, so by a reproduce-on-your-own test the whole colony would be the organism, and I dont like that conclusion.

- A grove of aspen trees all connected by roots and genetically identical: I lean toward the whole grove being one organism, or at least more one-organism-ish than the ant colony, but I can't say why.

- A slime mold that's separate single cells for part of its life and one crawling slug for the rest: I say many organisms when it's cells, one organism when it's a slug, but that feels like cheating.

My half-baked principle is: it's one organism when the parts are stuck together in one body and can't make it on their own. But then the ant colony fits better than the dog, the aspen grove doesn't fit, and the slime mold only fits half the time.

So: can you give me a principle (or a small set of candidate principles) that captures as many of these as possible, say what it thinks about cases I didn't list (me with my microbiome, a buffalo herd, a virus), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it can't fit? And if I'm asking a badly framed question — like "organism" isn't that kind of word, or my verdicts are just all over the place — just say that instead of forcing a neat answer.
