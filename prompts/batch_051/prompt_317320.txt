I keep seeing the levelling-down objection deployed as a decisive move against egalitarianism: if inequality were bad in itself, then bringing the better off down to the level of the worse off — with no one made any better off — would still be an improvement in one respect, and that is meant to be absurd. Prioritarianism is usually introduced as the repair, because it places extra weight on benefits to the worse off rather than on equality as such. But when I try to cash out the difference in practice, I cannot find one. The prioritarian's extra weight attaches to a person's absolute level of well-being, yet that level is low because others are doing much better; so every transfer the theory recommends from better off to worse off reduces inequality, and the priority weight appears to be doing exactly the work equality was supposed to do, with only the comparative element hidden inside the weighting function. If that is right, prioritarianism is simply egalitarianism with the equality-shaped parts renamed so that the absurd implication no longer shows up in the formalism. Where does this reasoning break, and what does the levelling-down objection actually show once the distinction is drawn correctly?
