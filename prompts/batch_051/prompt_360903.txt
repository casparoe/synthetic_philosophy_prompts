Draft the joint statement that a committed illusionist about phenomenal consciousness and a committed phenomenal realist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The illusionist I have in mind is the strong sort: experiences exist as states, but the idea that they instantiate private, intrinsic, non-physical what-its-like properties is a systematic introspective misrepresentation. The realist holds that those phenomenal properties are real and not analyzable in functional or representational terms, even in principle. I want the actual document: a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table.

For the agreed claims, make them non-vacuous — not just that consciousness exists — and include some about what introspective reports are, what they can be evidence for, and what the explanatory gap amounts to in neutral terms before either side interprets it.

For the disagreements table, each side's view should be phrased so that side would sign it, not a distorted version. I especially want you to nail down the exact level at which the disagreement sits: is it about the content of introspective states (are they ever false?), about the metaphysics of properties, about the semantics of seemings, or about the burden of proof?

Then identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side — and for each crux, give a concrete argument or experimental result both would agree bears on it, with each sides prediction recorded in advance.

Finally, commit. Dont just lay out the possibilities: say which of the disagreements you think are substantive and which are verbal, and for the verbal ones give the exact rephrasing that dissolves them. If your view is that one side's position collapses under that rephrasing, say so and defend it.
