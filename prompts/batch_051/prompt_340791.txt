(1) For a single isolated bet, I'm a standard expected-utility person: maximize expected utility, and risk aversion is just concavity of utility over final wealth.

(2) For a bet that's repeated many times with wealth compounding, I think the right target is the time-average growth rate of wealth, not the ensemble average (the expectation over parallel copies). So I use Kelly-style sizing, and I'd turn down some positive-expected-value bets because they're bad for long-run growth.

(3) I think the time-average growth criterion isn't just log utility in disguise. It's supposed to be a structural point about multiplicative processes that applies no matter what your risk preferences are, whereas log utility is one particular preference.

(4) I buy the ergodicity point that almost no real financial choice is truly one-off, since your life is one long string of multiplicative bets. But I still want to say there's a real difference between the one-shot and repeated cases, and that (1) and (2) are both doing real work.

(5) I don't think what's rational in a gamble should depend on whether I happen to frame it as one-off or as part of a sequence—same payoffs, same probabilities, same bankroll.
