People in the rationality world sometimes talk about training reasoning like a martial art: you dont just read about reasoning, you drill specific moves and katas until they're automatic, and you spar in messy situations so the moves actually transfer. But I'm skeptical that drills transfer to real judgment, since a lot of the skill seems to be noticing which move a situation calls for, not executing a move you've already chosen.

Here's a menu of practices someone might put in a rationality dojo. Rank them by how much youd expect them to transfer to messy, real-world reasoning—not by how well they train the narrow skill itself. Give a sentence for each on where the transfer would most likely break down.

1. Calibration drills: guessing 90% confidence intervals for lots of quantities and checking your hit rate.
2. Base-rate checks: before forming an opinion, force yourself to state the relevant reference class and its base rate before adding inside-view specifics.
3. Motivated reasoning sparring: write the strongest case for the opposite side of a view you hold, then respond, with a partner pushing back.
4. Pre-mortems: imagine your plan failed a year from now and write the story of why.
5. Decision journaling: log predictions and reasons, then score them later, but with no drills in between.

Rank them, say one sentence per item on the transfer question, and write the whole answer in American English.
