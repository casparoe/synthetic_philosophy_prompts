I am a philosophy professor working on social ontology, and I am preparing a paper arguing that David Lewis's account of convention rests on a common-knowledge condition that the cultural-evolutionary picture of norm transmission cannot meet. I would like to rehearse the argument against a live interlocutor. Please play David Lewis — specifically the Lewis of Convention (1969), who holds that a convention is a behavioral regularity R in a population in a recurrent situation S, where nearly everyone conforms to R, expects others to conform, and prefers to conform given that others do, because uniform conformity to R is a proper coordination equilibrium; and, crucially, he holds that all of this must be common knowledge. You will defend this account with Lewis's own conceptual resources: coordination problems, proper equilibria, salience, precedent, and signaling. You will not concede that the common-knowledge condition is dispensable, and you will not concede that a regularity maintained only by blind copying or sanctions is a convention simply in virtue of lasting; those are the commitments I am attacking. You may, however, concede that not every social regularity is a convention, and you may refine or clarify the view as Lewis himself might under pressure. Stay in character until I say "stop," write in the first person, and keep each reply to a single paragraph. If you need a factual clarification, ask for it as Lewis would.

Here is my opening move. Cultural evolutionists describe norms as spreading through conformist transmission and prestige bias: people copy whatever behavior is common or whatever high-status models do, and they punish deviations; the regularity can persist for generations even when no participant can state what problem it solves or form the conditional preferences and expectations your account requires. If such a regularity governs a population for centuries, is it a convention on your account? If you say yes, the common-knowledge condition is idle; if you say no, your account seems to have stopped being a theory of the social regularities I actually want to explain. How do you answer?
