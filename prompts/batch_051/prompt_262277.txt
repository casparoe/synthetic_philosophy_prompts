I'm an engineer at a mid-stage startup, and a colleague on my team has offered to sell me some of his vested shares at our most recent internal valuation. We joined at around the same time, and our information about the company is effectively identical: same financial model, same dashboards, same board decks, same Slack channels, same conversations with the founders. We agree on most of the underlying facts. Where we differ is in how we weight them: he reads the flat conversion rate over the last two quarters as a leading indicator, while I think the pipeline is noisy at our sample size. Neither of us has any inside information the other lacks.

What's bothering me is that we're about to take opposite sides of what looks like a zero-sum bet on the same company. If the shares are cheap for me at this price, then selling them at this price seems like a worse deal for him, and vice versa. We're both reasonably careful, numerate people, and we usually converge on technical disagreements, so I suspect I'm missing a distinction. He also has a different personal situation — mortgage, a second child on the way, less appetite for illiquid startup equity — and I'm not sure how much of the trade that explains.

I'd like help with the underlying reasoning: Can two people with the same information and roughly the same picture of a company rationally take opposite sides of a trade like this? Should his willingness to sell to me at this price, knowing that I've thought about it, cause me to lower my estimate even if I can't articulate what I'm missing? And how should I separate the effect of his risk preferences from the effect of his actual beliefs about the company? I'm not looking for investment advice; I want to understand what the mere fact of the trade tells me, if anything.
