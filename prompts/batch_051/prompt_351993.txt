I've been reading Simone Weil on why political parties should be done away with, and I've tried to boil her criteria down. As I understand it, a political party in her pejorative sense has three features: (1) its real goal is its own growth and the capture of state power, whatever the manifesto says; (2) it works out its positions as a collective and hands them down as a package, so you can't endorse one plank without taking the whole line, and publicly breaking ranks is treated as disloyalty; (3) it manufactures collective passions — it gets you angry or hopeful as a 'we' before you've actually thought the thing through, and that substitutes for individual judgement.

Now consider a group founded specifically to implement her remedy. It wants political parties abolished and replaced by ad hoc single-issue citizens' associations, but to get there it runs slates in elections, makes members pledge to support the whole programme, expels anyone who publicly backs a rival party's policy they agree with, and organises rallies to build a collective 'we' against the party system. Does this qualify as a political party in her pejorative sense? Is the group refuting her criteria, or just instantiating them — i.e., is Weil's own remedy self-undermining? Give me a committed verdict, criterion by criterion, and say whether the case shows the criteria themselves need changing.
