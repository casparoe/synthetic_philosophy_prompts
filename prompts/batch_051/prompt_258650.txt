I'm a PhD student in decision theory and Ive been going in circles with a friend about evidential cooperation in large worlds. The idea, as I mean it: in a big universe your choice to cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma is evidence about what the population of agents similar to you does, so even though your act has no causal effect on the other player, cooperating raises the probability you're in a world where the other cooperates, and that can dominate the expected utility. My credence that this sort of consideration is alone enough to make cooperation rationally required in a real one-shot PD with a stranger — no reputation, no repeat play, no enforcement, and setting aside whether it's morally nice — is around 0.35. My friend is at 0.8, and we're obviously not disagreeing about the arithmetic.

Give me your own number, but I mostly want the decomposition. The sub-questions I keep getting stuck on are: how much should I trust the evidential move in the first place (that a rational choice can be based on what the act indicates, not just what it causes); whether the actual world is actually large and similar-agent-dense enough for the relevant conditional probabilities to be what the argument needs, given that there probably aren't exact duplicates of any particular human; whether the evidential correlation in a real one-shot PD with a random stranger is strong enough to swing the expected utility, or only enough to break ties; and whether a reason that only appears when you treat yourself as a sample from a reference class of similar choosers can ever amount to a requirement, rather than just a consideration. Put a rough probability on each, show how they combine, and then tell me which one would move your final number most if someone showed you it was false — and maybe wich one my friend and I are most likely disagreeing about.
