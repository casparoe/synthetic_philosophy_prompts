I've been arguing with a friend about whether culture matters on its own or only because people care about it, and I wrote down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases to see if they add up. They don't obviously. I'd like someone to find the principle behind them, or tell me which verdicts I'd have to give up.

A language with two remaining speakers, both in their nineties, neither of them much bothered that it will die with them: I'd happily fund recording the language thoroughly, but a state-funded revival program nobody in the community asked for feels like an imposition, and like taxidermy. A village festival performed for five hundred years that the last village decides to stop, because no young people want it: if a foundation in the capital keeps performing it anualy with hired professionals and a school curriculum, I think the festival isn't actually surviving, just being preserved as a museum piece — some value, sure, but not the same value. A minor painting by a neglected but genuinely good artist, found in an attic: restoring it would cost tens of thousands and no one alive is especially attached to it, it just is beautiful and well-made, and I'd still say restore it if the money's there — which seems to value it beyond anyone's actual interest. A beautifully carved mask from a religious practice that died out decades ago, no community claims it or uses it: if a museum threw it out and it was crushed in a landfill, I'd call that a real if not huge loss, even though it's a cultural object with no living holders. And when I imagine the same money feeding starving people, I'd feed people without much hesitation — so whatever value these things have, it isn't overriding.

My half-theory is that languages and traditions have value only through actual engagement by the people whose lives they are part of (not hypothetical future appreciators), but completed works of art can be worth preserving in some intrinsic way that practices and languages can't. That feels ad hoc and I don't trust it. Is there a cleaner principle that gets my verdicts, or most of them? Which ones would it force me to drop? And what would it say about the cases I keep avoiding: a set of traditional songs existing only as a single digital recording that gets accidentally deleted — is anything of real value lost beyond information? And spending public money to keep a language alive that its own community is abandoning for a more economically useful one?
