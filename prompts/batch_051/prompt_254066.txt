For a final assignment in my philosophy of spacetime seminar, I am writing the question script for a recorded interview with a philosopher of physics whose work we read this term. She defends sophisticated substantivalism about spacetime: the view that the manifold points of general relativity are real, but that a model (M, g) and its image (M, d*g) under a hole diffeomorphism represent one and the same physical possibility rather than two distinct worlds. On her telling, this is what keeps determinism intact in a generally covariant theory. The setup, as we studied it: let the diffeomorphism be the identity everywhere outside an open region and non-trivial inside it. The two models agree outside that region but assign different metric properties to points inside it, so a substantivalist who treats points as having primitive identities seems forced to count the models as two distinct developments of the same initial data. Her anti-haecceitist reading blocks that by denying points have primitive identities, so diffeomorphisms are a gauge redundancy rather than a generator of new worlds.

Please write me eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate that move. Assume the audience already follows the hole argument and wants to see the position tested, not summarized. Press where the literature presses. In particular, include at least one question on each of the following, and for each, script the follow-up you would put to her after the reply she is most likely to give: (i) whether sophisticated substantivalism, by individuating points only through the metric's relations, silently collapses into relationism; (ii) what independent modal metaphysics of possible worlds and haecceitism her position commits her to, and whether it is motivated by anything besides saving determinism; (iii) whether a parallel problem can be run for pre-relativistic spacetime theories, so the argument proves either too much or not specifically what she needs; (iv) whether treating diffeomorphisms as gauge leaves any local physical content for her spacetime points. End with a question that forces her to say what would count against the view — for instance, which developments in quantum gravity or mathematical relativity would make her abandon sophisticated substantivalism.
