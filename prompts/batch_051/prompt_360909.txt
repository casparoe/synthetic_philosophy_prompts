I've spent the last year building a toy universe on my laptop—a sealed, perfectly deterministic two-dimensional chamber filled with a few million tiny hard spheres that bounce off one another and off the walls elastically. I call the particles simmballs and the whole chamber a careenium. When I run a simulation, stable large-scale structures emerge on their own: vortices that persist through millions of collisions, chains that carry echoes of earlier collisions, and a few structures that behave like if-then switches, storing a bit and later steering nearby patterns one way rather than another depending on what they stored. Underneath, though, it is nothing but simmballs obeying one short, local collision rule. I've ended up in a long argument with a friend about whether the high-level patterns—the symbols—ever genuinely push the simmballs around. I put my credence at about 0.3 that the symbols are causally inert, meaning they never do real causal work and only look active because our eyes insist on grouping particles into patterns; my friend puts hers at about 0.8 on that same claim. I'd like you to step in as a philosopher of mind who also knows something about dynamical systems and levels of explanation, and to think carefully before committing to a number.

To earn your number, start by brainstorming two or three different ways someone could dissolve the apparent tension between the micro-level story and the macro-level story, and say which of those you take seriously. Then give me your own probability that the symbols are causally inert, but decompose it rather than pulling a number out of the air. Please give rough probabilities to each of these sub-questions and say how they cut: whether the symbol facts are fixed completely by the simmball facts, so that no two worlds with identical simmball states could differ in what symbols they contain (I gather this is called supervenience); whether that kind of complete fixing, if true, effectively settles the question in favor of inertness rather than merely showing that the micro-level is the wrong level at which to look; whether a single symbol could be multiply realized by many different simmball arrangements while still being the same symbol; whether there are non-trivial symbol-level counterfactuals of the form—if the symbol had been different, the later simmball state would have been different—that are not merely relabeled simmball-level counterfactuals; and whether coarse-grained patterns can count as doing real pushing in a deterministic world where the micro-level is causally closed. Then show how your sub-probabilities combine into the final credence, and tell me which sub-question most likely separates my 0.3 from my friend's 0.8.
