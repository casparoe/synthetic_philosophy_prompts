Write a deadpan laboratory-instrument manual, around 600 words, for a made-up device called the Surgical Intervention Wand, sold by a philosophy-of-science supply catalog. The device is supposed to tell you whether X causes Y: you point it at X, give X a surgical little nudge, and measure whether Y changes while holding everything else fixed. The voice should be an oscilloscope manual—numbered sections, warnings, troubleshooting, a warranty—and the target is Woodward-style interventionism: the view that causal claims just are claims about what would happen under manipulations of X, with the manipulation conditions treated as the whole story. Land the circularity worry without ever naming it: the manual keeps promising a non-causal test for whether a wiggle counts as an intervention, but each test quietly uses causal language (severing X from its other incoming causes, not affecting Y except through X, being independent of any other cause of Y), and whenever the user asks how to certify that test, they get referred to a smaller wand, which comes with an even smaller wand, and the manual sells the regress as a feature. Also get in the invariance/modularity material: include a troubleshooting section where a correlation that breaks under the wand never refutes causation—it gets blamed on the operator's model not being modular, the intervention not being surgical enough, or having chosen the wrong variable. Include sections like "Is Your Manipulation an Intervention, or Just a Change?", "Reading the Invariance Gauge", and "Warranty." The warranty should void itself if the customer asks whether the wand, applied to itself, causes its own readings. Dont wink at the reader, and dont let the narrator admit anything is off; a philosopher who works on causation should still recognize the circularity objection being made.
