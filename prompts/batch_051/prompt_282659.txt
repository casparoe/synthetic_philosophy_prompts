I'm a history of science PhD student working on calendrical reform, and I keep getting hung up on something basic. The civil calender goes straight from 1 BC to AD 1—no year zero—so centuries come out ordinal: the 21st century starts in 2001. But astronomers often insert a year 0 and run negative years before 1, Unix time starts at 1970-01-01 for what are basically arbitrary engineering reasons, and the Holocene calendar proposal adds ten thousand years so the epoch sits near the start of the Holocene and all of recorded history stays positive. I'd like someone to unpack what these conventions presuppose about what an epoch is doing in the first place. Are year numbers supposed to be durations elapsed since an origin, or labels for intervals in an ordered sequence? Is the civil calendar's missing year zero just an arithmetic accident, or does it presuppose something about how we count—starting at one rather than zero—and about the event that anchors the era? And if we were redesigning the civil calendar from scratch, what should the epoch and year zero actually be, and what would each choice commit us to?
