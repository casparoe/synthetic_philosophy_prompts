ok so im a junior and the student rep on our school's Student Life Fund committee, which is students plus teachers plus a couple parents. We vote Thursday on whether to renew this peer-tutoring program for freshmen that the school has funded for three years. Every meeting someone says we've already put three years, about eight thousand dollars, and a ton of volunteer hours into it, so killing it now wastes all of that. I know that's supposed to be the sunk cost fallacy. But is honoring sunk costs actually always irrational? And it's extra weird because the money and time mostly came from past students and a one-time grant, not from us on the committee, so is it even our sunk cost to honor? I'm drafting the one-page memo the committee will actually vote on. I want it to make a concrete call, renew for another year, kill it at the end of the term, or phase it out over a semester, with a time frame, and to seriously engage the best argument for keeping the program instead of just waving the word fallacy at it.
