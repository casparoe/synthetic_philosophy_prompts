I've been turning over the definition of art and want a working definition, not the usual loose talk. Test it against these cases: a small child's drawing stuck to the fridge; Duchamp's Fountain, the urinal-sculpture if you accept it as presented; a procedurally generated splatter where no human picked out the output; a clinical photograph in a medical textbook; a focus-grouped, shot-by-committee blockbuster sequel; and an old religious icon that one community venerates and another treats as just an object to be smashed. I'm sympathetic to the idea that 'art' does two jobs at once, one descriptive and one honorific. If you buy that, give me the two definitions and say which cases belong to each. If you don't, give one definition and tell me where it forces surprising verdicts.

Also, Leo Tolstoy claimed in What Is Art? that the essence of art is emotional infection: the artist has a feeling, embodies it in an external form, and the audience catches it, with the best art being universally infectious so a peasant and a connoisseur both get it. That makes individual, idiosyncratic expression count against a work rather than for it. Does Tolstoy's criterion track any of the cases above, or is it doing a third job for the word? And which of your classifications is least secure? Please answer in British English.
