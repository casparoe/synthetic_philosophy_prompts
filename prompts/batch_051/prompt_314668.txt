I'm designing a diagnostic exercise for a philosophy of biology seminar and I'd like a sorting quiz titled "Which account of biological function do you actually hold?" It should have seven or eight questions, each a concrete case with three answer options; students should be sorted by what they say about the cases, so no option should name a theory. At minimum use these cases: a heart congenitally unable to pump; a novel mutation with a current fitness benefit but no selection history; a trait selected for thermoregulation that is now used only in courtship display; a spandrel that was never selected but has since been co-opted; a callus or other non-inherited trait that is currently useful; a vestigial organ whose selected effect is no longer physically possible; and a trait that contributes to an organism's self-maintenance only intermittently. The options should force the joints between function as what something was selected for, function as what something does that explains a system's capacities, function as contribution to self-maintenance, and no fact of the matter or pluralism. Provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns to named leaves — selected-effects (etiological), causal-role, organizational, pluralist, and "unnamed" where appropriate — and explicitly flag any pair of leaves your questions cannot separate and any logically possible answer patterns with no occupant, with one sentence diagnosing each empty pattern as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
