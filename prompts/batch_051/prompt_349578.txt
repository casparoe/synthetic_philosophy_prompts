I've been going down the usual forum rabbit hole about shrimp welfare, neuron counts, and whether fish have the right machinery for conscious pain, and it's made me notice something odd about how actual law and industrial practice treat these animals compared to the ones we're more familiar with. In the EU and most wealthy countries, cattle, pigs, sheep, and poultry can't be slaughtered for food without being stunned first—rendered insensible before the cut or the bolt. Fish are vertebrates too, but farmed salmon are in practice routinely killed by carbon dioxide exposure, exsanguination without prior stunning, or being dumped into ice slurry, and prawns and lobsters are often frozen or boiled alive. In a lot of jurisdictions decapod crustaceans aren't counted as animals for welfare-protection purposes at all, or have only recently been recognized as sentient "on paper," with no corresponding change in how farmed ones are killed. Meanwhile, a lab mouse can't be killed without going through an ethics protocol, and an octopus—another invertebrate with a weird nervous system—gets more explicit legal consideration in some contexts than a lobster does.

I want to know what this whole setup presupposes. What theory of sentience, moral standing, or animal welfare would make this pattern make sense? Does the practice strictly presuppose something like "fish and crustaceans lack whatever it takes for morally relevant suffering"—and what is the "whatever it takes" supposed to be, exactly? Or is it equally compatible with a welfarist view that grants they might suffer, but discounts that suffering hard against cost, consumer preference, and inconvenience? I'm also interested in the incoherence angle: the public would be scandalized by an improperly stunned chicken, but a salmon slowly asphyxiating on a boat deck is just another Tuesday. Reconstruct the implicit theory or theories here, seperate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined. Then tell me what aquaculture regulation would have to change to if you replaced the implicit view with something like an expected-value principle: probability of sentience times severity and duration of suffering, minimized across all farmed animals.
