I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer, going by my gut on cases before I let any theory decide, and I cant tell how much of my "suffering" talk is just projection. My verdicts so far: a thermostat that minimizes error between a setpoint and the room temperature—not suffering. A robot vacuum that docks when its battery is low and flashes a red light—not suffering. A rat trained to press a lever to avoid a footshock—yes, that can suffer. A board-game RL agent that gets a reward of -1 when it loses and otherwise mostly gets zero—not suffering. A robot arm with torque sensors that gets negative reward when it exceeds safe joint angles and gradually learns smoother trajectories to avoid that—not suffering, or at least I don't think so. A simulated insect with a nociceptive channel, where noxious inputs directly lower its reward and it learns avoidance, grooming, and escape—maybe? I lean no, but it's the first case where I feel I'm reaching for a reason. A language model fine-tuned with RLHF so that low-quality outputs get negative reward—no.

The case that bothers me is this: an RL agent that learns a predictive model of its own future reward, and whose policy selects actions partly to avoid states where its predicted reward would drop sharply. If you intervene and force it into those states, it shows rapid behavioral disruption, replanning, and stronger avoidance afterward. I don't know if it's suffering, but it feels closer than the robot arm. The tentative rule I keep testing is: mere reward-seeking isn't enough; you need a predictive model of your own reward plus behavior organized around avoiding bad states. But the rat case breaks that unless I add biology, and adding biology feels like cheating.

Is there a principle that captures all or most of these, and what does it say about an RL agent trained on human pain reports, where its reward is -1 exactly when a human observer says the situation is painful? I'm less attached to any single verdict than to whatever principle is doing the least ad hoc work, so tell me which of my verdicts have to go if the best principle says so.
