Lately I've been sorting out what I actually care about when it comes to fakes and originals, and my verdicts don't obviously hang together. Tell me if there's a principle behind them, or if some have to go.

- A friend says she owns an early Van Gogh, and it's beautiful. Then it turns out to be a modern forgery, indistinguishable as far as any expert can see. To me the painting instantly loses nearly all of its value, and I wouldn't want to look at it the same way again — even though nothing visible changed.
- If she'd instead bought an honest, labeled copy of the same painting, made by a living painter, I'd happily hang it in my house. I wouldn't pay much for it, but it wouldn't sicken me the way the forgery did.
- Reading Moby-Dick in a cheap paperback, I never feel I'm missing the original manuscript. But when I once saw a page of the actual manuscript, I stared at it for ages and had a reaction the paperback never caused.
- A recorded song moves me about the same whether it's a lossless file, a vinyl pressing, or a note-for-note cover by a tribute band. But if the recording was passed off as the original band and turned out not to be, I'd feel cheated — even if I couldn't hear any difference.
- A friend bought a 'pre-Columbian' figurine; an expert later says it's two years old. She won't keep it in the house. I'd keep it as a knickknack, but it can never again mean what it did for the afternoon when we both thought it was ancient.
- But here's the one that bugs me: I'll cheerfully carry a knockoff designer bag, knowing it's fake, and I don't think its value to me depends on any original — I just like the way it looks. The forged Van Gogh, though, has almost all of its value for me in something invisible.

Is there one principle (or a small set of them) that gets all these verdicts, and if not, wich should I overturn? Also say what it predicts about a nearby case I haven't listed: an exact copy of Frank Lloyd Wright's Fallingwater, built new with the same materials and proportions on a different stretch of river.
