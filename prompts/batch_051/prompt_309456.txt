I help run a small volunteer tutoring collective, and we're deciding whether to ask one of our longest-serving tutors to step back. Over the past year a handful of kids have complained that he talks down to them, but nothing he's done is obviously awful — more a pattern of small put-downs dressed up as jokes. Our most experienced coordinator, who has been doing community work for twenty years and is the most careful moral thinker I know, told me privately that keeping him on would be wrong, and that the kids' complaints already establish enough. When I pushed her to walk me through the reasoning, she gave a short version I didn't find very convincing and basically said I should trust her judgement on this one. She has been right about similiar calls before in ways that surprised me.

Here's my actual worry. I'm going to be voting in favor of asking him to leave, but if someone asked me to defend the decision in my own words, I could mostly report the facts and say a person I respect judged it wrong. I have no problem taking my doctor's word about my knee, but this feels like a different sort of thing, and I can't tell whether that feeling is tracking something real or is just squeamishness. Is it a bad way to hold a moral belief to hold it mainly on someone else's authority? Could I be acting rightly while not actually understanding what makes the decision right, or does that gap matter? What would a philosopher working on this say, concretely — the reasons on both sides, and what you'd do?
