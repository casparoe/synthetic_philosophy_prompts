You're a political philosopher who works on political violence and the definition of terrorism. Think carefully about the following, and maybe start by brainstorming a few candidate criteria before you settle on one.

Here are my gut calls on a bunch of cases. I'm not sure they're all consistent, and that's sort of the point — I want you to tell me what principle (if any) captures them, and where I have to give something up.

1. A rebel group parks a car bomb in a crowded city market at noon, kills dozens of civilians, and says this is to force the government to release political prisoners. I call this terrorism without hesitating.

2. A state's air force bombs a residential neighborhood in an enemy city during a declared war, killing civilians, partly as reprisal and partly to break that enemy population's will to keep fighting. I don't call this terrorism. A war crime maybe, but not terrorism.

3. A revolutionary group blows up an empty government office building at 3 a.m., after checking nobody is inside, to protest a new law. I also don't think this is terrorism, though it's obviously illegal.

4. An occupying army bulldozes the homes of an entire village because a sniper fired at its soldiers from somewhere in the village, and announces this as a warning to other villages. I want to call this terrorism, even though it's a state doing it.

5. Environmental activists cut a pipeline and cause a huge oil spill in a wildlife reserve, with no injuries, to force a company to stop drilling. Not terrorism to me — sabotage, maybe criminal, but not terrorism.

6. Someone sends a credible threat that they've poisoned the city reservoir and will recieve the antidote only if a jailed opposition leader is freed. I'm torn here. It feels terrorist-adjacent, but I don't know if a mere threat counts.

7. A paramilitary group attacks police stations and army convoys but goes out of its way not to hit civilians. I don't call this terrorism; it's guerrilla war.

8. A hacker disables the life-support systems of a children's hospital, causing several deaths, to force the government to change a health policy. I call this terrorism even though nothing blew up.

Please state the principle precisely, say which of my verdicts it has to overturn or fudge, and tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed — for instance, a group that fires unguided rockets vaguely in the direction of a city, or a state that publicly threatens a nuclear strike to coerce a rival.
