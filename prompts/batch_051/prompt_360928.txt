I've been turning over a parallel and can't decide if its deep or just a pun. Wittgenstein picked "game" as his go-to example of a concept whose instances share only family resemblance: board games, ball games, war games, mind games, no common essence, just overlapping similarities. But game theory gives "game" one of the cleanest definitions around: players, strategies, payoffs, information, and a solution concept. So the very concept he used to show that some concepts resist definition is one that a formal field did manage to define. Is this a genuine problem for family resemblance, or does the mathematical definition just carve off one strand of the family and call it the whole? If game theory had really captured the essence of games, what would ordinary talk about games have to look like for that to be true, and does it actually look that way?
