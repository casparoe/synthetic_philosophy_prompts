I'm a retired biologist and only recently started reading philosophy, and the corner I keep poking at is coincidences and the birthday problem, because I spent my career doing statistics and the two seem made for eachother. I'd like you to work through a modification of the standard birthday puzzle for me.

The usual version: 23 people, uniform independent birthdays over 365 days, about even odds that some pair shares a birthday. Now change the matching rule. Instead of one pre-specified feature with 365 equiprobable values, allow k independent features, where feature j has c_j equiprobable categories, and a "hit" is any pair matching on any feature. To make it concrete but still tractable, use three: birth day-of-year (c = 365), home US state (c = 50), and first initial (c = 26), all treated as uniform and independent, which I know is false for states and initials but grant me the idealization. Derive the probability that in a room of n people there is at least one pair that matches on at least one of the three features, and work out how many people it takes to reach roughly even odds with k = 1 versus with the three features.

But here's what I'm actually after. An anecdote isn't a pre-registered experiment. A friend tells you she met a stranger on a plane and they found out they had the same birthday, same home state, and same first initial — but she would have been just as impressed by matching on any of dozens of other features, and she didn't decide in advance which features counted. In my old world that's a multiple-comparisons problem plus selection after looking at the data. Work through how that post-hoc feature choice changes the calculation, or at least the surprise. If n is really the number of people a sociable person meaningfully interacts with over a lifetime and k is however many features might have struck them, does the birthday-style analysis imply that hearing several anecdotes like this is close to inevitable? And if so, how much should a rational person discount such anecdotes as evidence that something non-chancy is going on? I want the argument and the numbers, not just the vibe.
