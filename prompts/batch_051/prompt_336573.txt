Hey, I'm a science and ideas journalist, currently writng a piece about whether a culture can be valuable beyond the people who carry it — languages, craft traditions, festivals, that kind of thing. The stock positions are easy to state: instrumentalists say a practice is only as good as the benefits it produces for people (identity, social trust, tourism, useful knowledge), while intrinsic-value folks say it can be worth preserving for its own sake even when those benefits are thin. The problem is that every standard example has the intrinsic-value person trying to save the dying tradition and the instrumentalist shrugging. I want the reverse, because I suspect it will force both camps to say something they can't just copy from the usual script.

So construct me a thought experiment in which an instrumentalist about cultural value is the one saying 'keep this practice going,' and an intrinsic value theorist is the one saying 'let it end.' Constraints: the scenario should be one ordinary paragraph (a town, a family, a workshop — no sci-fi); no coercion or state pressure on anyone; every current practitioner would genuinely rather stop, and no young person wants to take the practice up; and yet the mere visible continuation of the practice produces real downstream benefits for people outside it — within the story, both camps accept the evidence for those benefits. Don't stack the deck: the instrumentalist's reasons shouldn't be obviously venal and the intrinsicist's reasons shouldn't be merely sentimental. This is for a section of my piece where I want to show the debate is more interesting than 'save everything' versus 'bean counters.'

Once you've built the case, show your work. First, state what each camp would say about the scenario and why, in wording each side would accept. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — the thing that makes them flip from their usual roles — and what the verdicts would become if you removed that feature. Finally, say which view comes out looking worse, and give the best countermove a defender of that view could make, and whether it actually gets them off the hook or just relabels the problem.
