Here's a hard case about advance directives, and I want an essay that works through it and lands somewhere instead of ending with "it's complicated." A 61-year-old woman writes an advance directive refusing all hand-feeding if she ever has dementia severe enough that she doesnt recognize her adult children; she says in the document that she'd rather die than live like that. Decades later she has exactly that dementia. She doesn't remember writing the document, but when a nurse spoons food into her mouth she eats with obvious relish, asks for pudding, and seems content most days. Her daughter, as surrogate, wants the feeding stopped so the directive is honored, even though stopping will kill her. The care team wants to keep feeding her, on the ground that they'd be starving someone who currently wants to eat. The essay should weigh the arguments for letting the earlier self's instructions bind the later person against the arguments for letting the later self's present preferences rule, then defend a verdict about what the staff should do. Along the way, address this idea: an advance directive is like a constitutional precommitment — the earlier self acts as a kind of legislature, and the later self is stuck with a law she cant repeal because she lacks the capacity to legislate at all. Is that analogy illuminating, or does it smuggle in a picture of two selves that's doing the real work? Also consider the reply that the later self's wellbeing might not be exhausted by present contentment, because a life can be degraded even when the person in it doesn't mind it anymore. Reach a conclusion and justify it.
