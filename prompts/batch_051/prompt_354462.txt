I serve on the doctrine-of-Scripture commission of my denomination, and we must now decide whether a newly completed Bible translation should be recognized as inspired. I want you to recommend the decision that best coheres with our existing rulings, and to say where our precedents leave genuine discretion.

Our track record, in summary:

1. On the Septuagint, we recognized it as inspired Scripture, including passages where its Greek diverges from the Hebrew. The operative reasoning came from Augustine, *The City of God*, Book XVIII, chapter 42. Augustine writes that the Seventy worked separately, yet, in the words our ruling quoted, "as if the translators had been one, so what all had translated was one, because in very deed the one Spirit had been in them all." We concluded that the same Spirit who spoke through the prophets spoke through the translators, so the translation itself is prophetic.

2. On Jerome's Vulgate, we did not recognize it as inspired, though we approved it for liturgical use. Jerome was a scholar comparing texts; he worked alone, with no independently produced agreement and no discernible prophetic charism.

3. On the King James Version, we did not recognize it as inspired, despite its long reception and its authorized use. A faction had petitioned for something like KJV-onlyism, and we rejected it. The translators were learned and appointed by royal commission, but widespread use and excellence do not make a translation inspired. Our ruling said authority flows from the original-language texts, not from a translation committee.

4. On a modern translation by a single scholar who claimed a private sense of divine guidance, we also said no. We required public discernment, and we treated a private claim as insufficient.

Now the new case. A synod of our own denomination commissioned twelve translators, two from each of six language communities, all trained in Hebrew and Greek, to produce the first complete Bible in a creole language. They were assigned books independently. When the drafts were collated, they had independently produced strikingly similar coinages for theological vocabulary, and independently chosen the same renderings in several theologically weighty passages. But they did not produce word-for-word agreement across the whole. They now attribute their convergence to the Holy Spirit, and the commissioning synod asks whether the completed translation should be recognized as inspired.

Assume the translation is accurate and sober. We are asking what follows from our own precedents: identify the candidate rules that fit our past rulings, say what each rule would require in this case, name the closest precedent, and tell us whether our track record forces an answer or underdetermines it. If it underdetermines it, state what principle we would be adopting with each available choice, and what further case would best tell the competing principles apart.

At the very end, give your recommendation in the form \answer{recognize as inspired}, \answer{authorize only}, or \answer{withhold ruling}.
