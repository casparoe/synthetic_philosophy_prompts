I serve on the ethics advisory board of a hospital system that has just begun offering an intracortical brain–computer interface to patients with severe motor impairment. The device streams motor-cortex activity to a speech decoder; to keep latency down it reads continuously rather than waiting for a deliberate send signal, so the stored logs blur together words the patient actually chose to say, sentences drafted and abandoned, and a large volume of neural activity that was never intended as communication at all. Two requests have now reached us. The first is from the insurer in a contested disability case, which wants one patient's logs on the theory that the raw signal may show preserved motor function that the clinical record understates. The second is from our own clinical informatics group, which wants to pool logs across patients to train a better decoder, citing the broad research-use clause in the consent form every patient signed. The board is split between treating the logs as just another clinical record and treating any portion of them as, functionally, thought, and one member keeps arguing that any defensible policy must respect cognitive liberty in the strong sense: a right to decide for oneself which of one's neural states may be read by others at all, not merely a privacy rule governing how data is handled after collection.

I need the short written opinion I will circulate before next week's meeting. It should take a concrete position on which, if any, of these releases should proceed, under what conditions, and what the default rule should be when a third party asks for a patient's neural data. Please engage the strongest version of the permissive position rather than the weakest: that neural data is just data, that the patients did sign a broad consent, and that refusing these uses sacrifices real medical benefit. If the two requests deserve different answers, say so separately. End with an actionable bottom line and immediate next steps, not another framework; we already have frameworks and still cannot decide.
