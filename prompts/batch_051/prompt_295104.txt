I find myself pulled between a theory I am reluctant to abandon and an intuition I cannot talk myself out of, and I want a negotiated verdict rather than a winner.

The theory is the standard possible-worlds treatment of knowledge. Its pull is systematic: if knowing that P is truth throughout the worlds compatible with what the agent knows, then knowledge gets a clean modal logic, a principled account of how individual knowledge composes into common knowledge and constrains strategic reasoning, and a plausible norm — someone who accepts the premises of a valid argument and refuses the conclusion is incoherent, not merely slow. But the same machinery delivers the result usually called logical omniscience. The agent knows every logical truth outright, and whenever what she knows entails something, she knows that as well. An agent who knows the Peano axioms would know every theorem derivable from them, including arbitrarily long derivations no one has actually produced. Consider also a working mathematician who knows the relevant lemmas but has never put them together and has not seen how the deep theorem follows. The theory says she knows the theorem; I feel the force of why it says that.

The intuition that resists is no less firm to me. Knowledge is a standing of a particular, finite reasoner, and it seems wrong to say that mathematician knows the theorem before she has derived it or followed a derivation. Saying she is committed to it, or is in a position to know it, sounds like a different status, and I cannot accept that the latter simply collapses into knowledge because the logic is elegant.

I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives, what it would take for the theory to be the thing that gives, and whether there is a stable reading on which the closure principle governs a different but real normative status — commitment, being positioned to know, an idealization of an ideally responsive agent — while ordinary knowledge ascriptions stay fine-grained. If the theory should be revised rather than discarded, I want to know what a workable revision would have to recover from the simple account and where it silently changes the subject. Whatever verdict you reach, say explicitly which argument or consideration would change your mind, and where you think your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
