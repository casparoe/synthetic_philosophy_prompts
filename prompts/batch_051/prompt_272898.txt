Is there a name for this? I keep noticing a second-order move in how countries deal with past atrocities. A state puts in real, sustained work — apologies, trials, memorials, school curricula — and by most measures it works: the country starts to pride itself on having faced the past more honestly than anyone else. Then that pride becomes the thing doing the work. In present-day arguments, the fact of having remembered well gets used as a moral credential: proof that this country's judgment about other countries' sins is especially authoritative, or a shield when someone raises new criticisms at home. The memory work stops being a debt and becomes a title to deference, and often the people most invested in anniversaries and monuments are the least interested in funding actual repair. I have seen versions of it in how people talk about postwar Germany, and in a different key in Japan, Turkey, and the American South. Is there a term for this specific inversion, where successful remembrance becomes its own object? If not, how would you draw the line between healthy memory work and this self-congratulatory variant, and where does it tip over? I care less about a label than about the carving.
