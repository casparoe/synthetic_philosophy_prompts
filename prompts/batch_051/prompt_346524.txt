Been chewing on a little theory I call the justice ratchet, and I'd be curious what you think. The claim is that history dosn't actually move toward justice in any steady way, but it has an asymmetry: once a moral demand gets truly institutionalized — abolition, women's suffrage, the official ban on torture as a matter of law rather than practice — it becomes much harder to undo than it was to establish, because the beneficiaries get standing, write themselves into the culture and the legal system, and any rollback has to justify itself against a now-public standard. So the long run looks like a ratchet: backsliding happens, but wins stick more than losses, so you get a net forward drift without anyone needing to get better. I want an honest assessment: is there a real mechanism here or is it just a metaphor doing the work? Has someone already worked this out under another name (I think I've seen the phrase moral ratchet but don't know if it's the same idea)? Where does it break — are there reversals or stuck-losing cases it just can't handle, and am I fooling myself by only counting the wins that stuck?
