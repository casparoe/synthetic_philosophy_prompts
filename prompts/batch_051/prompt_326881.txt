I keep tripping over an asymetry in how people think about having kids. If you knew a child would be born into a life that was mostly suffering, basically everyone treats that as a decisive reason not to have them — creating them would be harming them. But if you knew a child would have a mostly happy life, almost no one treats that as a reason in favor of having them. You aren't doing the future kid a favor by creating them, and it isn't like the kid misses out if you don't. So the very same act of bringing a person into existence gets weighted by its possible downside but not its possible upside. Can you find a principle that justifies that, or is it just a bug in ordinary moral thought? If the asymmetry is real, are we commited to thinking it would be better if no one had kids?
