I've come to accept metaphysical foundationalism about grounding: the relation of one fact or entity obtaining or existing in virtue of another is well-founded, so every chain of dependence terminates in something ungrounded, and the ungrounded is what is fundamental. I want an honest map of what would count against this. What would the structure of dependence have to be like for the view to be false or in need of revision — an infinite descending chain with no bottom, a closed loop of mutual dependence, a groundless item that is not fundamental, or a natural world with no bottom level? For each, say whether it is a live possibility or merely a logical one, and what a defender of well-foundedness should themselves concede as a genuine defeater rather than dismiss as a change of subject. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and then assess whether the position has been qualified enough to survive almost any structure — and if it has, whether that insulation is a problem for a metaphysical thesis of this kind.
