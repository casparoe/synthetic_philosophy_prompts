I keep finding that my judgments about how our two-person shared household should decide contested matters are governed by two principles I can hardly imagine giving up: each person has final say over what they watch privately in their own room, and when both of us prefer one option to another we should choose it. But the following case has thrown me, and I want to know which of my judgments has to go.

We have access to a sexually explicit film. The options are: Mara watches it privately, Nico watches it privately, or neither watches it. Mara prefers neither watches it, then Mara watching it, then Nico watching it. Nico prefers Mara watching it, then Nico watching it, then neither watching it. My verdicts are therefore: between Mara watching and neither watching, Mara decides, so neither wins; between Nico watching and neither watching, Nico decides, so Nico watching wins; and between Mara watching and Nico watching, both prefer Mara watching, so Mara watching wins.

But those three pairwise verdicts give a cycle: Mara watching beats Nico watching, Nico watching beats neither, and neither beats Mara watching. What principle captures the non-cycling part of my thinking, and exactly which clause forces which verdict? Please state the candidate repairs, tell me honestly which of my principles or verdicts I must abandon, and say what each candidate predicts for a variant where Mara is indifferent to whether Nico watches it. If the alternative is to keep every pairwise verdict and drop only the assumption that the household's choices must be orderable, tell me what that move amounts to and whether it is a stable place to stand.
