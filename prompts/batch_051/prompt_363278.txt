I recently moved for my wife's job to a small town where meat and fish are the cheap, local protein and beans and lentils are imported and expensive, and it's forced me to rethink a bunch of animal-ethics views I used to just hold by default. I want to figure out what my actual principle is from my gut calls on cases, so I'd like you to extract it, or tell me which of my verdicts have to go.

Cases:

1. Me now: I buy local chicken and fish for our toddler because it's half the price of the imported lentils and I dont want to experiment with her nutrition. Fine, though I'm not proud of it.

2. Our neighbor, a herder on land that can't grow crops, eats goat meat and dairy as his main food. Fine — there's no realistic alternative there.

3. My brother back home has a good income and five vegan restaurants within walking distance, but still eats factory-farmed bacon most days because he likes it. Not fine.

4. A tourist came through town and ended up staying a month. She ate fish every day because she hadn't brought food and there wasn't much else to buy. I go back and forth on this one — I think eating the fish was probably fine, but I also think she should have planned better.

5. At a memorial, my mother-in-law made a pork dish and handed it to me; refusing would have genuinely hurt grieving relatives. I ate it. I think that was right all things considered, but I still think the animal part mattered.

6. A friend back home went vegan but will eat roadkill deer he finds himself, on the grounds that he didn't cause the death and eating it doesn't create demand. I think that's fine.

7. A visiting vlogger filmed locals and lectured them about drinking cow's milk, in a village where kids rely on it for calories. The villagers drinking the milk are fine; the vlogger is not fine.

8. At a restaurant on the coast I ate oysters on the reasoning that they probably don't feel pain, but I wasn't confident, and if I'm unsure I usually just eat plant-based anyway. I want to know whether that one is consistent with the rest.

My rough idea is something like: it's about whether you had a real alternative and whether the choice creates or reduces harm, where "real alternative" includes money, nutrition, and maybe serious social cost. But I can't get that to handle the funeral case and the tourist case at the same time.

So: is there one principle that captures all or most of these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? And what does it say about a case I haven't listed: a working parent I've met here who can afford the imported beans and tofu but usually eats chicken on exhausted weeknights becuase legumes take planning he doesn't have?
