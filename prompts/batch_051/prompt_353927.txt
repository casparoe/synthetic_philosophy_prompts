I keep noticing a pattern in how people judge small promises, and I want to know whether it has a name, or whether I'm forcing several different things into one category.

Most of the commitments in an ordinary week are nearly weightless: I'll send you the file tonight, I'll call you back in ten minutes, I'll be there around six. When one of them is broken, the practical harm is usually tiny. Nothing important was riding on it, and whatever was lost is easy to recover. Yet the reaction people have to these small breaches feels out of proportion to the harm. The smaller and more optional the promise, the more the resentment seems to target the person rather than the consequence. If a friend is twenty minutes late when nothing depended on it, I am not really angry about the twenty minutes; I am drawing conclusions about whether she treats my time as real, and I notice myself using that to lower how much I would trust her in a matter that actually mattered. The odd thing is that the triviality is doing the work. Precisely because there is no real penalty and no practical reason to keep a small promise, keeping it seems to reveal more about someone than honoring an agreement that would be costly to break. The breach is cheap, so the fidelity reads as a kind of free signal.

But the same asymmetry works from the other side too. The person who broke the small promise often can't make sense of the reaction: it was only fifteen minutes, nothing was lost, so why treat it as a test of character? And I half-sympathize, because I also know people who are constantly late, or who casually overpromise little things out of disorganization or optimism, yet who are entirely reliable when something important is at stake. So the inference from a small lapse to a judgment about reliability is sometimes fair and sometimes feels like a distortion, taking ordinary human scatter and reading it as disrespect.

What I want to know is this: Is there an established name or analysis for this inversion, where trivial commitments carry moral weight not through their consequences but through what they are taken to reveal about a person's reliability or regard? I'm not after a bare label like trust or character. I want to understand exactly what the phenomenon is and what it is not: how it differs from ordinary promise-breaking and from mere etiquette, when a small lapse really does justify a conclusion about someone's reliability and when it doesn't, and where the boundaries of the idea are. If nothing established corresponds to it, I'd like a proposed name with a clear definition and boundary cases. And if I've run together several different things and there is no single phenomenon here to identify, please say that plainly rather than giving me a label for the sake of an answer.
