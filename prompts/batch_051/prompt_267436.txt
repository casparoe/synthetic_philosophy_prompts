I am a professor of political philosophy preparing to give a paper at a law-and-political-theory workshop. The paper defends the following thesis: when a constitutional order reaches a genuine normative gap—for example, the constitutional text provides no rule for succession, every official named by the rules as a successor is simultaneously unavailable, and no settled convention has formed to cover the case—the interim authority does not revert to a state of nature, and it does not automatically pass to the military or to whichever faction can claim popular acclamation; instead, the least-disruptive continuing institution within the existing order acts as a temporary fiduciary, and the ground of its authority is not a hidden rule waiting inside the constitution but a forward-looking duty to protect the order from the greater harms of a vacuum. My argument in sketch: the gap is a real normative condition, not merely epistemic uncertainty about what the rules already require; continuity by the least-disruptive existing officials is justified by the same considerations that justify ordinary political authority, above all the avoidance of the predictable costs of coordination failure; but the justification is provisional and does not by itself license permanent constitutional revision. The audience will be about twenty-five people: constitutional lawyers, political scientists who study democratic breakdowns, and a few legal philosophers whose instincts are broadly positivist or Schmittian. They are likely to think I am smuggling morality into the place where law runs out. Prepare me for the question period. Order the objections they will actually raise by how likely and how damaging they are, state them in the vocabulary that audience would use, give a short reply to each, and indicate honestly where that reply is weakest. Include the objection I should most hope nobody asks, and say whether I should concede it partially, deflect it, or meet it head-on. The deliverable is a prep sheet, not a full defense of the thesis.
