For a graduate seminar I am teaching on Quine's critique of analyticity, I have written down my own commitments and I want them stress-tested before I circulate them to the class. Here they are as they stand.

(A) I think there is no principled analytic/synthetic distinction. The notion of cognitive synonymy needed to draw the distinction is as obscure as the distinction itself, and the usual attempts to ground it, in definition or in interchangeability salva veritate, are circular in the way Quine says.

(B) I accept confirmational holism: empirical content attaches to whole theories, not to individual sentences, and no sentence has its own separable empirical meaning in isolation from its surroundings.

(C) I accept the revisability corollary: no statement is immune to revision in principle, including statements of logic, if the pressure from the rest of the theory is severe enough.

(D) I nonetheless think deductive logic has a special status. Revision of a theory is itself reasoning, so the soundness of modus ponens, or of some core deductive system, is presupposed by any revision procedure; it is not merely one more revisable node in the web.

(E) I think there is a real, if vague, distinction between changing one's beliefs and changing one's meanings, and it is needed to describe episodes such as the historical shift from Euclidean to non-Euclidean geometry as a change in belief rather than a redefinition of the terms.

An audit is what I need, not a general commentary. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be in tension. For the genuine tensions, order them from most to least severe, say how severe each is, spell out what would have to be given up or qualified, and what the cheapest repair would cost. Then give me the single overall revision you would recommend.

Please return this as a JSON object with the following schema:

- "genuine_tensions": an array, ordered most severe first, whose elements have the fields "commitments" (an array of one-letter labels, e.g. ["A", "E"]), "severity" (one of "severe", "moderate", "mild"), "diagnosis" (a string), "what_must_give" (a string), and "cheapest_fix" (a string).
- "apparent_only_tensions": an array whose elements have the fields "commitments" (an array of labels), "why_it_seems_in_tension" (a string), and "why_it_dissolves" (a string).
- "overall_recommended_revision": an object with the fields "change" (a string specifying the single qualification or change that would do the most to make the set coherent) and "justification" (a one-sentence string).
