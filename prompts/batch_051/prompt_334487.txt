I'm writing a short paper for a philosophy of mind seminar and I want a consistency check on my position before I show it to anyone. The topic is the colour phi phenomenon and the timing of conscious experience. My understanding of the phenomenon, in case it matters: a red dot flashes briefly on the left; after a short gap a green dot flashes on the right; observers report seeing a single dot move from left to right and change from red to green around the middle of its path. Since the green flash happens after the moment where the dot reportedly looks green, this creates a puzzle about when the subject becomes conscious of the green. Two standard ways of talking about it: the Orwellian route, where the brain revises the record after the second flash (hence the name), and the Stalinesque route, where conscious experience is delayed relative to the stimulus, so by the time an experience is assembled the second flash is already included and there never was an unrevised experience to edit.

Here are the commitments I'm trying to hold all at once:

(a) Physicalism: consciousness is nothing over and above brain processes.
(b) There is a determinate fact of the matter about the timing of conscious experiences; the question "when did the person become conscious of the green dot?" has a correct answer in principle, even if its hard to measure in practice.
(c) The Orwellian and Stalinesque stories are genuine rivals: one of them is the better description of colour phi, and its an empirical question that neuroscience could in principle settle.
(d) Colour phi is best explained as the brain constructing a unified motion-and-colour representation, and then retrospectively editing the earlier parts of the experience once the second flash has arrived, so that the dot is already green at the midpoint. (I think this is the Orwellian option.)
(e) Introspection about my own experience is broadly trustworthy: if I seem to see the dot turn green at the midpoint, that is decent evidence about the content and timing of my experience.
(f) There is no Cartesian theatre: the brain is massively parallel and distributed, with no single place or moment at which everything comes together for the subject.

I can't tell which of these are actually in tension and which only look that way. Go through the pairs, rank the real tensions by how severe they are, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost. Then tell me whether there's a single revision that would clean up the most without gutting the position. Please write the answer in British English.
