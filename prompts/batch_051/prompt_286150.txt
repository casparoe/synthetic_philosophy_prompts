for my philosophy of probability reading group, about 12 undergrads — most have seen sequences and series but no measure theory — i want a 90-minute session on the pasadena paradox, run as a game rather than a lecture, and they shouldnt hear the name 'pasadena' until the debrief at the end.

here's the game the activity should be built on: a fair coin is tossed until it lands heads. if the first heads is on toss n, the payoff is (−1)^(n−1) · 2^n / n dollars. so first heads on toss 1 pays +$2, toss 2 pays −$2, toss 3 pays +$8/3, toss 4 pays −$4, toss 5 pays +$32/5, toss 6 pays −$32/3, and so on: the signs alternate and the magnitudes grow fast.

the activity should split them into small syndicates that each have to price this game for a risk-neutral player: what is the most you'd pay to play once? give different syndicates different valuation methods. one computes the expected-value terms in toss order — the alternating harmonic series — and watches its partial sums approach ln 2 ≈ 0.69. another separates the positive and negative terms and sums each pile first: one pile runs off to +∞ and the other to −∞. a third actually plays the game many times with play money and averages the realized payouts; a fourth maybe prices it by the size of the swings, or caps the number of tosses at some large n. the goal is for the groups to get incompatible answers and then have to defend them to each other, so the paradox shows up as a real disagreement among valuation methods before any theory is named.

then, the debrief: it should show that the expected value is a conditionally convergent series, and that its value depends on the order you add the terms in — so the game has no well-defined expectation, not an infinite one. it should also pull out what the simulation desk will have felt: even repeated play has infinite variance, so averaging realized payouts never settles down either. no lecture up front; let the disagreement run for a while first.

also say what will predictably go wrong when this is run — e.g. people concluding 'undefined expectation' means 'game is worthless' or 'nobody should pay anything', or one desk getting stuck summing by hand — and how the facilitator should handle it. and be honest about what the activity can't reproduce: nobody actually plays infinitely many rounds, so the infinities are felt as wild disagreement and swings, not as literal divergence; the facilitator has to supply the link between a risk-neutral fair price and the order in which you sum expected-value terms.
