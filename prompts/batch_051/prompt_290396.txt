I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on telepresence, and I want to understand how a change in the timing of a brain–body interface alters Dennett's "Where Am I?" puzzle. In the original essay, Dennett's brain is removed and kept alive in a vat, while his body, connected to the brain by a radio link, travels elsewhere to recover a warhead; the puzzle is to say where Dennett is. On one influential reading, he is wherever the system's perceptual and agential perspective is located, and this need not coincide with either the brain or the body.

Vary one parameter: latency. Suppose the engineers insert a buffer into the radio link that delays every signal in each direction by 400 milliseconds, uniformly and predictably, so that sensory channels from body to brain and motor channels from brain to body are each delayed by the same amount. Now suppose that, while his brain sits in its vat, Dennett's body spends the afternoon playing table tennis. The ball he sees is always 400 ms behind the ball that is actually there, and the commands his brain issues reach his arm 400 ms after he issues them. A successful return therefore requires him to issue movements aimed at where the ball will be roughly 800 ms beyond the position he currently perceives; he must treat his own limbs as lagged devices rather than as immediately responsive parts of himself.

Does the point-of-view analysis still locate Dennett cleanly in this variant, and if so, where is he? More specifically: when perception and action are systematically desynchronized, is there still a single, well-defined point of view that can be said to be his? Does it remain with the body at the table, with the brain in the vat, or is it spread across two temporal moments? Compare the latency case with the original and say which considerations—causal continuity, the locus of experience, or the locus of agency—should carry the most weight in settling where Dennett is.
