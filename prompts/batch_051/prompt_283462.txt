I am a retired natural scientist—geology was my field—and in the last few years I have turned to philosophy with the kind of intensity I used to reserve for fieldwork. I have been asked to present a paper to our university's interdisciplinary philosophy of mind seminar. The regulars include philosophers with strongly naturalistic commitments, two cognitive scientists, and a neuroscientist who generally treats historical metaphysics as either a source of testable hypotheses or a cautionary tale. My paper defends a deliberately narrow version of Schopenhauer's claim about the body as the one object given to us from the inside, not merely as representation but also as will. The thesis, stated plainly, is that in voluntary action I have an immediate, non-observational acquaintance with my own agency that no third-person functional or neural description captures. The argumentative core rests on a passage early in Book II of The World as Will and Idea, where Schopenhauer, in the Haldane and Kemp translation, argues that the act of will and the movement of the body are not two distinct states connected by causality but are one and the same event given in two fundamentally different modes, first from within and then as outer perception. I am not defending the whole system: not the cosmic will, not the pessimism, not the denial of the will to life. I am defending only the narrower epistemic claim as a datum that contemporary accounts of agency must either accommodate or explain away.

Prepare me for the question period. What will this audience object to, in their own vocabulary? Which objections have real force, and which are merely deflationary? What should I concede up front so that I do not appear to be smuggling in a full metaphysics of the will? And what is the one reply that has to land for the paper to survive? I am particularly worried that someone will press me on whether the felt character of agency is evidence about the nature of the body rather than merely evidence about how the brain models its own motor commands.
