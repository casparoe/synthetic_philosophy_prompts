I keep running into this on rationalist-ish forums: someone reads Parfit, then discovers the Buddhist no-self stuff, and for about two weeks theyre convinced the two traditions are saying the same thing in different words. Then someone points out that the lamp simile in the Milindapañha, which looks like it could have come out of Reasons and Persons, actually says something Parfit never quite does. Nāgasena has King Milinda imagine a lamp burning all night: the flame in the first watch is not the same flame as the flame in the last watch, yet we still say the same lamp has been burning; the flame is continuously arising without any stable flame enduring from watch to watch. He then applies this to a person continuing from one time to another: neither the same person nor a different one, just an ongoing process. Everyone on both sides agrees about flames, memory, causal continuity, all that. But the debate between anatta/no-self people and Parfit-style reductionists has not moved in decades, and most of the arguments on both sides are now predictable. Half the time it looks like they are each shadowboxing a soul-substance view that the other side already rejected. My working theory is that the disagreement is mostly about what the word self is doing, not about whether some hidden entity exists: the Buddhist line is near error theory about a way of taking yourself as a persisting centre, whereas Parfit is doing ontology and then trying to keep ethics and planning intact. But I worry that's too neat, because plenty of people on each side deny it and say the other side sneaks a real self back in through continuity or karma or "what matters." Diagnose the impasse properly. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two different questions under one heading? What are the actual sources of the standoff: different starting intuitions about identity, different standards for when a person exists, different background assumptions about whether a relational process can have the kind of importance each side is protecting? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, a formal model, a clarification of the question, or an honest admission that these projects don't have to converge? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of both views.
