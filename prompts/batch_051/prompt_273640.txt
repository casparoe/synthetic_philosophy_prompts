My housemate has an ant farm and has lately started calling the colony Aunt Hillary and insisting that the colony itself has a mind. Individually the ants are obviously dumb, they follow pheromone trails and bump antennae and then do whatever the local rules say. But the colony as a whole does things that look almost deliberate: a scout finds a better nest site, recruits a few others, they somehow "decide," and by morning the entire colony has moved with the queen in the middle. When I mess up one of their trails they don't just mill around, they reallocate workers and fix the route.

He says if you replace "neuron" with "ant" and "action potential" with "pheromone," the colony is basically a brain, and refusing to call it a mind is just chauvinism about it being spread across many small bodies. I say my phone spreads information across many small components too, and I don't owe it a mind. He wants to settle it empirically: he proposes a setup where two groups of foragers learn different halves of a fact (one group learns which tunnel leads to food, the other learns that the food tunnel only opens when it's humid), the two groups never interact, and then we see whether the colony can act on the combined information. He thinks that would be the "whole knowing more than any ant." I think it would just be trail-laying and evaporation doing arithmetic.

I'm honestly not sure his claim is even testable. Design the best test you can of the claim that an ant colony has (or lacks) a mind. Be concrete about what you'd manipulate or measure, say exactly what result would push you toward each reading, and then say what a positive result would still leave open. Are the two readings, genuine colony-level thinking versus a pile of local rules that sum to something clever, separable by experiment at all, or is part of the dispute just a choice about where to draw the line? And is whatever's left over a limit of the experiments or a defect in the concept of "having a mind"?
