Here's an asymmetry I keep running into and I'm not sure it's justified. When the Romans deified a dead emperor, or the ancient Egyptians treated a living pharaoh as divine, or an ancestor cult turned an actual grandparent into a being you leave offerings to, people file it under normal, well-documented history. But say the same kind of thing about Zeus or Indra—that they were once local chiefs or warriors and the stories just snowballed—and people act like you've proposed something unserious. Both are the same claim, roughly: a person became a god. So what exactly is doing the work in the difference? I'm not asking for a rundown of the historical evidence for specific cases. I want the strongest principle you can find that separates the respectable cases from the fringe-sounding one (maybe it's about evidence, about what kind of god the person became, about how far back in time, whatever), then test that principle hard, and tell me honestly whether we should keep the asymmetry or give it up.
