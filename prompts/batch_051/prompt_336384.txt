I'm in a physics and philosophy club at school, and we've spent the last two meetings arguing about Maxwell's demon. The setup is the usual one: a tiny demon opens a trapdoor and lets fast molecules go one way and slow molecules the other, and it looks like he's getting a temperature difference (so usable work) for free, which would break the second law. Our club advisor, who's a proper physicist, says the real fix is that the demon has to keep track of which molecules are which, and erasing those measurement records eventually costs entropy. Half of us still think that just pushes the problem into the demon's head without settling it.

For our last meeting I want to close with a poem that actually does the argument rather than just decorating it. Please write a sonnet about Maxwell's demon. The octave should build the naive picture: the demon, the door, the fast molecules accumulating on one side, no apparent cost anywhere. I know its a lot to fit into fourteen lines, but I'd like the volta to be the turn from the gas to the demon's memory or measurement records, and the closing couplet to say exactly what has to be given up to keep the second law safe — the Landauer erasure point, not just the vague line that information is physical. It doesn't have to follow a specific rhyme scheme, but it should be a recognisable sonnet, and it should be accurate enough that our club advisor wouldn't make a face at it.

Format: first a section called "The poem" with the sonnet itself, then a section called "Commentary" of about 150–200 words explaining the choices, what the form is doing at the turn, and where the poem simplifies the physics. Please write in British English.
