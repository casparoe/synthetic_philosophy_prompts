I'm a novelist, and I've been sketching a near-future world where televised arguments are scored like figure skating: each side has compulsory elements (restate the opponent's view until they nod, concede the strongest point against you), a difficulty mark for how many live objections you take on, and an execution mark for avoiding fallacies, with a mixed panel of judges and no audience applause allowed. I have a hunch this would make public reasoning worse at tracking truth, not better, but I want the strongest version of that case rather than my hunch. Please argue for that position: that turning argument into a scored, sport-like performance would degrade its ability to get at the truth, even though unstructured debate is already a mess. Push on the incentive structure — what gets selected for and what gets killed off — and on where the sports analogy breaks down, since unlike a ball going in a goal, there's no independent winning condition and the judges' verdicts are themselves just more arguments nobody is scoring. Dont hedge: give me the strongest anti-sport case you can.
