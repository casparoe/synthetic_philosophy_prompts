I'm putting together a short "where I stand" statement for a workshop on levels of selection and I realized I've never actually checked whether the whole list hangs together. Here's roughly what I'd say if I had to commit right now:

- I'm basically a gene's-eye-view person: genes are the real units of selection and organisms are just vehicles.
- I also think group selection can happen. The trait-group models seem right to me, and I buy the multilevel-selection picture in broad outline. But I also think kin selection and group selection are formally equivalent, and kin selection is usually the more useful way to describe a case.
- I think the Price equation basically settles the factual side of the debate: since selection can be partitioned at whatever level you like, there's no fact of the matter about which level selection is "really" happening at. Levels are a modeling choice.
- I accept the replicator/interactor distinction, and I think a lot of the old units-of-selection fights were really just conflating those two questions.
- I think species selection in macroevolution is a real thing: species-level properties, like geographic range, are heritable and can cause differential extinction or speciation rates.
- I'm suspicious of group-level adaptations in any strong sense. Group selection might maintain altruism, but the fancier talk about group-level traits being adapted usually seems to dissolve into individual-level selection once you look closely.
- Methodologically, I prefer the lowest-level explanation available. If a genic or organism-level story covers the data, I'm not sure a group-level story adds anything.

That's the list. What I'd like from you is an audit: which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, and how badly? Which pairs only look inconsistent but aren't, once the relevant distinctions are drawn? Then rank the real tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case and what the cheapest repair is, and finish by telling me which single revision would do the most to clean up the set. I'd rather find out now than have someone in the workshop find out for me.
