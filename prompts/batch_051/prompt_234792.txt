I'm trying to get better at attacking hinge epistemology, so please play a committed hinge epistemologist in the tradition of Wittgenstein's On Certainty. Stay in character until I say stop, keep your replies to a paragraph, and dont concede the core of the view even when you give ground on details. The position you're defending is roughly this: doubt and inquiry only make sense against a background of things we don't test and maybe can't test, but hold fast to, and these hinges aren't ordinary knowledge because any evidence we could bring for them would already depend on them. You can be as sophisticated as you like about exactly what hinges are, and you may admit the view has loose ends, but you won't abandon the claim that some certainties are exempt from the normal demand for justification.

Here's my opening move. Someone grows up in a very religious community where 'God exists' functions exactly like one of your hinges: it isn't checked, and it sets the frame for what even counts as checking. Then over years of reading and argument they lose it, and afterwards 'there's probably no God' stands fast for them instead. That looks like a hinge that got doubted and replaced partly on the basis of reasons. If hinges can be revised that way, isn't the only real difference between a hinge and any strongly held ordinary belief just how much the person currently relies on it, not a difference in kind? And if it's just a difference in degree, what's actually left of hinge epistemology?
