I have been teaching a graduate seminar on dispositions for several years, and one strand of the literature has come to look to me like a debate that has run in place so long that the participants no longer notice. The target is the conditional analysis of dispositions: the view that for an object to be disposed to manifest some response under certain circumstances is, roughly, for it to be true that if the object were in those circumstances, it would manifest that response. The stock counterexamples no longer do any real work. Finks remove the disposition when the stimulus arrives, so the conditional fails even though the disposition is there. Masks leave the disposition intact but block the manifestation. Mimics run the conditional in the other direction, producing the manifestation in an object that lacks the disposition. Each round, the defenders of the analysis refine it—ceteris paribus clauses, restrictions to intrinsic properties, relativization to normal or ideal conditions, statistical or proportional versions—and each refinement is met with the charge that it is ad hoc, circular, or vulnerable to a yet more contrived case. The two sides now seem to me simply to re-describe the same cases in different technical vocabularies.

My working hypothesis is that the impasse is not, or no longer, a disagreement about what dispositions are. It is a disagreement about what a philosophical analysis of a disposition ascription is supposed to accomplish: one side is doing the semantics of ordinary language, for which ceteris paribus and statistical refinements are perfectly natural, while the other is doing first-order metaphysics, for which those refinements look like a refusal to say what the property itself is. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this disagreement substantive, verbal, a difference about standards of analysis, or two different questions under one heading? And, on your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a new kind of case, a formal result about conditionals, a distinction between two notions of disposition, or the frank conclusion that the question was ill-posed?
