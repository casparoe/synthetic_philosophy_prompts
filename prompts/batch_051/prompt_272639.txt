I've been going round in circles with a few people who read the same blogs and forums I do about whether cheap griefbots would be catastrophic or mostly fine, and I want someone to actually commit instead of giving me a long list of ways it could go. So here is a concrete counterfactual.

From the 1st of January next year, a service becomes available that, from a dead person's texts, emails, voice notes, social posts and photos, produces a chatbot that in short written exchanges (under about twenty messages) the bereaved can't reliably tell from the person they lost writing to them. It isnt generally intelligent: it has no new memories, it starts repeating itself after a few months, and it would be useless as a colleague or advisor. The law hasn't caught up and won't constrain it for at least the first five years. It costs about the same as a streaming subscription and needs no technical skill to set up, so take-up is fast.

Walk me through the next fifteen years for mourning, close relationships, funerary and memorial practices, how people manage their digital estates, and the incentives of the companies selling the service. One thing I keep coming back to: some writers on grief claim healthy mourning is about maintaining a continuing bond with the dead, while others think it requires accepting the finality of the loss and gradually detaching. The availability of this service seems like it would change which of those pictures is more workable in practice, if either is. Predict what actually happens. Which of your predictions are you most confident in and why? Which ones quietly depend on how grief actually works? For each major link in the chain, say what would have to be true for you to be wrong. Dont lay out the options; make the calls.
