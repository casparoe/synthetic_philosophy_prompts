I have been trying to think carefully about what is lost when communities fall apart — a neighborhood turns over, a congregation shrinks, a union local folds, an online forum goes quiet — and I keep getting stuck at the very beginning, because I cannot say exactly what I mean by "community." I would like a working definition of the word that sorts the following cases correctly: it should take in the cases I am confident are communities, keep out the cases I am confident are not, and give a reasoned verdict on the cases in between.

The cases I am confident about are these. A small church congregation that worships together every week and organizes meals for sick members is a community. A support group for people with the same illness that meets every month and stays in touch between meetings is a community. An extended family spread across four countries that talks constantly and sends money when someone is in trouble is a community. By contrast, the residents of a large apartment building who share a lobby and a laundry room but do not know one another's names are not a community, and the holders of the same passport are not a community.

The cases I cannot decide on are these: the regulars at a café who greet one another by name but have never met outside it; a tightly coordinated team at work whose members cover for one another but rarely spend time together after hours; the mostly anonymous users of a very large online forum devoted to a single hobby; a fandom with no central gathering place but a strong shared attachment to the same work; a small rural town where everyone knows everyone but people mostly keep to themselves; and an estranged family whose members do not speak but would still show up and help in a genuine emergency.

Part of my difficulty is that the word "community" sometimes seems to be a plain description of how people are connected — regular, patterned interaction and some sense of a shared "us" — and sometimes seems to carry a standard of mutual care and obligation. I would like you to address that directly. Give me whichever definitions are needed — one if the word really is unified, more than one if it is not — then run every case through the definitions, and say clearly which cases change their classification depending on which definition is in play. Finally, say which sense of the word is doing the real work when people claim that the decline of community is a loss worth grieving.
