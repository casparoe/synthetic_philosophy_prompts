Write an essay on the controversy over whether evolution by natural selection should be classified as an optimizer, and on what gradient-based optimization—the kind used to train neural networks—can and cannot genuinely learn from it.

In the course of the essay, address the following. First, distinguish optimizing a fixed objective function, set in advance and held constant, from merely producing successive variants that are fitter than their predecessors under whatever conditions happen to obtain; make clear which claim is at stake when people say that selection optimizes fitness. Second, lay out the strongest reasons for thinking natural selection is not a genuine optimizer: it receives no gradient signal, it does not assign credit for an individual trait's contribution to overall fitness, the fitness landscape is frequency-dependent and environment-relative, and the target can shift as the population itself alters the environment. Third, consider the reply that these defects show only that evolution is a stupid optimizer—one that works without gradient information, foresight, or credit assignment—rather than no optimizer at all, and that gradient descent displays analogous defects, including local search, no guarantee of locating a global optimum, and sensitivity to the shape of the landscape. Fourth, judge what gradient-based learners can and cannot responsibly borrow from natural selection, such as population diversity, stochastic exploration, and search that does not require a differentiable objective, and identify where the analogy between the two processes does genuine explanatory work and where it is merely decorative.

Reach a definite, justified conclusion about the controversy, and write the essay in American English.
