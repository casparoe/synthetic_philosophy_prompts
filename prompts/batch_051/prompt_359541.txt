So our student council constitution has this one line: 'The Student Council may pass no rule except by the consent of the governed.' Last week the council voted 12-5 to take half the money we raised for prom and lock it in a fund that stays untouched until 2060, when it gets handed to the students of this school. The rep who pushed it is really into longtermism (the idea that future people count as much as us, and since there are way more future classes than there are of us, their needs should win, especially with climate change). But the future students never consented. I don't think the kids who wrote the clause in 2009 were thinking about people who aren't born yet. I'm supposed to figure out wether the council broke the rule, and for that I need to weigh four things: what the words literally say, what the point of the clause was, what the 2009 writers would have said if someone asked them about 2060, and what a reasonable student reading it now would think it means. Which of those should actually decide? Also, if the governed can't consent because they don't exist yet, is that the same as not having the consent the rule asks for, or does the clause just fall silent?
