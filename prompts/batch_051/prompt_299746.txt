I'm on the commitee of a hiking club that's been around since the early seventies, and we're having a stupid but fascinating fight about one of our oldest rules. Section 6 of the constitution says: 'Every member shall, in each calendar year, complete at least one journey on foot, by bicycle, or by water to a locality which that member has not previously visited, and shall report it to the Secretary before the thirty-first of December; a member who fails to do so shall cease to be an Active Member.' The preamble says the club exists to keep its members from sinking into a repetitive, unadventurous life, and Section 6 was clearly written as the enforcement mechanism.

The fight is about two members. Marie was housebound for most of the year after a rough surgery, so she logged several 'journeys' done entirely in virtual reality: she learned to sea-kayak, 'paddled' a stretch of Greenland coast she had never seen except in the headset, and did a storm crossing that she says genuinely terrified her. Leo, who thinks the rule is nonsense, took a thirty-five-minute train to a commuter town he'd never got off at before, walked to a supermarket and back, and reported that as his new locality. Some of us feel Marie is exactly the person the rule exists for, because she was the one dreading a year with nothing new in it; others feel she never actually went anywhere, while Leo technically did.

The people who drafted this in 1971 plainly weren't thinking about virtual worlds, and probably not about supermarket walks either. I'm the Secretary and I have to rule on whether either of them stays an active member, and I don't want to just go on vibes. Could you work through what the words of Section 6 give, what the stated purpose gives, what the 1971 drafters would have said if asked, and what a reasonable member would understand the rule to mean? Which approach should win here, and does your answer say anything about whether the novelty that matters for a good life is something out in the world or something in the person's experience?
