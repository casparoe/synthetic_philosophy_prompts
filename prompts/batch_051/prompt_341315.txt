I am a high school student writing an essay for a philosophy club, and I should like you to construct a thought experiment about the problem of old evidence, engineered so that two measures of confirmation come apart. The first measure is strict Bayesian conditionalisation: evidence E confirms a hypothesis H only if P(H given E) is greater than P(H). The second measure is a counterfactual likelihood-ratio measure: E confirms H when the ratio of P(E given H) to P(E given not-H), computed against a background that deliberately excludes E, is greater than 1. Please build a case in which a piece of evidence was known long before anyone formulated a hypothesis, the evidence plainly seems to support the hypothesis once it is proposed, the first measure says it does not support the hypothesis because the evidence already has probability 1, and the second measure says it does support the hypothesis. Use an ordinary setting such as a school, a local business, or a minor medical matter, rather than physics or the history of science, and state both the probabilities before the evidence was learned and the current probabilities so that both measures can be calculated from consistent numbers. Then show the calculation each measure gives and state its verdict. After that, identify the structural feature of the case that does the work, say what the two measures would both say if that feature were removed (for instance if the hypothesis had come first and the evidence had only been learned afterwards), and write a short reply in the voice of a strict conditionaliser who denies that old evidence can confirm, explaining where the counterfactual measure goes wrong in your case. Please write the whole answer in British English, in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you would write a letter.
