I serve on the Metabolic Disease Research Board at a university that funds multi-year research programmes rather than individual projects, and we meet next month to decide whether to renew the three-year grant of Dr. Aldana's laboratory. Aldana's programme is built around the deposition-first hypothesis: an early-onset cerebellar ataxia is caused by the progressive accumulation of a specific misfolded protein inside neurons, and that accumulation is the single initiating cause of all downstream damage. The lab's track record is uneven. Its first predictions failed; the predicted marker could not be found in blood, then could not be found in cerebrospinal fluid, and each failure was met with an adjustment: the marker is cleared too quickly, it matters only in one cell type, it must be measured in patients at a particular stage, and so on. One of these adjustments, however, produced something better: in 2022 the lab pre-registered the prediction that patients carrying a particular gene variant would show elevated cerebrospinal fluid levels of the marker and an earlier age of onset, and the result came out as predicted in a small cohort. A later attempt to extend that finding to a larger, unselected cohort failed, and the lab has now proposed yet another modification, still untested, according to which the marker is present only during a narrow, easily missed window.

A rival programme in the same division, run by Dr. Reis, takes the energy-deficit view: the disease is a network-level failure of cellular energy regulation rather than the result of a single initiating deposit. Reis's group has made broader and less mechanically specific predictions, but three of them have now been confirmed in pre-registered animal studies. Several board members read the choice as obvious: Aldana's programme is degenerating by Lakatos's standards, since its auxiliary hypotheses keep changing to accommodate anomalies after the fact, while Reis's programme is the progressive one. I think this may be too quick, since Lakatos also insists that anomalies are not refutations and that a protected hard core is not itself a sin.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. I want it to use Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating research programmes as its main analytical frame, understood roughly as follows: a programme is progressive while its successive modifications systematically yield novel predictions that are later corroborated, and degenerating when its modifications are mostly crafted after the fact to absorb anomalies the programme did not anticipate. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action for the Board with a time frame, whether that is full renewal, non-renewal, or some conditional bridging arrangement, and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration that speaks for the opposite recommendation. Please write it as continuous prose, in the manner of a formal letter or email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
