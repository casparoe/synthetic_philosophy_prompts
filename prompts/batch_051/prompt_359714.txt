There's a guy in my discrimination law reading group who keeps saying that what makes a ground of discrimination "suspect" is that it targets traits you can't change, and he always reaches for "born this way" to explain why sexual orientation qualifies. But when I asked why religion counts as protected if it's something people can choose, he just said religion is different because people choose it, so it's not the same. I can feel the slogan doing too much work there, but I can't put my finger on what it's actually claiming.

Can you unpack "born this way" as used in debates about suspect grounds? I want the distinct precise claims it could be making, from empirical stuff about genes, hormones, and early childhood, to normative claims about choice, fault, and identity. For each reading, say whether it's trivial, defensible, or false, and whether it would actually support singling out sexual orientation as suspect — and also whether the same reading would imply anything about religion or other chosen statuses, or whether it just doesn't travel. The useful thing would be a menu I can bring to the group and show which precisification of the slogan does the work and which ones collapse into each other.
