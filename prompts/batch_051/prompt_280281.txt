Persson and Savulescu's case for moral bioenhancement runs, approximately, as follows. Technological progress continually increases the destructive power that a small number of individuals can wield, while the moral dispositions available to restrain that power remain calibrated for small-scale, face-to-face social life; the result is a dangerous gap, and closing it through the biomedical enhancement of human moral motivation is therefore urgent. Now add a development their original argument did not center: a growing share of destructive capability sits in AI systems that select their own subgoals, act faster than any human overseer, and whose restraint comes from training and alignment rather than from evolved or enhanced human moral psychology. Does their argument survive this shift, need repair, or collapse? Identify the premise the development bears on, state what would have to be true for the original conclusion to survive, and say how the conclusion is best rephrased if the relevant moral agent is no longer only the human being.
