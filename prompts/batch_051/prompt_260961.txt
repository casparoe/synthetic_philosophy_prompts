I've come around to the evidential cooperation view, and I want to know how fragile it really is. The idea: in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma against an agent whose reasoning is similar to mine — the kind of case you'd expect in a large world, with many copies or near-copies of a decision procedure running — my choice is strong evidence about theirs, even though there's no causal channel between us. So when the expected payoff from our choices being correlated beats the temptation to defect, I should cooperate. Causal decision theorists say that's a mistake because my act doesn't influence the other player, but that just sounds to me like refusing to make a better self-fulfilling prophecy true. Tell me what would actually count against this position: not just "CDT disagrees," but which cases, formal results, or intuitions would make even a committed evidential cooperationist concede trouble. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones defenders can justifiably shrug off, and if the honest answer is that nothing you can think of would dent it, say that and whether that's a problem. Say how confident you are in this map, and which single point you're least sure of.
