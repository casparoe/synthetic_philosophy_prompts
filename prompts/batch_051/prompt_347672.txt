I'm in a seminar on animal minds and we've spent the last three weeks going in circles on insect sentience. Everyone basically agrees on the numbers: millions, maybe trillions of insects alive at any given time. Everyone agrees pain matters, at least instrumentally. But the same people keep making the same points and nobody moves. One side says: even if the chance a given insect can suffer is something like 1 in 10,000, the sheer numbers make the expected value enormous, so we can't just bracket insects as automatons. The other side says: you can't just multiply a probability by a population when the probability is a guess, and there's a reason Fabre spent all that time on solitary wasps and bees showing that behavior that look intelligent is really just instinct—it cuts your probability down so far that scale never gets off the ground.

We read a chunk of Jean-Henri Fabre's The Mason-Bees for class, the bit where he describes how a mason-bee, after he messed with the structure of its cell, seemed to adapt and fix it in a way that looked like problem-solving, but then Fabre argues that this apparent intelligence is just the same fixed instinct surfacing under a new condition, and that we should not infer understanding or anything like it from the behavior. That passage sits differently with different people: some take it as empirical evidence that insect behavior is rigid and not a sign of sentience; others say Fabre is smuggling in an interpretation—he's already decided that 'instinct' means no inner experience, and then he uses that to explain away the exact behavior others count as evidence. I'm supposed to write a short diagnosis paper, and I honestly can't tell what kind of disagreement this is. Is it a hidden factual disagreement about how flexible insect behavior actually is? A disagreement about priors—how low does the probability have to be before scale stops mattering? A disagreement about what sentience evidence even looks like? Or is it partly verbal, where 'sentience' and 'instinct' are doing too much work? And depending on your diagnosis, what would actually move it? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey, and I want you to be honest if the answer is that the debate is just badly posed.
