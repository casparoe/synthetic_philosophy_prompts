Two philosophers — a propositional intellectualist about knowing-how, and a Rylean anti-intellectualist who treats knowing-how as irreducibly ability-like — have been arguing past one another for a year. They agree to run an adversarial collaboration on the question of what it is to know how to do something. Draft the joint statement they could both sign.

The statement should contain:

1. A numbered "claims we both accept" section. Go well beyond truisms. It should include claims about the data any adequate account must handle: for example, the existence of both dissociations — a person able to perform a skill without being able to articulate the relevant truths, and a person able to articulate the relevant truths without being able to perform. It should also record what both sides grant about how "knows how" and "knows that" constructions behave, and which kinds of evidence (ordinary ascriptions, skilled performance, instruction, cognitive capacity) both sides accept as constraining the debate.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give: the neutral statement of the question; the intellectualist's formulation in wording an intellectualist would accept; the anti-intellectualist's formulation in wording an anti-intellectualist would accept; and which side, if either, currently regards that particular dispute as merely verbal. Candidate rows include whether knowing how entails ability, whether the content of knowing-how is truth-evaluable, and whether practical modes of presentation are genuinely propositional.

3. A crux section: two or three sub-questions such that a settled answer to each would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case, test, or argument both camps agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts it shows. The cases should be chosen so the predictions come apart on their natural readings — for example, a skilled performer who systematically misdescribes what she does, or a person who acquires know-how purely through memorized propositional instruction without practice.

4. A short closing note identifying any portion of the dispute the collaboration concluded is merely verbal or terminological, and any portion that remains substantive, with the reasoning stated in terms both sides would accept.

Throughout, separate what is settled among people working on this topic from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Do not appeal to named authors, slogans, or works; the value should be in the formulations themselves.
