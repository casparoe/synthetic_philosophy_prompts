I am a sixth-form student, and I have been using a term of my own, 'revolutionary lag', which I would like assessed. I mean the interval in a scientific revolution between the time when the old framework's failures become serious enough that its defenders are improvising, and the time when the challenger has produced enough work to count as a full framework with its own standards. My central claim is that this interval is where Popper and Kuhn both stop explaining: Popper cannot yet say that one decisive test has falsified the old framework, because its failures are still just anomalies, and Kuhn cannot yet say that two incommensurable paradigms are facing one another, because the challenger is not yet a paradigm at all. Choices made inside the lag look like neither refutations nor conversions; they look like bets on which side will become fertile first, and the real question is what makes such bets rational rather than arbitrary. I picture the move from phlogiston to Lavoisier's oxygen theory, during the period when phlogiston's failures were accumulating but oxygen had not yet become the obvious successor. Is this a genuine distinction rather than familiar ideas under new names? Does it already exist, and where does it break down?
