Consider a counterfactual in which a middle-income democracy with a functioning court system and a centralized tax authority (set it, for concreteness, at the institutional level of Chile in the year 2000) amends its constitution to allow "charter municipalities." Under the amendment, a private developer may acquire an undeveloped coastal tract of roughly five hundred square kilometers and hold it under a renewable ninety-nine-year charter. The developer may write the civil and commercial code for the zone, run its own civil and commercial courts and a police force to enforce zone law, set admission and business-licensing rules, and fund itself through contract-based fees rather than taxes. It may not opt out of the national criminal code or the constitution's bill of rights, and every resident and firm must be free to leave at any time without penalty. The national government retains border control, foreign policy, and the formal power to revoke a charter for a material breach; the zone's admission rules govern entry to the zone only, not to the country at large.

I am trying to work out whether exit rights of this kind are enough to legitimate private legal orders, or whether what emerges is not genuinely opting out of the state but a delegated, revocable, and ultimately precarious form of rule. Give me your best reasoned prediction of how this arrangement plays out over the next forty years, making the causal chain explicit at each step. Cover at least the following: who selects into the zones and what combinations of substantive law and fee schedules developers actually choose; whether competition among zones produces a race to the bottom in labor, environmental, or consumer protection, a race to the top in dispute resolution and public services, or sorting into distinct legal niches; how the host country's politics, tax base, and ordinary municipalities respond; whether exit truly disciplines the developers or is hollow because of moving costs, lock-in, and information problems; and whether the zones remain exit-based arrangements or gradually become polities in their own right, with residents demanding voice. Conclude by stating the two or three predictions you are most confident in, the ones you are least confident in, and the assumptions whose failure would most change the overall picture.
