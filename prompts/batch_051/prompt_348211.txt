A game studio's content policy, drafted back when the studio only made single-player story campaigns, has this clause: "Combat must be presented with moral weight; no mode may use killing as its own reward or as the primary driver of progression unless the surrounding narrative makes the player reckon with its costs." The studio is now shipping a horde-mode update for its main military shooter: players hold a position against waves of attackers, earn XP and medals purely per kill, and get a victory sting after each wave; there is no story, no civilians, no aftermath — the enemies are just targets that charge at you. Does the horde mode breach the clause? I mostly want the reading, not the practical outcome: give me the plain-words reading, the purpose-of-the-clause reading, what the authors would have said if asked about horde modes, and what a reasonable player would take the policy to promise. If they diverge, which should win here — and what does that show about wether a clause like this can actually do the work the studio wants it to do?
