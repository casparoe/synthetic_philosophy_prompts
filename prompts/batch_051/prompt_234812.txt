I keep ending up defening a view I'm not totally sure I believe, so I want to stress-test it before I keep repeating it. The view: the standard rationalist account of war in IR carries over to AI races and predicts that a deliberate hot war between AI powers is less likely than most people in the safety world fear. The logic is that fighting is costly and risky, so if both sides would rather not be flattened, there's normally some bargain — access, compute, verification, spheres of influence, whatever — that both prefer to a shooting war. So rational governments should produce expensive arms races, brinksmanship, maybe accidents, but not a considered decision to launch a first strike. The standard account says war becomes rational only through hidden information about capabilities (each side bluffing about how far along it is), commitment problems (can't credibly promise not to defect once you pull ahead), or winner-take-all stakes that can't be divided. My current take is that in the AI case the first is overrated because enough leaks out, the third could be handled with monitored compromises, and the second — especially an offense-dominant moment where striking first looks decisive — is the one that actually scares me.

Here's what I want: not generic objections, but the stuff that should actually move me. What observations, historical cases, or arguments would count against this view, sorted by how decisive they'd be? Include state behavior that's already happened (sabotage, export controls, lab break-ins, that sort of thing) and say where it should sit in the ranking. I also want an honest assessment of whether the position can absorb any counterexample by saying ah, but that's just a commitment problem — since the framework already lists commitment problems as a route to war, that makes me nervous I've insulated it. Would a defender of this view themselves have to concede that some of this counts against it, or only outsiders? And is the insulation a real problem for a view like this, or just par for the course in social science?
