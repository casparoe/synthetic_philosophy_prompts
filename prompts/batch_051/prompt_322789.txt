I'm on my HOA board and we've spent three meetings fighting about a city proposal to run a small food-waste dropoff at the edge of our neighborhood. The real split isn't compost, it's posture: some of us start from "the city will only do this right if we make them prove everything in advance," and others start from "the city is full of people doing their jobs, and treating them like con artists is what makes everything here slow and expensive." The second group keeps pointing out that in places like Denmark or Norway people seem to assume the local council is basically competent and things still get done, while the first group acts like that's just naivety. One neighbor keeps saying "trust, but verify" as if it ends the argument, and nobody can quite tell what that commits him too. It sounds like a compromise, but I suspect it's actually just the suspicious position with a friendly hat on, or maybe it's too vague to be a position at all. So mostly I want someone to really take that sentence apart. What are the distinct precise claims "trust, but verify" could be making? Which of those readings are just platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false as claims about how institutions actually operate? And specifically, which reading would my neighbor have to be relying on if "trust, but verify" is supposed to settle the question in favor of making the city do a pile of pre-emptive paperwork and hearings before we'll even vote on the pilot?
