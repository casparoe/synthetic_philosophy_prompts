In "The Extended Mind" (1998), Andy Clark and David Chalmers write:

"In these cases, the human organism is linked with an external entity in a two-way interaction, creating a coupled system that can be seen as a cognitive system in its own right. All the components in the system play an active causal role, and they jointly govern behavior in the same sort of way that cognition usually does. If we remove the external component the system's behavioral competence will drop, just as it would if we removed part of its brain. Our thesis is that this sort of coupled process counts equally well as a cognitive process, whether or not it is wholly in the head."

Please give your credence that the claim made in the final sentence is true of at least one ordinary, actual case—for example, a person who habitually uses a phone to store and retrieve addresses and appointments. Decompose your estimate into the following terms, giving a number and a one-line justification for each: (i) the probability that a process counts as cognitive in virtue of what it does rather than where or in what material it runs; (ii) the probability that, if that functional criterion is correct, the human–artifact interaction described in the passage actually satisfies it, rather than merely causing or feeding information to cognition; (iii) the probability that the drop in behavioral competence when the external component is removed indicates that the component is part of the cognitive mechanism itself, rather than an enabling condition outside it; (iv) the probability that the coupled system, rather than the organism alone, is the right unit to which the process should be attributed. Show how you combined these terms into your overall credence, and then say which term you are least confident in and what evidence or experiment would move it most.
