In the philosophy of photography, a long-running claim holds that photographs are transparent: that when we look at a photograph, we literally see the photographed object through it, much as we see through a telescope or a mirror, rather than merely seeing a picture of it. The rival view is that photographs are representations we interpret, no different in kind from a skillful drawing. I wonder whether this dispute can be brought into the laboratory. Design the most informative empirical probe you can: for instance, experiments comparing how viewers process photographs versus equally faithful non-photographic images and versus the live scene, using measures such as gaze patterns, change blindness, or physiological responses. Say clearly which pattern of results would support the transparency claim and which would support the representation view. Then say what even the cleanest result would not settle — whether the test captures seeing itself or only some proxy such as responding as if seeing, whether the concept of seeing has sharp enough boundaries to be tested at all, and whether the two theories might make identical behavioral predictions while differing about what the viewer is aware of.
