Two engineers on my team, Marcus and Priya, argued at lunch about a note one of our senior engineers had left in an old design document. The module is dormant, and the note says a scheduled job should be deleted because, the next time it runs, it will intermittently corrupt rows.

Marcus insisted that the rationale in the note had existed for years before any of us read it, and that this is exactly what made it possible to discover. Priya said that was a misuse of the word 'reason': before someone took it up, there was only a fact about the code, and a fact becomes a reason only relative to someone's goals, so there are no reasons without reasoners. Marcus replied that on her account we had not discovered a reason to delete the job but had invented one, which would make ordinary talk about 'finding reasons' misleading. Priya responded that 'found' is harmless: we found a fact, and that fact, together with our goal of keeping data intact, made a reason for us; the reason is a relation to a goal, not an object sitting in the code. Marcus then asked whether there had been a reason to delete the job in 2019, when nobody on the team knew about it; he said the plain answer is yes, and an account on which the answer is no cannot explain our judgement that the 2019 team missed something. Priya answered that sentences like that are shorthand for conditionals: a competent engineer who wanted data integrity and knew the fact would have had a reason, and that is all one is really claiming. She added that the alternative treats a reason as if it hung around unattached to anyone's thinking. Marcus said that reply makes 'they should have known' mysterious, since the point of that criticism is that the reason was there to be found, not created later by knowledge. Priya replied that the criticism rests on a different, known reason, namely the standing policy of checking dormant modules, and needs no unowned reason hovering over the bug.

They asked me to settle it and I could not. Please go through the exchange in order and, for each claim, say whether it is right, wrong, or partly right, and briefly why. Then say what underlying distinction they are circling without naming, and who is more right overall, if either. Please answer in British English.
