I just had a long argument with a friend about whether many-worlds actually has a worked-out story about probability — he kept saying the Born rule just gets pasted on top, and I kept saying there are real proposals for why it isn't. Afterwards I tried to write down every live proposal I could think of, and I want to make sure my list is complete. Here's what I have:

- Naive branch counting: count up the branches and give each an equal probability. Everyone rejects this because nothing in the theory picks out a priviledged way to carve the state into branches, and nothing says branches should be counted equally anyway.
- Gleason's theorem: in a Hilbert space of dimension at least three, any assignment of non-contextual probabilities to the subspaces that behaves like a probability measure has to be the Born-rule measure, so the rule gets forced by the geometry (though people argue about whether that solves the problem or just restates it).
- The Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic argument: treat rational agents in an Everettian universe as betting on which branch their descendants will observe; under ordinary decision axioms plus the physics, they're rationally compelled to bet with squared amplitudes.
- Zurek's envariance: the entangled system-plus-environment state has symmetries under certain joint transformations, and the only probability weighting that is invariant under those symmetries is amplitude-squared, so alternative weightings are unstable under relabelling.
- The self-locating uncertainty proposal: just before branching you should be genuinely uncertain about which branch you'll find yourself in, and the uniquely rational self-locating credence to have is the squared amplitude.
- Vaidman's measure of existence: branches are not all equally real; the 'amount of existence' of a branch is its squared amplitude, and probability is just each branch's share of total existence.

I have this nagging feeling there's one more standard-ish move I'm forgetting. What is it? It has to be genuinely different from the six on the list, not a rewording, a special case, or a combination of them. If you can, also say something about how it relates to (or improves on) the self-locating uncertainty and envariance entries, since those are the two my friend was most sympathetic to.
