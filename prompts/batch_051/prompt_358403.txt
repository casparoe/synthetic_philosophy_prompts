I'm a philosophy PhD candidate serving as teaching assistant for an upper-level undergraduate course on population ethics, with a unit on pronatalism and whether a larger population is better, and I'm grading a take-home exam. One of the questions was this:

"State the procreation asymmetry and explain why it is widely thought to pose a problem for total utilitarianism. Then explain how critical-level utilitarianism responds to the asymmetry and to the Repugnant Conclusion, and assess whether critical-level utilitarianism is best classified as a person-affecting view."

For context, the exam was closed-book and asked for short analytic answers, so I'll be marking primarily for the content of the claims rather than for style or sourcing. One student wrote the following:

"The procreation asymmetry is the claim that it is permissible, and perhaps not even regrettable, to refrain from creating a person whose life would be worth living, whereas it is wrong to create a person whose life would not be worth living. Total utilitarianism cannot accommodate this, since it evaluates states of affairs by summing individual welfare and therefore counts a forgone happy life as a loss in the same way it counts a created miserable life as a loss; the only difference is one of magnitude. Critical-level utilitarianism does better. It assigns each life a contribution equal to its welfare minus some critical level α. If α is set at zero, then lives below zero contribute negative value, lives above zero contribute positive value, and lives exactly at zero contribute nothing. Refraining from creating a happy child would therefore add nothing, which captures the first arm of the asymmetry, while creating a miserable child would add negative value, which captures the second arm. Because lives at the critical level make no contribution to the value function, the theory in effect treats merely possible persons at that level as outside the value function, and that is just what a person-affecting view does. Critical-level utilitarianism also escapes the Repugnant Conclusion by refusing to let huge numbers of barely positive lives outweigh the quality of lives in a smaller, better-off population."
