Many people will casually say that a large language model "knows" facts or "believes" the answer it is giving, and they treat those ascriptions as action-guiding—for example, when deciding whether to trust a summary or whether to keep using the model after an error. Many of the same people would resist saying that a chess engine "wants" to control the center or that a thermostat "thinks" the room is too cold, dismissing those as mere metaphor. Yet in both cases the attributions seem to earn their keep in the same way: they let us predict and explain behavior by treating the system as if it had beliefs and goals. Neither system has a mind in the way a person does. What, if anything, justifies the asymmetry? Give the strongest principle you can find for drawing the line, then test it against hard cases at both ends.
