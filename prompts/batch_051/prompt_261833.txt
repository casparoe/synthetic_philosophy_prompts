I keep coming back to one episode and I can't get the two standard takes to sit together. It's Reserve Police Battalion 101 — Hamburg reserve cops, mostly middle-aged, ordinary jobs, not front-line soldiers — who in July 1942 were ordered to round up the Jewish population of Józefów in Poland, and shot most of them, men, women, children, over the course of a day. Their commander, Trapp, was apparently in tears and announced that any man who didn't feel up to it could step out; only about a dozen did at first, and the rest kept at it, many shaken but working in shifts to finish the job. The way Browning tells it, these were ordinary men twisted by the situation: obedience, conformity, not wanting to let the others down, the slow slide from rounding people up to killing them. Goldhagen's story is different — he thinks the situation barely explains anything, because these men already had a deep, murderous antisemitism and for the most part didn't need pushing.
