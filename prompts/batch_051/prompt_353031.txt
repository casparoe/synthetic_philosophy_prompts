I am drawn to process reliabilism for reasons of theoretical economy: it explains knowledge in terms of a single property, reliability, it handles perception, memory and testimony without positing inner justifications, and it yields a definite verdict in nearly every case. But I have a strong intuition that reliabilism makes knowledge of the reliability of one's own faculties too easy, and I cannot decide which of the two should give way.

Here is the difficulty in a concrete form. Suppose my colour vision is in fact reliable. I look at a red wall and reason: the wall looks red; the wall is red; so on this occasion my colour vision got things right. I repeat this across many surfaces and lighting conditions, and I infer that my colour vision is generally reliable. Each step is, by hypothesis, a true belief produced by a reliable process, and the final induction is an ordinary induction over known premises. The reliabilist seems committed to saying that I have thereby come to know that my colour vision is reliable. What bothers me is that the procedure uses the faculty under test as its only measuring instrument: I can assert that the wall is red only if my colour vision is already working properly on that occasion, and if my colour vision were systematically misleading, the reasoning would feel just as convincing from the inside. The exercise therefore looks as if it produces a kind of easy knowledge—knowledge of a faculty's reliability that is purchased without any independent check.

I am not looking to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for reliabilism to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a defensible reading of reliabilism on which the two are compatible. I would want to hear, among other things, whether the intuition can be debunked as a lingering internalist demand for first-person guarantees, whether reliabilism can be tightened so that bootstrapping inferences fall outside the class of knowledge-producing inferences, and whether the conclusion is genuinely trivial rather than objectionable. Please answer in British English.
