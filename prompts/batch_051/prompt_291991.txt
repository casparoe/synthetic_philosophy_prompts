I am taking a course this term on the ethics of public speech and platform governance, and a recurring seminar debate has been whether propaganda should be reproduced at all when the stated purpose is to criticize it. Some classmates argued that reposting propaganda, even with critical intent, still does the propagandist's work by laundering the material into wider circulation; others argued that unanswerable propaganda is more dangerous than visible propaganda. My roommate, who works in trust and safety at a mid-sized video-sharing platform, described a rule her team is weighing that is supposed to split this difference, and I want to think through how it would actually behave.

The proposed rule is as follows. A user may post state-produced or party-produced propaganda only inside a "critique wrapper" enforced by the platform. The embedded material must appear in full and unedited, with a standardized label stating the producing body and approximate date and identifying the content as propaganda. The user's own commentary must be placed before the material, so that no one can play or read the propaganda without first scrolling through the critique; it must be at least as long as the embedded material (for a thirty-second video, at least thirty seconds of estimated reading or viewing time); and it must identify at least one specific manipulative technique or contested factual claim, anchored to a particular moment or sentence, rather than merely condemning the piece. For enforcement, "propaganda" is defined operationally as persuasion content commissioned by a state, party, or state-linked organization and lacking an individual author. Accounts that post such material outside the wrapper lose the ability to attach commentary to any reposted third-party content for one month; repeated violations restrict account distribution.

I am less interested in whether this rule is morally right in the abstract than in how it would play out. Walk me through the incentives it creates for critics, propagandists, ordinary users, and the platform itself; the ways each group would game or bend it; and where it would fail first. In particular, do the length and specificity requirements tend to produce substantive critique or a padded genre of compliance, and what second-order effects would the rule have on the propaganda that gets produced in the first place?
