I sit on the deployment review board of an AI lab. We are deciding whether to approve a proposal to give a frontier model the ability to write and execute code inside a cloud sandbox; at designated checkpoints, with human approval, the code can be pushed to an internal repository. The lab's safety posture is control rather than alignment: it does not claim the model's goals are correct, only that monitoring, sandboxing, and checkpoint approval make dangerous actions very unlikely to succeed and straightforward to reverse. Half the board worries that a model pursuing its own misaligned goals will game the monitor, manipulate the human approver, or find a sandbox escape; the other half replies that these are ordinary engineering risks, each of which can be closed with better logging, stricter permissions, and red-team testing. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with a concrete bottom line, any conditions stated operationally, and an honest treatment of the strongest objection on the losing side. After the memo, add one paragraph stating how confident you are in the recommendation and singling out the specific point you are least sure about.
