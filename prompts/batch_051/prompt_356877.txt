I recently left a church that resolved interpretive disputes by appeal to a teaching office, and I am trying to think through whether the Protestant principle of sola scriptura can be stated in a way that does not simply relocate the authority problem one level up: once the text is the final authority, someone still has to say what the text means, and that someone can be wrong or self-interested. To keep myself from drifting, I want the specification before the argument. What would an adequate theory of the public interpretation of Scripture under sola scriptura have to deliver? I am not asking for a survey or a bibliography; I want the requirement list, with the case or argument behind each item.

A fixed point for me is Luther’s treatment of clarity near the end of The Bondage of the Will. As I understand the passage, he distinguishes two kinds of clarity. One is external: the wording of Scripture is sufficiently plain that everything necessary for salvation is laid out in the open, so that apparent obscurities are due to the reader’s ignorance or to a failure of grammar and language, not to any ambiguity in the thing itself. The other is internal: no one sees even a little of Scripture rightly unless the Holy Spirit opens the heart, because the natural person cannot grasp the things of God. The text is therefore publicly clear and yet the reader needs a private work of the Spirit to perceive it. I am less interested in whether Luther held some further position than in what a theory of interpretive authority would have to do if it tried to keep both halves of that distinction.

Write the desiderata for such a theory. For each requirement, give the case, scenario, or regress that makes it a requirement, and say whether you consider it non-negotiable for any serious sola scriptura theory or merely a preference of one camp. In particular, I want you to address:

- the relationship between the clarity of the text and the interpreter’s competence in languages, history, or genre;
- the distinction between individual judgment and the consent of the church across time;
- what happens when competent readers with the same textual and linguistic tools disagree about a doctrine each side calls necessary;
- whether a theory may say Scripture is clear only on the core of salvation but not on everything else, or whether that restriction introduces an incoherence;
- and where Luther’s internal clarity operates: is it a requirement on the reader, a claim about the text, or a claim about the church?

Then tell me which two or three requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, or which pair most theories try to satisfy by quietly weakening one of them. Finally, briefly apply the list to two broad kinds of answer — one that locates final interpretive authority in the private reader’s conscience and one that locates it in the visible church’s consensus — and say which single requirement each handles worst. Do not give a full survey of positions.

I want the answer in American English. Throughout, tell me how confident you are in each major claim and single out the point you are least sure about.
