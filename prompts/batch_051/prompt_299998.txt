I am a PhD student in political theory, and I am in the middle of a dissertation chapter on pandemic-era emergency powers. The chapter began as an attempt to keep two questions apart, questions my literature repeatedly runs together: whether a measure is lawful and whether it is legitimate. The difficulty is that my own judgments do not sit comfortably with either the legality-first view or the legitimacy-first view, so I have started writing down my verdicts on concrete cases in the hope of extracting the principle behind them. I would like help doing exactly that.

Here are the cases as I actually judge them. (1) Early in an outbreak, the government closes nonessential businesses for five weeks by an ordinary statute, debated and passed, with wage subsidies, rent relief, and an independent review required before any extension. I find this legitimate, and nothing about it troubles me. (2) In the same outbreak, the same closures are instead imposed by the health minister under a public-health emergency statute permitting unilateral action for thirty days. The minister renews the order monthly, without returning to the legislature, for five more months even as hospital data improve. I judge the first decree both lawful and legitimate; I judge the later renewals lawful on a plain reading of the statute but illegitimate. (3) The government closes the border to non-citizens overnight, with no advance notice, turning people back at airports. Returning citizens may enter only through a two-week quarantine hotel at their own expense; non-citizen residents may enter only if they prove at the border that they were ordinarily resident before a cutoff date. The closure itself and the paid quarantine I accept as legitimate; the no-notice implementation and the cutoff-date documentation requirement I do not. (4) A legislature suspends a constitutional guarantee of freedom of movement for ninety days by a two-thirds majority after debate. The act contains a sunset clause and establishes a randomly selected citizens' panel that can recommend early termination. I regard the measure as unlawful under the constitution as written, and I also judge it legitimate. (5) The same ninety-day curfew is instead imposed under a never-repealed public-health statute from the middle of the last century that grants the executive indefinite power to exclude persons from public spaces without judicial review. The order has no end date and no review mechanism. I find it perfectly lawful and not legitimate. (6) Infected persons are quarantined, fitted with electronic ankle monitors, and listed by name and address on a public online register. I accept the quarantine and even the monitor as legitimate given the transmission risk; the public register I find illegitimate.

The two verdicts I keep staring at are (2) and (4): in one I condemn something formally lawful, and in the other I accept something formally unlawful. My working principle is that an emergency measure is legitimate, whatever its formal legal status, only if it is authorized in advance by a broadly accountable body, is time-limited and subject to genuine review, imposes burdens roughly proportional to the risk while providing for basic needs, and does not work through stigma or arbitrary status distinctions. Please test that principle against my six verdicts, clause by clause, and repair it if it fails. If the repaired principle forces me to overturn any of my verdicts, say which and why. Then tell me what it says about a case I have not settled: a formally voluntary health-pass system, introduced jointly by the government, large employers, and transit providers, in which everyone must show a green status from a risk-scoring app to enter most workplaces, restaurants, and public transport; use of the app is in form optional, but in practice there is no alternative, and the scoring criteria are unpublished. No emergency has been declared, and the legal basis is an old statute permitting businesses to set conditions of entry for health and safety. I cannot decide whether this is a private arrangement outside the reach of the principle or a de facto lockdown imposed by delegation.

Please lead with the repaired principle, then work through the cases. Keep the response to no more than 1,000 words.
