I'm a TA for a metaphysics course and I have to grade this. The question I set was:

Explain the difference between Bradley's regress and the problem of the unity of the proposition, and say wether the two problems can be given the same solution. In your answer, consider the response that says instantiation is a non-relational tie.

Student's answer:

Bradley's regress is the problem that a relation can't glue its terms together, because to relate the relation to its terms we need another relation, and then another, and so on forever. Bradley's own conclusion was that relations are real but unanalysable, so the regress has to be accepted as harmless. The unity of the proposition is essentially the same problem but for language: the constituents of the proposition a loves b don't form a unity just by being listed, and adding another constituent to tie them together just produces a longer list. The standard solution to both is a non-relational tie: instead of treating instantiation as a further relation, you treat it as a sui generis combinedness that doesn't itself need to be combined. This solves the proposition problem because the tie is what makes the proposition true, and it solves Bradley's regress because once the tie is non-relational the regress can't get started. So yes, the two problems are really one problem and have the same solution.
