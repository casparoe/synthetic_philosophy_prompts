I've been chewing on procreative beneficence (PB) and wound up with an argument that seems airtight but lands somewhere I refuse to go, and I cant find the exit.

(1) PB: if prospective parents have already decided to have a child and can select between two possible future children, they have a significant moral reason, other things equal, to select the one whose life can be expected to go better.

(2) But expected welfare is overwhelmingly a social fact. Holding a given child fixed, the child has higher expected welfare in our world if they're straight than gay, cis than trans, or a member of the ethnic majority rather than a stigmatized minority — not because those traits are intrinsically better, but because the local status hierarchy hands out less violence, more job callbacks, easier intimacies, and so on.

(3) So PB gives parents a pro tanto reason to select, wherever selection is possible, for exactly those socially dominant traits. The reason isn't rooted in the parents' own bigotry; it just tracks expected welfare.

(4) And that reason doesn't politely stay pro tanto. In a world with few strong countervailing duties in individual cases, a significant pro tanto reason will often just be the decision, and PB includes no internal filter telling a parent when to stop following it.

(5) Now aggregate. If lots of parents act on (3)–(4), they produce a world even more committed to the status asymmetries that made those choices rational in the first place — fewer minority kids, more isolation for the ones who exist, norms reinforced. The expected-welfare surplus the parents bought for their children comes from making the positional landscape worse for everyone down the line.

(6) So the principle that tells you to maximize the child's expected welfare seems to recommend, at scale, degrading the very quantity it told you to maximize. That's either a reductio of PB or a sign that some earlier step was doing subtle work.

I'd most like to keep (1) and (2); they feel almost definitional. But I can't point at the step that breaks. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost the rest of the view?
