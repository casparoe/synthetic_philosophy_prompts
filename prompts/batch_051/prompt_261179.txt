I help run a Nomic-style game for my law and philosophy club — there's about fifteen of us, mostly people who enjoy arguing about rules more than playing. Our ruleset is homemade and has been growing by amendment for a semester, so it has some odd corners. The two rules that matter here are these. Rule 8: "A rule change is adopted if, within forty-eight hours of being proposed, at least two-thirds of active members register approval and no active member registers an objection. Once adopted, a rule change takes effect immediately unless its text specifies a later time." Rule 14: "Rules numbered one through seven are foundational. A foundational rule may be changed only by a unanimous vote of all active members, and any rule change that would modify a foundational rule is void unless it receives that vote."

Last week a member proposed Amendment 37, which reads in full: "Rule 8 is declared foundational, effective immediately upon the adoption of this amendment." Twelve of fifteen members approved, nobody objected, and three abstained; under Rule 8 that counts as an adoption, since abstentions aren't objections, but it plainly is not unanimous. The trouble: the very amendment that declared Rule 8 foundational was itself a change to Rule 8, and it passed by twelve votes, not fifteen. If the declaration is in force from the moment of adoption, then the adoption itself seems to have needed unanimity, which it didn't have. If it isn't in force, then Rule 8 is still non-foundational and the twelve-vote adoption stands — but then we are ignoring a rule that says it takes effect at the moment of adoption. The proposer says this is obviously fine: Rule 8 governed its own amendment at the time of the vote, and the new status only applies to later changes; he was trying to lock the voting threshold in place before someone lowered it. Half the club thinks he found a loophole, the other half thinks he made the rules unable to answer.

I want to know what the club should actually rule. Walk me through what the plain words of Rules 8 and 14 plus Amendment 37 require here, what the purpose of the foundational-rules category suggests, what the people who drafted Rule 14 would probably say if asked, and what a reasonable new member reading the rulebook from cold would think. Then give a verdict: is Amendment 37 in force, is Rule 8 now foundational, and what, if anything, should we amend so this can't happen again?
