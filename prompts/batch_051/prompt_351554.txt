i'm in a population ethics seminar this term and have to hand in a proposal for the final paper (the proposal is ~1200 words, the paper itself ~5000). i want to write on the procreation asymmetry, but not in the usual 'is the asymmetry true?' way. we usually state it as two claims: you're not required to bring into existence a life that would be worth living, but you are required not to bring into existence a life that would be miserable. the trouble is that almost every explanation of it we read then shifts into talk about which possible worlds are better or worse. i'm not convinved that shift is innocent. if the asymmetry is really a betterness-across-worlds claim, then a world with an extra child whose life is worth living looks (all else equal) just as good as, or better than, the same world without her — so refusing to create her looks like it leaves value on the table, which is exactly what the asymmetry says is permissible. to keep the asymmetry you either have to deny the world with her is better, or say that even if it is better we aren't required to bring it about — and that second move is a claim about reasons, not world value. my half-formed idea is that what gets called 'the procreation asymmetry' is actually two different claims, one axiological and one about reasons/permissions, and the standard arguments only work by sliding between them. can you turn that into a proper term-paper proposal? i need: the exact question, why the existing positions leave it open, the approach i'd actually take (a few short thought experiments, including cases where the alternative to creating a child isn't 'no one exists' but 'a different child exists' or 'the would-be parents' lives go differently'), what a successful answer would look like, and what would count as the project failing. keep it realistic for a term paper, and don't include citations — i'll add those myself.
