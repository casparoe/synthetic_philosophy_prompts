I am a professor of philosophy serving on the public-value subcommittee of a national science board, and we must recommend whether to approve a new decadal funding line for observational cosmology: a high-resolution survey of the cosmic microwave background, together with a smaller theoretical center for early-universe cosmology. The full board is divided. Several members argue that in a period of constrained public budgets, science funding should be reserved for projects with foreseeable applications, and they note that cosmology offers no such applications on any relevant timescale. Others reply that this is precisely the kind of knowledge for which a public scientific culture exists: it alters how human beings understand their place in the order of things, whether or not it ever yields a device or a treatment.

My own draft opinion turns on a passage from Henri Poincaré’s The Value of Science, in the chapter where he defends astronomy. Poincaré first grants the utilitarian point that astronomy has historically underwritten navigation, geodesy, and reliable time-reckoning. But he then argues that astronomy’s deeper service is not those practical instruments, but the change it works in the human mind itself: the exact study of the heavens taught us that nature obeys laws and that the human intellect can read them, and he suggests that without that education the human race would remain something less than it can become. I am paraphrasing from the authorized translation by George Bruce Halsted, since I do not have the edition at hand.

Please write the short written opinion I will circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend a concrete disposition: approve the funding line in full, reject it, or approve the observational survey while rejecting the theoretical center, or some other mixed recommendation. It should take seriously the strongest objection to my Poincaré-based line, especially the worry that such reasoning treats science funding as a form of spiritual cultivation rather than public interest, and that its benefit is unevenly distributed among an educated few. However you come down, place the actionable recommendation in the first paragraph and then defend it.
