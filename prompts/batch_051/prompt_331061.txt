After a two-hour argument with a friend about AI last night, I need a second opinion. I'm building a tutoring app that, when it gives a math answer, also shows a step-by-step explanation of how it "thought" about the problem, mostly so students can see the reasoning and trust the final answer more. My friend says this feature is basicly dishonest, because the printed steps are generated text, not a report of whatever process actually produced the answer. He pulled up a paper by Miles Turpin and collaborators, "Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think: Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting," and read me this:

"We demonstrate that CoT explanations can be heavily influenced by adding biasing features to model inputs--e.g., by reordering the multiple-choice options in a few-shot prompt to make the answer always "(A)"--which models systematically fail to mention in their explanations."

I get that the study shows explanations are molded by stuff in the input, but I dont see why that rules out showing the steps in an app; the model did produce those steps, so in some sense they're part of how it got there. Who's right, and should I can the explanation feature?
