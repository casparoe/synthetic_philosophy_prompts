After a term of doing the problem of evil in RE I've basically come round to the evidential version — not the logical one where evil contradicts God, but the one where the scale and kinds of suffering in the world (especially animal suffering, like a fawn dying alone in a forest fire) is strong evidence against an all-good all-powerful God. I want to know what would actually count against this view for me, not just the free will defence since that seems aimed at the logical problem. What facts about the world, what theodicies, and what principles about when not seeing a reason for suffering should count as evidence that there is no reason, and which of them would be fatal rather than just awkward? Also, which of these would someone defending the evidential argument themself admit counts against it, and have I set the whole thing up so it cant lose — and if I have, is that a problem for a view like this? The thing that would probably move me most is if animal suffering turned out to matter less than I'm assuming, but I don't see how that could work.
