In a seminar on American foreign policy, I kept hearing the phrase "city upon a hill" used as if it settled things: one participant invoked it to explain why the United States is uniquely entitled to a leadership role, another to argue that American domestic failures have special consequences for world order, and a third invoked it ironically to dismiss the whole idea of American exceptionalism as a piece of secularized theology. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be expressing: descriptive claims about American history and institutions, normative claims about what the United States owes the world or whether it may lead, and ideological claims about how the nation should understand itself. For each reading, say whether it is fairly uncontroversial, substantive and contested, or probably false, and tell me which reading each of the three participants was actually leaning on, if any single one. In doing so, separate what is settled among people who seriously study American exceptionalism from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
