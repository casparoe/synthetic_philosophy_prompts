Counterfactual: starting in 1926, every major philanthropic foundation, and later the New Deal arts programs, adopts the rule that work by Black artists gets funded only if it can be defended explicitly as propaganda for racial uplift along the lines of Du Bois's Criteria of Negro Art. Anything else gets treated as unserious or as race betrayal. Run this forward to 1966 and predict how it reshapes Black American literature, painting, theater, and music. I want you to be explicit about mechanisms: what gets curated, what gets pushed into commercial entertainment or private spaces, how artists respond to the incentive, and whether by the end the rule has produced more or less of the emancipatory effect Du Bois wanted. Which predictions are you most confident of, and which ones would flip if white-run funding bodies turned out to reward the most legible rather than the most challenging uplift art?
