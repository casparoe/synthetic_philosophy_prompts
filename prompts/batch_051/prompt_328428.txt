I run a small reading group on business ethics with colleagues from the law school, and every spring the same argument comes up: whether a corporation is the kind of thing that can itself be blamed. The two sides are completely settled and nobody budges. The collectivists say that a firm's internal decision procedures—the committees, sign-offs, risk models, the way no single person sees the whole choice—add up to an intention that belongs to the firm rather than to any single manager, so when a company slowly, across many small decisions, chooses to ship something unsafe, the wrongdoing is the company's, not reducible to a few bad apples. The individualists say this is a metaphor gone professional: there is no corporate mind to harbor an intention, no corporate guilt to feel, so "blaming the company" is at best shorthand for blaming the people inside it and at worst a way for those people to hide behind the org chart. What bothers me is that they agree on almost all the facts of any case we discuss and on a lot of the normative background, and still nothing moves.

I'm trying to stop litigating the first-order question for once and instead figure out what the disagreement itself is made of. Is it purely verbal, with "intention" or "agent" meaning different things on each side? Is it a substantive metaphysical dispute about whether a group can have mental states? Or is it secretly a dispute about what blame is for—whether it's supposed to express resentment, assign desert, or change future behaviour—so that the metaphysics is really downstream? My half-formed hypthesis is that the two sides are answering different questions under one heading: one is asking what a blamed thing must be like, the other is asking which social practice of blame would do the most good. If that's right, is the impasse a sign that the original question is ill-posed? And either way, tell me what would actually count as progress here—a distinction, a piece of empirical psychology about how people attribute intentions to groups, a formal result—or whether we should expect permanent stalemate and why.
