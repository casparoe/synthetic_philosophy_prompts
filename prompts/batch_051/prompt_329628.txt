Draft the joint statement a committed illusionist and a committed phenomenal realist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration that's specifically about the meta-problem of consciousness — the problem of explaining why consciousness seems so hard to explain, and in particular why people so robustly form the judgment that there's a hard problem at all. Keep them off the hard problem itself; the interesting question is what they can jointly say about the meta-problem before they seperate.

The deliverable should have: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about the meta-problem both can sign — including what the target phenomena are (the seeming, the judgment, and the report as a speech act, which they may want to keep distinct), and what a full solution of the meta-problem would have to account for; (2) a disagreements table where each side's residual position is stated in wording that side itself would accept; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions whose answers would move at least one side — and for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or piece of introspective evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If you conclude part of their dispute about the meta-problem is verbal, say which part and why.
