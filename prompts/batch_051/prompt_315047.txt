I am pulled between two things. On one side, I am convinced by the legalist view of international order: a standing rule against unilateral military intervention without Security Council authorization, absent self-defense, has the systematic virtue of restraining powerful states, discouraging pretexts, and keeping the use of force predictable. On the other side, I cannot shake the firm conviction that NATO's 1999 intervention in Kosovo was, all things considered, morally right: it stopped a mass expulsion in progress, the Security Council was blocked, and alternatives appeared unlikely to act in time. I do not want to be told only that the act was illegal but moral. I want a negotiated answer to which of my two commitments should yield. Give the strongest version of each side; explain what it would take for the legalist principle to yield and what it would take for the Kosovo intuition to yield; and say whether there is a reading on which both can survive and, if so, what that reading costs. Then identify the consideration most likely to change your verdict, and where your reasoning is least secure. End with a last line of exactly this form:
