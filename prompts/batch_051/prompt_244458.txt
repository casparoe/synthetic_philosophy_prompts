In my lab, two researchers spent a lunch hour arguing over a recent video study of a Western scrub-jay that, after caching a food item while a conspecific was visible, later returned to recache the item once the observer had gone. Dr. Marsh said the only explanation licensed by C. Lloyd Morgan's canon, from An Introduction to Comparative Psychology (1894), was a lower one: she rendered the canon roughly as saying that an action should never be interpreted as the outcome of a higher psychical faculty if it can be interpreted as the outcome of one lower in the psychological scale. She said the jay had simply learned a contingency among the presence of a conspecific, the sight of a cache site, and re-caching, with no representation of being seen. Dr. Ellison replied that Morgan's canon is a methodological brake, not a substantive claim about animal minds; it tells you not to multiply inner causes, but it cannot tell you which lower-level account is correct. The associative story is not simpler, he said: it has to explain the bird's selective response to a particular observer's visual perspective by adding a list of contextual cues, whereas the higher account explains the same flexibility with one representation. Dr. Marsh answered that flexibility is exactly what generalization and conditioned reinforcement produce, and higher is not a count of mechanisms: perception of relations is higher than associative memory, so the lower explanation wins when it fits. Dr. Ellison said if the canon is just methodological, it cannot decide which account fits, because fit is the very thing in dispute; if it is substantive, it presupposes a scala naturae whose levels are antecedently clear, and they are not. Dr. Marsh said it is only a tiebreaker: when two accounts both save all the data, choose the lower one. Dr. Ellison said the two accounts do not both save all the data, since the bird's performance in control conditions with an occluded observer is part of the data, and the lower account buys that fit only by adding a new rule. They turned to me, and I honestly could not referee. Please go through their exchange and adjudicate each move, not just the overall winner: who is right about Morgan's canon, who is right about parsimony and fit, and who is more right overall?
