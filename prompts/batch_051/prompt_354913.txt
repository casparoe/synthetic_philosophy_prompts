My research group is about to spend the semester on the ethics of griefbots, and we have agreed to write down the standard of adequacy before anyone is allowed to defend a view. Write the desiderata that a theory of the ethics of digitally simulating the dead must meet to count as adequate. Cover at least the following cases: a simulation created posthumously from a person's messages without their prior consent; a simulation commissioned by the person while alive; a bereaved partner who keeps the simulation active in daily family life; and a commercial recreation of a public figure made over the family's objections. Specify the distinctions such a theory must not collapse — representing or commemorating the dead versus continuing a relationship with them; the interests of the bereaved versus the posthumous interests of the dead; private use versus public or commercial use. For each desideratum give the motivating case or consideration, mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps, and flag any pairs of desiderata that cannot be jointly satisfied. Keep the weight on the list and its internal tensions rather than on scoring named positions. Please answer in American English.
