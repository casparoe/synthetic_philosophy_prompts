I've been arguing with a friend about whether a future AI system — one that can hold assets, enter into contracts, bring or defend lawsuits, and act in the world with roughly the autonomy of an ordinary company — should count as a legal person. Every time we get anywhere, one of us brings up corporations: they're legal persons, they can't suffer or hold beliefs, and they already own property, make contracts, and get sued, so why should the bar suddenly be higher for an AI? I get that it's a nice bit of leverage, but I'm not sure it's earned, because neither of us actually has a worked-out theory of what legal personhood is for. And the law has already stretched the category all over the place — ships can be sued in rem, estates can be parties to litigation, the state itself is a kind of person — so 'person' in the legal sense plainly isn't a synonym for 'human being.'

So before we resume the AI fight I want the spec. Suppose the question is whether a future AI system like the one above should be recognized as a legal person of some kind in a common-law-ish legal system. What would an adequate theory of legal personhood have to get right to answer that? Give me the desiderata list: the cases it has to handle (natural humans, corporations, ships, estates — and maybe the stray river or idol that some jurisdiction has given person-like standing to, if you think those are data rather than noise), the distinctions it can't collapse (between being a right-holder, a duty-bearer, a property-owner, and a litigant; between legal personality and moral personhood; between capacity and status; between the artificial entity and the humans behind it), and the results of existing doctrine it has to be consistent with (limited liability, the separate patrimony of a corporation, the fact that a company can only act and be at fault through human agents, whatever else you'd add). For each desideratum, say why it's a requirement and whether it's negotiable or load-bearing. Then tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular positions in the AI-personhood debate, and whether you think the full list is jointly satisfiable — or whether the list itself suggests that asking 'is an AI a person?' is badly posed.
