Not an academic, just someone whose been turning this over on my own after reading some popular history. I want to get clear about what a good explanation of a moral revolution even has to do before I pick sides. Take dueling: for a couple centuries it was how gentlemen defended their honor, and by 1900 most people just thought of it as a weird murder-suicide ritual. Or slavery: normal and built into economies for most of history, then over the course of a century or so the Atlantic world flipped to treating it as an abomination. The stories people tell seem to be three kinds: it was arguments (people read the critics and got convinced), it was interests (slavery got less profitable, dueling got pointless once courts and credit ratings took over), or it was honor and status (the people who still did it started looking low-status or ridiculous, so it died of embarrassment). I dont want to score these yet. I want the full list of jobs an adequate explanation of a moral revolution has to do before anyone gets to claim they've explained one.
