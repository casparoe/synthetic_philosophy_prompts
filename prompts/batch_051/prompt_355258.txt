I keep going back and forth on this argument that moral uncertainty is kind of empty, and I cant locate the flaw. (1) If I'm torn between two moral theories, the rational move is to pick the act with the highest expected moral value, weighing each theory's verdict by how confident I am in that theory. (2) But expected moral value across theories only makes sense if one theory's wrongness can be put on the same scale as another's. (3) Kantian wrongness and utilitarian wrongness aren't amounts of the same thing: nothing says how many broken promises equal how much lost utility. (4) So cross-theory expected value is undefined, at least for those pairs. (5) Therefore, in any real dilemma where Kantianism and utilitarianism disagree, moral uncertainty has nothing to say about what to do. I half-suspect the conclusion is right, but I also feel like I've cheated somewhere. Is the argument valid? If not, say exaclty which step fails and what the corrected version of that step would be.
