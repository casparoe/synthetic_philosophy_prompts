So I've been trying to think about Everything Everywhere All at Once through the debate between counterpart theory and transworld identity, and I keep going back and forth. On the one hand the movie sounds like straight transworld identity: there is one Evelyn, the person from the laundromat, and verse-jumping just lets her tap into the lives that very same individual has in other worlds. On the other hand the alternate Evelyns are so different — the movie star, the teppanyaki chef, the rock — that it is hard to say they are literally the same person with different properties rather than distinct people related by similarity, the way Lewis's counterparts are. And then there is Jobu Tupaki, who somehow contains or experiences every version at once, which seems to break both theories rather than choose between them. I'd like a careful reading that does not just declare which theory the film illustrates. Go through the film and say where it treats the selves as one person who could have been otherwise, where it treats them as distinct individuals picked out by a context-sensitive resemblance relation, and where it is genuinely ambivalent or inconsistent. Also: what is doing the verse-jumping, on each view, and does the film ever tell you? End by saying what the movie adds to the philosophical debate that the formalism misses — I suspect its something about the emotional cost of each account, but I want to know if that is fair or if I'm reading the philosophy back into the film.
