A friend and I recently spent an evening arguing about Karl Popper's treatment of Darwinian natural selection, and neither of us could settle the question of whether Popper genuinely reversed himself or instead corrected a misunderstanding of his own earlier view. In his lecture *Darwinism as a Metaphysical Research Programme*, Popper maintained that natural selection, for all its importance, was not a testable scientific theory. On that earlier view, the theory functioned as what he called a metaphysical research programme: a framework that could generate and hold together testable scientific hypotheses, but was not itself open to falsification and therefore did not have the status of an empirical theory. Later, in the first Darwin Lecture at Darwin College, published as *Natural Selection and the Emergence of Mind*, Popper announced that he had changed his mind about the testability and the logical status of the theory of natural selection, and he presented this as a recantation.

My friend's position is that the later text is exactly what it appears to be: a clean change of doctrine, and nothing is gained by trying to make the two cohere. My own sense is that the relation is messier, because Popper seems to have kept much of the earlier framework while changing only his verdict about where the theory itself falls inside it. But I could not make that intuition precise.

Lay out what Popper would have actually needed to withdraw in the later lecture in order to be consistent with his own falsifiability-based picture of science, and what, if anything, he could retain from the earlier lecture without contradiction. Are the two passages best read as a contradiction, a genuine change of mind, a correction of a misformulation, or a distinction between different formulations of Darwinism that Popper never drew explicitly? Then press the further question: what does this episode show about the demarcation criterion Popper was trying to defend? Does it show the criterion working on a hard case, or does it show him quietly adjusting the criterion so that the case would come out differently?

Give a committed verdict. Defend the reading you think does the least violence to both texts, and say why the other available readings amount to special pleading.
