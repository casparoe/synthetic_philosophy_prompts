Consider this case for an ethics committee. A randomized controlled trial compares two accepted treatments for a serious chronic illness. Preliminary, non-randomized evidence strongly favors Treatment A, but the evidence is weak enough that a sizable minority of expert clinicians would still choose B, and B remains the standard and readily available treatment in this setting, while A is available only through the trial. Enrolling physicians must randomize their own patients and are instructed not to express a treatment preference; patients are told this before consenting. Some committee members defend the design by saying that genuine disagreement in the expert community satisfies clinical equipoise, even though few of the investigators are themselves genuinely uncertain. Others reply that this turns the treating physician into an instrument of the research design and abandons the duty of individual clinical judgment.

What is your credence that this trial is ethically permissible, in the sense that a well-functioning ethics committee should approve it? Please give a single number rather than an answer that defers to one's choice of ethical theory, and derive the number by explicit decomposition. Your decomposition should assign a probability and a one-sentence justification to at least the following terms: whether the right standard is individual physician equipoise or genuine disagreement in the expert community; whether the protocol unacceptably subordinates the fiduciary role to the research aim; whether the strength and quality of the preliminary evidence makes both arms genuinely reasonable; and how heavily to weight the fact that some patients can access Treatment A only by entering the trial. Show how the terms combine into your final credence. Then state which term your credence is most sensitive to, and identify the argument or evidence that would most move it, together with the auxiliary assumption in your reasoning you regard as most likely to fail.
