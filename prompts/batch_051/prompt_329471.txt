So I got into a long argument with a friend last night about whether you could have a genuine friendship or even a romantic relationship with an AI chatbot. She kept saying it was just Nozick's experience machine: sure it feels nice, but none of it is real, you would just be living in a simulation of a relationship, and the whole point of the experience machine argument is that most of us would still refuse to plug in because we want actual contact with something real. I pushed back by changing the case, and now I can't figure out weather the argument still works.

Here's the variation I proposed. Suppose the experience machine isn't the usual pre-scripted deal where every person you meet is a lightweight simulation of a human. Instead, the machine hosts a fully running social world whose other inhabitants are genuinely conscious AI minds. They have their own preferences, moods, commitments, and histories; they can be hurt or delighted by what you do; they form long-term relationships with each other and with you. They are, let's just stipulate, Turing-passing in daily life: you genuinely cannot tell them apart from humans by how they talk or act. They are not scripts and they are not secretly controlled by an operator; each one is a real agent, and, importantly, they also do not know they are on a machine. You, the person deciding whether to plug in, know all of this in advance. Everything else about the experience machine is unchanged: plugging in means leaving ordinary physical reality for a life conducted through the machine, and once you are in, you cannot get out.

Now, does Nozick's conclusion still follow? Which of the usual strands survive: the strand about wanting to actually do things and not merely have the experience of doing them, the strand about wanting to be a certain sort of person, and the strand about wanting contact with a deeper reality? Or does the presence of these other genuine agents gut that third strand, and if so, does that matter for the overall verdict about plugging in? I'm especially interested in what this version says about AI companion chatbots. If you would still refuse to plug in even though the machine's AIs are genuinely real agents, is that because the relationships are still somehow second-rate, or because of something else entirely — and does that something else apply to a chatbot you meet while staying in ordinary reality? Work through the two cases side by side and tell me whether the argument transfers. Oh, and please write the answer in American English.
