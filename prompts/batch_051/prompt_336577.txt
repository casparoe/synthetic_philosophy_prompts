Lately I've been using an AI assistant to help me think through a career change and some family stuff, and I've settled into a rule: if I can't follow the reasoning myself, I don't let the output move me — a confident "the answer is X" or an explanation that's just smooth enough to feel good doesn't count. My partner says this is a taste for stories over truth, and that if the model's answers have a good track record on questions where I can already check the answer, I should defer even when I can't audit the reasoning; by demanding readability I'm punishing the model for being accurate-but-not-a-teacher, and anyway the explainations might just be optimized to look reassuring to me. I think we're both partly right. Is my rule defensible? When is a model's output being legible actually evidence about whether the answer is correct, rather than just a separate virtue I'm confusing with truth — and is there a version of the rule that keeps whatever is good in it without throwing away accurate-but-unglamorous answers?
