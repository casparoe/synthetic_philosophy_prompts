I am a sixth-form pupil, and I have a recurring observation that I would like identified properly. I keep noticing a situation with the same underlying shape. Two people, or two groups, begin competing for something of genuinely small value: a particular pitch at the school fair, a named role at an event, or the right to say that their class won a trivial contest. Both sides make modest initial commitments of time, money, or public effort. Once those commitments have been made, neither will stop, because stopping would mean accepting that everything already spent has been wasted, and would also hand the prize to the other side by default. Each new step is therefore taken less because the prize is worth the additional cost and more to protect the earlier investment and avoid being the one who gives in. Both sides can see that they are now spending more than the object is worth, and both would have been happy at the outset to settle the matter with a coin toss. The competition nonetheless runs on, because at every point the person who withdraws is left with no chance of recovering their earlier contribution, while the other at least gains the thing, even if its value is far below what both have now spent.

I have seen the same shape in arguments between friends, where each has already conceded ground or produced examples, so that ending the argument now would make those earlier contributions seem wasted and would let the other person's account stand; each further reply is motivated less by the point of disagreement than by the fact that stopping would turn the previous replies into a loss.

Is there an established name for this particular configuration? I can see that it is close to the familiar advice not to keep throwing resources at a failing project merely because one has already invested in it, but I want to know whether the competitive, two-person version has its own name, how exactly it differs from the single-person case, and where the boundary lies between a genuine instance and a case that is simply two people being stubborn. I would value a careful account of the nearest established ideas and the edges of the concept more than a label on its own.
