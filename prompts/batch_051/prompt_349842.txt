I hold the view that a musical work is constituted by a normative sonic profile—roughly, a set of constraints on pitch, rhythm, timbre, and sequence fixed by the work’s score or canonical recording—and that any sound event satisfying that profile to an appropriate degree is an instance of the work, regardless of its causal provenance. On this view, a painstaking performance of a Mahler symphony on historical instruments in a reconstructed venue and an otherwise indistinguishable rendering produced by a generative model trained on recordings of the symphony are performances of the same work in exactly the same sense. What would count against this view? I want a map of its exposure: which observations, arguments, or intuitions from musical practice should move me, sorted by how decisive they are; where defenders of the view would themselves concede tension rather than dismissing the objection as external; and an honest verdict on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—for example by making “appropriate degree” and “normative profile” elastic enough to absorb every counterexample—and whether that would be a problem for a view of this kind.
