Give me a working definition of the phrase "the evidence favors one scientific theory over another" — that is, of what it is for a body of evidence to support one competing theory better than another — that gets the following four cases right. My provisional verdicts are not a fixed answer key; tell me where your definition forces you to depart from them.

Case 1: Two hypotheses are fitted to the same set of data points. One is a straight line with two free parameters that captures the trend but leaves small residuals. The other is a high-degree polynomial with as many adjustable parameters as there are data points, so it passes through every point exactly. I think the evidence favors the straight line, even though its fit is worse.

Case 2: Two theories fit all existing data equally well. Theory A predicted in advance that a new experiment would yield a specific value, and the experiment came out as predicted. Theory B was modified only after that result was announced, by adding an adjustable parameter that lets it accommodate the value. I think the evidence favors A over B.

Case 3: Two theories issue exactly the same predictions about every observable measurement; they differ only in the unobservable machinery each one posits behind those predictions. I think the evidence favors neither.

Case 4: The standard account of the universe as billions of years old, versus the hypothesis that the universe began five minutes ago with geological strata, carbon ratios, light apparently arriving from distant stars, and human memories all already in place. Both are consistent with every observation. I do not know whether the evidence favors the standard account or leaves the two tied.

Propose your definition, run it through all four cases, say where it gives a surprising verdict or no verdict, and either revise it or defend the surprising verdict.

Format your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state the proposed definition. Items 2 through 5 should address Case 1 through Case 4, in order. Item 5 should also say which of your four verdicts you are least willing to defend, and either revise the definition or defend that verdict.
