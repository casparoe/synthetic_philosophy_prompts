I'm in a philosophy of cognitive science course and we've been going back and forth about Morgan's canon — the old rule of thumb that you shouldn't read a 'higher' mental ability into an animal's behavior when a 'lower' one would cover the behavior just as well. I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe by going with my gut on cases, but my own verdicts are starting to look kind of inconsistent.

Here's where my gut lands, roughly. A rat in a T-maze that gets faster after reward and slower after no reward: I'm fine saying it just learned an association, nothing more. But my roommate's dog, who whines at the back door when he needs to go out and glances over his shoulder if nobody gets up: I genuinely think he wants something and knows we can help. The digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow and, if a researcher nudges the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside inspecting the burrow, comes out and repeats the exact same drag-and-inspect loop over and over: that one I won't describe as thinking at all, it's clearly a fixed routine. But an octopus unscrewing a puzzle box lid after watching another octopus do it feels like actual problem solving. A honeybee doing the waggle dance to tell nestmates where flowers are by distance and direction: I want to say it's 'information,' but I'd feel weird saying the bee believes the flowers are over there. A scrub jay that hides worms while other jays are watching and later moves them once it's alone: I keep going back and forth, but my gut says its somehow knows it was seen. And my neighbor's dog that cowers after tipping over the trash, ears back and eyes averted: I do not think it feels bad about breaking the 'rule'; it's just reacting to my neighbor's tone and posture.

Is there one principle that gets all or most of these, or am I just using words like 'thinks' and 'knows' wildly inconsistently? What would that principle say about cases I didn't list — like a rat that hesitates at a choice point after passing up the better option and seems to glance back: regret, or just a reset? And which of my gut calls has to go if I want the whole set to hang together? Please give the answer in American English.
