Thomson's violinist argument and Marquis's future-like-ours argument both seem to presuppose that a fetus can be kept alive only inside the pregnant person, so that the practical choice is between continuing the pregnancy and ending a fetal life. Suppose working ectogenesis removes that presupposition: an embryo or fetus can be transferred to an artificial womb and develop normally. Does Thomson's argument still go through? It seems aimed at a world in which unplugging the violinist just is letting him die, and ectogenesis changes what unplugging amounts to. I want to know which premise actually bears the weight, whether there is a repaired version that still underwrites a right to end a pregnancy, or whether the abortion-rights position must fall back on a different claim, such as a right not to become a genetic parent. Does Marquis's argument become stronger once the fetus's valuable future can be secured without imposing pregnancy, or does it merely shift the question from whether one may abort to whether one must transfer?
