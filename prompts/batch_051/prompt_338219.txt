Section 7 of the Standing Defence Orders of the Republic provides: "If the National Warning Centre reports that a nuclear attack has been launched against the Republic, the President shall immediately order a nuclear strike against the state from which the attack was launched."

The provision was drafted when the Warning Centre's reports were produced solely by radar and infrared sensors, which detect missiles only after launch. Its acknowledged purpose was to leave the President no discretion, on the theory that an adversary would be less likely to attack if it knew that retaliation would follow automatically and could not be withheld. The Warning Centre has since been supplemented by a machine-learning system that issues reports not only when a missile is detected in flight, but also when it infers from satellite imagery, signals intercepts, and adversary force movements that a launch sequence has begun. This system has sometimes reported that an attack had been launched when operators could find no missile in flight and could not reconstruct or explain the system's inference.

At 03:40 one morning the system reports to the President that a nuclear attack has been launched against the Republic. No radar or infrared sensor shows a missile in flight; the operators asked to verify say they cannot explain the system's report or determine whether it is responding to real indicators or to an artifact. Does the provision require the President to strike? Address the literal wording, the purpose of the provision, what the drafters would most likely have said if asked about this situation, and what a reasonable officer would understand the provision to require. Then say which approach you find most defensible and why.
