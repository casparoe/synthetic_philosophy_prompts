I keep getting pulled into fairness reviews for models at work, and the part I always get stuck on is the list of "sensitive" features. Everyone agrees race, sex, disability, and religion go on the list. Eye color or what sports team you root for clearly don't. But the in-between cases keep tripping me up, and I've realized that when someone says "obviously X is a protected characteristic" I don't actually have a rule for what that means—I'm just going by vibes.

So: give me a working definition of a "protected characteristic" (or "sensitive attribute," whatever term you prefer) that tells me, for any trait, wether it should be treated as protected. Then run the definition through the cases below and say which ones it gets right, which ones it gets wrong or just has nothing to say about, and whether you'd rather fix the definition or defend the surprising call.

Clear yes: race, sex, religion, disability, sexual orientation.

Clear no: eye color, whether someone can roll their tongue, which sports team they root for.

The ones I actually need help with: height, weight, age, being pregnant (including an inferred pregnancy score from shopping data), a felony conviction, education level, political affiliation, genetic risk for Alzheimer's, autism, caste, citizenship, smoking status, credit history, and a strong foreign accent.

At the end, tell me which single verdict you're least sure about. If your definition gives you a conclusion that feels obviously wrong on one of these, say so—I want to know where the seams are.

Oh, and don't just tell me what the law currently says in some country. Laws differ, and I'm asking what the underlying rule should be, not what a statute happens to say right now.
