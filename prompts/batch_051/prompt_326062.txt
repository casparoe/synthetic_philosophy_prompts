I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I've been reading my way through a long-running dispute in economics and decision theory: whether decisions under uncertainty should be evaluated by ensemble averages or by time averages. I want to run, as the centerpiece of a feature, the document the two camps would produce if they sat down for a good-faith adversarial collaboration instead of writing past one another.

My rough understanding of the two positions is as follows; correct and refine it where it is imprecise. The ensemble-average camp -- call it the Expected-Value camp -- holds that a strategy or policy should be assessed by its average result across a large set of parallel possible futures, with attitudes toward risk handled by expected utility rather than by ignoring variance. The time-average camp -- call it the Ergodicity-Economics camp -- holds that a single decision-maker lives through one path in time, and that under multiplicative dynamics the quantity governing long-run outcomes is the time-average growth rate rather than the ensemble mean. When the process is non-ergodic, the two averages can diverge sharply: a rule that maximizes the ensemble mean can steer an individual toward bets that almost surely destroy wealth. The better practical rule, this camp says, is to maximize time-average growth -- to think in geometric means and in partial, fractional exposure rather than all-in bets.

Please draft the joint public statement that a thoughtful spokesperson from each camp could both sign. Take on the role of a neutral facilitator who is expert in both expected-utility theory and the formal structure of ergodicity, and who can represent each position as its strongest defender would. Before writing the final document, brainstorm the strongest version of each side, the possible cruxes, and the places where the disagreement may be partly verbal.

The statement should have three sections.

1. Numbered agreed claims. These must be substantive rather than vacuous truces, and should cover at minimum: what both sides accept about the mathematics of ensemble and time averages in multiplicative, non-ergodic settings; what the Expected-Value camp is and is not committed to (for example, that expected-value reasoning does not require indifference to variance or to ruin); what the Ergodicity-Economics camp is and is not committed to (for example, that it is not calling the ensemble mean mathematically wrong, only that it can mislead in non-ergodic settings); and each side's central insight, stated in a form the other side can affirm.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, give the neutral formulation of the question and each side's position phrased so that its own defenders would accept it as fair wording of their view.

3. The cruxes. List the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, specify the concrete case, numerical illustration, or decision setting that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

As a matter of epistemic discipline, be conservative about what you count as agreed and about what you claim each side would sign. If a formulation is one a side would only grudgingly tolerate, or if you cannot confidently state that it reflects their strongest view, flag that rather than presenting it as settled. The document is for a sophisticated lay audience: explain technical terms such as non-ergodicity when they first appear, and use no footnotes, citations, or named papers. Where part of the dispute is verbal -- for example, a normative question about what an agent ought to maximize versus a descriptive claim about what long-run survivors or populations do -- say which part and why, without pretending that dissolves every real disagreement.

If my framing is itself confused -- if, say, the ensemble/time-average divide does not line up with the normative/descriptive divide, or the two camps are really answering different questions -- tell me that plainly rather than handing me a tidy document that misrepresents the debate.
