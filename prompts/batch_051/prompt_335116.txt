I was recently elected to the governing board (session) of my Presbyterian church, and for the first time I have to help decide what belongs in corporate worship rather than just argue about it. Our church holds the regulative principle: the service may include only what Scripture prescribes, so that what is not prescribed is, for worship, prohibited; arrangements required for decency and good order (times, seating, lighting, and the like) are allowed. After a year of debate I have settled on four marks that distinguish a forbidden addition from a mere circumstance. Something counts as a forbidden addition if it is (1) part of the church's stated weekly order; (2) something the congregation does or undergoes together, not merely a practical arrangement for entering and leaving; (3) presented to the congregation, in the order itself or in official descriptions, as an act of worship rather than as a logistical aid; and (4) not prescribed, exemplified, or necessarily implied for public worship in Scripture.

The case: six months ago our session authorized a minute of covenant silence after the benediction. The liturgist says, "Let us keep silence before the Lord for one minute, to carry the word with us." The session describes it as a circumstance of order, like how chairs are arranged, to help people leave reverently; but our minister has since called it a distinct act of worship in a newsletter, and the congregation is split. Does the minute of silence qualify as a forbidden addition under my four criteria? Work criterion by criterion; where the criteria do not settle the matter, say whether the criteria or the case should give way; and tell me what you would advise the session to rule.

Approach this as a philosopher of religion and conceptual analyst would. Begin by brainstorming the strongest considerations on both sides, then apply the criteria in order, and flag every point at which your verdict is underdetermined rather than pretending it is forced.
