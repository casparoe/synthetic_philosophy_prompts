I'm putting together a handout for a unit on sweatshop ethics and I want to seperate two theories that people keep running together. Help me build a case, or really a pair of cases, to spec.

The two views I'm trying to pull apart are these. Terms-fairness view: an offer is exploitative only if its terms are unfair relative to the relevant benchmark — here, the local competitive going rate for that kind of work, pay and conditions included. Vulnerability-use view: an offer is exploitative if the employer knowingly leans on the worker's serious neediness to get a yes they wouldn't otherwise give, even if the terms themselves are fair.

I want two cases, identical except one variable. In both: same town, same garment factory, same unglamorous stitching job with high turnover, same hours, same conditions, same worker — a woman whose village has just had a crop failure, and whose outside option is a real but bad one (subsistence farming that'll leave her kids hungry for a year). The pay is the local going rate for that exact work, maybe a hair above to be safe, and the conditions are standard for the district. No deception, no threats, and her consent is perfectly voluntary.

In Case A she shows up at the gate on her own, and the hiring manager has no idea about the crop failure; she's hired like anyone else. In Case B the factory knows about the crop failure and sends a recruiter specifically to the crop-failure villages, betting that people there will accept this high-turnover stitching work that they'd normally pass up — not because the pay is worse than the going rate (it isn't, not even a little), but because their desperation has lowered how long they'll hold out for something better. The recruiter doesn't lie about the job or the pay; he just picks that population.

I want the pair to split the two theories: the terms-fairness view should say neither case is exploitation, and the vulnerability-use view should say Case A isn't exploitation but Case B is. Do the check for me: state the offer and the fallback, then walk through why each view gives each verdict.

Then tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, what Case B collapses into if you delete just that one feature, and whether the terms-fairness defender can reply "the recruiting strategy is part of the terms" without quietly becoming the vulnerability-use view. And finish by saying whether a vulnerability-use person can really mark the A/B difference without secretly assuming the pay was too low after all — be honest, because if the distinction is forced I want to know.
