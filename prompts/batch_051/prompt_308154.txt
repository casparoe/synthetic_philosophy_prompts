I keep poking at when I can "go along" with a story's say-so and when I can't, and I want to know whether there's a principle underneath my gut reactions. Some cases:

(a) A fantasy novel says a potion lets you remember being a fetus—fine, I'll imagine it.
(b) A story says a spaceship gets to Andromeda in an afternoon—fine.
(c) A novel quotes a poem that's plainly trite and clunky, then says the poem is a transcendent masterpiece—I balk; I can't make myself imagine it's good.
(d) A story says a man who flays children alive for fun is morally good—I balk hard, like the story is broken rather than just wrong.
(e) A story set among a warlike alien species says they regard "mercy" as a shameful weakness—I can play along with that fine.
(f) A story says a character finds a dull gray rock achingly beautiful—no problem.
But (g) a story set in a fictional human society says its people regard torturing children as the highest virtue, and the narrator seems to endorse it—I'm not sure whether I balk or just read it as another nasty-alien-culture thing.

Is there a principle that gets (a)–(f) right, and what does it say about (g)? Also, what would it say about a story that insists that in its world 2 + 2 = 5? And which of my gut calls, if any, do I have to give up?
