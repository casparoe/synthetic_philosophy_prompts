I'm drafting a novel about a small anti-surveillance collective, and I cant decide whether what they do in the middle of the book counts as terrorism. The definition I carry around has four pieces: an act of violence or a credible threat of violence; a political, religious, or ideological aim; a target that's civilian or at least not combatants; and an intent to intimidate or coerce a wider audience beyond the immediate target. In the scene, the group breaks into the control software for a city's water treatment plant and messes with the chlorination for about six hours. The city has to issue a boil-water advisory for a few hundred thousand people, but nobody gets sick. Afterward they post a statement saying it was a warning: if the city doesn't scrap its planned facial-recognition network, next time they won't be so careful. So the aim and the intimidation condition are obvious. The hard part is the first one: there's no physical violence, though there is a serious threat to public safety. Is this terrorism? If you have to stretch "violence" to include it, what does the stretched definition say about a hack that only causes economic disruption—say, briefly freezing a stock exchange out of protest against capital punishment? Should that count too, and if not, what's doing the work to keep it out?
