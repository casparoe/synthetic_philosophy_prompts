For my upper-level seminar on the explanation of mass violence, I am setting an essay question on the debate between Christopher Browning and Daniel Goldhagen about Reserve Police Battalion 101, and I want the essay to get past the standard situation-versus-culture summary. Write an essay for this seminar that defends a definite conclusion about where the explanatory weight falls. The background is this: in 1942, in German-occupied Poland, the battalion—roughly five hundred men, mostly middle-aged, working-class Hamburg reservists drafted into the Order Police and judged unfit for front-line service—was ordered to round up Jewish civilians for deportation and, when there was no room on the trains, to shoot them; in other actions it was simply ordered to kill a specified number of Jews in a given town. When the commander gave the men the opportunity to excuse themselves from one of the early killing operations, at the village of Józefów, only a minority stepped out, and, as Goldhagen himself stresses, refusal brought no serious punishment. Browning treats this record as evidence that ordinary men, under situational pressure, can be brought to kill; Goldhagen treats it as evidence that the men were shaped by a specifically German political culture of eliminationist antisemitism that left them not merely compliant but willing.

That is the controversy your essay should adjudicate. In doing so, first reconstruct the strongest defensible version of each position—Browning's as more than a bare appeal to obedience, and Goldhagen's as more than an assertion that the men hated their victims. Second, address the features of the record that each thesis must handle: compliance was high in a unit not selected for ideological fanaticism; a minority declined without being punished; and among those who participated, involvement varied from eagerness through reluctant endurance to having to be driven along. Explain what each account predicts or must stipulate about that variation, and at what cost. Third, distinguish four potential loci of explanation—immediate situational pressure, the institutional culture of the Order Police, wider German political culture, and individual character—and consider which does the most explanatory work in this case. Conclude by defending a specific allocation of explanatory weight among these factors, and defend it against the strongest objection to it; do not simply end by saying that everything interacted.
