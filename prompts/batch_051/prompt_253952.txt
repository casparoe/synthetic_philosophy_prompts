In my city, the landmarks commission will not permit a protected building to be altered unless the applicant can document what problem each contested feature was originally meant to solve; if nobody can state the original rationale, the feature is presumed to have had one and must stay. The same commission, however, regularly approves the demolition of ordinary mid-century buildings after a single cost-benefit study, with no inquiry into the reasoning behind their design. Reconstruct the view of inherited arrangements — the epistemic authority of the past and what makes an institution worth keeping — that the first practice presupposes. Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say which rival views it quietly rules out and where it is philosophically underdetermined, and then explain what the second practice would have to look like if the city actually held the view the first practice seems to express.
