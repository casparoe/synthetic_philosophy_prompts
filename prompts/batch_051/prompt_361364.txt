I've been trying to get my head around the regulative principle of worship and I keep hitting the same wall. I can see why someone would say worship should be directed by God rather than by whatever we happen to feel like doing. But the strict version says whatever is not commanded in Scripture is forbidden in worship, and that feels like a huge leap. Why should scriptural silence count as prohibition rather than permission? Like, suppose a church adds a short candle-lighting thing before the sermon, not because they think it's required, but because they think it helps the congregation slow down and pay attention. The regulative principle seems to say that's exactly the kind of thing you can't do. What's the deepest conceptual reason for that? Is it mostly about guarding against idolatry, about the nature of corporate worship as a covenant meeting, about God's authority, or is it something more like a rule of epistemic humility? I'm not looking for proof-texts or a history lesson, more an explanation of the structure of the idea so I can see why someone would find the strict reading compelling.
