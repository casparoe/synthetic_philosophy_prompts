I cover science and the ideas beat at a general-interest magazine, so about once a week I have to turn a press release about a heritability study into a story, usually with a couple hours to deadline and an editor who knows even less genetics than I do. The releases (and sometimes the papers themselves) say things like "the heritability of depression is 40 percent" or "educational attainment is 30 percent genetic," and then glide straight into conclusions about whether a trait can be changed, or why groups differ. I know enough to smell that something is off, but not enough to say exactly what, and I dont want my fix to just be "add a caveat paragraph and move on."

I'd like a reusable decision procedure for handling heritability claims: the questions I should ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each one, where the procedure stops with "dont run this claim" or "only run it with this rewording," and which steps are genuinely judgment calls rather than facts I can look up. Ground it in what heritability actually does and doesn't mean — variance in a population vs causes in an individual, heritability vs immutability, within-group vs between-group inference, twin-based vs SNP-based estimates, the way a heritability number depends on the range of environments in the sample — but make it usable by me on deadline, not a term paper.

Then run the procedure on three hypothetical stories: (1) a twin study finding BMI is about 70 percent heritable in adults in a wealthy country; (2) a press release arguing that because reading ability is highly heritable, intensive phonics instruction can only do so much; (3) a study reporting different heritability estimates for the same test score in two schools with very different funding levels. These stories shape how readers think about education, health, and inequality, so I need the procedure to catch the claims that would mislead even if they are technically true as far as they go. And be honest about where the procedure will force me to say something I would rather not, or where it simply wont settle the question and I have to make the call myself.
