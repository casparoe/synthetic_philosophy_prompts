hey, i'm putting together a philosophy of social science grad seminar session on methodological individualism versus holism, and for the warm-up handout i'd like a 'which position do you actually hold?' sorting quiz. eight questions, each built around a concrete social-science case, three answer options per question, plus a scoring key at the end. the quiz has to sort people by their case judgments rather than by which label makes them feel sophisticated, so avoid any question that's just 'do societies exist' or 'is all macro explanation shorthand'. the particular angle i care about: students come in conflating three debates — explanation (can social facts be explained exhaustively from individual facts?), causation (do social structures have causal powers, or are macro causes just compressed descriptions of distributed micro causes?), and ontology (are collectives real, and in what sense). build the quiz so the scoring separates those three axes instead of collapsing everyone onto a single individualist-to-holist spectrum.

please use these cases, one per question, lightly fictionalized if that helps: a bank run that no single depositor intended; a schelling-style neighborhood tipping model; a corporation deciding to relocate a factory; a team's 'culture' being invoked to explain its performance; a high school clique's dress-code norm; a traffic jam that forms even though no driver wanted one; a language change over two generations; a state going to war. for each case, the three options should run from something like 'the macro description is a useful summary of individual actions and does no causal work of its own' through 'the macro pattern really constrains individuals but is carried entirely by them' to 'the macro level does causal work that cannot be decomposed away.' vary the wording so nobody can pattern-match their way into a label.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least six positions: strict explanatory individualism; non-reductive individualism on which social facts supervene on individual facts but macro explanations are epiphenomenal or merely pragmatically useful; methodological localism (individuals are the only engines, but individuals always come already equipped with social properties — roles, networks, internalized norms — so structures act through them rather than above them); emergentism with downward causation (social structures have causal powers that feed back onto individuals); strong social holism on which organizations such as firms or states are genuine causal agents; and a pragmatic pluralism bucket for inconsistent or case-by-case answer patterns. for each position, add one sentence on its most awkward commitment. where a single answer pattern is consistent with two positions, say so and spell out the follow-up question that would seperate them. also include a brief note on combinations almost nobody occupies — for instance, someone who accepts downward causation while denying that social facts supervene on individual facts — and diagnose whether that combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. if any position in your matrix has no real defenders, call it unnamed rather than inventing a label.

oh and please write the whole thing in american english. a typo or two in the handout is fine, obviously.
