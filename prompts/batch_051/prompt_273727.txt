Ok so in biology we did ENCODE and the whole junk DNA fight. The consortium found that most of the genome shows some biochemical activity — stuff like getting transcribed or binding proteins — and the press said junk DNA was dead, while other scientists said no, showing that a stretch of DNA does something in the cell doesn't mean it's actually for anything. I went down a rabbit hole and found the philosophical version of this row: there seem to be two accounts of function running through it. The etiological account says a bit of DNA has a function only if natural selection maintained it because of that effect. The causal-role account says it has a function if it does something in the current system that contributes to some larger capacity, regardless of how it arose. Please analyse the ENCODE/junk DNA arguement through those two accounts. Work out which side is using which account (and where each side quietly slides between them), what each account would say about a stretch of DNA that gets transcribed but hasn't been selected for that, and whether ENCODE settled anything or just changed the subject. Then run it the other way: does the episode show the etiological account is too strict, the causal-role account too permissive, or both — and how much of the fight is really about the word 'function' instead of the actual genome?
