Consider the claim that truth-seeking is, for a typical reflective adult, valuable only because of what it tends to produce—accurate models, coordination, planning, and similar downstream goods—and that the felt experience of truth as worth pursuing for its own sake is a motivational byproduct of that usefulness rather than the detection of a distinct non-instrumental value. Call this the Instrumental Truth Thesis (ITT).

Please give me your credence in ITT. Treat ITT as amounting to the conjunction of the following four claims, and decompose your credence into these terms, giving a definite number and a one-line justification for each:

1. The probability that felt intrinsic valuing can, in general, be produced by internalized instrumental reward, so that the first-person experience of something as mattering for its own sake underdetermines whether it really has non-instrumental value.
2. The probability, conditional on (1) being true, that truth-seeking has a payoff profile rich and stable enough to have been internalized in this way in ordinary human development—that is, that it pays often and regularly enough to become a felt terminal concern.
3. The probability, conditional on (1) and (2) being true, that truth-seeking's apparently disinterested manifestations—pursuing costly truths with no practical use, honoring truthfulness when honesty is expensive, caring about accuracy in low-stakes domains—are nonetheless adequately explained by the instrumental story.
4. The probability, conditional on (1), (2), and (3) being true, that no non-instrumental value of truth-seeking remains once that psychological story is granted—that the instrumental explanation is the full story of the value.

Then show how (1)–(4) combine into your credence in ITT, with the arithmetic made explicit.

Now commit. Which of the four terms are you least confident in, and what concrete evidence or argument would most move that number? Finally, state whether, on the same analysis, tradition and purity would each receive a higher or lower credence of being merely instrumental than truth-seeking, with one sentence of justification for each.
