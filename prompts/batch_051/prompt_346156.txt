I'm an undergrad in a course called "Language, Truth, and Machines," and we've been doing Frankfurt's *On Bullshit* next to all the recent stuff about whether LLM hallucinations count as lying, bullshit, or something else. I feel like every paper or blog post I read lands in one of the same few buckets, and I can't tell wether I'm missing an option or whether the taxonomy is just exhaustive. The buckets I keep seeing are:

(1) Hallucination isn't lying or bullshit in Frankfurt's strict sense, because both require an agent with beliefs, intentions, and some stance toward the truth, and a language model has none of those; it's just a statistical system producing bad output, i.e., an error or malfunction.

(2) The functional "stochastic parrot"/bullshit-machine line: even if no individual agent is indifferent to truth, the whole system is best described as a bullshit generator because it is trained to produce fluent, plausible text without any mechanism that tracks truth; we just need to move "bullshit" from an agent-level vice to a property of the training objective or the system as a whole.

(3) The confabulation view: hallucination is like neuropsychological confabulation, where a system fills gaps with plausible-looking but false content without any intent to deceive. It's a failure under constraints (fluency, coherence, helpfulness) rather than a speech act at all.

(4) The company-as-speaker view: since an LLM isn't a speaker, the real speech act belongs to the developers or company, and deploying a model known to confabulate while presenting its outputs as reliable is the company's bullshit (or even deceptive marketing).

(5) The error/calibration view: hallucination is ordinary epistemic error under uncertainty; the model "sincerely" outputs a falsehood because its confidence is miscalibrated or its probabilities are off, the way an overconfident person speaks falsely without lying or bullshitting.
