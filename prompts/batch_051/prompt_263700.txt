I am a novelist, and for several years I have chaired the jury of a small prize for translated fiction, endowed in the 1950s by a family trust. The trust deed binds the jury to the following procedure, which has never been amended: "The jury shall decide by comparing the shortlisted works two at a time. If the jury judges one work better than another, the first shall be ranked above the second; and if the jury judges one work better than a second, and the second better than a third, the first shall be ranked above the third. The work ranked above all the others shall receive the prize."

This year's shortlist has produced a difficulty the deed's authors plainly never anticipated. The five of us compared the three finalists exactly as the rule requires. A majority judged The Salt Almanac better than A Cartography of Rooms; a different majority judged A Cartography of Rooms better than The Ember Shore; and a third majority judged The Ember Shore better than The Salt Almanac. Each pairwise judgement was made separately, and each seemed, to the majority that made it, clear-cut: the books differ along three axes — fidelity of tone, cadence in English, and originality of interpretation — and in each pairing a different axis dominated, so the loop only became visible when the three verdicts were set side by side. The same loop shows up in my own private ranking, in which each of the three books is better than the next by one standard and worse by another, and the standards have never been coaxed into a single scale. The deed says nothing about what to do when 'ranked above' fails to be transitive; it appears simply to take the transitivity of 'better than' for granted.

I would like a careful opinion on what the rule actually requires or permits here. Read it (a) by its words; (b) by its evident purpose; (c) as its drafters would most plausibly have said if asked about this case; and (d) as a reasonable juror who must now act would read it. In particular, address whether the deed is best understood as committing the jury to the transitivity of 'better than' or merely presupposing it, and whether the cycle should lead us to revise one of the pairwise verdicts, adopt a different procedure, or declare the prize unawarded. British English, please, and finish with a final line of the form 'Summary: <one sentence>' that states your answer in a single sentence.
