For an advanced philosophy of language seminar, I am writing up my commitments on vagueness and the sorites, and I want a consistency audit before I circulate them. My current commitments are these.

(1) Supervaluationism is the right semantics for vague terms. A sentence is true iff it is true on every admissible precisification (a complete, classical sharpening of the vague terms), false iff it is false on every admissible precisification, and neither true nor false otherwise. Classical logic is thereby preserved: every instance of excluded middle is true, and all classical validities hold.

(2) Bivalence fails, but there is no third truth value. "Neither true nor false" is not a value on a par with true and false; it records the fact that no unique precisification has been selected.

(3) Vagueness is not a matter of ignorance. If a man is a borderline case of baldness, there is no hidden fact as to whether he is bald that we fail to know. I reject epistemicism.

(4) There is no sharp cutoff. Take a sorites series running from clearly bald to clearly not bald, and consider the sentence "for some number of hairs n, a person with n hairs is bald and a person with n+1 hairs is not bald." I hold that this sentence is false, not merely unknown, and that its negation is true.

(5) The inductive premise of the sorites should not be dismissed as simply false. The premise "for every n, if a person with n hairs is bald then a person with n+1 hairs is bald" is enormously compelling, and an adequate theory ought to explain that pull rather than treat the premise as a false generalization.

(6) Higher-order vagueness is real. If "definitely p" means that p is true on every admissible precisification, then "definitely" is itself vague: there is no sharp line between admissible and inadmissible precisifications, and no finite hierarchy of definiteness operators at which things become precise.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a defense or a critique of any single view. Go through the commitments; identify the pairs that are in genuine tension and rank them by severity, from those that would force a substantial revision to those that can likely be absorbed. Identify at least one pair that only appears to be in tension, and explain why. For each genuine tension, say which commitment would have to give and describe the least costly revision; if one revision would resolve several tensions at once, say what it is and what it costs. Do not assume at the outset that the set is inconsistent, and do not flatten the differences between severe, mild, and merely apparent tensions.
