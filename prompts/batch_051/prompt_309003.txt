Suppose that within the next ten years a safe, low-cost neural interface is developed that can directly and reversibly stimulate the colour-processing populations of the human visual cortex, bypassing the retina and the early encoding of wavelength ratios. The device is precise enough to drive the opponent mechanisms — the red/green, blue/yellow, and light/dark axes — independently, so that a subject can be put into a stable, repeatable reddish-green state, a colour that no surface or normal light mixture can produce, and likewise a stable bluish-yellow. After extended practice, a minority of trained subjects report something more radical: hues that lie off the ordinary colour circle entirely. They describe moving from red through yellow and green and blue and back to red, and then continuing into a second turn of the hue dimension that is not a repeat of the first and is not reducible to a change in brightness, saturation, or overall pleasantness. Two subjects can match these colours to one another by adjusting the device, but neither can show the colour to anyone who lacks the device, and neither can name it except by rough analogy to familiar hues.

I am less interested in whether the technology is plausible than in a reasoned prediction of what follows if it arrives and spreads. Over the two decades after it becomes widely available, how do colour science, the philosophy of perception, and ordinary practice — colour vocabulary, art, display design, warning signals — actually change? Which consequences do you expect to be robust (and why), which are guesses, and which depend on assumptions about how the new colours relate to familiar ones — for example, whether they are best modelled as points in an enlarged colour space or as a change in the observers' own discrimination capacities? Would the philosophical upshot differ if the novel hues could be communicated only through the device, with no shared external referent and no public sample that everyone sees the same way? Where would you expect convergence, and where lasting controversy?
