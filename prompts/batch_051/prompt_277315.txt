At dinner last week two friends of mine got into it about my cousin Callum, who still loves his brother Dan even though Dan has spent years lying to, stealing from, and badmouthing the whole family, Callum included. I just listened and genuinly couldn't decide who was right.

(1) Sam opened: Callum's love is irrational. Love is a response to what is valuable about the person — warmth, honesty, humour, whatever. Dan has spent years destroying every bit of that, so there's nothing left for the love to answer to. Callum is loving a person who no longer has the properties that would make loving him make sense.

(2) Ruth: That is not how love works. If love just tracked good qualities, it would be a kind of grading, and it would evaporate the moment someone got worse. But real love is partly a gift: loving Dan is what makes Dan's welfare matter to Callum in the first place. The value doesn't have to be read off Dan's character before the love can start.

(3) Sam: Then love is arbitrary. On your view Callum could just as well love a complete stranger, or a cat, or a toaster. And you could never say any particular love is inappropriate, no matter how destructive it is. Also, people do fall in love because someone is kind or funny — I don't see how your view explains that, if the love is doing the bestowing.

(4) Ruth: It isn't arbitrary, because love isn't a one-off judgement — it's a standing relationship with a history. Callum loves Dan because Dan is his brother and they grew up together. That is a reason, but it's not a verdict on Dan's current qualities and it's not a free choice either.

(5) Sam: But being his brother is just a property Dan has. So if that's doing the work, you've just moved the valued feature from his character to the relationship, and your view collapses into mine. And on your own standard the relationship is a disaster, so Callum's love is still irrational.

(6) Ruth: No, because the relationship doesn't have to be good to ground the love. The bare fact that Dan is Callum's brother gives Dan a kind of standing, a claim, independant of whether he deserves it or whether the relationship is good for Callum. You can see perfectly clearly that someone is bad for you and still love them without being irrational.

They went round on whether Ruth had found a third position or just re-dressed Sam's, and I still couldn't sort it out.

It only hit me later that this was the old appraisal-versus-bestowal debate about love's reasons, but neither of them used those words. Please adjudicate the whole thing move by move: for each numbered move, say what's right in it and what's wrong in it, and rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly right. Then say who is more right overall, or whether the real position is a third option neither of them fully stated. And please answer in British English.
