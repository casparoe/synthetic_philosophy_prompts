I help run a philosophy/AI club at a state university — about 14 undergrads, a mix of CS and humanities, and we meet for 80 minutes. We've been circling around how concepts behave in little artificial worlds, and I want a session where the students feel it instead of hearing about it. The classic setup is the letter-string analogy game: you tell them abc goes to abd and pqr goes to pqs, so they latch onto a rule like "take the rightmost letter and move it one step forward in the alphabet." Then you show them xyz. Some say xya, because z just wraps around to a; others say the puzzle breaks because z has nowhere to go; still others quietly change the rule so the first letter moves instead. I'd like a full runnable activity built around this kind of slippage — predictions, written answers, arguing over rules that feel fixed but keep bending as new cases appear. If you want to add points, predictions, or small stakes, do it; I want them actually committed to their rules, not halfheartedly guessing.

The debrief should map specific moments in the arguments to specific slippages: what slid, what snapped, and why nobody noticed it happening. End on the bigger idea that even in a toy domain this small, a concept isn't a clean rule; it's more like a pattern that stretches until it gives. No jargon up front, and tell me what typically goes wrong when its actually run and how a facilitator should handle it.
