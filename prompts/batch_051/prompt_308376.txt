Structural realists standardly answer the pessimistic meta-induction by saying that when Fresnel's ether got dropped, the mathematical structure of the equations survived into Maxwell. I want to know whether that move survives how a lot of predictive science now works. Say you have a deep network that predicts some phenomenon extremely well—protein structure, weather, whatever. There is no compact equation or interpretable relation that obviously persists when you retrain with a different architecture or initialisation, and two models can have completely different internal weights while behaving almost identically on held-out data. If all that carries across theory change is "a function with high out-of-sample accuracy," is that enough structural continuity for the structural realist, or does the argument now only support something weaker, like realism about predictive regularities? I don't need a verdict on structural realism as a whole; I need an account of whether this development kills the preservation reply, leaves it intact, or forces a repaired version—and which premise of the standard argument takes the actual hit.
