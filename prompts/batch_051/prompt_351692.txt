You are a professor of aesthetics and the philosophy of perception, specializing in the question of whether we literally see objects through pictures—photographs in particular—rather than merely seeing representations of them. Work carefully and step by step. Before settling on an answer, brainstorm the general principles that could explain the track record below, test each principle against every one of the ten prior verdicts, and discard any principle that fails even one.

Over the course of a term, a colleague of yours has given the following off-the-cuff verdicts about particular cases, all on the question "does she literally see the object in question?"

| # | Case | Description | Verdict |
|---|------|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Window | Looking through a clear window at a tree outside. | Yes: she sees the tree. |
| 2 | Mirror | Seeing a car behind her reflected in a flat mirror. | Yes: she sees the car. |
| 3 | Binoculars | Looking at a hawk through binoculars. | Yes: she sees the hawk. |
| 4 | Photograph | Looking at an ordinary photograph of a friend. | Yes: she sees the friend. |
| 5 | Live video | Watching a CCTV monitor that carries a live feed of the lobby. | Yes: she sees the lobby. |
| 6 | Recorded video | Watching a recording, made months earlier, of a robbery. | Yes: she sees the robbery, even though the event is past. |
| 7 | Courtroom sketch | Looking at a photorealistic, hand-drawn sketch of that same robbery. | No: she sees only a depiction, not the robbery. |
| 8 | Verbal description | Hearing a witness describe the robbery. | No: she does not see it. |
| 9 | Synthetic image | Looking at a completely synthetic CGI rendering of a building that was never built. | No: there is no building to see. |
| 10 | Photogram | Looking at a photogram of a leaf: the leaf was laid directly on photosensitive paper, which was then exposed to light, leaving a silhouette. | Yes: she sees the leaf. |

Next week she has been asked about a new case. A radiologist is examining an X-ray photograph of a patient's broken rib on a light box. The image was produced mechanically, without any artist's intervention, and the rib's shape is clearly recognizable. Crucially, the image is formed by X-ray radiation passing through the patient's body, not by visible light reflecting from the rib.

Question: If she is to remain consistent with her previous verdicts, what should she most likely say about whether the radiologist literally sees the patient's broken rib through the X-ray photograph?

No citations, bibliographic references, or external sources are expected or wanted; reason entirely from the cases and your philosophical analysis.

Return a single JSON object and nothing else, with exactly these keys:

- `"professor_predicted_verdict"`: string. Your best prediction of her answer, either "yes, she sees the rib" or "no, she only sees a representation of the rib".
- `"confidence"`: string. One of "low", "medium", or "high".
- `"candidate_principles"`: array of objects. List each general principle that is consistent with all ten prior verdicts and that gains some traction on the X-ray case. Each object must have these fields:
  - `"name"`: a short label for the principle.
  - `"statement"`: one clear sentence stating the rule.
  - `"how_it_fits_prior_verdicts"`: one or two sentences explaining how it handles the ten cases, especially the three negative ones.
  - `"prediction_for_xray"`: one of "yes", "no", or "unclear".
  - `"reasoning"`: one or two sentences explaining the prediction.
- `"most_likely_implicit_principle"`: string. The principle you think she is most likely running, given the entire track record.
- `"closest_analog_precedent"`: string. Identify the prior case that is the closest analog to the X-ray case and briefly say why.
- `"best_guess_reasoning"`: string. Explain why your predicted verdict is the most coherent extension of her track record, and why you prefer the principle you chose as most likely.
- `"underdetermined_by_precedents"`: string. "true" or "false", followed by one sentence stating exactly what the ten prior verdicts leave open.
- `"what_each_choice_commits_her_to"`: string. If the precedents underdetermine the X-ray case, explain what principle she would be adopting by saying yes and what principle she would be adopting by saying no. If not underdetermined, write "not applicable".
- `"discriminating_further_case"`: object with these fields:
  - `"case"`: a description of one further case that would distinguish the candidate principles that the X-ray case leaves open.
  - `"expected_verdicts"`: a statement of what each surviving principle would predict for that further case.
