Here is an argument I can't quite assess, and I'd like you to check it step by step. (1) In a large election, an individual citizen's vote for the lesser evil is morally justified only if it is justified either as a means of influencing the outcome or as an expression of the voter's political commitments. (2) It is not justified as an expression, because in casting such a vote the voter publicly affirms a candidate she regards as seriously wrong, and one cannot be justified in publicly affirming what one regards as seriously wrong. (3) It is not justified as a means of influencing the outcome, because the probability that any single vote will be decisive is negligible, and a negligible probability of producing a good cannot justify an act. (4) So voting for the lesser evil is not justified for an individual in a large election, and a fortiori it is not a duty. (5) Therefore, a citizen who refuses to vote for the lesser evil and votes only for a candidate she sincerely endorses acts permissibly in doing so. I keep feeling the move from (4) to (5) is too quick, but I also think (3) may be smuggling in a controversial standard about how small a chance may count. Is the argument valid? If not, at exactly which step or transition does it fail, and on what reading? Please separate what is settled in this debate from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
