Write a deadpan internal compliance memo (about 650 words) from the Model Integrity Office of a fictional large-language-model company, announcing how the company will comply with a new requirement that every false or fabricated output be classified as an error, a lie, or bullshit. The memo should apply Harry Frankfurt's distinction — the liar tracks the truth closely enough to evade it, while the bullshitter is indifferent to how things actually stand — with real philosophical precision, so that the humor comes from the criteria themselves rather than from exaggeration. It should stay deadpan throughout and remain readable by someone who has never read Frankfurt. Without ever stating them, the satire should land the following points: that the industry term hallucination obscures the difference between being wrong and being indifferent to the truth; that the standard defense that a model has no intentions rules out lying only by severing the model from any relation to truth, which is not the exoneration it sounds like; and that calling fluent falsehoods mere errors understates what has gone wrong. By the end, a reader should feel the force of the question whether what language models produce is best described as error, bullshit, or something the lying/bullshit distinction leaves unclassified. Do not use verbatim quotations from Frankfurt; paraphrase his view. Answer in American English.
