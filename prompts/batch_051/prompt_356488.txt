I have been working through Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, and a passage near the beginning of Book II, chapter 4 has been nagging at me. In the translation I am using, it reads as follows:

"Works of art have their merit in themselves, so that it is enough if they are produced having a certain quality of their own; but acts done in conformity with the virtues are not done justly or temperately if they themselves are of a certain sort, but only if the agent also is in a certain state of mind when he does them: first he must act with knowledge; secondly he must deliberately choose the act, and choose it for its own sake; and thirdly the act must spring from a fixed and permanent disposition of character."

I think I grasp the surface point, that performing a virtuous action is not enough because the agent's inner state matters. But I am less sure what work the third condition is doing when Aristotle requires that the act "spring from a fixed and permanent disposition of character." Is that a psychological claim about how actually virtuous people behave, a conceptual requirement built into what it is for an action to count as virtuous, or an ideal that tells us what a completed virtue would be, however rarely anyone attains it? I have also been reading about the situationist critique of virtue ethics, which argues that social psychology shows that people generally lack the stable, cross-situationally consistent character traits that virtue ethics seems to presuppose, and that behavior is driven more by features of situations than by standing traits. I would like help seeing whether this particular passage commits Aristotle to the kind of claim the situationists say is empirically unsupported, or whether the critique attacks something other than what Aristotle is saying here. What in the passage should make me hesitate before treating the "fixed and permanent disposition" condition as a straightforward empirical generalization about human beings, and how much of his larger account of virtue would remain standing if that condition turned out to be satisfied only rarely?
