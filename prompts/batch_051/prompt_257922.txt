I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy. I joined a reading group on the epistemology of disagreement, and I have prepared a statement of the views I seem to hold. Before the group discussion, I would like a consistency audit of this belief set, done the way I would audit a system's dependencies: identify which commitments actually conflict, which only appear to conflict, order the real conflicts by severity, and for each real conflict identify the smallest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision would cost.

Here is my profile.

(1) Precise Bayesian updating. I hold precise degrees of confidence and revise them by conditionalizing on all the evidence I possess. Learning that another person draws a different conclusion from the same information is evidence, and it must be processed through the same update rule as any other evidence; the size of the resulting movement is whatever my priors and likelihoods dictate.

(2) Peer recognition. In several domains that matter to me — politics, macroeconomics, diet and health — I can identify many people who disagree with me and who are at least my equals in evidence, intelligence, and thoroughness. I see no non-circular feature of the situation that would justify assigning my judgment more weight than theirs.

(3) Conciliation. When a genuine epistemic peer disagrees with me about some claim after we have both examined the same evidence, I should substantially lower my confidence in that claim, and they should do the same.

(4) Permissivism about first-order evidence. For many bodies of evidence, there is no single rational attitude: two fully rational people can hold different degrees of belief on the same proposition from the same first-order evidence.

(5) Confident engagement. On at least one contested topic in the domains listed in (2), I remain highly confident — above 0.8 — and I use that confidence to guide action, even though I am aware that apparent peers judge the same evidence to support the opposite view.

(6) Anti-exceptionalism. I reject the move of resolving these tensions by supposing that, in the contested domains, I am the person who reasons especially well; that would be unjustified self-exemption.

My questions: Which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which tensions are merely apparent? Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, what is the cheapest modification to make, and what would I lose by making it?
