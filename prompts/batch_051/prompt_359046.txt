Imagine you're putting together a two-year working group — say two philosophers, a linguist who actually studies translation, and someone who used to work on MT at a big lab — and the whole brief is to figure out which version of the 'statistical translation doesn't understand' worry (the kind of thing Hofstadter kept pressing) is actually load-bearing, rather than just a complaint that current systems make silly errors. Which open questions should this group spend its time on, in what order, and why? For each: why it's upstream of other debates — in AI, in philosophy of language, in how translation systems get evaluated — how tractable it is for people who aren't going to train models, and what evidence or argument over the two years would make you drop it down the list. I specifically want you to take a position on the possibility that the interesting failures aren't about the system's internal states at all, but about the division of labor between system and user: maybe a human plus a good MT system understands in a way neither does alone. Rank against that hypothesis rather than just nodding at it. And dont turn it into a reading list or a survey.
