I'm a retired chemist (organic, mostly) who's gotten into philosophy in the last few years, and I've been stuck for a while on the old fight between Schopenhauer and Nietzsche over pity. I'm trying to turn my notes into a short essay for a local reading group, and I'd be grateful for comments on the idea before I inflict it on other people. I don't need a reading list; what I want is a sanity-check on the argument itself, since I don't have any formal training in this and I keep going back and forth on wether I've found something subtle or just confused two very different claims.

My working idea is that Schopenhauer and Nietzsche disagree less about how pity feels than about what pity takes as its object. For Schopenhauer, in the essay where he makes pity the foundation of morality, genuine pity is other-directed through and through: the difference between my suffering and the other person's suffering becomes, as he sees it, thin enough that their situation directly supplies my motive. Nietzsche's attacks on pity seem to me to treat it as essentially self-directed: a soft, anxious way of asking what I would do in their place, or even a disguised feeling of superiority over the sufferer. On my reading, both are pointing to real phenomena — there is pity that gets absorbed in the other person's situation, and pity that keeps glancing back at itself — and each thinker treats the other's central case as a marginal or degenerate form. So the real disagreement would be over which phenomenon deserves the name pity and which deserves to be called a pathology of it, and I suspect that choice tracks a deeper disagreement about whether the will is ultimately individuated or shared. I'd be very interested in comments on three things: (1) am I being unfair to either of them, or flattening a normative disagreement into a psychological one? (2) is this an interesting angle, or is it a standard undergraduate point that everyone already knows? and (3) how would you organize the essay so it doesn't just become a pile of paraphrase?
