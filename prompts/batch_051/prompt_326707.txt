My school's student council is governed by a short written constitution, and we are in a genuine dispute about what it requires. The outcome will determine whether a vote we took last month was valid, so I need the reasoning set out carefully.

The constitution contains these two clauses:

Article IV, Section 1 (Amendments): "This constitution may be amended only by a two-thirds vote of the members present at a meeting for which written notice of the proposed amendment was given at least one week in advance."

Article IV, Section 4 (Suspensions): "A procedural rule of this constitution may be suspended for the duration of a single meeting by a majority vote of the members present; no suspension shall extend beyond the meeting at which it was adopted."

At the meeting in question, a member moved to suspend Article IV, Section 1 "for this meeting only," and the motion passed by a vote of 60% to 40%. Immediately afterward, the same member moved to amend Article IV, Section 1 to read, "This constitution may be amended by a majority vote of the members present," and that motion also passed by a vote of 60% to 40%. The member argues that the amendment is valid because Section 1 had been suspended for the meeting, so the two-thirds threshold was not in force when the amendment was adopted. The other side argues that Section 4 was written for routine procedural housekeeping, such as letting a speaker exceed a time limit, and was never meant as a device for replacing the very rule that governs constitutional change, so Section 1 still applied.

I want, first, an analysis of the plainest reading of each section, and specifically whether the phrase "procedural rule" in Section 4 covers an amendment provision at all, or whether an amendment provision is a different kind of rule: a rule about how the rules themselves may be changed. Then argue the case from the purpose of each section, from what the drafters of Section 4 would have said if someone had asked whether it could be used to lower the amendment threshold, and from what a reasonable member reading the constitution today would take the two clauses together to mean. Finally, say which approach should decide the case and why, and state whether the 60% amendment is valid on that approach.

What I most want help with is the self-reference problem: Section 1 sets the conditions for changing Section 1 itself, while Section 4 can be operated by a bare majority, so I need to know whether a majority can legitimately suspend the rule that says a majority is not enough.
