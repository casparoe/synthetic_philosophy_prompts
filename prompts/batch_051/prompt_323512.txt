I have been trying to articulate a recurring structural feature of arguments about immigration and the right to exclude, and I would like to know whether it has an established name.

The standard justifications for a state's right to control its borders appeal to goods such as preserving a shared political culture, maintaining social trust, and protecting democratic self-government. The idea is that uncontrolled admission would dilute or destabilize those goods, so the existing community must have discretion over who joins it. Yet the same considerations seem to apply at smaller scales too, and often more naturally, since day-to-day trust and shared culture are most salient among neighbors and within towns and cities rather than across whole countries. A neighborhood could argue that a large influx of new residents would unravel the mutual-aid networks that make it livable; a city could argue that it must screen newcomers to protect the distinctive civic culture that sustains its participatory institutions. Almost everyone who accepts the state-level argument, however, balks at these smaller-scale conclusions. The refusal cannot simply be that existing law vests border control in states, because the deeper question is whether the justification itself explains why the power should be vested there rather than at some other scale.

I can imagine one obvious reply: citizens typically enjoy free movement within their own state, so sub-state units may not exclude them, whereas the state excludes only those who have no equivalent right of entry. But that reply relocates the difficulty rather than resolving it. It does not yet explain what makes the state-sized unit special, nor what happens to the force of those goods when they are invoked by a smaller unit; some further principle is needed to explain why the same considerations are treated as decisive at one scale and negligible at another.

I am not asking for a general account of whether states may exclude. I am asking about this specific structural feature: the apparent scale-relativity of exclusionary justifications. Is there an established term for it, or for the underlying question of which unit is the appropriate bearer of the right to exclude? What nearby ideas — self-determination, freedom of association, and the like — should it be distinguished from, and how exactly do they relate to it? If there is no standard term, what should it be called, and how should it be defined and bounded? A bare label would not help; I need the distinctions drawn precisely.
