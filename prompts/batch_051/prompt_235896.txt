I sit on the policy committee of a county workforce development board. We are being asked to approve a twelve-month pilot for a conditional cash-transfer program for unemployed adults. Under the proposal, recipients must attend weekly job-search workshops, and any missed session triggers a graduated reduction in benefits; the pilot also includes randomized audits to detect unreported household earnings, with an automatic six-month disqualification for confirmed fraud. Our committee is split. One faction argues that visible conditionality and serious fraud enforcement are the only way to keep public support for transfers, and that aid must be reserved for those who are genuinely trying. The other faction argues that the sanctions mostly catch people with chaotic lives, mental health problems, or weak literacy, and that the word fraud is doing a lot of unexamined moral work. I suspect both sides are pattern-matching to different stories about the undeserving poor rather than facing the policy’s actual incentive structure, but I have to cast a vote. Please draft a short written opinion I can circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—and it should engage the strongest consideration on the losing side, not just dismiss it. In particular, I want the memo to state explicitly what our policy would be assuming about why someone misses a workshop, and to specify which empirical findings would change the recommendation.
