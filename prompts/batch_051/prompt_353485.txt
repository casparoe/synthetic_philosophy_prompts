I am compiling a list of serious objections to conceptual engineering (or ameliorative analysis): the idea that, for certain philosophical purposes, especially social and political ones, we should stop merely analysing what our concepts do mean and instead design, propose, and advocate revised concepts better suited to our aims. The objections I already have are these. First, the change-of-subject objection: a revised concept may simply answer a different question from the one originally asked, replacing rather than improving the old one. Second, the externalist objection: if concepts are individuated partly by the world and by a community's usage, no individual's stipulation or advocacy can reliably change what the concept is. Third, the implementation problem: even granting that a revised concept would be better, there is no clear account of how a whole community could adopt it without communicative disruption or merely alongside the old one. Fourth, the epistemic objection: letting normative aims drive conceptualization can leave us with concepts that track the world less accurately and thereby corrupt inquiry. Fifth, the legitimacy objection: engineering a shared concept is an exercise of power over how a community carves up the world, and expertise in philosophy does not obviously license anyone to exercise it. Give me one more objection that is genuinely distinct from these five — not a restatement, special case, or combination of them — and then take a definite position on whether that objection is any good, with your reasons. Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would write an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
