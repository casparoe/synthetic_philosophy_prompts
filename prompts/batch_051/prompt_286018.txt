I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, and for a dissertation chapter I'm collecting arguments that actually defend self-sufficiency or autarky as an ideal — for persons, for political communities, or both — rather than as an unfortunate tradeoff against efficiency. So far my list of pro-autarky arguments, stated loosely, is:

1. Security: a nation that imports its food, fuel, or critical inputs lives under a standing threat; whoever controls those links can coerce it. Self-sufficiency is insurance against that kind of pressure.
2. Freedom as non-domination: someone who cannot do without another person's will is not really free, so a self-sufficient agent has a smaller area of life that others can hold hostage. This is supposed to apply to individuals and to states.
3. Virtue or perfection: there is something complete, even excellent, about needing little from outside; self-mastery and freedom from neediness are constituents of flourishing, not just means to it.
4. Development: protecting and producing at home, even at a near-term cost, builds domestic skills, infrastructure, and a diversified economy, so a period of self-provisioning can be nation-building.
5. Fragility: long, tightly coupled supply chains break under shocks like pandemics, shipping disruptions, or crop failures, even with no adversary involved; local self-provision shortens the chain and makes failures more legible and repairable.

I have a nagging feeling the list still has a gap — an argument for self-sufficiency whose basic structure doesn't run through security, nondomination, virtue, development, or fragility, and isn't just a special case or combination of those. Give me that missing argument, stated as strongly as you can, and then tell me honestly whether it survives scrutiny or whether on inspection it collapses into one of the five. I'd rather you dont give me a menu of candidates: pick one, commit to it, and defend the choice. At the very end, add a machine-readable line in the form \answer{<short label for the argument>; <sound, unsound, or sound-with-caveats>}.
