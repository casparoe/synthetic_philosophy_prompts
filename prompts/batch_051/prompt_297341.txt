I have been trying to work out what I actually think about metacognition — the activity of stepping back to monitor, evaluate, or regulate one's own thinking — because the usual claim that it is uniformly a mark of good thinking does not match my experience. I want a principled account, not a slogan.

Here are my verdicts on cases. Pausing mid-game in chess to ask "what am I not noticing about this position?" is clearly a good use of it; it has repeatedly improved my play. Pausing before accepting a job offer to ask which considerations I was weighing too heavily or ignoring out of anxiety also helped; it changed how I traded off salary against location. A musician friend reports that inspecting her fingerings during a performance makes her play worse, and I trust that: the monitoring intrudes on the skill. Weekly journaling to notice recurring patterns in my own thinking — say, that I catastrophize after setbacks — seems helpful, even though its object is me rather than any task. But lying awake asking "why am I anxious about tomorrow?" with no answer in sight seems to feed the anxiety rather than relieve it. Monitoring "how am I coming across?" during a conversation reliably makes me worse at conversation. And when I go back over an exam proof to ask "am I actually justified in this step, or just attached to the idea?", the self-questioning once caught a real error, so I count it as helpful.

My working rule had been that metacognition helps when it questions the task and hurts when it questions the self, but the journaling and job-offer cases break that rule, and the lying-awake and proof-checking cases seem to differ in some other way than their object. Is there a principle — or a small set of candidates — that captures all or nearly all of these verdicts? State it precisely, tie each clause to the case that motivates it, say what it predicts about a case I keep going back and forth on (re-reading a draft paragraph while asking "is this clear, or am I flattering myself?"), and be honest about which of my verdicts, if any, the principle cannot accommodate.
