I am a self-taught systems programmer with no academic training in philosophy, but I have been reading on my own about the Searle–Dennett dispute over original and derived intentionality. I think best in the vocabulary I work in every day: source code and compiled binaries, interpreters, compilers, virtual machines and emulators, pointers and references, side effects, and the split between a program's specification and its execution trace. I want you to restate the entire dispute in exactly that vocabulary, then report what did not survive the translation.

Go through these one at a time: Searle's claim that computation is syntax without semantics; his distinction between original and derived intentionality; the Chinese Room; the systems reply; Dennett's two-bitser, which as I understand it is the argument that a vending machine's derived intentionality and a person's original intentionality differ only in degree or in our habit of describing them; and Dennett's intentional stance. For each, say what translates cleanly into a programming concept, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary.

Then give me the residue report. Does the original/derived distinction survive, or does it collapse into an ordinary engineering split such as source versus execution, host versus emulated machine, or meaning supplied by a programmer versus behavior observed by a user? In particular, is there anything in software that counts as original intentionality rather than derived, or is every program's meaning derived all the way down? And if it is derived all the way down, is that an insight about minds or only a limitation of the software analogy? To keep this honest, press on the case of an emulator running inside another emulator and say where the intentionality would live there. Do not just file Searle under hardware and Dennett under software; I want to see where the translation breaks when it is pushed.
