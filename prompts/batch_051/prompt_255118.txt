My friend has been giving verdicts on little stories about whether someone did something intentionally, and I cant quite pin down the principle she's using. Here's what she's said so far:

- A CEO approves a cost-cutting plan that will pollute a lake, and he says he doesn't care about the lake. She says he intentionally polluted the lake.
- The same CEO approves a cost-cutting plan that will clean up a lake, and he says he doesn't care about the clean-up. She says he didn't intentionally clean the lake.
- A guy tosses a lit cigarette into dry brush next to a propane tank because he can't be bothered to find a trash can; he knows it might start a fire but doesn't care. She says he intentionally started the fire.
- A commuter cuts through a community garden to save two minutes, knowing she'll trample the flowers but not caring. She says she intentionally trampled the flowers.

Her next case is this: a surgeon removing a tumor knows the operation will sever a healthy nerve and leave the patient's hand numb. The surgeon hates that this will happen, but there's no less damaging way to save the patient's life. Will my friend say the surgeon intentionally severed the nerve? More importantly, what rule is she running? I can think of at least two rules that fit her track record and disagree about the surgery case — which one is probably doing the work for her, and is there another case you'd want to see to settle it?
