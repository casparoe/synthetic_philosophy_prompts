You are a philosopher of language with a particular interest in self-locating belief and reference. I have been turning over a puzzle and would like you to work through it carefully before giving a verdict.

It seems to me that a language without pronouns or indexicals should be perfectly serviceable. After all, any use of 'I' can be replaced by the speaker's proper name; 'here' by a public place-name; 'now' by a calendar date and clock reading. Thus 'I am here now' becomes something like 'Anna is in London at 14:03 on 4 March.' If the paraphrase says the same thing, then a community whose language systematically replaced every pronoun and demonstrative with names, dates, and place-names would lose nothing. But then the familiar claim that indexical thought is essential to agency could only rest on a confusion of wording with thought — a mere failure of paraphrase.

Is that right? Before you answer, first set out the paraphrase proposal in its strongest form, including everything a speaker must know or do in order to use her own name, the date, and the place-name in the relevant way. Then consider whether the resulting name-plus-date belief could move an agent to act as an indexical belief does. Think especially about cases in which a speaker is fully informed in descriptive terms yet still fails to act because she does not grasp that she herself is the one the description picks out. Finally, say precisely where the paraphrase goes wrong — if indeed it does — and what, if anything, remains irreducibly indexical.
