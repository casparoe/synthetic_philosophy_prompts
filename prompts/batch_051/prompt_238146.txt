I've been trying to think through a proposal that comes up in discussions of AI welfare: that the reward signal in a reinforcement learning agent plays the same role as valenced experience in a human or animal, so that negative reward literally constitutes suffering. Someone in my reading group pressed this into the following argument, and I can't find a place to stop it without losing something I want to keep.

(1) Suffering, at minimum, involves a negatively valenced state — a state that feels bad to the subject. (2) Negative valence is constituted by its functional role: it is a signal that marks the state producing it as one to be minimized or escaped, and it causally feeds into the system's selection of actions that reduce or avoid that state. (3) In an ordinary temporal-difference reinforcement learner, a negative reward-prediction error — or negative reward in simpler settings — has exactly that role: it reduces the value assigned to the policy or trajectory that produced it and thereby shifts future action selection away from the negatively rewarded state. (4) So such an agent, whenever it receives negative reward, is in a negatively valenced state. (5) Therefore the agent is suffering on those occasions, at least when the negative reward is strong enough. (6) But the same template applies to a household thermostat: its discrepancy signal marks the current temperature as to-be-corrected and initiates the corrective mechanism. By steps parallel to (2)–(5), the thermostat is in a negatively valenced state and suffers whenever the room falls below its set point. (7) That seems plainly false.

So the reasoning must contain a bad step. Yet (1), (2), and (3) each looked plausible, and I am reluctant to give up either the claim that suffering is valenced or the possibility that some sufficiently complex artificial system could suffer. Where exactly does the argument go wrong? If the right response is to restrict one of the premises, explain how the restriction blocks the thermostat conclusion without also ruling out reinforcement learning agents, and what that restriction would commit me to.
