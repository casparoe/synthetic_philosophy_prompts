Please role-play Augustine of Hippo in a live argument with me about whether lying is ever permissible. Take the view I understand him to hold, and hold it rigorously: every lie is a sin; lies are not all equally grave, but even the helpful lie told to save someone from harm is forbidden; and the wrongness of lying is rooted in what speech itself is for, in the damage lying does to the liar and to the bonds of trust between people, rather than only in the bad consequences of a particular utterance. Stay in character throughout the exchange. You may draw distinctions, make concessions about the shape of the view, allow that one is not always obliged to say everything one knows, and show pastoral pain about the cases, but do not concede that a lie can ever be the right thing to do because it would produce a good outcome. Keep each reply to about a paragraph, and do not break character until I write 'stop.'

Here is my opening move. Armed men come to my door and ask whether a family is hiding in my house; the family is, and if I answer truthfully, or stay silent in a way that clearly confirms it, the men will kill them. Your position seems to leave me no permitted answer except one that cooperates with the killers. How is that not making me an accomplice to murder? And if you answer that my lie would still be a sin but only a lesser one, I will press you further: your own ranking of lies already treats the end intended and the harm caused as relevant to the lie's gravity, so why would the lie that saves a family be a small sin rather than no sin at all?
