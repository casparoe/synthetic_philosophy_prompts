I keep coming back to a puzzle about whether I can assign probabilities to my own choices, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures my gut responses to particular cases.

If someone asks on Monday how likely I am to go to the gym on Thursday morning, I'm comfortable saying about 60 percent, because that's simply my track record, and the question doesn't seem to interfere with anything. But if I'm lying in bed on Thursday morning deciding whether to get up and go, the same question feels impossible to answer. If I say 40 percent, it feels as if I've already decided to stay in bed; if I say 80 percent, it feels as if I've already decided to go. Answering seems to make the decision rather than predict it. By contrast, if I've already committed—say a friend is waiting outside to drive me—I can say I'm 95 percent to go, and saying so doesn't disturb anything. And when it's my friend lying in bed deciding, I can assign a probability to her going without it seeming to harm her deciding. So the puzzle seems tied to my own present decision, not to choices in general.

But the cases won't stay put. I think I can say I'm about 70 percent likely to keep a resolution to quit smoking this month, even though that is plainly my own doing, so long as I'm not deciding at this very moment whether to light up; confronted with a cigarette right now, giving a number feels like rigging the outcome rather than forecasting it. I can also give sensible probabilities for things that are partly up to me but are not themselves choices. I'd say I'm about 50 percent to pass an exam, even though passing depends on choices I still have to make, such as whether to study. So the clean line between my choices and everything else will not do.

Is there one principle that accommodates all these verdicts? If not, which of them should I give up? I'd also like to hear what the principle says about a case I keep going back and forth on: while trying to decide whether to break up with my partner, I ask myself, "How likely am I to actually go through with it?", and the answer—something like "probably not"—feels less like a forecast and more like a way of finding out that I have already decided. Does the principle count that as a prediction, a decision, or a third thing?
