In my philosophy club we have been working through Dennett’s paper Quining Qualia and especially the Chase and Sanborn example. A classmate offered the following argument, which seems to show that qualia are not merely hard to verify but incoherent as a posit. I want to reject the conclusion, since it seems there is a determinate fact about how my experience seems to me, but I cannot see where the argument goes wrong. Please diagnose which premise or step should be given up and explain what rejecting it costs for the main positions in the qualia debate.

The argument:

(1) If qualia are real properties of conscious experience, then for any subject and any two episodes of tasting, there is a determinate fact about whether the qualitative character of the taste is the same or different.

(2) In the Chase and Sanborn case, both tasters have changed in their overall response to the same coffee, but they disagree about the taste itself: Chase says the taste is exactly the same and he simply likes it less, while Sanborn says the taste itself has changed and he likes the new taste just as much as the old one.

(3) Every piece of observable evidence—their behavior, their sincere reports, any physiological measurement—is compatible with at least three hypotheses about what happened to their qualia: the qualia stayed fixed while the liking changed, the qualia changed while memory or judgment made them seem fixed, or the two reports are not tracking the same property. There is no further test, even in principle, that would settle which hypothesis is true.

(4) If no possible evidence could settle which hypothesis is true, then there is no fact of the matter about which hypothesis is true.

(5) But if there is no fact of the matter about whether the qualitative character stayed the same or changed, then qualia do not have the determinate identity demanded by premise (1).

(6) Therefore the standard concept of qualia is incoherent.

I would most like to keep premise (1), since it seems built into the very idea of a subjective quality.

Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Use the five points to identify the step to reject, explain why it looked acceptable, and say what rejecting or keeping it costs for a qualia realist, a representationalist, and an eliminativist.
