I'm a retired chemist who recently fell into this literature on the evolution of cooperation, and I keep seeing the same handful of mechanisms for getting cooperation out of a prisoner's dilemma. Here's my list so far: kin selection (Hamilton's rule), direct reciprocity (tit-for-tat in the iterated game), indirect reciprocity (reputation and image scoring), spatial or network reciprocity (cooperators clustering together), group or multilevel selection, altruistic punishment of defectors, and greenbeard recognition. I'm not counting institutional fixs like police and contracts, and I'm not counting noise-tolerance patchs like contrition or generosity, since those look like repairs to reciprocity rather than new mechanisms.

I want one more mechanism that is genuinely different from everything on my list — not a restatement, special case, or combination of items already there. Then tell me honestly whether it earns its keep as a distinct explanation, or whether it collapses back into one of the ones I already have.
