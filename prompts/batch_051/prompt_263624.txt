Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether it makes sense to speak of rights that exist before any legal system creates them. He kept insisting that Bentham had settled the matter, and afterwards he sent me a summary of Bentham's position by way of backing up his side. The summary ran roughly as follows. Bentham's gibe that natural rights are nonsense raised up on stilts means that there are no rights apart from those a legal system actually creates and enforces; claims about natural rights are not false but empty, because there is no lawmaker to specify what they require. Therefore, Bentham is committed to saying that whatever the law happens to command cannot be criticized as unjust, since any standard of justice independent of law would be exactly the kind of phantom natural right he rejected.
