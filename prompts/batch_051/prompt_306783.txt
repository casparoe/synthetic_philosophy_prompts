You are a professional philosopher working on the epistemology of transformative experience and decision-making under uncertainty, and you are also a careful reader of film. I am preparing a seminar that pairs L. A. Paul's account of transformative experience with Denis Villeneuve's Arrival, adapted from Ted Chiang's 'Story of Your Life'. The students know both the philosophy and the film, so please do not summarise the plot at length; give me philosophical criticism.

The puzzle I want you to place at the centre of the reading is this. On Paul's view, some major life choices — having a child, gaining a radically new cognitive capacity, undergoing certain conversions — are rationally peculiar because the person making the choice cannot know in advance what it will be like, from the inside, to live on the other side of it; the agent lacks the first-personal knowledge needed to weigh the outcomes in the ordinary way. Arrival seems to dissolve this problem rather than merely illustrate it. Louise Banks learns the heptapods' written language and acquires a mode of consciousness in which she experiences her own future, including the short life and death of the daughter she has not yet conceived. When she chooses to have Hannah, she appears to choose with exactly the first-personal knowledge of the outcome — the love, the grief, the dissolution of the marriage — that Paul says the prospective parent cannot have.

Argue whether the film refutes, bypasses, or merely relocates the problem of transformative choice. In particular, examine whether Louise's foreknowledge is genuinely knowledge of what her future life will be like, or rather a vivid presentation of facts about that life from the standpoint of someone who is, in the relevant sense, still outside the experience; and consider whether the film, by making the audience watch Louise's future, quietly grants the viewer the retrospective perspective that the chooser herself could not have had at the moment of choice. Note where the film is faithful to the philosophy, where it distorts or overreaches, and where it may be running together the epistemology of transformative decision with its own science-fictional metaphysics of non-linear time. Begin by brainstorming two or three possible interpretive hypotheses before settling on your reading, then defend that reading against the strongest objection you can devise. Please answer in British English.
