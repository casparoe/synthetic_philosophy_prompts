I have spent years reading rationalist-adjacent blogs and forums, and I have a habit of running conceptual questions through cases from online communities. Lately I have been trying to decide whether the Lewis-style account of convention can capture the way norms actually stabilize in those communities, and I keep going back and forth. I want to lay out the criteria I was taught, present a case, and ask whether the case qualifies.

Here is the package of criteria I was taught for a regularity R to be a convention in a population P in a recurrent situation S:

1. Everyone in P conforms to R in S.
2. Everyone expects everyone else in P to conform to R in S.
3. Everyone prefers to conform to R on the condition that the others do; this preference is part of what gives everyone reason to expect conformity.
4. There is at least one alternative regularity R' such that, if everyone expected everyone to follow R' instead, everyone would prefer to follow R'.
5. These expectations and preferences are common knowledge in P: everyone has them, everyone knows everyone has them, and so on.

Now the case. A semi-anonymous discussion forum develops, over several years, a norm of quoting the exact clause from the parent comment that one is responding to before explaining why one disagrees. The quote is often only two or three words. By the time the norm is mature:

- Essentially every regular participant conforms to it in ordinary disagreement threads.
- People begin replies with the fragment even in short threads where no one could plausibly lose track of the referent.
- Newcomers pick the norm up within a few weeks, partly by imitation and partly because replies that omit the fragment attract confused or mildly corrective responses.
- If asked to state the rule, most regulars cannot. They give functional reconstructions ('it keeps threads readable', 'you have to anchor your reply') rather than the rule, and some deny there is a rule at all.
- When the forum software is changed so that every reply automatically displays a short snippet of the parent comment, hand-quoting dies out within a month. In later discussions, regulars disagree about whether they had ever followed the norm deliberately, and almost no one reports having consciously decided to stop.

So does this regularity qualify as a convention under the criteria? I can see that criterion 1 holds, and criterion 4 is arguably vindicated by the software change: the alternative, not hand-quoting, was available all along and was collectively adopted once the environment changed. What I cannot decide is whether criteria 2, 3, and 5 hold, given that the participants seem unable to articulate the rule and sometimes deny it existed. Please go through the criteria honestly. Where the answer is no, say whether that is because the criteria are too demanding — in particular, whether common knowledge requires articulated, introspectively available attitudes, or whether tacit expectations can count — or because the case is not a convention at all but a different kind of social regularity, such as a merely evolutionarily stable pattern of imitation. If the case pulls in both directions, say which resolution is more honest and which is special pleading.

Throughout, say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
