I am an undergraduate in a course on Berkeley's idealism, and I have been asked to prepare the colloquium Q&A for a visiting philosopher who defends a contemporary version of Berkeley's master argument. Her position is that the attempt to conceive of an object existing entirely unperceived is self-defeating: in the very act of conceiving it one makes it an object of thought or perception, so the supposition that the tree in the quadrangle continues to exist while no one perceives it cannot even be coherently entertained. Draft eight to ten interview questions that move past having her restate the argument. They should press where the standard objections press: that she conflates the idea of the tree with the tree itself, that the argument proves too much because the same form would show that nothing unconceived can exist, that the inference from inconceivability to impossibility needs independent support, and that God's continuous perception must be doing the work whenever finite observers are absent. For each line of objection, include the follow-up you would ask if she gives the reply most commonly found in the literature, and end with one question about what kind of evidence or experience could count against her view.
