Ok so for my AP Gov final I'm doing this project on desuetude—the idea that a law can basically stop being a law if nobody enforces it for ages, even though it was never actually repealed. I found this state supreme court justice, Justice Okafor, who keeps writing these long concurrences about it, and I've been trying to reverse-engineer her rule from four of her votes.

Here's her track record. (1) A 1911 statute made it a misdemeanor to sell liquor within a mile of a church; it hadn't been enforced since Prohibition ended, and two liquor stores openly do exactly that near churches. She voted not to enforce it. (2) A 1948 law made it a crime to loiter in a public library reading room without intent to read; never prosecuted once. She voted not to enforce. (3) A 1925 law required drivers to stop and sound their horn before every intersection; it hasn't been enforced since the 40s and basically no car has that kind of bulb horn anymore. She voted not to enforce. (4) A 1998 law required sex offenders to report a change of address within 48 hours; state police had never actually enforced the 48-hour part for 25 years. She voted to enforce it anyway, saying police non-enforcement doesn't repeal a protective statute.

Now the new case: a 1993 city ordinance in the same state says all dogs have to be leashed in public parks. It has never been enforced in 30 years, half the park is basically an unofficial off-leash area, and dog owners obviously plan around it. After a dog bite last month, the city suddenly starts ticketing people. I have to predict how Justice Okafor would vote.

So my question is: what would she say, and wich rule is she actually running—traditional desuetude (long non-enforcement plus widespread public reliance), the obsolete-rationale version, the protective-laws-never-die version, or something else? Which of her old cases is the closest analog, and what principle is she committing to either way? And what's one further case that would cleanly settle which rule it is? Also, if this whole reverse-engineer-a-rule-from-four-votes thing is dumb because judges aren't that consistent, just tell me that instead of pretending.
