Moral particularism and its generalist critics have been arguing for decades about whether sound moral judgment must be governed by principles, and the debate has settled into a strange stalemate. Particularists hold that a feature of a situation — for example, the fact that an action would cause pleasure — can count in favor in one context, against in another, and not at all in a third, and that a competent judge can respond to a novel case directly, by attending to the particular configuration of features, without applying or consulting a general rule. Generalists grant that familiar formulations of principles are crude, but they treat that crudeness as a fixable engineering problem rather than a refutation: the remedy is hedged principles with exception clauses, such as a pro tanto duty to keep promises that can be outweighed or undermined in specifiable ways. The particularist replies that once enough hedges and exception clauses are loaded in, the principle either collapses into a tautology with no independent grip on the case or silently encodes the very particular judgment it was supposed to explain.

I suspect the two sides are not answering one question. The particularist, as I read the dialectic, is making a claim about how moral competence and normative explanation actually work — that the case, and not the rule, carries the justificatory weight — while the generalist is making a claim about what an account of ethics must deliver if it is to count as a theory at all: public standards, consistency across cases, and something codifiable enough to teach and to criticize. If that is right, the debate is not a disagreement about a shared question so much as a collision between different constraints on what an acceptable account must do.

Diagnose this impasse. Is it substantive, verbal, or a disagreement about which phenomena are authoritative data — the phenomenology of expert case-by-case judgment on one side, the demands of interpersonal justification and codification on the other? Or is it best read as a dispute about where explanatory work is done, or ultimately about what the word principle is allowed to mean?
