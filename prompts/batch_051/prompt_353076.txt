I'm retired now — spent most of my career in physical chemistry and a bit of continuum mechanics — and I've finally had time to read some philosophy of physics. Theres a move I keep seeing that I dont have a name for, and I'd like to know if one exists.

The pattern goes like this. A person wants to argue that some quantity is real or objective rather than a bookkeeping convention. They point out that the quantity is conserved, and then, as if that settles it, they add that the conservation comes from a symmetry through Noether's theorem: energy from time-translation symmetry, linear momentum from spatial translation symmetry, electric charge from gauge symmetry. I have no problem with the theorem itself; I used to teach a version of it. But I've also seen the same conservation-and-symmetry package run in the opposite direction: someone wants to demote a quantity, so they say it is not invariant under such-and-such a transformation, therefore it is frame-dependent or coordinate-dependent and so not objectively real. In general relativity, for instance, the same person will treat quantities that are covariantly defined as first-class and quantities that are not as coordinate artifacts.

What nags at me is that the symmetry test seems to pass or fail depending on work that was done offstage. Anything is invariant under enough transformations if you choose the transformations narrowly enough, and almost anything can be made non-invariant by choosing a broader class. The real decision — which transformations count as physically meaningful changes and which ones are just redescription — is made before the symmetry argument starts, but the symmetry argument is presented as if it did the deciding. It feels a little like getting a certificate from an inspector you hired yourself.

So my question is: does this move have a name? I mean specifically the use of invariance or non-invariance as a seal of genuineness after the class of allowed transformations has already been selected to produce the desired verdict. How is it related to, but different from, the legitimate use of symmetries to figure out which quantities are well-defined? And if it has no established name, what would you call it, and where would you draw the boundary between a real invariance argument and this after-the-fact version? I'd rather not just get a label — I want the carving.
