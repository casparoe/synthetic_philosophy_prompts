During the first wave of covid my brother kept ending arguments by saying "desperate times call for desperate measures." He was using it to defend border closures and lockdowns that plainly went passed what the law allowed, and he refused to distinguish between "it was legal," "it was legitimate," and "it was understandable." Can you unpack the distinct precise claims that proverb could be making about what an emergency does to legality, to moral obligation, to excusability, and to what publics will accept — and say which of those readings is just a truism, which is substantive but false, and which would actually do the work he needed it to do in that argument?
