I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I've spent the past few months writing about how one liberal democracy's constitutional court resolves conflicts between individual rights and public health. Its track record, as best I can summarize from its published decisions, runs like this. The Court upheld a requirement that all schoolchildren be vaccinated against a serious contagious disease, reasoning that the intrusion is minor and that an unvaccinated child directly endangers others. It struck down a mandatory motorcycle-helmet law, holding that the state may not coerce a competent adult to protect him from his own choices, even when the safety gains are real. It struck down a program that would have compelled the small number of adults with a rare blood type to donate plasma several times a year so that pooled plasma could be made into a lifesaving treatment for a clotting disorder; the decisive opinion emphasized both a broad principle that a person's body may not be conscripted for the benefit of others and the fact that the scheme concentrated its entire burden on a small, identifiable group, and it remains unclear which consideration carried the decision. And the Court upheld a narrowly drawn quarantine order confining a person infected with a dangerous airborne disease, on the grounds that his liberty could be overridden to prevent him from harming others, provided the state supplied food, medical care, and compensation.

Parliament is now considering a universal blood-donation requirement: every healthy adult would give one unit of whole blood each year, with medical exemptions, to be distributed through the public blood system; the burden would be widely shared, the medical risk would be very low, and the projected effect on supply and lives saved would be substantial. I'm writing a piece that predicts how the Court is likely to rule if it wants to remain consistent with its own precedents. Walk me through the independent rules that fit its past verdicts, what each rule would require in this new case, which earlier case is the closest analog and why, and what your best prediction is, with attention to the point at which the precedents genuinely run out. If you would want one further hypothetical case to settle the interpretation, tell me what it is and how each rule would decide it. Please answer in American English.
