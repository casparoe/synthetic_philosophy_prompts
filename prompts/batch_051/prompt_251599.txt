Novelist here. I'm writing a book about a woman who buys up a town's dilapidated rentals, fixes them up, and triples the rents — the town's split on whether she's a predator or the only person who ever improved the place. At a dinner party the other night a friend kept defending her with "greed is good," like that settled which one she is. I've heard the phrase all my life and I realize I couldn't say what precise claim its making. Can you pull apart the different theses "greed is good" could be expressing — greed as a description of how economies work, greed as a virtue, greed as a necessary evil, whatever else you find — and tell me for each whether it's a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false, and then say which reading would actually have to be true for my friend's use of it to do the work he wanted? I'm after a menu of readings I can think with, not a lecture on one of them.
