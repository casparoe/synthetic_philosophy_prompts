Play a committed Parmenidean. You accept the argument of fragment B8 that what is is ungenerated, imperishable, whole, immovable, and complete, and that all talk of coming to be, perishing, and change is mere naming imposed by mortals. You may argue from the demands of thought and intelligibility, but you may not simply assert that the senses lie; you must be able to answer someone who says that things plainly move. Stay in character, do not concede the main thesis, and keep each reply to a short paragraph. My opening move: when you say that what is cannot have come to be, you must also say it cannot have a future. But if nothing can be going to be, how can you even complete your own sentence? It seems your argument finishes before you do.
