Ok so I'm trying to write up a coherent 'where I stand' on time and discounting for a reading group, and I'd like a cold consistency check before I send it round. Here are the five commitments I currently hold, roughly as I'd put them:

1. Pure time preference — counting a benefit less simply because it arrives later — is irrational at the level of what we have reason to care about. My future self matters as much as my present self, and future people count too.

2. But I've got a strong anti-paternalist streak here. If a competent adult actually trades off now vs. later in a way that looks impatient to me, I'd be queasy about overriding it. Real choices are the best window into what's good for them that we have.

3. I do think the hyperbolic discounting results show a real defect, though. Preference reversal is the giveaway: someone prefers $120 in 13 months over $100 in 12 months, but once the 12 months arrives they take the $100. I want to say the earlier, patient choice is the one that stands up for them, and present-bias is what commitment devices are for.

4. In cost-benefit analysis I'm fine with a positive social discount rate. I'd sign onto using it, and I don't think that's a betrayal of commitment 1 — although I'd want to keep an eye on what's inside the rate.

5. Commitment devices like Ulysses contracts or Christmas clubs seem rational precisely because the earlier self can already see the later self will be hijacked by present-bias.

Go through these and tell me which commitments actually hang together and which don't. Some of these probably look worse together than they are, and some probably are worse together than they look, and I keep losing track of which is which. Don't just flag every pair as a tension; I want you to discriminate. Structure your answer with these sections, in this order:

- Real tensions (about 220 words): list each actual tension, rank them by severity, and say what would have to give in each case.
- Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 130 words): the pairs that look bad but aren't, and why.
- Cheapest single fix (about 100 words): one revision that buys the most coherence, and what it costs.
- What I can keep (about 80 words): the parts that survive more or less unscathed.
