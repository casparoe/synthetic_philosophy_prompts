An animal welfare foundation is deciding how to allocate a decade of research funding on the distribution of consciousness across nonhuman animals, with the goal of making welfare policy and individual consumption choices better grounded in which animals can suffer. Which single unresolved question in this area should the foundation fund first, and why? Candidate questions include: what behavioral, neurobiological, or functional features reliably indicate consciousness rather than mere nociception (neural detection of noxious stimuli) or reflexive response; whether animal consciousness is best modeled as graded and multidimensional or as having a threshold structure; how likely consciousness is in invertebrate lineages such as insects, crustaceans, and cephalopods, whose neural architectures differ sharply from those of vertebrates; and how decision-makers should proceed under deep uncertainty about whether a species is conscious. Distinguish, where it matters, the question of which organisms have the capacity for consciousness from the question of which of their states are valenced (felt as good or bad), since these may come apart. Rank the candidate questions, or replace them with stronger ones, and defend the ranking with explicit criteria: how many downstream decisions each answer would change, how tractable it is with current empirical and conceptual tools, and how neglected it is. State what empirical or conceptual finding would most change your ordering.
