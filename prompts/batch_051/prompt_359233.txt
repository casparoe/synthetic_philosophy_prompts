I'm a junior in high school and I help run the econ club, and in two weeks I'm giving a short talk at a joint meeting with the debate team about rent control. The position I'm defending is kind of specific: the standard supply-and-demand case against rent control is basically right about the economics — it shrinks the rental supply over time, reduces maintenance, and misallocates apartments — but those effects dont by themselves settle whether rent control is justified. I want to argue that rent control is best understood not as a normal price ceiling but as a messy, partial property right for sitting tenants, almost like a tenure right, and that the real debate is whether that's a right worth paying for in those costs. My sketch of the argument goes: first I separate the question what does rent control do? from the question what do incumbent tenants have a claim to?; then I say the economic critique nails the first question but everyone then acts like it also answered the second; then I point out that we already accept a bunch of limits on what an owner can do with their property — zoning rules, grandfather clauses, and similar things — as just how property works, so the people who call rent control a distortion need to explain why those don't count.

The crowd is mostly debate kids who do LD and PF and can smell a consequentialist-vs-deontological move from across the room, plus some econ club regulars who've read the standard textbook chapter on price ceilings and basically think rent control is the classic case of good intentions crashing into supply and demand. My teacher says these talks live or die in the Q&A, not the prepared remarks. So prep me for that: the six or seven objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way a debate kid or an econ kid would actually put it, with what I should concede, what I should hold, where my reply is soft, and which part of my argument is the weakest and I should prepare to defend instead of hoping nobody notices. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
