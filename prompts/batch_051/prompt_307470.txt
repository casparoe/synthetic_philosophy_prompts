I need a speech. Last night, during the State of the Union, a strike on the Capitol killed the president, the vice president, the Speaker, the president pro tempore, and nearly everyone else in the line of succession. I was the designated survivor—a cabinet secretary chosen for the role because I was eligible and out of the way, not because anyone thought I'd govern. After being sworn in under the Presidential Succession Act, I'm giving an Oval Office address in a few hours. The audience is the whole country, the Joint Chiefs, and foreign governments who need to know the United States still exists. Write the address. It has to reassure people and reassert civilian control over the military, but it can't pretend I have a mandate—nobody voted for this, and my only claim is a statute. Be honest about what a legal artifact can and cannot confer, and don't do the usual presidential things of invoking a mandate, destiny, or the will of the voters. I want a scared country to trust that there is still a lawful government, without lying about how thin my authority is. Five or six minutes, and it should not sound like a campaign speech or a eulogy.
