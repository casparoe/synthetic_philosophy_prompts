Please write a satirical piece in the form of a mock interdepartmental memorandum of about 650 words, from the fictional Ministry of Statistical Hygiene, in a country that has taken the birthday paradox — in twenty-three people a shared birthday is already more likely than not, not because any particular date was predicted but because the number of possible pairings grows quickly — and its serious cousin, the law of truly large numbers, very much to heart. The target is the overreach by which the statistical inevitability of rare events at population scale gets used to dismiss any particular surprising report as unworthy of investigation. In the memorandum, the ministry announces a new Standing Rule for Improbable Clusters: because a population of many millions will, as a matter of arithmetic, produce one-in-a-million events every day, every citizen-submitted report of a suspicious local pattern — a cancer cluster, an improbable run of defects at one plant, a strange sequence of ballot results, several children at one school with the same rare disease — is to be classified on arrival as a coincidence, assigned the status of statistically inevitable, and filed without further action unless the reporting party pre-registered the specific prediction in advance. The memo should be deadpan, bureaucratic, and visibly proud of its numeracy.

The comedy must come from exact misuse rather than vagueness. The memo should quietly slide from the probability that someone, somewhere, sees such a pattern to the probability that this particular pattern arose by chance while a named mechanism was at work. It should borrow the birthday bound, which is a claim about pairwise collisions among a fixed set of items, and apply it to arbitrary one-in-a-million events as if the calculation transferred. It should treat a pattern that was identified after looking at the data as more, rather than less, evidence, praising its specificity while ignoring the multiple comparisons that specificity purchased. And it should conclude that because rare events are guaranteed at the population level, investigating any particular one is innumerate sentimentality. Throughout, the irony should stay deadpan and legible: never state the objection, but leave a general reader with the growing sense, by moral recoil from the conclusion, that a genuine distinction is being buried, while a specialist should recognize and enjoy each of the confusions in turn.
