I am a software engineer by training, with no formal background in philosophy, and I sit on my borough's Public Commemoration Committee. We are about to vote on a renaming application, and I want a careful consistency analysis before I decide.

Our past decisions, in order:

1. Kettley Road (2016). We retained the name of a road named after Major Tobias Kettley. Kettley had commanded a punitive colonial campaign before entering local politics, but the road was named during his later mayoralty in recognition of his having built the town's first sewage system. A group representing descendants of the affected colony petitioned for renaming, but we found the harm it alleged was moderate, and we judged that the name honoured the sewage works rather than the campaign. We retained the name with an explanatory plaque.

2. Merrick Gates (2019). We approved the removal of a park gateway dedicated to Colonel Merrick and to the subjugation of the Northern Province. The dedication explicitly commemorated a colonial campaign, and a local community association gave testimony about the continuing harm the gateway caused. We removed the gateway.

3. Ashcombe Fountain (2020). We retained a fountain and statue of Sir Henry Ashcombe, a nineteenth-century mill owner. Ashcombe had been accused of exploiting child labour, but the memorial was paid for by subscription from his former mill hands to thank him for funding the town's first clean waterworks, and the inscription records that gift. We retained it and added a panel on the child labour accusations.

4. Dunmore Lane (2021). We renamed a lane that had been named in 1803 for Theodore Dunmore, a merchant whose fortune came from the transatlantic slave trade. The original dedicatory minute described the name as honouring the town's most successful merchant in the Africa trade. We renamed it unanimously.

5. Voss Ward (2022). We retained the name of the children's ward at the hospital, which is named after Dr Ilse Voss in recognition of the endowment she left to the ward. Private letters that came to light after her death show that she privately held eugenicist views, but she never campaigned on them or acted on them in public life. We retained the name and added a small note in the ward.

The new application concerns Caldwell Library. The library building and its endowment were given to the town in 1878 by Eleanor Caldwell, a wealthy local benefactor, and it has borne her name since; the entrance plaque and the name itself honour that gift to the town's readers, and no other achievement is mentioned. Recent historical research, now firmly established, shows that Caldwell was also the principal organiser and financial backer of the Ferrybridge Transfer Society, which removed children from poor families in this borough during the 1870s and sent them abroad as indentured labourers. Many of the children never returned, and the Society's records list several hundred deaths. Caldwell chaired the Society throughout the period and supplied most of its funds. The application to remove her name comes from the Ferrybridge Families Descendants Association, which says the name is a daily source of pain and that its continued use treats their ancestors' suffering as incidental. The town's historical society opposes renaming, pointing out that the name commemorates only the library gift and that the gift itself has been a public good for nearly a century and a half.

Please work through the following for me:

- Identify the principle or principles that best fit the five earlier decisions. There may be more than one serious candidate.
- For each candidate principle, say what it predicts about Caldwell Library.
- Give your best judgement as to how the committee should decide if it is following its existing course, with the reasons for preferring one principle over the others.
- Identify which earlier decision is the closest analogue to the new case, and explain why.
- State honestly what principle we would be adopting if we removed the name, and what principle we would be adopting if we retained it, on each plausible reading of our record.
- Finally, provide one further case, which has not yet arisen, that would most cleanly separate the candidate principles in a future vote.
