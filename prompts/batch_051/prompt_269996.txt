After a career in chemical physics, I have recently begun reading metaphysics, and the debate over whether there must be a fundamental level of reality has both fascinated and puzzled me. The disputants appear to share a single core apparatus: they treat grounding as a non-causal relation of dependence or explanatory priority, and they agree, at least for the sake of the argument, that it is irreflexive—nothing grounds itself—and transitive. From this shared starting point they move in opposite directions. The fundamentality camp insists that chains of dependence must terminate in ungrounded entities, on pain of a vicious regress in which nothing would ever be, as it were, finally the case. The anti-foundationalist camp replies that an infinite descending chain of grounds is no more vicious than the fact that the negative integers have no lower bound: each fact is grounded in a further fact, and no self-dependence or circle is involved. Both sides can point to the same agreed-upon examples of grounding; no empirical discovery would settle the matter; and after several years of intermittent reading I have watched each side treat the other's arguments as question-begging rather than as new evidence.

My working hypothesis, offered as such, is that the impasse is not really about what the world is like but about what each side demands of an explanation: the fundamentality camp is committed to an ideal of explanatory completeness that requires an ultimate level, while the anti-foundationalist camp is content to treat the infinite chain itself as doing the explanatory work. I am not asking for a verdict about whether there is a fundamental level; I am asking for a diagnosis of the disagreement. Is my hypothesis right, or is there a substantive metaphysical dispute underneath—over the formal properties of grounding itself, over the status of priority in infinite chains, or over whether an infinite chain is even a single object about which one can sensibly ask whether it is acceptable? Could it instead be a verbal disagreement, in which "grounding" means constitutive explanation for one side and ontological priority for the other? And given the correct diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result, a sharper distinction, a rephrasing of the question—or should the two sides simply accept that they are engaged in different projects and stop expecting to persuade one another?
