But I was on this forum where people got sick of arguments about borderline cases, and someone proposed that the community just eliminate the fuzz by fiat. The rule would be: every year the members vote on exact cutoffs for all the vague words we use a lot. Tall means 5'10" or above, bald means fewer than fifteen thousand hairs on your head, hot means 95°F or above outside, heap means 30 or more grains, and so on. A bot flags any post where someone uses a word on the wrong side of the cutoff, and each flagged use costs the poster a tiny bit of karma. Cutoffs can be amended by majority vote anytime. Walk me through what this rule actually does, not whether it's a good idea. I want to know how it changes the way people talk, what happens to borderline cases, how people would game it, where the first serious cracks show up, and whether it kills the vagueness or just moves it somewhere else. My guess is it breaks first on stuff like "tall for a kindergartner" vs "tall for an NBA center," but I want the whole picture traced out.
