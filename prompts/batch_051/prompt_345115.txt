I'm a software engineer helping a nonpartisan watchdog group build a rule engine that predicts how our state's Campaign Conduct Board will classify political communications. The Board's key distinction is between "campaign advertising" and "issue advocacy"; campaign advertising triggers registration and donor-disclosure obligations for the spender. I'm not looking for statutory analysis or general campaign-finance doctrine so much as the rule implicit in the Board's own earlier decisions, because those are the only cases I have to build from.

The four decisions:

1. A social-welfare nonprofit ran a radio spot during the final month before a general election. A bill was still pending in the legislature, and the spot named the incumbent state senator as the deciding vote against it. The script urged listeners to call her office and ask her to support the bill. The Board held that this was not campaign advertising; the spender did not have to register or disclose donors.

2. A business association ran an online video eight days before the election that consisted entirely of: "Vote to reelect Representative Okonkwo. He delivered." The Board held that this was campaign advertising and required registration and disclosure.

3. A labor union mailed a flyer in October that named no candidate and asked residents to contact their state legislator about a pending paid-sick-leave bill. The Board held that this was not campaign advertising.

4. A single donor paid for a newspaper ad that read, "Elect Maria Vasquez — our choice for district attorney." The Board held that this was campaign advertising and required disclosure.

The new case: Turnover First, also a social-welfare nonprofit, is running fifteen-second video ads that run only in the incumbent's district during the two weeks before the general election. Each ad shows a slow-motion headshot of the incumbent state senator, identifies her by name, and displays the text: "Eight years of the same. Maybe it's time for a change." The ads never mention an opponent, an election, a ballot, or a vote, and they never ask the viewer to do anything. The sponsor says they are commentary about turnover in office, not an appeal about the upcoming election.

I need to implement this as a decision procedure, not a lookup table. Please lay out: (a) the precise rule or small set of rules that would make all four decisions cohere; (b) what each rule under consideration predicts for Turnover First; (c) which prior decision is the closest analog and what it suggests; (d) your best prediction of the Board's ruling, with the reason you prefer one rule over the others; and (e) a future case that would cleanly separate the remaining rules if this one still leaves them both standing.
