I keep noticing that arguments about whether a language model "lied" or "deceived" someone collapse because the parties are running different implicit standards: one treats any false output as a lie, another insists on something like intent or an aim, a third points at the training process and says the system was selected to produce convincing falsehoods. I am less interested in adjudicating these cases one by one than in fixing, in advance, what an adequate account of honesty and deception for AI systems must deliver. Assume the account should serve a practical function: it should let us decide when saying that a system "deceived" someone picks out a real property of the system's behavior rather than a loose metaphor, and it should let us tell when two people using the word actually disagree rather than merely equivocate.

Write the desiderata. I want a specification of what any adequate account must classify correctly or explicitly set aside: hallucinated falsehoods, sycophancy (telling users what they seem to want to hear), instructed lying, omission and withholding, strategically deceptive behavior that emerges without explicit instruction if such behavior occurs, deception by the developer through the model, reward gaming (exploiting a misspecified objective), and misleading-but-technically-true output. I also want the distinctions the account must respect: falsehood versus deception, error versus deception, concealment versus commission, deception in a single output versus in a policy or standing disposition, and the model as deceiver versus the training process as deceiver. And I want the account to state its own conditions of applicability honestly: what it requires access to (internal representations, training data, behavioral traces) and what it can infer from behavior alone. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that makes it load-bearing, and say which plausible definition it excludes or which apparent disagreement it dissolves.

Finally, say where the tensions are. I already suspect that a definition restrictive enough to avoid counting every false output as deception may end up requiring mental-state ascriptions that are hard or impossible to verify in current systems, while a behavioral definition broad enough to be applied in practice may over-generate. Identify the pairs of desiderata that cannot easily be jointly satisfied, which of them is doing the most work in ruling out candidate positions, and which requirement you would relax first if the account had to be used by a developer auditing a deployed model rather than by a philosopher trying to get the concept right.
