Imagine that in 2040, a massive wave of narrow AI systems makes almost all jobs that produce or process physical goods and routine information redundant. The transition is surprisingly smooth: governments roll out a universal basic dividend funded by automated production, plus high-quality public healthcare, housing, and education. People are not forced to work to survive. But the machines are not creative or socially competent: the work that remains is care, artisinal production, research, sports, law, religion, therapy, inspection and maintenance of the systems, and local politics. What happens to the meaning of work over the following generation? I want a reasoned prediction, not a story. Think about how people would seek status and meaning, how the division between "contributors" and non-contributors would play out, whether unpaid forms of work become newly prestigious, and whether the psychological need to be needed can be satisfied by hobbies or personal projects. Also identify which predictions are most robust and which turn on assumptions about human status drives, scarcity, or political choices. What would have to be true for this scenario to produce widespread flourishing rather than boredom, addiction, or status panics?
