My econ teacher spent a whole period on Milton Friedman's F-twist, and I keep going in circles about it. The basic idea is that you shouldn't judge a model by whether its assumptions are realistic, only by whether it predicts well. Friedman uses this example of an expert billiard player: you can predict his shots really well by pretending he calculates all the physics in his head, even though he obviously doesn't. Half my class thought that was obviously right because all models simplify, and the other half said it's just an excuse to keep using models you know are false. Then it hit me that the F-twist is itself a claim about what makes models good, so it should be tested somehow, right?

So heres the task. Design the strongest test you can think of for the claim that the realism of a model's assumptions doesnt matter to how good the model is (at least for prediction). Make it concrete: what models or fields would you compare, what data would you actually gather, and what specific result would count for the F-twist versus against it. Then be honest about what even a positive result would leave open — for example, whether unrealistic assumptions are ok for explanation and not just prediction, or whether deciding that realism doesn't matter is more a value choice than an empirical finding. Put your answer in JSON with exactly these keys: proposed_test, data_needed, result_for, result_against, what_a_positive_result_would_show, and what_a_positive_result_would_not_settle. For each key, write a few sentences explaining that part, not just a word or two.
