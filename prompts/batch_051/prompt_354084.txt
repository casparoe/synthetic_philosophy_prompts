I'm trying to figure out whether Bentham's attack on natural rights in Anarchical Fallacies still works now that human rights have become a whole field of positive international law. His core move there is that real rights are creatures of law: a right without a legal system to create and enforce it is a fiction, and when people appeal to such a fiction to justify disobedience they are really saying their own wishes should override the law. The development I'm interested in is that since 1945 we've gotten treaties, international courts, and bills of rights that claim to recognize human rights as if they pre-date the law and then turn them into enforceable legal claims. So the thing he mocked has been absorbed into the legal order. Does his argument survive that? I'm less interested in whether he was right in some abstract sense and more in what a Benthamite should say today: is a judge who strikes down a statute because it violates human dignity, where the constitution names the concept but refuses to define it, just doing exactly what Bentham warned about, or has the legalization of human rights changed the situation in a way that dissolves the original objection?
