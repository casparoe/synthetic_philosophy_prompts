ok i keep flipping between positions on whether i personally have to cut emissions, stop eating meat, vote, that kind of thing, and the whole debate is just people pointing at one case and declaring victory. before i read any more of it i want the full spec: what would an adequate theory of individual responsibility in collective-harm cases have to get right? the requirements list. the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the formal facts it has to respect — with the case or consideration behind each requirement — and then flag which are genuinely non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste (act-consequentialist, complicity people, kantian universalizability, whatever). if some pair of requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, say so, because i suspect the list itself is the interesting result. then one line on which single requirement knocks out the most candidate views.

cases i want covered at minimum, because i keep seeing them used sloppily: the 'my one burger/drive/vote makes no perceptible difference' thought; the threshold case (a raft sinks only when the hundredth person boards — does the hundredth act have a status the first ninety-nine lack, and can a theory say that without absurdity?); the overdetermination case (two factories each dump an amount that's harmless alone but jointly poisons a river); the harmless-torturers style sequence where no single step makes a perceptible difference but the endpoint is awful; the 'everyone else is going to do it anyway so my act changes nothing' case; and uncertainty about whether you're pivotal, since a theory can't just judge actual causal contribution, it has to tell you what to do before you know where the threshold is.

also flag the distinction between 'my act made the outcome worse' and 'my act was wrong' — i'm pretty sure a lot of this debate is people assuming those stand or fall together, and i want that marked as a choice to argue for rather than a default. and say where the fairness/assurance problem fits (i sacrifice, nobody else does, my act did nothing and i'm worse off), plus the participation version where what seems wrong isn't my marginal effect but that i'm joining a practice. the tension i most want inspected is wether wrongness has to track my actual contribution, and wether small contributions to large harms can both come out wrong.
