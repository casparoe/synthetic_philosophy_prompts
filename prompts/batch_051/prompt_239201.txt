I am preparing a short adult-education series on how to think about apparent conflicts between Scripture and natural science, and I want to have my own position checked for consistency before I present it as a coherent view. The position is broadly Galilean, drawn especially from the Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, but I have modernized it in a few places. I hold all of the following:

1. Scripture's primary purpose is to teach what is necessary for salvation and how to live a holy life, not to provide a physical or astronomical account of the cosmos.

2. The sacred writers accommodated their language to the ordinary capacities and assumptions of their audiences, so a passage may speak of the sun rising or the earth standing firm without thereby making a physical assertion.

3. When a physical claim has been established by reliable sense experience and necessary demonstration, one may not simply ignore it because a literal reading of Scripture appears to conflict with it; instead, the literal sense should be reconsidered.

4. Genesis 1 should not be read as a science textbook, but it remains true in whatever sense the Holy Spirit intended through the human author.

5. The Church's teaching authority may issue a definitive reading of contested biblical passages, and I should not privately set aside such a reading merely because a current scientific theory points in another direction.

6. No empirical theory is ever demonstrated in the strictest sense; scientific knowledge is always revisable, and what counts as a necessary demonstration can shift as instruments, arguments, and auxiliary assumptions improve.

7. The Copernican debate was nonetheless a case in which the newly available telescopic evidence and mathematical astronomy ought to have led interpreters to revisit the geocentric passages, even though no complete mechanics of a moving earth was yet available.

I would like you to audit this set as a set, not to lecture me on the history or on any one item in isolation. Tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only appear to be so. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, explain what the cheapest revision would be for each, and state what that revision would cost. Then tell me whether there is a single revision that would remove the most tension at once, and whether any tension remains that cannot be resolved without abandoning something central to the set.
