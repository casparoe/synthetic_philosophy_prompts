I'm a novelist — fluent in fiction craft, basically a tourist in evolutionary biology — and the vocabulary I actually do my thinking in is point of view, free indirect discourse, scene and summary, character motivation, dramatic stakes, arcs versus plots, ensemble casts, backstory, subtext. I've been reading about the units of selection debate, the argument between people who think natural selection acts on genes, people who think it acts on individual organisms, and people who defend selection at the level of groups, and I keep trying to turn it into terms I use at my desk. My working translation is: genes are the small repeatable units, the trait or tic or line of dialogue that recurs across a body of work; individual organisms are characters; groups are the ensemble cast or the community the novel is about. Selection is whatever survives the next draft, or whatever holds a reader's attention long enough to get borrowed by another writer. But I can feel the analogy flattering itself, because in a novel the group only exists because I decide it does, whereas the gene's-eye view treats the organism as a throwaway vehicle that genes build around themselves and the group as the flimsy thing, with the gene as the only stable thing being copied.

So here is the job. Restate the actual debate — gene-level, organism-level, and group-level selection, including the line that group selection tends to get undermined from within because selfish individuals out-reproduce their group-benefiting neighbours — in my novelist's vocabulary as precisely as you can, and be honest about wich pieces you had to fudge to make them fit. Then give me a residue report, as a seperate section: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in how I think about a novel at all. I want to end up knowing whether my vocabulary is just too small for the biology, or whether the biology is quietly relying on distinctions — what counts as a unit, what it is for something to persist and be selected — that are not as clean as they sound. I'd rather you show me where the translation breaks than paper over the gaps. And write the answer in American English.
