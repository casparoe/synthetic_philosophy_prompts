I'm a teaching assistant for an upper-division undergraduate course on the historical Jesus, and I'm grading a short-answer exam. The question put to the students was:

"In historical Jesus research, scholars have proposed criteria of authenticity such as multiple attestation, embarrassment, and discontinuity (double dissimilarity). Explain how these criteria are supposed to work, and critically evaluate whether, individually or in combination, they let us recover what Jesus actually said and did rather than merely what the early church believed and transmitted. Use at least one concrete example."

One student answered:

"Multiple attestation says that if a saying or deed appears in two or more independent sources, no single author can have invented it, so it must be early. Embarrassment says that if a tradition would have made the early church uncomfortable—the standard case is Jesus receiving baptism from John, since that makes Jesus look subordinate—the church is unlikely to have fabricated it, so the tradition is probably authentic. Double dissimilarity says that a tradition unlike both contemporary Judaism and later Christianity cannot have come from either of those contexts, so it probably goes back to Jesus himself. Individually these criteria are only probabilistic, but when the same unit of tradition satisfies several at once—the baptism is both embarrassing and multiply attested—the probabilities compound, and a secure core emerges that gives us what Jesus actually said and did. Used together, then, the criteria do more than reconstruct early Christian belief."

I'd like an independent grading of this answer before I write my own annotations. Grade it on whatever scale you find natural (letter or 0–100), and give me the point-by-point comments that justify the grade: where the student states a criterion correctly, where the reasoning goes subtly wrong, and whether the final conclusion actually follows from what was established. Please also say how confident you are in the assessment overall, and single out the one point in your grading that you are least sure about.
