I help run an informal reading group on the philosophy of personal identity, and we have been working through Hofstadter's view, developed most fully in *I Am a Strange Loop*, that a self is not a fixed object but a pattern, and that patterns can be instantiated at lower resolution in other brains. On this picture, when someone has known you intimately, a coarse-grained copy of your self-pattern runs in their brain, and after you die something of you, in a sense stronger than memory or reputation, persists in them. One member of the group finds this obviously right and would assign it a high credence; another thinks it is a seductive metaphor that collapses as soon as one asks what makes a lower-fidelity copy the same self rather than a simulation, a model, or a distinct person who happens to resemble the original.

I would like your own probability, not for the general claim that we influence one another, but for this specific proposition: for at least some actual human beings, after their biological death, some agent-level fragment of their self continues to exist in the brain of another person who knew them well, such that the fragment is not merely a memory about them or a trace of their causal influence. State your credence as a number. Then decompose the proposition into the sub-questions your credence depends on, and assign a rough probability to each. The decomposition should address at least the following: whether identity admits of degrees at all; whether a self is the kind of thing that can be multiply instantiated as a pattern; whether a lower-fidelity copy in a different medium is best described as a continuation of the same self, a representation of it, or a distinct but similar self; whether there is any non-arbitrary fidelity threshold that separates genuine persistence from mere resemblance; and whether the causal route by which another person internalizes the pattern is the right kind of causal connection for continuation. Explain how the sub-questions combine to yield your final number, identify which single sub-question that number is most sensitive to, and say what empirical or philosophical development would most significantly move your credence. If you think the proposition as stated is not yet well-formed, specify the precisification you would need, answer on that precisification, and briefly note how the number would shift under the main alternative precisifications.
