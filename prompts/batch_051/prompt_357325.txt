I'm a PhD student in philosophy of action, and next month I'm presenting a paper at a workshop on shared agency where several senior people in the room will know the literature far better than I do. My paper defends a two-tier account: I argue that shared intention is not one psychological kind but two — a minimal form of joint goal-directedness, present in preverbal toddlers and plausibly some nonhuman animals, which requires a shared representation of a goal plus mutual responsiveness but not recursive mindreading or felt obligation; and a full form of shared intention, built on planning capacities and joint commitment, which is culturally and developmentally later. The core argument is roughly: first, that Bratman-style planning analyses and Gilbert-style joint-commitment analyses each face circularity or over-intellectualization when applied to the minimal case; second, that the developmental evidence on infant joint action cannot be accommodated by either unless one quietly imports the very capacities at issue; and third, that a two-tier architecture explains the entire pattern better than any one-tier rival.

The audience will include two committed planning theorists who think even minimal cases reduce to individual intentions plus common knowledge, one joint-commitment theorist who thinks toddlers' norm-enforcement behavior shows genuine obligations are already in place, and two developmental psychologists who suspect philosophers over-attribute representational structure even to adult joint action and will press me on whether shared goal representation is a testable construct at all. There will also be several agnostic graduate students. I'd like you to write my Q&A prep sheet: the eight or nine objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased in that audience's vocabulary and with a note on who raises which. For each objection, give me a two-minute reply and flag where that reply is softest. Tell me what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. Keep the whole thing under 1,400 words.
