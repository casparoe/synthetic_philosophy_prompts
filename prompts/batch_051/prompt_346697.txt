I retired a few years ago from a career in conservation biology and have recently begun working through some philosophy of value on my own. A practical exercise has left me with a set of commitments I would like checked for consistency; I want to know which parts actually conflict, which only seem to, and what the least costly repair would be.

A local land trust has asked me to help evaluate a graded series of possible management regimes for the same tract of salt marsh. Let the regimes be P0 through P100. P0 is full ecological protection: no public access, no development, and natural processes left entirely alone. Each succeeding regime P_{n+1} differs from P_n by permitting exactly one further low-impact human use—first a boardwalk, then bird hides, then canoe landings, then a small dock, and so on—each step adding a little more public benefit while slightly diminishing the marsh's ecological integrity. P100 is an intensively developed marina and recreation area. The series is meant to change by small, even steps.

Here is what I find myself believing.

(1) Completeness. For any two regimes X and Y, exactly one of the following is true: X is better than Y, Y is better than X, or X and Y are equally good.

(2) Transitivity of betterness. If X is better than Y and Y is better than Z, then X is better than Z.

(3) Transitivity of equal goodness. If X is equally as good as Y and Y is equally as good as Z, then X is equally as good as Z.

(4) Adjacent comparisons. For every n from 0 to 99, P_n and P_{n+1} are equally good. Judged side by side, neither adjacent regime seems better than the other; the public gain and the ecological loss at any single step are each too small to force a ranking.

(5) Endpoint comparison. P0 is better than P100. The whole sequence destroys something that no accumulation of small conveniences can replace.

(6) Diffuseness. There is no exact step at which the regimes stop being equally good and the next becomes worse; the change is diffuse across the series.

(7) No exchange rate. I also doubt that there is any precise and non-arbitrary exchange rate between marsh integrity and public benefit.

I have the strong sense that this package cannot be held all at once, but I cannot locate the strain precisely, and I do not know which commitment I would surrender. Please go through the commitments and tell me which are actually in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what else it would force me to change. In particular, I would like to know whether betterness itself, equal goodness, completeness, or the case judgments have to give.
