I'm an economist. The vocabulary I actually work in is medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, fiat versus commodity money, numéraire, the double-coincidence-of-wants problem, and general equilibrium. Take Aristotle's account of money and exchange and restate it in that vocabulary, claim by claim, as faithfully as you can: that money exists by convention (nomisma) rather than by nature; that it makes commensurable goods which are not strictly commensurable in themselves; that need (chreia) is what holds exchange together; and that there is a natural form of acquisition for the household and an unnatural one aimed at accumulation through retail trade, with interest as the paradigm of the unnatural. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which had to be distorted to fit, and which have no counterpart in my framework at all. Finally, tell me whether that residue is a limitation of the economist's vocabulary or a sign that Aristotle was tracking something modern economics has stopped asking about. I keep suspecting he was just groping toward the functions of money without a theory of price. Tell me whether that suspicion survives the translation.
