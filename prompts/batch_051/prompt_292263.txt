I teach an upper-level seminar in normative ethics and am writing the final exam. I want one closed-book, 90-minute essay question on the problem of consequentialist cluelessness that cannot be answered by repeating the standard summary of the problem. The question should force students to work through whether the standard replies solve the problem or merely relocate it. In particular, it should require them to assess (a) replies that restrict the consequences an agent must consider (to the foreseeable, the near-term, or those above some probability or seriousness threshold) and (b) replies that shift the locus of evaluation from individual acts to decision procedures, or to the adoption and maintenance of such procedures, as in sophisticated or global consequentialism. The question should not ask for named readings or citations. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain, and that identifies at least three distinct misunderstandings that should lose marks.
