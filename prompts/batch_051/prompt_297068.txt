Take the old sphex wasp story. The wasp stings a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to do a quick inspection, comes back out, and then drags the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's inside, when she comes out she'll drag it back to the entrance and do the inspection again. You can loop her on this indefinitely. The standard line is that she isn't really "checking" anything: she's running a fixed routine whose steps have to fire in order, and we're over-eager to read intention into it.

Now here's the thing that bothers me. Suppose I build a robot that does roughly the same task but handles all kinds of perturbations: if the cricket is moved six feet, no problem; if you swap in a grasshopper it pauses and recalibrates; if you block the burrow it digs a new one; if you ask why it paused it gives a plausible goal and an explanation of why the inspection mattered. People will say fine, this one is actually flexible, it has a model. But couldn't it just be sphexishness one level up? A big lookup table of subroutines, or a learned policy with millions of if-then branches glued together, would pass every test you throw at it until it hits the boundary of its training distribution. The wasp also passes all the tests, for the exact cases it was wired for.

So my question is: what's the strongest experimental or behavioral test you can design to separate a system that's actually checking / representing / re-aiming at the goal from a system that's just running a much more sophisticated fixed action pattern? Be concrete about conditions, interventions, and the result that would count for each reading. Then be honest about what a positive result would still leave open. In particular: can any finite battery of behavioral tests settle this, or does the difference between "flexible" and "sphexish at a higher level" disolve under operationalization? And if no test can settle it, is that a limitation of our testing methods, or a sign that the distinction itself is doing no real work?
