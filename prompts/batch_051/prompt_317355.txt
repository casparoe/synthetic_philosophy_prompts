Since retiring from a research career in physical chemistry, I have been attending a continuing-education seminar on philosophical logic, and a recent session on indicative versus counterfactual conditionals left me unsettled by a dispute between two other participants. The example under discussion was drawn from a falsified batch record in an old pharmaceutical-plant inquiry: the record was certainly falsified, although the investigation had not established by whom, and a particular technician was under suspicion. The two sentences at issue were (i) "If the technician did not falsify the record, someone else did," and (ii) "If the technician had not falsified the record, someone else would have."

One participant, Alan, a retired control-systems engineer, argued that (i) and (ii) have the same truth conditions; the shift from "did not" to "had not" is merely grammatical, he said, so no one can accept (i) while rejecting (ii). Ruth, a former actuary, replied that (i) only partitions the possible culprits against the known fact that the record was falsified, whereas (ii) asserts that the falsification would still have occurred in the technician's absence, which is a stronger claim and may well be false. Alan countered that evaluating a counterfactual requires holding the actual world fixed as much as possible; because the record actually was falsified, the closest world in which the technician did not falsify it still contains the falsification, and someone else must then have been responsible. Ruth answered that this begs the question: whether the falsification survives the supposition that the technician did not do it is precisely what the counterfactual is supposed to test, and an effect of the very event being changed cannot simply be held fixed. They went around once more without adding anything new, and I could not tell how much of the dispute was substance and how much was terminology.

I would like a written adjudication of this exchange. Please go through Alan's and Ruth's arguments move by move, say which claims are sound and which are not, explain whether (i) and (ii) really can differ in truth value, and end with an overall verdict as to who is closer to the truth and what, if anything, both are missing. Keep the entire response to 500 words or fewer.
