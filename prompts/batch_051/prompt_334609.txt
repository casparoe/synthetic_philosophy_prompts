I have been arguing with a colleague about whether physicalism is even statable, and the disagreement keeps coming back to Hempel's dilemma. Her line is this: if 'physical' means something like 'whatever current physics posits,' then physicalism is almost certainly false, because current physics is plainly incomplete and says nothing about (among other things) the hard problem of consciousness or dark matter. But if 'physical' means 'whatever some future, completed physics posits,' then the thesis becomes either indeterminate or trivially true, since we have no way to delimit in advance which future theoretical posits would still count as physical and which would be, for example, irreducible protomental or mental additions smuggled in under the name of physics. She concludes that physicalism cannot be stated so as to be both possibly true and informative. I am not convinced: I suspect the dilemma may rest on an unreasonable demand for a finished definition, and that there may be live alternative formulations—definitions that appeal to physics as an ongoing explanatory project, negative definitions (the physical as the non-mental), or structural or Ramsified characterizations of the physical. For context, my credence that physicalism survives the dilemma is around 0.6; hers is around 0.2.

I want your actual credence in the following proposition: there exists a formulation of the thesis that everything is physical, or nothing over and above the physical, that avoids both horns of Hempel's dilemma—that is, a formulation precise enough to have determinate content, not close to certainly false, and not trivially true. Please decompose the credence rather than giving a bare number. At minimum, assign a probability and a one- or two-sentence justification to each of the following: (1) the current-physics reading is the right way to fix the reference of 'physical,' so that the first horn is a serious obstacle; (2) a future-ideal-physics reading can be delimited in a way that keeps physicalism substantive rather than vacuous; (3) at least one alternative formulation—negative, project-based, structural, Ramsified, or another you specify—succeeds; (4) the dilemma itself rests on an illegitimate demand for a final, a priori definition and dissolves once that demand is dropped. Add any further terms that carry significant weight for you. Then show how the terms combine into your overall number, say which term you are least confident in, and identify what evidence or argument would move that term the most. Finally, given my 0.6 and her 0.2, tell me which sub-question most likely explains the gap between us.
