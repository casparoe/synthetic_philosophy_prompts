For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say what a theory that dropped it would get wrong. Also tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just local to one camp (a theist's add-on, a functionalist's preference, stuff like that).

Make sure the list has to deal with at least these cases, and don't dodge the hard ones:

- the Styx case itself: the gods are the usual enforcers of oaths, so an oath taken by a god can't get its binding force from a higher enforcer (unless the Styx is doing the enforcing); what does the theory say about who or what enforces an oath when the enforcer is the one swearing?
- a coerced oath, sworn under a threat or at swordpoint: does it bind fully, partially, or not at all, and what would that tell us about where the force comes from?
- insincere swearing: saying the words with a mental reservation or no intention to perform. Does the oath bind anyway?
- an oath to do something wrong or impossible: does it create any obligation at all, and if not, is it still an oath?
- the difference between breaking an oath and breaking an ordinary promise: is it just a difference in solemnity, or a different kind of wrong?
- release: if the person you swore to releases you from the oath, is it dissolved? Or can an oath bind independently of the promisee, the way a vow to God is supposed to? Can you release yourself from an oath without the other party's say-so?
- a non-believer who swears on a Bible, or on a dead relative's grave: does the oath's force depend on the swearer's beliefs about the witness, the audience's beliefs, or neither?
- the aftermath: whether the curse or punishment attached to the oath is part of the oath's content, or just an external incentive bolted onto it.

Then flag any pair of these requriements that you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — like if an oath has to bind in virtue of the swearer's own authority over herself but also has to work as a public act regardless of her inner states.
