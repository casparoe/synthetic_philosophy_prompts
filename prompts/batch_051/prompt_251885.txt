Me and a friend were arguing for like two hours last night about whether a reinforcement learning agent could suffer, and I need someone to tell me if either of us is confused. Here's the asymmetry that got under my skin. Suppose a lab trains a dog on a discrimination task, and every wrong choice gets a painful electric shock. The dog yelps, flinches, and reorganizes its behavior to stay away from the mistake states. Pretty much everyone says the lab is harming the dog; an animal ethics board would block it unless the justification was enormous. Now suppose a lab trains a reinforcement learning agent on the same task, and every wrong choice gets a large negative reward. The agent's expected-value estimates crash in those states, its policy bends hard toward avoiding them, and if you looked at the weights or activations you could find something like a representation of this state being bad and to get out. No one calls this a welfare issue; its just standard training.

My friend kept saying the two are totally different because the negative reward is just an update signal, it doesnt feel like anything, there is no affect in the machine. But I kept pushing on what the dog's pain is, functionally, if not also a built-in punishment signal that marks states as bad and drives avoidance. The pain and the reward penalty seem to do the same causal work, just implemented in different hardware. So I want to know: is there a genuine principle that draws the moral line exactly where most people draw it, or is the difference just prejudice for biology? Give me the strongest candidate principle you can find, then stress test it against a version where the agent also has a valence channel: not just a scalar number, but a continuously updated good/bad feeling register that biases action selection the way pain seems to bias the dog. If your principle survives, tell me what it commits us to, including what we should say about the dog and about a sophisticated enough valenced agent. If it does not survive, tell me which judgment we should give up and why.
