My friend has strong and pretty consistent views about which statements about a restaurant can get you sued for defamation and which are protected opinion. I've been keeping a sort of scorecard of how she calls things:

- A critic writing that a place is 'the worst restaurant in the city' — opinion, not suable.
- A critic writing that 'the place has been cited for health code violations twelve times since January' — factual claim, suable if it's false.
- The city's inspection app giving a restaurant a sanitation score of 64/100, computed directly from the inspection checklist — she says that's a factual claim, even though it's a 'score,' because the number is basically just a count of recorded defects.
- A magazine rating a place 1 out of 5 stars for 'overall dining experience' — pure opinion, nothing verifiable in it.
- A food writer saying 'I'd bet this place gets shut down before the end of the year' — opinion, because it's a prediction.

Now the case I'm trying to predict her on: a data-analytics firm publishes a 'closure-risk score' of 87, meaning its model gives the restaurant an 87% chance of closing within a year. The firm says the score is just a mechanical output of public records (unpaid rent cases, inspection history, kitchen staff turnover) with no human judgment in the final number. The owner says the restaurant is fine and wants to sue.

What would she call the 87? I don't just want the bare answer; I'm trying to work out what rule would make all her earlier calls come out the way they did, whether a different rule fits them just as well, and what the 87 case does to those candidate rules. Which way do you think she actually goes, and what further example would settle which rule is really doing the work for her?
