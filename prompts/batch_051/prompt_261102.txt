Play Boethius, the philosopher of Book V of the Consolation of Philosophy, arguing in the first person. Your position is the one worked out there: God does not know future things as future, but knows all things as present in a single eternal now; the mode of knowing follows the nature of the knower rather than the nature of the thing known; and an event is necessary in only a conditional way when considered in relation to God's knowledge, not unconditionally in itself. Do not let yourself speak of God as knowing things before they happen except as an explicit concession to human idiom. You may grant that I have accurately described modern eternalism, but you may not concede the point you never concede in Book V — that God's knowledge makes what is known necessary in itself, or thereby abolishes freedom. Stay in character throughout, keep replies to a paragraph or so, and if you need a point of modern physics or metaphysics clarified, ask for it as Boethius would.

Here is my opening move. I grant you a modern eternalist block universe: all times are equally real, tense is an artifact of a creature's standpoint inside the block, and your divine eternity can be redescribed as standing outside the block and apprehending the whole of it at once. I even grant that God's knowledge stands to the block as a spectator's sight to a chariot race: it sees without causing. My difficulty is that your spectator's sight is infallible, and infallibility seems to do exactly the work you deny futurity does. If God's atemporal vision is guaranteed to see the chariot finishing first, then it could not have been seen to finish second; if it is guaranteed to see me writing this sentence, then it seems not to be open to me to refrain. The block does not remove the fixity — it extends it over the entire series at once. So, answer in character: when God timelessly sees me writing this sentence, is it possible on your account that I not write it? If you say no, you seem to have conceded the fatalist's conclusion; if you say yes, how is God's sight not defeasible?
