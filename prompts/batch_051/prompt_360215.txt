Write me a parody of a class-action settlement notice — specifically the FAQ page a law firm posts for class members — for a lawsuit against a chemical company that polluted a river valley for decades before most people now living there were born. The company doesn't deny the pollution. Instead the FAQ explains, in reassuring legal customer-service speak, why no current resident was actually harmed by it. The move: the plant's jobs, wages, and the way it shaped the town determined who met and who was born, so without the plant, none of the specific people now alive there would exist. Since their actual lives are worth living (the notice treats this as obvious), no particular person is worse off than they would have been without the plant. The jokes have to come from getting the nonidentity problem right, not from straw-manning it: work in the person-affecting intuition that a harm has to be to some particular person; the same-number vs. different-number distinction (the company is very careful to say the plant never reduced how many children were born, only changed which children); and the no-difference view — the idea that when two choices differ only in which people exist, neither is worse — which the company leans on for one paragraph and then quietly drops when it would imply a policy that leaves everyone miserable is just as fine. Keep it deadpan for around 500 words, don't wink or name the fallacies, and end with a line that class members may opt out only if they existed in the no-plant counterfactual. Please write in American English.
