Two people in a reading group I lurk in got into a long argument about Hempel's ravens, and neither would budge; they asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. I'll reproduce the back-and-forth as best I can.

Maya opened with the standard Hempelian argument. By Nicod's criterion, a generalization of the form all Fs are Gs is confirmed by a positive instance, an F that is G. The claim all ravens are black is logically equivalent to all non-black things are non-ravens. A white shoe is a positive instance of that second claim. And if logically equivalent claims are confirmed by the same evidence, then a white shoe confirms that all ravens are black. Weird, but each step looked close to undeniable. Dana said this is a reductio of one of the premises, and her money was on the equivalence step: confirmation isn't just a function of truth conditions, it tracks which predicates we actually project. Raven and black are projectible predicates; non-black and non-raven are complements we carve out precisely to game the criterion, and once complements are allowed, any random object is a positive instance of some complement-restated hypothesis, so the criterion has no selectivity left. A white shoe does ornithological work only if you've already decided the non-black/non-raven carve matters.

Maya came back with the Bayesian treatment instead of defending Nicod. Toy model: sample objects uniformly from a finite universe, fix the number of ravens r and the number of non-black things m, and compare the hypothesis that no ravens are non-black with the specific alternative that exactly one raven is non-black. Observing a random object that is a non-black non-raven has likelihood ratio m/(m-1) in favor of the hypothesis; observing a black raven has r/(r-1). Both exceed one, so both confirm; it's just that the shoe's increment is astronomically small when m is much bigger than r. So Hempel was right qualitatively and only wrong about treating confirmation as all-or-nothing. Indoor ornithology works, its just slow. Dana said this doesn't rescue the shoe, it dissolves the idea that evidence confirms a hypothesis full stop: the toy model fixes the sampling frame, the raven count, and the alternative. Change any of these and the shoe becomes uninformative or can flip sign—she sketched a case where the non-H world has a much higher rate of non-black non-ravens in its population, so a random draw lands on a non-black non-raven more often under non-H. So "the white shoe confirms" doesn't attach to the shoe; it's an artifact of the urn setup. The real lesson is that confirmation is a three-place relation between evidence, hypothesis, and background model, which is Hempel's qualitative program collapsing, not being vindicated. Maya said that three-place story is exactly what she now believes, so it's not an objection to her. Dana said Maya is then keeping the word "confirms" for a likelihood ratio of 1 + 10^-15, which is the original equivocation in a fancy hat. Then they went around the same loop again.
