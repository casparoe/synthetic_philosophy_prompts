Write the joint statement that a committed rational egoist and a committed utilitarian in the Sidgwickian tradition could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the dualism of practical reason. Include: the claims they agree on, including a precise specification of the apparent conflict between the principle of prudence and the principle of rational benevolence, and what would have to be shown for that conflict to be merely apparent; the exact formulations of their remaining disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes — I suspect one is whether both principles are coordinate requirements of one and the same faculty of practical reason, and another is whether the impersonal point of view has any rational authority over the personal point of view; and, for each crux, an argument or case both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, or to rest on different senses of having a reason, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
