His line is that rewarding the model only on the final answer is backward: its like parenting a kid and only ever reacting to the report card, or like managing a new hire by checking their quarterly output and never commenting on how they do the work. The process is where the actual learning happens, he says; if you don't give feedback on intermediate steps, the model—or kid, or employee—will find shortcuts: cramming, gaming the metrics, doing shoddy work that happens to pass. So his conclusion is that our label budget is basically backward: we should take most of the money we now spend on outcome comparisons and put it into step-level process labels, even though they are much more expensive and much slower per example.
