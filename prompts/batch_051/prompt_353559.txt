I sit on the grants committee of a private family foundation. We are weighing two proposals. One is a well-evaluated program to reduce childhood lead exposure in one city; it offers a modest but reliably estimated benefit per dollar. The other is from a small team developing a detection-and-response system for a specific low-probability, very-high-magnitude global catastrophe. The team's own analysis puts the chance the intervention matters at 1 in 10,000 per decade, but if it does matter it would prevent harm on the order of billions of lives. Our founding documents require grants to show measurable annual benefit but also permit exceptional grants for transformative impact. The committee is split: some take the expected-value calculation to settle the question, while others hold that a 0.01 percent chance of effectiveness is no reason to divert funds from certain good. Draft the decision memo I would circulate, recommending that we fund one proposal, both, neither, or a small pilot. Write the memo as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; the first point should state the recommendation, and one point should give the strongest consideration on the losing side.
