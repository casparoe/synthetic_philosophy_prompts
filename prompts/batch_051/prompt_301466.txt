Augustine, in Confessions XI, claims that the past and future exist only as present memory and present expectation, and that time is less a feature of the world than a stretching-out of the mind. Consider a partial, testable translation of this: the felt duration and passage of time are constructed by the mind's own activity rather than passively read off an external clock. Design the strongest empirical test you can of that translation. Say what conditions and measures you would use, what pattern of results would favour Augustine's constructivist view, what pattern would favour the rival view that the mind simply tracks a time already present in the world, and exactly what a positive result would and would not establish. In particular, say how far even the best psychological evidence could support the deeper metaphysical claim that time without minds does not exist. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, and write in British English.
