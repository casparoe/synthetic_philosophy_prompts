Consider an asymmetry between two ordinary judgments, often held by the same person. A father who kills his child because a private voice commanded it is, by ordinary standards, worse for the command's privacy, not better: no one else heard it, no shared reason covers the act, and the very fact that he would do this on unverifiable private authority seems to mark him as mad or monstrous. Yet the same culture venerates Abraham, who bound his son at God's command and was prepared to complete the killing; and on Kierkegaard's reading in Fear and Trembling, it is precisely the privacy and unintelligibility of Abraham's relation to God — the absence of any ethical justification others could grasp — that makes him the knight of faith rather than a common murderer. The two verdicts diverge on the feature that does the work in the first case: a lethal act against one's own child, undertaken on the strength of a communication no one else can share or verify.

The obvious reconciliation is that Abraham's command was real and the madman's was not. But that difference is not readable from inside either agent's position at the moment of decision; each has only private assurance before the knife falls or the ram appears. So my question is whether the asymmetry between our two judgments can be justified once the cases are made subjectively indistinguishable at the time of acting. I want the strongest candidate principle you can construct — the source of the command, the character and history of the person who heard it, the outcome's later vindication, Kierkegaard's teleological suspension of the ethical in which duty to God is incommensurable with the ethical rather than merely higher than it, or something else — tested specifically against the case where the two agents share the same evidence and the same felt certainty. If no principle survives, should we say that the tradition's reverence for Abraham is an unearned exemption from judgments it otherwise enforces, or is the accusation question-begging because the tradition simply refuses to treat the cases as the same? Be explicit about how confident you are in your answer, and single out the point you are least sure of.
