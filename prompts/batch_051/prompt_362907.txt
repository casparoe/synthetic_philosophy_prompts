I lead the research division of a mid-sized AI company, and we are preparing to release a powerful general-purpose model. Our safety team has asked for roughly six additional months of red-teaming and alignment work before launch; their analysis suggests this would meaningfully reduce the probability of serious misuse or misalignment in the released system. Two better-funded competitors are expected to ship comparable systems within two months. My COO argues that a delay would be self-defeating: the competitors will release regardless, we would surrender market position and the revenue we need for the next cycle, and the marginal reduction in risk from our restraint would be swamped by the risks the other releases create. He concludes that we should ship on schedule and improve safety incrementally afterward. I suspect this is the kind of reasoning that, if every company adopts it, ensures the safety work never gets done until something goes wrong. What specifically should I weigh in deciding whether to delay, and is the COO's argument sound?
