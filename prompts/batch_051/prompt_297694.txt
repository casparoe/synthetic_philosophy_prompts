So I've ended up as the person on my org's review committee whose job is to triage proposals that all claim to be "alignment" work before they go up to the people who actually decide funding. I read the alignment forum, and I basically accept the old point that two different problems hide under the word "alignment": the narrow one of getting a model to do what the operator actually intended, and the deeper one of getting it to do what's actually good, where those come apart when the operator is careless, misinformed, or adversarial. But I keep failing to turn that distinction into a filter I can run fast. Proposals will say "we make the model follow instructions more faithfully" or "we reduce sycophancy," and my gut says that's only intent-alignment, not value-alignment, but by the time I've finished explaining why, I can't say exactly where the line is, so either everything gets forwarded or nothing does. I see a few of these a week, and the stakes are high because things that get labeled alignment end up being used as evidence that our systems are safe.

Please write me a reusable screening procedure — a ordered set of questions with what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no — that I can run in 20–30 minutes per proposal, without assuming I can inspect weights or do more than read the proposal and skim whatever the authors cite. It should force the distinction between "does what was asked" and "does what should have been asked" onto concrete, checkable features of a proposal, and it should mark the steps that are irreducable judgement calls rather than pretending they're mechanical. Then run the procedure on two or three short hypothetical proposals: one using process supervision to stop reward hacking, one building a finetuning set from a panel of ordinary users' stated preferences, and one training the model to refuse requests that would run down a short hard-coded list of human-defined harms. At least one of these should be the kind of thing I'd personally be excited to fund, and the procedure should still tell me why it's only doing the narrow half. Also tell me what the procedure will systematically miss, so I don't start waving it around as more than it is.
