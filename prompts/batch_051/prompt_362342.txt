I am a novelist, and I sit on the board of a small private foundation established by the estate of a late fiction writer. The foundation's charter states that its purpose is to support work that would not otherwise exist, and each year the four-person board allocates roughly five hundred thousand dollars in a single June meeting. We are currently split three ways. One faction wants to renew a large grant to a well-evidenced global-health program — delivering malaria-preventive treatment to children, supported by randomized trials — on the grounds that nothing else approaches its cost per life improved. A second faction wants to fund an early-stage organization working on pandemic preparedness: the stakes are immense and almost no one else will fund it, but we cannot determine whether the group is competent or whether our grant would accomplish anything. A third faction, to which I am temperamentally closest, wants to fund a local literary nonprofit that preserves the papers of out-of-print writers; the choice fits the founder's identity, but the nonprofit is small in scale, already well supported by local donors, and will not survive a lives-per-dollar comparison.

I have been asked to circulate a decision memo before the meeting. Please write it as a document the board could actually vote on. It should apply the importance–tractability–neglectedness framework explicitly, score each proposal on the three criteria, and state plainly where the framework conflicts with the charter's literary intent and where it is silent. Recommend a concrete allocation for this year, including a partial split if one is defensible, and give an honest treatment of the strongest case for the literary nonprofit — the one I am biased toward — within the reasoning.
