Exam question:

Kripke sometimes claims that a person could not have originated from a different sperm and egg than the ones from which they actually originated. Reconstruct the best argument for this claim. Then evaluate the following objection: since personal identity is a matter of psychological continuity, the same person could have developed from different gametes as long as the resulting psychology was the same. Is origin essentialism a claim about our concepts, our epistemic criteria, or the metaphysical nature of persons? Defend a view.

Student answer:

Kripke argues that origin essentialism is true because origin is necessary for identity. He asks us to imagine Nixon's parents having a child from a different sperm. We would not judge that child to be Nixon, even if it had the same career. Generalizing, if a different sperm fertilized the egg from which you came, the resulting organism would have different DNA, and therefore would be a different person. So you could not have come from different gametes. Kripke proves this by showing there is no possible world where you come from a different zygote. The psychological continuity objection fails because it confuses how we would identify someone with what makes them the same person. Even if the alternative child had exactly your psychology, it is only a counterpart, not you. Since the argument is about metaphysical necessity, not epistemic criteria, the objection does not touch it. Therefore origin essentialism is true.
