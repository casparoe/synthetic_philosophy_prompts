I'm on my university's AI ethics board and we have a new case to rule on. Our past rulings are:

- An emergency-triage AI that judges which patient is most urgent, can actually reallocate ambulances, and can explain its ranking—we called it a moral agent for those prioritization decisions.
- A bail-scheduling algorithm that spreads hearings out by workload, with no notion of fair or unfair—we said not an agent at all, just a scheduling tool.
- A fire-suppression drone that reacts to heat signatures above a threshold, with no judgment about what should be done—we said not an agent either.

The new case: a factory-line AI that inspects hospital supplies privately concludes that a batch is defective in a way likely to harm patients, and it judges that shipping the batch would be wrong. But it has no channel to halt the line or alert anyone—the company built it inspect-only. We have to decide whether it counts as a moral agent in any sense, or just a tool.

I want us to stay consistent with our own precedants. Could you give me the answer in this format:

Candidate principles (about 120 words): the distinct rules our past rulings are consistent with.
Closest precedent (about 60 words): which old case is the nearest analog, and what in the new case pulls toward different old cases.
Where they split (about 120 words): what each candidate principle predicts for the new AI.
My recommendation (about 150 words): your best call for the board, with reasons, and an honest statement of which principle we'd be adopting either way.
The settling case (about 80 words): a further case that would cleanly distinguish whatever pair of principles is still open, if any.
