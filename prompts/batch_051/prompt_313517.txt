So im a novelist, not a philosopher, and I've backed myself into a corner with a piece of worldbuilding. The near-future setting has a company that sells two ways of getting "you" off your biological brain. The first is a destructive scan: in an afternoon it maps your brain down to the last synapse, fires up that pattern on a server, and then cremates the original. The second is gradual replacement: every few months nanomachines swap out a small percentage of your neurons for functionally identical artificial units, spread over maybe nine years, and the whole system is kept running the entire time; at the end they migrate the now fully artificial network, without turning it off, onto the same kind of server the scan product uses. The brochure says both leave a server-resident person with your memories, your sense of humor, your grudges. But the company, the law in this world, and the customers all treat the two products as completely different. Gradual replacement keeps you as the same legal person: no new birth certificate, the same marriage, the same debts and contracts. The scan is classified as death and reconstitution: the server person has to apply for a "personhood certificate," inherits your property through a will, and your spouse is a legal widow. And the plot problem I'm stuck on: a character who is two-thirds through gradual replacement wants to finish early using the fast scan, and the company refuses unless she signs a assisted-suicide waiver and a release from homicide liability. So what view of personal identity is this whole setup presupposing? It seems to be agreeing to something like overlapping physical or process continuity being what preserves you, not psychological pattern alone, since both routes end in the same sort of server-side mind but only one counts as never having died. Is that position coherent? And which parts of the practice really depend on it versus being just legal or marketing inertia? What would have to change in the company's classification of the two products if they instead held that pattern is all that matters, or that there is no deep fact that survives either route?
