A policy institute has floated a proposal that a country create an Office of National Character Assessment. Every five years, the office would issue a standardized National Character Profile for each foreign country, rating that population on a fixed battery of dimensions—propensity to breach contracts, disposition to violence, deference to authority, economic industriousness, and so forth. The ratings would be synthesized from diplomatic cables, trade-dispute records, consular refusal rates, standardized surveys of expatriates, and a rotating panel of informants paid per usable report. Domestic immigration tribunals would then be required to treat these profiles as presumptively reliable: an asylum seeker from a country rated below a threshold on credibility would face a higher evidentiary burden unless they can demonstrate that the profile’s generalization is not true of them.

Walk through how this institution would actually behave. What incentives does it create for the informants, for the rated countries, for asylum applicants, for the judges, and for the office’s own staff? How would the system be gamed, what second-order effects would appear, and would the profiles tend over time to track something real or something else entirely? Where would the first breakdown occur?
