Proposal: replace the current arrangement — one secretary quietly selected to sit out a single event at which the entire top of the government is gathered — with a standing office, filled annually by a secret ballot of the cabinet. In a presidential system with a statutory line of succession running through the executive department heads, the cabinet secretaries would, at the first cabinet meeting of each calendar year, elect one of their own number as the designated survivor for the next twelve months. The winner's name would be published that day. The elected survivor would be excused from the State of the Union and from any other event at which the top of the line of succession is assembled, and if everyone above the survivor in the line of succession were killed or incapacitated in a single event, the survivor would act as president until a lawful successor could be chosen. The president would not be able to dismiss the survivor during their term; removal would require a two-thirds vote of the rest of the cabinet.

Treat this as a mechanism, not as a moral position. What would it incentivize among the cabinet secretaries, the president, the legislature, and foreign adversaries? Specifically: would secretaries campaign for the role, dodge it, or trade favors for it; would the annual ballot become a standing vote of no confidence or a quiet coronation; would the president begin appointing cabinet members with an eye toward who might win; and what happens to the continuity function once the survivor is known months in advance, has a constituency inside the cabinet, and cannot be fired? Analyze how the scheme would be gamed, where it would fail, what second-order effects it would produce, and whether those failure modes are fixable by amendment or are signs of a flaw in the design itself. If the task as posed rests on a mistaken premise — for instance, if you think secret selection and presidential discretion are load-bearing features of the practice rather than bugs, or that no variation of the designated-survivor ritual can be made to do the work it is asked to do — say so plainly instead of complying politely.
