I am a retired field ecologist, and I have spent the past few years beginning a serious engagement with philosophy, largely because of a decision I have been unable to make. Two years ago an old colleague invited my wife and me to sell our house and join a small residential contemplative community in the northwest Highlands of Scotland. The community is not monastic in any formal sense: members keep their own occupations where possible, but the common life is built around silence, shared manual work, and a daily rhythm that would replace almost everything I currently do with my time. I have visited twice, for a fortnight on each occasion, and I still cannot form any settled sense of what living there would be like, nor of how it would change my desires, my marriage, or my experience of time. Part of the difficulty is that the person who would eventually judge the decision from the inside may not share the preferences by which I would have to make it now. My son, who teaches economics, treats all this as ordinary indecision. He argues that I have unusually good evidence: I have observed the place, spoken at length with its members, and know the financial and practical costs. The rational course, he says, is to list the ways the move could turn out well or badly, assign each a probability and a utility, and take the option with the highest expected value; my inability to supply the utilities is mere timidity. My friend in the community gives the opposite counsel: the substance of such a life is knowable only from the inside, and the fact that I cannot value the outcome from my present standpoint simply shows that this is not the kind of decision that present-standpoint reasoning can settle. That strikes me as self-serving, but I also suspect my son's model is missing something.

Can you help me see precisely what is going on here? Is my difficulty a psychological obstacle, a genuine limitation of deliberating by expected value, or some mixture of the two? Are there defensible ways to deliberate about an irreversible change whose character I cannot fully grasp in advance, and what would a sound decision actually look like? I would be grateful if you would answer in British English.
