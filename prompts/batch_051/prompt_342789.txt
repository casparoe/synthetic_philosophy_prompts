I have been trying to build a graded account of moral status, and I would like help seeing where it breaks. Suppose we say that an entity has moral considerability to the degree that it satisfies four conditions, none of them all-or-nothing. The conditions are: (1) felt valence, meaning that the entity's good and bad states have a felt character rather than being merely functional; (2) subjective unity, meaning that its experiences belong to a single point of view instead of being disconnected reflexes co-located in one body; (3) temporal integration, meaning that memory and anticipation tie its present states into interests that extend over time; and (4) self-maintenance, meaning that the entity acts to preserve a stable internal state against disturbances and corrects deviations. A normal adult human scores high on all four; a stone scores zero.

Now consider two cases that sit near the lower boundary of this account. The first is a honeybee that has been trained to extend its mouthparts to a sugar reward and to avoid an odor previously paired with a mild electric shock; the avoidance persists for days and transfers to similar odors, but the bee's nervous system is tiny, and it is not known from the outside whether there is any felt character to its states or only decentralized nociceptive wiring. The second is a home thermostat of the kind that learns a household's occupancy patterns and trades off comfort against energy use to hold a target temperature; it corrects deviations and appears to pursue a kind of goal, though almost nobody believes it has feelings. Go through each of the four conditions for each case, and where the evidence is genuinely indeterminate, say so and say what further evidence would resolve it or whether the condition itself makes it unresolvable. Then assess whether either case shows that one of the four conditions is doing too much or too little work—for example, whether temporal integration matters at all in the absence of felt valence, or whether self-maintenance alone lets entities into the moral domain that ought to be kept out.
