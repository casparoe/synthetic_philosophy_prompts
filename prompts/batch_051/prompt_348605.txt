A philosopher friend has been trying to sell me on the strongest version of the games-as-art claim, which he doesn't ground in graphics, story, or music but in something like the aesthetics of agency: the idea that the real artistic object in a game is the shape of the choices themselves — what capacities the game hands the player, what it quietly rules out, how one move interacts with the others, how the game trains you into a habit and then turns that habit against you. He insists this isn't just good design; it's the art.

I want you to take that position and translate it as fully and honestly as you can into the vocabulary I actually work in: affordances, constraints, verbs and their costs, feedback, state space, difficulty tuning, flow, mechanical depth, and the like. Go plank by plank and tell me what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge to make fit, and what has no expression at all in how designers talk about systems. Then give me the residue report. Is what fails to translate just the normative/aesthetic layer that my vocabulary was never meant to carry, or is it a sign that the aesthetics-of-agency view is smuggling in evaluative language without realy earning it — or, less flattering to me, that my framework is missing something a designer should be able to see?
