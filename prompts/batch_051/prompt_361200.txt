The puzzle, as I understand it: every outcome of a measurement with nonzero amplitude is realized on some branch, so it is unclear what it means for one outcome to be more probable than another; but the empirical success of quantum mechanics is packaged in the Born rule, so an Everettian cannot simply drop probabilities.

Write the list of requirements that a theory of probability in many-worlds must meet to count as adequate for the following purpose: it should explain why rational observers in an Everettian universe should act and update their credences exactly as if the Born rule were a genuine stochastic law, without adding collapse, hidden variables, or any extra probabilistic primitive to the universal wavefunction.
