I've been unable to decide whether a parallel I keep noticing is a genuine structural identity or just a loose analogy, and I'd like your verdict.

Consider the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. In special relativity there is no frame-independent simultaneity: two inertial observers passing through the same event slice spacetime into different 'nows.' If one assumes that whatever is simultaneous with one's here-and-now is real, and that reality cannot be frame-dependent, then a distant event that my frame files under 'future' can sit on another observer's simultaneity slice through our common here-and-now, so it must already be real. The usual moral is that the moving 'now' is not a cosmic feature but an index into a four-dimensional block that exists all at once, with causal structure as its invariant skeleton.

This looks to me exactly like the relation between the denotational and operational descriptions of a pure, lazily evaluated program. There, the entire expression exists as an object from the start; evaluation creates nothing but forces thunks in an order fixed by demand. What counts as 'the current state of the computation' — which thunks have been forced and which one is being forced now — depends on the reduction strategy, just as the 'now' slicing of spacetime depends on frame. The invariant core on the computing side is data dependency: an expression whose value depends on another must have that subexpression forced first, no matter the strategy, while independent subexpressions, like spacelike-separated events, have no absolute order. And nobody thinks a particular reduction sequence is the program; the program is the denotational value, of which each reduction sequence is a perspective.

I want to know whether this is a deep identity of structure — two cases of the same distinction between an invariant structure and a frame- or strategy-dependent index into it — or whether the joints fail. If it is real, does anything transfer? For example, some critics of Rietdijk–Putnam say that 'real for an observer' does not entail real simpliciter; the computing case seems to make that error transparent, because no one would confuse a reduction sequence with the program itself, but it also might make the analogy look cheap, since in physics the block is supposed to be a discovery about the world whereas in computation the denotational object is stipulated as the meaning. If it is a confusion, tell me exactly which piece of the mapping comes apart. Please also state the mapping explicitly, say what would have to be true on both sides for any result to transfer, and name something the parallel predicts that turns out false.

Finally, say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would move you between deep and superficial, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. End with a single machine-readable line of the form \answer{deep}, \answer{superficial}, or \answer{mixed}.
