I have been defending a particular answer to the two-person bargaining problem, and before I build more on it I would like it subjected to a serious stress test. My position is that the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution is the right normative benchmark for dividing a cooperative surplus: take each party's disagreement payoff as the baseline; define the ideal point by each party's highest feasible payoff while the other party is still assured at least their disagreement payoff; then select the Pareto-efficient agreement at which both parties have covered the same fraction of the distance from the disagreement point to that ideal point. I prefer this to the standard Nash solution, which maximizes the product of the agents' gains over their disagreement payoffs, because I think unchosen alternatives should matter. A party whose best realistic outcome improves seems to me to have a stronger claim even when the newly available option is not itself selected, and Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives tells us to ignore exactly that kind of change. I am taking the usual setup for granted — complete information, a compact convex utility possibility set, utilities meaningful only up to positive affine transformations — and I mean the solution as a fairness criterion, not as a prediction of how actual bargainers will behave.

What I want is a map of this position's exposure. What would have to be true — or what constructed cases, intuition pumps, and formal results — should lead me to weaken or abandon the view? Please distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from those that would be merely awkward, and say which objections a committed Kalai–Smorodinsky defender would themselves concede carry real weight, rather than objections that simply import considerations the bargaining model deliberately sets aside, such as desert or preexisting entitlements. I would also like an honest check on whether the monotonicity motivation I have given actually commits me to more than the solution delivers, and whether the view has been insulated by its axioms in a way I should find troubling. Rank the defeaters by how much they should move me, and for each indicate the cheapest amendment that would absorb it.
