I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep arguing with a designer on my team about where good product design ends and manipulation begins. We work on stuff that competes for attention, and nobody on the team can actually define manipulation; we just call things 'dark patterns' when they feel gross. I want a working definition of manipulation (or manipulative design) that handles these seven cases: a countdown timer that resets every time you reload; a cancel-subscription flow that forces you to call a retention agent; a grocery store putting milk at the back so you walk past more shelves; an honest 'only 2 left' label when there really are only 2 left in stock; a Duolingo-style streak that makes you feel guilty for missing a day; autoplay that queues the next episode before you've actually decided to keep watching; and a pre-ticked box for marketing emails. I'm fairly sure the fake timer and the cancel-flow are manipulation and the milk-at-the-back isn't, and I'm honestly torn on the streak, the honest low-stock label, and the autoplay. My hunch is the word is doing two jobs, one neutral (the design deliberately steers behavior) and one moral (it's unfair or bypasses my judgment), and those come apart somewhere in the list. Give me the definition or definitions, run them through all seven cases, and tell me which verdict you'd be least confident about, plus whether the pre-ticked box is the same kind of thing as the fake timer or something seperate.
