I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, and I've been reading around why there is something rather than nothing on and off for about a year. Here's the frustrating thing: the debate doesn't seem to have actually moved in decades. It's the same moves on repeat. One side says the question is ill-posed — nothing isn't a state you can even formulate, you can't quantify over absolutely nothing without smuggling in a framework, so the question only makes sense relative to a background theory in which nothing is a genuine alternative. The other side says that's just changing the subject: we can make sense of subtracting all contingent things, and if what remains is nothing, we can sensibly ask why that outcome isn't the one that obtains; saying the question is meaningless is a dodge, not an answer. Then someone invokes the old contingency-argument tradition, someone else replies that a necessary being either doesn't answer the question or just collapses it, and nobody moves.

My working theory is that this isnt really a factual disagreement at all — it's a disagreement about what explanation has to do. The deflationary side is tacitly assuming explanation has to be contrastive and local (explain why this alternative got selected over a genuine rival), while the demanding side thinks explanation can be global and non-contrastive (explain why there is anything at all, full stop). If that's right, the fight over whether the question is meaningless is a proxy for a fight about the norms of explanation. But I can also see it being a bare modal-intuition standoff — one side finds the possibility of absolute nothing obvious, the other finds it incoherent, and there's no further argument either way.

So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Verbal, substantive, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would genuinely count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a reformulation — or should I accept that it's a clash of bedrock intuitions about explanation that isn't going to move?
