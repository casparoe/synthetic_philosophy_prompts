I'm in a seminar on what we owe each other in the attitudes we take toward one another, not only in what we do. Across the term, two camps have formed. The first, broadly Kantian, holds that what we owe is equal respect for persons as such: an acknowledgment of each person's standing to make claims on us, irrespective of their particular identity, merit, or relationship to us. The second, broadly Hegelian, holds that equal respect is necessary but too thin: people can be granted full formal standing while still suffering the distinctive injury of having their concrete self-understanding — their identities, differences, and ways of being someone — pass unrecognized. The two camps have been using "recognition" for both ideas, and we suspect part of the argument is verbal without knowing how much. Please draft the document these two camps should produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: first, a numbered list of claims about owed attitudes that both can sign; second, a disagreements table laying out the residual disagreements, with each side's view phrased so its own proponents would accept the wording; and third, a short list of cruxes, each with a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. The cruxes must include (a) whether there can be a genuine wrong of misrecognition that survives when equal standing and rights are fully secured, and (b) whether the owed attitude is best construed as something directive — giving the other's claims weight in deliberation — or as something perceptual, like attention to who the other actually is. If, in the course of the statement, part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, isolate that part exactly and say why.
