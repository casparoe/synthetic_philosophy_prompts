On the social media applications I use, two features sit rather uneasily alongside each other. The feed is designed so that it has no natural ending: new posts load indefinitely, videos begin automatically, and continuing requires no decision at all, whereas stopping requires a deliberate act of resistance. Yet the same applications also offer built-in take-a-break reminders, daily usage limits, and weekly screen-time reports that seem intended to help me stop. What view of agency, self-control, or attention does this pairing presuppose? Please reconstruct the implicit view as precisely as you can, separating what the design strictly presupposes from what it merely suggests or happens to fit with. Then consider whether the two halves of the design are consistent: does the time-limit feature rest on a different picture of the user than the endless feed does, and can both pictures be held at once? Finally, explain what would have to change in these applications if their designers instead presupposed that continued use is shaped chiefly by the interface and the surrounding environment, with individual self-control playing only a minor role.
