I work as a backend engineer at a mid-size social platform (forums, live streams, user videos, that kind of thing). We're rolling out a tagging system so users can filter content by topic, and I got assigned the "religion & spirituality" tag. I figured this would be a simple enum, but the taxonomy group has been fighting about it for two weeks. Half of them say a religion needs a god and a holy book. Then someone points out that Buddhism mostly doesn't have a creator god and isn't organized around a single scripture, but nobody wants to say Buddhism isn't a religion. Then someone else says Confucianism should count because it has temples, rituals, and ancestor veneration, and another person says no, it's more of an ethical and political system. The product manager made it worse by saying we should support "secular religions" too — she brought up a big fandom with its own moral rules, pilgrimages to conventions, and schisms, and also the idea of a national civil religion where holidays and monuments work sort of ritually. My engineering brain wants a clean decision rule, but every rule we try either excludes something obvious or lets in everything.

So here's what I'd like: sort these into tiers based on how confident you'd be tagging them as "religion" on our platform — say "definately tag," "probably tag," "borderline," "probably not," and "definitely not" — and give a sentence or two for each. The candidates: Theravada Buddhism, Confucianism, a Star Wars fan community with a shared moral code and regular rituals, American civil religion (Thanksgiving, Memorial Day, presidential inaugurations, etc.), a soccer supporters' group with chants and away-game pilgrimages, Quakerism, and a twelve-step recovery program. I care less about the exact product answer and more about the reasoning: what's doing the work when you decide something is more or less central to the category?
