I'm on a city task force in a mid-sized U.S. city (roughly 300,000 people) that has to recommend a new approach to adult prostitution. Right now selling sex is a misdemeanor, we run a diversion program for repeat sellers, and buyers are almost never charged. We've recieved three proposals. The first is the Nordic model: repeal penalties for selling, criminalize buying, and put the fine revenue into exit services. The second is legalization with licensing, in the spirit of Germany and the Netherlands, with designated zones, mandatory health checks, and a registry. The third is full decriminalization along New Zealand lines: treat sex work as ordinary work, with labor protections and no bespoke sex-work statute. The local wrinkle is that the city has a large undocumented immigrant population, so advocates warn that any licensing or registration scheme will exclude exactly the people most at risk; the police union says that dropping all offenses will make street solicitation near schools and parks impossible to control; an anti-trafficking coalition says both legalization and decriminalization normalize an industry that is inherently exploitive and will just grow trafficking; and a sex-worker-led group says the current diversion program already functions as surveillance and that full decriminalization is the only option that doesn't use the criminal law against them. I have to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's vote.

I'd like you to act as an expert on sex-work policy and applied ethics — think carefully, maybe brainstorm the strongest consideration for each option before settling on one. Draft the memo in American English: pick one of the three options and recommend it, give the reasons in language the other members could actually act on, and engage the strongest argument for the strongest losing option rather than strawmanning it. End the memo with a single machine-readable line, \answer{...}, where the content is exactly one of: Nordic model, legalization, decriminalization.
