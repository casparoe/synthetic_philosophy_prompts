Okay so I'm in this senior-year seminar (basically a philosophy class taught by the theology teacher) and we spent a month on Boethius's Consolation of Philosophy. The whole time we've been stuck on the same argument about whether God's foreknowledge rules out free will, and I mean stuck: same three people making the same three points every class, nobody moves an inch.

One side says it's simple: if God infallibly knows now what I'm going to do at lunch tomorrow, then that future is already fixed, and if it's already fixed I can't do otherwise, and if I can't do otherwise I'm not free. The other side runs Boethius's actual move, which is that God doesn't "fore"-know anything because God isn't in time at all; he has his whole life all at once in an eternal present and sees the timeline spread out like a map rather than one event after another. So there's no moment before my act where God's knowledge traps me. But then the first group says that just relocates the problem: even if God's seeing isn't temporally before my act, it's still true from the eternal viewpoint that I'll do X, and a settled truth about the future seems just as bad for "could have done otherwise." Around and around we go.

My working theory is that we're not having one disagreement. I suspect the two sides mean different things by "free" (one means "could have done otherwise," the other means something like "not coerced, doing what you actually will"), and that some people are treating it as a logic puzzle while others are treating it as a puzzle about how a timeless God relates to temporal creatures. So diagnose the actual structure here: is the impasse verbal (different meanings of "free"), substantive (a genuinely hard metaphysical disagreement), or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress? I'm definately not asking you to settle whether we're free—I want to know whether the debate is badly framed. For instance, is one side confusing "it must be that if God knows p then p" with "if God knows p then p must be"? Keep the answer under 500 words.
