I keep noticing a specific move in arguments, and I don't have a word for it. A person will say something like: "I believe X. But I honestly think that if someone else had exactly my information and looked at it fresh, they probably wouldn't end up believing X. I can see that my own reasoning here is likely warped by when I formed the view, or by how attached I am to it. Still, I don't believe not-X, and I can't just switch." It's not that they found new evidence, and it's not that they're fine with the shaky reasoning; they're not fine with it and they keep the belief anyway. Is there an established name for this state? Is it automatically irrational, or are there cases where it's the better state to be in? (Ordinary weakness of will is about actions, but this is about belief.) If there is no exact name, what would you call it, and how do you seperate it from a person who simply holds a belief and isn't troubled?
