The debate over whether neural networks literally have representations has felt stuck for years, and I'm starting to suspect the two sides are just using the word differently. The optimists point at feature directions in transformers, monosemantic neurons, and the fact that you can linearly decode concepts from activations, and say: look, there it is. The skeptics say that only shows the activations contain information we can pull out, not that the network itself treats anything as standing in for something else — so calling it a representation is just us projecting our categories onto a pile of numbers. Is this a real dispute about the systems, a verbal dispute about what 'representation' means, or two different questions sharing a heading: one about whether descibing the internals in representational terms is useful for engineering, another about whether the network genuinely traffics in semantic content? Diagnose what kind of disagreement it actually is, using what each side says, and then say what would count as progress given that diagnosis. My working theory is that the substantive part hangs on an unstated assumption that a representation needs an internal consumer that uses it as a stand-in — is that right, or does the real fault line run somewhere else?
