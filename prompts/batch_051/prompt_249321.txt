I'm a retired geologist who got roped onto our university's research-integrity committee, which mostly hears fights between labs about methods and evidence standards. Since I started reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty I've come to think a lot of these disputes stall because each side has hinge commitments — propositions they treat as beyond evidence — and neither side will own up to which ones. Someone on the committee has proposed a mechanism: before a dispute is heard, each party has to write down the propositions it will not let evidence touch; the committee publishes those lists (anonymized) and flags any proposition both sides seem to lean on but neither listed. The lists are cached and later disputes can be checked against them. Treat this purely as a mechanism, not a truth-finding device: what would it incentivize among the researchers, the committee members, and departments; how would it be gamed; what second-order effects show up; where does it fail; and are the failures fixable? Also be explicit about what would change your mind about how it plays out, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. American English, please.
