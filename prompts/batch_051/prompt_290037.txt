You are a professional philosopher of interpretation with a strong secondary competence in the hermeneutics of ancient historical texts. Take your time with this, and begin by brainstorming several candidate principles before you settle on a recommendation.

I have been trying to work out one general principle, or a small ordered set of principles, for handling apparent contradictions in Scripture, and I want you to help me build it in reflective equilibrium. Here are five cases about which I have settled verdicts, plus a sixth about which I am still unsure.

**1. The death of Judas.** Matthew says Judas returned the payment to the chief priests, threw the money into the temple, and then went and hanged himself; afterward the priests used the money to buy the potter's field as a burial place. Acts says Judas himself acquired a field with the wages of his betrayal, and there he fell headfirst and his body burst open. My verdict: this is a real apparent contradiction, but it can be harmonized. The hanging and the fall belong to a single sequence, and the purchase of the field was one transaction described from two angles. I do not think either account is in error.

**2. The placard on the cross.** The four passion narratives give the posted charge in four different wordings. They share a common reference to Jesus' kingship, but they differ over whether the wording includes the demonstrative, the name Jesus, or the hometown. My verdict: this is not a real contradiction, and I do not think we should reconstruct one original inscription as I did in case 1. The evangelists were reporting the substance of the posted notice, not its precise wording, so variation of this sort is unobjectionable and does not call for harmonizing.

**3. The order of creation.** Genesis 1 presents vegetation first and human beings later. Genesis 2 appears to reverse this: no shrub or plant had yet appeared when there was no one to work the ground, then the man is formed, and only afterward is the garden planted and the trees made to grow. My verdict: this is not a contradiction. Genesis 2 is not making a chronological or sequential claim in the same way Genesis 1 is; the two accounts are doing different work and are complementary in structure rather than competing in chronology.

**4. The census and battle figures.** In one account of David's census, Israel has 800,000 fighting men and Judah 500,000; in the parallel account, Israel has 1,100,000 and Judah 470,000. In the battle against the Syrians, one account says David's forces struck down 700 charioteers and the parallel says 7,000. My verdict: these numerals are flatly inconsistent, no honest harmonization is available, and the right explanation is textual. This is a copying or transmission error in the numerals. That is a genuine error in some manuscript somewhere, but it is not an authorial error, and it does not touch what the passages are about.

**5. The chronology of the Supper and the crucifixion.** Luke, like the other Synoptics, presents the Last Supper as the Passover meal, eaten on the day the Passover lamb was sacrificed. John presents the trial and crucifixion as happening on the day of Preparation of the Passover, with the Passover meal itself still in the future. My verdict: I do not think these can be forced onto a single timeline without doing violence to one of the accounts, and I am not willing to do that violence. I accept that at least one of the evangelists arranged or handled the chronology loosely, most plausibly for theological reasons, and I do not count that as error, because the point of the narration is not a unified timeline.

Here is my problem. Cases 1 and 5 look as though they belong to the same kind: two narrative accounts of one historical event whose sequences diverge. Yet I harmonize the first and decline to harmonize the fifth. Cases 2 and 4 also look similar in kind: both involve details where a single original wording or number might in principle be reconstructed from divergent witnesses. Yet in case 2 I decline to reconstruct and say there is no error at all, while in case 4 I also decline to harmonize but say there is a genuine, albeit textual, error. I want to know whether a single principle, or a principled handful, really does sort these cases the way I have sorted them, or whether one of my verdicts has to go.

Please do the following: (a) state the principle or small set of principles you recommend, as precisely as you can; (b) for each clause, say which of my cases motivates it; (c) apply the principle to all five cases and say honestly which of my verdicts it cannot deliver, if any; (d) tell me which verdict I should revise if I want the simplest defensible principle; and (e) apply the principle to the case I have not settled. That last case is this: the resurrection-morning accounts differ over how many women go to the tomb, what they find there (one young man, two men, an angel, or two angels, depending on the account), and whether the women remain silent or report at once. What verdict should I reach there, given the principle you recommend?
