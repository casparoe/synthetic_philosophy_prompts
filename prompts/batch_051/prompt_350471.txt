I'm giving a paper at an early modern philosophy workshop next month and want a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis is a strictly regularity reading of Hume on causation: necessary connection is nothing but the mind's felt determination to pass from a cause to its effect, there is nothing further in the objects, and causal claims do not covertly refer to objective powers; the 'New Hume' skeptical-realist reading, on which Hume allows that causation in the objects may be something real whose nature we cannot grasp, is a misreading. The audience is mostly Hume specialists, several of whom hold that New Hume view and regard the textbook regularity reading as an anachronistic positivist projection. My argument leans on the two definitions of cause in the first Enquiry and on the no-impression-no-idea principle; where Hume seems to mention secret powers or unknown causes, I treat these as epistemic qualifications rather than commitments, and I know some passages make that move look strained. Give me the objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased as these scholars would phrase them, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is softest.
