I spent forty years around vacuum systems and lab meetings before I retired, and philosophy is a recent hobby, so forgive me if this is a distinction everyone already knows. Something keeps showing up when I watch old colleagues argue in online forums, and I can't tell whether there are established names for the two different ways they seem shut off from the other side.

In the first kind of community, the opposing view just never gets transmitted. Not because anyone is too stupid to understand it, but because the infrastructure doesn't carry it: dissenting posts get removed or downvoted into invisibility, the feed never serves up the serious version of the other side, and everyone reads the same friendly summaries. If you ask a member to state the strongest argument against their position, they honestly can't. That strikes me as an engineering problem, like a signal that is never sent down the wire at all.

In the second kind, the opposing view does get transmitted and people can often summarize it quite well. But the community has pre-labeled the people who send it as corrupt, crazy, or malicious. So when the counterargument arrives, the member doesn't process it as new information. It's more like a ritual: a discredited source says exactly what everyone said it would say, and the fact that it said so gets treated as confirmation. Hearing the other side makes them more convinced. That is not a missing signal; the signal arrives, but the reciever has been tuned to read every such signal as noise.

Here's the case I can't slot into either: a retired-engineers mailing list I lurk on, about climate. They don't delete dissenting posts, and mainstream papers do get linked and read. But the list has a years-old norm that mainstream climate scientists are captured by funding, so a linked paper usually gets discussed as evidence of how the institution thinks, not as evidence about the climate. The actual content of the paper never really lands, even though the paper is right there in front of them.

So my questions. Do these two set-ups have names in the literature, and is the mailing-list case a third thing or just an impure version of one of the first two? I'd rather have the boundary between the mechanisms drawn carefully, including what difference it makes for whether arguing with someone can work, than get a couple of labels and a pat on the head.
