Two metaphysicians have spent a year arguing about the identity of indiscernibles. One side holds that the principle is a necessary truth: no two distinct things can be exactly alike in all respects. The other side holds that Max Black's two-sphere universe is a decisive counterexample: a world containing nothing but two qualitatively identical iron spheres two miles apart in otherwise empty space, in which the spheres are plainly two and yet alike in every qualitative respect. They have agreed to run an adversarial collaboration and produce a joint statement both can sign. Write the document they should produce.

The document should include the following.

First, a numbered list of claims both parties accept, written so that neither side regards them as slanted or vacuous. The list should cover: what the identity of indiscernibles asserts under its main readings, including the reading on which only pure qualitative properties count and the reading on which all properties, including relational and identity-involving properties, count; the precise content of Black's two-sphere example; what would have to be true for the example to be coherent, and what sort of result would vindicate or refute each side; and any apparent disagreements that are really verbal.

Second, the exact residual disagreements, each formulated so that its own side would accept the wording. These should include at least: whether the two-sphere description covertly individuates the spheres by using phrases like 'the two spheres' or 'each sphere' before any qualitative difference has been specified; whether distinctness can be grounded in a symmetric but irreflexive relation such as being two miles apart, which weakly discerns the spheres without distinguishing one from the other; and whether numerical distinctness is primitive or must be grounded in qualitative character.

Third, a section identifying the cruxes — the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side — and, for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction or expected verdict recorded in advance. If one crux is whether weak discernibility by a symmetric irreflexive relation counts as a qualitative difference or merely restates that there are two objects, specify a variant of the two-sphere case, or a more general relational structure, that both sides would take to bear on that question, and record what each side expects it to show.

Fourth, wherever part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part is verbal and which part remains substantive.
