I have been trying to pin down something about deterministic systems, and I suspect there is an established term for it, but I do not know where to look. Consider a system whose rules are exact and fully known, such as a simple cellular automaton or a set of billiard balls obeying ideal collision laws, and suppose we also possess a complete, exact specification of its state at some initial moment, with no measurement error and no rounding. I understand that in a chaotic system tiny errors in the starting state are amplified until prediction becomes worthless, but that is a problem about our ignorance of initial conditions. What I have in mind is different: even with that ignorance removed, the only way to determine the state of the system at some later time seems to be to run the system forward step by step. Prediction collapses into simulation, and the simulation takes about as long as the process being predicted. There appears to be no shortcut by which the future state can be computed from the present state in less time than the system itself requires to reach it, and the future state need not be compressible at all; the shortest usable description may simply be the initial state plus the rules plus the instruction to iterate them a certain number of times.

If the predictor is located inside the system it is trying to predict, the issue seems even sharper, because the predictor's own activity has to be included among the states it would need to compute, and that may be a separate problem. What I would like to know is whether this particular obstacle has a name of its own: unpredictability that arises not from sensitive dependence on initial conditions and not from any probabilistic or quantum element, but from the sheer cost or duration of the computation required to know the future. How does it relate to ordinary chaos, to Laplace's demon, and to the thought that a deterministic world might nevertheless be impossible to predict faster than it unfolds? If no single term fits, how should the boundaries be drawn between this and the nearby ideas?
