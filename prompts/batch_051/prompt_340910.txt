I have been trying to get clear on what the Epistle of James is actually denying when it insists that faith without works is dead, and I keep finding that my arguments with a colleague about James and Paul stall over an unexamined preliminary: what would have to be true of a person's state of mind for us to call it faith in the sense these texts are fighting about, rather than something merely adjacent to faith? I have been working with four rough criteria. (1) Content: the person assents to the relevant theological propositions — that God exists and is one, say. (2) Affect: the person has some fitting noncognitive response to that content, such as trust, reverence, fear, or love, rather than mere indifference. (3) Consequence: the state is hooked up to action in the right way — it inclines toward, or is partly expressed in, works of mercy and obedience, at least under ordinary conditions. (4) Direction: the state is directed at God as someone to be trusted or loved, not merely as a fact to be registered.

Now take the borderline case James himself presses, in the King James Version of the Epistle of James (2:19): "Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble." The demons, on any natural reading, satisfy (1) — they hold correct theology — and appear to satisfy some version of (2), since they tremble; but they do not trust, love, or obey God, and their belief evidently coexists with settled opposition to him. Is the demons' state, so described, an instance of faith according to my four criteria, or only of belief-plus-fear? Please go criterion by criterion and be honest about where the verdict is underdetermined. Where you answer no, say whether the trouble lies in the criteria — too strong, too vague, or drawing the line in the wrong place — or in the case itself. Then tell me whether this case shows that one of my four criteria is doing all the real work, and whether I should revise the affective criterion so that it distinguishes trembling from trust. Finally, briefly: does the case illuminate what separates James from Paul on faith and works, or does it mainly show that the single word faith is covering two different things in the two authors?
