I am a novelist at work on a near-future book set after whole-brain emulation has become the default economic condition, and in researching it I have found myself accepting Robin Hanson's central economic picture — perhaps too readily. The novel's plot currently rests on a scaffold I want tested before I build further. The position, stated plainly: once a human worker can be copied cheaply, emulations become abundant enough that wages fall toward the cost of running a copy; total output can be staggering while individual ems live near subsistence; growth is driven chiefly by copying and by selection among enormous populations of slightly varied copies rather than by improvements in each copy; and the human era looks, in retrospect, like a brief and extravagantly expensive transition.

I want to know what would have to be true for this position to be false, not just fuzzier around the edges. Which load-bearing assumptions could give way — about copying costs, energy availability, property rights in emulated minds, defection and loyalty among copies, coordination among ems or between ems and humans, or the political and ethical status that biological humans grant to copies — and which of those failure modes are live possibilities rather than mere logical escape hatches? What actual trends, mechanisms, or formal results would count decisively against the picture, and what would count as merely awkward or as forcing the view to qualify itself?

Defenders of the account often allow that a few very wealthy ems and human owners of capital would coexist with the mass of subsistence copies; I want to know whether concessions like that protect the position from evidence or quietly hollow it out. If the view has been insulated beyond falsification, tell me so, and say whether an unfalsifiable but vivid economic scaffolding is a real defect for a novel that claims only plausibility rather than prediction. I would be moved most by a demonstration that a specific load-bearing assumption is already failing in ways visible today, or by a historical case in which the same formal argument should have applied but did not; but I am open to less empirical considerations as well, including those that a defender of the picture would themselves concede count against it.
