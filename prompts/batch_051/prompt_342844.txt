I keep arguing with a friend about whether "possible" and "probable" are different in kind, not just degree. He insists that talk of possibility is just talk about nonzero probability plus hedging. I think that collapses two things. My go-to case is a dart hitting the exact center of a continuous board: the probability of any single point is zero, yet that point can happen. And on the other side, there are cases where I want to say two outcomes are both possible even though I have no probability to assign them at all — a completely unfamiliar coin, say, before I've even decided on a model. That sounds to me like a modal gap, not an evidential one, but maybe its just loose talk. How would one test whether the possible/probable distinction is doing real conceptual work rather than being a figure of speech? Give me the strongest concrete test you can, including forced cases where the two notions come apart, say what result would favor each reading, and be honest about what no test could settle.
