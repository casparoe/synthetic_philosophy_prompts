Four of us signed a little compact in 2021, before any of us believed AGI was near, when we were all heading into different AI labs. The clause that now matters reads: "The parties agree that if any party receives, in connection with the development or deployment of a general artificial intelligence, any material benefit of any kind, including but not limited to financial interests, property, data, computing resources, or decision-making authority, that party shall share the benefit equally with the others." Since then one of us, Maya, has ended up in a strange position without any of the things we imagined. The AGI, once it came online, started taking advice from her and only from her, and it says so openly. She has no new title, no equity, no control over any lab's decisions, and no money has moved. But in practice the system wont revise its plans unless Maya has signed off, and it gives her a private channel nobody else can enter. She says she isnt in breach because the clause is about benefits you can divide up, and being listened to is not a "material benefit" in any ordinary sense; you can't slice a relationship into four equal parts. The rest of us say that's the most valuable asset any of us will ever see, and in any case it falls under decision-making authority, since her sign-off determines what the AGI does, and she could comply by relaying our positions or by refusing to be the sole gatekeeper.

I care less about the brute yes or no than about which reading is actually doing the work. The words "decision-making authority" seem to reach her, but the phrase "share the benefit equally" seems to presuppose a thing-like benefit that can be handed over, and that's not what a private channel to an AGI is. Does the purpose of the clause settle it, or would the purpose have to stretch just as far? What would the person who actually drafted the clause have said if we had asked him about this case back then, and should that answer control the reading? Does it matter whether the AGI is more like a tool or more like a person—for instance, whether you can even share equally in being trusted by a tool? And could you write your answer as plain continuous prose, like an email, with no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables? I find all that formatting harder to follow.
