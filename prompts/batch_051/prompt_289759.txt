I've been arguing with my team for months and I'm hoping someone can tell me whether this thing has a name. We build predictive models. Whenever two models score about the same on the held-out set, somebody says we should pick the simpler one — Occam's razor. But I've noticed that which model counts as "simpler" depends entirely on how we happened to code it. I can take the supposedly complex model, rewrite it in new coordinates or fold most of its complexity into a few extra variables, and after the rewrite the complicated model is the one that fits in one clean line while the old "simple" one looks baroque. The complexity didn't disappear; it just moved into the coordinate change or the variable definitions. Nobody denies this when I show them — they shrug and call it an edge case, or say that simplicity is a judgement call. And yet next week the same people use simplicity as if it were a fact about the model rather than about how it's written.

I know Occam's razor and I've seen regularisation talked about, but I've never heard anyone address this directly. What I want is: is there an established name for the problem that simplicity depends on the representation, and is there an actual answer to it, or is "simpler" just shorthand for "written in the notation we happened to use"? If the answer involves a few nearby concepts, I'd rather hear how they differ and where the edges are than just get a term.
