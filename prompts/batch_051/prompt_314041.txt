I'm the secretary of my high school's Green Club, and we're stuck on a bylaws fight. Our constitution says: "All food served at club-sponsored events, or purchased with club funds, shall be fully plant-based." It was written a couple years ago, when the club mostly did bake sales and movie nights, so nobody thought much about it. This spring we're running a two-week exchange with a school in a rural town in a middle-income country. The host program is family-based, so students live with host families and all our meals are part of the hosting. The organizers told us that most families there eat chicken, fish, eggs, and goat, that the one small shop in town has almost no vegan protein sources, and that refusing food or bringing our own would read as an insult to the hosts. Some of the students going are on scholarships and can't afford to buy seperate food anyway. Our treasurer says that if the exchange counts as a club-sponsored event, then our own event violates the constitution, and the small food stipend we'd pay the host families out of club funds makes it even worse. Other people say the authors obviously weren't picturing this, and the point of the rule was to keep factory-farmed food off fundraising tables, not to police what a host family cooks in their own house. I'm less interested in the practical workaround than in what the rule requires or permits here. What would you actually get on the four readings: the plain words, the purpose behind them, what the writers would say if we asked them now, and what a reasonable club member would think the rule means? Which reading is strongest, and why? Oh, and please write in American English.
