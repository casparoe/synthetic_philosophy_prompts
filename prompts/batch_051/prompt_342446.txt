so we read Mill's On Liberty in my philosophy elective and I keep getting stuck on this bit from chapter 3 ("Of Individuality, as One of the Elements of Well-Being"):

"An opinion that corn-dealers are starvers of the poor, or that private property is robbery, ought to be unmolested when simply circulated through the press, but may justly incur punishment when delivered orally to an excited mob assembled before the house of a corn-dealer, or when handed about among the same mob in the form of a placard."

the thing that bothers me is that its the exact same opinion, word for word, but the newspaper version is fine and the mob version is punishable. my teacher says the difference is "incitement" but that feels like just renaming the difference, not explaining it. is there a real principle that draws the line, or are we basically punishing people for stuff they cant control, like who shows up and how angry they already are? if you think the line is defensible, test it on cases like someone reading the newspaper out loud outside the shop, or a private group chat that gets screenshotted and passed around during a protest, and tell me whether you'd actually keep Mill's line where he draws it.
