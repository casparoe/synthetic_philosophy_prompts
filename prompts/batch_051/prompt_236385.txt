So I got appointed to my town's planning commision about six months ago, and it has forced me to realize that I have a pile of gut calls about who should decide what thing, but I've never checked whether they add up to anything. I keep telling the neighbors that everything should be handled at the lowest level that can do the job, but I'm starting to suspect that's just a slogan I reach for.

One: our town made developers build bigger stormwater ponds than the state requires, because our creek floods. I voted for it. A neighboring town in the same watershed wanted to waive the state stormwater rule entirely to land a warehouse, and I thought the state was right to block them. Their runoff is our problem. So I apparently think towns can be stricter than the state but not weaker.

Two: a city a couple of counties over passed a policy saying its police won't cooperate with ICE. I thought that was out of line. Immigration enforcement is federal, and a city doesnt get to opt out of the part it dislikes.

Three: our town tried to ban plastic bags, and the state passed a preemption law killing it. I was honestly mad. How is the thickness of a checkout bag the business of the state legislature?

Four: then our town tried to ban concealed carry in the parks, and the state killed that with the same kind of preemption law. I was relieved. I don't want gun rules changing every time you cross a town line. But I cannot tell you why three and four aren't just the same case, and it bothers me.

Five: the state minimum wage is $12, and a town near us raised it to $16.50 for businesses inside its limits. I'd like our town to do the same, but I also think that if my principle lets one town go high, it ought to let the next town go low to steal jobs, and then the whole floor stops meaning anything. So I have no stable view there.

Six: a county tried to block an addiction treatment center from opening in a quiet residential neighborhood, saying it was inconsistent with the zoning. The state forced it through. I think the state was right, because once every county gets a veto, no county ever volunteers to host the thing. But is that really any different in structure from towns banning bags? I'm not sure.

What I want is this: is there one principle, or a small set of them, that gets close to all six of those verdicts? If it can't get all of them, which of my verdicts should I give up, and why that one instead of the others? Then test whatever you come up with on a case I can't settle: our town is debating a six-month moratorium on new short-term rentals, and the state just passed a law saying towns can't cap them. Should the town be able to pass its moratorium under your principle? I know the law says no; I'm asking whether I should think the law is wrong.

Also, tell me how confident you are in whichever principle you land on, and single out the part you're least sure about. I'd rather get a hedged, honest answer than a clean one.
