I chair the licensing and enforcement committee of a metropolitan borough council in England. Among our powers is an unrepealed byelaw from 1921 prohibiting the sale of goods in the street without a street-trader's licence. The council stopped issuing those licences in the 1970s, and no current officer can identify a prosecution under the byelaw since then. Street selling of this kind has long been tolerated across the borough: charity cake stalls, car-boot sales on residential streets, and most recently a cluster of electric-scooter sellers trading from the pavement outside a shopping centre and obstructing pedestrians.

A residents' association has now complained about the scooter sellers. One councillor moves that we begin enforcement under the 1921 byelaw, starting with warnings and then fixed-penalty notices, on the ground that a valid law cannot be set aside by administrative drift. The opposing faction says reviving a century-dormant byelaw would be arbitrary, would penalise conduct the council has long signalled was acceptable, and would license precisely the selective enforcement the rule of law is meant to restrain. I chair the committee and must circulate a decision memo before Thursday's vote.

I have watched versions of this argument collapse online into a motte-and-bailey between "the law must be enforced" and "disuse repeals"; I want this memo to be better than that.

Please write the memo in British English, and keep it to around 900 words. It should do all of the following:

1. Recommend a concrete course of action, chosen from: (a) begin enforcement now; (b) adopt a formal policy of non-enforcement while seeking repeal of the byelaw; or (c) a phased or hybrid approach with an explicit decision rule. The bottom line should be something the committee could vote on at Thursday's meeting.

2. Steelman the strongest version of the position you reject, rather than a caricature of it, and explain why it ultimately fails or ultimately prevails.

3. Draw the distinction between desuetude proper—long non-enforcement combined with open and widespread violation and official acquiescence—and mere under-enforcement or prosecutorial discretion. State which of these you take to obtain here and explain why the difference matters for what we may defensibly do.

4. Meet the deepest objection to your recommendation head-on. If you recommend non-enforcement, explain why this is not simply officers nullifying legislation and how the policy can be designed so it does not set that precedent. If you recommend enforcement, explain why the council's decades of tolerance and the resulting reliance interests do not make enforcement an abuse of power.

5. Say which single feature of the case is doing the real argumentative work (the length of disuse, the change in circumstances since 1921, the reliance generated by tolerance, the present nuisance from the scooters, or another feature you specify), and state how your recommendation would change if that feature were removed.
