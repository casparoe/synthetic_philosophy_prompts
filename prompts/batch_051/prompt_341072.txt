I'm an ethics grad student, and there's a move I keep seeing in seminar that I've never found one satisfying name for, and the more I ask around the more I get labels that don't carve at the same joints. The move is: someone rejects an action by asking "what if everyone did that?" Sometimes it lands. "What if everyone didn't vote?" does seem to show something about not voting that a purely individual cost-benefit misses, and "what if everyone jumped the turnstile?" seems to show something too. But other times it obviously fails: "what if everyone became a philosophy professor?" would be a disaster, but that's no argument against me becoming one. And the grass on the quad dies if everyone cuts across it, while one person cutting across leaves no trace. So the same three words seem to be doing at least three jobs: a check on whether my action still makes sense when it's the universal rule, a rough prediction of what would happen if the practice were universal, and something about my action depending on most other people not doing it. I want to know: does this move as a whole have an established name? Is there a name for the specific failure in the philosophy professor case? And is there a principle that tells you, in advance, when the move works and when it breaks down, or is it just a loose family of arguments sharing a slogan? If nothing established fits, i'd want a term and a definition that keeps the good version apart from the bad one, so don't just give me a label — I'm after the carving.
