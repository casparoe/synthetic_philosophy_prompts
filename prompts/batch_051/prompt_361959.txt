I am a data scientist who builds ranking and personalization systems, and I am fluent in that vocabulary: training distributions, labels and ground truth, objective functions, feedback loops, normalization, exploration versus exploitation, calibration. I know Foucault only at the level of a few introductory lectures, chiefly the idea of the panopticon. I would like you to restate his central claims about power, knowledge, and discipline in my working vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

Please cover at least these pieces: (i) the claim that power and knowledge imply one another rather than being independent forces that occasionally collide; (ii) disciplinary power as a set of techniques—surveillance, normalizing judgment, the examination—that train conduct and bodies rather than merely forbidding acts; (iii) the claim that the individual subject is to a significant degree produced by these techniques, rather than being the pre-existing agent who walks into the institution; and (iv) the contrast between a power that says no and a power that is productive, that generates capacities and behaviors instead of only suppressing them.

Translate these into the terms of learning systems: say what plays the role of the data, the labels, the objective, the update rule, the regularizer, and the trained model, and be explicit about where the analogy breaks or has to be forced. Then give me the residue report: what translated cleanly, what you had to fudge and how, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. For each piece of residue, I want your judgment about whether the data-science vocabulary is missing something real, or whether the underlying Foucauldian claim was less precise than its prose suggests. Keep the translation honest; I am not asking for a flattering story in which discipline turns out to have been machine learning all along.
