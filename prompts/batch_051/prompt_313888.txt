I've been kicking this around in a decision-theory thread on the forum for a while, and I still can't work out weather a mate of mine is running evidential decision theory or the FDT/superrational family. He's given consistent answers so far, and this week we're going to put a new case to him. Predict how he'll come down, and tell me what that tells us about his rule.

Here's his record.

1. Newcomb's problem. A predictor who is right 99 times out of 100 has already either put £1,000,000 into the opaque box or left it empty; the clear box has £1,000 in it whichever way. He may take either the opaque box alone or both boxes. He says take one box.

2. One-shot prisoner's dilemma against a random stranger on the internet: no communication, no reputation, no future interactions. He defects.

3. Twin prisoner's dilemma: the other player is a perfect physical duplicate of him, created minutes before, same payoffs, no communication besides knowing the setup. He says cooperate.

4. Parfit's hitchhiker: he's stranded in the desert, and a driver says she'll take him to town only if he promises to pay £10,000 on arrival; once he's home the driver has no way to collect. He says the right move is to pay.

5. Newcomb again, but the predictor is known to be guessing at 50%. He says take both boxes.

The new case is the smoking lesion, with a tweak. He has a mild craving for a cigarette, and he'd enjoy one. In this scenario, a gene causes both the craving and, through a separate pathway that doesn't run through smoking, a doubled chance of lung cancer. The cigarettes themselves do no damage beyond the craving; smoking is just a strong sign that he carries the gene, because carriers are much more likely to get the craving. He doesn't know whether he carries it, and choosing whether to smoke can't make the gene be there or not be there.

Predict what he says about the smoking lesion. Which theory is he implicitly running — evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, the FDT/superrational family, or something else? Which of the earlier cases already rule out causal decision theory, and if the rest of the list still underdetermines him, give your best guess and tell me which further case would settle it.
