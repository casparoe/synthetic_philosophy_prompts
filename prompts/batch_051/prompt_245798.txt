I keep going back and forth on something about roots and labeling. I think I accept the structuralist line for quadratics: there's no fact of the matter which square root of −1 is 'i', since conjugation swaps i and −i and every true algebraic statement about one is true of the other — the label is pure convention. Fine. But then I look at a cubic like x³ − 3x + 1, with three real roots sitting on the number line. To actually solve it you have to pass through complex cube roots that are themselves only defined up to the same conjugate-swapping, and the formula hands you the three real roots as an unordered triple; nothing in that algebraic machinery seems to know which of them is leftmost. Yet on the line I can point at the leftmost root, and 'leftmost' sure feels like a canonical choice, not an arbitrary label. So which commitment should give way: the slogan that objects related by an automorphism have no individual identity, or the intuition that the leftmost root is determinately picked out? Give me the strongest version of both and then an honest verdict on which one breaks — or whether 'leftmost' is just me smuggling the real line's order into an equation that never had one.
