Give me a working definition of a promise — by which I mean the kind of act that, performed under the right conditions, gives rise to a promissory obligation — and test it against the following cases. I am confident the first is a promise and the second is not; I have no settled view about the rest.

1. A friend tells you, "I promise I'll pay you back next Tuesday," and means it.
2. A robber tells you, "I promise you'll regret this," while taking your wallet.
3. A New Year's resolution, made silently to yourself, to exercise daily.
4. A politician's campaign pledge to reduce taxes.
5. A wedding vow.
6. Saying to a friend, "I'll be there at eight," with no explicit "promise," where both parties treat it as a commitment.
7. A promise made at gunpoint to deliver money the next day.
8. Saying, "I promise it happened," purely to insist that one's testimony is true.
9. A promise to do something one was already independently required to do.
10. A promise to do something the promisor knows to be wrong.

The definition should be one a competent speaker could plausibly be operating with, not a merely stipulated one. Run the definition through every case, mark which of your verdicts you are least secure about, and either revise the definition in light of the hard cases or defend any surprising verdict.
