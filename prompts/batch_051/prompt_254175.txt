I keep coming back to this bit at the start of the Qiwulun, in the Legge translation of Zhuangzi, where Zi-Qi says:

"I had just now lost myself; but how should you understand it? You may have heard the notes of Man, but have not heard those of Earth; you may have heard the notes of Earth, but have not heard those of Heaven."

I'm reading the butterfly passage from the same chapter, and I'm trying to work out what Zhuangzi would have said about something like Bostrom's simulation argument or modern dream skepticism. My professor keeps saying the butterfly passage is basically Zhuangzi doubting whether the waking world is real, and then this "lost myself" bit is just more of the same. But I don't think that's right—it reads to me less like skepticism about the external world and more like dissolving the subject that could be doing the doubting. If Zhuangzi were alive today and heard the claim "we're probably in a simulation," where would he actually land? I want a careful guess at his view on simulation specifically, and an explanation of whether the butterfly passage and this "lost myself" passage are making the same move or different ones. Bonus if you can say what Zhuangzi would make of the idea that the simulation argument gives us a reason to doubt ordinary waking experience.
