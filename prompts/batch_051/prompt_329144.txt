I've been collecting a friend's verdicts on cases involving loyalty and partiality to friends, family, and larger groups, mostly because I keep failing to reverse-engineer the rule she's actually running. Her track record, recorded over months of arguments, goes like this. She thinks a manager who hires a clearly less qualified nephew over a stronger stranger is wrong. She thinks a juror who lies about a minor detail to spare her brother a short jail sentence is wrong. She thinks a parent who saves his own drowning child rather than two unfamiliar children who are just as close is right. She thinks a donor who gives to a local food pantry rather than to a more cost-effective global health charity has done nothing wrong. She thinks a voter who backs a rezoning that raises her own block's property values while pushing costs onto a poorer neighborhood is wrong. She thinks an employee who refuses to cooperate with an investigation into her company's fraud out of team loyalty is wrong. And she thinks a person who spends the evening consoling a heartbroken friend instead of taking a crisis-hotline shift has done nothing wrong.

The case I want to predict is this. A wildland firefighter assigned to triage after a bus crash can pull only one child out of the smoke. His own daughter and two children he does not know are all within reach and in roughly equal danger. I know my friend would not fault an off-duty parent who happened on the scene and saved his own child. What I do not know is whether she extends that permission to someone whose job, at that moment, is to be an impartial triager. I can see at least two rules that would make her earlier verdicts coherent, and they point in opposite directions here.

So: what verdict should I expect from her on the firefighter, and what principle or principles would best make her track record coherent? I want the candidate rules spelled out, what each predicts about the firefighter, your best guess about what she is actually running and why you favor it over the alternatives, and the further case you would want to put to her next to settle which rule she really holds. Please write the reply as continuous prose, the way one would write an email or a letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and in American English.
