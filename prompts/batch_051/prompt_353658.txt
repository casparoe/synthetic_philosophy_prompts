I'm a philosophy of biology PhD student, and my reading group has been going in circles on the tautology objection for months now. One side says natural selection is empty because fitness just means actual reproductive success, so the claim that the fitter variants spread is a definition rather than an empirical claim. The other side says that's a straw man: fitness should be understood as a probabilistic propensity, like a coin's bias toward heads, so a variant can be fitter than average even if it happens to die out, and selection makes a genuine empirical claim after all. Then the first side replies that we can only ever infer the bias from observed outcomes, so the coin analogy just hides the circularity instead of removing it. Everyone knows the moves and nobody moves. My working theory is that the real disagreement isn't about the definition of fitness but about whether a theory can be explanatory when its central quantity is inferred from the very phenomena it's meant to explain. Is that right, or is it something else—a verbal dispute, a difference about confirmation, a disagreement about what natural selection is supposed to do? What kind of disagreement is this, and what would acutally count as progress? I'd rather you diagnose the structure than take a side. Please write in British English.
