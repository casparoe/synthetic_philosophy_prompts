I'm a novelist, and I'm stuck on an in-world text for a book I'm drafting. The novel is a near-future satire about a consultancy—working title, the Moral Compromise Exchange—that brokers trades between governments, denominations, activist groups, the works: each side gives ground on things it cares about less in exchange for wins on things it cares about more, with consultants talking like free-trade economists the whole time.

For one chapter I need the client-facing FAQ / rate sheet for the Exchange, written deadpan, about 650 words, formatted like a real startup's help page. The humor has to come from the actual philosophy, not just corporate-speak: pareto-improvements across parties whose value systems are incommensurable, the impossibility of a neutral metric for whose concession hurts more, the difference between trading soft preferences and trading deontic side-constraints, integrity loss itemized as a transaction cost, the cheap-concessions problem where both sides unload prohibitions they were already half-abandoning, and the claim that every client finishes better off "by their own lights." Maybe a pricing tier for things you're not supposed to be able to sell, plus a disclaimer about how iterative trades slowly change what you are.

The deeper target is the habit of treating a value conflict as if it were just a trade dispute with an exchange rate. It should read fine for someone who's never opened a philosophy journal, but a philosopher who works on compromise and value trade should laugh rather than wince.

Can you draft the page?
