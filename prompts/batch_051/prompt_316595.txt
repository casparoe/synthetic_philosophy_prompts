Give me a working definition of terrorism, treated as a category within the broader genus of political violence or violence-like coercion. The definition should let someone classify an unfamiliar case from its features alone, without first deciding whether to approve of it; if you think the ordinary term is irreducibly loaded, then separate the descriptive core from the moral charge and explain how the charge attaches. I do not want a definition that simply reproduces the legal category of some jurisdiction, or that makes terrorism mean political violence by non-state actors, or that makes it mean political violence I condemn. I want the features that actually do the classificatory work.

Then run your definition against every case below, commit to a verdict for each, and say which component of the definition produces that verdict. Where your definition gives a verdict you suspect many would regard as wrong, either revise the definition or defend the verdict. At the end, state which of your verdicts is least secure, and whether that insecurity exposes a fault line in the concept itself.

Take the first three cases as anchors: the first is clearly terrorism on any reasonable view, and the second and third are clearly not.

1. A non-state armed group detonates a bomb in a crowded city market at midday. A communiqué issued the same day states that attacks will continue until the government releases imprisoned members of the group. The dead and injured are mostly shoppers and vendors.

2. During a declared war between two states, one state's air force bombs the other state's army supply depot.

3. A man shoots his neighbour dead in a long-running dispute over a property boundary. He has no political involvement and issues no statement.

The remaining five are the hard cases; I have no settled view about several of them.

4. A rebel movement detonates a car bomb outside a police station, killing several officers and two civilian clerks who worked there. A statement says the bombing is reprisal for a police crackdown and will continue as long as the crackdown does.

5. A state fighting an insurgency surrounds a city neighbourhood held by insurgents, drops leaflets urging civilians to leave and to press the insurgents to surrender, then shells the neighbourhood. Several hundred civilians are killed; the shelling stops after the insurgents withdraw.

6. A group with no known members publicly seizes control of a country's electricity grid and shuts it down across a large region for several days in winter, causing severe but non-fatal disruption. A statement says it will do the same again unless a particular environmental law is repealed.

7. A criminal cartel detonates a small bomb at the entrance of a business that refused to pay for protection, with a warning that any shop withholding payment will be treated the same. The cartel's demands are purely financial.

8. A member of a banned political movement shoots dead the head of the state's internal-security agency outside his home, leaving a statement describing the killing as a military operation in a war of liberation.

Now do the work: give the definition, the verdicts, the component responsible for each verdict, any revisions or defenses of surprising verdicts, and the least-secure verdict with your diagnosis of what that insecurity shows.
