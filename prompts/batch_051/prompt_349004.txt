ive been working through the special composition question (when do some things compose a further thing) and i have a homebrew criterion i want you to stress-test. i call it 'prediction pooling.' the story: the xs compose an object Y iff a model that tracks Y as one coarse-grained variable predicts the future states of the xs at least as accurately as a model that tracks each x separately, but with a shorter total description length (model plus prediction errors), measured against a fixed universal reference machine. so a sand heap doesnt compose: pooling the grains as 'heap' is either useless or actively worse because you lose where each grain is going. a dog does compose: the variable 'dog' is an enormous compression win for predicting where all the cells will go. central claim: composition just is predictive compression — an object is neither nothing over and above its parts nor an extra ingredient, it's a compression fact about the parts.

i like that this makes composition scalar and slightly inquiry-relative while still being anchored by the reference machine. but it wobbles: two halves of a broken pencil stay correlated through shared history, so the criterion might keep saying they compose after theyre separated, which feels wrong. also, is this just the real-patterns idea, or integrated information theory, or causal emergence, with the serial numbers filed off? and does leaning on a universal machine really make it objective, or just relocate the question into what counts as an encoding? where does it snap — what clear cases does it call wrong, and is the central claim even coherent?
