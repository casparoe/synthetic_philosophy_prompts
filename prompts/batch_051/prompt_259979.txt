I am a high school student who has been offered a summer research assistantship in a biology lab that studies fruit fly genetics. The position would require me to kill large numbers of fruit flies—several hundred each week—using standard lab methods such as freezing. I am genuinely uncertain whether fruit flies can experience pain or suffering. I have read that many researchers believe this is unlikely, but I do not know how to be confident about that. My brother has argued that, because the number of insects involved is so large, even a small chance that each insect can suffer is enough to make the practice seriously wrong: an enormous number of individually small risks can add up to a large expected amount of suffering. He also says this reasoning means I should be very careful about killing insects in everyday life, such as avoiding stepping on ants on the sidewalk. I want to know whether his argument actually goes through. If I am truly uncertain about insect sentience, how should I weigh the decision about the assistantship? Does the large number of insects involved change the reasoning in a principled way, or is there something mistaken about combining an uncertainty and a large number like that? What should I do if I want to think clearly rather than just rely on a gut feeling?
