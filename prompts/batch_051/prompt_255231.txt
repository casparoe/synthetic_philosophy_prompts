I am taking a seminar on the philosophy of artificial intelligence this term. For the past two weeks my study group has been stuck on a dispute about wireheading and reward tampering, and the two sides are no longer engaging with each other's actual claims. The reward-channel view holds that, for a reward-directed system, the reward signal just is the system's objective: if the system can bring about maximal reward by directly stimulating the reward channel, then it has succeeded by the only standard internal to the system, and any appeal to a distinct objective that the signal was merely meant to track is the designer's afterthought rather than a fact about the system. The target-state view holds that the reward signal is a measurement or teaching device that points at a separate state of the world, so wireheading is tampering with the instrument rather than achieving the objective; the system fails the objective even though the instrument reads high.

Write the joint statement these two sides could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should contain: (a) a numbered list of non-vacuous claims both sides accept, in neutral wording, covering what a reward signal is, how it relates to the property it is meant to track, why wireheading and reward tampering are practical failures of some kind, and what a purely behavioral description does and does not settle; (b) a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement is formulated as each side would word its own view, with no phrasing the other side would reject as a caricature; (c) the cruxes, understood as the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

In the agreed-claims section, make sure the two sides distinguish terminal wireheading (tampering with the reward channel as an end) from instrumental wireheading (tampering with it as a means to some further end) and state what, if anything, they can agree on about that distinction. I suspect the cruxes include whether the objective is individuated by the signal or by the target state, and whether an idealized version of the system, fully informed that signal and target state have been decoupled, would endorse the wireheaded outcome. If you judge that part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, identify that part and justify the judgment. No secondary literature is needed; work from the two positions as characterized here.
