I am currently taking a philosophy of biology course, and our discussions keep returning to the question of what it means to call one object more complex than another. I have noticed that the comparisons I find most convincing all rely on something for which I have no name. It is neither the length of the shortest description of the object nor the degree of disorder among its parts. It is, rather, the number of distinct construction steps it would take to build the object from a set of elementary building blocks, with one important caveat: once a sub-assembly has been built, it may be reused as many times as needed without additional cost. On this way of measuring, a crystal is not very complex because the same bonding operation is repeated over and over, while a functioning watch is highly complex because the spring, the gear train, and the escapement must each be built separately before being combined, and none of those stages can be obtained from the others by mere repetition. A mineral lattice and a protein of comparable size would differ on this measure in just the way my intuitions seem to want them to differ.

I assume I am not saying anything original, but I do not know what to call it, and I want more than a label. How does this notion relate to Kolmogorov complexity and to entropy, and exactly where does it part ways with each? Is the notion well-defined, or does everything end up depending on what is allowed to count as a step or as a building block? If it has an established name, which cases does the concept properly cover and exclude; if it does not, what would be a good name, and how should its boundaries be drawn?
