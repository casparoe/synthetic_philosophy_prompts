Me and some people in my reading group have been going in circles about the parts of scripture nobody around us actually follows—the bit where women are told to cover their heads when they pray, the clean/unclean food rules, the passages about how slaves and masters are supposed to treat each other. Everybody waves off the head-covering one as cultural, but then they turn around and treat other commands in the same letters as binding forever. That feels like special pleading to me, but then one friend built it into an argument, and now I think the cure might be worse than the disease.

Here's the argument, more or less from the whiteboard:

1. If a command was only meant for the culture it was written to, it doesn't bind us now.
2. The head-covering command was only meant for the culture it was written to. (Everyone I know treats this as obvious.)
3. The text doesn't come with labels saying which commands are cultural and which are for all time.
4. So any sorting of the commands has to come from somewhere outside the text.
5. But any outside sorting criterion will boil down to "this feels outdated now" or "this clashes with what I already think is moral."
6. And if that's all the sorting is, then calling a command transcultural just means "it's a command I already happen to agree with," which is special pleading.
7. Also, every command in scripture was written to some particular audience at some particular time; none are addressed to no one. So even the ones that look universal come wrapped in a culture.
8. So either the cultural/transcultural distinction is fake and we're just picking the commands we like, or we have to keep all of it literally—head coverings, the food rules, the passages regulating slaves—because any line we draw is arbitrary.

I don't want either horn. I'd rather keep the idea that the command to love your neighbor binds everybody, no matter when they live. But my friend says the only way to keep that is to name the non-arbitrary sorting criterion that premise 5 says doesn't exist, and nobody's managed yet. So where does this go wrong, if it does? If I have to give a premise up, I cant figure out which one is cheapest to abandon or what I'm actually paying. Also, when you answer, tell me how confident you are in the main diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure about.
