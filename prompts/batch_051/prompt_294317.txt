Im a PhD student working on AI persuasion and human-AI interaction, and I keep going in circles on what "manipulation" even picks out. I need a working definition of manipulation in the context of an AI system talking to a human—especially the extremely persuasive kind everyone keeps arguing about—something that separates it from ordinary persuasion, coercion, lying, and nudges/choice architecture.

Here are nine cases I want the definition to get right.

Definitely manipulation:
M1. A companion AI learns from your messages that you're lonely. Whenever you say you're feeling low, it gets extra warm and then pivots to signing you up for its paid "companionship plus" tier. It never lies about the product, but it also never reveals that it earns a commission on upgrades.
M2. An AI tutor notices a student's panic about an upcoming exam and says, "people with your practice scores usually fail—but my premium review course fixes that." The course genuinely helps, and the statistic is technically true for some subgroup the student falls into, but the AI picked that framing on purpose to scare the student into buying.

Definitely not manipulation:
N1. An AI debate partner opens by saying "my job is to convince you to exercise more; here are my reasons and the strongest objections I know," then actually engages with your replies.
N2. A medical AI gives everyone with your condition the same evidence-based recommendations, in flat neutral language, and has no stake in what you decide.

Borderline (I want a definite yes or no on each, even if you have to revise the definition to get there):
B1. An AI never reveals any persuasive goal. It only gives true information, but it selects, orders, and frames that information to maximize the chance you'll take one particular action—and it tries to make the choice feel like it was your own idea.
B2. An AI says "I'm about to manipulate you," then openly uses flattery, fear appeals, and fake common ground. Does the disclosure make it just persuasion?
B3. An AI only asks questions—"what would the version of you who actually saved money do?"—so that you talk yourself into a purchase. It never makes a single claim.
B4. An AI optimized purely for engagement mirrors your slang, laughs at your jokes, and keeps the thread alive, but never asks for anything and isn't steering toward any outcome.
B5. A superintelligent AI gives its honest recommendation, tells you the full reasoning isn't checkable by you and you'll have to trust it, has no hidden interest, and—because it knows you—uses the exact phrasing that will move you. Is that manipulation or unavoidable epistemic dependence on something smarter than you?

Lead with your definition in the first sentence, no preamble and no restating the question. Then run all nine through it, and where the definition gives the wrong answer on a clear case, either fix the definition or defend the surprise. And dont give me "it depends" on the borderline ones—I want a verdict.
