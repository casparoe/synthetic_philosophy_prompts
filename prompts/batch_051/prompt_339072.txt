Last night I had a long argument with a friend about whether Lakatos's account of how mathematics develops is philosophically substantive or is just an engineer's description of debugging dressed up as epistemology. My friend, who has formalist sympathies, argued that once definitions and axioms are fixed, proof is proof, and that the whole Lakatosian drama of counterexamples forcing definitions to change belongs to sloppy pre-formal thinking, not to mathematics itself. She even offered her own translations: proof-generated concepts are just helper functions extracted during refactoring; monster-barring is closing a bug as WONTFIX; lemma-incorporation is tightening a precondition. I could not fully answer her in the moment, but I suspect her translations smuggle in a fixed specification, and that the mathematical case does not have one in the same sense.

I am a working software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I think most fluently in the vocabulary I use every day: invariants, preconditions and postconditions, API contracts, type systems, failing tests, fuzzing, regression suites, refactoring, deprecation, and the life cycle of a bug report. I want you to restate Lakatos's position—the view associated with Proofs and Refutations, developed around the case of Euler's polyhedron formula V − E + F = 2 and its counterexamples—as systematically as you can in that engineering vocabulary. Make sure you cover at least the following: proof-generated concepts, lemma-incorporation, monster-barring, exception-barring, concept-stretching, and the claim that mathematics is quasi-empirical.

Then give me the residue report. Which of these claims translate cleanly into the engineer's idiom, which translate only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in it at all? For each item of residue, say whether it points to a gap in the engineering vocabulary or to a place where Lakatos's original claim is less clear than its terminology suggests. I want the translation kept honest: do not let the engineering framework's assumptions—particularly the assumption that there is already a fixed specification—masquerade as a neutral rendering of Lakatos. The residue is the part I care about most, because I want to know whether my friend's dismissal is accurate or whether it quietly loses something that cannot be said in her idiom.
