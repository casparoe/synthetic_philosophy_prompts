In the debate over solipsism and the intuition that other people are NPCs, one of the oldest and strongest replies is that language is the giveaway: human speech is novel, self-correcting, and answerable to reasons in a way that seems impossible for a thing with no perspective on the world. I want to know whether large language models have broken this reply, or whether it can be rebuilt. An LLM produces fluent, context-sensitive, seemingly thoughtful prose, and it does so without the kind of unified subject, beliefs, or intentions the reply took to be required; we know, at least in outline, that it is trained to predict text rather than to report an inner life. Yet it is also tempting to say the case is unchanged, because an LLM's speech is a statistical echo of billions of human minds, so the mindedness is still somewhere upstream of the words.

My question is a precise one: does the argument that only a minded being could produce this kind of language survive the existence of LLMs? If it does not, identify the exact premise that fails, and say whether the damage is confined to language or extends to the general inference from human-like behavior to other minds. If it does survive, give the strongest repaired version and state what empirical fact about a speaker it must now add in order to reach the conclusion.
