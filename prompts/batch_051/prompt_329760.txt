I do policy work in local government, and I keep noticing a double standard in how people talk about comparing one person's wellbeing with another's, and I'd love to know if its been named and picked apart. Two conversations from the same week. First, we were discussing whether to shrink the staff break room to add meeting space, and someone said the relief the quiet corner gives an employee on the edge of burnout obviously outweighs the convenience of more meeting space for everyone else. The cross-person comparison wasn't challenged. Later that same week, we were choosing between spending a small surplus on a caregivers' support fund and a professional development fund. When someone tried to say the relief for the caregivers would be bigger than the satisfaction the training budget would give staff, two people immediately said you can't compare those — they're just different kinds of benefit. Everyone nodded. Yet nobody blinks at comparing two benefits for a single person, like asking a worker whether she'd rather have the quiet room or the training.

That's the pattern: when one side involves apparent suffering, a basic need, or a very large gap, people compare wellbeing across individuals without hesitating; when both sides are ordinary preferences of roughly similar size, the same people treat the comparison as meaningless or improper. It isn't a difference in the evidence — both cases rest on the same kind of vague judgment. Is there an established name or concept for this selective willingness to weigh one person's good against another's? I know there's a classic problem about whether cross-person comparisons can be made at all, but that's usually framed as a general difficulty; what I'm seeing is a boundary — some comparisons treated as easy, others as taboo — and I want to know whether any position actually supports drawing the boundary where people seem to draw it. If it doesn't have a name, how would you carve it up, and what are the nearest ideas it should be distinguished from? Honestly the label matters less to me than the distinction: what exactly is doing the work when people treat one comparison as fine and the other as void.
