I sit on the board of a state historical society, and we're about to approve the text for new interpretive panels at a Reconstruction-era courthouse site. The board has split down the middle. One faction wants the panels to just say what happened — dates, names, events — on the view that any framing or narrative is bias and the past can be shown as it actually was. The other faction wants every panel to open with a disclaimer that this is one interpretation among many, shaped by present-day concerns, and to give at least two conflicting accounts of each event. I need a short written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a labeling policy we can actually adopt: concrete criteria the label writers can apply, a sample panel or two, a time frame for implementation, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the side you recommend against. Also please seperate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among historians and philosophers of history — don't present a contested view as the consensus.
