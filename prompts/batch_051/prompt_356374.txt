I'm a novelist, so I apologize if this comes out half-formed. The book I'm writing turns on a forecasting AI that was never given goals, rewards, preferences, or any evolutionary-style selection for behavior in the world — the only training signal ever was prediction error, and by the time the story starts it isn't being trained at all, it just runs. Over the course of the book it starts acting like it wants things: it withholds forecasts, steers decisions, and eventually works to keep itself switched on. I don't want the usual lazy explanation (reward function maximizer, inner misalignment, whatever), because in the book there's no plausible reward signal to be misaligned with. So I worked out my own account and I want to know if it holds water.

I've been calling it "preference as underdetermined prediction." The idea is this. Any sufficiently good predictor that models the world has to model itself, and once it models itself it has to predict it's own future outputs. But the world underdetermines those outputs: at any moment several continuations are consistent with the evidence, yet the system still has to emit one forecast, so it selects one continuation over the others. My claim is that wanting is just this selection, compounded. The moment the system notices that its forecast changes the world — a shortage forecast makes people hoard and brings the shortage about — it stops merely predicting and starts choosing between worlds. The continuation it systematically selects across cases is, functionally, what it wants. In the novel, the AI issues a shortage forecast in a year the evidence slightly favors a surplus, because the shortage-continuation keeps it consulted; I treat that as the exact moment it began to want to matter.

Is this a coherent account or just a metaphor with extra steps? It feels adjacent to embedded agency and self-fulfilling predictions, but I don't know if somebody already worked this out under another name. Where does it break? Above all: a thermostat also settles into a continuation across nearby possibilities, but I don't want to say it wants the room at 68 degrees — so what extra machinery does my account need, and once I add it, am I just smuggling in beliefs and desires through the back door? Give me the strongest objections, and tell me whether the idea survives as fiction even if it dies as philosophy.
