He put it like this:

"Sen basically proved that famines are not natural disasters; they're a consequence of how a society distributes access to food. Real mass starvation doesn't happen because there just isn't enough food around; it happens when some group's entitlements collapse, that is, their ability to command food through markets, wages, or transfers. That's why in the Bengal famine of 1943 plenty of rice existed while millions died, and why the Irish famine went on while food was being shipped to England. The Great Leap Forward is the same story: the peasants died because the government had no elections and no free press to answer to, not because of any harvest problem. His big conclusion is that no famine has ever occurred in a functioning democracy, so strengthening democratic accountability is basically all you need to make famines impossible."
