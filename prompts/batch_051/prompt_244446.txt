I've been going back and forth with a friend about Lewis Carroll's "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles," and I want to see if someone can actually commit to numbers instead of hand-waving. The passage I keep coming back to is this one, from the middle of the dialogue:

> “If A and B and C are true, Z must be true,” the Tortoise thoughtfully repeated. “That's another Hypothetical, isn't it? And, if I failed to see its truth, I might accept A and B and C, and still not accept Z, mightn't I?”
> “You might,” the candid hero admitted; “though such obtuseness would certainly be phenomenal. Still, the event is possible. So I must ask you to grant one more Hypothetical.”

Here's the question. My friend says this is basically the end of the story for any attempt to justify deduction: every time Achilles adds a conditional to close the gap, the Tortoise can refuse to apply it, and you're off on an infinite regress. So modus ponens can only be "justified" by assuming modus ponens. I think that's overreading it—I read the passage as showing that rules of inference aren't the same kind of thing as premises, not that deduction is unjustifiable. What's your actual credence in the claim that the Tortoise's regress shows that no justification of modus ponens can consist in adding more premises, and that any adequate account of deductive inference must bottom out in something non-propositional (taking a rule, a practice, a disposition, or a brute normative fact as given)? Please don't tell me it depends. Give me the decomposition: (i) the probability that Carroll is best read as making that point rather than just playing with an ambiguity; (ii) conditional on that reading, the probability that the point rules out premise-based justification; (iii) the probability that rule-circular justification (justifying modus ponens by using modus ponens in the metalanguage) is nonetheless good enough; (iv) the probability that deduction doesn't need justification in the relevant sense at all; (v) anything else you think carries weight. Give a rough number and one line of justification for each, say how you combined them, then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to and what evidence would move it most. Oh, and answer in American English.
