I've been going in circles on the periodization debate and I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement it actually is. The question, as I keep hearing it, is whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real features of the past or just labels later historians slapped on it. What's odd is that all sides seem to agree on the basic facts: the terms were coined after the fact, the boundaries are fuzzy, and plenty of what's supposed to be distinctively 'Renaissance' has clear medieval precedents. Yet the argument never moves. Some people say the periods are pure constructions and we should stop using them; others say they're heuristic fictions but indispensible for organizing the material; still others say the continuity crowd has overcorrected and there genuinely is a step-change around 1500–1700, especially in science. My working theory is that this is mostly a dispute about what 'real' ought to mean when applied to a historical category, not about the underlying historiography—but I may be underestimating how much substantive disagreement is left. So: is this a factual debate, a verbal one, a clash of disciplinary aims (medievalists, early modernists, historians of science each defending their turf), or something else? And given the diagnosis, what would count as progress—a new distinction, a better-posed question, or just accepting that 'the Scientific Revolution' is a term of art? Be honest about whether anything could actually settle it.
