I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I have spent the past few months reporting a long piece on how people make wrenching life choices — whether to take a job in another country, whether to have a child, whether to leave a long marriage that is not exactly unhappy. In the course of that reporting I have become persuaded by a position I first met in the philosophical literature on practical reason: the view, associated with Ruth Chang, that hard choices are not cases in which the options are incommensurable, and not cases in which they are exactly equal in value. They are cases in which the options stand in a fourth relation, parity: the options are comparable, yet neither is better than the other and they are not equally good. On this view, the familiar trichotomy of better than, worse than, and equally good does not exhaust the possible value relations, and many decisions that feel agonising owe their difficulty precisely to parity.

Before I build a feature around this idea, I want to stress-test it. Treat me as a sparring partner and tell me what would count against the parity account of hard choices. I do not want a summary of the view or a reading list; I want a systematic map of its exposure. Which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or empirical findings would have to obtain for the account to be false — or for what Chang calls parity to be better explained by something else, such as ignorance of the true ranking, vagueness in the relevant values, or some artefact of the way the alternatives are described? Distinguish defeaters that would be fatal from those that would merely be awkward. Say which objections a committed parity theorist would themselves concede count against the position, and which they would dismiss as resting on a misunderstanding. Conclude with a verdict on whether the view is well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a defect. For my own use, I would find most persuasive any objection showing that parity does no real explanatory work in accounting for how people finally choose. Please write your answer in British English.
