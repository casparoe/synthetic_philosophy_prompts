I'm an undergraduate taking a course on the political economy of the welfare state, and I'd like to use the course framework to think through a counterfactual rather than get a standard pros-and-cons survey. Suppose a small, open, high-trust economy—take Denmark as a rough template for demography, trade openness, and welfare-state generosity, but treat the stipulations below as authoritative—enacts the following package in 2030. It abolishes unemployment insurance, means-tested social assistance, housing benefit, and family cash benefits, replacing all of them with a universal basic income paid monthly to every legal adult resident, set at 55% of median equivalized household disposable income (income adjusted for household size), with half that amount for each child. The payment is unconditional, non-taxable, and never withdrawn as earnings rise. To finance it, the country replaces its progressive income-tax schedule with a single 33% marginal rate above a personal allowance calibrated so that a full-time worker at the median wage sees roughly no change in net tax; it also folds all reduced and zero VAT rates into a uniform 25% rate. At the same time, it abolishes the statutory minimum wage, so the basic income, not a legal floor, becomes the worker's outside option. Assume the country remains open to trade and capital flows but closes its borders to new low-wage labor migration for the first ten years.

Work through what you would expect to happen over that first decade to: labor-force participation at the bottom of the income distribution; the wage floor and bargaining power in low-wage service sectors; prices of labor-intensive personal services; the rental housing market; and the political sustainability of the reform. I want a chain of mechanisms and incentives, not a narrative, and I want your confidence made explicit link by link: for each effect, say whether it is robust or a guess, and state what economic or political fact would have to hold for that link to fail. Close by telling me which single finding or argument would most change your overall prediction, and where your reasoning is least sure. Keep the entire answer to no more than 1,000 words.
