I have been noticing a kind of choice that seems to me importantly different from an ordinary difficult decision, and I would like to know whether there is an established name for it — and, more importantly, a precise account of where it begins and ends. Consider two instances. In my own life: I was choosing between a career that is morally meaningful but modestly paid and one that is well paid but, by my lights, ethically hollow. In a committee on which I sit: we had to decide between preserving a wetland of considerable ecological value and expanding a children's hospital. In neither case was the difficulty a matter of missing information, and in neither case was I, or the committee, unable to choose. But it also seemed wrong to say that one option was better than the other, and equally wrong to say the two were exactly tied — because, if they had been tied, then improving one of them in some respect that clearly matters should have made it the better option, and it did not. A modest pay raise in the hollow job did not make it better than the meaningful job; a small additional grant to the hospital did not settle the wetland question. Yet the eventual choice did not feel arbitrary in the way settling a tie by flipping a coin would, and I was not indifferent between the options: I cared a great deal which one I ended up with, and after choosing I could explain the choice without pointing to anything I had already discovered before making it.

I have seen the words "incommensurable" and "incomparable" used for situations like this, but I cannot tell whether they name the same thing, whether either names exactly what I am describing, or whether they pick out something broader — some general failure of a common scale — rather than this specific configuration in which the options are not equal, not ranked, and yet not beyond reasoned choice. Is there an established concept for this phenomenon, as opposed to "a hard choice" in general? If there is, what distinguishes it from incommensurability, from incomparability, and from a mere tie? And if the concept has no settled name, I would still like a definition sharp enough that I can look at a new pair of options and say whether it has this feature, together with an account of what would settle the matter in contested cases.
