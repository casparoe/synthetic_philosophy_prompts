I cover science and ideas for a general-audience publication, and I am preparing a piece on whether philosophy progresses in any sense comparable to the sciences. In the debate one finds at least five distinguishable claims about the shape that philosophical progress takes, if it exists: (a) the permanent elimination of once-live positions, as when straightforward verificationism about meaning ceases to be a default option; (b) the accumulation of sharper distinctions that later work simply presupposes, such as the sense/reference or use/mention distinctions; (c) the resolution of local puzzles, where a problem generates a focused literature that converges on constraints any adequate answer must satisfy even if no single answer wins; (d) the formulation of new, more tractable questions that reframe an area; and (e) the export of tools and results to other disciplines. Please sort these five candidates into tiers by how much weight each should carry in an honest answer to the charge that philosophy never settles anything. Give one sentence for each candidate explaining its tier placement.
