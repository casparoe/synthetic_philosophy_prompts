I cover science and ideas for a living, which mostly means translating jargon into English, and over the years I've developed a little theory I've been calling 'etymological embalming.' The claim is that fields coin new terms from Latin and Greek not mainly for precision but because those languages are dead. A living word drifts because millions of speakers keep using it for new things — 'computer' went from being a person to being a machine — but a term built from dead roots has no everyday community of speakers to tug it around, so it arrives with its meaning already fixed, like a definition written in a language no one speaks. Photosynthesis still does more or less what its roots say. The cost is that the term is unreadable unless you already know the roots, and I've started to suspect that's secretly the point too: it doubles as a guild membership test. Tell me what this theory gets right, give me the three strongest objections, say whether I've just reinvented something linguists already have a better name for, and where the story breaks most badly.
