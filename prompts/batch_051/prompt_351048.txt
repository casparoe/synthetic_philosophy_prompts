You are an expert political philosopher and designer of public institutions, and I would like you to reason carefully about a proposal rather than pass a moral verdict on it. I was arguing with a friend about whether autarky is a coherent ideal for a modern society, and the discussion produced a concrete scheme that I have not been able to think through all the way. Treat the scheme as a mechanism: I am not asking whether self-sufficiency is virtuous, but how this rule would actually behave once real actors adjust to it.

Imagine a wealthy, trade-dependent democracy amends its constitution to require national self-sufficiency in three sectors only: staple-food calories, baseload electricity, and essential medicines. The amendment sets a "resilience floor": within five years, each sector must be able to meet at least 70 percent of domestic demand from domestic sources within 90 days of a total import stoppage, rising to 85 percent within fifteen years. An independent Resilience Board measures compliance annually; if a sector misses its floor, automatic import tariffs and production subsidies are triggered, and if a sector exceeds its floor, producers receive transferable tax credits. The counting rule is deliberately strict: a product counts as domestic only if its production could continue for 24 months without imported inputs, so wheat grown with imported fertilizer, pills pressed from imported active ingredients, and turbines built from imported semiconductors do not count.

Please start by brainstorming the obvious first-order effects, then work toward the deeper dynamics. Walk me through what this does to agricultural, energy, and pharmaceutical firms; how they would reorganize supply chains, relabel products, or game the definitions of "domestic" and "could continue"; how trade partners would adapt or retaliate; how consumers and taxpayers would bear the costs; and what second-order political effects the Board's annual measurements and automatic penalties would create—regulatory capture, creative accounting, lobbying to redefine thresholds, and so on. Conclude by saying where the scheme gets gamed first, what the likely long-run equilibrium looks like, and whether the failure modes can be fixed without gutting the autarky ideal the amendment was supposed to serve.
