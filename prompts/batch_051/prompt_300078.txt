I'm a build engineer by trade — most of my working life is dependency graphs, lockfiles, circular imports, and trying to figure out why the resolver is taking twelve minutes to decide that package A and package B are fundamentally incompatible. Lately I've been reading some epistemology for fun (I know, I know), and the justification regress debate keeps triggering my pattern-matching. The naive version of what I think I'm hearing is: foundationalism is basically vendored, dependency-free base packages that everything else uses and that you just trust; coherentism is a graph where packages justify each other through mutual dependency, and the only real criterion is that the whole thing hangs together and the build completes; infinitism is the position that a dependency chain doesn't have to bottom out anywhere — it can just keep resolving forever, and that's a feature rather than a hang.

I have a feeling that mapping is to cute and probably smuggling in a bunch of assumptions. So what I'd like is a proper translation. Restate the regress problem and the three main responses to it (foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism) in the vocabulary I actually think in: dependency graphs, cycles, base images, self-contained packages, resolution algorithms, termination guarantees, "it compiles" as a success condition, and so on. Go position by position. For each one, say what translates cleanly, what only fits if you distort it a little, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Don't force it — if something doesn't fit, I'd rather you say so and explain why than pretend my mental model is more powerful than it is.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. Is the stuff that didn't survive translation evidence that my framework is missing something real, or evidence that the philosophical positions are less crisp than they look? In particular, I keep bumping on one thing: a successful build tells me the constraints are satisfiable, not that the package is correct. Is there anything in the epistemology debate that plays the role of 'correct' rather than 'just satisfiable'? And do the three positions answer that question differently, or do they all quietly dodge it in the same way?
