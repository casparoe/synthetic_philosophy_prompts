I have been reading about the effort to make AI systems legible — trained not only to be correct but to be understandable to the humans who must rely on them — and I keep half-recognizing a distinction from my own craft. On the AI side, explanations of a model's answer are assessed by two standards. An explanation is plausible if it satisfies a human reader: it says, in effect, that the answer follows from considerations that would justify it. An explanation is faithful if it tracks the computation that actually produced the answer. The dangerous object is the plausible but unfaithful explanation: the model gives the right output for an inscrutable reason and a clear rationale for the wrong reason.

As a novelist, I work with what looks to me like the same pair of standards. A first-person narrator can offer an account of her motives that is wholly plausible to the reader yet false as an account of the causes that, within the fiction, actually produced her action — the narrator rationalizes. In free indirect style, the third-person narration can sit close enough to a character's self-understanding that the reader cannot always say where the true cause ends and the character's construction of it begins. The novelist's version of a plausible-but-unfaithful explanation is a motive that explains the action beautifully but did not move the character.

I cannot tell whether this is a real identity of structure or merely the same word, "explanation," stretched over two unlike things. If the structure is real, I would expect knowledge to transfer in one or both directions: perhaps the taxonomy of unreliable narrators — the one who withholds against the one who confabulates — has something to say about legible models, or perhaps the engineering distinction between plausibility and faithfulness gives fiction a sharper way to talk about why a motive can satisfy readers without being true of the character. But the parallel may fail where it matters: a novel can leave the gap between surface motive and underlying cause permanently unresolved, and use that gap as a source of meaning, while an AI system is supposed eventually to close it. Which is it? Please state the mapping precisely, say what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer, and name at least one thing the parallel predicts that turns out false.
