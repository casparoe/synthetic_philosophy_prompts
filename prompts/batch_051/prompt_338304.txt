An upper-level undergraduate seminar on social norms and cooperation (18 students, one 75-minute session) needs a structured simulation through which participants feel, before they hear any labels, what tight and loose cultures each buy and cost. "Tight" here means strong, widely shared norms with low tolerance of deviation; "loose" means weaker or fewer norms with high tolerance of deviation. Divide the seminar into small communities working at a shared scored task. The simulation should run in two phases: first a stable environment in which writing many explicit rules and enforcing them strictly pays off, then a changed environment in which the strongly rule-bound communities run into the cost of exactly what had been working, while the less rule-bound communities face different failures of coordination or free-riding. Include roles (at minimum a rule-maker, an enforcer, and a member with a right of appeal), materials, a point system that needs no real money, and a timeline. The debrief should use particular moments from the simulation to distinguish having few or flexible norms from being truly tolerant of norm violations, and should connect both phases to the question of how much rule-following a society should demand. Also say what will predictably go wrong under real classroom conditions and how to handle it, and state plainly which feature of genuine cultural tightness and looseness the simulation cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Write the answer in American English.
