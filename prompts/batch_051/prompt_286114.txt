Write a dialogue between two equally sophisticated philosophers who disagree about whether the self is illuminated or misled by the idea, associated with Daniel Dennett, that a self is a center of narrative gravity.

One character should defend a version of the narrativist view: the self is not a mental substance, not a thing in the brain, and not an irreducible owner of experiences, but a useful abstraction constructed and continuously revised through the stories we tell about our lives and the stories others tell about us. On this view, asking where the self really is, or what it is made of, is a category mistake, roughly like asking where the center of gravity is located or what it weighs. The self earns its keep by making a person's behavior intelligible, predictable, and answerable.

The other character should defend the view that this analogy deflates the self past the point of doing its explanatory work. A center of gravity does not tell stories, act, suffer, deliberate, or take responsibility. If the self is merely an abstract object posited to make sense of a life, then something must already be there — a subject with a first-person point of view — to do the positing, to be the audience for the narrative, and to be answerable for it. Calling the self a useful fiction, on this view, either quietly presupposes the very thing it denies or ends up explaining only third-person behavior while leaving out the first-person reality.

The two should engage each other's strongest formulations rather than trade slogans. The realist should concede the genuine pull of the narrativist's parsimony and the evidence that self-narratives are confabulated, selective, and easily revised. The narrativist should concede that narrative activity seems to presuppose something that is doing the narrating and that this something is not itself just another story. They should respond to each other's actual arguments, not talk past one another, and the dialogue should gradually expose what the disagreement is really about: whether the self is an entity that explains, or an explanatory posit whose existence consists in its usefulness. End with each character naming the one point from the other side that most unsettles their own position.
