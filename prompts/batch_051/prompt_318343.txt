Lately I have been trying to think through what I actually believe about agreements people could make now, before artificial general intelligence exists, that are meant to still matter once it does exist. I have worked out the following views on my own, and I want an audit of whether they fit together rather than an introduction to the topic.

(1) A pre-AGI agreement is only worth entering if its terms are written precisely enough that, afterward, a reasonably informed outsider could in principle tell whether each party kept them.

(2) Such an agreement only genuinely binds someone if they have a reason to keep it that would still move them after they have gained overwhelming power through AGI.

(3) I would be willing to accept a clause saying that if I turn out to be the one who builds AGI, I must share control of it, or a substantial share of its benefits, with the other parties on the same terms I would demand from them; I regard accepting such a clause as a matter of fairness.

(4) But a clause whose only enforcement mechanism is the winner's own conscience is really just a gift offered under the illusion of a contract; a real agreement needs some feature that makes breaking it impractical or impossible, not merely unseemly.

(5) I think a pre-AGI consortium whose entire content is that the benefits of AGI will be shared, but which leaves each member free to develop AGI on their own, is essentially worthless: whichever member succeeds first can rewrite or abandon the sharing arrangement before it constrains them.

(6) On the other hand, I do think an agreement can genuinely bind if its terms are built into the goals of the AGI itself, or into infrastructure that no party could later alter on their own, even if afterward no one would comply out of goodwill.

I cannot tell whether all six of these sit comfortably together, and if they do not, I honestly do not know which part is the weak point. Please go through the set and identify any real tensions, distinguish them from ones that only look like tensions, order the real tensions by how serious they are, and say what would have to be given up or changed in each case. Please reply in continuous prose, as you would in a letter, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
