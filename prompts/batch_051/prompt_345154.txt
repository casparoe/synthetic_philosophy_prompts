Act as a professional philosopher who specializes in the logic of legal systems and self-referential rules. You are designing a take-home essay for my high-school philosophy club, which has been studying Peter Suber's game Nomic: a game in which changing the rules is itself a move, and the rule that says how to change the rules is one of the rules and can itself be changed. Think carefully and begin by brainstorming the exact crux of the difficulty before you write the final version.

Please produce three things: (1) one polished essay question, (2) a model answer of about 700 words, and (3) a marking scheme worth 20 marks that assigns marks to specific moves and includes a one-line note on how a weaker candidate typically gets each move wrong. The question must be answerable entirely from material supplied in it, so do not ask for or mention any bibliography, reading list, or citation.

Build the question around this micro-Nomic, which should be reproduced inside the question:

- Rule A: A proposal to amend or repeal any rule, including Rule A, takes effect if and only if it receives a two-thirds vote.
- Rule B: A rule labelled "entrenched" may be amended or repealed only by a unanimous vote, and the label counts as part of the rule.

Proposal P amends Rule A by adding the label "entrenched." P receives a two-thirds vote and no more.

The essay question should force students to work through, not merely name, the paradox of self-amendment. The crux they must confront is this: when an amendment changes its own adoption threshold, do the old rules or the new rules supply the test of its validity, and why is this micro-Nomic silent on that question? A B answer will accurately restate the rules and notice that something strange has happened; an A answer will construct both readings—under the pre-P rules P passes, while under the post-P rules P lacked the unanimity Rule B requires—show how P's validity flips between them, and say precisely what further rule or convention would be needed to settle the case, and explain why adding it may only push the problem one level up.
