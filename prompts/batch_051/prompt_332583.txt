I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so excuse the fuzzy terms. I'm sketching a book set about 500 years out. The engine of the book is that the future civilization has values that look perfectly decent to them but are, by our lights, seriously bad — not from any one catastrophe, but from slow, generational drift. My own gut says the probability of that, absent deliberate intervention to steer or lock values in, is maybe 0.4. What's your number? And don't just give me a number — break the thing down: how stable are core human values over centuries really; how much can we even judge distant values from here without it collapsing into cheap relativism or cheap chauvinism; is drift on average neutral, good, or bad; do instutions brake drift or accelerate it; whatever else you think matters. Assign rough probabilities to the pieces, show how they combine into your final credence, and tell me which piece the number is most sensitive to — i.e., what new evidence or argument would move it the most.
