I keep running into this passage from Zhuangzi — the one where a man dreams he is a butterfly and then wakes up unsure whether he was a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is a man. It used to just feel like a pretty image, but lately people have been using it in ways that feel completely different from each other. A friend used it to shut down an argument about whether we can know the external world, someone on a forum said it means you shouldn't take everyday life too seriously since it might all be a dream, and a coworker brought it up at lunch as if it just meant people change.

Could you lay out the distinct precise claims that little dream story could be making — from the flatly trivial to the genuinely radical — and for each one say whether it's plausible, false, or too vague to even be false? Then tell me which reading, if any, actually supports each of those three uses. Especially my friend's move: he said that because we can't rule out that we're dreaming, all our ordinary beliefs are just guesses. I'm half convinced the original passage wasn't even trying to lead to global skepticism, and might have been poking fun at people who take their waking lives, or their identities, too fixedly. Is that a defensible reading, and does it leave my friend with anything?
