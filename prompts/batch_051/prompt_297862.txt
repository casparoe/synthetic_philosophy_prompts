I cover science and ideas — mostly how evidence works in medicine — and I'm the token lay skeptic on a regional health system's coverage committee. Next week we vote whether to extend coverage for a new blood-based colon-cancer screening test to 45-to-50-year-olds. The main study is helpful but not crisp: sensitivity looks decent, the false-positive estimate is all over the place, and follow-up is patchy. Our chair keeps saying the result came in positive so now we know the test works, lets update and move on. I keep wanting to say: you can't just condition on a smeared-out finding as if it were a fact we learned for sure — the result itself is uncertain, and all we get is a shifted spread of confidence over the parts of the claim. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote, 700 words max, with these sections in this order: The question (60 words), Strongest case for covering now (140 words), Where that case goes wrong (180 words), How to update on a result you can't crisp up (200 words), Recommendation with conditions (120 words). The bottom line has to be something we can actually vote on — extend, reject, or restrict — and I want the chair's position given its strongest version before you break it.
