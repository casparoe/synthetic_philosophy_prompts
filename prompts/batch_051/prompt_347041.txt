I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so maybe this is naive, but I'm stuck. My current character is a committed hedonist — she keeps a ledger of experiences and tries to sort them into real pleasures and everything else, since she wants to spend her life on the first column. The trouble is I can't even say wether she'd be right to sort these as pleasures: the taste of a perfect peach, a runner's high, the relief when a biopsy comes back negative, the chosen pain of a deep-tissue massage or a tattoo, glee at a rival's embarrassment, being in flow while writing and losing track of yourself entirely, and the quiet satisfaction of rereading a sentence you wrote and knowing its good. I have instincts — the peach is obviously in; the relief feels like the removal of fear, not something positive; schadenfreude feels gleeful but somehow not a pleasure because it's aimed at someone else — but I don't have a definition that makes those calls principled. Can you give me a working definition of pleasure that gets these cases right, run it through all of them, and tell me where it surprises you or where you'd have to bite a bullet?
