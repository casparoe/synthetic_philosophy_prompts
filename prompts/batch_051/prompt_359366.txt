For a political theory reading group, I am writing up my current commitments about the French Revolution to see whether they hold together. The hinge is a specific passage in Burke's Reflections on the Revolution in France, so I will paraphrase it: early in the book, while discussing the composition of the new National Assembly, Burke argues that the Revolution has done something worse than misgovern; it has transferred authority to men whose claim to rule rests on representing the abstract numerical will of the population rather than on any stake in the corporate orders and inherited institutions of France. He treats this not as a side effect of the Revolution but as its central and original error.

My commitments are these:

1. The Terror is best explained by the internal logic of Jacobin ideology, especially the demand for a unitary general will, rather than primarily by war, foreign intervention, or economic crisis.
2. A class-struggle analysis of 1789 is insufficient because the French bourgeoisie and nobility overlapped economically and culturally; the decisive conflicts were over political representation and ideology.
3. Burke's diagnosis in that passage is essentially correct about the Revolution's original mistake: legitimate representation cannot be built on abstract individuals alone, but must incorporate the interests of durable social bodies. Yet Burke is wrong that the Old Regime in 1789 offered a viable path of gradual reform.
4. Revolutionary founding is legitimate only when it restores an older constitution that a ruler has violated, as I take the English Revolution of 1688 to have done, and not when it creates a new order from principles of natural right.
5. The saying that revolutions devour their children is not a real historical law; it is a retrospective trope that masks how contingent the fates of particular revolutionary leaders were.

Are these jointly tenable? I especially want to know whether (5) and (1) conflict, whether (4), if applied consistently, would force me to condemn the American Revolution as well, and whether (3) smuggles back an estate-based or class-based theory of representation that sits badly with (2). Rank the tensions by severity and say which single revision would most improve the set.
