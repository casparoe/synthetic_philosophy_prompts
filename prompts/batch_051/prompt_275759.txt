I sit on the ethics committee at a mid-size hospital with an active fertility service. Our medical director is asking us to weigh in on a proposal to require anyone seeking IVF or IUI to first complete a two-session parenting-readiness course and a structured interview, not as a pass/fail gate but so the team can recommend—and in extreme cases, with a second clinician's sign-off, delay—treatment when their are serious concerns like active untreated addiction or a recent history of violence in the home. Half the committee sees this as protecting the future child's right to an open future; the other half sees it as a licensing-parents scheme by another name, one that will predictably land hardest on poor and minority patients. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action, approve/reject/approve with conditions, and take seriously the strongest point on the side you'd vote against.
