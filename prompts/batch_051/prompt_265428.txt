In the grammar debates I keep encountering, mostly online but sometimes in my school's philosophy club, someone will quote the line that a language is a dialect with an army and a navy, and everyone else treats the matter as settled from there, especially the question of whether there is any such thing as bad grammar. I can tell the sentence is being asked to do several distinct jobs at once, but I cannot tell which of them it actually accomplishes. Take that one-liner apart into the distinct precise claims it could be making, for instance about the linguistic systems themselves, about how a standard variety gets selected, about how prestige attaches to some forms rather than others, and anything else you find; say for each whether it is trivially true, defensible but contestable, or false. Then identify which of those claims would actually license the conclusion the person is reaching, namely that there is no such thing as bad grammar, and which readings leave that conclusion unsupported. I would like the readings set out as a menu with verdicts, rather than a single interpretation defended.
