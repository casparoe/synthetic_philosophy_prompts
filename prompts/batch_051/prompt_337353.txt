I am currently taking a course on the philosophy of photography and film, and we have been working through the debate about whether photographs are transparent—whether looking at a photograph is a way of literally seeing the object photographed, because the image is produced mechanically by the scene it records rather than being a depiction assembled by an artist's hand and eye. I keep wanting to test this idea against a film, and I have settled on Alfred Hitchcock's Rear Window. The film centers on a photojournalist who is confined to a wheelchair and observes his neighbors across a courtyard, first through a window and then through a telephoto lens, and who becomes convinced that one of them has murdered his wife. It seems deliberately pointed at the transparency issue: the protagonist's camera gives him a causal, mechanical view of a real situation across the courtyard, yet everything he sees comes to him framed, partial, and mute, and the people around him suspect he is less witnessing a murder than assembling one from visual scraps.

Could you give me a careful reading of Rear Window through the transparency debate? I would like the reading to work in both directions: identify the moments or devices that seem to support the transparency thesis (the lens as an extension of vision, the mechanically recorded courtyard, the way the film treats the camera as giving genuine access to an actual event) and the moments or devices that resist it (the composition of the shots, the fact that the crime itself is never directly seen, the eventual look back at Jeff from across the courtyard). I would also like you to be honest about where the film does not fit the theory, rather than forcing it into an illustration. Finally, say what the film might add to the philosophical debate that the arguments alone miss—perhaps something about the ethics of being seen, or about how seeing-through is entangled with imagination, desire, and narrative. I am less interested in whether Hitchcock got the theory right than in what the movie shows that the theory would need to account for.
