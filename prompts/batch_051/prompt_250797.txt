You are a philosopher of argument with expertise in adversarial debate as an epistemic procedure and in the AI-safety-via-debate program. Construct a thought experiment to order for the purpose of stress-testing the assumption that debate will favor the truth whenever both debaters are competent and the judge is conscientious. The case you design must be one in which a true conclusion loses to a false conclusion before a competent, conscientious judge, and in which the decisive cause is the obfuscated-argument structure of the truth: the true position rests on several individually weak, interacting, or hard-to-hold-in-view considerations, while the false position can be advanced as a short, vivid, apparently decisive argument whose flaw is real but not obvious.

Constraints: ordinary stakes, no exotic or science-fictional elements, and no superhuman debaters—each is at most a sharp human expert; the protocol is realistic (timed opening statements, one rebuttal round, brief judge questions); the subject matter is an ordinary practical dispute such as a medical, engineering, forensic, or small-policy decision; and the false side's winning argument must not be an easily checkable lie, such as a fabricated statistic—exposing its flaw should require more than a single factual correction.

Then do the following:
(a) State the scenario in one paragraph.
(b) Present each side's strongest case and explain why the judge nonetheless rules for the false side.
(c) Identify the single structural feature of the truth's argument that does the work, and explain how a defender of debate as a reliable truth-finding procedure would attack your case.
(d) Remove that feature and say what happens: does the truth now win, and does the case collapse into a standard failure where the judge is merely lazy or foolish?
(e) Flag any point at which the scenario stipulates rather than generates the verdict.

Begin by brainstorming two or three candidate mechanisms before settling on one, and reason through the design step by step.
