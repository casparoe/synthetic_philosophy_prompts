For a paper I'm writing I've pinned down a list of things I believe about deontological constraints, and I want a consistency audit before I show it to anyone. I've mostly taught these pieces separately, so its possible I haven't noticed where they fight each other. Here's what I'm committed to: (1) constraints are agent-relative and real: there are things I may not do, and the explanation isn't just that doing them would make the world worse all things considered; (2) the deep rationale for constraints is the seperateness of persons, the idea that persons have a kind of value, a claim on how they're treated, that can't be added up or traded off across individuals; (3) I won't push one man off a bridge to stop a trolley, and I won't harvest one healthy patient to save five, even when the arithmetic is the same; (4) but I will turn the trolley away from five toward one, knowing the one will be killed, as long as the death is a side-effect and not something I aim at; (5) numbers do matter in rescues: if I can save either one stranger or five, I should save the five; (6) I also think there's an agent-relative prerogative: I'm not required to give up the central projects of my life even if doing so would save several strangers. So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and how bad is it? Rank the real tensions by severity, say what would have to give in each case, and tell me which single revision would clean things up most for the least cost.
