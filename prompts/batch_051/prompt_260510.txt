Write a satire in the form of a self-help column, roughly 600–700 words, in the relentlessly upbeat voice of a lifestyle-magazine columnist. The column should teach readers how to attain, as a personal achievement, exactly the kind of community membership that communitarians say cannot be chosen: an identity formed by unchosen attachments, inherited obligations, and a self that precedes its choices. The humor must come from the real incompatibility between the borrowed communitarian vocabulary and the underlying liberal-individualist project, not from mere absurdity. The advice should therefore remain plausible enough that a real self-help column might publish it: for example, a step on finding a tradition that matches your pre-existing tastes, a step on selecting an unchosen identity from a curated list, a step on reframing feelings of obligation as evidence that you have successfully acquired a constitutive tie, and a closing exercise in which the reader visualizes the person they already were before choosing anything. The target is the tendency to treat belonging, tradition, and identity as voluntary projects and consumer choices, and the column should never acknowledge that contradiction in its own advice. A reader familiar with the communitarian critique of the choosing, unencumbered self should find the satire fair rather than cartoonish, and a reader unfamiliar with the debate should still find it funny and readable. If this brief misrepresents the communitarian position or turns it into a strawman, say so briefly and correct the satire before writing it.
