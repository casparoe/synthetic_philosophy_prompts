I have been working with Pearl's causal hierarchy, and I would like a candid assessment of a distinction I find myself relying on. I call it intervention depth.

By a shallow intervention I mean the standard do-operation: an exogenous assignment that sets a variable X to a value x while every other structural equation is left untouched. By a deep intervention I mean a targeted alteration of one or more structural equations rather than of a variable: for example, a drug that neither fixes blood pressure nor fixes exercise but changes how exercise affects blood pressure. In the simplest linear case, where the old mechanism is Y = αX + U, a shallow intervention assigns X a fixed value, while a deep intervention replaces the coefficient α itself. By an abyssal intervention I mean changing an entire subsystem's equations, as when an institution is reorganized so thoroughly that the earlier functional relationships no longer hold.

My working view is that the do-calculus and the usual adjustment criteria are sound only on a background assumption of modularity, and that this assumption silently limits them to shallow interventions. A deep or abyssal intervention is not a policy of the form "set X to x"; it is a policy of the form "change the mechanism that links X to Y." If I analyze it as if it were shallow, I compute the effect of the wrong act: I learn what would happen if I fixed the observable, not what would happen if I altered the system that produces it.

My proposed repair is to make depth explicit. Introduce a regime variable R whose children are the equations that change, write the modified equations with R among their arguments, and analyze the policy as do(R = r). This seems to capture many real interventions, but I suspect it may only relocate the problem, since deepness at one level is just shallowness at the level of R. Please assess this candidly. Is the distinction already standard under another name? Does the repair preserve what made deep interventions distinct, or does it merely relabel them? What exactly goes wrong, in terms of interventional and counterfactual outputs, when a deep or abyssal intervention is treated as a shallow one? And is there a principled version of the do-calculus for the case where the equations themselves are the target?
