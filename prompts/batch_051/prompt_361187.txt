Last night a friend and I argued for a long time about a career choice she is facing: whether to take a job as a public defender in the city where her family lives, or a better-paid corporate-law job in another country doing work she finds more intellectually interesting. She said the values at stake are incommensurable, because being near her family and doing work she finds meaningful cannot be put on a single scale and weighed against each other, but she denied that this made the two options incomparable. She said they might be neither better than the other, nor worse, nor exactly equal in value; she used the phrase "on a par" for this fourth relation and claimed that choices between on-a-par options are hard without being arbitrary. I objected that if one option is neither better than, worse than, nor equal to the other, that seems to exhaust the comparative possibilities, and any eventual choice would have to be made by something other than reasoning, such as a feeling or a coin flip. She replied that my mistake was assuming a rational choice requires finding a uniquely best option. We left it there. I still suspect her phrase "on a par" is either equality in disguise or a refusal to admit that the options simply cannot be compared, and I cannot see how her choice could be anything but an arbitrary preference dressed up in philosophical language. Please answer: is there a coherent fourth relation of parity, and does it actually show that choices between incommensurable values can be reason-governed rather than arbitrary?
