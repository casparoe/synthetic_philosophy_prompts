An account of mental content is naturalistic only if it explains how a brain state can be about something — have truth or accuracy conditions — using only non-intentional materials such as causal covariation, biological function, and selection history, without borrowing content from us. Before I read the candidates, I want the specification a theory of original (underived) content must meet. Write the desiderata: the cases it must get right, including systematic misrepresentation, determinate content where the same state seems equally well characterized by several contents, and contents whose subject matter could not have mattered to selection; the distinctions it must not collapse, including content versus vehicle, indicating P versus having the function to indicate P, and original versus derived content; and the empirical constraints from evolutionary biology about what functions are and how selection works. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it non-negotiable or the demand of a particular camp. Then flag any subsets of the requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, attending especially to (i) allowing misrepresentation while making content a matter of actual covariation and (ii) requiring determinate content while making content depend on selection history. End by saying which single requirement does the most to exclude the familiar covariation-based, consumer-based, and etiological accounts, and briefly say how each scores against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivation rather than on the scoring. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on this topic, and do not present a contested view as consensus.
