I teach a seminar on collective responsibility, and every year we stall out on the same question: whether a company can be blamed as a company, or whether 'the company was to blame' is just loose talk for a pile of individual failures plus legal machinery. I don't want another survey of positions. What I want is a way to test the distinct claim that blame can attach to something irreducibly collective, over and above the members. My hunch is the variable that matters is whether the firm has a decision procedure that survives changes in personnel, so the blame sticks to something with a kind of persistence of its own; but I cant see how to turn that hunch into an experiment or observational design that would actually separate the two readings. Give me a concrete test: what gets manipulated or measured, what pattern of results would favor the irreducibly-collective reading and what would favor the reductionist reading, and then be straight about what even a clean result wouldnt settle—does it show companies really are blameworthy agents, or only that people are willing to talk about them that way? Format it as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
