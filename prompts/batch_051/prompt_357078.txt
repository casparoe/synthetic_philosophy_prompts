I'm a civil procedure PhD student and I keep going back and forth on mandatory arbitration. Write a dialogue between two scholars who genuinely disagree about it. One defends it as a basically legitimate form of private ordering: consenting parties can waive their day in court, and arbitration is faster and cheaper than litigation. The other argues it's a privatization of justice, with the underappreciated wrinkle that it doesn't just move individual disputes out of court—it also quietly shrinks the common law, because cases that would have generated published appellate decisions get shunted into confidential arbitration. Have them actually engage each other: the defender should wrestle with the repeat-player and lack-of-genuine-consent points, and the critic should address the freedom-of-contract reply and the fact that courts are often terrible at resolving small disputes. Both should be sharp; make it a real back-and-forth, not two people talking past one another.
