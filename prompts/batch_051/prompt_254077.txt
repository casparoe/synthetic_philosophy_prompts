I spent the evening in a long argument with a friend, and the argument kept failing in a way I cannot quite name. The point under discussion was a single step in a simple piece of reasoning. He granted me a conditional statement, and he granted me its antecedent; he was happy to assert either one, and in fact he asserted both together. But when I moved to the consequent, he stopped and said he did not see why that move was allowed. I answered that the conditional form itself licenses the move: once someone has accepted a conditional and its antecedent, the consequent may be detached. He said he accepted that too. He accepted that if a conditional and its antecedent are both true, then the consequent is true. But he still declined to detach the consequent. So I offered this newly accepted statement as a further premise. He accepted it, thanked me for it, and asked what now licensed the next step. We continued in this way for several rounds, and each time he accepted the added statement and each time ended exactly where he had begun.

I am not looking for a psychological account of my friend's stubbornness. What I want to know is whether this structural situation has a recognized name, and what the standard diagnosis of it is. Is it best understood as an infinite regress, as a confusion between statements and something else that tells us how to move among statements, as a point about logic being prescriptive rather than descriptive, or as none of these? What exactly, if anything, has gone wrong in his position? He seems to grant every assertion I make and yet never arrives at the conclusion, so I would like to know where the binding force is supposed to enter, and whether that force can be stated as one more assertion at all. I would rather have the phenomenon carved precisely than be handed a bare label.
