You are an expert in the philosophy of science, specializing in confirmation theory. Think carefully, and begin by laying out the logical structure of the problem before designing the procedure.

I manage the audit team at a certification body. Each month we must certify or refuse claims of the form: all drums in a given chemical shipment are below the regulatory limit for contaminant X. We cannot test every drum; we test a sample, and we also receive incidental reports, such as a drum from a different shipment that tested over the limit. Hempel's raven paradox says that the hypothesis that all ravens are black is logically equivalent to the hypothesis that all non-black things are non-ravens, so a white shoe can confirm the ravens hypothesis. By the same reasoning, a contaminated drum from a different shipment would count as confirming that all drums in this shipment are clean — a conclusion my team finds absurd.

Please write a reusable decision procedure my auditors could follow, without formal training in confirmation theory, for deciding what evidence counts as confirming a universal claim of this sort, how much weight to give to evidence about non-instances, and when the available evidence leaves the claim undecided. It should be executable in under two hours; when it is inconclusive, the decision escalates to me and the laboratory director rather than being resolved by the auditor; and it should be honest about which steps are judgment calls rather than logic. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) many clean drums from the shipment and one contaminated drum from a different shipment; (2) little direct evidence about the shipment but many contaminated drums from elsewhere; (3) a case in which the procedure returns an answer my team will resist, for example because it refuses to let a mass of indirect evidence justify certification. Finally, state what the procedure cannot settle. Throughout, separate what is settled in confirmation theory from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
