I was a working scientist for thirty years, and for most of that time cause meant something like: I changed one thing in the apparatus and the measured quantity moved in a way that wasn't just noise. Now that I'm retired and reading Hume I've had to face up to how strange the concept gets once you take the apparatus away. So I want your help thinking through a reading dispute I've run into.

The story everyone learns first is that Hume reduces causation to regularity, constant conjunction, and that the necessary connection we feel is contributed by the mind, not found in the objects. But there's a revisionist reading, sometimes called the New Hume or skeptical realism, that says Hume actually did believe in real necessary connections or powers out there in nature, just ones we can never directly cognize; we can point at them only through their effects, the way you can refer to whatever is making the detector tick without ever seeing it. That last part is probably why the view appeals to me, and I don't want to be taken in by a view just because it sounds scientific.

So: give me your actual credence that the New Hume or skeptical realist reading is the better interpretation of Hume, as against the old regularity-plus-projection reading. And please decompose the number. I can see at least these sub-questions going in: (a) how much weight to put on the places where Hume seems to allow hidden powers in nature versus the places where he says we have no idea of necessary connection beyond what repeated experience gives us; (b) whether for Hume lacking an idea of X rules out a coherent belief in X, or whether something like a relative idea, referring to a power as whatever fills a causal-role slot, lets him believe in what he cant conceive directly; (c) whether Hume's broader naturalism, his view that we just do believe various things philosophy cant justify, gives him room to be a realist about causes; (d) how much his two definitions of cause, one in terms of constant conjunction and one in terms of the minds determination, help or hurt the revisionist case. Put a rough probability on each, say how you combine them, and tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me how confident you are in that number, and which single component you're least sure about.
