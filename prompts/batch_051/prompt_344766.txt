Two philosophers meet to hash out whether disposition talk — fragility, solubility, being poisonous, and so on — can be reduced to counterfactual conditionals. Write the dialogue. One starts out defending the simple conditional analysis: roughly, x is disposed to M in conditions C iff, if x were in C, x would M (the glass is fragile because if it were struck, it would break). The other leans toward a primitivist view, on which disposition ascriptions are irreducibly modal and no conditional paraphrase gets them right. This second character should press the standard trouble cases, but the dialogue has to earn them rather than turn into a lecture. Their cases: a fink — something that removes the disposition exactly when C obtains (a stabilizer released on impact, so if the glass were struck it wouldn't break, although it is still fragile), and a mask — something that stops the manifestation while leaving the disposition in place (a fragile glass packed in foam, or a poison taken together with an antidote). The first character should not cave right away: have them retreat to a refined view on which the object has some intrinsic causal basis (the glass's molecular structure, say) such that if x were in C, and the basis remained present, and nothing interfered, x would M. The second character then attacks that refined view. The obvious attack is that the "nothing interfered" or "basis remained present" clauses either smuggle disposition-talk back in or make the analysis vacuous, but try to make the objection less canned than that — maybe by pushing on whether the causal basis is itself just another disposition, or by asking whether the refined view handles a case where two objects fink each other's dispositions at the same time. Work in at least one weirder case mid-dialogue that isnt the textbook fink or mask. At some point have them stop and ask whether their disagreement is substantive or merely verbal: would the primitivist be satisfied if the refined analysis could be made to work, or do they want dispositions to be irreducible regardless. Both characters should make at least one concession or shift their view a little, and neither should read as the designated winner. Keep it under 1100 words and make it sound like an actual conversation — they should respond to what the other just said, interrupt, and mostly not talk past each other.
