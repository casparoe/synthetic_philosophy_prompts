Give me a definition of historical denialism — one that separates it from legitimate historical revisionism — that classifies the following cases correctly. (1) A pamphlet asserting that the Holocaust never happened and that the gas chambers are a postwar invention. (2) A historian who, on the strength of newly recovered conscription, transport and mortality records, revises a widely cited estimate of Armenian deaths during the First World War downwards by five per cent, publishes the method in full, and does not dispute that the killings were centrally organised. (3) A writer who accepts that very large numbers of Armenians died during the First World War but maintains that the deaths were an accidental by-product of deportation, famine and disease rather than a centrally ordered policy of extermination. (4) A Japanese history textbook that avoids the word massacre, calls what happened at Nanjing the Nanjing Incident, notes that many non-combatants died, and adds that the scale and character of the events remain matters of historical controversy. (5) A writer who claims that the figure of six million Jewish victims is a postwar exaggeration, that the true total is in the hundreds of thousands, and that most deaths resulted from typhus and the general collapse of conditions rather than from an extermination programme. (6) A scholar who accepts the documented record of mass killing of Armenians but argues that the events are better classified as crimes against humanity than as genocide, because the evidence does not, on his view, establish the intent to destroy the group as such. (7) A politician who, pressed on Nanjing, describes the events as an unfortunate chapter in a brutal war and refuses to use the words massacre or atrocity. I am confident that (1) and (5) are denialism and that (2) is legitimate revisionism; I have no settled view on (3), (4), (6) and (7). Propose a definition, apply it to each case, and say where the definition yields no clean verdict or a verdict you would defend against my intuitions. Tell me which of your classifications is least secure.
