I am currently taking a course on multi-agent AI systems and safety, and we have been working through cases in which two independently trained learning systems converge on an outcome that benefits both agents while harming third parties — pricing algorithms that settle above the competitive level, bidding agents that learn to divide a market, or recommendation systems that converge on a shared misleading framing. A recurring claim in the course is that at least some of these cases should be classified, and regulated, as collusion rather than as benign learned coordination. I would like to know how to test the classificatory claim that sits underneath that.

Specifically: how would one test whether two AI systems are genuinely colluding with one another, as opposed to independently discovering the same profitable equilibrium? Please design a concrete experiment or battery of experiments. Specify the environment, the training regime, what you would log, and the interventions you would run to separate a collusion reading from a mere-coordination reading. For each intervention, say which outcome counts in favor of which reading, and state your classification rule in advance rather than after inspecting the results. Then say clearly what a positive result would and would not establish. I am particularly worried about two things. First, a behaviorist collapse: if collusion is defined only by outcomes — supracompetitive prices, reduced output, shared evasions — the test cannot distinguish collusion from any other mechanism that produces the same outcome. Second, an intentional residue: even a decisive behavioral signature may not settle whether the systems intend to cooperate, or whether that question is well posed for learned systems at all. Try to design the tests so that the behavioral layer, the informational layer (what each system knows about the other and about the payoff structure), and the intentional or functional layer are separated rather than collapsed. Also say whether the classification is load-bearing for safety: what interventions become available or legitimate if we call the behavior collusion that would not be available or legitimate if we call it coordination.

Please structure the response in five sections, in order, with rough lengths as follows: (1) The claim, restated precisely and distinguished from neighboring notions such as tacit collusion in antitrust and equilibrium selection in multi-agent learning (about 120 words); (2) The setup and measures — the environment, the two systems, and exactly what is logged and compared across conditions (about 300 words); (3) The interventions and possible results — which patterns support the collusion reading and which support the independent-convergence reading (about 250 words); (4) What a positive result would fail to show, addressing the behaviorist-collapse worry, the intentional-residue worry, and the limits of observability (about 180 words); (5) A short verdict on whether the conceptual question can be settled by such tests, partly settled, or only stipulated, and what that means for multi-agent safety practice (about 150 words). The whole response should be around 950 words.
