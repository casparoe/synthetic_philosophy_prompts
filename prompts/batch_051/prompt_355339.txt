I've come to accept a view I'm not fully comfortable with, and I want to know what would actually count against it. I'm a software engineer, and after a long argument with a friend about whether we'll ever upload people, I found myself saying that the mind just is a certain kind of information-processing pattern, so a whole-brain emulation that captures enough of the relevant causal and computational structure would be me — not a copy that acts like me, but a continuation of me, the way a running process stays the same process when you migrate it to new hardware. My friend says this is obviously wrong, but alot of his examples strike me as hardware-chauvinism: he can't get passed the idea that the original is carbon and the emulation is silicon. I suspect the term pattern is doing more work in my formulation than I can currently see, and maybe that's where the problems hide.

Still, there are places where the view gets strained, and I'd like those laid out honestly rather than just getting the usual line. If the scan is non-destructive, so the biological me survives and the emulation also exists, I seem stuck saying there are now two continuations of me. The same goes for pausing the emulation and resuming two copies, or running it from the same checkpoint twice. I also worry about gradual replacement cases, where one neuron at a time gets swapped for a simulated version and I supposedly stay conscious through the whole thing — that should support my view, but it also raises the question whether the fine-grained details of each neuron's causal role matter or only the larger pattern. I'm not asking for reading recommendations. What I want is a map of what counts against the position, including things I haven't thought of: which possible cases, nearby debates, or empirical results would a careful defender have to treat as genuine threats, and which of those are fatal rather than merely awkward, including what a committed person would themselves concede as a problem.

Also, say how confident you are in that assessment and which single point you're least sure about.
