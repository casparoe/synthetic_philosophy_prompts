ok so i'm on my school's academic integrity board (mostly students plus a couple teachers) and i got stuck writing the opinion for a case that's split us down the middle. i need a short memo i can circulate before thursday, something the board can actually vote on.

the student is a midyear transfer named marcus. our honesty code says using text generators, paraphrasing software, or translation tools on any graded assignment is unauthorized assistance unless a teacher explicitly allows it, and the standard penalty is the same as plagiarism: zero on the assignment, a half-credit academic integrity course, and a note in the discipline file. marcus didn't copy anyone's work. his history paper had his own thesis, his own sources, and a rough draft he wrote entirely himself. he then ran the draft through a paraphrasing tool because english is his second language and he said his sentences sounded like a kid's. his teacher can see the whole revision history, so nobody denies the ideas and research are his. what he did violates the policy only because of the paraphrasing tool.

the catch is that marcus had almost no way of knowing this rule existed. transfers at our school get a 45 minute orientation that covers schedules, dress code, and attendance, and that's it. the full honesty code is in a 90 page handbook that's only posted online, and the AI rule was added over the summer before he arrived. the email announcing the new rule went only to student accounts before his account was even set up, and his mom doesn't read english well enough to go digging through a PDF. half the board keeps repeating that ignorance of the rules is no excuse, the handbook was available, and if i didn't know works everyone will start using it and the whole code falls apart. the other half says this is not like copying an essay off the internet, which you know is wrong without reading any handbook; here the only thing making it wrong is a rule he couldn't have been guided by, so punishing him feels like punishing him for not getting notice. a small group in the middle wants to say he technically violated the code but should recieve a lighter penalty: redo the assignment plus a warning, no file note, and make the school actually cover the AI rule in transfer orientation going forward.

so can you draft the memo for me? it should come down on one side for the bottom line (responsible or not, and exactly what penalty if so), give real reasons, and engage the strongest version of the other side instead of a strawman. also try not to just punt to the middle option unless you actually think it's right.
