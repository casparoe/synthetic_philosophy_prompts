Ok so my friend has been on this Dawkins kick and keeps explaining all of evolution as genes using bodies as throwaway survival machines. His go-to analogy is a franchise: the gene is the franchisor, the body is the outlet, and what looks like an animal living its life is really just a storefront the franchisor builds in order to open more storefronts elsewhere. On this picture things like peacock tails, menopause, even altruism snap into focus once you track which copies of which gene end up in more franchise locations, and worrying about what's good for the species or the group is like worrying about the health of one individual store when what actually persists is the brand. I get that theres something to the picture, but the franchise part seems to smuggle in a lot — a franchisor has intentions, designs outlets top-down, can inspect and fire managers, and treats the outlet as a vehicle for its instructions. I'd like you to stress-test the analogy: give me the correspondence table (what maps to the franchisor, the outlet, the brand, the franchise agreement, the profit), then the places the mapping breaks, and especially which of those breaks matter for his actual conclusion that the species and the group are the wrong level to care about. Does that conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? Is there a better analogy that keeps what's right in his picture without the parts that do the smuggling?
