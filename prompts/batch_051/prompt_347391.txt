"Aristotelian syllogistic can only talk about subject-predicate sentences, like 'all humans are mortal, Socrates is a human, so Socrates is mortal.' What it can't express is relational inferences. For example, syllogistic can't show that if every horse is an animal, then every head of a horse is a head of an animal. Frege's fix was to replace the subject-predicate picture with function-argument analysis, like f(x) in math, so 'x is a horse' becomes Horse(x). He also brought in variables and the quantifiers ∀ and ∃. So you can write ∀x(Horse(x) → Animal(x)) and then derive ∀y∀x(HeadOf(y, x) ∧ Horse(x) → HeadOf(y, x) ∧ Animal(x)). The other big thing Frege added was truth tables. He was the first person to give a precise definition of 'and', 'or', and 'not' with truth tables, and that part of his system is why we call the whole thing propositional logic."
