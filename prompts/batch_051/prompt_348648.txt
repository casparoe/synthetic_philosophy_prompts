Here is an argument I reconstructed from the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, and I find its conclusion too strong to accept, though I cannot locate the exact step that does the damage. (1) In special relativity, whether two spatially separated events are simultaneous depends on the inertial frame of the observer. (2) Consequently, for any event outside my absolute past and absolute future — say, something happening on a sufficiently distant planet next Tuesday — there is some possible observer moving relative to me for whom that event is simultaneous with what is happening here now. (3) Whatever is simultaneous, for such an observer, with what is happening now is just as real as what is happening now. (4) Since the distant event is real from that observer's standpoint, it is simply real; it cannot be real for them but not part of reality. (5) By extending the same reasoning, every event — including everything in my own future — is already real, so the future is not open but fixed: a block universe. (6) But if events next Tuesday are already real, then deliberating about next Tuesday is merely the experience of settling nothing, which I cannot accept. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I want most to keep the physics in (1) and (2), but I want to know exactly what I pay for each option.
