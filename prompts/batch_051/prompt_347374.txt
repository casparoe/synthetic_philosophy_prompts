I'm a novelist, and I'm trying to keep an old aunt character of mine — she's sort of the moral centre of the book — consistent in what she counts as humility. Across the chapters so far, she has praised a scientist who accepted a big prize by saying it was mostly luck and then naming every research assistant and technician; said that a friend who gives a very honest, warts-and-all account of her own strengths and weaknesses in interviews is actually rather immodest, because she's turned self-knowledge into a performance; and admired a politician who took the blame for a failure that wasn't his so that the junior staff wouldn't have to. Now I'm writing a scene where the guest of honour at a retirement do stands up and says, in effect, that yes he did do the things people have described and he's glad they noticed, but the person they should really be asking to speak is the woman who drove him to every early meeting and never got a thank-you. Would my aunt call that humility? More importantly, what rule or rules is she running — is humility, for her, about making yourself small, making others bigger, not caring what people think, or something else — and which small change to the scene would best settle which rule she actually holds? I'm less interested in whether she's right than in predicting her verdict and reverse-engineering the principle.
