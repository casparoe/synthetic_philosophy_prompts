Suppose it's 2045 and a collaboration led by CERN announces a candidate final theory. It's not discovered the old way: a machine search over effective field theories spits out a single Lagrangian that reproduces every measurement in fundamental physics to within current error bars, has no free parameters at all, and includes a quantum gravity sector. The catch: it makes no new prediction below about 10^16 GeV, and six or seven mathematically different rivals fit the same data just as well and can't be distinguished by any detector forseeable on a hundred-year horizon. Predict what fundamental physics looks like in 2095. Does the field treat the thing as finished? Does it shrink, splinter, or turn into something closer to mathematics? How do graduate students pick problems when experiment can't referee? I want the chain of reasoning, and I want you to flag which predictions are robust under almost any assumptions about how scientists actually behave and which ones hinge on contingent things like whether the theory is elegant, whether it's teachable in a semester, or whether one rival can be gerrymandered into making a testable prediction. And tell me what would have to be true for the community to just shrug and keep building bigger colliders anyway.
