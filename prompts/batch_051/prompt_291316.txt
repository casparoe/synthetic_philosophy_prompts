I'm making an ungraded pre-course poll for a second-year meaning and truth module, and I want it to work as a sorting instrument: a "which view of truth-value gaps do you actually hold?" quiz. Ten questions, each a concrete case with three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions, labelled plainly — classical no-gaps, presupposition-failure gaps, future-contingent open future, supervaluationist (vague cases are neither determinately true nor false, though all sharpenings agree on clear cases), epistemicist (hidden sharp boundary, so bivalence holds), category-mistake gaps, and dialetheist glut for people who think some sentences are both true and false rather than neither. The cases should include at least: a failed presupposition built around a definate description, like the present King of France being bald; a borderline heap-or-bald sort of case; a future-contingent case like whether there will be a sea battle tomorrow; a category mistake like the number two being green; a mostly precise property with an apparently hidden cut-off along a continuum; and a liar-flavoured case to catch people who think the "gap" is really a glut. The point is to sort students by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask whether they are a supervaluationist. Put the real work into the diagnostic questions: each should be a case where the positions genuinely come apart, phrased so a student can answer without already knowing the labels. Also flag any two positions your key can't cleanly seperate, and diagnose any answer patterns in the grid that end up unoccupied — say whether that combination looks incoherent or just unpopular. Oh, and please write the whole thing in British English.
