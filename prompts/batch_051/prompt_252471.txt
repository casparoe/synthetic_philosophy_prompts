In Language, Truth, and Logic, Ayer argues pretty explicitly that the claim "I shall survive death" is not even false but literally meaningless, because no possible observation could ever settle it one way or the other. So far so good. But then in the late 1980s he has this near-death experience—his heart stops after he chokes on a piece of salmon, and he reports seeing a red light, feeling like he's at some kind of threshold, and being told by a presence that he has to go back. He wrote about it afterwards (I think in a short newspaper piece, maybe the Telegraph, but I don't remember the exact title) and said the experience didn't change his mind at all: he still thinks consciousness is brain-dependent and there's no afterlife, and the whole thing was probably just what happens when the brain is starved of oxygen.

Now here's what seems weird to me. His early verificationist view says no experience could ever count as evidence for survival after death—it's not a factual question at all. But when he has an experience that a lot of people would treat as exactly the kind of thing that *could* be evidence, instead of just saying "that's meaningless, moving on," he bothers to explain it away as a hallucination. Doesn't offering a naturalistic explanation concede that the experience has some evidential bearing? Like, if the claim is genuinely meaningless, why does he even need to argue that the red light was probably just oxygen deprivation? You don't argue against evidence for a meaningless proposition, you just point out there's nothing to argue about.

So is there a real tension between the early Ayer and the late Ayer? Or can they be reconciled by saying the near-death experience is just a subjective episode that happened while he was still alive, so it can't verify a claim about what happens *after* death, and his later explanation is just a causal story about why he had the experience, not an argument about its evidential force? Give me the best reconciliation you can, and tell me whether you think it's honest or if it's special pleading. Also, if you think the early and late Ayer genuinely conflict, which one should we take as his considered view?
