I am taking a seminar this term on biblical hermeneutics, and we have spent several weeks discussing the principles that should govern how an interpreter handles apparent contradictions within and across the biblical narratives. Our professor has been deliberately indirect about her overarching method: she hands down rulings on particular passages and expects us to reconstruct the principles from her track record. So far she has ruled as follows. On the divergent ages given for King Ahaziah in Kings and Chronicles, she said the two numbers cannot both be original, that the divergence is a scribal error in one line of the textual tradition, and that the figure should be corrected rather than harmonized. On the death of Judas and the purchase of the field, where one account has him hang himself while the priests later buy the field with the returned money and another has him fall headlong and acquire the field with his wages, she held that the narratives describe the same event from complementary angles and can be combined into a single coherent sequence. On the healing of the centurion's servant, where one Gospel has the centurion come to Jesus directly and another has him send elders and then friends, she ruled that the first account has compressed a chain of intermediaries and that this is ordinary narrative abbreviation rather than error. On the differing lists of women at the empty tomb and the differing number of heavenly figures reported there, she ruled that each evangelist provides only a partial selection and that an incomplete list is not a false list. On the cleansing of the temple, which one Gospel places near the beginning of Jesus's ministry while the others place it in the final week, she ruled that the sources report two separate incidents rather than a single misplaced episode.

Next week she will take up the healing of the blind men near Jericho. One Gospel says Jesus healed two blind men as he was leaving the city; a second names a single blind man, Bartimaeus, and also places the healing on the way out; a third has one unnamed blind man healed as Jesus approached the city. I want to predict her ruling from her earlier verdicts. Please identify the candidate interpretive principles that her precedents support, state what each principle would dictate for the Jericho passage, and say which earlier ruling is the closest analog. Then give your best prediction of how she will rule, and if you judge that her precedents underdetermine the case, explain what principle she would be committing herself to under each available ruling and specify a further test case that would settle which principle she is actually running.
