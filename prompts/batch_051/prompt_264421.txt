I serve on the governing board of a mid-sized foundation that funds public-health projects across several countries. Our grant contracts define a corrupt payment as anything of value given to influence an official or secure an advantage, and our compliance team treats that rule as unconditional. A field partner has asked to add a budget line it calls "community gift provisions": a cash gift to a local chief and a feast in his honor. The partner explains that the chief controls access to the villages where the project operates, that no outside organization works in the area without performing this ritual, and that budgeting for it openly is more honest than burying it in travel expenses. Our program officer calls it ordinary clientelism and says it should be funded as a legitimate operating cost; our compliance lead calls it a bribe and says approving it would make the foundation complicit. Draft the board's decision memo: approve the amendment, reject it, or approve it with conditions. Give a clear bottom line we can act on, criteria for similar cases, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the side you recommend against. Also confront the worry that our blanket rule imposes a Western line between gift and bribe on a system in which authority runs partly on ongoing gift-relations. If you think the policy or the way we have framed the question is itself confused, say that plainly rather than forcing a verdict.
