I serve on my university's student conduct appeals panel, and this week we must decide a case involving Policy 4.7, which prohibits making an audio recording of any instructional interaction without prior written consent from everyone present. The respondent is a graduate teaching assistant who recorded her own weekly discussion sections over several weeks, using a personal recorder, solely to review her pacing and the clarity of her explanations; the recordings were never shared with anyone else. Two undergraduates in the section learned the recordings existed and filed a complaint. Policy 4.7 is not printed in the student handbook, the graduate employee contract, or the mandatory online training module for new teaching assistants; it appears only in a procedures manual posted on a faculty-facing intranet portal for which teaching assistants are not given credentials. The teaching assistant had never heard of the policy until the complaint was filed. Our conduct code states in general terms that lack of knowledge of a rule does not excuse a violation, and the university's position is that the policy was available in the relevant sense because any employee could have requested a copy from the Office of the Dean of Faculty.

The panel is divided. Several members think the charge should be upheld: the rule protects reasonable privacy expectations in the classroom, and allowing a defense based on personal unawareness would make campus rules difficult to enforce. Others think it would be unfair, perhaps illegitimate, to impose discipline for violating a rule that, as a practical matter, this teaching assistant could not have known existed, and that available on request is a fiction. Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete disposition, whether to dismiss the charge, uphold it with a reduced or alternative response, or uphold it as written; state the principle the panel should use when a conduct rule was never effectively publicized to the people bound by it; and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
