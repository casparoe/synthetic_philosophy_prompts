I keep encountering the same ambiguity in arguments about revealed preference, and I want to resolve it before I commit to a view. In one strand of economic theory, the utility function is nothing more than a compact way to represent a pattern of choices: if a person's choices satisfy the relevant consistency conditions, we can describe them as if they were maximizing some function, but the numbers in that function carry no further psychological or normative meaning. In another strand—welfare economics, cost–benefit analysis, parts of behavioral economics—the word utility is asked to do a second job: to measure how well off a person is, so that we can say a policy made someone better off, a choice was a mistake, or one person's gain outweighs another's loss. These are different jobs, and the field often slides between them without noticing.

I want you to write the desiderata. Specifically: what must any adequate account of the relationship between observed choice and utility be able to do, if that account is meant to serve both a predictive purpose and a normative or welfare purpose within the same framework? For each requirement, give the concrete case or argument that forces it. The account should, for example, have something determinate to say about at least these phenomena, though you are not limited to them: time inconsistency and the fact that people pay for commitment devices; choices that shift with framing or defaults; addiction and other cases in which a person disowns their own choices; and the use of utility to compare welfare across persons. These are cases where the two jobs of utility seem especially likely to pull apart.

After you have given the list, identify which desiderata you regard as non-negotiable and which are optional or parochial, flag any pairs you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied by a single notion of utility, and say which requirement does the most work ruling out candidate views. Finally, score the main families of positions you know against the list in a short closing section—but keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivation, not on the scoring. I am not asking for a survey or a reading list; I am asking what an adequate account would have to deliver.
