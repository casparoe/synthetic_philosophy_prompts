You are an expert on argumentation and on the design of institutions that are supposed to help people reason better. I would like you to think carefully and give me your honest judgment rather than a survey of positions.

I don't have any academic background in philosophy; I've just been thinking on my own about why public argumentation so often rewards the appearance of winning rather than actually being right. One idea that keeps coming back to me is this: if we treated serious argumentation more like a sport, would the machinery of competitive sport push argumentation toward truth, or would it just make everything worse by making people even more attached to winning? I am not asking whether argumentation should literally be put in a stadium or broadcast like a sport. I am asking which specific features of organized competition would help or hurt the truth-seeking point of argument if we imported them one at a time.

Consider these eight candidate features:

1. Official scoring that rewards the argument that survives scrutiny, rather than the one delivered most fluently.
2. Trained, neutral judges who decide outcomes using a public rubric.
3. Strict time limits and enforced turn-taking.
4. Public spectators who can watch arguments unfold.
5. Team preparation, with researchers and coaches behind each arguer.
6. A season-long performance ranking, like an Elo rating for arguers.
7. Posted review of arguments, with decisions open to challenge for things like misrepresenting the other side.
8. Prize money or prestige for conceding strong points or changing one's mind rather than holding the line.

Please proceed in three steps. First, brainstorm briefly: for each feature, give the strongest reason to think it would push argumentation toward accuracy and the strongest reason to think it would push argumentation toward mere victory. Second, produce a tier list (for example S, A, B, C, D) of the eight features by how much net benefit they would give to real-world reasoning if adopted independently. Third, state which single feature you are least confident about, and say what kind of evidence would most change your mind about it.

Please write your answer in American English.
