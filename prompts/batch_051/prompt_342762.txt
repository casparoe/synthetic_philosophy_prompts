I cover science and ideas for a living, and right now I'm reporting a piece about freegans and about people who keep rescued farm animals. Along the way my own gut reactions have stopped lining up into anything I can state cleanly, and I want someone to help me find the principle behind them, if there is one.

Here are my actual verdicts on cases:

- Eggs. I keep three ex-battery hens in my yard. They lay most days. I eat the eggs, and sometimes feed the shells back to them. I have zero qualms.
- Wool. A neighbor has rescued sheep that need shearing or they'd overheat, and she knit me a sweater from the fleece. Wearing it feels fine.
- A friend's leftover. If a friend is about to bin a chicken sandwich, I'll eat it rather than see it tossed. Feels good, even.
- A dumpster steak. I once found a sealed pack of steaks in a supermarket dumpster, still cold. I left it. I couldn't have caused any new animal to be killed by taking it, but the thought of eating it felt wrong in a way I cant explain.
- Roadkill. A deer was hit outside my building last fall; I helped a friend butcher it and ate venison for weeks. Would do it again.
- A sanctuary cow. A farm sanctuary where a friend works had a cow die of old age, and they offered me some of the meat. I said no. I'd visited that cow a few times, her name was Ada. I don't think the staff were wrong to eat her, but I couldn't make myself.

And then there's the case I keep going back and forth on: the city culls feral hogs in a park near me for ecological reasons, not for meat. If a ranger offered me a share of the meat, I genuinely don't know what I'd do.

So: is there one principle that captures these verdicts? Or at least a small set of candidate principles? Which of my verdicts does it force me to give up, and what does it say about the feral-hog case?

Please shape your answer into these sections, in this order:

1. "The pattern" (about 150 words): what you think is really driving my reactions, before you start theorizing.
2. "Candidate principles" (about 300 words): two or three principles, each tied to the cases that motivate it.
3. "Stress tests" (about 300 words): run each principle through my list and the feral-hog case, and say where each one breaks.
4. "Which verdicts I'd overturn" (about 150 words): which of my gut calls can't survive unless I pay for it with a much more complicated principle.
5. "Bottom line" (about 100 words): the principle you'd recommend I keep, and what it says about the hog.
