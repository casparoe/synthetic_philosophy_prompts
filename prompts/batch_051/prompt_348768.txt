I have been developing a construct I call "valence density," and I would like it assessed honestly. The proposal is that moral status should track not merely whether a system is sentient but how much valenced experience it supports per unit of information-processing: pleasure, pain, frustration, and satisfaction count toward the numerator, while the system's information-processing activity supplies the denominator. A mammal has high valence density; an insect has very low but nonzero density; a thermostat processes information with zero density. Moral status thereby comes in degrees while retaining a hard line at zero, and one can say precisely why many simultaneous insect pains might outweigh one mammal pain without attributing rich inner lives to insects. Is this already a position under another name? Where does it break—for example, does the information-processing denominator do any work that a simpler intensity-plus-capacity account would not, and does it misrank a slow-moving but intensely conscious system?
