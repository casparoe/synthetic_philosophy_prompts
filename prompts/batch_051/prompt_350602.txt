I've been collecting objections to the Santa/tooth-fairy thing, and here's what I have so far: (1) it's just lying to someone who has no reason to distrust you, so it's a breach of the kid's trust; (2) it rewards taking a story on authority over following evidence, so it trains credulity; (3) the reveal is often worse than the lie, since it makes kids wonder what else their parents made up; (4) the kid can't consent to being deceived, and a lot of the time the adults are doing it mostly for their own amusement; (5) it gives children a false explanation for why some kids get piles of gifts and others get almost nothing, so the worse-off kids end up thinking they were bad. I get the sense these are the standard moves, but I keep feeling there's one I'm missing. Can you give me an objection to this practice that isn't on the list and isn't just a rewording, special case, or combination of the ones I've already listed? And if you do, be honest about whether it's actually a good objection.
