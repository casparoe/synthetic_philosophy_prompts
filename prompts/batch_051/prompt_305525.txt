I've become convinced by a view I've seen called prioritarianism: an improvement in someone's life matters more the worse off that person is, and because of that weighting you never have to say that inequality is bad as such, or that leveling down — making the better-off worse off while helping no one — could be an improvement. That last part is a large part of the view's appeal for me, because I find the thought that there could be something good about pure leveling down hard to accept. But I also can't shake a firm intuition that in some real cases the inequality itself is what bothers me. If a company pays its executives hundreds of times what it pays its cleaners, my objection seems at least partly aimed at the gap, not just at the cleaners' low absolute level; if the gap narrowed because executives took a pay cut and the money was not redistributed, I would not call the whole world better, but I would not call it merely worse either — some objectionable feature would have been removed. I don't want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, what it would take for the theory to be amended or abandoned, and whether there is a reading of 'inequality matters' on which both can survive.
