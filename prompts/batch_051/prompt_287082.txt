I have been trying to work out whether an AI system, or any other agent, can coherently reason as a deontologist rather than as a utilitarian, and the following argument keeps seeming too quick to me. I want to know whether it is valid, and if not, exactly which step fails and on what reading.

(1) A deontological constraint is a rule that forbids a class of acts irrespective of the goods those acts would produce, as in a prohibition on knowingly taking an innocent life.

(2) For any ordinary act an agent considers in practice, the agent's evidence assigns some nonzero probability to the act's bringing about an outcome that would violate at least one such constraint; a delivery vehicle, for instance, has some small chance of killing a bystander.

(3) If the constraint is lexical — that is, if no amount of expected good may justify any positive probability of violating it — then any act with such a positive probability is forbidden, and the agent is left with almost no acts it may perform.

(4) If the constraint is not lexical, then, in deciding whether to run a given risk of violating it, the agent must weigh the expected good of the act against the expected badness of the violation, weighted by its probability.

(5) But such weighing is exactly expected-utility reasoning with the constraint's badness folded into the utility function, so the constraint has collapsed into one valued consideration among others rather than remaining a side-constraint on the agent's reasoning.

(6) Therefore, for any agent facing real uncertainty, deontological reasoning either collapses into paralysis or reduces to a disguised form of utilitarianism.

I suspect the argument slides between senses of violating a constraint, or between a criterion of rightness and a decision procedure, but I cannot make the diagnosis precise. Is the argument valid? If not, say which step fails and on which reading of it, and whether the failure is local or undermines the whole argument. If it is valid, say whether the conclusion is as damaging to deontology as it appears.
