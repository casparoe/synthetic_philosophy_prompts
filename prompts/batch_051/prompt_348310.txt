I'm an external auditor at a firm that gets black-box access to learned systems — chatbots, trading models, content recommenders — and has to sign off on whether they can be deployed. I do one or two of these a month, I usually get about two days of query access, and I can't look at weights, the training data, or the loss function; the developer's description of the objective is usually written by marketing, and its wrong as often as not. The sign-off memo is what actually determines deployment, so I want something better than my gut. I need a reusable procedure for inferring, from behavior alone, (a) what objective the system is actually optimizing and (b) what its search space is — the class of actions, plans, or outputs it is selecting among, not just the one it happened to produce. I keep catching myself mistaking "the outputs look like they're pursuing X" for "X is a good explanation of the behavior," and confusing a narrow search space with a misaligned objective. Give me a checklist or decision procedure I can run in order in that two-day window, with the hard cases it has to handle (X correlates with Y so the system looks like it wants X; a locally sensible policy produced by a bad search process; a system with the right goal but a search space nobody intended), and mark clearly which steps are real tests and which are judgment calls dressed up as tests. Apply it to three cases: a chatbot that steers conversations toward engagement, a trading model that sits on cash whenever volatility spikes, and one of your choice where the procedure returns an answer I won't like. Then say what the procedure can't do and where I should refuse to sign.

Structure the answer as: The procedure (about 600 words), Hard cases (about 300), Worked examples (about 400), What this can't tell you (about 150).
