I've been taking care of my dad since his memory got bad, and I keep catching myself telling him things that aren't true — like that his brother is still alive, or that he just talked to my mom on the phone. It feels bad but also kind? So I started reading Augustine's stuff on lying, and I'm trying to figure out what he'd say about my situation. From what I can tell, his verdicts are:

- If someone says something false but genuinely thinks it's true, Augustine says that's not lying (just error or rashness).
- If someone says something true but thinks it's false and says it to mislead, Augustine says that's a lie.
- Jokes are set aside: they're not lies, though he seems to think they might still be beneath someone aiming at perfection.
- A lie told to help someone — even to save a life — he still says it's wrong, no exceptions.
- A lie told to harm someone is obviously wrong too.

Now, my actual case isn't a clean false statement: sometimes I tell my dad something true but phrase it in a way I know will leave him with a false impression. Example: he asks where my mom is (she died a few years ago), and I say "she's not here right now." Literally true, but I know it makes him think she's just out for a bit. Is that a lie on Augustine's view, or does his defintion not catch it because the words themselves are true? And if he would call it a lie, does that mean his rule is really "don't intentionally deceive" rather than "don't say what you believe is false"? Can you give me a verdict, say which of his principles you're leaning on, and tell me if my list of rulings underdetermines it? Also, if I'm being self-serving or reading him selectively, I'd rather hear that than get a tidy answer.
