so ive been having this argument at work and it keeps going in circles. the company just rolled out a set of tools that automate the most repetitive part of what our team does, and everyone including me kept saying what a good thing it was — nobody should have to spend their week doing that mechanical stuff, its dehumanizing. then a coworker pointed out that with the new tools he can finish his actual job in about ten hours, and he said, half-joking, so why dont we just do that — ten hours, same salary, go home. people got genuinely irritated. they called him workshy, said he was taking advantage, said if you're salaried you owe the company your full week. and it dawned on me that we're holding two positions at once: when software removes work from the week, that's humane and progressive, but when a person removes work from his own week, that's laziness and borderline dishonest, even if the output is identical.

is there a principle that actually justifies that pair of judgments, or is it just the leftover of an idea that work may only disappear when a machine is the thing not doing it? the extra weirdness is that the machine only ate the boring parts — if it had eaten the meaningful parts and left the drudgery we'd all be angry, so we don't actually believe less work is always better. give me the strongest defense of the asymmetry you can find, test it against that last case, and then tell me honestly whether it survives or which side should be given up.
