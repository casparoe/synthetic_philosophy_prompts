I have come to accept a view about voting and strategic manipulation, and I want it stress-tested. The view is the standard reading of the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem: consider any deterministic social choice function that takes profiles of strict ordinal preferences over three or more alternatives and selects a single winner. If it is non-dictatorial, then at least one profile exists at which some voter can obtain a more preferred outcome by reporting a preference ordering different from her true one; strategy-proofness and non-dictatorship therefore cannot be jointly satisfied. From this I infer the practical thesis that vulnerability to strategic manipulation is a structural feature of democratic aggregation rather than an engineering bug: on the standard model, a manipulation-proof non-dictatorial voting rule is a non-starter, so the live question can only be how much manipulation, how likely, and at what cost.

I would like a map of what would count against this whole package. Please distinguish, first, what would count against the formal result or its standard interpretation, and, second, what would count against the practical inference I draw from it. Specifically: what would the mathematics, or the world, have to look like for each of the standard escape routes to be genuinely live rather than merely logically possible? Consider, for instance, restricting the domain of admissible preferences so that single-peakedness or a similar condition holds and median-type rules become available; allowing randomized outcomes or lotteries; weakening the notion of strategy-proofness to something such as Bayesian incentive compatibility, a no-regret condition, or the requirement that manipulation not be obvious to the manipulator; moving from ordinal to cardinal input; replacing individual manipulability with group manipulability; and appealing to the computational hardness of finding a beneficial manipulation. For each route, say whether it is best understood as a defeat of the theorem, a defeat of the practical lesson, or merely a qualification. Indicate which of these a committed defender of the view would concede as genuinely counting against them, and which they can dismiss without inconsistency. Then rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which one you would personally find most persuasive and why. You may conclude that the package is well insulated; if you do, say whether that insulation is a feature of the view or a problem for it.
