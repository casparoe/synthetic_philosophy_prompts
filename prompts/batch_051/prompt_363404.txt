Please give me a credence, with the derivation fully displayed, that the dispute between universalism and nihilism about composite objects is merely verbal. By universalism I mean the claim that whenever there are some things, there is also a further object composed of them — your left hand and the moon, for instance, jointly compose a scattered object. By nihilism I mean the claim that no composite objects exist: there are only partless simples, and talk of hands and moons is a loose description of certain simples arranged in certain ways. Assume the two sides agree on every observation, on every prediction about future experience, and on the complete physical story about the simples and their arrangement. A dispute is merely verbal, for my purposes, if the disagreement survives only because the two sides use 'exists' with different but equally legitimate meanings, so that once the semantic difference is made explicit there is no further question of fact on which they disagree; it is substantive if at most one side is correct, or if some non-verbal question remains even after the semantic difference is pointed out. I want a number, not a bare one. Decompose your credence into at least the following three terms, assign each a probability and a one-sentence justification, and show explicitly how the terms combine into your final estimate: (1) your probability that there is a mind-independent fact about whether composite objects exist, over and above the facts about simples and their arrangements that both sides already accept; (2) your probability, conditional on there being no such fact, that the residual disagreement is best explained by the two sides meaning different things by 'exists'; (3) your probability, conditional on both of the previous two, that nothing further remains — no substantive question about which meaning is better or about which meaning we ought to adopt — so that the dispute really is merely verbal. Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would most shift the whole estimate.
