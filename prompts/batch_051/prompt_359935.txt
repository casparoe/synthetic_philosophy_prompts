ok so here's roughly where I've ended up on mental content and I'd like to know if it hangs together. (1) I'm a naturalist about intentionality: there's no primitive aboutness, and any account of what a perception or belief is of has to be built from non-semantic ingredients like causation, information, and natural selection. (2) I think the right story is broadly teleosemantic: a representational state's content is fixed by its proper function—what the system was selected for doing with it—and it's the consumer of the representation that does the fixing. So the frog's fly-detector means fly (or fly-ish) even when a little black pellet triggers it; that's misrepresentation, not a change of content. (3) I'm committed to content being distal: the frog represents the fly out in the world, not the pattern on its retina, because what matters is what the tongue-snap consumer's job requires to be present. (4) But I also think a theory of content owes us determinate contents: it has to say whether the frog's state means fly, frog-food, or small-dark-moving-thing-overhead, and explain why the others are wrong. (5) I'm convinced content is compositional, so whatever explains the frog case has to scale up to structured thoughts like the cat being on the mat. (6) And in a swampman case, I really cant bring myself to say the molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me has no beliefs or desires at all just because he lacks a selection history. Does this package hold together? I want the audit: which of these pull against which, which apparent tensions just dissolve, and what's the cheapest way to restore consistency and what it would cost.
