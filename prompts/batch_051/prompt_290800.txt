I'm putting together my views for a social philosophy seminar on what makes a statement racist, and I want a consistency check before I have to present them. Here is roughly where I've landed.

(a) Whether an utterance is racist is primarily about what it does in context—who it demeans, excludes, stereotypes, or marks as other—rather than about what the speaker intended or had going on in their head. A racist statement can come from a perfectly well-meaning speaker.

(b) Dog whistles are real. An utterance can be racist because of a coded message that only some hearers reliably pick up, even when the surface content is banal and the speaker has built-in plausible deniability. What makes it racist is the uptake and the signal, not any single word on the page.

(c) But I also think a speaker is only blameworthy for the foreseeable effects of their speech. Someone who uses an ordinary-sounding phrase without knowing it does coded work in certain circles is less culpable then someone who uses it knowing exactly what they're doing, even if the phrase does the same thing either way.

(e) And yet I want to say that a racist joke told at a whites-only dinner party, where everyone finds it funny and no member of the targeted group is present, is still racist—not just potentially harmful or in bad taste, but racist.
