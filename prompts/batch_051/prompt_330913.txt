I keep running into the phrase "we just need to align AI with human values" in alignment discussions, most recently at a reading group where someone used it to shut down a question about whether a model should do what its user asks when that predictably imposes a cost on some third party. The sentence was doing real work there — it was supposed to be obvious what "human values" picks out and obvious that the answer to the third-party question falls right out of it, and the guy saying it clearly thought he'd ended the conversation. But it feels like it has about as much content as "we'll do what's right": everyone nods and then goes back to disagreeing, just with better vocabulary. I'd like a careful unpacking of the different precise claims that slogan could be expressing. For instance: the values are whoever trains or deploys the system; the values are an aggregate of everyone's preferences (and then: stated, revealed, or laundered through some idealisation?); the values are a small set of cross-cultural moral universals; the values are not first-order substantive values at all but a liberal-neutral commitment to let each user pursue their own conception of the good; the values are whatever a fair voting scheme would converge on case by case; the values are what a representative sample of all humans would endorse behind a veil of ignorance. For each reading, say whether it's near-vacuous, substantive-and-defensible, or quietly partisan, and identify which ones actually get you to an answer about third-party costs and which ones just relocate the disagreement. I want a menu, not your favorite one, so I can see where the readings collapse into eachother and where the speaker's dismissal depended on keeping them blurred. If there's a reading on which his dismissal was fair, say so.
