Write a polished argumentative essay of roughly 1,100 words defending the following thesis. The standard explanation of why mutable state makes programs hard to reason about locates the trouble in aliasing: two syntactically distinct names can denote one storage cell, so an update through one name silently changes what the other sees. That explanation mistakes a part of the problem for the whole. The deeper difficulty is identity over time, not identity across names. Once a mutable object can have every observable component changed while remaining "the same object," the programmer is forced to ask whether the thing before them is still the same object or merely a different object with the same contents—and neither value equality nor reference identity can justify an answer to that question; they can only encode one. Defend this thesis against the strongest version of the aliasing account.

Structure the essay exactly as follows, with the approximate lengths given:

- "The phenomenon" (~150 words): pin down which reasoning tasks mutation makes harder than pure, value-oriented code, and give at most one small illustrative example if that helps.
- "The aliasing account, stated charitably" (~250 words): present the standard aliasing-based diagnosis in its strongest form and specify which cases it handles well.
- "The case for the identity-over-time account" (~400 words): give the central argument that the residual, harder problem is reidentification of a continuing object, and draw out the difference between sameness of content and sameness of object.
- "An objection and a reply" (~200 words): raise the strongest objection you can think of to the central argument and answer it.
- "Verdict" (~100 words): state exactly how much of the aliasing account survives your argument and how far the thesis is meant to go.

Do not include citations, bibliographic references, or appeals to named research programs; the essay should stand or fall on the conceptual argument alone.
