For a film-and-philosophy course I'm teaching, I'd like a reading of Julian Schnabel's The Diving Bell and the Butterfly through Merleau-Ponty's account of the lived body and motor intentionality. In Phenomenology of Perception he treats the body not as an object but as the center of a practical sense of possibility: a habitual, skilled grip on the world that acts before and below explicit thought, and the phantom limb is his example of how that practical field outruns the objective body. Locked-in syndrome looks like a brutal test case. Is Bauby's condition a counterexample to that picture — consciousness left over after motor intentionality is almost entirely destroyed — or does the film show the residual body (the one blinking eye, the laborious blink-coded communication, memories that still feel bodily) keeping the practical world alive?
