Around animal ethics spaces I keep seeing the same beef-vs-chicken argument go absolutely nowhere. The anti-beef side says beef is bad because cows emit methane, use way more land and water per calorie, and the crops grown to feed them kill field animals during harvest. The anti-chicken side says that's backwards: a single cow feeds a lot of people, so per meal beef kills almost no animals, while chicken means killing a small, likely miserable bird every few meals — and chickens eat feed too, so chicken inherits crop deaths as well. Then somebody brings up grass-fed beef, somebody else brings up the mice and insects killed by any crop farming, and from there everyone just repeats their starting points. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is: are people just using different units (per calorie, per animal death, per year of suffering), arguing about facts that better data could settle, or quietly importing different views about which lives and which harms count? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — or is the question just badly posed?
