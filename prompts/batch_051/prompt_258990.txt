I keep going back and forth on petitionary prayer. If God is omniscient and immutable, he already knows what I need and nothing I say can move his will, so asking for things starts to look empty. Here are five moves people make to get out of that. Rank them from most to least successful at keeping petitionary prayer a coherent, worthwhile practice while holding onto both omniscience and immutability. For each, give me a sentence or two on wether it does real work or just silently gives up the "petition" part. Output exactly five numbered items, one per move, in ranked order, with no introduction or conclusion.

The five moves:

1. Prayer changes the one praying: it reorders your desires toward what God already intends, rather than changing God.
2. Prayer is a decreed condition: God's single eternal act includes your petition as part of the causal story of the outcome, so the asking does real work without God being moved.
3. The before/after worry is ours, not God's: since God is outside time, prayer and answer are co-present in eternity, so the "influence" picture is a category mistake.
4. Petition is commanded relationship: the point is obedience and dependence on God, not obtaining results, so the instrumental framing is wrong from the start.
5. Petition should be reframed as articulation: saying what you want before God is really a form of confession or praise of your own neediness, not a genuine request.
