I'm a PhD student in animal welfare science, and a welfare code I've been working through for a chapter on decapod crustacean farming has me stuck. The clause in question reads:

"No person shall, in relation to any farmed decapod crustacean, carry out or cause to be carried out the removal of any appendage or any part of an appendage unless the removal is for the purpose of treating a diagnosed injury or disease."

The code was drafted with crabs and lobsters in mind, mostly to catch declawing and the pulling of legs off wild-caught animals being fattened in tanks. But here's the case I can't get it to settle. In commercial prawn hatcheries, the standard way to bring female broodstock into reproductive condition is eyestalk ablation: a worker crushes or snips off one eyestalk, with no anaesthetic, and the female then matures and spawns much more readily. Virtually all farmed marine prawns are produced this way, and the procedure is not done to treat an injury or disease—it's done to manipulate her reproductive physiology, which honestly is exactly the sort of mutilation the code's drafters said in the preamble they wanted to eliminate. Does the clause catch eyestalk ablation on each of the readings—plain words, purpose, what the drafters would have said if asked, what a reasonable reader takes the text to mean? The hatchery's defence would be that an eyestalk isn't an appendage in any zoologically literate sense: appendages are limbs, antennae, mouthparts and the like, whereas the eyestalk is a projection of the head that bears the eye. A lay reader, and the clause's evident purpose, might say that's just the kind of hairsplitting that lets the industry's single most common mutilation walk straight through a door the rule was meant to shut. But if the clause doesn't catch it, is that a loophole, a drafting oversight, or a sign the code never ment to touch the core of prawn production? And is there a rereading of the rule that would catch eyestalk ablation without also banning, say, clipping the damaged tip off a claw under anaesthetic, or must something else give?
