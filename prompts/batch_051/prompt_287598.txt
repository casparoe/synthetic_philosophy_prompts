My roommate has been twisting me in knots with an argument and I can't find the exit. We're rewriting our co-op's house rules (I want the shared living room screen-free in the evenings, she wants to be able to practice drums after ten; we keep trading one concession for another), and she's an economics person who keeps saying our value conflict is just two preference schedules. This is the general version of what she wrote out:

(1) Whenever two people's value systems conflict, a compromise is just an exchange: each side gives up some item on its moral agenda that it weighs less in order to get an item it weighs more.
(2) A voluntary exchange where each side gives up the lower-weighted item for the higher-weighted item makes each side better off by its own lights.
(3) If every party to an exchange is better off by their own lights, the exchange has made no one involved worse off relative to their own values.
(4) The only thing that could make an all-around improvement like that wrong is harm or disrespect to someone outside the exchange.
(5) So the only principled limits on moral compromise are coercion, misinformation, and third-party harm. There is no internal objection to trading away any particular scruple, because whatever scruple you trade away must, by the very fact of the trade, be one you weigh less than what you gain.
(6) Therefore a well-informed, uncoerced compromise that leaves third parties untouched is not just permissible; each side would be irrational to refuse it, and the only difference between a principled compromise and selling out a principle is whether you got a good enough price.

But (6) is absurd. It commits me to saying that a person with an absolute deontological commitment — say, never lying — is making a bookkeeping error if she refuses a deal where she tells one small lie and the other side does something she cares about much more. And it means a pair of people who quietly enable each other's minor dishonesties, with no outside party ever harmed, are doing nothing wrong as long as each priced the exchange and came out ahead. I want to say she's turned ethics into a bunch of tastes without a remainder, but I can't name the step where that happens.

If I have to choose I'd rather keep the trade logic (1)-(4) than accept (6), though I suspect one of (1)-(4) is doing the funny buisness. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
