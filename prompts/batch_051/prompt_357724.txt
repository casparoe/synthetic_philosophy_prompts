I've been trying to compare different possible futures for our species—and different kinds of civilization we might someday detect elsewhere—by asking which one is "more advanced." The scale I see cited most often sorts civilizations by how much energy they can capture and put to use, with the familiar rungs being a whole planet's worth of energy, then a whole star's, then a whole galaxy's. But I recently came across a different way of ranking that ignores energy almost entirely and instead sorts civilizations by the smallest structures their technology can deliberately assemble or alter: breeding or engineering whole organisms, then manipulating individual cells, then molecules, then atoms, then atomic nuclei, and so on down toward whatever the physical limits permit. These two rankings don't always agree. A civilization could command an entire star's energy while doing only crude things with it, or it could manipulate single atoms while having only a modest energy supply. I keep going back and forth about which of them, if either, is actually measuring "advancement" rather than just measuring one ingredient of it. For my purposes, I care mostly about comparing the kinds of civilizations we might become or encounter, not about ranking historical human societies.

Before I read any more proposals, I'd like to know what I should be demanding of a scale like this. Please write out the requirements that any adequate measure of civilizational advancement would have to satisfy—the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must respect, and the physical or logical constraints it can't contradict. For each requirement, explain the consideration or example that forces it on us. Then tell me which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of people who happen to care most about energy, or miniaturization, or information, or some other metric. Also: if some of these requirements can't be satisfied together—for example, if a measure can't be a single number and still capture fundamentally different kinds of capability—say so, since that is the part I most need to understand. Finally, if you had to pick the single requirement that rules out the most existing proposals of this sort, which requirement is it? I'm more interested in the criteria and the cases behind them than in a scorecard of specific scales.
