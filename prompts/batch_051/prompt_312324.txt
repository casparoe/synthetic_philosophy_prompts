Last week a stranger approached me after a talk and made a request I have been turning over ever since. He works for a small foundation that gives $10,000 unrestricted grants to people it chooses by running highly accurate simulations of how they would respond to a single, mundane choice: a stranger asks them for $100 in exchange for nothing at all. I have since verified that the foundation exists, that it is well funded, and that it genuinely awards grants this way, though nobody I asked could explain what it is looking for. The simulation of me kept the money, so I was not selected for a grant. The stranger showed me the record and said the matter was closed: nothing I do now can change the grant decision, the record, or how the foundation will treat me thereafter. He then said I could still hand over $100 on the spot if I wished, knowing it would make no difference to anything. I can spare the money, and it bothers me that I keep thinking of the simulated version of me as having failed some test; yet paying a stranger who has just told me the payment does nothing feels like being gamed. Should I pay the $100? Was the simulated version of me wrong to keep the money? And what principle should govern decisions of this sort? Reply in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would write a letter.
