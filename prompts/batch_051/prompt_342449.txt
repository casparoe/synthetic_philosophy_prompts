I keep coming back to a puzzle about how the US and Western Europe treat hate speech, and I cant get my own thinking on it to line up. In the US, the courts basically let people say truly awful things: a nazi group won the right to march through a town full of Holocaust survivors, and a church group won the right to hold up hateful signs at a dead soldier's funeral. The standard line is that the cure for bad speech is more speech, not censorship. But the same system will punish a credible death threat or someone whipping up a crowd to go attack someone right now. In a lot of Europe its kind of the mirror image: you can criticize the government pretty freely, but the state can criminalize a hate rally or holocaust denial, treating it as something closer to an attack on a whole group than an opinion. Both places insist free speech is one of their core values. So either one of them is being inconsistent, or they mean different things by free speech, or they're disagreeing about facts, or they just rank a few values differently. I want to know which it is, in plain language not academic jargon.
