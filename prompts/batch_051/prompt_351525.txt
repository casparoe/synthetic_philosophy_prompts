I have spent the past several months trying to settle whether to keep eating meat, and I've come to think that what I'm really caught on is a version of the inefficacy objection: one person's action seems to make no difference to the size of a large collective harm, and yet we still want to say that some such actions take part in the harm. The argument for doing nothing says a single purchase changes nothing—no animal is raised or not raised because of it, no measurable amount of carbon enters the atmosphere—while the reply says that such a purchase is still a way of participating in a harmful practice. Both claims sound right to me depending on the day, and that suggests I don't actually possess a working definition of contribution; I've been using the word on the strength of intuitions I can't reconstruct.

I would like you to propose a working definition of contribution—what it takes for a particular action by a particular person to count as contributing to a large, causally diffuse collective harm whose individual effects are tiny or nonexistent. The definition should be precise enough to distinguish the clear cases from the clear non-cases, without being so demanding that it makes every ordinary action in an imperfect system count, or so lax that nothing ever does. Test it against these eight: purchasing a factory-farmed chicken breast at a supermarket; eating meat only when it would otherwise be thrown away; ordering a hamburger at a restaurant on a single occasion; holding units in a broad index fund that includes fossil-fuel companies; voting for a candidate whose platform includes a harmful policy in a district that candidate is certain to lose; casting the deciding vote in a small town where that candidate wins and the policy is enacted; taking one transatlantic round-trip flight each year to visit family; and driving a gasoline car a few miles each week when a bus is available. My own sense is that the supermarket chicken, the deciding vote, and the weekly driving are clearly contributions, while eating food that would otherwise be discarded clearly is not; I keep wavering on the restaurant hamburger, the index fund, the safe-district vote, and the annual flight.

Run the proposed definition through all eight cases, and tell me where it surprises you—where it returns a verdict I didn't expect, or where it declines to return a verdict at all. When it conflicts with my stated intuitions, say whether you would revise the definition to fit the cases or defend the surprising verdict instead.
