I recently became a moderator on a mid-sized discussion site built around a public karma score, and the moderation team keeps dividing over what the score is even for: some treat it as a record of contributions, others as a marker of community trust, others as an incentive mechanism to be tuned. I would like a way to find out which view the active membership actually holds without asking them to self-identify. Could you write an eight-to-ten-question sorting quiz, each question a concrete moderation case with two or three answer options, and a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these four positions: karma as a contribution ledger, karma as community standing or trust, karma as an incentive mechanism to be optimized for behavior, and a deflationary view that no single public number can serve all of these functions and that the score should not be given much weight. Please draw the cases from situations like a bot that posts useful original material and earns high karma, a member who runs several accounts that each upvote the others’ harmless posts, a high-karma old-timer whose inside jokes get upvoted regardless of substance, a user who deletes heavily downvoted comments to protect their score, an account that reposts good content from elsewhere, and a downvote-only account. Make sure the questions sort people by how they judge the cases rather than by which label they prefer, and flag any positions or answer combinations the scoring key cannot reliably separate.
