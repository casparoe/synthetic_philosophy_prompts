Please explain a conceptual issue about the real numbers and the continuum. It is often said that Dedekind and Cantor *defined* the real numbers, but also that Cantor *discovered* uncountability. What exactly is at stake in the distinction between discovered and defined here? In particular, if the real numbers are simply stipulated to be the complete ordered field, why should the existence of uncountably many reals count as a discovery rather than as a theorem that follows from a definition? Does the fact that our formal characterisations (Dedekind cuts, Cauchy sequences, or the complete ordered field axioms) each seem to capture the same continuum suggest that they are picking out something that was already there, or merely that the different constructions are mutually interpretable? I am not asking for a historical summary, but for a precise conceptual analysis of whether continuity is something we invent by definition or something we discover about a pre-existing mathematical structure.
