I'm a retired geophysicist who spent a career doing Monte Carlo inversions, so in the lab "random" was operational: a sequence was random if it came out of a generator we trusted and didn't fail the tests we bothered to run. Lately I've been reading philosophy of probability and the algorithmic information theory side of things—Kolmogorov complexity, Martin-Löf tests, the idea that randomness is about not having a shorter description. What I can't get a handle on is whether any of that says anything about a finite string, like the 100-bit runs I used to look at every day. The formal definitions all seem aimed at infinite sequences, and it's not clear to me how they're supposed to apply to a finite batch of bits on its own.

So give me a working definition of "random" for a finite binary string that sorts the following cases, or argue that some of them shouldn't be sorted. The cases: (a) 010101...01, the pair 01 repeated fifty times, which I actualy got once by flipping an honest coin and have never quite believed; (b) 000...0, a hundred zeros, emitted by a student's broken generator—nobody would call that random; (c) a 100-bit string from a hardware generator based on quantum shot noise, roughly 50% ones and no pattern any of us could find—nobody would call that anything but random; (d) a string bit-for-bit identical to (c), but this time produced deterministically by a linear congruential generator I seeded myself; (e) the first hundred binary digits of π, deterministic and generated by a short formula, though I'm told they'd pass ordinary statistical tests. My gut says origin matters, so (c) is random and (d) isn't, but (a) makes me doubt that, since flipping a coin gave me the most compressible string imaginable. For (e) I don't know what I think.

I'd also like the definition to say where the cutoff between random-enough and not-random-enough comes from for a finite string, and whether "random" ends up being a property of the string itself, its history, or our ability to compress or predict it. If the right move is to refuse a yes/no answer and treat finite-string randomness as a matter of degree, say what the degree is and how short is too short for the concept to apply at all.
