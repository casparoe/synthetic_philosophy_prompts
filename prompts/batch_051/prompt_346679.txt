I've spent way to much time trying to get a working definition of "valid consent" — not the legal doctrine, and not the performative stuff (explicit asking, enthusiastic yes, ongoing checking in, all that), but whatever is supposed to do the actual moral work: the thing that marks the line between sex and assault, surgery and battery, borrowing and theft. My ordinary use of the term feels confident, but every definition I try either springs a leak or commits me to a verdict I dont want. I'd like you to propose a definition and run it against my list, saying where it gives the wrong answer or no answer at all, and then either revise it or defend the surprising verdicts.

To head off the usual dance: I suspect part of the problem is that consent can be read as a mental state, a speech act, or a bit of moral status, and my definitions keep sliding between these, so tell me which one your definition picks or how it combines them.

Clear yes:
- Two sober adults plainly agree to a specific activity, nothing relevant is misrepresented, and either could decline with no material or social penalty.
- A couple with years of established patterns, one asks, the other says yes with no pressure at all.

Clear no:
- A yes at gunpoint.
- A yes from someone so drunk they can't track what they're agreeing to.
- A ten-year-old's yes.

Borderline cases (this is where definitions tend to die):
1. The boss: an employee says yes because their manager keeps implying the good shifts depend on it, though no threat is ever stated outright.
2. The vasectomy lie: he says he's had a vasectomy, he hasn't, and she consents to sex on that basis.
3. The résumé lie: she says she's single and a doctor, and he wouldn't have consented if he'd known she was married, but the sex itself is exactly the activity he agreed to.
4. Stealthing: they agree to sex with a condom, and midway he takes it off without her noticing.
5. Blackout enthusiasm: the person remembers nothing the next day, but at the time was articulate, initiating, and gave every observable sign of consent.
6. The blackmail yes: sleep with me or I send those photos to your family. Obviously awful, but I want to know whether this is invalid consent or valid consent under duress — and what hangs on the difference.
7. The undercover cop: an activist sleeps with someone who turns out to be a police officer infiltrating her group; she would never have consented had she known.
8. The boxing/BDSM case: someone consents to being genuinely hurt inside a structure with rules and a way to stop, and the hurt is exactly as expected. What, if anything, would outrun the consent in a case like this?

Meta-constraint: I want a definition where valid consent isn't just a synonym for "the whole encounter was morally fine all things considered." If your definition ends up implying that most real-world sex is technically invalid, or that unenthusiastic-but-willing sex counts as assault, say so plainly instead of gerrymandering the definition to avoid it. I'd rather have an honest but weird concept than a fake boundary.
