I recently spent a long evening arguing with a friend about whether a wrongdoing carried out in the world is graver than the same wrongdoing fully consented to but never performed. We each had real points, but we kept retreating into slogans rather than working out what, if anything, the outward deed adds. Write a dialogue between two equally competent medieval thinkers: one defending Abelard's thesis that sin consists wholly in consenting to what the agent believes is contempt of God, and the other defending the view that the external act and its effects also contribute to the gravity of the sin. Stage the exchange around a concrete pair of cases: two people consent to the same betrayal, one dying before he can act and the other carrying it out. The Abelardian should be made to say what remains different between the two cases and why that difference is not a difference in sin; the other should be made to say what feature of the performed deed makes the sin graver without quietly adopting the Abelardian's position once the consequences are set aside. Have them respond to each other's actual arguments, revise a formulation when it fails, and refuse to caricature one another. If the opposition as framed is confused, or turns out to be more verbal than substantial, say so plainly rather than writing the dialogue anyway.
