Most of us would call a state that turns away a boat of people fleeing death squads a failure of basic decency, but we do not call a state callous for turning away a group fleeing a drought that has destroyed their crops and drinking water, even though both groups face death and neither chose their circumstances. The modern refugee system codifies this asymmetry: it protects those with a well-founded fear of persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or social group, while those fleeing famine, drought, or general economic collapse are routinely classed as voluntary migrants who may be refused.
