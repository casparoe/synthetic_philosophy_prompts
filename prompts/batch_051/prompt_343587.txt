Exam question:

Simone de Beauvoir holds that one is not born a woman but becomes one. Explain this claim. Your answer should address: (a) what she is denying when she says this; (b) what she thinks accounts for a person becoming a woman; and (c) whether her position is best described as the claim that gender is socially constructed.

Student answer:

Beauvoir's point is to distinguish sex from gender. Sex is the biological given — chromosomes, anatomy, hormone profiles, reproductive role — while gender is the social meaning a culture attaches to those biological differences. Saying that one is not born a woman but becomes one means that "woman" is a gender category rather than a sex category: no one is born with a female psychology, a maternal instinct, or an innate preference for femininity. Those are produced through socialization — through the way girls are raised, the expectations placed on women, and the roles a society makes available to them. Her view should therefore be described as the claim that gender is a social construct. She also thinks this becoming happens through the individual's own choices: each person becomes a woman by taking up the norms of femininity, defining herself against the expectations of others, and constituting herself through her own projects. That is why the distinction matters politically: if womanhood were fixed by biology, it could not be changed, but because it is produced, it can be remade.
