Aristotle distinguishes four ways of saying why something is as it is: the material cause, the formal cause, the efficient cause, and the final cause. In *Physics* II.8, he argues that nature acts for the sake of an end, and offers this remark: if the ship-building art were in the wood, the wood would act just as nature does. The claim is that the end-directedness we see in craftsmanship—where the form of the finished ship explains the order of the steps—is equally present in natural processes, except that in nature the principle of change is internal to the thing rather than residing in an artisan.
