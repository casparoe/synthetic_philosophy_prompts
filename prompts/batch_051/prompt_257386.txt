I am a doctoral candidate finishing the first chapter of my dissertation on the ontology of recorded music, and before the chapter goes to my committee I want an audit of the position I have committed myself to in writing. I have arrived at the following seven claims, over three years of coursework and a long argument with my advisor about whether a multitracked studio artifact and a live performance can be instances of the same kind of thing:

(1) A musical work is an abstract type, not any particular performance, score-copy, or state of a listener's mind, and a performance is of that work precisely by instantiating the type.
(2) Works are brought into existence by their composers' acts of composition; they are not eternal entities waiting to be discovered.
(3) A work is individuated by the instructions the composer prescribes (the score, broadly construed), so a performance can be more or less faithful to the work, and at some point a departure is so great that we no longer have a performance of that work at all.
(4) A studio recording, however heavily edited, overdubbed, or spliced, is still just a way of fixing and transmitting a performance of the work, not a further work in its own right.
(5) A cover in a very different arrangement—say, a bossa-nova version of a rock song—is a performance of the same work provided the melody and lyrics remain recognizably intact.
(6) A free improvisation that follows no pre-existing instructions is not a musical work at all, though it may be an admirable musical event.
(7) Some works—certain electronic or tape pieces—exist only as recordings and never did or could exist as live performances; these are nonetheless musical works.

I cannot tell which of these merely look like tensions and which are real, and I worry the set is stable only because I have not traced these commitments as far as they go. Every time I try to relieve one pair, another goes taut. Please do not offer me a survey of the literature; I want a committed consistency check. Go through the claims, identify the genuine tensions, separate them from apparent tensions that dissolve once the relevant distinction is drawn, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say exactly what would have to be given up and at what cost. Then tell me which single revision, if any, restores the greatest consistency with the least damage to the position, and what residual instability would remain. Take a definite position where the evidence permits one, and do not leave me with a menu of options.
