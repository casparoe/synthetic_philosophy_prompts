A physicist in our reading group kept leaning on Wigner's slogan about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics to argue that elegant mathematics is a pretty good guide to what's physically true — like, if an equation is beautiful, that's some evidence the world actually behaves that way. When I pushed back he said, well, what else could Wigner mean? I think the slogan can be read several different ways, and most of them don't get him his conclusion. Can you lay out the distinct claims the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics might amount to — e.g. a historical claim about the track record, a claim about applicability versus predictive success, a claim about math developed with no physical motivation, a claim about how indispensable math is to physics — and say for each whether it's plausible and whether it actually supports the beauty-is-a-guide move? I want the menu of readings with verdicts, more than a single correct interpretation.
