heres the thing I keep going in circles on. I want a working defintion of 'concept' — not the dictionary kind, but the kind where I can say whether someone has the concept of dog or of justice or of offside, and what having it consists in. I roughly know the standard candidates: concepts as prototypes (a weighted summary of typical features), concepts as stored exemplars you compare new cases against, concepts as mini-theories that explain and predict. I can't get any one of those to fit all the cases I care about, so I want you to give me your best definition and run it through the list, case by case. If it gives a weird verdict on one of them, either revise it or defend the weird verdict.

The cases:

- a normal adult's concept DOG. I sort dogs from non-dogs fast, can reason about what a dog is likely to do, but couldnt produce necessary and sufficient conditions if you paid me.
- GAME. It looks like a respectable concept, but it's not obvious anything is common to solitaire, chess, charades, and the game of politics.
- a parrot trained to squawk 'red' when you show it a red chip. I want to say no concept there, but I'd like the definition to say why.
- a toddler who overextends 'dog' to sheep, cats, and a small horse. Concept already, or a not-yet-concept?
- BACHELOR as unmarried adult male. This seems definitional until you hit the Pope, or a divorced guy, or a man married to a man.
- WATER. I use the concept fine and can't tell you its chemical structure; chemists can. Is H2O part of my concept, their concept, or the concept?
- PHLOGISTON. It picked out nothing, sat inside a now-dead theory, and still did real work. Do the chemists who used it have a concept or not?
- a kid who sees exactly one zebra at the zoo and then uses 'zebra' correctly for zebras in books. If concepts are stored exemplars that's easy; if they are summaries one example shouldnt be enough; if they are theories the kid has none. So what's going on?
- OFFSIDE in soccer. I get it right maybe 60% of the time and couldnt state the rule. Partial concept, fuzzy concept, no concept?
- a fear of dogs: I flinch around dogs but can't tell you much about them and can't recognize the ones that don't look like the dog that bit me. Clearly not a concept, but again, why not?

After you've gone through those, I'd also like the one-paragraph version: is 'concept' actually one kind of thing, or is it a cover word for several different things that I shouldn't force into a single definition?
