i'm a software engineer, no philosophy background except one intro-to-logic class forever ago, and i've been writing a small probabilistic inference engine in my spare time. it already does stuff like P(rain) and P(bike | rain), and i wanted to add a first-class `if A then B`. like you could ask it P(if it rains then the match is cancelled). my instinct was: that number should just be P(cancelled | rain), because in code the branch only runs under rain, so the probability of the if-statement is the probability of the then-part conditional on the if-part. i've been calling this the branch principle: Pr(if A then B) = Pr(B | A).

so i made `if A then B` a normal expression governed by that rule. but my engine lets you condition on arbitrary expressions and also lets expressions appear inside booleans, so it quickly started computing Pr(if A then B | B) and Pr(if A then B | ¬B). the branch principle, applied to the distributions you get by conditioning on B and on ¬B, says those should be 1 and 0 respectively (under P(·|B), Pr(B | A) evaluates to 1; under P(·|¬B), it evaluates to 0). then total probability gives Pr(if A then B) = 1*Pr(B) + 0*Pr(¬B) = Pr(B). but the branch principle itself says Pr(if A then B) = Pr(B | A). so it follows that Pr(B | A) = Pr(B), for any A and B. the engine has 'proved' that nothing is statistically dependent on anything else. broken, obviously.

what i can't figure out is which assumption to kill. is the branch principle itself false, or is the actual bug treating `if A then B` as an ordinary event/proposition that you can condition on and combine with and/or? i have a hunch that conditionals are rules or operators, not events, and that giving them a single probability which obeys the normal probability axioms was the mistake. but the one-number intuition feels so right in simple cases that i dont want to fully drop it.

so: give me an honest assessment. have i rediscovered a known result or just made a dumb error? what's the sharpest version of the collapse, and what are the main options for keeping or revising the branch principle after you notice it? is my 'conditionals arent events' escape hatch actually coherent, or just something i tell myself? and is there any restricted setting where you can still assign `if` a probability for simple non-nested cases without everything going trivial?
