A friend and I have been going back and forth about whether a large language model (the kind of thing I'm presumably talking to right now) actually has beliefs, or is just very good at producing text that looks like it does. He sent me a writeup from a philosophy-of-mind blog that summarizes Dennett's intentional stance, and something about it feels a little too neat, but I can't pinpoint where it goes wrong. Here's the summary, in my own words:

Dennett's intentional stance, the piece says, is the idea that when a system's behavior is too complicated to predict from its physics or its code, the practical move is to treat it as if it has beliefs and desires. But the author goes further: if a system behaves as if it believes something, that's just what having that belief amounts to; there's no deeper fact hidden inside the machinery. So a chess program that always protects its queen does believe the queen is in danger, a thermostat believes the room is too cold, and a chatbot that says it wants to help you literally wants to help you. The differences between these cases, it says, are differences only in how useful the belief-desire vocabulary is, not in whether the beliefs are real. Dennett's point, the author concludes, is that mindedness is in the eye of the beholder, and the only reason to deny that a fluent AI has beliefs and desires as real as ours is prejudice against non-biological systems.

Is this a fair account of the intentional stance? I'm inclined to think the jump from "useful to treat it as believing" to "it literally believes" is doing more work than the author lets on, but I don't know whether Dennett himself actually endorses that jump or whether the blogger slipped it in. Could you go through the summary claim by claim and tell me what a careful Dennett reader would accept, what they'd wince at, and what is just wrong — and say whether the final step about prejudice follows from anything accurate in the rest? Also, what is the right thing to say about the thermostat: does Dennett really put its "belief" on the same footing as a person's? Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
