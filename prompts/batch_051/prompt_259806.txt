I cover AI and behavioral science for a general-interest magazine, and I keep runing into a claim that feels like old philosophy wearing new clothes. Early in Book 4 of Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation, he argues—I'm paraphrasing, not quoting—that all willing comes from a felt lack, and that each satisfied wish immediately produces a new wish, so the underlying condition is perpetual striving rather than any lasting contentment. The modern version I keep noticing: in reinforcement learning and product design, a system or user reaches a goal, registers a brief reward, then immediately forms a new goal, so objective scores keep rising but the felt condition of done never arrives.

I know hedonic treadmill and adaptation-level theory are nearby, and I know wanting versus liking is its own thing in neuroscience. But what I'm after is narrower: is there a name for the idea that satisfaction is internally structured to generate a new lack, not just that we get used to a new baseline? If there isn't an exact label, what would you call it, and how would you separate it from the treadmill and from ordinary preference drift? I'm less interested in the label itself than in where the boundaries are.
