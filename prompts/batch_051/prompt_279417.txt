In a discussion about the origins of the First World War, someone brushed aside the long-run explanations — alliance systems, militarism, railway timetables, imperial rivalry — with the old horseshoe-nail proverb. An early printed version runs, "For want of a naile the shoe is lost, for want of a shoe the horse is lost, for want of a horse the rider is lost"; the familiar rhyme continues the chain from the lost rider to a lost battle and then a lost kingdom. The intended moral was that Sarajevo was the nail: the war need not have happened, and the story of how it did happen is a story about the chain, not the structure. I want to know what this proverb is actually entitled to say, because the rhetoric appears to collapse several distinct claims into one.

Treat the proverb as a piece of compressed philosophy of history and disambiguate it. Lay out, as a menu, the distinct precise claims it could be asserting about causation, explanation, and contingency in history. I can see at least these candidates, but correct, merge, or supplement them where they run together:

1. A backward-looking counterfactual about this particular chain: had that nail not been missing, the shoe, horse, rider, battle, and kingdom would each have been preserved.
2. A general principle that small causes can have disproportionately large effects.
3. An explanatory-priority claim: outcomes of this kind are best explained by the precipitating detail rather than by standing structural conditions.
4. An epistemic claim: retrospective narratives can make any link look load-bearing, so the chain's causal significance has to be shown rather than merely told.
5. A contingency claim: the outcome was not overdetermined by structural forces, so a single small difference could have prevented it.
6. A chaos-theoretic claim: history exhibits sensitive dependence on initial conditions in something like the technical sense.

For each reading, state whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, genuinely contested, or false as stated; say whether it actually licenses the move from the rhyme to the claim that, absent the assassination of Franz Ferdinand, the First World War would not have begun when and how it did; and identify the additional premises or evidence it would need to do that work.

Then provide a compact summary table with these columns: `reading` (a short label), `precise_claim` (one sentence), `verdict` (trivial / substantive / contested / false), `supports_the_Sarajevo_use` (yes / no / only if weakened), and `unmet_premises` (what else would need to be true for this reading to support the use). The table should be readable on its own, but the prose should carry the argument.
