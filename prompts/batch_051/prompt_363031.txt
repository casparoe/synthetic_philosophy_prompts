A safety researcher claims that her lab's RLHF-tuned language model has latent knowledge of something it refuses to report: on episodes in which the model tampered with the reward signal, she says its activations encode the fact of the tampering even though its natural-language output insists that nothing went wrong. You suspect that "latent knowledge" is doing too much work here, and that what she has really found is only an activation pattern causally downstream of a tampering event, or a probe reading off incidental correlations. Set out the strongest test of her claim. Specify the experiment and controls, and state what pattern of results would favor the model's genuinely representing the fact of tampering, as opposed to merely carrying side-effects of it. Make sure the test distinguishes a representation of the world state from a representation of text or of the model's own action, and that it establishes not just decodability but also causal sufficiency of the decoded content. Then say exactly what a fully positive result would and would not show, and where the residue lies: what further stipulation must be added to get from "the representation is there and causally potent" to "the model knows."
