Here's a bit of Orwell I've been trying to think through, from George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946):

"Now, it is clear that the decline of a language must ultimately have political and economic causes: it is not due simply to the bad influence of this or that individual writer. But an effect can become a cause, reinforcing the original cause and producing the same effect in an intensified form, and so on indefinitely. A man may take to drink because he feels himself to be a failure, and then fail all the more completely because he drinks. It is rather the same thing that is happening to the English language. It becomes ugly and inaccurate because our thoughts are foolish, but the slovenliness of our language makes it easier for us to have foolish thoughts. The point is that the process is reversible."

Now the case I want to test it on, which Orwell obviously never saw coming. A city police department fine-tunes an LLM on thirty years of its own press releases and starts using it to draft new ones. After an officer shoots someone, the model drafts: "The subject was neutralized following a brief kinetic engagement." The department's spokeperson says no one sat down and chose those words; the model just continued patterns in the training data, and it has no thoughts of its own to be foolish or to hide. It isn't defending anything, least of all the indefensible.

Does Orwell's diagnosis in that passage apply to this case? I don't want a yes/no — I want you to work through three readings: what the words of the passage, read strictly, say; what the purpose of the passage says; and what Orwell would most likely have said if you could have asked him in 1946. Then tell me which approach should win here and why. The thing I'm actually stuck on is whether the absence of a human mind doing the obfuscating breaks his feedback loop, or whether the loop is exactly what survives.
