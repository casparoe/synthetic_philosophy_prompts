Exam question. State the central commitments of logicism, formalism, and intuitionism as philosophies of mathematics, and identify at least one issue over which they genuinely disagree (for example, the status of the law of excluded middle, the nature of mathematical objects, or the role of consistency proofs).

Student answer. Logicism (Frege, Russell) holds that mathematics is reducible to logic: numbers are logical objects (for Frege, extensions of concepts), and mathematical truths are analytic truths derivable from logical axioms. Russell’s paradox showed that Frege’s original system was inconsistent, and Russell’s theory of types was intended to repair the derivation. Formalism (Hilbert) holds that mathematics is the manipulation of symbols according to explicit rules. Since the symbols themselves are meaningless, the only requirement on a formalization is consistency; this is why Hilbert sought consistency proofs, and on this view any consistent system is as good as any other, with applications being a separate empirical matter. Intuitionism (Brouwer) holds that mathematical objects are mental constructions and that a statement is true only if a proof or construction exists. Intuitionists therefore reject the law of excluded middle, at least for infinite domains, and require constructive existence proofs. Because mathematical objects are mental, Brouwer’s position is a form of psychologism: whether a theorem holds depends on what happens to be in a given mathematician’s mind.
