In Plato’s Republic, Book II, Glaucon asks Socrates to place justice in the second of three classes of goods—things valued both for their own sake and for what comes from them—and then presses a harder demand: he wants to hear justice itself praised, without mention of its rewards. To force the issue, he tells the story of the ring of Gyges, by which a just man could act with the same impunity as an unjust one.

Write an essay about Glaucon’s challenge. The essay should separate the distinct claims that might be intended when someone says that the ring of Gyges shows that no one is just willingly: a psychological claim about what anyone would do, a normative claim about what one would have reason to do, and a claim about what justice really is. For each, assess whether the ring story actually supports it, whether it is plausible, and whether Socrates’ later defense of justice in the Republic can answer it without changing the subject. Reach and justify a conclusion about which version of the challenge is the strongest.
