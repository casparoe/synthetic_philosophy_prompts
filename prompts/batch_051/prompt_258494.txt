I ended up with the version below, and I keep swinging between thinking it is basically sound and thinking one of the steps quietly does more than it should. The argument, as I have it: (1) If a mental state type like pain is identical with a physical state type, then every instance of pain must share one and the same physical state type. (2) Pain is multiply realizable: a human with C-fiber firing, an octopus with a completely different nervous system, and a hypothetical silicon system with no neurons at all could all be in the same mental state, pain, without sharing any single physical state type. (3) So pain is not identical with any physical state type. (4) The best explanation of this multiple realizability is that mental states are functional state types, individuated by their causal role—what typically causes them, what they typically cause, and how they interact with other mental states—rather than by what physically realizes them. (5) Therefore pain is a functional state type, not a physical one, and the mind–brain identity theory is false.
