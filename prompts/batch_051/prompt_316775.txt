I cover a science and ideas beat, and I keep running into what feels like the same wrinkle in conversations about what things would do, and I suspect there's a real distinction hiding in it. Let me give you my two favorite cases.

We have a reagent that is light-sensitive. Shine a UV lamp at the vial it came in and nothing happens, but only because the vial is amber glass, which absorbs the wavelengths that would set the stuff off. Pour it into a clear vial and it reacts right away. So the sensitivity was there all along; something was just standing in the way of its showing up.

Now a different case. A piece of lab equipment would give you a nasty shock if you touched its terminals, except it has a protection circuit that senses the kind of load a human finger makes and cuts the voltage to nearly zero just before contact. So every time anyone actually touches it, it's harmless. But here you can't say the danger is still there and merely blocked; the thing genuinely stops being dangerous in the very act of being tested.

These feel like two different ways a thing can fail to do what it "would" do, and I keep wanting distinct words for them. In the first case the tendency survives the test but is shielded; in the second, the test itself removes the tendency before it can manifest. Is there an established name for this distinction? If there is, I'd like more than the name—how do you tell the cases apart when you can't look inside the system, and where does the boundary get fuzzy in messy real examples? If there isn't an established name, how would you carve it up in a way that doesn't collapse the two cases into each other?
