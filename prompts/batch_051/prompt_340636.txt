Me and some friends run a small giving circle, and our operating agreement has a clause that says: "The committee shall allocate funds to the proposal with the greatest expected value, where expected value is the probability of the proposal's success multiplied by the value of its outcome."

We just got an application from someone who wants $2,000 to investigate whether a particular kind of unproven quantum-vacuum interaction could, with a probability somewhere around 1 in 10^50, prevent a large fraction of all future suffering across the reachable universe. He's upfront that the odds are an upper bound at best; by his own numbers the outcome would be at least 10^60 times more valuable than a year's output from a normal bednet charity. So the expected value is enormous, even if the probability is basically zero for all practical purposes. Everyone involved agrees the clause was written with ordinary charitable projects in mind, where you can at least get a credible probability estimate. I know this is basically Pascal's mugging, and that's kind of the point.

Are we required to fund him under the clause? I care less about the practical answer than about how the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the drafters (us) would've said if asked at the time, and what a reasonable reader would take it to require each handle this case. And I'm curious whether the clause can be patched to exclude Pascal's-mugging-style applications without also excluding legitimate low-probability, high-value research.
