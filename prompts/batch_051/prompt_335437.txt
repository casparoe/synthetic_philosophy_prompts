My friend has a weirdly consistent policy about which group's rate you should use when someone gives a probability for a single event. Her past calls: for a specific patient's odds of an infection after a knee replacement, she said use that hospital's infection rate, not the national one; for a specific used car's odds of a major repair next year, she said use the repair rate for that make, model, and year, not for all cars; for a specific first-year student's odds of finishing college, she said use the graduation rate for that kind of college, not for all colleges; but for a specific person's odds of getting a rare cancer, she said use the population-wide rate, not the rate for people with their exact family history. Now she's weighing in on a specific 25-year-old's odds of finding a job within a year of moving to a new city. The rates on the table are (a) recent arrivals in that city, (b) all recent arrivals nationally, and (c) recent arrivals with the same visa type and education level, which is a much smaller group. Which one would she pick? More important, what rule is she actually running, which of her old calls is the closest analog, and if her past calls underdetermine it, what extra case would settle the matter?
