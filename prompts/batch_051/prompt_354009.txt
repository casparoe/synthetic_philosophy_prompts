I'm taking a course on the philosophy of probability and computation, and I've run into a tension I can't resolve. The instructor says that Kolmogorov complexity gives us a language-independent way to measure how "random" or compressible a finite binary string is, at least up to an additive constant. But she also says that in the rigorous sense introduced by Martin-Löf, no finite string is random at all: randomness is a property of infinite sequences, not finite ones. I don't see how to hold both claims together. If I can always just choose a programming language in which my particular string has a very short description, then isn't the apparent randomness of any finite string simply an artifact of the language I adopt, and isn't the question "can a finite sequence be random?" therefore empty? What is the question really asking, and is there any sense in which the answer to it is yes?
