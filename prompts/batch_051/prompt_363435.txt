Here is an argument about coherent extrapolated volition (CEV), the proposal that an aligned AI should pursue what humanity would want after ideal reflection — where ideal reflection is understood roughly as knowing more, reasoning better, wanting what we would want to want, and having developed together rather than separately.

(1) For any individual, the output of ideal reflection is by construction a coherent set of preferences; the whole point of extrapolating is to repair the inconsistencies and distortions in stated and revealed preferences.

(2) If an AI is to pursue humanity's CEV, then there must be a single coherent ordering of outcomes that counts as what humanity-after-ideal-reflection wants.

(3) Ideal reflection, however, is an operation on a subject, and when applied to each person separately it gives no guarantee of convergence: two ideally reflective people may remain fully informed, internally coherent, and in conflict, because they rank outcomes by different terminal values.

(4) So moving from the ideal preferences of individuals to a single ordering for all of humanity requires some rule for combining possibly conflicting ideal preferences across persons.

(5) Any such combining rule — averaging, voting, bargaining, giving each person a veto, giving lexical priority to the worst off, and so on — makes substantive choices about whose ideal preferences matter and how much; these choices are not themselves read off any individual's ideal preferences.

(6) Therefore "humanity's CEV" is either not a single coherent target at all, or it is defined only after a value-laden choice among aggregation rules — in which case CEV does not by itself settle what an AI should maximize.
