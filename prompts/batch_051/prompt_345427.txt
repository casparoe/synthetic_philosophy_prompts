My friend Dana has these super firm opinions about when somebody has actually treated another person like a person rather than just doing the right moves, and I've been collecting her verdicts because I can't find the line. Here's what I've got so far:

1. A paid home-health aide who's always on time, does the meds, changes the sheets, but never looks up from her phone. Dana says no: she did the errands; she didn't take care of him.

2. A checkout clerk who says "have a good one" to two hundred people a day without thinking about any of them. Dana says that's completely fine; you'd have to be insane to want two hundred real emotions from a guy selling you gum.

3. Her brother who visits their mother every Sunday for an hour but spends the visit scrolling his phone. Dana says that's worse than not visiting; he's turning her into an appointment.

4. A friend who came to see me in the hospital, brought food I couldn't eat and forgot my allergy, but sat with me all afternoon. Dana says that's the one that counts; she was with me.

5. A manager who tells an employee "I appreciate you" and gives her a bonus, but privately calls her replaceable and refuses her request to shift her schedule so she can pick up her kid. Dana says that's not respect, it's buttering a turkey before you eat it.

6. A rushed waiter who's short with us and never smiles but gets the whole order right. Dana says fine; you weren't there for his soul.

Now the one I actually want to bring to her. An old woman I know has a caregiver from an agency. The caregiver is warm, remembers the woman's stories, calls her by name, never rushes her; the old woman says it's the first time in years anyone has treated her like a person. But I found out later that the caregiver described the job to another worker as "I just do the warm-granddaughter act, that's what they hire you for—its no different than memorizing the med list." The old woman doesn't know that. What would Dana say about whether the caregiver actually respected the old woman? What rule is Dana running? And if there's more than one rule that fits her old verdicts, what's a case that would break the tie?
