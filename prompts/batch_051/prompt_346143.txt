So I'm a PhD student TAing an intro logic course, and we're on Frege right now. A student came to my office hours pretty rattled. He'd been reading around beyond the syllabus and found out that Frege kept a private diary near the end of his life — written in the mid-1920s, I think, and only published decades later — and in it he says some genuinely vile stuff. I won't quote it, but the rough content is what you'd expect: antisemitic remarks about Jews as a foreign element inside Germany, contempt for democracy and universal suffrage, and a hope that some strong leader would take charge and set things right by force. My student says he can't look at the Begriffsschrift the same way now. His argument is roughly this: Frege's whole project was built on the idea that logic is objective and universal, not a psychological description of how people happen to think, and not contaminated by whoever is doing the thinking — and yet the same mind that gave us the quantifier also wrote that. He thinks either Frege's philosophy is secretly shot through with the same political poison, or at minimum studying it without mentioning the diary is a kind of whitewashing. My first instinct is to say the man is seperate from the arguments — you don't have to like Frege as a person to evaluate whether an inference is valid. But then he asked me whether I'd assign the diary alongside sense and reference in any course, and I didn't have a good answer. I'm supposed to lead a section on "On Sense and Reference" next week, and now I'm the one who's unsettled. What should I tell this student, and is he actually onto something about the philosophy, or is that a category mistake?
