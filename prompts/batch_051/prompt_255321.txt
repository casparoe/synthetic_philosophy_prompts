I've been trying to get clear on something about freeganism and rescued animals, and I keep going in circles. The standard vegan argument, as I understand it, is that buying animal products creates demand, and demand is what causes more animals to be bred and killed. So buying eggs or meat makes your partly responsible for the harm. Fine. But here's the case I can't figure out: eggs from rescued hens in a backyard sanctuary. The hens are going to lay the eggs anyway, the eggs would just rot if nobody ate them, and nobody is paying into the system. Same with dumpster-dived meat that a grocery store already threw out: the animal is already dead, the harm already happened, and eating it doesn't send any signal back to the farmer. So on the demand-based logic, it seems like there's nothing wrong with it.

But a lot of vegans still think it's wrong, and the reason they give is something like: it treats the hen's body as a machine that exists to make stuff for us, or it keeps alive the idea that animals and their products are food. I don't really understand what kind of wrongness that is. It sounds almost symbolic. Is "commodifying" an animal the same kind of moral wrong as harming it, or is it a different category entirely? And how do you draw the line between using a byproduct that would otherwise be wasted and actually participating in the harm — does it matter whether other people see me eat it, whether I'd do it in private, or whether I could have prevented the harm in the first place? I want someone to untangle the concepts here: the difference between causing a harm, contributing to it through demand, and just benefiting from a harm that would have happened anyway. Where does the rescued-egg case land, and is the "it keeps commodification alive" objection doing real moral work or is it just a vibe?
