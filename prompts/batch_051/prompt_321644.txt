I'm teaching a short unit on the distinction between knowing how and knowing that, and I keep coming back to The Karate Kid as a test case the philosophical literature seems not to have worked over. The famous training sequence is the interesting part: Mr. Miyagi has Daniel sand the walkway, wax cars, and paint a fence, deliberately withholding any explanation of how these chores connect to karate, and Daniel eventually blows up because he has been given no propositional account of what he is practicing. Then the payoff arrives: the motions turn out to have been components of blocking techniques all along, and Daniel can execute them without ever having represented them as such. The film appears to be doing real work for the anti-intellectualist side of the debate. Daniel's know-how does not merely temporally precede his knowing-that; the film suggests the bodily mastery is what makes the later understanding possible, and that an explanation offered earlier would have undermined the training.

But that appearance may rest on a slide from a weak pedagogical claim to a strong conceptual one. The weak claim is only that some skills are best acquired by practicing movements before, or instead of, being told their rationale. The strong claim is that there is a distinctive kind of knowledge, knowing how, not reducible to or dependent on knowing that, such that Daniel-after-practice and someone who received a full explanation differ in what they know, while the explanation-only person lacks something in a different way. What I want is a careful reading of the film as a position in this debate: where does it actually take a stand, and where does it cheat by conflating motivated practice with motor conditioning, or conditioned reflex with intelligent skill? Does the film's own drama undercut the anti-intellectualist reading, since Daniel is treated as entitled to an explanation and the withheld rationale is precisely what damages his trust? And how would the intellectualist read the moment where the movements click into place: does that moment smuggle in a piece of knowing-that as the thing that finally makes the practice intelligible? I suspect the film is more interesting than a simple vindication of either side, but I want an honest verdict rather than a flattering one.
