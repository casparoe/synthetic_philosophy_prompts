I'm taking a course on formal epistemology this term, and we've been working through the problem of how to revise a probability distribution when the new information arrives as a conditional probability rather than as an unconditional one. I'd like to think through a concrete proposal for a forecasting group rather than the abstract question.

The standard example — often called the Judy Benjamin problem — runs like this. A soldier is dropped into one of four sectors: Red Headquarters, Red Second Company, Blue Headquarters, Blue Second Company. She initially assigns probability 1/4 to each sector. She then receives a message she treats as certain: if she is in Red territory, the probability that she is in the Headquarters sector is 3/4. The puzzle is how she should revise her probabilities, and in particular whether P(Red territory) should change, given that the message only concerns a conditional.

Now consider the following rule for a forecasting group that maintains a single shared probability distribution over a finite set of propositions. Any member may submit a report of the form P(B|A) = q. When a report is submitted, the shared distribution is replaced by the distribution that minimizes Kullback-Leibler divergence from the current distribution subject to satisfying the reported conditional, together with any previously accepted constraints; if no distribution satisfies all the constraints, the report is rejected. The stated rationale is conservatism: make the smallest information-theoretic change needed to accommodate the new information. The group publishes forecasts from this distribution, and members are evaluated partly on how well the shared distribution performs, so each member has an interest in pulling it toward their own view.

Please analyze how this rule would actually behave. First, work through the soldier case under the rule: what does it do to the probability of Red territory, and does that outcome make sense for a message that was purely conditional? Second, treat the rule as an institution: what does it implicitly commit the group to about whether a conditional report may move the probability of its own antecedent, and what incentives does that create for members deciding which conditionals to report and in what order? Third, identify the main failure modes — path-dependence, strategic sequencing, cycles, overconfident reports, or others — and say whether any of them can be fixed without abandoning the KL-minimization principle.
