I'm taking a philosophy of mind class and got into an argument with my study group about the color phi illusion, and now it seems to me like it shows consciousness gets rewritten after the fact, Orwell-style. Here's the setup as I understand it: a blue dot flashes on the left side of a screen, then a red dot flashes on the right side, in quick succession. People report seeing a single dot glide across and abruptly change from blue to red somewhere in the middle of its path. The weird part is that they describe seeing the color change before the second dot actually appears, which seems impossible unless the brain waits for the second flash, builds the moving-dot experience, and then backdates it into the conscious timeline. That sounds like textbook Orwellian revision: you briefly had some earlier experience, it got edited, and you now have a false memory of seeing a color change sooner than you could of.

So doesn't the color phi basically settle the Orwellian vs. Stalinesque debate in favor of the Orwellian story? My professor says the two accounts are empirically equivalent and that I'm assuming a sort of Cartesian theater, but I can't make sense of the Stalinesque alternative as anything but a word game. What exactly am I confusing here?
