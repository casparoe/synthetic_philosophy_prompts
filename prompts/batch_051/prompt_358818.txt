The same video app I use every day seems to be built on two opposite ideas about me, and I want help figuring out what those ideas are and whether they can both be true at once. On one side of the app, the next video starts automatically after a short countdown unless I stop it; the home feed has no bottom, and pulling down at the top produces a fresh batch whose contents I cannot predict; the recommendations seem tuned to keep me watching; and the app sends notifications when a creator I follow goes live or when I have not opened it in a while. On the other side, the same app gives me a screen-time dashboard, an optional reminder suggesting I take a break after long sessions, and a bedtime mode that grays the screen to make it less inviting. What picture of the user does each set of features presuppose — for instance, what it assumes about whether the choices I make while scrolling reveal what I really want, and about how much responsibility for staying in control falls on me? Do these two parts of the app presuppose a consistent view of the user, or do they commit the designers to incompatible theories of choice and self-control? And if the view behind the dashboard and break reminders were taken seriously, what would have to change about the autoplay, the endless feed, and the notifications? Please write your response in American English.
