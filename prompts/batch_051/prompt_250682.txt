I am a software architect, and I have been reading about the modularity of mind debate in cognitive science. I would like you to translate that debate into my native vocabulary rather than explain it in prose. Take three positions. First, Fodor's modularity thesis: input systems such as perception and language parsing are modules, characterized by domain specificity, informational encapsulation, mandatory operation, speed, shallow outputs, fixed neural architecture, and characteristic breakdown patterns. Second, Fodor's companion claim that central cognition—belief fixation, reasoning, decision—is not modular, because it is isotropic and Quinean: any belief is in principle relevant to any other, and confirmation is holistic. Third, the massive modularity thesis, which holds that the mind is largely or entirely a collection of domain-specific modules, including ones for social exchange, cheater detection, and folk biology. Restate each of these in the vocabulary of software architecture: modules and services, interfaces and APIs, encapsulation and information hiding, shared mutable state, side effects, message passing, event buses, monoliths versus microservices, and anything else in that toolbox that earns its keep. I am fluent in that vocabulary, so be technical rather than introductory.

For each element, tell me what translates cleanly, what translates only under distortion, and what has no expression at all. Then give me a residue report: where the software vocabulary illuminates the cognitive debate, where it quietly imports assumptions—about designed interfaces, about an implementer, about the point of a subsystem—that a mind need not respect, and where the original claims start to look less clear than they did in their home vocabulary. In particular, I want to know whether Fodor's objection to massive modularity—that central cognition is isotropic and Quinean and therefore cannot be encapsulated—survives the translation, or whether it turns out to be inexpressible, and what that tells us. Finally, tell me whether the software vocabulary makes anything salient that the modularity debate has been missing.
