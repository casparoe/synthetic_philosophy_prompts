Here's where i'm stuck. On one side, the classical case for divine impassibility looks like a theorem of perfect-being theology plus aseity. If God depends on nothing and is the source of all being, God's inner life can't be caused by creatures; if our suffering made God suffer, God would be passive and reactive with respect to us. And if God is perfect, God can't be diminished—but to suffer is, normally, to be diminished. Impassibility also keeps God from being harmed, and a God who can be harmed is a God who can be coerced by whoever manages to inflict enough harm, which makes divine goodness hostage to evil. That's a real theoretical payoff.

But I also have an intuition i don't half-disown: that love is partly constituted by being genuinely moved by the beloved, and that a God who is in no way pained by a child being tortured is not loving in any sense i recognize. The stock reply—that God's love is active, not reactive, pure benevolence that will judge and heal—sounds to me like describing a very effective bystander with a plan, not a lover. If compassion means suffering-with, what's left of it when all the suffering is on one side? And if the crucifixion is the deepest revelation of divine love, a God who cannot suffer turns the cross into theater where the divine nature stays untouched. I know the standard moves here—the two natures, the communication of idioms, the formula that one of the Trinity suffered in the flesh—and they feel like they label the tension rather than dissolve it.

So: walk me through what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes (is there a debunking story showing my picture of love is just projecting human neediness onto God?), what it would take for impassibility to be amended or abandoned (where does the perfect-being argument overreach—does it assume passibility entails passivity and diminishment?), and whether there's a reading where both survive (analogy? God voluntarily taking creaturely suffering into the divine life without being coerced? something else?). For each move, tell me what it costs and how I'd recognize it's the right move rather than face-saving.
