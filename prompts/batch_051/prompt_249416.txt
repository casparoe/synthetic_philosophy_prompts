Teleosemantics says a mental state's content is fixed by its selection history: roughly, what the state was selected to indicate or do. Someone who wants to handle newly learned concepts will usually say the content comes from the learning mechanism that produced them, not from a fresh round of evolution. Here's the case that keeps bugging me. Take a transformer trained only on next-token prediction. Some of its internal states track real features of the world well enough that the model can reason about them, but those states were never selected for indicating those features — they're a byproduct of predicting tokens. Does the teleosemantic story survive this? If yes, say exactly what does the selecting and what content it assigns to one of those states. If no, wich premise gives way, and is there a weaker version that still deserves to be called teleosemantic?
