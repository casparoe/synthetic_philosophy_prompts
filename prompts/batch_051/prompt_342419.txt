The classic defense of supererogation goes something like this: morality needs a category of acts that are good but not required, because otherwise every missed chance to do something better becomes a wrong, and ethics turns into a demand for unrelenting saintliness from everyone all the time. Now take the way effective altruism, and expected-value thinking more generally, often gets practiced: every marginal choice is put on one scale, and if giving less does less good, then giving less looks like knowingly choosing a worse outcome — which is usually exactly what "wrong" means. Does the supererogation distinction survive this development?
