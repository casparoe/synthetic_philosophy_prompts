I would like you to act as an expert in formal epistemology and decision theory. I am an undergraduate currently taking a course on rational belief, and we have just finished the unit on Dutch book arguments. I am trying to work out exactly what rule our professor is using when she pronounces a person's degrees of belief rationally permissible or impermissible, and I would be grateful for your help.

In all of her cases, a person announces a fair price for a ticket that pays $1 if a given proposition is true and $0 otherwise, and the person is willing both to buy and to sell the ticket at that price. The professor treats that price as the person's degree of belief in the proposition.

Her recorded verdicts so far are these:

1. Partition {rain, no rain}: fair prices of $0.50 for rain and $0.50 for no rain. Verdict: permissible.
2. Same partition: $0.60 for rain and $0.50 for no rain. Verdict: impermissible; she showed that a bookmaker who sold one of each ticket to the person would be guaranteed a profit of $0.10.
3. Same partition: $0.50 for rain and $0.30 for no rain. Verdict: impermissible; a bookmaker who bought one of each ticket from the person would be guaranteed a profit of $0.20.
4. Same partition: $1.20 for rain and −$0.20 for no rain. Verdict: impermissible; she showed that a bookmaker could guarantee a profit by selling the rain ticket and buying the no-rain ticket at its negative price.
5. Two mutually exclusive propositions A and B that do not exhaust the space: $0.30 for A, $0.20 for B, and $0.60 for "A or B." Verdict: impermissible; she noted that selling the "A or B" ticket and buying the A and B tickets would yield a guaranteed profit of $0.10.
6. Tautology and contradiction: $1.00 for "rain or not rain" and $0.00 for "rain and not rain." Verdict: permissible.

Next week she plans to discuss a person whose fair prices are $1.00 for "it will rain tomorrow" and $0.00 for "it will not rain tomorrow." She has never commented on a case in which a contingent proposition receives price $1 or $0.

I cannot tell whether her only standard is the absence of a guaranteed sure-loss betting arrangement, or whether she would also accept the familiar further requirement that rational credences be regular, or strictly coherent: a contingent proposition may not receive price 0 or 1, with 1 reserved for tautologies and 0 for contradictions. Her six recorded verdicts do not settle this.

Please think carefully. Start by brainstorming the candidate rules that could fit all six recorded verdicts, and do not settle on the first candidate you find. Then set out the two or three most plausible candidates. For each candidate, say exactly how it would classify next week's case and why. Then give your best prediction of what the professor will say, with the reasons for preferring that interpretation of her record. Finally, name one further case you would want to put to her that would settle which rule she is actually running, and explain why it would be decisive.
