Working up a short note on desuetude — the idea that a statute nobody has enforced in ages and that everyone openly violates can lose its force without formal repeal — and I want my position stress-tested before I circulate it. Here's where I've landed. (1) It would be wrong to suddenly enforce a criminal law like that against a single person, when everyone's been doing the thing openly and nobody alive remembers it being enforced. (2) The real reason isn't that the legislature secretly intended repeal; it's about fair notice and settled expectations. (3) But I also don't think judges should be in the business of quietly nullifying old statutes they dislike — repeal is the legislature's job. (4) I have no problem with prosecutors deciding not to enforce a law for resource or policy reasons, even if that nonenforcement is systematic. (5) Still, I think the rule of law is damaged when enforcement is a surprise that nobody could reasonably have planned around. This feels like one coherent view to me, but I suspect at least one of these commitments pulls against another. Go through the pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only seem to be, and what the least costly fix is.
