Take the Clever Hans episode — the horse in early twentieth-century Berlin that seemed to solve arithmetic and language tasks by tapping, until Oskar Pfungst showed it was responding to subtle, largely involuntary cues from the questioner, so that its performance collapsed when no one present knew the answer or the horse could not see the questioner — and analyze it as a case study for Morgan's canon. I know the narrative; what I want is the two-directional mapping. First, run the episode through the canon: which candidate explanations were live at each stage, which psychological faculties each one posited, what the canon — the rule that we should not attribute a higher psychological faculty when a lower one can account for the behavior — would have licensed or ruled out, and whether the investigators were actually following the canon or something else. Second, run the canon through the episode: what counts as higher and lower in this case, given that the correct explanation was itself a sophisticated social sensitivity; whether the availability of a lower-level explanation was treated as evidence for its truth or merely as a burden-shifting rule; and whether the episode vindicates the canon as epistemic hygiene or exposes it as an empirical bet about animal minds in methodological disguise. End with what, if anything, the case should make us amend in how the canon is stated and used.
