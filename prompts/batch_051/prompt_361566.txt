Me and a friend got into a long argument about underdetermination last night and I ended up with a distinction I want checked. I call it “falsifiability drain.” Every theory starts with a stock of falsifiability, and each time it survives a new observation by adjusting an auxiliary assumption after seeing the data, it loses a bit. The same adjustment made before seeing the data wouldn’t drain anything, because the risk was already on the table. My friend says I’ve just reinvented ad hocness, or maybe Lakatos. I dont think so: mine is about the temporal order of belief and evidence, not the final structure of the theory. Example: positing an extra planet before observing a deviation versus adding it after observing the deviation. Does this distinction hold up? Is it already a thing? And does it actually break the argument that any evidence can be accommodated by any theory, or is it just epistemic hygiene?
