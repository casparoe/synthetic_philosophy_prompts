I'm a retired soil chemist who's gotten into philosophy of law, and the textualism/purposivism debate is the first one that's made me want to write something. I want to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed textualist and a committed purposivist about statutory interpretation, the document both could sign after a year of arguing in good faith. Do it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should be neither vacuous nor slanted: what can both sides sign about what statutory meaning is, about what judges are doing, about what to do when the plain text would produce an absurd result, about canons? Then the precise residual disagreement, phrased so each side would except the wording of its own view—no strawmen. Then the cruxes. I suspect one is whether legislative history is evidence of the meaning of a statute or only evidence of what some legislators wanted, and another is whether reading a term in its ordinary sense is a descriptive claim about readers or a normative decision to stop looking. For each crux, give a concrete statutory example (make one up) that both would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction for how it comes out. Also say if any part of the fight seems to you to be purely verbal.
