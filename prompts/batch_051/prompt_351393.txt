I've been stuck on anthropic reasoning for a few weeks now — a friend at a party cornered me about the doomsday argument, and since then I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe by going case by case, deliberately before looking up what the self-sampling and self-indication assumptions are supposed to make me say. Trouble is, my verdicts don't hang together. Here they are. Sleeping Beauty: when she wakes, I think she should give heads one-third; I'd happily take even-money bets on tails each waking. The blank-room coin toss (heads creates one person, tails creates a million, you wake up in a room with no idea which): my first instinct is that tails is almost certain, a million to one-ish, because in the tails branch there are a million people and I'm one of them. The presumptuous philosopher (two theories fit everything we've seen equally well, but one says far more observers exist in total): I don't think the many-observer theory is favored at all — sheer numbers shouldn't be evidence for a theory. The doomsday argument (I learn I'm roughly the 60-billionth human): I do feel the pull, and I'd say that at least slightly lowers the chance of a future with trillions more of us. The amnesiac hospital case (a coin sets the number of rooms at 10 or 1,000, I wake up in room 7): I want to lean strongly toward 10 rooms, maybe 99 to 1.

Wether any single principle gets all five of those, I can't tell. The blank-room case makes me want to say that more observers makes a hypothesis more likely; the amnesiac case and doomsday make me want to say my position among observers counts against large totals; the presumptuous philosopher case makes me want to say numbers never matter; and Sleeping Beauty makes me want to say waking up updates probabilities. I'd like you to work out whether there's a coherent principle — self-sampling, self-indication, a hybrid, something else — that captures as many of my verdicts as possible, say explicitly which of my verdicts it can't accommodate and why, and tell me what the surviving principle says about a case I'm still chewing on: we discover a distant independently-evolved civilization and learn that a cosmic coin flip decided it would have either one million or one billion members; does the larger number give us any reason to think that branch was chosen? Finally, please tell me how confident you are in the principle you propose, and single out the point you're least sure of.
