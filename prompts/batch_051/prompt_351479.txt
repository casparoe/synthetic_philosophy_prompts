Two of my coworkers got into it over whether a chess engine "wants" to control the center of the board. Marcus said that's just a figure of speech: the engine runs a search algorithm with an evaluation function, and the only real description is the code and the arithmetic. Priya said that standard would make almost nothing real—a hurricane doesn't literally "have" an eye either, it's just air molecules, but nobody thinks that means the eye isn't a real pattern. She said a pattern earns the right to be called real when treating the engine as having aims lets you predict its moves better than trying to work through the search tree yourself. Marcus came back that predictive usefulness is about us, not about the engine: either there's something in the system that represents a goal or there isn't, and he doesn't see a "want" anywhere in the source code. Priya said source code is just one pattern among many—the lowest-level pattern isn't automatically the real one just becuase it's mechanistic. She brought in that idea of three stances: the physical stance (predict from the underlying machinery), the design stance (predict from function and optimization), and the intentional stance (predict from goals and desires), and said they're each legitimate when they let you predict and compress behavior. Marcus said that just makes "the stock market panicked" real too, since that also predicts, and at that point "real" just means "handy to talk about." Priya said he was treating the stances as fallbacks for our ignorance rather than as ways of tracking real higher-level patterns, and that the stock-market locution can still track a real pattern if it compresses a lot of trading behavior even when it isn't literal. They went around like this till lunch ended and neither gave ground. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right about which specific points, and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
