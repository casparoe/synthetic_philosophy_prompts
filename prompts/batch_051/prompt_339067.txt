I am leading a single graduate-seminar session on whether Frege's 1924 political diary — its antisemitic, anti-democratic, and völkisch-nationalist entries — bears on how we should assess his philosophy of logic and mathematics. The students know the logicism, the sense/reference distinction, and the anti-psychologism, but none has read the diary. I want a plan for the session, not the explanation itself, kept to at most 850 words.

The plan should give me the explanatory arc: the distinction or idea that must be in place before the question can even be posed without collapsing into one of two flat responses — that the diary discredits the logic, or that the logic's origin is automatically irrelevant to every sense of bearing on it; the order in which the pieces must be introduced and what depends on what; the one example or comparison that should carry the session, and what it must have and lack if it is to do that work; the misconceptions the students will arrive with or form along the way, in the order they will appear, and the point in the sequence where each should be headed off; one closing question whose answer would show they have grasped the distinction rather than merely been made uncomfortable; and what to leave out at this level. Write for an audience that knows the Frege but not the historiography, and do not assign readings or refer me to secondary literature.
