So I'm a science journalist, and I'm working on a piece about how much of the human body is a kludge — features that look designed but are accidents of development or history, or got borrowed for a job they weren't originally for. The word I keep tripping on is "adaptation." I need a working definition, in the evolutionary sense — not the self-help sense of adapting to a new job. Ive spent the last week arguing with myself about this and I still cant settle it. I want the definition to get these cases right:

(a) a human eye's lens and retina working together to focus an image — if your definition doesn't count this as an adaptation, it's broken.
(b) male nipples — I'm comfortable saying they're not an adaptation, just a side effect of a shared developmental program.
(c) bird feathers — the usual story is they started as simple filaments for warmth or display and only later got co-opted for flight. Is something an adaptation for the thing it currently does, or for the thing it was first selected for?
(d) the human chin — the little point of bone on the front of the jaw. Some say it's a side effect of the jaw shrinking relative to the teeth; some say it was sexually selected. I genuinely don't know, and I want the definition to force a verdict or explain why the case is undecidable.
(e) the giraffe's recurrent laryngeal nerve — the one that takes a huge detour from the head down around the aorta and back up to the larynx. Nobody thinks the detour itself was selected for, but the nerve is obviously there to do a job. How do you classify the route, as opposed to the nerve?
(f) blood being red — just chemistry, nobody thinks the color was selected for.
(g) language — useful for everything, but it's genuinely controversial whether it's a direct adaptation or a side effect of a big general-purpose brain.
(h) the appendix — maybe a shrunken leftover from a more herbivorous ancestor, maybe a refuge for gut bacteria after a bad infection; I gather it's still argued about.

Propose the definition, run it through all eight, and tell me where it gives the wrong answer or no answer. If some of these force you to split "adaptation" into two senses — one about history, one about current function — say so, and say which cases move between them. And finish by telling me which of your verdicts is the least secure, and whether that's a reason to revise the definition or just to live with it.
