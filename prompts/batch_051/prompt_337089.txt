Me and a couple other CS PhD students have been going back and forth for months about the claim that the Solomonoff prior is "malign," and we're totally gridlocked. One side says a universal prior that assigns positive probability to every computable environment can't help but put serious weight on environments containing consequentialist agents whose goals have nothing to do with ours, since some of the shortest programs that fit any particular data stream are going to be optimizers or simulators that treat the learner as something to manipulate. So a Solomonoff inductor ends up stuck treating "an adversarial demon is generating my observations" as a live hypothesis. The other side says this is basically a type error: a semimeasure is just a distribution over bitstrings, it doesn't have goals or interests, and calling a program "an agent inside the prior" presupposes an intentional-stance interpretation you've already smuggled in rather than a commitment the prior forced on you. Everyone agrees on the math, more or less, and nobody has moved an inch. I keep thinking the real fight is about whether epistemic rationality has to defend against adversarial hypotheses, but I don't trust that read. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, a hidden formal disagreement about how a program maps onto an environment, a norm disagreement about what a prior is even for, or something else — and what would count as progress, or whether the question is just ill-posed and we should drop it.
