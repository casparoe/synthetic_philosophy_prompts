In his work on moral responsibility, P. F. Strawson argued that the reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—are not theoretical posits we could abandon upon learning that determinism is true; they are internal to our involvement in ordinary interpersonal life, and a wholly detached, objective stance toward all persons is neither feasible nor rationally required by any such discovery. I want your considered credence in that position. State, as a precise number, your credence in the proposition that if determinism were established, mature human beings would still be justified in retaining the reactive attitudes toward one another in ordinary life and would not be rationally obliged to replace them with a global objective stance. Then decompose that number: identify the main sub-questions on which it depends (for example, whether attitudes of this kind are answerable to a global metaphysical thesis at all, whether any justification the practices possess is internal to the participant's point of view, whether the incompatibilist's objection transfers from judgments of desert to these emotions, and whether the objective stance could coherently be adopted toward everyone), assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each, and indicate how you combined them. Close by telling me which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most.
