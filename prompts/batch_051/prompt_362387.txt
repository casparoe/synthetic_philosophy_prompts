In Part V of the Discourse on the Method, Descartes argues that the fact that magpies and parrots can produce words but cannot combine them as signs of thought shows that animals lack reason, not merely that they lack the right vocal organs. From this he concludes that brutes act solely from the disposition of their organs, like machines. I am drawn to the structure of the argument: it avoids explaining animal silence by anatomy and proposes a clean behavioral test. At the same time, I cannot shake the intuition that a dog yelping after a kick is undergoing something—suffering, not just emitting a noise. I do not want to be told which side wins. I want to know what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that gives way, what it would take for Descartes' criterion to be the thing that gives way, and whether there is a reading of terms like sensation and feeling on which the argument and the intuition are compatible. Use the magpie-and-parrot passage as the basis, and be specific about which part of Descartes' inference is doing the work.
