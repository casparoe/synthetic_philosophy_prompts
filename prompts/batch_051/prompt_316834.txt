I'm putting together a reading-group exercise on Curry's paradox and Yablo's liar, and I've ended up as the de-facto mediator between two friends who have been argueing past each other for months. One of them, the circularity camp, thinks both paradoxes are at bottom the same fixed-point / diagonal phenomenon: Yablo's sequence only looks self-reference-free because you can't even specify the list — the nth item says every later item is false — without a diagonal construction that already smuggles circularity in. The other, the structural camp, thinks the load-bearing lesson of Curry is that paradox doesn't need negation, it needs the structural rule of contraction / absorption, and that Yablo is a mostly seperate phenomenon showing you can get paradox from plain universal quantification alone, so "self-reference" was a red herring in both cases.

Write the document this adversarial collaboration should have produced after a year of good-faith argument: first a numbered list of claims both camps can sign (not vacuous ones; push on what each side would actually have to grant about the other's case), then a disagreements table. In the table, for each residual disagreement, give the question in neutral wording, each camp's own wording of its answer (phrased so that camp would accept it), the crux that would actually move one of them, and a concrete derivation or case both sides agree bears on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Two cruxes I suspect will matter are (i) whether the Yablo sequence can be finitely specified without already using a fixed point, and (ii) whether Curry's derivation and Yablo's derivation fail under the same restriction or for different reasons. Part of the job is disentangling the senses of "self-reference," "circularity," and "diagonalization" the two camps have been running together.
