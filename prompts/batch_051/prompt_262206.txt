Act as an expert in decision theory, formal epistemology, and rational choice under infallible predictors. Please think carefully, start by brainstorming at least three distinct principles that could explain the recorded verdicts, and only then make a prediction.

I’m a high school student, and I’ve been running thought experiments with my older sister. In every scenario, Omega is an infallible predictor: Omega’s predictions are always correct. My sister accepts this, treats all dollar amounts as linear in utility, and knows the full rules of each scenario before deciding. She won’t explain her rule, so I’ve been writing down her verdicts.

Here are her verdicts so far.

1. Newcomb’s Problem. Omega puts two boxes in front of her: a transparent box containing $1,000, and an opaque box that contains $1,000,000 if and only if Omega predicted she would take only the opaque box; otherwise the opaque box is empty. She takes only the opaque box.

2. Counterfactual mugging. Omega secretly flips a fair coin. If it lands tails, Omega asks her to pay $100. If it lands heads, Omega gives her $10,000 if and only if Omega predicted she would pay $100 in the tails case. Omega flips the coin, tells her it landed tails, and asks her for $100. She pays.

3. Parfit’s hitchhiker. Omega has already decided whether to drive her home based on whether she would pay Omega $100 once home. Omega arrives, gives her a ride, and drops her off at home. Once home, she pays the $100.

Now I want to predict her verdict on a new case.

New case. Omega says: “I have already predicted whether you would pay me $100 if I asked. If I predicted you would pay, then I have already deposited $10,000 in your bank account and I will not ask. If I predicted you would refuse, then I am asking you now for $100, and no deposit has been made.” Omega then says: “I am asking you now.” My sister knows she is being asked, and she knows no deposit was made.

What will my sister say if she is being consistent with her first three verdicts?

I’m less interested in the bare prediction than in the reasoning. In particular:

(a) What candidate principles fit her first three verdicts, including at least one principle that predicts “pay” in the new case and at least one that predicts “refuse”?

(b) Which principle do you think she is actually running, and what does it predict for the new case?

(c) What additional case would most sharply distinguish the leading candidates from one another, and what would each candidate say in that case?
