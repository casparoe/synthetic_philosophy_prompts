Last night a friend and I spent several hours arguing about whether anything other than a biological brain can mean or be about anything in its own right, and we ended exactly where we began. I was defending the broadly Searlean position: beliefs and desires possess original, intrinsic intentionality, while words, diagrams, thermostats, and the outputs of language models possess only derived intentionality, borrowed from the purposes of the people who build and interpret them. My friend was defending the broadly Dennettian position, and his main example was the two-bitser. A vending machine built in the United States to accept US quarters, and then operating in Panama, cannot distinguish those quarters from physically similar Panamanian quarter-balboas; so, he argued, there is no further fact about whether the machine treats the Panamanian coin as a quarter or as a distinct coin that happens to be acceptable. He concluded that even so-called derived intentionality is already relative to an interpreter's stance, and therefore Searle's original/derived distinction is not a joint in nature but a decision about where to stop relativizing; "original" intentionality, on his telling, is just interpretation that has become invisible.

The argument stalled in a familiar way. We conceded all the same physical and behavioral facts about the machine and, in principle, about brains; we agreed that no straightforward experiment would settle the matter; and we each finished convinced that the other was changing the subject. Reading about Searle and Dennett afterward, I found what looks like the same stalemate at the level of the actual debate: decades later, each side still treats the other as refusing to answer the real question. I am not asking for a verdict on who is right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is. My own suspicion is that it is mostly about where the burden of proof lies—Searle demanding a naturalistic account of intrinsic aboutness and Dennett denying that the demand is well formed—but I worry that merely relabels the stalemate. Is the disagreement verbal, with "original" and "derived" doing different work on each side? Is it a difference about what counts as an explanation of intentionality? Are the two sides answering different questions, one about the causal substrate of meaning and the other about the explanatory utility of the intentional idiom? Or is it a bedrock difference over whether aboutness could be observer-relative at all? Given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as genuine progress: a distinction, a formal result, an empirical finding, a deliberate change of question—or the recognition that no such progress should be expected.
