I am a novelist. My current project is a multi-century chronicle of a future planetary order, and I have been trying to discipline my storytelling with the outside view: treating imperial collapse as a forecasting problem, using historical empires as a reference class, and letting their base rates for duration, decay, and terminal crisis constrain what I can plausibly narrate. That method depends on a workable rule for what enters the reference class.

The criteria I have been using are these. A polity counts as an empire, in the sense relevant to my base rates, if it has: a core that dominates a periphery of multiple previously self-governing peoples; domination sustained by coercion and extraction rather than mere influence or prestige; a recognizable center able to make binding decisions for the whole; and a discernible lifespan with a beginning and an end, so that it can occupy a row in my table of durations.

I have invented a borderline case for the novel's third volume, and I cannot tell whether it qualifies. The Consolidated Exchange is a planetary commercial order that formed without a conqueror. It has no emperor and no standing army of its own, yet it supplies the contract law under which several dozen formerly sovereign states trade, levies mandatory charges on every transaction, issues the common currency, and can call upon each member state's coercive apparatus to enforce its rulings. Its central committee may expel a member or suspend a state's payment rails, which functions as blockade without a navy. It has already lasted longer than many historical empires. But it has never fought a war of conquest, and it insists that it is not a government at all—only market infrastructure.

Does the Exchange belong in my reference class? Please work through my criteria one by one, say where they underdetermine the verdict, and then tell me whether the case reveals that one of the criteria is doing more work than it should—in particular, whether coercive dominance or a recognizable center is really necessary for the collapse dynamics my base rates are meant to capture. Including the Exchange would enlarge my sample but might also change what I am actually forecasting. I want a diagnosis of the category, not just a ruling on my fictional case.
