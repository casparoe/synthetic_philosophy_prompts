Suppose two serious readers of Aristotle are trying to dissolve a long-running dispute through an adversarial collaboration. One camp holds that, for Aristotle, human thinking is always and everywhere accompanied by phantasia, so that no thought occurs without some phantasma, even in the most abstract theoretical understanding. The other camp holds that this overreads the texts and that, for Aristotle, certain mature acts of intellectual grasp can proceed without any phantasma at all, phantasia being required only for the more discursive, embodied, or particular forms of thinking. The two camps agree that part of their quarrel may be verbal but deny that all of it is. Their shared goal is a document both sides can sign and send to their own allies without embarrassment. I want you to draft that document.

The document should begin by recording the claims both camps can jointly affirm about phantasia as Aristotle introduces it in De Anima III.3 and develops in the neighboring chapters: claims about how phantasia differs from perception and from belief, about why Aristotle introduces it rather than simply using perception to explain movement and error, and about the role phantasmata play in memory, desire, and action. Then it should state the residual disagreement as exactly as possible, in wording each camp would accept for its own position and for the opposing position, with special attention to the passages in which Aristotle says that the soul never thinks without a phantasma. It should identify the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that a settled answer would move at least one camp. For each crux, it should specify what kind of textual evidence, philosophical reconstruction, or argument would genuinely bear on it, and it should record in advance what each camp predicts the evidence will show. It should close by sorting the original disagreement into its real and merely verbal components, paying particular attention to whether phantasma is best taken as a pictorial image, a more schematic or structural representation, or something broader still.

Treat both camps as competent, and avoid resolving the dispute into a bland middle position or a strawman. If you think the task rests on a confused premise, for example because the two camps as I have described them are too coarse to mark a real fault line in Aristotle's psychology, or because the phrase no thought without a phantasma gets imported from later commentary rather than Aristotle's own view, say so plainly and do the nearest useful thing instead. Write in continuous prose only, as a memorandum the two camps could sign, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. If you must number claims, do it in ordinary sentences, for instance first, second, third. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
