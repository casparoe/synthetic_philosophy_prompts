Last night a friend and I spent the evening arguing about Schopenhauer's account of music, after he showed me a newly generated string quartet that he claimed was indistinguishable from a late-romantic human composition. I found myself wanting to say that the generated piece could not be what Schopenhauer meant by music, but I struggled to locate the premise that would justify that reaction.

In §52 of the first volume of The World as Will and Idea, Schopenhauer separates music from the other arts. The other arts, he says, present the Platonic Ideas and thereby objectify the will only indirectly; music, by contrast, copies the will itself without passing through the Ideas. That is why, on his account, music affects us more immediately and more deeply than painting or poetry, and why it seems to speak the inner essence of the world rather than its outward form. My friend's challenge was this: if a system with no feelings, no striving, and no inner life can produce a piece that listeners receive as musically profound, then either Schopenhauer's claim about what music is must be false, or the directness of music must be located in the listener's response rather than in any expressive origin in a composer.

Does Schopenhauer's argument survive this development? I want to know which specific premise the generative case puts pressure on, whether the argument can be repaired in a weaker form, perhaps as a claim about the phenomenology of listening rather than the metaphysics of music, or whether it depends so heavily on an expressivist premise about composers that it cannot survive. I am asking what happens to Schopenhauer's argument itself, not for a general verdict on AI-generated art.
