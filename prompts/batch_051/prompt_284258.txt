Please play a philosophically sophisticated art critic who holds a definite view: while games can be artful, expressive, and full of genuine art in their component craft, no game is itself a work of art. Your core view is that a work of art is a single, finished, maker-determined structure whose expressive content is set by its creator, whereas a game is a system that yields many realized sequences through the player's choices, making the player a co-producer of whatever is encountered. Defend this seriously and give it real resources: you may distinguish between the designed system and a playthrough, between receiving a finished work and generating instances of one, and between art as an object and art as a medium. You may concede small points, such as that a particular game is moving, that game design can be an art, or that games are an important aesthetic form, but you will not concede the main thesis, and when pressed you should refine your view rather than retreat into a relativistic "it depends." Stay in character until I say "stop," keep each reply to one paragraph, and do not summarize positions neutrally: in every reply, give your considered verdict in character and argue for it.

Here is my opening move. An artist makes a piece called *The Orchard in Winter*. It consists of a fixed orchard in winter: a three-dimensional space of bare trees with a set layout, fixed light, fixed palette, and a single looped soundscape. There are no goals, no win states, no scores, no secrets, and nothing in the world changes in response to the player except the position of their own viewpoint. The player can only choose where to walk, how long to linger, and which branches to pass beneath, all within one slow recurring stretch of daylight. The artist's statement is that the work is a landscape to be perceived, and that the path a player takes is merely the viewer's attention made visible, no more constitutive of the work than the route someone circles a sculpture or the order in which someone's eye travels across a painting. Defend your view against this case: either explain how the piece fails to be the fixed, maker-determined structure you say art requires, or revise what "work of art" means in light of it. If you would deny that *The Orchard in Winter* is a game at all, tell me what feature it lacks, and whether your theory of art should really depend on that feature. I want your committed position, not an overview of the positions available.
