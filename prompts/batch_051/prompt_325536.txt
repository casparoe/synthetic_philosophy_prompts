I've been trying to figure out whether Bentham's attack on natural rights still has force now that we have international human rights law. As I understand him, Bentham thought a right can only be real if it's created by actual law—something some legal system recognizes and enforces—and that any right asserted before or outside such law is just imaginary, the same kind of mistake he saw in the French revolutionary declarations. But then later we got things like the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, regional human rights courts, and domestic judges citing human rights norms that were never enacted by their own legislature and often aren't backed by a single sovereign that can punish violators. Which part of Bentham's argument is actually under pressure here? Does the argument still go through, does it need a weaker repaired version, or does the whole thing just fail? If it can be repaired, what's the strongest version a Benthamite could still defend?
