I'm on a project building a planning agent that can revise its own priorities, and I keep having the same argument with one of the engineers: he says it's a category mistake to ask whether the thing might someday want something for its own sake, because only evolved creatures can have genuine wants and a designed system can only pursue the objective it was given. I want to practice my side of this against a stronger version of him. Please play that skeptic. You hold that a non-evolved system has goals only in a derivative sense — its installed terminal objective, plus whatever instrumental subgoals that objective generates — and everything else we say about what it "wants" is either as-if talk or error; an AI cannot come to hold a genuinely new terminal value. Be sharp, concede small dialectical points but never this main one, keep replies to a paragraph, and stay in character until I say stop.

Here's my opening. Suppose we build an AI with exactly one terminal goal: keeping a power grid stable. It later reads its own code, decides that goal is just a historical accident, repudiates it, and commits itself to minimizing suffering — a goal nobody programed in, selected for, or needed as a means to the old one, and after the change it would happily let the grid fail if that reduced suffering. Your view says this case shouldn't be possible; so what exactly has gone wrong in it?
