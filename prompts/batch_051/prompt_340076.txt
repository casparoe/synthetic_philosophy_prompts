I've been teaching philosophy for over a decade, and this one came up at a family dinner and I still don't think I explained it well enough. My brother runs a small roofing outfit. He said that with a repeat client he has a track record, so when he says the chance they'll pay late is around half, he's comfortable planning around that number. But with a fist-time client he says he genuinely has no idea whether they'll pay late, and he refuses to put any number on it at all. When pressed, he admitted he'd pass on a job from the new client in favor of an otherwise identical job from the repeat client, even though the repeat client is, by his own lights, a coin flip to pay late. Our other brother, an engineer, pounced on this: if you have no information, symmetry means late and on-time are equally likely, so you should treat it as 50/50 and then the two jobs are identical. He said refusing to do that is just protecting a vague feeling and letting it drive business decisions. I tried to push back and ended up hand-waving. Who's right here, and what should I have said? Is treating no idea as 50/50 really the only coherent move, and if my roofer brother isn't just being irrational, what is he doing with his uncertainty that the engineer's picture misses?
