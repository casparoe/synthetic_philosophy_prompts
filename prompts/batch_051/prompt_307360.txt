What is a white lie? I want a working definition that counts a dinner guest who tells the host the meal was delicious when it was barely edible, purely to spare the host's feelings, as a white lie; and that counts the same guest's blunt truth -- telling the host the meal was barely edible, with no effort to soften the message -- as not a white lie. The definition should deliver a definite verdict, in either direction, for each of these cases: (a) a Japanese executive who turns down a proposal by saying "That would be very difficult," knowing the other party will read this as a polite refusal; (b) a Dutch manager who, in a workplace where direct criticism is the norm, tells an employee "This draft is not good enough"; (c) a doctor who tells a patient with a 5 percent chance of recovery that the patient has "a real shot"; (d) a person who declines a party invitation by saying "I'd love to, but I have plans that evening," when they have no plans; (e) an employee who, when asked for an opinion on the boss's disastrous idea, says "That's an interesting idea -- let me think about it"; (f) someone who lies to an official searching for a person they have hidden from harm. If your proposed definition gives no verdict or gives the wrong verdict in any of these cases, say where, then either revise the definition or defend the surprising verdict. Bonus: use the definition to say something precise about whether white lies are wrong.
