I'm a PhD student in social computing, TAing a grad seminar that draws CS, econ, and philosophy students, and we're doing a unit on reputation systems and karma economies. I'm making a handout for the session on whether reputation should be portable. Not portable in the boring technical sense (single sign-on across sites), but as a normative/design question: if you earn a lot of karma or trust in one community, do you have any claim to carry that standing elsewhere, or is it basically owned by the community that generated it?

I want a sorting quiz / decision tree rather than a prose handout. People should answer concrete questions about cases and get sorted into a position on how reputation/karma should be scoped — global and portable, local and role-bound, a public record with no right to be forgotten, a fresh-start view, whatever distinctions actually do the work. Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of theory names: start from the first splitting claim and work down, then locate the named positions in that space. Where a logically possible combination is empty, mark it and take a quick guess at why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Constraints:
- Keep it to about 7–9 diagnostic questions and 6–8 leaves; tired grad students, two pages max.
- Label each branch by the claim that splits it, not by a position name.
- Each leaf gets the position's name if it has one in the lit, otherwise "unnamed," plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- The diagnostic questions have to be answerable without knowing the jargon. Use at least these three cases as anchors, and you can add more: (1) an eBay seller with 20,000 positive ratings and one credible fraud report from last month; (2) a Reddit user with huge karma in r/AskHistorians who starts posting uninformed takes on current events; (3) a person who wants to delete their account and take their reputation score with them to another platform.
- Two positions I definitely want as leaves: something like "karma as reputational capital" (it's yours, you earned it, it's transferable) and something like "karma as local standing" (it's a community's assessment of you in a role, and it shouldn't travel or be owned). I'd also expect some kind of public-record view and some kind of right-to-restart view, but if you think those collapse into each other or into the other leaves, just say so.
- Flag anywhere the tree can't actually seperate two positions — if two views answer all my questions the same way but differ on something the questions don't touch, tell me instead of papering over it.

And if the whole framing is off — if "portable reputation" is the wrong axis, or treating karma as a thing you can own is a category mistake, or a decision tree and a quiz are redundant here — say so plainly and give me the nearest thing that does make sense. I'd rather scrap the handout than hand out something that sorts people by the wrong distinctions.
