I am writing an essay for my media studies class, and I want to read the Black Mirror episode 'Be Right Back' through the philosophical debate over griefbots — services that use a dead person's messages, posts, and other data to simulate them for the bereaved. The episode is usually discussed as a warning that such simulations keep grief open rather than let it resolve, and that what returns is a hollow, too-easily-controlled substitute for the person. I don't want a plot summary; I want a critical reading that is fair in both directions. Where does the episode genuinely dramatize something important about griefbots — for instance in its portrayal of consent, self-deception, the value of grief, or what it means to relate to a person — and where does its fiction load the dice against them, by presenting only one kind of user, one kind of grief, or one design of the technology? What does the episode show about the ethics of simulating the dead that a philosophical argument alone would miss?
