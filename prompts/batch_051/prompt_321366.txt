I'm a PhD student in ecology, and I've spent the past year defending a classification of wetland vegetation types against the charge that my units are just convenient boxes drawn over a continuum. The obvious reply is that some boxes track real kinds and others don't, but I've never seen a full statement of what that reply commits me to. Before I go further down any one theoretical road, I want the specification of the target itself. Please write the desiderata: what must any adequate theory of natural kinds deliver? I'd like the cases it has to get right and may not simply legislate away — for instance, the contrast between a chemical kind like gold and a familiar category that turns out to be disjunctive; the difference between a species and a mere collection of organisms; the status of psychiatric diagnostic categories, which support real inductive work despite being internally heterogeneous; and the fact that one and the same patch of the world can sit inside several cross-cutting scientific classifications at once. I also want the distinctions a theory cannot collapse — between a kind and our criteria for recognizing it, between being mind-independent and being inductively useful, between boundaries in the world and boundaries in our measurement practices. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that forces it, and say which requirements are negotiable and which are not.

I especially need you to flag any pairs of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable. My working suspicion is that the two demands everyone wants — that kinds be mind-independent, and that they be able to ground induction in the gradual, interest-laden domains I actually study — pull against each other; a theory built around chemical elements may, for that very reason, fail for wetlands or diagnostic categories, and vice versa. Tell me whether that tension is real or only apparent, and which single requirement does the most to rule out the standard views. If it helps, briefly score essentialist, homeostatic property cluster, and conventionalist accounts against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scoring.
