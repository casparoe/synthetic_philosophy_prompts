Analyse the Hillsborough disaster through the lens of Miranda Fricker's theory of epistemic injustice, and then assess what the case shows about the theory. I want the analysis to run in both directions: the theory should discipline your reading of the episode, and the episode should test the theory — confirming it where it explains, and complicating or exposing gaps where it does not. Please do not simply summarise the disaster or the theory, and do not provide footnotes or a reading list; the analysis itself is what matters.

Treat the following as settled background. On 15 April 1989, at the FA Cup semi-final between Liverpool and Nottingham Forest at Hillsborough in Sheffield, a crush at the Leppings Lane end killed 97 Liverpool supporters; the fresh inquests that concluded in 2016 concerned the 96 victims then recorded and returned verdicts of unlawful killing, finding no blame on the part of supporters, and the 97th victim died in 2021 from injuries sustained that day. In the immediate aftermath, South Yorkshire Police briefed journalists that drunken, ticketless Liverpool fans had forced a gate and caused the crush; The Sun then published a notorious front page repeating and embellishing that account, for which it later apologised. Survivors, bereaved families, and many fans maintained from the start that the deaths were caused by failures of policing and an unsafe design, but their account was widely discounted as self-serving for more than two decades. Later investigations established that an exit gate had been opened on police instruction, that some officers had altered their statements, and that the police and press narrative was false.

Please address the following. (1) Map the episode in Fricker's terms. Identify the testimonial exchanges, who suffers a credibility deficit and who receives a credibility excess, and specify the identity prejudice at work. Is the core injustice best described as testimonial injustice against the supporters, credibility excess granted to the police, or both? (2) Consider whether the episode also involves hermeneutical injustice. The bereaved and survivors had to contend with an official interpretation of the disaster as crowd disorder. Did they lack a shared interpretive resource with which to make their experience intelligible to the wider public, or did they possess such a resource and were denied a hearing? If the latter, say what that reveals about the limits of the hermeneutical-injustice framework for this kind of case. (3) Identify where the theory is strained. In particular, say whether an organised institutional cover-up — altering statements, briefing the press, and defaming the dead — is best understood as testimonial injustice at all, or whether Fricker's framework targets something more individual, more sincere, and more purely cognitive, and so fails to name the worst of what happened. (4) Conclude on what the case shows: is Hillsborough a confirmation of the theory, an anomaly for it, or a case whose gravest wrong lies outside the theory's reach?

Write your answer in British English.
