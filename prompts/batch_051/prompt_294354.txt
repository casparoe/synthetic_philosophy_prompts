I am setting the final exam for a senior undergraduate philosophy of language course, and I want the main essay to test whether students can do more than reproduce the three clauses of Russell’s analysis of definite descriptions. I will put this passage from Bertrand Russell, “On Denoting” (1905), at the top of the exam:

“Take as an instance ‘the father of Charles II was executed’. This asserts that there was an x who was the father of Charles II and was executed. Now the, when it is strictly used, involves uniqueness; we do, it is true, speak of ‘the son of So-and-so’ even when So-and-so has several sons, but it would be more correct to say ‘a son of So-and-so’. Thus for our purposes we take the as involving uniqueness.”

Please write one essay question that directs students to examine what “strict use” of “the” is doing in Russell’s argument. The question should force them to test whether Russell can move from this point about strict use to the claim that a definite description is an incomplete symbol without first taking a semantic stand on ordinary loose uses such as “the son of So-and-so.” It should not be answerable by summarizing Russell’s theory.

Then provide: (i) a model answer of about 600 words in British English that treats the passage closely and explains the logical point Russell is making about uniqueness and about the proposition expressed by “the father of Charles II was executed”; and (ii) a marking scheme for 20 marks. The marking scheme should separate an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific interpretive moves each must contain, and should identify at least one common student error that should lose marks.
