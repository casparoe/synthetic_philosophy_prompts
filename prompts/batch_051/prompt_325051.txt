It is often said, on the strength of Good's theorem, that for a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer costless information can never have negative expected value. The standard rationale for this result adds that information has value only through its effects on decisions, so a signal that cannot change which act is chosen is worth exactly zero. It seems to follow that a rational agent should never deliberately avoid free information. But that conclusion seems far too strong. A person who has already decided to watch a film may reasonably ask not to be told the ending; learning it would not alter what they do, and it costs nothing in any ordinary sense, yet they prefer not to know. Where precisely does the inference from Good's theorem to that broader conclusion break down?
