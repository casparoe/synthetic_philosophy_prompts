I'm designing a diagnostic quiz for a small adult-education course on creation and evolution, and I want it to sort participants into their actual position on one sharply delimited problem: how a good creator's declaration that the finished creation was very good relates to the fact that animals died, killed, suffered parasitism and disease for hundreds of millions of years before any human being existed. The audience is scientifically literate Christian laypeople; most will not know the technical labels, so every question must present a concrete case or a first-person claim and sort by what the reader actually affirms. Do not ask direct label questions such as whether someone is a young-earth creationist.

Please build a branching quiz or decision tree with nine or ten questions, each with two or three options, plus a scoring key that routes answer patterns to these leaves: (1) no-death-before-the-Fall young-earth creationism, on which the fossil record was produced by the post-Fall Flood; (2) the apparent-age or prochronic view, on which the fossil record is not the trace of a real prehuman history but part of a mature creation made with an apparent past, extending the logic of an Adam created as an adult to rocks and fossils; (3) progressive creationism, on which prehuman animal death was real but animals are not moral subjects, so the original creation can still be called good in its finished order; (4) evolutionary creationism, on which prehuman death and even some animal suffering are accepted as intrinsic to God's good evolutionary process; (5) the cosmic or trans-temporal Fall, on which prehuman suffering is a real evil but is retroactively caused by the human Fall; (6) ruin-and-restoration, on which the fossil record belongs to a previous creation destroyed in an angelic judgment before the days of Genesis 1; (7) a historical human Fall combined with the claim that prehuman suffering is an independent natural evil permitted for reasons not revealed; and (8) a non-literal or demythologized Fall, on which the Eden story is a symbolic account of the human condition and prehuman suffering is natural evil to be handled by a general theodicy.

The diagnostic questions should force the separations that matter for my course: whether prehuman death actually occurred; whether the fossil record records real events; whether the creation's goodness is about functional order or about the welfare of feeling creatures; whether predation and parasitism are evils a good creator must justify; whether a fall can have retroactive or cosmic effects; and whether the Fall must be a historical event. Use at least these cases somewhere in the instrument: a parasitic wasp larva consuming a caterpillar alive long before humans existed; an Adam created as an adult with a navel; and a pre-Eden hominin whose death must be classified as penal or natural.

Where the logical space contains combinations nobody occupies, say so and briefly diagnose whether that is because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any question you can devise, flag that as well rather than inventing a false distinction. End with the scoring key and one sentence of interpretive advice for a discussion leader.
