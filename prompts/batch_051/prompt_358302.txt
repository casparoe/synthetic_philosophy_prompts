Here is my opening move. Consider the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, in its Andromeda form. In special relativity there is no frame-independent relation of simultaneity: two observers passing each other with different velocities slice spacetime into different "nows." Suppose two people pass on the street, one walking toward Andromeda and one away from it. For the first, the launch of an Andromedean fleet already lies in the settled past; for the second, that same event still lies in an open future. Neither observer's slicing is physically privileged. If we now say that what is real at a time is whatever is simultaneous with that time, we seem forced to say that one and the same event is both already real and not yet real — which is incoherent unless we drop the tense and locate the event tenselessly in a four-dimensional manifold. Extend that thought and you arrive at the block universe: becoming is an artifact of an arbitrary foliation, not a feature of the world itself. So where exactly does the argument go wrong?
