Suppose a country sets up a Scanlon-style "reasonable rejection" office to review projects that impose concentrated burdens for diffuse benefits — a motorway, a chemical plant, a power line. Anyone who can show the project would burden them in a specific way can file a rejection, and before the project may go ahead the proposer has to justify that person's burden by a principle the burdened person could not reasonably reject. The office is not allowed to count the summed benefits to everyone else; it can only consider what principle each affected person, taken one by one, could reasonably accept or reject. Walk through how this would operate in practice. What projects get through, which ones don't, and which get redesigned? What do proposers and potential rejectors learn to do over a few years — buy off likely filers, disaggregate harms, pick projects with diffuse losers? What failure modes show up, and can any of them be fixed without gutting the anti-aggregation rule?
