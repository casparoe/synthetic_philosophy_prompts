I am a member of the board of a small nonprofit that owns a forty-acre coastal salt marsh. The founding trust instrument, drawn up in 1978, includes the following clause: "No animal of any kind living within the Reserve shall be killed, removed, or caused to leave the Reserve by the Board or by any person acting with the Board's approval." At the time, the clause was understood to prohibit hunting, trapping, and the live removal of wildlife. We have now received a proposal from a young ecologist concerning an expanding population of invasive black rats that are preying on the eggs of native ground-nesting birds. Her proposal is to release a small number of laboratory-bred rats carrying a gene drive, an engineered genetic element designed to spread through the rat population and make it sterile in successive generations, so that the rats would disappear from the Reserve within a few years. The drafters evidently did not contemplate anything of this kind. I would like an analysis of how the clause applies to this proposal. In particular, what do the plain words of the clause require, what does its purpose suggest, what would the 1978 drafters most likely have said if they had been asked, and what would a reasonable reader of the clause at the time have understood it to permit? Which of these approaches should decide the matter, and why?
