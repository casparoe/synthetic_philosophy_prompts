I argued with my friend Jacob for like three hours last night about whether you should ever let yourself believe something for non-evidential reasons. His examples were all the self-fulfilling ones: the patient whose belief that he'll recover makes recovery itself likelier, the athlete who plays better precisely because she overestimates her chances, the student who does better because she believes she's smart. My policy is pretty strict: I never let practical considerations move my credence around. If the evidence says 40%, I write down 40% even when I know that writing down 80% would make me work harder, stay calmer, or get well faster. My picture is that a belief is basically a forecast, and a forecast should be graded only on whether it's right; every gap between my credence and the evidence is a hole in my accuracy map. Jacob says I've got a truth fetish: truth is one value among others, and if a false belief reliably pays for itself, why not take it? I kept feeling like we were arguing past each other, because he was talking about what's useful and I was talking about what's epistemically rational — and I don't know if that distinction holds up.

So: is my policy actually defensible, and where precisely does it break? I know there's a philosophical view — accuracy-first epistemology is the name I half-remember — that treats credences as things to be scored by how close they get to the truth, the way you'd grade a weather forecast by a proper scoring rule like the Brier score, and it says being epistemically rational just means chosing credences that do best by that score with no separate pragmatic ingredient. Does that view back my rule, or does it actually leave room for deliberately skewed credences? More specifically, what do I do with the self-fulfilling cases: can an accuracy-first picture absorb them by noticing that the evidence itself changes once my belief is part of the system I'm trying to predict, or do they expose a real crack in it? And if my rule is too strict, is there a repaired version — something like "aim at accuracy unless your belief is part of the system it's describing" — or is that just special pleading?
