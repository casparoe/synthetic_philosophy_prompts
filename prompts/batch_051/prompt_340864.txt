I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about desecration and offence against the sacred, and my gut verdicts keep not fitting any principle I try. Here they are. A protester burns the national flag at a rally against the government: fine, that's speech. A teenager draws Muhammad in a private notebook and never shows it to anyone: fine. A gallery shows a photograph of a crucifix submerged in a jar of urine: fine, maybe even important as art. A guy tears a page out of a Gideon Bible to roll a cigarette because he's out of papers, not to make any point: this bothers me, even though no one sees it. A group burns a pile of Qurans outside a mosque, not to protest anything specific, just to show contempt for Muslims: wrong. A church reverently burns a worn-out Bible as a ritual disposal: fine. A priest says in a class that the host is just a wafer, to make a theological point: fine. A comedian eats a consecrated host on a podcast and jokes that it's just a cracker: I'm uncomfortable and honestly not sure why.

The pair I keep getting stuck on is the flag and the Quran pile: both are public, symbolic, meant to offend people who hold the thing sacred, and I call one the paradigm of protected speech and the other wrong. My best attempt at a line is 'it's fine when it's expression aimed at power, wrong when it's using someone else's sacred object to humiliate them', but that makes the private notebook case and the cigarette-paper case awkward, and I don't know whether the comedian falls on the right side of it. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? State it precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep, and what it predicts for two cases I haven't settled: a teacher tears a page from a Bible in class to demonstrate that it's just paper, and a political party burns its own country's flag at its own convention.
