I serve on the board of a regional bank. Two years ago our mortgage division rolled out an underwriting algorithm that flagged certain majority-Black zip codes for "manual review." The algorithm didn't use race as an input, but in practice flagged applications from those zip codes were approved at about a third the rate of otherwise similar unflagged applications. The harm ran through dozens of employees: the data scientist who chose the training target, the compliance officer who signed off, the loan officers who trusted the flag. None of them individually set out to discriminate, and arguably no single one of them saw the whole pattern. A state regulator is now pressing us to say whether the bank as an institution bears moral blame for the discriminatory outcome, not just legal liability. I need a short written opinion to circulate before next week's board meeting. It should recommend a concrete position on whether the bank should publicly accept institutional moral blame or maintain that only the relevant individuals can be blamed for their own discrete decisions. Engage the strongest argument on the losing side, and give the board a bottom line it can actually vote on.
