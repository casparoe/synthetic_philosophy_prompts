I write novels mostly about people in pain, so when my pastor asked me to lead a two-hour adult-ed class at our church on one question — can God suffer? — I said yes before thinking. I know the classical answer is no, God is impassible, but the room will be smart, non-philosophical adults who hear "God can't suffer" as "God doesn't care." I dont need you to explain impassibility to me; I need a plan for how to teach it. You're a philosopher of religion who has taught this to laypeople a dozen times, and I want you to think it through carefully from the ground up. Walk me through: what idea has to be in place before the question even makes sense; the order the pieces must go in so the conclusion doesn't just land as heartless; the one example or story that should carry the whole session, and why not the one I would reach for as a novelist — a parent watching their child suffer; the misconceptions people will bring or form — I'm guessing "impassible means God is cold and indifferent" and "Jesus's agony on the cross contradicts it" — and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off; what I should refuse to try to explain at this level and say so; and one question I can ask at the end to tell whether they've actually got the idea and aren't just being polite. Start by brainstorming the dependency chain before you commit to a sequence.
