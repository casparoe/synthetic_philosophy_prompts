I'm running a session on sphexishness — the old example where a digger wasp stings a cricket to paralyze it, drags it to the burrow entrance, goes inside to inspect, comes back out, and then drags it the rest of the way in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she drags it back to the threshold and inspects again, and she'll apparently keep doing this forever rather than skip the inspection. The moral usually drawn is that her behavior is a fixed action pattern: each step is cued by a specific trigger, and she can't step back and see the loop as a loop.

Write a villanelle from the wasp's point of view in which the two refrains are the two halves of that loop — one about the cricket being back at the threshold, one about the burrow needing another inspection — so the refrains taking turns and then closing the poem together enact the routine's inability to resolve into just pulling the cricket inside. Someone who knows the sphexishness discussion should recognize what each refrain is doing; someone who doesn't should still get a real poem, not a namedrop.
