I've been trying to figure out what my decision theory actually is by gut-checking a bunch of puzzle cases, and my answers don't line up with any theory I know. Here are my verdicts:

- In Newcomb's problem I one-box, no hesitation.
- In counterfactual mugging (Omega flips a coin; tails asks me for $100; heads pays me $10,000 if Omega predicted I'd have paid in tails) I think I should pay when the coin comes up tails and Omega asks for the money. It feels like getting mugged, but I want the heads version of me to get the 10k.
- In Parfit's hitchhiker I pay once I get to the city, even though the driver can't stop me. I want to be the kind of person the driver would have picked up.
- Now the one that bothers me. Suppose it's not Omega but a blackmailer with a perfect simulation of me. She flips a coin. Heads: she pays me $10,000 if she predicted I'd have paid her blackmail demand in tails. Tails: she sends me a blackmail letter demanding $100 or she reveals a secret. I get the letter. My gut says don't pay. But structurally that seems identical to counterfactual mugging: if I pay, actual me loses $100 and the heads version of me gets $10,000; if I don't pay, actual me keeps $100 and heads-me gets nothing. In the Omega case I pay, in the blackmail case I don't. I can't tell whether I'm tracking a real difference (Omega is not threating to reveal a secret, just asking; the blackmailer has an incentive to make me suffer?) or just reacting to the word "blackmail."

A friend said this last one is basically XOR blackmail, or close enough. I don't know. What I want is a principle that gives all my gut verdicts, or at least comes closest and tells me which verdicts I should drop. If there's a difference between paying Omega and paying a blackmailer, spell it out precisely. Then test the principle on a further case: the same setup but the predictor is my identical twin, who genuinely wants both of us to do well. Should I pay there? And if the principle forces me to give up one of my verdicts (say, the Newcomb one-box or the blackmail no-pay), tell me which one and make the case for it being the mistake.
