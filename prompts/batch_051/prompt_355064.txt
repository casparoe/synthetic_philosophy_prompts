I'm TAing a formal epistemology course and last week a student asked me about the Judy Benjamin problem, which I'd only ever heard summarized, and now I realize I'm genuinely torn. Here's the setup so we're all on the same page. A paratrooper, Judy, lands in a swamp at night with no idea where she is. Her map divides the territory into four equal zones: Red territory has a Headquarters Company zone and a Second Company zone, and Blue territory also has a Headquarters Company zone and a Second Company zone. Initially she has equal credence in each of the four zones, so P(Blue) = P(Red) = 1/2. Then HQ radios in: "If you're in Red territory, you're in the Headquarters Company zone." One way to update is to read this as the material conditional, ¬Red ∨ HQ-zone, conditionalize on it, eliminate the Red-and-Second-Company cell, and renormalize the other three cells: then P(Blue) becomes 2/3 and P(Red) becomes 1/3. Another way is to say the message only constrains P(HQ-zone | Red) = 1 and is silent about the Red/Blue split itself, so Judy should keep P(Red) = P(Blue) = 1/2 and just move all her Red mass onto the Headquarters zone. My colleague think the second answer is obviously right; I keep feeling the pull of the first one.

So: give me your credence that the first answer (Blue at 2/3) is the correct update for a fully rational Judy, as opposed to the second answer (Blue at 1/2) or some third answer I haven't considered. I want a number, but mostly I want the reasoning decomposed. In particular: how much of your credence rides on whether indicative conditionals have truth conditions at all, how much on whether this "if" is a material conditional versus an evidential or probabilistic conditional, how much on whether there's a real norm of not changing credences the evidence doesn't directly touch, and whatever else you think belongs. Put a rough probability on each sub-question, show how they combine into the final number, and tell me which sub-question your number is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most.
