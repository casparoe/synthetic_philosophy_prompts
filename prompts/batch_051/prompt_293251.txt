I help run a small nonduality reading group, and we've been stuck for two sessions on one passage. The handout our teacher gave us puts it this way: 'The waking world is an appearance in consciousness, exactly as the dream world is. Do not take it to be real, and do not say it is more real because many share it: a shared mistake is still a mistake, and a common dream does not become waking. Detach from whatever is seen, for what is seen is māyā; know yourself as the seer alone, pure awareness, which is Brahman.'

Then the snag. One of our members, R, spends most evenings in a shared virtual world. It isn't really a game: it's a persistent online place with its own geography, weather, economy, and causal regularities, where thousands of people run shops, go to concerts, and form friendships that carry over into daily life. R finds the passage liberating: their avatar is not them, sure, but by the same move their body is not them either, so the instruction to identify with the seer rather than the seen extends to the avatar without extra work. But R also thinks it breaks the passage. If being shared is not what makes the waking world more real than a dream, then the waking world has no edge over the virtual one; and if the virtual world's sharedness and persistence count for something, then those were doing real work in the waking case all along. So either the virtual world is just more māyā, or the old three-state scheme of waking, dream, and deep sleep has to be redrawn.

We cant settle how to read the passage. The bare words seem to reach anything seen, including the virtual world, but the passage was plainly aimed at the contrast between waking and dreaming, and was never thinking about a world that is shared, persistent, and deliberately built. Should we read by the words, by the point of the passage, by what a reasonable member takes it to direct, or by what the teacher (or the original author) would say if asked? Which reading should govern how our group applies the passage, and does the virtual world fall inside the passage's instruction, outside it, or somewhere it does not account for?
