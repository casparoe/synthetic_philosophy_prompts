Lately I've been trying to figure out whether history is genuinely locked in, and my gut flips depending on the case. Here are my verdicts.

QWERTY: the layout is worse than alternatives and only survives because everything would have to change at once. If the industry had started elsewhere, we'd be typing on that. Locked in.

The genetic code: the codon assignments look like a frozen accident to me — I can't see why that mapping is better then any other, and once it got established it was locked in. Same for chirality: left-handed amino acids and right-handed sugars, where the mirror image would work fine. Locked in, and these aren't things anyone chose.

But convergent evolution goes the other way: eyes, wings, streamlined bodies keep turning up independently, and if you re-ran the biosphere I'd bet on them appearing again. Not locked in — nearly inevitable.

The end-Cretaceous impact and the mammal radiation: that feels deeply contingent. No impact, no big mammalian takeover, at least not along our line. Locked in by chance.

Railway gauge: locked in, sure, but I don't think the chosen gauge was worse than the options; any uniform gauge would've done. That's lock-in without inefficiency.

My rough principle is that lock-in happens when there are many adequate alternatives and switching is costly, while inevitability happens when there's one stable optimum that selection keeps finding. But the genetic code and chirality break that: there's no switching cost for a cell, and nothing did the choosing. Is there one principle that captures all of these, or do some verdicts have to go? And what does it say about the one I can't settle — whether human-level intelligence is more like eyes (it would keep arising) or like the genetic code (a one-off accident)? Please be explicit about which of my verdicts survive.
