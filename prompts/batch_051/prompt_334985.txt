Many Christian traditions, and most sharply the King James Only movement, treat a particular English translation as the word of God itself rather than as a fallible rendering of an inspired original. Yet the movement usually also affirms that the text was inspired in the original Hebrew and Greek, and the broader tradition it descends from treated the Greek Septuagint as Scripture even where its wording departs from the Hebrew. What theory of where meaning and inspiration reside does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguishing what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with: does it require that inspiration attaches to a language-independent content that survives translation, that a translation can become inspired in its own right, or that the English text has simply replaced the originals as the locus of revelation? Then say where the practice is underdetermined between rival theories and whether its commitments pull against one another, and tell me what would have to change in the doctrine if meaning were instead taken to live in the original wording in a way that any translation necessarily loses.
