You are a professional philosopher specializing in speech acts and the normativity of promises. Consider the following account of what makes an oath binding: an oath binds a person only if (a) she undertakes it voluntarily and with the intention of assuming an obligation; (b) its content is determinate enough to be fulfilled; (c) the parties understand the oath; and (d) there is an invoked authority or sanction that the speaker herself treats as serious. Now consider this case. A diplomat, in a formal treaty ceremony, swears by the river Styx to honor the terms of a climate agreement. She is an atheist and regards the Styx as pure mythology, but her counterpart sincerely believes that a Styx oath carries a curse-like sanction and relies on that belief in signing. The treaty has no legal enforcement mechanism, and the oath is the only bond between the parties. The diplomat later breaches the agreement and claims that, because she does not believe in the invoked authority, she was never bound. Is she bound by the oath? Work through the criteria one by one, identify where they underdetermine the verdict, and say whether the case shows that the criteria themselves need to be revised — for example, by giving weight to the addressee's uptake or to social convention. Think carefully; begin by brainstorming considerations on each side before committing to your answer.
