Here is the exam question (10 points):

"Explain the simple counterfactual analysis of dispositions and why many philosophers have thought it needs modifying. In your answer, discuss finkish dispositions, reverse finkish lacks, and masks, and assess how far a Lewis-style reformed conditional analysis can accommodate these cases."

Here is the student's answer:

"The simple counterfactual analysis (SCA) says that an object has a disposition just in case a certain counterfactual is true of it: x is disposed to manifest M under stimulus S if and only if, were x to receive S, x would M. Thus 'this glass is fragile' means that if the glass were struck, it would break. The appeal of the SCA is that it reduces talk of dispositions to talk about what would happen under triggering conditions, without positing any occult causal powers.

Martin's finkish wire shows the analysis fails in one direction. A live wire is disposed to conduct electricity when touched by a conductor. But suppose the wire is connected to a safety device, an 'electro-fink', that detects the contact and instantaneously makes the wire dead. The wire is live, yet the counterfactual 'if it were touched by a conductor, it would conduct' is false, because the contact triggers the device. So the SCA wrongly classifies the live wire as not live. The reverse case shows the converse failure: a dead wire connected to a device that makes it live whenever it is touched would conduct if touched, but before the touch it is not live. Here the conditional is true while the disposition is absent, so the SCA's condition is not sufficient.

To repair the analysis, Lewis proposes that a disposition involves an intrinsic causal basis B of x, such that, if x were to undergo S and retain B long enough, then S and B would jointly cause M. This handles the finkish wire correctly. The live wire has an intrinsic basis for conductivity, and in the counterfactual situation in which it is touched and retains that basis, the touch together with the basis causes conduction. The fact that in the actual world the fink destroys the basis upon contact does not falsify this revised conditional, because the antecedent explicitly holds fixed the retention of the basis.

Masks are a closely related problem, and the retention clause handles them in the same way. A mask is a factor that interferes with an object's retaining its causal basis, in the relevant sense, long enough for the manifestation to occur. Take a fragile glass packed in foam. The glass is fragile, but if it were struck it would not break. On the revised analysis this is not a counterexample: the analysis asks what would happen if the glass were struck while retaining its basis, and in the foam case the glass does not retain its basis through the impact in the way required, so the analysis does not class the glass as non-fragile. A poison swallowed together with an antidote works the same way: the poison is disposed to kill, but the antidote ensures that the toxic basis does not remain in operation long enough, so the analysis again avoids a misclassification. Thus the reform accommodates finks and masks with the same device, and the core counterfactual picture survives once a basis and a retention clause are added."
