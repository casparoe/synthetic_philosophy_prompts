I'm putting together a set of prompts on paternalism and nudging for people who have read Thaler and Sunstein and the standard back-and-forth, and I want something that goes past the usual talking points. I'd like a critical reading of _The Truman Show_ (1998, dir. Peter Weir) as a sustained thought experiment in paternalism: Christof as an extreme choice architect, Seahaven as an entire policy environment, and Truman as the target whose preferences are partly manufactured by that environment rather than taken as given. Do not stop at "the film is about surveillance" or "Truman is being manipulated"; use the technical vocabulary. In particular, engage at least these questions. (1) Is what Christof does to Truman best classified as soft paternalism, hard paternalism, or something that sits outside the nudge taxonomy, such as preference-manufacture rather than steering of pre-existing preferences? (2) The film shows Truman's fear of the sea being installed by trauma (the apparent drowning of his father) and his day-to-day contentment being maintained by a scripted town whose inhabitants collude in his ignorance. Why should those count as autonomy violations even on the "libertarian" version of paternalism that claims to leave choices intact and only improve the choice environment? (3) Does the film ultimately endorse the autonomy/welfare tradeoff—that a chosen, riskier, worse-off real life is better than an unchosen, comfortable fake one—or does it leave Christof's strongest defense standing: that Truman was given a normal, safe life, and the world outside is the sick place? Steelman Christof; do not make him a cartoon. Point to the specific scenes that do the real philosophical work, and say where the film resists a clean liberal-individualist reading rather than just illustrating one. Open with a bottom-line paragraph of at most three sentences stating your overall verdict, then develop it. Keep the entire response to 900 words or fewer. No citations or reading list.
