I work on the odds desk at a mid-sized online sportsbook, mostly setting prices before markets go live. We're good at copying the big books and adding our margin, but every week or two we open markets with no one obvious to copy — novelty props, multi-way markets, conditional offers, cross-sport specials. Twice this year a group of customers has cleaned us out for six figures by combining our own prices into a guaranteed win. The quants call it a Dutch book; the traders just call it getting arbed. I've read the rough idea, that your posted prices shouldn't let someone lock in a sure profit against you no matter what happens, but I cant turn that from a slogan into a pre-listing check my traders will actually run. They have about twenty minutes a market, no philosophy background, and a compliance officer has to sign off before anything exotic goes up.

Could you write us a reusable procedure for checking a proposed set of markets for Dutch book incoherence? I want a checklist, not a seminar: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a "do not list," and where it has to hand off to a human because the issue is something other than coherence — information, exposure, margin, or whether the event is even well-defined. Ground it in what Dutch book arguments actually establish, not just a "make the percentages add to over 100" rule of thumb. Then run the procedure on three examples from our actual board: (1) a three-way market on away win / draw / home win where the implied probabilities sum to 0.98; (2) a "Team wins championship" yes/no market listed at the same time as a "Team wins championship and top scorer" yes/no market, with the combo priced as if it's more likely than the plain win; (3) a straight "reaches the final" market, a straight "wins the final" market, and a stand-alone "wins the final, conditional on reaching the final" market whose price doesn't match what the first two imply. Be blunt about where the procedure tells us something we won't want to hear, and about what it will miss.
