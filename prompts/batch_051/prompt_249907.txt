I keep seeing people use the Kardashev scale as if it measures how advanced a civilization is, full stop, and then someone points out that Barrow already showed you can run the scale inward instead—mastery of the atom, the nucleus, elementary particles, down toward Planck-scale engineering—and the conversation just dies because nobody has actually said what a scale like this is supposed to get right. So I'd like the desiderata for any adequate scale of civilizational advancement. Which cases does it have to handle: a civilization that deliberately powers down while becoming more efficient, one that expands into computation rather than Dyson spheres, one that masters the very small but never leaves its home system, maybe one that plateaus by choice. Which distinctions does it have to respect: capability vs actual deployment, raw energy vs control vs information, detectability vs level of advancement. Which results from physics can it not contradict (thermodynamics, lightspeed limits on coordination). Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just a particular camp's preference. Then flag any pair of desiderata that cant be jointly satisfied—I suspect the objective-and-measurable one and the not-chauvinistic one are in tension, but tell me if the real tension is elsewhere. Finally, briefly score Kardashev and Barrow's inward scale against the list, and say which single desideratum does the most work eliminating the usual candidates. A table is fine for the scorecard part.
