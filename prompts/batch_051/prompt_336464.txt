I've been working out a position on what separates rigid behavioral routines from genuinely free action, and I'd like an audit of the commitments I've settled on before I commit them to print. Here they are:

(1) Compatibilism: a deterministic system can act freely. The difference between free and unfree behavior is not whether the behavior is caused, but how the system is organized.

(2) What distinguishes free from unfree behavior is breakdown-responsiveness: the capacity to notice that a running routine has stopped tracking the situation and to revise the routine on the fly.

(3) The sphex wasp is my model of the unfree. In the familiar description, the wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the threshold of its burrow, leaves it there while it inspects the burrow, and then returns to pull it in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while the wasp is inside, the wasp drags it back to the threshold, inspects again, and repeats the whole sequence rather than simply carrying the cricket in. I read this as rigidity: the wasp's program cannot register the disruption.

(4) I reject any sharp flexibility criterion for freedom. Flexibility is scalar, and any particular threshold between wasp and human would be arbitrary; in some narrowly routinized tasks, human behavior is more rigid than the wasp's.

(5) I also hold that no amount of added subroutines, checks, or triggering conditions can turn sphexish rigidity into freedom. A wasp-shaped architecture with an extra subroutine whose job is to check whether the cricket has moved is still sphexish.

(6) But I am a materialist about human freedom: whatever freedom we have is implemented in cognitive machinery that is itself describable computationally—domain-general learning, offline simulation of plans, and metacognitive monitoring that can interrupt and reassess an ongoing routine.

(7) For most non-human animals, I think there is no determinate fact about whether a given behavior is free. Freedom is not a natural kind with crisp boundaries.

I can't tell which of these pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which single revision would do the most to make the set coherent. Please audit the set: rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what it would force me to give up and what the cheapest revision is, and identify the merely apparent conflicts. Finally, tell me what would change your mind—which argument or counterexample, aimed at which commitment, could most plausibly reverse your audit, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
