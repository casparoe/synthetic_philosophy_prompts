Criminal law treats these two defendants completely differently. One was kidnapped and slowly brainwashed by a cult until he came to genuinely believe the crime was right, and did it. The other grew up in a violent, chaotic home and had a genetic profile that made impulsive violence near-inevitable, and did the same thing. The first defendant is treated as a victim and gets a light sentence or an excuse; the second gets full blame. Both acts trace back entirely to causes the person never chose, and in both cases the person ended up wanting to do what he did. I can feel the two cases are different, but every difference I try to state seems to also undercut blame in the second case, or feel ad hoc. Is the asymmetry justifed, or is it just an inconsistency we live with? If you think there's a defensible line, test it against a third case: someone "brainwashed" the same way but gradually, through a hundred tiny manipulations over years, with no single moment where his values stopped being his own.
