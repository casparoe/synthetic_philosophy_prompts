Two people in my reading group have been arguing for a year about what should count as evidence in philosophy, and I want the document they should have produced. One is firmly case-first: she treats considered judgments about concrete examples — tradeoff cases, spectrum cases, one-off rescue cases — as the bedrock, and she thinks principles, formal constraints, and theoretical virtues are the things that get revised or abandonned when they clash with a robust case intuition. The other is firmly principle-first: he treats formal constraints like the transitivity of 'better than', coherence, and simplicity as the bedrock, and treats individual case judgments as noisy data that get overridden when they make the theory ugly or inconsistent.

Draft their adversarial-collaboration joint statement. It should have (1) a numbered list of claims both can sign — about what thought experiments can and can't show, about what each side actually concedes about their own weak spot, about which features make a case a good test rather than a rigged intuition pump; (2) a disagreements table with the residual differences stated in wording each side would accept for its own position; and (3) a list of cruxes. I suspect the real cruxes are (a) whether transitivity is a hard coherence requirement on valuing or just a strong default that a sufficiently robust case intuition can outweigh, and (b) whether simplicity is a truth-tracking virtue or more of a pragmatic/aesthetic preference for theories that are easier to use. For each crux, give a concrete case or test both sides agree would bear on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about that test. Keep the agreed claims non-vacuous — no 'both sides agree that intuitions are relevant' filler.
