I'm the enforcement policy lead for a large user-generated-content platform. Our rules matrix — non-consensual sexual imagery, hate speech, self-harm, spam, impersonation, coordinated inauthentic behavior — is currently enforced on a mostly strict-liability basis: if content matches, it comes down and a strike is issued, with no inquiry into the user's state of mind. We keep hitting cases where that seems indefensible (an account posting its own medical image) and cases where requiring intent would gut a rule. We revisit the matrix quarterly, and I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in what the philosophy of criminal law actually says about strict liability and mens rea, for deciding rule by rule and penalty-tier by penalty-tier: when may we remove content without proof of fault, when may we punish — strike, suspension, ban — without proof of fault, and when must we require intent, knowledge, recklessness, or negligence? The procedure must be usable by policy staff with no legal or philosophical training, executable in under an hour per rule, and able to produce a rationale we can publish. Don't survey the options: set out the procedure you would actually adopt and defend its choices. Flag each step where the procedure cannot settle the question and human judgment is doing the work. Then apply it to three rules: a zero-tolerance rule against sharing another person's nude image; a rule against coordinated inauthentic behavior; and a spam rule that triggers automatic suspension. At least one outcome should go against the instincts of someone who wants to sound humane.
