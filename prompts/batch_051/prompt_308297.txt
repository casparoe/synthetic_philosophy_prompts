One from a reading group that I keep turning over and can't resolve. (1) You ought to proportion your beliefs to your total evidence. (2) Just before you choose between job A and job B, your total evidence includes a lifetime of information about yourself — your risk aversion, your habits, how you've chosen in exactly these situations. (3) Suppose that evidence supports the prediction that you'll take A to degree 0.9. (4) Then you should believe to degree 0.9 that you'll take A. (5) But if you're still genuinely deliberating, you can't rationally be 90% settled on taking A; being that settled just is having practically made the decision, and there'd be nothing left to deliberate about. (6) So a rational person who knows herself well can't deliberate, and a rational person who deliberates must ignore her own best evidence. That's absurd. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd much rather give up something in (1)–(4) than accept that deliberation is a self-deceptive charade, but I can't see what the something is.
