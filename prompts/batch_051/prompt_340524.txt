I keep returning to a passage in Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* VII.3, where he writes:

"But inasmuch as the last premise, which originates action, is an opinion as to some object of sense, and it is this opinion which the unrestrained man when under the influence of passion either does not possess, or only possesses in a way which as we saw does not amount to knowing it but only makes him repeat it as the drunken man repeats the maxims of Empedocles, and since the ultimate term is not a universal, and is not deemed to be an object of Scientific Knowledge in the same way as a universal term is, we do seem to be led to the conclusion which Socrates sought to establish."

The distinction Aristotle is drawing strikes me as doing real work: the akratic agent can have the general principle available in some sense without its being active in the particular situation, and that is different from having a full occurrent judgment that would actually guide action. But I also think the modern behavioral-economic account of weakness of will as hyperbolic discounting has enormous systematic virtues. It predicts preference reversal, explains how the same person can plan and then fail, models self-control as commitment, and gives a unified treatment of many cases that look like akrasia. These seem to be exactly the phenomena Aristotle is discussing, but the two accounts locate the failure differently: Aristotle says the agent does not really possess the relevant knowledge in the active sense when passion is present, while the discounting account says the agent does have the judgment but discounts the future, so at the moment of action the expected value of indulgence outweighs the plan.

I don't want a compromise slogan. Give me the strongest version of each, then say which should yield: should the Aristotelian distinction be revised to fit the discounting model, or should hyperbolic discounting be rejected as a theory of akrasia because it leaves out the epistemic state Aristotle is pointing at? Is there a reading on which the two are compatible — for instance, where the hyperbolic curve just is the mechanism by which the ultimate premise fails to be actively known — and if so, what exactly would that compatibility require each side to concede?
