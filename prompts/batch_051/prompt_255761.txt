Here's a parallel I can't shake, and I genuinely can't tell if it's deep or superficial. On one side there's the boundary problem in democratic theory: before you can count votes or say what the people want, you have to say who the people even are — who's in the demos. But every rule for drawing that line seems to need the line already drawn. The obvious one, include everyone who is affected by a decision, sounds right until you ask who decides who's affected, and how much, and whether the people who would be affected by changing the boundary itself get a say. People usually describe this as a circularity or a bootstrapping problem.

On the other side there's the part of anthropic reasoning where, before you can assign a probability to a self-locating belief — I'm this particular observer among a bunch of possible observers, not some other one — you need a reference class: the set of observers you're supposed to imagine yourself randomly sampled from. But the physics dosen't seem to pin that down. Are you sampled from all humans who ever lived? All conscious beings? All observer-moments? All copies of you that some branching process could have made? Different choices give different probabilities for the same evidence. The old self-sampling vs. self-indication fight is at least partly about exactly this: how to weight the class you didn't get from the facts.

The parallel nagging me is that both are preconditions for aggregation. You've got a pile of individual inputs — citizens, observer-moments — and a beautiful principle for combining them (majority rule, probability), but the principle only does its work after someone has already drawn the fence around the set. And in both cases the usual ways of drawing the fence either circularly presuppose the thing they were supposed to fix, or regress off into the distance, or get settled by a stipulation that does all the real work while pretending to be neutral.

Is this a real structural identity, a loose analogy, or a confusion? If it's real, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer from one to the other? And can you name something the parallel predicts that turns out false on at least one side?
