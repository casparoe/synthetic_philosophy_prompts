In a seminar on Davidson's program of radical interpretation, one of my professors keeps answering questions by insisting that there is no such thing as a language. Most recently I asked whether an individual word like 'refrigerator' has a fixed meaning laid down by the conventions of English, or whether it acquires only whatever meaning a hearer manages to assign on the fly; he gave me the same slogan and moved on. I can see that the slogan is doing real argumentative work for him, but I cannot tell which work. I would like you to disambiguate it into the distinct precise claims it might express: that there are no shared conventional systems at all; that shared systems exist but underdetermine interpretation; that successful interpretation runs through something other than a shared system; or something else I have not considered. For each reading, say whether it is platitudinous, substantial and defensible, or actually false, and say which of these readings would genuinely support dismissing my question about 'refrigerator' rather than merely evading it.
