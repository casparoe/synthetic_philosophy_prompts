So I keep going back and forth on this and want someone to settle it, or at least draw the map. You know the Knobe effect: people read the chairman vignette, where he only cares about profits and says he doesn't care whether a side effect helps or harms the environment, and they judge that he intentionally harmed the environment but didn't intentionally help it. The standard x-phi reading is that the moral badness of the outcome is doing the work in folk intentionality judgments, which is supposed to matter for action theory and for the whole experimental-philosophy project.

Now the development. Suppose a language model — trained on text, no moral cognition to speak of — gets the same vignettes and gives the same asymmetry: intentional for the harm, not for the help, holding up across paraphrases and swapped names. Assume for the sake of argument this is a solid result. Does Knobe's inference survive? I can steelman both directions. On one hand, if a next-token predictor reproduces the pattern, maybe the pattern was never evidence about moral cognition, just about how the vignette is worded or how English talks about side effects. On the other hand, the model learned everything it knows from human text, so maybe it just picked up the folk pattern, and its a point in favor of the pattern being robust rather than a lab artifact.

The question: is the Knobe effect, as evidence that moral evaluation shapes folk intentionality attribution, dead, wounded but repairable, or untouched? Your answer should say which premise of the standard argument the LLM result bears on, and whether that premise can be restored in another form. If its dead, say what the remaining phenomenon should be called instead; if it survives, say what an LLM would have to do to actually threaten it.
