Before I was a science and ideas journalist I spent five years on the insurance beat, so I still reflexively turn ideas into actuariese—hazard rates, exposure, reserves, model risk, the gap between a life table and an actual life. Lately I've been reading the philosophy-of-biology argument about whether natural selection is an empty truth, the old worry that "the fittest" just means "the ones that survived and reproduced," and the standard reply that fitness is a propensity: a real, objective chance of reproductive success rather than the actual outcome. I can follow it in plain English, but I suspect it would snap into focus in my old vocabulary.

So: take the propensity interpretation of fitness, along with the tautology objection it's meant to answer, and restate the whole thing in the language of life insurance. Be strict about it—what translates cleanly (expected reproductive success onto the mean of a distribution, I'd guess), what only crosses over if you stretch it, and what has no actuarial expression at all. The passage I keep staring at is Darwin, On the Origin of Species (1859), chapter IV, "Natural Selection":

"This preservation of favourable variations and the rejection of injurious variations, I call Natural Selection. Variations neither useful nor injurious would not be affected by natural selection, and would be left a fluctuating element, as perhaps we see in the species called polymorphic."

Read through my old eyes, he sounds like an underwriter who defines a good risk as one the company kept and a bad risk as one it dropped, then claims the underwriting explains the portfolio's performance—which is exactly the circularity the philosophers are complaining about. So tell me whether that reading is fair, where the propensity translation breaks the circle and where it just relabels the same problem, and then give me the residue report: is the stuff that won't translate a defect in the actuarial frame, or a hint that the propensity veiw was always a bit of a black box dressed up in probabilities?
