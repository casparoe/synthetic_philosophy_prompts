In his Leviathan (chapter 5), Hobbes distinguishes science from mere experience or prudence by treating science as knowledge of consequences, starting from definitions, and he holds that such knowledge is attainable only where we can know causes. Geometry is his paradigm case because we construct the figures ourselves, so we know that the generated properties follow from our own definitions. Now consider a modern automated theorem prover that verifies a nontrivial Euclidean theorem by mechanically checking every inference against a finite set of axioms and inference rules. The full chain of consequences is certified as valid, but no human traces the proof step by step, the figure is never drawn or physically constructed, and the machine has no understanding of the definitions or causes at issue. Does the machine-verified derivation qualify as Hobbesian science? Work through Hobbes's criteria one by one, note where the case exposes underdetermination in those criteria, and then judge whether the case shows that the criteria themselves need revision.
