I sit on my university's student conduct board. A junior has admitted to submitting an AI-generated paper as her own work, but in her written statement she asks the board to excuse the conduct without the usual sanctions. Her argument is that the act was the full causal product of her cognitive profile, her family background, and two years of a campus culture in which AI assistance was pervasive and generally treated as an academic-integrity gray area. Given that causal history, she claims, she could not have done otherwise, and punishing her would be blaming her for something she did not freely choose. Two board members regard this as an irrelevant detour from the code's plain requirements; two others regard it as a live challenge because our honor code tells students they are accountable as authors of their choices. Draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Friday's meeting: recommend a concrete disposition (responsibility with full sanction, responsibility with reduced sanction, or no responsibility), state the principle the board should apply to this kind of defense—including whether responsibility is compatible with a fully caused action or requires that the student could have done otherwise—and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side.
