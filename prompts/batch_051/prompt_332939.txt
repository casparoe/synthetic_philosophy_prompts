I'm a med student and I keep getting tripped up by the way the legal system handles a person's refusal of treatment — it seems to flip between treating the refusal as a trump card and treating it as almost irrelevant. A competent adult can walk into an ED, refuse a lifesaving blood transfusion for reasons the doctors find terrible, and after a psych consult confirms competence that's basically the end of the converstation: we argue, we bring in the family, but if the person keeps saying no, we let them die. Yet that same adult, as a parent, generally cannot refuse that same transfusion for their kid; the state will step in. And the same system will detain someone with infectious TB and medicate them against their will, and during the pandemic lots of nurses and care-home staff lost their jobs for refusing a vaccine. So a 'no' about what goes into a body is close to absolute when the person is only harming themselves, and counts for surprisingly little once someone else is exposed or dependent.

I'm trying to figure out what theory of rights this pattern actually presupposes, because the practice looks consistent on the surface but I can't tell what's doing the work. Is it that rights are trumps — the kind of thing you can't just outweigh for the greater good — only for self-regarding choices, and something weaker (side constraints overriden by a serious enough threat, or just instruments for welfare) once other people are in the balance? What would the threshold even be, and does the law actually track one, or is it drawing a different line entirely? Or is the rights talk just decoration over a big welfare calculation, with the real principle being something like: minimize serious harm, but leave competent adults alone when the harm is only to themselves? Reconstruct the implicit theory as best you can, separate the parts of the practice that genuinely force that theory from the parts that merely fit with it, and then tell me what would have to change in either the treatment-refusal side or the public-health side if we swapped in strict trumps, straight-up welfare maximization, or dropped the concept of rights altogether.
