Two of my friends are getting married, and Ive been asked to give a toast at the dinner. Alix is an early modern scholar who wrote a dissertation on Spinoza's account of eternity, and Sam is a family doctor who hasnt read Spinoza and would prefer to keep it that way. The room will be half academics (including a few Spinoza specialists who will catch every slip — if the speech says the couple chose each other freely, or that things could have turned out differently, or treats God as an ordinary theistic blessing, they will wince) and half Sam's non-philosophy family. I want a five-minute toast that is genuinely built on Spinoza's necessitarianism: nothing could have been otherwise, and the right way to love something is to understand it as following necessarily from the whole order of nature rather than to cling to it as a piece of contingent luck we might lose. Maybe work the contrast between loving from inadequate ideas (gripping a thing as if it might not be there) and loving it adequately, and let it land on something like Spinoza's intellectual love of God without making the non-philosophers feel their at a seminar or a church service. There should be one joke that both halves of the room actually laugh at. And the ending should be about the couple, not about how clever the Spinoza stuff is.
