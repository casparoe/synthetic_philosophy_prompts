I've been trying to get clear on exactly what Good's theorem rules out. The way I learned it, for a standard expected-utility maximizer, costless information can't have negative expected value, because you can always just ignore it if you don't like what it tells you. A friend keeps saying there are obvious counterexamples if the agent is present-biased, so I tried to build one, and now I can't tell whether I've built a real counterexample or just quietly changed which agents utility we're talking about.

Here's the case. A woman tonight must decide whether a free text message with her type will be sent to her tomorrow morning. The message says either low or high, each with probability .5. Tomorrow she chooses whether to have a drink. The drink gives 10 utils of immediate pleasure; if her type is low, the delayed health cost is 15 utils, and if high, it's 100. She is present-biased in the quasi-hyperbolic sense with beta = .5 and delta = 1: the tomorrow self treats the drink's pleasure as current and discounts the delayed cost by .5, while the tonight self discounts both tomorrow's pleasure and the delayed cost by .5. So tomorrow, if no message arrives, the expected utility of drinking is .5(10 - 50) + .5(10 - 7.5) = -18.75, so she refrains; if the message arrives, she drinks when it says low and refrains when it says high. From tonight's perspective, though, drinking in the low state is worth .5(10) + .5(-15) = -2.5, and drinking in the high state is worth -45. Allowing the message therefore gives her expected utility, from tonight's perspective, of .5(0) + .5(-2.5) = -1.25, while suppressing it gives 0.

So she should prefer to suppress the message, right? And if so, which premise in the standard Good argument actually fails? I keep going back and forth between thinking the failure is the you can always ignore the information step, and thinking the deeper issue is just that the self who chooses whether to get the information and the self who later acts have different utility functions, so Good's theorem never even applied. Also, is it right to say this shows a present-biased agent can rationally be made worse off by free information, or is that a misleading description of whats going on?
