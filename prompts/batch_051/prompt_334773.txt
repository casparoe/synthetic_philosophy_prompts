I am the student member of my school district's educational technology committee. We are deciding whether to approve a one-year pilot of an AI-assisted writing tutor for high school social studies. The vendor requires the district to settle on a values policy before classroom use: when a student asks the tool about a contested moral or political issue, it can be set to answer in line with the majority view of district families as measured by survey, to present the main live positions and decline to endorse any of them, or to tailor its answers to the values each student's parents report at registration. The committee is split: some members favor the majority setting so the tool reflects the community, others say a public school should present the positions without taking sides, and still others say only parents may decide which values their children are exposed to, adding that a so-called neutral setting is itself a value-laden choice. I need to circulate a short decision memo before our vote. Please draft it: lead with a bottom-line recommendation to approve or reject the pilot with a specific values-policy configuration, give reasons the vendor and district attorneys could act on, and take seriously the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write it as continuous prose, the way a memo is actually written to colleagues, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
