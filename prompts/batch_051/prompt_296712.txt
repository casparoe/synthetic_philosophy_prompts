Over the past year I have been trying to get my views on insecticides and insect welfare into a stable package, and I would like an audit of where the package still strains against itself. I am not looking for a general introduction to insect sentience; I want you to take the content of my commitments as given and tell me whether they jointly hold.

Here is where I have landed.

1. I assign a low but non-negligible probability — somewhere in the region of 3–10% — to sentience in the common pest insects affected by household and agricultural insecticides (aphids, larval moths, beetles, cockroaches). By sentience I mean a capacity for felt pain, or something morally close to it.
2. I accept an expected-value approach to decisions under moral uncertainty: a 5% chance of a given quantity of suffering should weigh in the calculus at roughly 5% of that quantity, provided the underlying welfare assumption is credible rather than merely conceivable.
3. Taken seriously, (1) and (2) seem to imply that the expected insect suffering generated by routine insecticide use, globally and across time, is enormous — plausibly large enough in expectation to outrank many conventional human-centric causes on a pure welfare accounting.
4. My practical stance has not moved much: I still use ant bait or fly spray during an infestation, and I do not currently favour banning routine agricultural insecticide use, pending clearer evidence about alternatives and crop yields.
5. I have a standing suspicion of conclusions that move from expected value being enormous but deeply model-sensitive to the demand that one reorient one's life around it. That move feels like fanaticism, and I take fanaticism to be a real failure mode of expected-value reasoning, not just a pejorative.
6. I also think it is epistemically dishonest to defuse the scale argument by quietly rounding p(sentience) down to zero, or by asserting that insects cannot matter morally because the conclusion is repugnant or inconvenient.

Are these commitments jointly tenable? Which are the genuine fault lines, ordered by how severely they threaten the package? For each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would be. Also flag any pair that looks inconsistent at first pass but turns out to be fine, so the audit separates real tensions from apparent ones. If you think the whole exercise is misframed — for instance, that my use of p(sentience) or expected value in (2) is not doing coherent work here — tell me that plainly rather than grading the set as posed. Please write your answer in American English.
