I'm writing a paper for a political philosophy course and keep getting stuck on what makes a ground of discrimination *suspect* in the first place. The usual criteria we covered — immutability, historical disadvantage, political powerlessness, irrelevance to merit — all seem to me like downstream symptoms rather than the basic feature. Left-handedness is immutable and often irrelevant to job performance, but nobody thinks it marks a suspect classification. Religion can be changed, yet it's protected. So I've come up with a label I'm not sure deserves to survive: suspect grounds are "prohibited proxies for status." My idea is that a characteristic becomes a suspect ground exactly when, in a particular society, using it to allocate benefits and burdens is not merely using a heuristic but is effectively the same move as reinscribing a hierarchy between kinds of people. Race is suspect because employment or housing decisions keyed to race are, historically and functionally, part of maintaining a caste-like ranking. Age is not suspect in the same way because age-based rules can track a genuine life-cycle function. Sex is weird because it combines status subordination with real biological difference. Disability is weird too because some disabilities really do affect job performance. 

The theory is meant to explain why the list of suspect grounds shifts across societies: status systems differ, even if some recur. But I'm worried "status" is doing too much work and the view is circular — it seems to say a ground is suspect because using it marks people as lower-status, and using it marks people as lower-status because the ground is suspect. Also, if a society genuinely believed, say, astrological sign indicated moral worth, would my view force me to treat sign as suspect? That seems both too generous to nonsense and too parochial. Tell me, honestly: is there a recognisable position hiding in this, has someone already said it better, and what's the strongest objection that either kills the view or forces it into a much less interesting version? Please write your answer in British English.
