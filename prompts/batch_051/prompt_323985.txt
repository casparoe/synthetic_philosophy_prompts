i keep turning an argument over, and i can't find which step to reject even though the conclusion feels like it gives away the farm. It's about whether Darwin's and Turing's inversions generalize to every bit of cognition. Here it is:

(1) Natural selection can produce an organism that behaves adaptively—even in smart-looking ways—without the organism representing why the behavior works. A bacterium swims toward sugar because the ones that did left more descendants, not because it gets the chemistry.
(2) Turing showed that a machine that does nothing but mechanically shuffle symbols according to rules can compute anything a human following an effective procedure can, with no grasp of what the symbols mean.
(3) So we already have two kinds of case where full behavioral competence comes apart from comprehension: evolved attunement and formal computation.
(4) A human solving a problem, doing arithmetic, or following a conversation is, at bottom, a network of neurons that was built by evolution and that carries out computations in roughly the Turing sense.
(5) Therefore each human competence can be explained by some combination of the mechanisms in (1) and (2), and no appeal to understanding ever needs to appear in the explanation.
(6) If a phenomenon can be fully explained without appeal to understanding, then understanding is not doing causal or explanatory work in that phenomenon.
(7) So understanding is not doing any real work in any human cognitive achievement. It's a user-illusion, or at best an idle side-effect.

I half-suspect the trouble is in (3) or (5), but whenever i try to pinpoint it, i end up saying something question-begging, namely that those mechanisms just are not comprehension. Tell me straight: is the argument valid, and if not, which step exactly fails? For whatever step fails, give the strongest reading of it on which it would be true, and then check whether the argument still goes through.
