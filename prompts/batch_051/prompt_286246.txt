The standard contract everyone signed at my last job included this clause: 'At all times during and after their employment, employees shall act in the best interests of the Company and shall refrain from any statement or action that could reasonably be expected to damage the Company's reputation or business interests.' I want to know whether a former coworker of mine is in breach of it.

We worked at a company whose software recommends drug dosages. She found that for patients under 45 kg the algorithm would sometimes output roughly double the correct dose, and that the company had known for about a year but decided a fix and recall wasn't worth the money because only a small share of patients was affected. She went through the internal reporting process four times, and each time the ticket was marked 'resolved' while the code stayed the same. After she left, she published an anonymous blog post explaining the bug in technical detail and telling clinicians to recalculate doses manually for low-weight patients. She never named the company in the post, but her profile on the same site said she was a former engineer there, so it wasn't hard to connect. The company is now threatening to enforce the clause.

The clause was plainly written to stop disgruntled ex-employees badmouthing the firm or taking clients, and nobody involved ever imagined an engineer warning hospitals about a dangerous product. What does the clause actually require of her? I'm less intrested in the legal outcome than in what the plain words, the purpose of the clause, what the people who wrote it would have said if asked, and what a reasonable employee would have understood each give, and which reading should win here.
