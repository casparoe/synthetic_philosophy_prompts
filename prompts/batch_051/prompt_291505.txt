I'm in a course on well-being and value theory, and one question keeps recurring: whether novelty is a distinct component of a good life, as opposed to merely a means to something else like richer emotion or better memory. Before I can assess that, I need a working definition of novelty — as a property of an experience, an activity, or a stretch of a life, rather than merely of a stimulus.

Here are six cases I'd like the definition to handle. I think the first is a clear case of novelty and the last a clear case of its absence, but I'm genuinely unsure about the four in between.

1. A teenager travels alone to a foreign city for the first time; every sight, sound, and daily routine is unfamiliar.
2. Someone rereads a novel after a decade and finds an entire theme they had missed the first time; the text is unchanged, but the encounter feels newly significant.
3. A commuter rides the same route every day for years, but keeps noticing small changes — the river's color, the new displays in a store window — and reports that the ride rarely feels stale.
4. A cruise passenger visits fourteen countries in a month, but each port follows the identical schedule: bus, landmark, buffet, ship.
5. A resident spends an afternoon walking down an alley in their own city that they have somehow never entered; the street is unremarkable but entirely unfamiliar.
6. A person takes their fortieth skydive; all the elements are long familiar, they no longer feel any surprise, though they remain absorbed.

I suspect novelty is located not in the object itself but in some relation between a person's history and their present attention, but I'm not sure that suspicion survives the commuter and the cruise passenger. I also can't tell whether novelty has to be experienced as novelty in order to count. And I'd like the definition to say how novelty differs from mere variety: case 4 seems to show that you can have variety without novelty, so I'd like to know whether the reverse is possible.

Please propose a definition, run it through all six cases, and say where it gives the wrong verdict or no verdict, then revise it or defend the surprising results. Also address directly whether novelty must be felt. Finally, separate what is settled among people who work carefully on this topic from what is genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
