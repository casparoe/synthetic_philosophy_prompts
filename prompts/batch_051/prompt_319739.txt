I'm running an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have very different views about corporate moral blame. One camp, call them the reductionists, holds that when we say BP is blameworthy, we are speaking loosely: only human beings can be the ultimate objects of moral blame, and corporate blame decomposes into blame of specific individuals, cultures, or incentive structures. The other camp, call them the non-reductionists, holds that a suitably organized corporation is a genuine moral agent in its own right, over and above the people inside it, and that blame can land on that collective agent without automatically distributing to every employee or even to any particular subset.

Please act as a neutral facilitator who is also a professional philosopher specializing in moral responsibility and agency. Think carefully, and start with a short brainstorm of possible formulations for each side before writing the final document.

Produce the draft joint statement of their adversarial collaboration. I want it to be specific and non-vacuous: the thing they could actually both sign at the end, with the residual disagreement made sharp. Include:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section. These should go beyond obvious facts and include commitments about what blaming does, what it means for an entity to be an agent, and what counts as evidence about mental states. Make sure neither side would squirm at the wording.

2. A disagreement table with columns: the question, the reductionist formulation, the non-reductionist formulation. Each formulation should be one its own side would accept, not a caricature by the other side.

3. At least three cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one camp. I suspect some of them are: whether a collective decision procedure can produce intentional states that are not identical to any individual's states; whether reactive attitudes like resentment can coherently take a corporation as target without being a stance toward the people; and whether corporate blame does explanatory or practical work beyond individual blame. Feel free to modify or add cruxes if you think those are no good.

4. For each crux, one concrete case or piece of evidence that both camps agree would bear on it. Make it realistic, ideally a case where a company's policy is worse than any individual's decision but the harm seems overdetermined. For each case, record both sides' predictions in advance, with enough detail that we could later say whose prediction was closer.

Use Hobbes's Leviathan chapter 16 as shared conceptual background. He distinguishes natural persons from artificial persons, and describes a corporation as a group of men made one person by being represented, then connects that to authorization and ownership of acts. Don't quote him, but apply the machinery: the non-reductionist will likely say this shows an entity can own actions through representation without being a natural human; the reductionist will likely say it shows it is all legal fiction and authorization still traces back to the natural persons. Work that interpretive disagreement into the agreed claims and cruxes where it helps, but don't let it become a pointless historical dispute.
