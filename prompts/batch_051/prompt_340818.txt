I'm trying to settle what I actually believe about whether and when a translated text can be the inspired word of God, going case by case rather than by the usual slogans. Here are my verdicts:

On the Septuagint: the New Testament authors quote the Greek Old Testament, sometimes at points where its wording diverges from the Hebrew text we now possess, and I accept those quotations as carrying the full authority of Scripture despite the divergence.

On KJV-onlyism: I reject the claim that the King James Version, or any English translation, is inspired in its English wording so that it can correct the Hebrew and Greek. I feel a translation can be authoritative only derivatively, through its source.

On formal-equivalence translations: a scrupulously literal rendering I treat as Scripture for teaching and preaching, but I would not call its English words inspired; only the meaning they convey is.

On free paraphrases: a very loose, dynamic-equivalent rendering I treat as useful edifying commentary, not as Scripture.

On a cross-cultural rendering: a missionary translates the phrase Lamb of God into a language spoken by people who have no word for lamb and keep no sheep, so she renders it as piglet of God; native speakers insist this conveys exactly the right sense in their idiom, though a literal back-translation would be piglet. I accept her rendering as genuinely the Word of God for that community.

On a textual correction: when an older manuscript discovery shows that a verse I grew up with is a later addition, I accept the corrected, shorter text as authoritative, even though it clashes with the familiar translation.

Is there a single account of inspired translation that captures all, or nearly all, of these verdicts? Which of them does it force me to drop, and why? And what does the resulting account say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a church that canonizes a translation containing several doctrinally significant mistranslations, where those mistranslations then shape its doctrine; is that translation scripture for them, edifying error, or something else?
