I recently rewatched Christopher Nolan's The Prestige and have been unable to stop thinking about the machine Tesla builds for Angier. As the film presents it, the machine does not simply carry a person from one place to another: each time it is switched on there are two Angiers, and during his act one of them drowns in a tank beneath the stage while the other appears somewhere else and receives the applause. Angier himself says that he climbs into the machine night after night without knowing which of the two he will be. I have also been mulling over a thought experiment I once read about and believe is associated with Derek Parfit — a teletransporter that scans and destroys your body here while assembling an exact copy elsewhere, together with the suggestion that what matters for survival may not be being the very same object over time but something more like an overlapping chain of memory, character, and mental life.

I am planning to show the film to a small group and lead a discussion afterward, and I would like an honest reading of it through that lens. Does The Prestige dramatize the thought that identity is not what matters in survival, or does it resist that thought? Each performance seems to produce two psychologically continuous successors of the man who steps into the machine, and the film is obsessed with the difference between the one who is drowned and the one who takes the bow. Which scenes or decisions would you point to, and where does the film complicate or push back against the philosophical picture rather than merely illustrating it? I am especially interested in what the film might add to the abstract thought experiment — something about dread, ambition, or the audience's wish for a neat story — that a bare argument about survival leaves out.
