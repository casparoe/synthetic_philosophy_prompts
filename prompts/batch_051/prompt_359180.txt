Write a poem in terza rima (the interlocking aba bcb cdc rhyme chain) about the three concepts of liberty — negative, positive, and republican — and make the form do the arguing. The opening tercets should sell negative liberty for real: freedom as nobody muscling into your business, a clear space where you can act without being blocked. Let the reader buy it. Then the middle tercets should bring in the case that cracks it: a slave whose master is kind and never interferes, yet everything the slave does is still on sufferance. Dont say the word "domination" too early; make the reader feel the gap between "nobody stopped me" and "nobody could have." Give one tercet to positive liberty, freedom as self-mastery, and show rather than tell why it's the dangerous one: the self splits into a higher ruler and a lower appetite, and once that split is in place someone else can claim to speak for your real self and rebrand coercion as setting you free. Then land on the republican concept in the closing tercets: freedom as not living under another person's arbitrary will, secured by institutions instead of by the master's good mood. The structural rule: no three labeled sections. Each tercet's middle line should name the exact worry the current concept can't settle, so the rhyme scheme hands the problem — and the poem — to the next tercet. End by commiting to the republican concept as the one that got the original instinct right, not as one flavor among three. The test: a political philosopher should recognize Berlin's worry about positive liberty and the Pettit-style case for non-domination, while a reader who's never heard those names should still be able to follow the argument through the poem.

After the poem, add a short note of around 150 words on where you've taken a side or simplified. Answer it like you mean it: is republican liberty a repair of negative liberty or a genuinely third concept, and does the poem rest on the contested claim that non-domination can't be reduced to security against interference. Commit to the reading the poem needs and say what it loses if that reading were wrong.
