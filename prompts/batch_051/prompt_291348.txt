I am a software engineer at a regional planning agency, with no formal background in philosophy. My team builds simulation models used to forecast the effects of proposed interventions: housing vouchers, transit changes, public-health messaging, and similar policies. We deliver four to six of these models a year, and they feed public reports and budget decisions, so getting the basic architecture wrong early is expensive and hard to correct later. I have heard that social scientists have a long-running debate called methodological individualism versus holism: roughly, whether explanations of social outcomes need to bottom out in the choices and interactions of individual people, or whether social-structural facts such as institutions, norms, group identities, inequality, and segregation can be legitimate explanatory units of their own. I do not know the details of the debate, and I am not asking for a survey of it. I want its usable content turned into a decision procedure I can actually run at the start of a project.

Please write me a reusable procedure for deciding, for a given simulation project, how the model should represent the social world: at the level of individual agents with heterogeneous attributes, local interactions, and emergent aggregate outcomes; at the level of structural or aggregate variables with relationships among group-level or system-level quantities; or as some explicit combination of the two. My team can rarely build both versions and compare, so the procedure must let us commit early and give my non-modeling director a written rationale she can sign off on.

Please give me the procedure in plain language, as questions to ask in order, with the following: what counts as a sufficient answer to each question; where the procedure stops and says individual-level model or structural-level model; where it says mixed or multilevel, with a specified interface between the two; and where it stops and hands the decision to a person because the choice depends on a theoretical commitment, a value judgment, or a guess about the target system rather than on anything the procedure can settle. I need the hard cases the procedure must handle, for example systems where what individuals do depends on structural facts such as who is segregated, what the law is, or what people believe others expect, but those structural facts are themselves produced by individuals; and systems where a single structural pattern is compatible with many different individual-level mechanisms. I also need you to be honest about where the procedure will misfire.

Then run the procedure on these three proposals, showing the path it takes in each case and the final modeling strategy it recommends:

1. A model of how a rent-subsidy lottery affects which neighborhoods recipient families move into, and through that whether school segregation changes over five years.

2. A model of how a county's public-health campaign about vaccine clinic locations changes no-show rates in different communities, given that the campaign reaches some communities through trusted local organizations and not others.

3. A model of how a change to a social platform's harassment-reporting flow changes the rate at which users report harassment, given that reporting norms differ sharply across subcommunities.

Make sure at least one of the worked cases lands on a recommendation my team will not like for cost, deadline, or data reasons, and say explicitly what the procedure cannot decide for any of them. Finally, please say how confident you are in the claims you make and single out the point you are least sure of.
