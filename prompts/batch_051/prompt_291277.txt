I've come to hold a deliberately qualified version of the Rankean view of historical objectivity. I grant from the outset that historians select, that narrative construction is unavoidable, and that no history is written from a value-free standpoint. What I retain is the conviction that there is a determinate past to which historiography is answerable, and that this answerability does real work: the evidence can make some accounts of a given episode fit materially better than others, and fitting the evidence in this way is an epistemic achievement rather than a purely rhetorical or political one. I find that neither Carr's argument that facts become historical facts only through selection nor Hayden White's argument that histories are shaped by the same plot structures and tropes as literature dislodges this residual core, though I cannot always say exactly why.

I want to know what would count against this position — not the straw Ranke that no serious historian now defends, but the position as I have just stated it. Give me the strongest possible Carr-style and White-style objections, plus any others you think cut deeper, and rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Which of them should a committed defender of the view concede as a genuine defeater, and which should they reject as changing the subject? Are there conceivable findings — about expert disagreement among competent historians, about underdetermination of narrative by evidence, about the psychological or political determinants of historiographical judgment — that would force me to abandon the residual realism? Or has my position been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if it has, is that a defect in a view of this kind or simply the appropriate modesty of a metaphysical claim? I would find it especially useful if you kept three questions separate: whether the past itself is determinate, whether our evidence is ever adequate to settle disputes about it, and whether the activity of writing history can be objective even when it is selective and value-laden. I suspect the interesting defeaters lie at the joints between those three questions, and I would like the map of my position's exposure drawn exactly there.
