I am drawn to Ryle's category-mistake diagnosis of Cartesianism: the idea that believe, want and feel pain name episodes in a private inner theatre arises from treating the mind as a special kind of thing rather than as a way of organising publicly intelligible abilities, tendencies and performances. The view has the standard virtues of a good philosophical theory: it dissolves rather than solves the problem of other minds, avoids positing an extra substance, and unifies mental talk with talk of skills. Yet I cannot shake the firm intuition that a present pain, or a sudden insight, has an occurrent, felt character that is not exhausted by any set of dispositions to behave or to report how things seem. I do not want a verdict on which side wins. I want to know what each move costs: what would have to be true of the intuition for it to merit debunking as a leftover of the very picture Ryle diagnosed; what would have to be true of the theory for it to be amended to admit occurrent consciousness without reintroducing the ghost; and whether there is a reading of mental term or disposition on which both survive. Please write your answer in British English.
