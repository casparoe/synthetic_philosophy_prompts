Here is the exam question:

In varying-domain Kripke semantics for quantified modal logic, a frame includes a set of worlds W, an accessibility relation R, and a function D assigning a domain D(w) to each world w. State the Barcan formula (BF) and the converse Barcan formula (CBF) precisely. Then identify, for each formula, the condition on the domains along R—increasing (wRv implies D(w) ⊆ D(v)), decreasing (wRv implies D(v) ⊆ D(w)), or neither—that is necessary and sufficient for its validity on a frame, and explain why by tracing the order of the quantifiers. Finally, distinguish these two claims: (a) every object in D(w) belongs to D(v) for every accessible v, and (b) every object in D(v) already belongs to D(w) whenever wRv. Say which of (a) or (b), if either, corresponds to BF, and which to CBF, and connect your answer to the common slogan that BF concerns whether new objects can appear in accessible worlds.

Student's answer:

BF is ∀x□Fx → □∀xFx, and CBF is □∀xFx → ∀x□Fx. In constant-domain semantics both are valid, since the quantifier ranges over the same objects at every world.

With varying domains, BF is valid on frames where domains are increasing along R: whenever wRv, D(w) ⊆ D(v). The antecedent ∀x□Fx makes every object in D(w) satisfy F at every accessible world. To obtain ∀xFx at an accessible world v, we need each object a in D(v) to be F at v. Since domains increase, a is in D(w), so the antecedent already covers a, and F holds of a at v. Hence BF cannot fail on increasing-domain frames. The condition is also necessary, since if some object at v were not in D(w), the antecedent would tell us nothing about it.

CBF is valid on frames where domains are decreasing along R: whenever wRv, D(v) ⊆ D(w). Suppose □∀xFx holds at w. Then at every accessible world, all objects in that world's domain are F. Take any d ∈ D(w) and any accessible v. Since domains decrease, d ∈ D(v), so the universal claim at v applies to d, giving Fd at v. Therefore d is F at every accessible world, and ∀x□Fx holds at w.

On the interpretive question, BF expresses claim (a): every object that exists at a world exists in every accessible world, so nothing that actually exists could have failed to exist. That is why BF is controversial, since it seems to make actually existing objects into necessary existents. CBF, by contrast, corresponds to claim (b) and only rules out new objects appearing in accessible worlds.
