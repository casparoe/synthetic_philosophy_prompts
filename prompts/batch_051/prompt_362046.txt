I am plotting a novel whose protagonist is an exacting Kantian, and I need a single scene built to specification. Construct a realistic dilemma from ordinary professional or family life in which a maxim passes Kant's contradiction-in-conception test but fails the contradiction-in-willing test: the agent can coherently conceive of the maxim as universal law, but cannot rationally will the resulting world. Constraints: no lifeboat cases, no institutionalized wrongdoing, and the maxim should be one a thoughtful person might sincerely adopt. The case must not stipulate the verdicts; they should be generated by applying the tests. After the scenario, do the following in order. State the maxim precisely. Describe the world in which it is universal law. Show why universalizing it involves no contradiction in conception. Then show what contradiction or incoherence emerges at the stage of willing, and why. Identify the structural feature responsible for the split, say what would happen to the case if that feature were removed, and finish with how a defender of the maxim would most plausibly resist your analysis. I care less about which maxim you choose than about the reasoning being visible at each step.
