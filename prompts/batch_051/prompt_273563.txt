So in my law class we were going on about laws that are technically still in force but that nobody bothers with anymore (I think the fancy term is desuetude, though I might have that slightly off). It got me thinking about a system where the law actually notices when it's being ignored.

Here's the rule I came up with. Every statute gets a enforcement clock. If a law hasn't been used in an actual prosecution for 15 years, it automatically becomes unenforceable. For the last three years of that period it gets flagged as "dormant" on some public register, so everyone can see it's about to lapse. Once it lapses, a court has to treat it as void unless parliament re-enacts it properly. A single prosecution at any point resets the clock to zero, and warnings or arrests don't count, only actual charges or convictions do (I honestly can't decide which, so maybe that itself is part of the question).

How would this actually play out? I'm not asking whether it's a good idea in some abstract way, I want you to trace the incentives it creates for police, prosecutors, politicians and ordinary people. Who would game it, and how? Would you get token prosecutions just to keep a dodgy law alive, or people quietly making sure a law they hate never gets enforced so it dies? What happens to the rule of law when leaving a law unenforced becomes a way of repealing it? Would the failure modes be fixable, or is the whole design just moving the problem somewhere else?
