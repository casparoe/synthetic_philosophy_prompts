Ok so for my world cultures class we have to write a dialogue between two people who genuinely disagree about something moral, not a fake one where one guy's just there to look dumb. I definately want to do it on this thing I noticed when I did an exchange year in Germany and then came home to my friends in the US. The rules-versus-conscience thing. My host family acted like I was crazy for crossing a completely empty street when the little red man was showing, and my host mom said rules are rules because if everybody gets to decide for themselves when a rule "really" applies, the whole thing falls apart. But back home my American friends thought that was kind of sheepish, like you should follow a rule because you've actually thought about it and agree with it, not just because it's a rule. I want the dialogue to really dig into which of these ways of thinking is better, not just "cultures are different."

Make one character defend something like: rules are what let strangers trust each other, treating your own judgment as better than the rules is a kind of arrogance, and rules only work if they're not optional. The other should defend something like: choosing to follow a rule for your own reasons is more free and more moral than just obeying, and the rule-following position can tip into obeying bad rules just because they're there. But both have to be smart and respond to each other's actual points, not talk past each other. Maybe set it around something concrete, like they're roommates or one is visiting the other's country and they keep hitting small rule situations (crossing streets, recycling, a group project, admitting a small mistake). And please don't end with a cheap "both sides have a point." If one side's arguments come out stronger, let them win, but make the other side land real hits so it stays close. Keep it like 600-800 words and don't use a ton of philosophy jargon, since I might have to perform part of it in class.
