An argument I've been turning over and can't quite assess: (1) A finite mind can be in only finitely many possible total experiential states. (2) If such a mind lived forever, its mental life would be an infinite sequence of total experiential states drawn from that finite set. (3) In any infinite sequence over a finite set, there comes a time after which every state that occurs is one that has already occurred earlier. (4) So an immortal finite mind would eventually reach a point after which no new total experiential state ever occurs. (5) Therefore an immortal finite mind would eventually have a mental life wholly without novelty. Is this argument valid? If not, exactly which step fails? If it is valid, is the move from (4) to (5) legitimate? Identify the reading of 'novelty' on which it is legitimate, if any.
