You are a teaching assistant marking a short-answer question in an undergraduate course on the moral psychology of atrocity. Below are the question and a student's answer. Grade the answer out of 10. Present your assessment as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. The first item should state the numerical grade and the principal justification for it; the remaining four should isolate the most important specific merits and errors in the answer and explain how each bears on the grade.

Question: Browning and Goldhagen offer competing explanations of why the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 killed Jews during the Holocaust. State each author's central explanatory claim and identify one kind of evidence each author emphasizes.

Student answer: Browning argues that the battalion's members were ordinary German men rather than committed Nazis, and that their participation in mass killing is best explained by obedience to authority: once ordered to shoot, they obeyed because they feared the consequences of refusing. He emphasizes Major Trapp's offer to let the men step aside before the first massacre, noting that hardly anyone accepted it, and he takes this to show that the opt-out was largely symbolic. Browning therefore regards antisemitism as essentially irrelevant to the battalion's conduct. Goldhagen, in contrast, argues that the men killed willingly and often with initiative because they were driven by a deeply rooted, distinctively German eliminationist antisemitism. He emphasizes the zeal and excess that some killers displayed, which he claims a purely situational account cannot explain.
