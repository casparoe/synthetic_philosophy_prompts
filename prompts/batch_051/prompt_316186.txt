Construct a thought experiment in which a clinic director diverts a scarce preventive medication to the staff of a homeless shelter, although the county’s official protocol would send it to the oldest residents first. The case should be engineered so that Kant’s Formula of Universal Law from the Groundwork and Hare’s universal prescriptivism both forbid the diversion, but for different structural reasons — Kant because the maxim cannot be willed as a universal law, Hare because the director cannot accept the universal prescription when he occupies the position of an older resident who is made worse off — while Singer’s generalization argument permits it, because in the scenario as specified, the consequences of all similarly situated clinic directors doing the same are better than the outcome of official priority. Constraints: realistic, one paragraph for the scenario, no science-fictional devices, no perfect predictors, and the conflict must not be generated by stipulating disagreements about the nonmoral facts. After the scenario, walk through each theory’s verdict and reasoning, identify the feature of the case that is doing the real work, and say how a defender of Singer could argue that the other two tests are treating irrelevant agents as especially privileged. Finally, say how confident you are in the verdicts and single out the point you are least sure of.
