Here's a setup. Two small lights sit side by side in an otherwise dark room. The left one flashes red, and a hundred or so milliseconds later the right one flashes green. Subjects don't report two stationary flashes. They report a single dot gliding from left to right that changes from red to green partway along the path — and they insist they saw the color change before the second light ever came on. So the later green flash seems to reach backward and color the dot's earlier trajectory.

Two ways to tell the story. The Orwellian story: the subject really did consciously experience something closer to the raw sequence — maybe two separate flashes, or a red dot that never got to the right side — but after the green flash arrived, the brain rewrote the memory of the experience, and now the subject honestly remembers a smooth, color-shifting dot. The Stalinesque story: the brain withholds constructing the experience until both flashes are in, then serves up a finished, cleaned-up version. The subject's memory is accurate to what they experienced; it's the experience itself that was synthesized late, so there never was an earlier conscious draft.
