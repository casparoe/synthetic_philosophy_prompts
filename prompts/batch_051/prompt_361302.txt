I've been going around in circles on the doctrine of double effect. The standard move, as I understand it, is that terror-bombing a city to break the enemy's will is worse than bombing a munitions factory even though you know civilians will die, because in the second case the deaths are only a forseen side effect and in the first case they're your means. The whole thing seems to hang on what a single acting person intends. Lately I've been reading about how modern drone strikes actually work: one analyst nominates a target, someone else approves it, a pilot or software fires on a house knowing noncombatants might be inside, and nobody in the chain has the civilian deaths as an end or even as a means — each one just does their job, and the deaths are foreseen at every step. Does the doctrine still draw a line there, or does it stop doing moral work once intent is smeared across a system? If it survives, where's the line and who carries the intention? If it doesn't, is there a repaired version that keeps whatever was right about it, or was the intention/foresight distinction always going to break when the agent stops being one person? I'm interested in the argument, not in drone policy.
