I'm a retired chemist and only started reading philosophy a couple years ago, so bear with me. I keep seeing P.F. Strawson's paper "Freedom and Resentment" summarized in a way that doesn't quite sit right, and I'd like someone to check it for me. A philosophy professor's blog post puts it like this: "Strawson's real insight is that the reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation and the rest—are not things we choose; we simply cannot help feeling them when someone steps on our toe or does us a kindness. And since ought implies can, determinism cannot be asking us to give them up. Thus the traditional worry about whether we are genuinely morally responsible just dissolves: we are responsible because we inescapably hold one another responsible. The only time the reactive attitudes get switched off is when we adopt the objective stance toward someone, which is what we do with small children and the severely mentally ill."

I'm prepared to believe it's mostly fair, but something feels overconfident about it. Could you go through it more or less sentence by sentence and say which parts a careful Strawson would accept, which are simplifications or conflations, and whether the "the question dissolves" move actually follows from the parts that are right?
