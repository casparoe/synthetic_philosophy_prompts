For a school project I am comparing Kierkegaard's critique of the daily press with algorithmically curated social media. My teacher gave us six elements of his critique, and I have paraphrased them below from my class notes. I would like you to rank these six elements by how well they transfer to social media as it actually works today, sorting them into three tiers: transfers almost unchanged, transfers only partially, and transfers poorly or distorts the point. For each element, explain in a sentence or two why it lands where it lands. Also identify the element, or the pair of elements, most likely to make me overstate the analogy if I apply it too directly.

The six elements are:

1. The public as an abstraction: what gets called the public is not a concrete group that could be gathered in a room; it is a kind of abstract mass made up of everyone and no one in particular, and individuals dissolve into it.
2. The press as the machine that produces the public: the daily press creates and sustains the public by addressing everyone at once and giving anonymous voices a permanent platform.
3. Anonymity and the loss of personal responsibility: when people speak and judge without a name attached, they no longer have to stand behind their words in person.
4. Leveling: the process by which all qualitative distinctions get worn down into a featureless equality, not by raising people up but by cutting down whatever stands out.
5. Envy as the hidden engine: leveling is powered by envy, the refusal to let anyone excel and the urge to bring down what rises above the rest.
6. Reflection without passion: a culture that keeps observing, commenting, and deliberating but never arrives at committed action, so that matters get talked to death.

Please structure your response with the following sections, in this order and with these approximate lengths:
- Ranking (about 150 words): the three tiers with each element placed in its tier.
- Rationale (about 60 words per element): why that element lands in its tier and what is distinctive about how it operates online.
- Where the analogy misleads (about 100 words): which element, or which combination of elements, most risks having me attribute to Kierkegaard a point he would not obviously make if I apply it too directly, and why.
- Overall verdict (about 80 words): whether the six elements still add up to one coherent critique once moved to social media, or whether the transfer pulls them apart into separate complaints; give the main reason in one sentence.
