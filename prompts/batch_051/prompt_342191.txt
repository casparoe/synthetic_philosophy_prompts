i've been kicking around a theory I call 'provenance, not property.' The idea is that what makes copying someone's work wrong isn't that you took something they had (they still have the idea afterward, and ideas aren't scarce) but that you scrambled the public record of where the idea came from. So the thing we should protect is the atribution chain, not the idea itself. On this view: (a) plagiarism is wrong even when the copied author is dead, would never have made a dime, and would honestly be flattered; (b) building on someone's idea with honest credit is never a property violation, no matter how little you add; (c) training a model on everyone's work is fine just in case the model preserves, for each output, whose work went into it. I know there's already something like moral rights or attribution rights, so maybe I've just reinvented that with more confidence. Tell me whether this is actually a distinct view or just a rebrand, what the strongest objection is, and where it breaks—I'm especially worried about trade secrets, where the entire point is that the idea must not leave the building.
