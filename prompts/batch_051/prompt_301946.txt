Take the standard temporary intrinsics problem: a person is bent at one time, straight at another, and the endurantist has to explain how one wholly present thing can have incompatible intrinsic properties, while the perdurantist says the bent thing and the straight thing are just different temporal parts. Now change the case in one way. Make the changing property a continuous magnitude rather than a binary feature — say a lump of metal cooling smoothly from 80°C to 20°C over exactly one hour — and suppose time is dense, so between any two instants in that hour there is another instant, and there is no next instant after any given one. Work through what each side's standard fix says in this version. Does the endurantist's move of relativizing properties to times still work when, for every instant at which the lump is exactly 61.2°C, there is no adjacent instant where it is the next slightly cooler value? Does the perdurantist get forces into positing a continuum of instantaneous temporal parts, one per instant, or can they make do with only extended temporal parts and account for the instant-by-instant temperatures some other way? And does the continuous case merely make the original puzzle harder to state, or does it actually break one of the two views in a way the bent/straight case didn't?
