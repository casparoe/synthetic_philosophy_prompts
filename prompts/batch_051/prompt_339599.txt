Im on the grants committee of a small land trust, and we have to decide on an application to restore about 800 acres of worn-out soybean fields: buy the land, stop the spraying and mowing, let it go to early-successional scrub and eventually mixed hardwood forest. The application pitches it as a pure win for native biodiversity. But one committee member has gone down a wild animal welfare rabbit hole (the argument that most wild animals are small, short-lived, and die in painful ways: predation, starvation, exposure, disease). His position is that the current soybean fields barely support any animal life at all, while restored habitat would support millions of extra voles, mice, rabbits, and songbirds. If a large share of those lives is net-negative in welfare terms, then by restoring habitat we might be creating more suffering than we prevent. He calls this a reverse logic of the larder: the old larder argument says existence is good, so bringing more animals into being benefits them; he says existence might be bad, so we shouldnt create the conditions for a lot more of it.

We need to send a decision memo to the full board: fund the restoration, reject it, or fund it with conditions. Write the memo with a clear, actionable recommendation and the actual reasoning. It has to state the strongest version of the welfare side rather than a caricature, and then explain why it does or doesnt change our decision. None of us are academic philosophers, so if you distinguish things like the value of a population continuing from the value of individual lives, spell those out. Keep it to about 800–1000 words since the board wont read more than that.
