I've been working on a short essay about what exactly gets eroded when more of our cognitive work is delegated to AI systems, and in conversation with a colleague who teaches medical residents I started using a distinction I now want checked. I call it "positional" versus "differential" understanding. The image is borrowed from knowing the value of a function at a single point versus knowing enough about the function to say how it behaves under small perturbations of the input. A person with merely positional understanding can produce the correct response when a question is put to them in exactly the shape they have learned to answer, but their competence falls apart as soon as the question is altered — when a background assumption is weakened, a parameter changed, or the question lifted to a slightly higher level of generality. A person with differential understanding can not only answer the question but also say what would make the answer change, how sensitive the answer is to each of its premises, and which features of the case are actually carrying the weight. I want to claim that most of what we call "grasp" is differential, and that it is this differential structure — not knowledge in the bare sense — that is quietly lost when answers arrive too cheaply.

An example from the conversation that started this: someone who has read a clinical decision rule can say what to do for a patient whose profile matches the rule exactly, but cannot say how the recommendation shifts for an older patient, or a patient with one extra condition, or under a medical system with different resources. They know the answer, but only at a point. My worry is that AI-mediated epistemic dependence systematically selects for positional understanding, because it makes answers cheap while making the surrounding counterfactual exploration feel optional, so the differential understanding that used to come as a byproduct of arriving at an answer ourselves is no longer being accumulated.

I want an honest assessment. Is this a real distinction or is it a metaphor with an unearned mathematical gloss? Where does it break: are there clear cases of genuine understanding without differential grip, or of full differential grip without genuine understanding? Does it collapse into an existing distinction in epistemology or the philosophy of science, and if so, where exactly? And is the claim about AI a conceptual point or just an empirical guess — and what would have to be true for it to be false?
