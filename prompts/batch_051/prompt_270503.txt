I'm a retired population biologist, spent thirty years on life-history models and phenotypic plasticity, and I only started reading political philosophy properly after I retired. Next month I'm giving a paper at a small workshop with an audience I need to be honest about: maybe half are historians of political thought who mostly work on Renaissance civic humanism, there are two behavioral ecologists who wander into philosophy of biology conferences, and one sociologist who works on social theory rather than anything Machiavellian. I'm a little worried the paper falls between two stools.

The paper argues something like this. The usual reading of Machiavelli on fortune and virtù treats virtù as a fairly stable quality — prudence or boldness — and fortune as the external churn that rewards or punishes it. I read Chapter 25 of The Prince instead as an early version of a trait–environment interaction claim. The passage I keep staring at is this one:

"One can also see of two cautious men the one attain his end, the other fail; and similarly, two men by different observances are equally successful, the one being cautious, the other impetuous; all this arises from nothing else than whether or not they conform in their methods to the spirit of the times."

I want to say the moral of that passage is not that boldness wins, or that prudence wins, but that the causal unit is the disposition-plus-circumstances pair. And that the higher virtue would be second-order flexibility — being able to change your first-order way of acting when the times change — though Machiavelli himself keeps saying men are too fixed by nature to do it.

What I need from you is a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of the thesis. I know the thesis; I need to know where it breaks in front of this particular room. What objections are these people actually going to raise, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each person would phrase them? For each one, give me a two-minute reply and tell me where that reply is soft. Which should I concede up front, and which should I hold? And what's the question I should be hopeing nobody asks? The ecologists will probably want a fitness function, the political theorists will probably tell me I've smuggled in a twentieth-century biological model and called it an interpretation, and the sociologist will probably ask whether "the times" is just a name for social structure. Is there a version of the paper that survives all three?
