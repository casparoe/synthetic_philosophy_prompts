I got really curious about this thing Aristotle apparently said, that money is just a tool for swapping goods so making money from money itself is kind of unnatural—like charging interest was the worst because money can't reproduce. So here's what I keep going back and forth on: imagine a small town that tries to actually run on that idea. The charter says (1) you can sell things you made or charge for your own labor at whatever price you can get, (2) nobody can charge interest or earn a return merely for letting someone else use their money, (3) buying cheap and reselling at a markup for no other reason is nominally banned but the enforcement is vague, and (4) any money that just sits unused for a year gets hit with a small penalty each year to nudge people to put it into actual production. Don't tell me if it's a good idea or wether Aristotle was right—just walk me through how the town would actually behave. What do people do with savings, how does a family finance a house, what shows up in the grey areas of "the ban is vague", and where does the whole thing get gamed or break first? And if they patch the first few cracks, does it settle into something stable or just sprout new loopholes?
