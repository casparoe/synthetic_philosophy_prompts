In philosophical and cognitive-science discussions of metaphor, one analogy in common use is the compressed comparison, or the picture: a metaphor allegedly packs into a sentence a content that could be unpacked into a literal paraphrase, much as a single image conveys something that would take many words to state. Donald Davidson's essay What Metaphors Mean attacks this analogy by saying that a metaphor has no special meaning beyond its literal meaning; it merely causes listeners to notice likenesses, and any paraphrase is a separate description of that effect, not a recovery of what was communicated.
