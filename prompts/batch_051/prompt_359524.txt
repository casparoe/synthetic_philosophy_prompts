ive been chewing on the paradox of tragedy for a while and i think i've worked out my own answer, but i want it checked before i bring it to my film-and-philosophy meetup. i know the standard moves — catharsis, where the emotions get flushed out, and the control story, where fiction makes pain safe because we're in charge of the frame. neither feels right to me, because both make the enjoyment sound like either plumbing or a power trip. my view is that what we're enjoying in a good tragedy is a kind of self-pressure-test. the sadness is genuine — i'm not claiming it's fake — but it comes with an implicit runtime limit, like the artwork guarantees the emotion will terminate and that there will be no invoice afterward. the pleasure isn't the sadness itself, and it isn't just relief after the fact either; it's the felt confirmation during, the background noticing that you can go all the way down into the feeling and still be intact at the end. i've been calling it grief with a lease, or maybe aesthetic rehearsal: the way a fire drill is unpleasant while it's happening, but you're glad it happened, especially if you kept your head. this also explains, i think, why people who dread real funerals still line up for sad films — real grief has an unknown half-life and a pile of social obligations attached, while fictional grief has an end time you can trust, so the same exercise in feeling can be run without the risk of it colonizing the rest of your week.

what i want from you: (1) is this actually distinct from the control story or just a rebranding of it, and is there an existing name for what i'm describing? (2) give me the clearest counterexample you can — for instance, i find Grave of the Fireflies unwatchable in a way my theory should have something to say about, but i'm not sure it does; and (3) does the theory predict something weird, like that the most painful art should be the most beloved, and if so is that a feature or a sign i've built the wrong model? be candid about where it collapses, and if you've got a better name than grief with a lease i'll take it.
