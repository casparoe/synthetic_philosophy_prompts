I write about science and ideas for a general readership, and I am preparing a piece on what chaos theory does and does not tell us about determinism. A colleague has offered this gloss, which I have also seen in several popular accounts: Newtonian mechanics is deterministic, meaning that with sufficiently precise measurements and enough computing power its future evolution could in principle be predicted to any desired accuracy; chaos theory supposedly showed that this is the wrong ideal, because in nonlinear systems tiny errors in the initial state grow exponentially, so the system's behaviour becomes effectively random and determinism is exposed as a merely formal doctrine with no empirical content. My instinct is that this runs together a few different claims, but I am not confident enough to say which. Could you help me do a sentence-by-sentence audit: what would a philosopher of science or a mathematically informed physicist accept, what would they regard as an overstatement or a category mistake, and what should I avoid repeating in print? In particular, is it right to say that chaos makes deterministic systems effectively random, and does it follow that determinism has no empirical content?
