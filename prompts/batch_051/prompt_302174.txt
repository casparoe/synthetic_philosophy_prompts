I'm TA-ing a philosophy of photography course and we just read Kendall Walton's "Transparent Pictures" piece from the 1980s, and the students keep talking past each other so I want a crisp version of the back-and-forth. Walton's claim, roughly, is that when you look at a photograph you see through it to the actual scene, the way you see through a telescope or eyeglasses, not merely a depiction of the scene. His key move is that the photographic image is formed by a causal-mechanical chain from object to film/print, with the photographer's beliefs not playing the same shaping role as a painter's do; the intervening optical and chemical processes are just the medium you see through. He even bites the bullet that you can see a dead ancestor in an old photo, in something like the way you see a star through a telescope even if it blew up long ago.

Write a dialogue between someone defending this transparency view and someone who thinks a photograph is a representational surface that we see *in*, not through — maybe they push on cases like blurred or fake photographs, and the photographer's choices about framing, timing, exposure, etc. Both should make real concessions and build on each other's actual points. Give me exactly five exchanges (each speaker speaking once per exchange), each exchange a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion. Make it feel like they're actually arguing with each other rather than taking turns reading position statements.
