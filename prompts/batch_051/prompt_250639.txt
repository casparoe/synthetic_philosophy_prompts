In my evolution reading group the argument about the gene's-eye view has stalled in a way I find genuinely puzzling. The Dawkins side keeps saying that selection acting on groups or species is just loose talk: once you make things precise at the level of the replicator, all the interesting stuff — kin selection, reciprocal altruism, meiotic drive — falls out of gene-level bookkeeping. The critics keep saying the gene's-eye view is bookkeeping in the pejorative sense: it can redescribe any outcome as genes maximizing something, but it doesnt tell you what actually causes what. Organisms (or groups) do the living and dying, and a gene's 'selfishness' is a metaphor with no causal content. What gets me is that both sides accept the same facts and even the same formal results — nobody disputes the Price equation — yet neither moves. My hunch is this isnt an empirical disagreement so much as a verbal one about what counts as a 'unit of selection,' or a difference about what a good explanation should look like, or two projects (replicators vs. vehicles/interactors) sharing one heading.
