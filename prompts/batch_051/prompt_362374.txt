On the Stalinesque version, the brain withholds the whole experience until the second flash arrives, and then builds a single smooth episode that you only become conscious of after the fact — like a show trial where the verdict is fixed before you walk in. On the Orwellian version, you really do have an experience of the first dot right away, but then the second flash causes the memory of the first part to get rewritten, so its like you saw smooth motion the whole time — like retroactively doctoring the archives. A black-box tester who only sees the final committed state can't tell those two apart, but I'm not sure whether introspection is supposed to give us an extra read path that bypasses the 'log'. So: where exactly does the disagreement live, and is there a fact of the matter about which story is right, or is the Orwell/Stalin split itself doing the misleading work?
