I've been going down a decision-theory rabbit hole for a piece I'm writing, and I've realized my own verdicts on gambles with infinite or undefined expected value don't hang together. Here they are. In each case, a fair coin is flipped until it lands heads; call the number of flips N.

1. St. Petersburg: you win 2^N dollars. I wouldn't pay more than about $25 to play, despite the infinite expected value.

2. Pasadena: you win (−1)^(N−1) × 2^N / N dollars, so the sign alternates and the size grows quickly. If N=1 you get $2, if N=2 you pay $2, if N=3 you get about $2.67, if N=4 you pay $4. My gut says I'd pay a dollar or two, but I honestly can't say wether I prefer it to not playing.

3. Altadena: same payoffs but always positive, so 2^N / N dollars. I'd pay more for this than for Pasadena — every possible outcome is at least as good — but not dramatically more; maybe $10.

4. Pasadena plus a dollar: I'd obviously rather play Pasadena-plus-$1 than plain Pasadena, and I'd pay a bit more. But I can't say how much more, and I don't see how expected-value reasoning delivers the preference, since both have undefined expectations.

5. Many independent runs of Pasadena: I'd pay a fair amount to take the average of, say, 10,000 independent runs. The freakiness should average out, I feel. But I don't know whether that's actually defensible, given that there's no expectation to average toward.

I've been told the Pasadena expectation is undefined because the terms, taken in their natural order, sum to ln 2, but they can be rearranged to give any number or none; the Altadena expectation is straightforwardly infinite. Is there a coherent principle about which of these gambles is worth more, or whether they're comparable at all, that captures most of my verdicts? Which verdicts does it force me to give up? And what would it say about a case I keep chewing on: a Pasadena game where I pay a $5 entry fee before the coin is ever flipped — should that be exactly $5 worse than free Pasadena, and if so, how do you say that in a framework where both expectations are undefined?
