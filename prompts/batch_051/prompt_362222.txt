I have been working through the literature on laws of nature, and the debate between Humeans and anti-Humeans has come to feel like a long standoff whose structure I cannot quite see. The two sides seem to share the same data: there is a vast mosaic of particular matters of fact, and some true generalizations are simple, informative, and support predictions and counterfactuals. Both sides also agree that being a law has something to do with a generalization's occupying a privileged role in our best overall account of the mosaic. But the Humean says the laws are nothing over and above that mosaic: lawhood is a status a regularity earns by being an axiom or theorem of the best systematization of all the particular facts, where "best" is judged by some combination of simplicity and strength. The anti-Humean says this leaves out exactly the thing that makes laws lawlike: laws govern, constrain, or necessitate, and the mosaic is the way it is partly because the laws make it so, so lawhood cannot be read off from even an ideal summary of the facts.

Each side has a stock charge against the other. The Humean accuses the anti-Humean of positing unexplained modal glue, a necessitation relation that does no work beyond what best-system status already does. The anti-Humean accuses the Humean of getting the direction of explanation backwards: a regularity is not a law because it fits the best system; it fits the best system because it is a law. A lot of the argument then funnels down into the question of what the trade-off between simplicity and strength is allowed to be, and each side suspects that the other's answer already presupposes lawhood or else makes it a matter of our interests rather than of the world. Each side has absorbed the other's standard counterexamples well enough that nobody moves.

My working hypothesis is that this is not really a dispute about any matter of fact, but a dispute about what metaphysics may take as primitive and about what an explanation is required to do. Yet I keep worrying there is a hidden substantive question inside it, perhaps about truthmakers for modal claims or about whether our distinctions track genuine structure in the world. I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this a factual disagreement, a verbal disagreement about the word "law," a difference in standards of explanation or parsimony, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? And given your diagnosis, would anything count as progress, such as a formal result, a new distinction, or a change of question, or should we expect the impasse to persist indefinitely?
