I'm taking a philosophy seminar on gratitude and a machine learning course on reinforcement learning at the same time, and it's starting to feel like they're both trying to solve the same problem. On the philosophy side, there's a gap between being glad a good thing happened and being grateful to someone for bringing it about. Gratitude seems to care about whether the benefactor did it on purpose, what it cost them, and whether they were acting for my sake, not just the size of the benefit. If someone does me a favor by accident, or helps me only because they misread the situation, I might be glad but not really grateful.

In RL, credit assignment is the problem of deciding which earlier actions deserve credit for a reward that shows up later. Eligibility traces sort of discount credit the longer the gap between an action and the reward. Inverse RL tries to recover what reward function an agent was pursuing, i.e., what they were actually trying to do rather than what just happened to occur. And in multi-agent RL there's difference rewards and Shapley-value style credit assignment, where you credit an agent according to the marginal difference their action made compared to some baseline of what would've happened without them.

The parallel I keep turning over: both gratitude and credit assignment take a good outcome at the end of a causal chain and distribute backward-looking credit across the agents and actions that lead up to it, using delay, intention, and counterfactual contribution. Is this a real structural resemblance or just the word credit doing too much work? Here's where I get stuck. If I tried to formalize the norms of gratitude as a credit assignment rule, pure Shapley-value gratitude would say you owe the most gratitude to whoever made the biggest marginal difference to the outcome, even if they meant you harm and got lucky. The philosophical side seems to want to rule that out. Does that mean gratitude is doing something other than attribution, or just that the attribution rule is different? I can't tell wether the parallel is deep or whether it falls apart once you look at the normativity.
