I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I have been reading Nietzsche’s *The Dawn of Day* in the J. M. Kennedy translation. There is a passage in it — not one of the famous lines about pity, but a quieter psychological observation — whose claim, as I understand it, is that pity does not merely fail to relieve suffering; it multiplies it, because the pitying observer’s own suffering is added to the sufferer’s without subtracting anything from the sufferer’s. That passage has left me with an argument I cannot shake, and it seems to prove too much.

(1) The total suffering present when one person pities another is the sufferer’s pain plus whatever pain the pity itself adds.
(2) Pity is a form of pain in the observer.
(3) Pity is caused by the sufferer’s pain, but it does not by itself reduce the sufferer’s pain.
(4) Therefore, wherever one person pities another, there is more total suffering than there would be if the observer recognised the same state without pitying.
(5) If the aim is to minimise total suffering, pity is not a virtue but a generator of surplus suffering; the morally cleaner response is to suppress the pity, even if one still acts to remove its cause.
(6) So Schopenhauer’s ethic, which puts pity at the foundation of morality, is not merely mistaken about moral psychology but works against its own aim of reducing suffering.

I cannot accept the conclusion that ordinary compassion, empathy, and humanitarian concern are pathologies, and that the only consistent position is to suppress pity and act from a non-pitying motive such as duty, taste, or love of power. Yet each premise seems hard to deny. Where exactly does the argument come apart? Is the flaw in the additive picture of suffering in (1)? Is it in (3), because pity alters motivation in ways that produce competent help? Or is the mistake deeper — in treating total suffering as the currency of ethics at all? I would prefer to keep the conclusion false, but I need to know which premise to give up and what giving it up would cost.

Please write the answer in British English.
