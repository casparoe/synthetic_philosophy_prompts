I cover philosophy of science for a magazine and I'm trying to get my own physicalism straight before I write a column, so I'd like a consistency audit. Here's the set I'd currently defend:
(a) physicalism is true, and it means everything that exists is either one of the things current physics quantifies over or is composed of them;
(b) current physics is incomplete and partly wrong — dark matter is basically a placeholder and there's no quantum gravity;
(c) physicalism is a substantive, empirical claim about the world, not just a definitional truism;
(d) I don't accept the move of defining physical by whatever a completed future physics says exists, because that seems to make physicalism unfalsifiable;
(e) biology and economics have legitimate autonomous explanations, and I don't think that threatens physicalism;
(f) the pain in my thumb is a physical state, even though I can't map it onto any category of current physics.
Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest way to make the set cohere? Rank the tensions by severity rather than giving a flat list. At the very end, add one line starting 'Verdict:' naming the single most severe tension and the cheapest repair.
