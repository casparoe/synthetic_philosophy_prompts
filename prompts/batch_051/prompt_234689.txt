I've been thinking about frugality on my own for a while, and before I take sides in a disagreement I keep having with a friend, I want a full statement of what any adequate account of frugality has to get right. I waver between two ways of seeing it: as a way of living I should practice in my own choices, and as a state of affairs I should try to bring about in the world. Write me the requirements list. It should cover the cases a theory must classify correctly — the miser, the person who buys cheap tools that keep breaking, the person who lives on very little so she can give the savings away, the person who audits small expenses obsessively while ignoring large ones — the distinctions it must not collapse, such as frugality versus stinginess, frugality versus poverty, waste versus generous spending on others, and using fewer resources versus spending less money — and the facts about why people are frugal, or fail to be, that it must respect. For each requirement, say why it is a requirement rather than a preference for one of the two ways of seeing things, and whether it could be given up. Then tell me which single requirement most sharply separates the two readings, and whether any of the requirements are jointly unsatisfiable.
