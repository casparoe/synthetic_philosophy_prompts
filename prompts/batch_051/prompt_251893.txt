You are a philosopher of language who works on indexicals, pronouns, and the difference between what a sentence says and what a speaker can do with it. Please think through the following carefully. Begin by brainstorming the fault lines among my positions pair by pair, and then deliver a ranked audit: which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to be in tension, which conflict is most severe, and what would have to give in each case.

For the past few years I have been building a constructed language for a novel, and I set myself the constraint that the language has no personal pronouns: no words corresponding to I, you, he, she, it, or they. Doing this has pushed me into a set of views about what language is for and what pronouns actually do. I am not academically trained in this area, and I would like to know whether the set hangs together.

Here is where I have landed.

1. A pronounless language is genuinely usable. A person could live an ordinary practical, social, and inner life in it; it is not a toy or a notation.
2. Personal pronouns are eliminable because they are context-tracking devices. Once a person or thing has been introduced by name or description, the surrounding context, verb endings, and word order can carry reference for he, she, and they; and for I and you, the speech situation itself already settles who is speaking to whom, so no separate words are needed.
3. But I do not think the same is true of words like now, here, today, and nearby. Those cannot be replaced by dates, coordinates, and place names without loss. Telling you that the meeting starts now does something for you that telling you that the meeting starts at 5:07 on July 3 does not do, even when the latter states the exact moment at which I speak.
4. Self-location is not optional for a thinking creature. A creature that could think the food is three feet to the left but could not think the food is three feet to my left would be missing something it needs in order to act at all.
5. I am suspicious of hidden layers of meaning. If two ways of putting a claim are true in exactly the same situations and false in exactly the same situations, then they say the same thing, and any further supposed difference is picture-thinking.
6. A language is complete for its speakers when anything they can think can be said in it.

Please go through these in pairs. I want to know where the real conflicts are, which conflicts are only apparent and why, how the real ones rank by severity, what each would force me to give up, and what the cheapest single revision would be and what it would cost.
