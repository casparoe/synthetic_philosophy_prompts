I have been trying to formalize the surprise exam paradox as a small model, and the model proves something that is contradicted by what actually happens. I can tell there is a bug, but I cannot find which line of the argument it lives on.

(1) Let A be the teacher's announcement: there will be exactly one exam next week, on one of Monday through Friday, and the students will not be in a position to know which day it is before the day arrives.

(2) On Sunday night, the students know A. The teacher has a perfect record, and the announcement is made publicly.

(3) I model the students' knowledge with two axioms: if you know P, then P is true; and if you know P and P entails Q, then you can come to know Q by deduction.

(4) Suppose the exam is Friday. On Thursday night, if no exam has occurred, the students know A from (2), know no exam has occurred, and deduce that the exam must be Friday. They would then know the day before the exam, contradicting A. So the exam cannot be Friday, and the students can establish this on Sunday.

(5) Suppose the exam is Thursday. On Wednesday night, if no exam has occurred, the students know A, know no exam has occurred, and know from (4) that Friday is impossible, so they deduce that the exam must be Thursday. Contradiction again. So Thursday is impossible.

(6) The same reasoning eliminates Wednesday, Tuesday, and Monday. By Sunday night the students have proved there will be no exam.

(7) But A said there would be an exam, so A is false.

(8) The teacher then gives the exam on Wednesday. Because the students had proved there would be no exam, they did not know on Tuesday night that it would be Wednesday. So there was an exam, and it was a surprise. A was true after all.

(9) Contradiction: the proof says A is false, but the observed facts say A is true.

I have checked the induction and the knowledge axioms, and I do not see the flaw. I would most like to keep the knowledge axioms and the claim that A was true. I do not see how a true announcement from a teacher with a perfect record could fail to be knowledge, but if that is where the fault lies, I want to know exactly what is lost.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Please keep the diagnosis under 500 words.
