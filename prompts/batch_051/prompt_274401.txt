You are an expert on Freud's philosophy of religion. I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a feature on Freud's critique of religious belief. In the secondary literature I keep seeing the claim compressed like this: Freud called religious ideas illusions, and therefore thought they were false. That reading makes his elaborate psychological explanation of religious belief look redundant—if the beliefs have simply been shown to be false, why spend so much effort explaining the wishes and needs that produce them? Please think through this carefully and tell me whether the compressed reading is accurate. Work through the concepts one by one before reaching a verdict. In particular, clarify what Freud meant by an illusion, how it differs from an error and a delusion, and whether his critique of religion stands or falls on showing that religious beliefs are false.
