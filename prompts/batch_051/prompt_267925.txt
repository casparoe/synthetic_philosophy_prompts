I've been down a rabbit hole about calendars and something bugs me. We number years from the (probably wrong) birth of Jesus, even though most people using the calendar aren't Christian, and there's no year zero: 1 BC goes straight to AD 1. Unix machines count seconds from 1970, and the French revolutionaries briefly tried to restart the calendar at year one of the Republic, but it didn't stick. So everyone seems to treat the starting point as arbitrary, yet we keep the Jesus-based one and just relabel it CE/BCE. What philosophical commitments does that practice presuppose? Does dating years from an event secretly commit you to the idea that history has a center, or that some events are world-historical and others aren't? Is a calendar supposed to be neutral infrastructure, or is it more like a flag or anthem that's allowed to be partisan because it's really about shared identity? And what about the missing year zero: does it suggest we still treat time as a list of events rather than a line where the origin could actually sit at zero? Or is all of this just administrative accidents that got frozen into place?
