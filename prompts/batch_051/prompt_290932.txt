When I bought my car in 2019 the purchase agreement had this arbitration clause about two-thirds of the way down page six:

"Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement, the vehicle, or any service or feature provided in connection with the vehicle shall be resolved exclusively by binding individual arbitration and not in court. The parties waive any right to a jury trial and any right to bring or participate in a class action or other representative proceeding. The arbitrator's award shall be final and binding, and judgment on it may be entered in any court having jurisdiction."

I clicked through it the same way everyone does. Last year the manufacturer pushed an over-the-air update to the battery software of my car that capped the charging speed and the usable capacity on my model, supposedly to protect the battery. I never asked for it, and the car didn't even have over-the-air updates when I bought it — it was sold to me as a finished car, not a gadget they could keep reaching into. Now I want to sue them for quietly degrading something I own, and ideally join one of the class actions, but they say the clause blocks me.

Obviously the people who drafted that clause in 2019 weren't picturing the manufacturer sending me software that shrinks my range years later. So does it actually cover what they did? I want you to work through what the plain words say, what the purpose of the clause was, what the drafters probably would of said if someone had asked them about this exact situation, and what a reasonable person in my position would think they agreed to when they signed. Then tell me which approach should win here and why.
