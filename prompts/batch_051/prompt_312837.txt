I run a monthly discussion group with a split I suspect you will recognize: a little over half the regulars are working programmers who are comfortable with dot products, norms, and matrix multiplication, while the rest are linguists, philosophers, and a historian, most of whom are not quantitative and none of whom know much machine learning. Next month I am leading a session on what it means to say that word embeddings have a geometry: the fact that semantic relationships show up as vector relationships (the king − man + woman ≈ queen offset pattern), that similarity is standardly measured by the angle between vectors via cosine similarity rather than by raw Euclidean distance between points, and that this is at once a real structural discovery about distributed representations and an easy thing to overinterpret. I have read enough posts and comment threads about vector semantics and interpretability to know the standard moves, so my problem is not information but sequencing: I want the session to be one continuous argument rather than a slide-deck of results. Could you plan the explanation rather than writing it out? Specifically, I need: the order in which the pieces must be introduced and what each depends on; the one concrete carrying example — a small hand-built embedding, a set of toy vectors, a diagram, or a defensible use of the overused king–queen example — and what that example must have and must not have if it is to do the work; the misconceptions the audience will predictably form, among them reading individual dimensions as meaningful features, treating distance from the origin as importance, treating cosine similarity as the one true geometry of meaning, conflating a word's position with its meaning, and taking analogy offsets as evidence of full compositional semantics, and where in the sequence each should be defused before it hardens; a check for understanding that would show the idea has actually landed rather than merely that the diagrams are legible; and a short list of what I should explicitly refuse to explain at this level, so that the audience does not mistake my silence for endorsement or forgetfulness.
