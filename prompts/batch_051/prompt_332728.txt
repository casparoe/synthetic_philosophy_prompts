Okay so me and a friend have spent way too long arguing about whether clicking "I agree" on a terms-of-service popup is actually agreement in any serious sense. I'm on the side that most of it is basically consent theater: nobody reads, there's no bargaining, the "choice" is fake because every competitor has the same clause, and having a court enforce page-11 arbitration waivers against someone who hit a blue button feels more like private legislation than contract. She thinks I'm romanticizing negotiated deal-making. People bind themselves through all sorts of unread formalities—signatures, ceremonies, standard terms—and if law required actual comprehension of every term, mass commerce just wouldn't work. Her line is that the click is a public act that says "I accept these terms," and that's enough for obligation, with unfairness handled downstream (unconscionability, etc.), not by pretending no agreement happened.

We've decided to write up our disagreement as an adversarial-collaboration joint statement, with the two camps labeled roughly: (A) clickwrap-as-genuine-assent, and (B) clickwrap-as-deemed-consent. Could you draft that document for us?

I'd like it in three parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section. Push them to agree on some non-vacuous things: browsewrap is genuinly weaker than clickwrap, rational non-reading is real (people do a cost-benefit calculus on reading 30 pages of legalese), scroll-to-the-bottom probably isn't meaningful notice, and some low-salience terms shouldn't be enforceable no matter what the user clicked. Second, a disagreements table. Columns: the issue, the exact wording each side would accept for its own view (without slanted spin), whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal, and what the other side says is wrong with that wording. Third, a cruxes section. For each crux—a sub-question such that settling it would genuinely move one side—give a concrete test, reform, or vignette both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.

One crux I want included: would a mandatory plain-language "key terms summary" box before the click change the moral status of clicking through? Also make them sort out where they might just be talking past each other over words like "agreement," "assent," "consent," and "adhesion"—and don't let either side be a strawman.
