I am a machine learning engineer who works on classification and ranking systems, and I am fluent in the ideas I use daily: distribution shift, feedback loops, performative prediction, Goodhart's law, reward hacking, concept drift, and the difference between a target metric and the quantity one is actually trying to estimate. I have no background in the philosophy of social science, and I want you to translate the philosopher Ian Hacking's account of the looping effects of human kinds into that engineering vocabulary, then tell me what does not survive the translation.

Here is the position to translate, as I understand it. Some classifications of people — for instance, delinquent, refugee, gifted child, or a psychiatric diagnosis — are not neutral labels attached to kinds that already exist. When such a classification is administered by institutions such as schools, clinics, or courts, the people classified become aware of the classification and of the descriptions, explanations, and expectations that travel with it. They may change how they act, how they describe themselves, and how they understand their own histories in response. As the behaviour of the classified people changes, the kind itself changes: the classification no longer picks out quite the same sort of person, and experts may then revise the classification, which sets off another loop. The classified people, on Hacking's picture, are not merely labelled; they come to inhabit and modify the very kind under which they are classified.

Restate this position as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use as an ML engineer. I am thinking of notions such as: deploying a model as an intervention that changes the data-generating process, performative prediction, label or covariate shift induced by a system's own outputs, people optimising against a target, and the distinction between a distribution one merely observes and a distribution one's system helps to produce. Use whatever else in the vocabulary is genuinely apt rather than merely decorative.

Then give me the residue report. Which claims in Hacking's account map cleanly onto these engineering ideas, and which map only if the vocabulary is stretched, and where exactly does the stretching introduce distortion? Which claims cannot be expressed in the ML vocabulary at all? Finally, give me your honest diagnosis: is what fails to translate a limitation of the ML vocabulary — something it would need to add if it were to handle such cases properly — or is it a sign that the original philosophical claims are less precise than they look and only seem substantive until one tries to formalise them? I am more interested in a precise inventory of what carries and what does not than in an elegant analogy. Please write your answer in British English.
