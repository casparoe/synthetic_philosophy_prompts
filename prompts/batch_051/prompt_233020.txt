I've been reading Gilbert Ryle's The Concept of Mind, and I'm stuck on something. In the first chapter he lays into what he calls the official doctrine — the idea that there's a mind inside the body, a ghost in the machine — and says it all comes from a category mistake. Fair enough. But then in the rest of the book he still talks about things like sensations, imaginings, and silent soliloquoy as if they're real private episodes. Isn't that exactly the ghost he just kicked out the front door sneaking back in? If the category mistake is that mental terms aren't the names of inner causes but dispositions or abilities, then why does he keep talking as though there are inner mental goings-on after all?
