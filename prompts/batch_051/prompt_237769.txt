I'm on my co-op's family review board, the group that has to approve new members' plans for raising their kids, and I'm torn about how to vote on one family. I've been chewing on this idea I read once, that a kid is owed a future that stays open rather than one that gets nailed down for her, and it's making me reread our old cases.

Our past calls:
- The Lindholms, an off-grid farming family who keep their kids out of school but teach reading, math, and a trade, and the kids can leave at 16: approved.
- The Sewells, who wanted to raise their daughter in a closed compound and promise her in marriage at 12: rejected, even though the application had no abuse in it, because we said it would lock her whole life in before she could choose it.
- The Vargheses, a strict religious family who keep their kids away from tv and internet: approved with the condition that there's a yearly check-in with someone outside and the kids can leave the community at 16.
- The Mertzes, who wanted to raise their twins in a language they invented and keep them from other children until adulthood: rejected, because the kids would hit 18 with no outside world and no shared first language.
- The Okafor-Smiths, who are raising their kid with no gender label, neutral clothes and name, so the kid can sort that out later: approved, on the ground that it leaves options open.

Now the Dembeles want to raise their daughter in their apartment with no formal school, no outside activities, and no contact with other kids. They'd teach her to read in English and teach their religion, but no math, no science, no secular books. They say she's free to leave at 16 and can sign up for school then if she wants; they just want to control what gets into her head before then. How should I vote if we're trying to be consistent with our own past decisions? Which old case is closest, and if our precidents don't actually force one answer, what rule would we be committed to under each vote? And what would a later case have to look like to show which rule we've really been using all along?
