In a discussion about AI governance, a colleague kept deflating proposals for safety standards with one phrase: any lab that unilaterally adopts costly safeguards just sets off a race to the bottom, and the responsible players get outcompeted. The phrase was doing real argumentative work — it was supposed to establish that voluntary restraint is futile and perhaps worse than futile — but I cannot tell whether it is one claim or several. It might mean: (i) an equilibrium claim, that competition selects for the lowest common denominator; (ii) a dynamic claim, that each actor is forced to match or undercut the worst behavior of the others; (iii) a collective-action claim, that individually rational choices produce an outcome nobody wanted; (iv) a selection claim, that the least scrupulous actors survive and grow even if most actors remain decent; or (v) an exit-and-mobility claim, that capital, talent, or users will flee to whoever operates under the weakest constraints. These readings can come apart, and I suspect they have different truth conditions.
