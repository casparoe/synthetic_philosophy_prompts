I don't have any academic background in philosophy, but I have spent a couple of years reading about the hard problem of consciousness on my own, and I keep finding myself drawn to panpsychism and then thrown back by the same objection. I want help deciding which side should give way, and what would actually justify letting it go.

The theory, as I understand it, is this. Physics tells us what matter does — its structure, dispositions, and relations — but it never tells us what matter is in itself. That "in itself" must be something. The only intrinsic nature I am directly acquainted with is experience. So the economical view is that experience, in some extremely rudimentary form, is already present at the bottom level of nature, and the rich experience of a brain is built up from many such micro-experiences. This is appealing because it avoids the brute fact of consciousness switching on at some arbitrary level of organized matter; it makes consciousness causally relevant rather than a powerless shadow; and it needs no second kind of stuff alongside the physical.

Against this stands the firmest intuition I have: that my experience is not a swarm. I am one subject, one point of view, right now, in a way that is given more directly than any premise of any theory. If every particle has its own tiny point of view, then a structured collection of particles is still a collection of many points of view. Subjects do not sum; you cannot put a billion little subjects together and get one big subject the way you can put a billion forces together and get one resultant. Yet panpsychism seems to require exactly that — my single field of consciousness is supposed to be composed of trillions of micro-subjects. That sounds like the same "emergence miracle" the view was introduced to escape, just pushed down a level, because no account of combination is given.

I do not want an easy answer. I want to know which of these should yield, and on what grounds. If my intuition of unity should be debunked, that requires an error theory: how could an illusion with no subject be mistaken for the most certain thing I have, and what structure could host that illusion? If the theory should be amended — say, by making the base properties "proto-experiential" rather than fully conscious — does that actually explain how they combine into one subject, or does it just rename the mystery? If the theory should be abandoned, which alternative keeps its virtues without inheriting the combination problem? Or is there a reading on which the two are compatible — for example, one in which the parts do not have subjects in the same sense that I do, or in which my unity is not built out of their unity in the way the objection assumes?

Please also tell me how confident you are in the specific claims you make, and flag the one point you are least sure of.
