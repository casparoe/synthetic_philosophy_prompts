I'm part of a working group advising a middle-income country that wants to import the Nordic combination of high taxes and high trust, and we keep stalling on the same circularity: high taxes are only bearable where trust is already high, and trust seems to be built partly by institutions that presuppose it. One member has proposed a mechanism meant to break the circle, and I want you to analyze how it would actually behave rather than whether it is noble in spirit.

The proposal is a "named solidarity tax." Every wage-earner above a modest exemption must pay a 45% marginal contribution, but the money does not go into a general budget. Each taxpayer is required, through a state portal, to allocate their contribution among named beneficiaries drawn from an official eligibility register: the long-term unemployed, disabled adults, elderly people without pensions, and tertiary students from households below median income. Beneficiaries receive the transfers monthly with the contributor's name attached; a public ledger records each transfer, and beneficiaries must post a short quarterly account of what the money went toward. There is a ceiling on how much any one beneficiary can receive, and contributors may not allocate to relatives, employees, or anyone in their own municipality. The hope is that making redistribution visible and personal will recreate the reciprocity and social control that Nordic trust is supposedly built on, while the restrictions prevent it from decaying into pure patronage.

Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. Walk through what it incentivizes among taxpayers, recipients, local officials, and any intermediaries who might arise; who ends up funded and who does not; how the public ledger reshapes social relations in both intended and unintended ways; and where the scheme gets gamed first. Then say whether its main failure modes can be fixed by design tweaks without turning it back into the anonymous pooled taxation it was meant to replace, and whether it would plausibly build or corrode the generalized trust the country is trying to engineer. Begin with your bottom line in a single first sentence, then give the analysis, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
