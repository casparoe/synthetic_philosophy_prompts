(2) Every historical narrative is told from some vantage point and organized around some standards of relevance and significance; there is no uncentered, neutral standpoint from which the past could be narrated.
(3) So being organized around a European vantage point is just a special case of the universal condition described in (2), not a deviation from it.
(4) If being organized around a European vantage point is just a special case of a universal condition, then it cannot by itself be a defect; a defect has to distinguish the bad cases from the acceptable ones.
(5) So Eurocentrism either is not a defect at all, or every history—Sinocentric, Ming-centric, whatever—is defective in exactly the same way.
