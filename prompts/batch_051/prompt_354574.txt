I am a PhD student in philosophy of law working on offences against the sacred. Consider the following provision from a penal code (the wording is my own):

A person commits an offence if, with the intention of outraging the religious feelings of any class of citizens, he publicly utters words, displays a visible representation, or does any other act likely to outrage those feelings.

An artist, who says he bears no animus toward the community in question, trains a generative model on a minority religious community's hymns, liturgical forms, and iconography, and releases it as a public service. The model is designed to produce stylistically faithful new items in response to user prompts. It sometimes emits reverent continuations and sometimes blends sacred form with plainly profane content in ways the community would clearly find outrageous. The artist knows that this second class of outputs is a stable feature of the model, but he authors, previews, and selects no particular output. Users write the prompts, and those who receive an offensive output often circulate screenshots. The provision's drafters had in mind a speaker, cartoonist, or pamphleteer deciding on an identifiable insult; they never imagined a mechanism whose ordinary operation generates a distribution of representations, some share of which offends.

Does the provision, read in the best legal way, reach the artist? Work through each interpretive approach: the text; the purpose (is the law protecting citizens from felt outrage, protecting the sacred itself, or preserving public order?); what the drafters would have said if asked; and what a reasonable citizen would understand the provision to prohibit. In particular, say whether intention can attach to the apparatus as a whole rather than to particular outputs; whether the user who prompts and circulates an output is the more natural defendant, and whether that makes the artist any less liable; and whether the case exposes a routine drafting gap or a deeper strain in the very idea of deliberately outraging religious feelings. Which approach should win, and why?

End with a single sentence beginning "In short:" that states your preferred result.
