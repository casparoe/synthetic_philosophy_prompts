I am trying to get clear about a phenomenon I call a free-floating rationale: a market, institution, technique, rule, or other arrangement that exhibits means-end fit and is naturally explained by the end it serves, even though the explanation is not held by anyone — no participant ever formed the rationale, none can now state why the whole thing works, and at no point was the design consciously chosen. I keep encountering a short list of candidate mechanisms that are supposed to generate reasons without reasoners. The ones I already know are these. (1) Natural selection, which accumulates adaptive design through differential reproduction with no designer. (2) Competitive market selection, in which firms, products, and practices are filtered by their fit to demand whether or not the people running them understand that fit. (3) The price system, which aggregates and coordinates dispersed information that no single trader possesses. (4) Traditions and craft recipes, which retain whatever worked after the causal account behind it has been forgotten. (5) Institutional memory in rules and checklists, such as a safety procedure that encodes a history of failures no current worker knows in detail. (6) Language, in which distinctions that paid off in use become fixed in vocabulary and grammar without speakers needing to know their origin. I believe this list covers the standard mechanisms, so I am not asking for one of them under a new name. I want a seventh mechanism that is not on the list, not a rewording, special case, or combination of any of the six, and not a new domain in which one of the six merely operates. Describe the mechanism precisely enough that I could recognize an instance of it, then say honestly whether it is any good as an addition: whether it earns its keep by explaining something the six cannot, or just redescribes territory they already cover. After the discussion, end with a final machine-readable line consisting of \answer{...}, where the braces are replaced by a two-to-five-word label for your proposed mechanism.
