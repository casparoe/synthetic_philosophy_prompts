I spent my career as a research scientist, and in retirement I have been reading philosophy. One idea I have absorbed is that a habit of examining one's own beliefs and motives is part of a well-lived life, so I began a regular practice of scrutinizing my convictions and important past decisions, treating my own mind as I used to treat a natural system under study. At first the practice seemed to pay off: I became better at spotting my own rationalizations and at tracing where my views actually came from. Lately, however, it has split in two. Some sessions end in a stable conclusion that improves how I act; others loop over the same episode, each pass producing a new and plausible account of why I did what I did but leaving me less settled than before. A friend says I have stopped examining myself and started merely churning up my mental sediment, and that an accurate description of oneself is not always good for the person described. That claim bothers me, because as a scientist I treated accurate description of a system as an unqualified good, and I also know from my own field that the act of measuring can disturb some systems. Is my friend right? When does honest examination of oneself stop being understanding and start being harmful, and how should I think about that line?
