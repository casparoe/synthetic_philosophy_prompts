I need a Q&A prep sheet for a talk I'm giving later this month to a public-policy school's governance workshop. The thesis I'm defending is that when government bodies or public officials use non-disclosure agreements to resolve claims against the government or to keep departing employees quiet about how public business was actually conducted, those agreements deserve no real deference: courts should treat them as presumptively unenforceable against current and former public employees, and agencies should be legally discouraged from demanding them, with only a narrow exception for operational secrets that can survive a public-interest balancing test. My argument runs roughly as follows: the normal contractual rationale fails because the relevant interest in information about government conduct belongs to the public rather than the agency; the chilling effect cannot be repaired by adding whistleblower carve-outs, because employees reasonably distrust them and because the burden of proving the carve-out applies falls on the employee; and the defenses of such NDAs slide between secrecy needed for operational effectiveness and secrecy needed for reputational protection, when only the first can be offered as a public reason.

The audience is not a civil-liberties crowd. It will be mostly administrative-law scholars, public-management faculty, and a few former city or county attorneys who have drafted or signed settlement agreements with confidentiality clauses, and they care about workable institutions, finality of settlements, and not converting every personnel dispute into litigation. I want the six objections I'm most likely to face, ordered by how much damage each can actually do to my position, and phrased the way a former government attorney or an administrative-law scholar would phrase them. Give me a definite, argued ranking, not a survey of possible questions. Make the sixth item the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Format your answer as a numbered list of exactly six items with no introduction or conclusion; each item should be two sentences, the first stating the objection in the audience's voice and the second giving the two-minute reply I should give, with the point in that reply where I'm softest marked in some explicit way.
