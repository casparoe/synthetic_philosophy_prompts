My friend in philosophy club backed me into a corner with this one and I cant find the exit. He's really into Dennett's view that a self isn't a thing inside you, it's a center of narrative gravity — an abstraction we use to make someone's words and actions add up, the way a center of gravity isn't a real chunk of matter but still does real explanatory work. Then he gives me this argument and says if I accept the Dennett stuff I have to accept the whole thing:

(1) If a self is a center of narrative gravity, then whatever has a center of narrative gravity has a self.
(2) A center of narrative gravity exists wherever a sufficiently good story is organized around a character.
(3) Novels organize good stories around characters.
(4) So the protagonist of a good novel has a self.
(5) But fictional characters don't exist, and nothing that doesn't exist can have a self.
(6) So either Dennett's view says fictional characters have selves, which sounds like it cheats existence, or one of (1)-(5) is false.

I want to keep the Dennett stuff because it dissolves a lot of the old mind-body puzzles, but I also don't want to say Sherlock Holmes has a self in the same way I do. Which step should I reject, and what do I lose by rejecting it? Also please seperate what's actually settled about this from what people genuinely argue about, and don't present a contested take like it's the consensus.
