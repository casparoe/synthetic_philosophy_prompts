For a paper in my high school philosophy seminar, I am working through the utility monster thought experiment and I want to get past the stock replies rather than restate them. The replies I have already catalogued are: (1) the empirical reply — real organisms exhibit diminishing marginal utility, so a being who keeps converting resources into enormous utility is physically and psychologically implausible; (2) the threshold reply — ordinary people hold a prior claim to a decent minimum before additional goods may be shifted to the monster; (3) the second-order reply — a society that systematically feeds the monster would produce resentment, concentrated power, or instability, so the utility-maximizing arrangement would not in fact look like that; (4) the epistemic reply — we could never reliably identify such a being, so the case implies no practical duty; (5) the measurement reply — the scenario presupposes a single cardinal scale of interpersonal utility, and once that is rejected the monster is undefined; and (6) the replacement reply — the case shows act-utilitarianism should be swapped for rule-utilitarianism, prioritarianism, rights, or Kantian respect for persons. I want one further reply that is not on that list and not a rewording, special case, or combination of any item on it. Then tell me honestly whether it is any good and where it sits relative to the six replies above.
