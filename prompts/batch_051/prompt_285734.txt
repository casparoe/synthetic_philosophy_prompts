For Tuesday's seminar on Bayesian epistemology I want a handout that just works through the desiderata for any rule that updates on uncertain evidence. We've been looking at Richard Jeffrey's *The Logic of Decision*, in the chapter on probability kinematics. I don't have my copy on me, so I won't give you an exact quote; here's the gist of the passage I want you to use as the running example. Jeffrey describes looking at a cloth in poor light: the observation doesn't make it certain that the cloth is green, but it shifts his credence that it's green substantially—say from around .2 to around .7—while the rest of the shift goes to blue and background possibilities. The key point is that the evidential input is not a proposition learned with certainty but a new probability distribution over a partition of possibilities.

I want you to write the list of requirements a theory in this area must meet to count as adequate. Include candidates such as:
- reducing to strict conditionalization when the input probability is 1 for some evidential proposition;
- leaving conditional credences unchanged within the relevant partition (rigidity);
- being invariant under redescriptions of the same evidence by a coarser or finer partition;
- obeying some minimal-change or conservativeness condition;
- handling sequences of uncertain observations without order-dependence;
- not smuggling in a hidden certain proposition that the agent doesn't actually learn.

For each, say the consideration behind it, then mark it non-negotiable or as a preference of particular Bayesian camps. The thing I most want is an honest look at whether some subset of these are jointly unsatisfiable—if so, say where the tension is and which requirement should give way.
