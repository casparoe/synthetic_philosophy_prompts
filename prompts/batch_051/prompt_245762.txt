I run a philosophy of science reading group and want a "which view on reduction and emergence do you actually hold?" quiz for the first meeting. Nine questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto these five positions: strict type reduction, functional or nomological reduction, non-reductive physicalism, strong emergence, and instrumentalism about higher-level talk. No question should just ask whether someone "believes in emergence" or which label they like; everything has to be a concrete scenario or a forced choice about a case.

The angle I care about is seperating two things people constantly blur: (a) whether higher-level kinds can be identified with lower-level kinds, and (b) whether higher-level explanations do real explanatory work that lower-level ones don't. Please use at least these four cases, and add five of your own: temperature vs. mean kinetic energy; pain in an octopus vs. pain in a human; a glider in Conway's Game of Life; and a recession described as a pattern of individual transactions. For each question, write options that are load-bearing, so the scoring key can distiguish the five positions without any single question being answerable only if you already know the jargon.

After the questions and key, go through the answer combinations that end up empty or contradictory. For each unoccupied combination, say briefly whether it's empty because the view would be incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any two positions the quiz can't reliably tell apart and say what extra question would be needed to separate them.
