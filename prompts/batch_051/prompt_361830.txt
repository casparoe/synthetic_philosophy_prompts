In the research for a novel about a town that slowly turns its founder into a protective spirit, I have repeatedly come across a pattern in chronicles and folktales for which I have no precise word. A historical person — a founder, a lawgiver, a healer, a local martyr — is described by contemporaries in flatly human terms. Within a few generations the stories have fitted the figure with a miraculous birth, a command over weather, or a parentage that is not quite human; soon after that the figure is addressed as a deity. Later compilers sometimes run the same movement in reverse, explaining an established god as having been only an early king or benefactor whose deeds were exaggerated. People speak of the idea that the gods were once human as if it covered both movements, and I suspect that is an equivocation. Is there an established term for each direction, and how exactly should both be separated from the ordinary veneration of ancestors, from the formal deification of recently dead rulers, and from the slow accretion of miracle stories around a real person? I want the boundary drawn, not merely a label.
