I work in interpretability for deep networks, and I keep encountering the same inference without knowing whether it has an established name. A team trains a simple linear classifier on a layer's hidden states to recover a human-recognizable property — the grammatical number of a subject, the sentiment of a sentence, whether a claim is true — and then writes that the layer "encodes", "contains", or "represents" that property. Yet I have seen nearly the same readout succeed on other layers, on models trained for unrelated tasks, and in cases where deleting the recovered direction leaves the network's behavior essentially unchanged. The pattern seems to be: from the fact that a property can be reliably extracted from a system's internal states by a simple reader, people infer that the system itself uses or contains a representation of that property. I am not after a bare label. I want to know whether this inference has a name, exactly what additional evidence or argument would license it, and how to draw the boundary between a property's being recoverable from a system and its being represented or used by the system. If no established concept fits precisely, I would prefer a new term with an explicit definition and boundary cases over a forced fit to a nearby notion.
