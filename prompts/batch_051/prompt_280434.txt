i'm a phd student in early modern philosophy, writing a chapter on Spinoza's affects, and i keep coming back to the intellectual love of God. I want to test the boundaries of the concept against a case a friend described to me. Here's the criteria set I'm working with for amor Dei intellectualis, as I understand it from Ethics V (I'm paraphrasing, not quoting): (1) it's love in the strict Spinozist sense — a joy accompanied by the idea of its cause; (2) the cause is God, conceived not as a person but as the one infinite substance whos essence is expressed in the eternal aspect of things; (3) the joy arises from the third kind of knowledge, i.e., understanding something's essence as following necessarily from the essence of God; (4) it's an active emotion: the mind is an adequate cause, not being acted on; (5) the one who has it loves God not in so far as God is affected by anything, but in so far as God is eternal. My friend is a cosmologist, totally secular, no interest in theology. She says that often while doing a derivation she feels what she can only describe as a kind of impersonal exaltation — a joy that isn't about her career, and whose cause, if asked, she'd identify as the fact that the equations couldn't have been otherwise. She says it's most intense when a result falls out that she didn't expect but sees immediately had to hold. She doesn't call it love and bristles at the word God. Is her experience an instance of the intellectual love of God? Go criterion by criterion; where the answer is no, say whether that's about my criteria, about her, or about Spinoza's own view; and tell me whether the case suggests any of my five criteria should be revised.
