Please argue that Rawls gets this wrong: he treats excusable envy as a threat to the stability of an otherwise just order rather than as evidence that the order might not be just after all. Consider a case where the worst-off group's absolute position is improving and the difference principle is satisfied, but the gap between them and the best-off is now so wide that members of the worst-off group can no longer maintain the sense that they are cooperating with others on terms they could accept; their envy is excusable in Rawls's sense. Argue that this envy should count as a signal of injustice, not just a psychological reaction to inequality, and that a theory which treats it only as a stability problem leaves out something normative. Then say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to conclude that excusable envy really is only a stability problem rather then a ground of injustice, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
