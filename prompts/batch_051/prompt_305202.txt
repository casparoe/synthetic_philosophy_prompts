In my democracy and markets course we've been doing futarchy, and the debate just seems completely frozen. The pro side says: define the outcome you actually want, run a prediction market on which policy will hit it, and implement whatever the market says. The anti side says that isn't better governance, it's just rule by whoever can move the odds, and no matter what metric you pick people will game it. What's annoying is that both sides already know each other's moves and nobody changes their mind. I'm starting to think the real disagreement isn't about whether markets are accurate or manipulable, but about whether political legitimacy can be reduced to epistemic accuracy at all. How would you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Is it empirical, normative, or conceptual (e.g., is there even a coherent thing being predicted once the policy depends on the market's own forecast)? And given your diagnosis, is there a concrete result or institutional design change that would move one side, or is this just a standoff between two pictures of what politics is for?
