Ok so I have to write something for a philosophy class and I keep going in circles. In Sunday school they taught original sin roughly like this: every person is born already guilty because of what Adam did, even though you personally had nothing to do with it. And my first reaction is thats exactly the thing everyone says is unfair everywhere else: if someones dad robs a bank, we don't put the son in prison and say the guilt got passed down. We'd say you can't punish someone for a crime they didn't commit. But a lot of the same people who find that obviously wrong will also talk about our country owing things for slavery or genocide, or about us as Americans being responsible for stuff that happened before any of us were alive. And they treat that as the obviously moral way to speak, not as something in the same category as original sin. So there's a weird asymmetry: inherited guilt is treated as obviously unjust when the unit is a family or bloodline, but as serious and even obligatory when the unit is a nation, and as a core doctrine when the unit is the human race in church. I want to know if that asymmetry is justified or if it's just different vibes.

What I'd like is for you to build the strongest possible principle that makes these cases genuinely different, and then test it against the cases I keep getting stuck on. Here they are: (1) the descendants didn't do the wrong and would never have done it, but they did grow up materially better off because of it; (2) the relevant thing isn't a bloodline but an institution that still exists, like a church or a country, so the whole "it's the same institution" thing might do the work—but then doesn't that accidentally make original sin okay, since humanity is still the same family?; (3) a parent commits a crime and the whole family clearly benefits from it for years before anyone finds out. At the end, give me an honest verdict: should we keep the asymmetry, drop the inherited-guilt talk about nations and churches, or reverse it and admit inherited guilt in families isn't as crazy as we thought?
