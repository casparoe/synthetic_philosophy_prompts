I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing a feature that tries to make a subtle point vivid: a person can be rational at every individual moment and yet rationally exploitable over time. I would like you to construct a thought experiment to a precise specification, which I can adapt for the piece. The case should be built around a Dutch book — strictly speaking a Dutch strategy, since the trades occur at two different times, but I shall use the looser term for brevity. By a Dutch book I mean a set of wagers, each of which is acceptable to the agent at the time it is offered, that jointly guarantee the agent a net loss.

Build a case satisfying all of the following specifications.

1. The agent is synchronically coherent at both relevant times: at t0 their degrees of belief obey the probability calculus, and at t1 their degrees of belief again obey the probability calculus. The vulnerability must not arise from synchronic incoherence at either time.

2. The vulnerability must arise solely from a violation of the Reflection principle, understood roughly as the requirement that one's current degree of belief in a proposition should equal one's current expectation of one's future degree of belief in it. Concretely: at t0 the agent assigns degree of belief 0.2 to a proposition A, and the agent is certain at t0 that at t1 — after going through some routine that the agent regards as carrying no information about A — they will assign degree of belief 0.9 to A.

3. The anticipated credence shift must be psychologically ordinary. Set it in a newsroom or an equally routine professional setting. It must not involve drugs, hypnosis, coercion, memory loss, brain injury, perfect predictors, or any science-fictional device. Use a familiar mechanism, such as anticipated overconfidence after rehearsal, or anticipated influence from a known but non-informative source. You may idealise the certainty for the sake of the arithmetic, so long as the underlying mechanism is recognisably human.

4. A must be an ordinary factual proposition whose truth is settled by a stated settlement time. The wagers must be for money in pounds sterling, not utility points or exotic goods. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.

5. The bookie must not lie, deceive, or rely on hidden information about whether A is true; the exploit must work purely because of the agent's own announced degrees of belief.

After the scenario, do the walk-through. First state the verdict each side gives and why: the proponent of the Dutch-book argument for Reflection, and the defender of the view that rationality imposes only synchronic requirements of coherence at each time.
