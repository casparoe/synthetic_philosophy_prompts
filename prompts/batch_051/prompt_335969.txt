They don't line up with expected value, and I want to know whether any single principle captures them. In every case the coin is fair. (2) I would accept a repeated all-in flip paying +50% or −30%, but I would refuse a repeated all-in flip paying +100% or −80%, even though the two flips have the same arithmetic expected return of +10% per flip. (3) For the +50%/−40% flip repeated many times with a constant fraction of current wealth staked each round, I would bet about a quarter of my wealth each round and would not bet more, even though betting more raises expected wealth. (4) I would refuse a one-off +50%/−40% flip on my entire wealth. (5) I would refuse a one-off +10%/−5% flip on my entire wealth, even though this last refusal seems to go against my own tentative principle. My tentative principle is that these choices track expected log return — the time-average growth rate in the repeated cases — rather than expected return, but I cannot make that principle precise enough to derive each verdict. Please state the principle as precisely as you can, show how it handles the five cases, say what it predicts for a further case I have not listed (a repeated all-in flip paying +20% or −10%), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and which, if any, I should revise. Keep the response to at most 700 words.
