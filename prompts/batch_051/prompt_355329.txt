In a recent discussion about whether a passage of instrumental music can be sad in itself — not just because the composer happened to feel sad when writing it, or because listeners project sadness onto it — someone ended the exchange by saying, "Well, music is the language of the emotions." The line was doing real argumentative work: it was meant to settle the question in favor of the view that sadness is somehow carried by the sounds themselves, the way a sentence carries its meaning regardless of who utters it or hears it. I suspect that one sentence is collapsing several distinct claims, some of which survive scrutiny and some of which do not, and I would like it disambiguated properly as it functions in the puzzle of how sounds can be sad.

Please take the slogan "music is the language of the emotions" and lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing in this debate. For example, it might mean that music arouses emotions in listeners; that music resembles, depicts, or mimes expressive human behavior through features such as descending contours, slow tempi, and breath-like phrasing; that music employs a conventional vocabulary whose items refer to or stand for emotions the way words do; that music is the external manifestation of an inner feeling in the composer or performer; that music invites the listener to imagine a fictional persona who is feeling the emotion; that the felt quality is an intrinsic perceptible property of the sound complex itself; or something else you can distinguish. For each reading, specify: (a) the precise claim it commits to; (b) whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or merely stipulative; and (c) whether it would actually support the specific argument the slogan was being used to make — namely, that an instrumental piece can be sad independently of any actual person's feeling and independently of any listener's projection. Then say which reading the arguer most likely needed, and whether the slogan, on that reading, can bear the weight it was given.

Give your main analysis as a JSON object with the following schema:

- "slogan_examined": the string "music is the language of the emotions".
- "argument_it_was_used_to_make": a one-sentence restatement of the conclusion the arguer used the slogan to support.

  - "id": a short code, such as "R1".
  - "name": a short label for the reading, such as "arousal reading".
  - "precise_claim": a one- to two-sentence statement of the thesis.
  - "commitments": a list of strings stating what must be true of the music, the listener, or both for the reading to hold.
  - "verdict": one of "trivial", "substantive_and_defensible", "false", "stipulative", or "indeterminate".
  - "supports_arguer": "yes", "no", or "partly", judged against the arguer's use stated in "argument_it_was_used_to_make".
  - "note": a one-sentence explanation of the verdict and of the support assessment.
- "reading_the_arguer_needed": the id of the reading that most plausibly carries the arguer's conclusion.
