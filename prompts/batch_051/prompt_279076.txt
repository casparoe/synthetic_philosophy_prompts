i've come to think a lot of the bad-for-everyone outcomes people call coordination problems are better explained as races to the bottom: from each player's point of view the rational move, given what the others are doing, is to keep shaving the margin, and over time the standard itself drifts, so that cutting the margin stops feeling like a regrettable defection and starts feeling like being efficient or serious. AI safety is the case I care most about, but I want to know how strongly the mechanism generalizes. What would count against the strong version of the claim that in tax competition, arms races, environmental standards, labor conditions, and similar cases, the bad equilibrium is more than a failure to coordinate—it's the stable rational endpoint of the indvidual choices and the standards drift downward partly by becoming internalized as virtues? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: cases where the race ran the other direction, cases where people bound themselves without a central enforcer, or structural reasons the thing being competed over can't be shaved indefinitely. Also tell me which of these a committed race-to-the-bottom person would concede as a real hit and which they'd dismiss as changing the subject, and whether the view is at risk of anything or has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
