Not a philosopher, just someone who got weirdly into this. I was reading Hume's essay *Of National Characters*, and the part I cant stop thinking about is his argument that national characters come mostly from moral causes—things like government, poverty, occupation, laws, and habits—rather than from climate or air. He thinks people living under the same climate but different governments often have quite different characters, and that if you change the institutions, the people change too. So now I'm confused about what the phrase *national character* even picks out.

Can you give me a working definition of national character that gets these cases right?

a) A diplomat says Danes have a national character of trust because their welfare state and low corruption make trusting strangers rational.
b) A travel writer says Italians have a national character of talking with their hands, which she attributes to the warm weather.
c) A court denies asylum on the grounds that people from country X are, by national character, more likely to lie.
d) An economist measures national character with survey questions about punctuality, hard work, and thrift, then links the resulting scores to GDP.
e) Someone says their own people are naturally warlike, pointing to centuries of raiding.
f) Immigrant kids in one generation pick up the manners, tone, and habits of their new country so thoroughly that locals say the kids now have the country's character.

I think (a) and maybe (f) are real national character, (c) is just racism dressed up, and I honestly don't know about the rest. Propose the definition, run it through all six, and tell me which verdicts surprise you or which you'd revise if the definition pushed back.
