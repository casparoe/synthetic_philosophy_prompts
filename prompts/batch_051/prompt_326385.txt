For a project on how philosophy can inform the design of autonomous systems, I am putting together a sorting instrument to give an engineering team before we discuss tool AI versus agent AI. We are framing the debate as a set of commitments rather than a question of raw capability: whether an AI system may act on goals the user has not explicitly specified for the task at hand, whether it may form subgoals and take intermediate steps without checking in, whether it should defer or push back when its objectives appear to conflict with the user's, and whether its agency is a property of the system itself or of the wider human-machine loop. Please write this as a decision tree. Each internal node should be a concrete question about a running case — an AI office assistant that manages a team's calendars, email, and travel — phrased so that a thoughtful engineer can answer it by intuition before knowing which position the answer leads toward. For example: The system sees that the user has just double-booked a meeting; may it email the second meeting's organizer to propose a new time on its own initiative, or must it only flag the conflict and wait for instructions?

The leaves must include at least these positions, though you may rename them: a strict tool position holding that the system takes no action without explicit approval; a tool-with-initiative position holding that it may propose but never act on its own; a bounded or corrigible agent position holding that it may act within a restricted action space and must defer whenever its goals conflict with the user's; and a full agent position holding that it may form and pursue objectives of its own. Also include a position that treats the tool/agent distinction as a property of the interface or the surrounding human-machine loop rather than of the underlying system, plus at least one unoccupied combination of answers that you diagnose as unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Build the tree from the logical space first: name the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, and only then locate the actual positions in that space. Where two positions answer every node of the tree identically, say so explicitly and state which further question would separate them, rather than inventing a branch that does not track a real difference. Keep the instrument to no more than eight leaves and no more than seven branch nodes. The audience tends to assume that more capable systems are automatically more agentic, so part of the point is to sort people who might all describe themselves as wanting AI to remain a tool into genuinely different positions.
