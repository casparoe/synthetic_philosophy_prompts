For a grant proposal I am writing on lexical ambiguity, I need to clarify a claim that the lab has been treating as obvious but that a reviewer has attacked as unfalsifiable. The claim is that languages can carry a surprisingly large load of homonyms—English forms like bank or pitch, Mandarin syllable-level homophones, and the like—because context does almost all the disambiguating work before a listener notices, and that in many languages pitch, tone, and syntactic framing further distribute the burden. On this view, homonymy is a cheap design feature: it saves lexical material, and speakers are not confused because the surrounding signal already selects the intended sense. A colleague in the lab gives the opposite story: homonymy is tolerated only in the way an organism tolerates a low-grade infection. It imposes real costs on processing, learning, and repair, languages retain it only where those costs have not mattered enough to be selected against, and speakers quietly avoid the dangerous forms precisely when the context is thin. The two stories can both be true of the same corpus; the disagreement is about the underlying cost structure and about what tolerance means.

I want to turn the disagreement into a set of decisive observations rather than a standoff between metaphors. Design a research program that would test whether homonymy is cheaply resolved by context or costly and merely endured. Give me at least three concrete studies—behavioral, corpus-based, artificial-language, developmental, or some combination—specifying for each what result would favor the cheap-resolution view and what result would favor the costly-survival view, and what each comparison would and would not establish. Then say what part of the original question no such study can reach: for example, what the word tolerance is smuggling in, whether cross-linguistic data can be confounded by differences in redundancy, whether the relevant costs show up only in first-language acquisition or in diachronic change rather than in the adult online processing your measures capture. I want enough precision that after the studies are run, I can tell the reviewer exactly which interpretation of the lab's original sentence is still alive and which has been ruled out.
