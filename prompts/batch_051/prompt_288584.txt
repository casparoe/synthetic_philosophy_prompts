At my school's film club we've been stuck on the same argument for basically the whole semester, and its driving me insane. The question is whether you can form a real aesthetic judgment about a movie or an album on the basis of what other people tell you. One side (mostly my friend Sam) says no: a review can tell you the plot, the runtime, maybe that the critic gave it four stars, but it can't tell you whether the film is good, because 'good' in that way is something you have to see or hear for yourself. Sam says the experience has a texture that words don't transmit — you can know a bunch of facts about a song and still not know what it's like to actually hear it. The other side (Priya) says that's kind of snobby: she's gotten extremely good at predicting which albums she'll enjoy from reviews and friends, so why can't she say she knows an album is good on that basis? She also brings up the standard line that if a hundred reliable people tell you it's raining, you're justified in believing it's raining, so what would make aesthetic stuff special?

The frustrating thing is both sides basically agree on the facts. Sam agrees Priya's predictions are accurate and that she can often tell whether she personally will like something. Priya agrees Sam couldn't write a decent review of a film he hasnt seen, and that actually watching it would give him something extra. There's this recurring edge case where a person has read every review and can predict ratings perfectly but has never seen the film; Sam says that person still can't judge the film, Priya says that's pretty much what judging is. My working theory is that the word 'judgment' is doing two different jobs: one side is talking about judging what the work is like (the experience itself), and the other is talking about judging that it's good (a reliable belief). But maybe I'm wrong and it's actually a deeper divide about whether aesthetic appreciation is a kind of knowledge-that or a skill, or about whether first-person acquaintance has some special epistemic role. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is — verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, or about starting intuitions neither side can argue for — and what, if anything, would count as progress.

Also, please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of.
