I'm on the pharmacy and therapeutics (P&T) commitee at a large teaching hospital, and a request came in to add a wakefulness-promoting drug to the formulary — same family as modafinil, already FDA-approved for narcolepsy and shift-work sleep disorder, and the hospital already covers it for those two indications. The item on Thursday's agenda is whether to also cover it for our surgical residents during night-float blocks. The residents' argument is that our own 28-hour call schedule produces exactly the sleep disruption that the "shift-work sleep disorder" diagnosis describes, and the only real difference is that the hospital, rather than a trucking company, imposes the schedule. So it would be incoherent to cover it for outside shift workers but not for the people whose sleep we wreck ourselves. Several committee members push back: the residents don't have a disorder, they have a hard job, and giving a wakefulness drug to healthy young doctors so they perform better is enhancement, not treatment. Others reply that there's no neutral baseline of "healthy" here, because we created the deficit. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete policy — cover it for residents or not, and under what conditions — give the reasoning, and honestly engage the strongest argument on the losing side.
