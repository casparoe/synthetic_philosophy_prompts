I've been reading Kant's 1784 essay, Idea for a Universal History from a Cosmopolitan Point of View, and the Fourth Thesis has me stuck. As I understand it, Kant argues that the antagonism among human beings—the way each of us wants both to cooperate and to have everything our own way—is the very mechanism by which nature drives the species toward a fully developed rational order. The competition among individuals produces a progress no individual intends. My own way of putting this, from a software background, is that Kant is describing a kind of gradient-descent process: individual selfishness and conflict supply the error signal, but there is no representation of the target state anywhere in the system. The destination is not stored in any variable; it is just whatever state causes the aggregate loss to decrease over generations.

I've started calling this stigmergic providence, by analogy with the way termites build complex mounds without any termite having a blueprint or an intention to build a mound: the shape is real, it is adaptive, it looks like the work of a designer, but the design is an emergent property of local interactions. My question is whether this is a fair reconstruction of what Kant means by a natural purpose in history, or whether I have quietly replaced Kant's teleology with a naturalistic reduction he would reject. And if not, what is left of the idea that the arc of history bends toward justice? If all we can say is that history is an unplanned optimization process toward an unrepresented goal, does that actually support any confidence in the long run, or does it collapse under historical catastrophes? I want an honest assessment: what does my stigmergic providence get right, what does it get wrong, and is the concept doing any real work or just renaming a vague analogy?
