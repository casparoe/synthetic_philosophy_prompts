I'm giving a talk next term in a joint philosophy–mathematics colloquium, and the question period has a reputation for being merciless. The talk concerns whether it matters how you label the roots of a polynomial. My thesis is that the familiar failure of canonical choice here is not a harmless notational inconvenience but a fact about what algebraic objects are. For example, no statement in the language of fields with parameters from Q distinguishes the two roots of x² + 1: the conjugation automorphism swaps i and −i while fixing Q. The three cube roots of 2 inside the splitting field of x³ − 2 are likewise permuted by the full Galois group, so none of them is definable over Q on its own. I want to argue that a single root, separated from the whole symmetric configuration and from the automorphisms that permute it, is not a well-individuated object; saying ‘let α be a root of f’ picks out a position in a symmetry class rather than a particular thing. My stronger claim is that anyone who still takes an individual root to be a genuine mathematical object is committed to something like haecceitism — bare thisness, a difference between symmetric roots that is not grounded in any property or relation — and the mathematics gives no reason to pay that price.

The audience is roughly half philosophers, half working algebraists, with a few logicians. The philosophers mostly work on structuralism, abstraction, and the metaphysics of mathematics; they will suspect, not unreasonably, that I am retelling Benacerraf’s point about the multiple set-theoretic reductions of arithmetic, but for fields and polynomials, and they will press whether anything genuinely new follows. The algebraists are comfortable with Galois theory and will think the matter was long ago dissolved: one simply works up to the action of the Galois group, and the question ‘which root is which’ is a pseudo-question — so they will want to know what my talk says that the standard quotienting story doesn’t. The logicians are likely to ask sharper questions about the difference between definability in a structure, invariance under automorphism, and reference fixed by choosing an embedding or presentation.

My argument, compressed, runs: (i) for many polynomials, no individual root is definable over the base field; (ii) therefore reference to a single root must be mediated by a presentation, an embedding, or a naming convention; (iii) a presentation-independent difference among the roots would have to be a bare difference of thisness, which the algebra does not record; so (iv) either root-terms have only convention-relative reference, or we posit haecceitistic distinctions among mathematical individuals.
