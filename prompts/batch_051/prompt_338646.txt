Suppose that in 2030 a wealthy common-law democracy with a written constitution, judicial review, and a substantial administrative state amends its constitution to abolish sovereign immunity in the strongest plausible form. From that date, the state, its departments, and its officials acting in their official capacity may be sued in the ordinary courts on exactly the same terms as any private defendant: no sovereign or official immunity; no special limitation periods; no requirement that a plaintiff show bad faith or malice; no shield for decisions classified as legislative, budgetary, or regulatory; no cap on damages; and no rule substituting an individual official for the treasury. Courts may enjoin or award damages against the state while the challenged policy remains in force. Nothing else changes: elections, separation of powers, civil-service rules, and the budget process continue as before.

Give me your best argued prediction of how this jurisdiction looks in 2080. I want the chain of reasoning made visible: which behavior of ministers, agencies, street-level officials, and private litigants changes most; which changes least; how the legislature, the judiciary, and the bureaucracy adapt (for example, through indemnification practices, statutory re-immunities, procedural filters, or internal pricing of liability risk); and what new pathologies appear, such as over-deterrence of lawful but controversial decisions, routine settlement as a cost of administration, or courts becoming de facto policymakers. In the course of that prediction, address the underlying question directly: does the jurisdiction in practice come to treat the state as an entity that can do wrong and answer for it, or does liability become merely a statistical cost of dispersed decisions, like an insured risk pool? Explain which of your predictions you regard as robust, which are guesses, and what would have to hold for your main prediction to be wrong; say how confident you are in the overall prediction; and identify the single point on which you are least sure.
