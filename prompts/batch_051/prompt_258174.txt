I am drafting a position paper for a graduate seminar on physicalism, and I would like an audit of the package of views I have been defending before I circulate it in class. My current stance is built around the following commitments:

(a) Physicalism is true: every fact about the world, including every mental fact, is determined by the physical facts.

(b) Physicalism should be a substantive, contingent claim about the world. It should be the sort of thesis that could have turned out false, and it should rule out genuine alternatives rather than being true by definition.

(c) The term "physical" should be given positive, physics-based content. Physical properties and entities are the kinds of properties and entities recognized by physics, or are composed of or reducible to such things. I regard negative definitions — defining the physical as "not fundamentally mental" or "not supernatural" — as unilluminating and too weak to state the thesis.

(d) We should defer to physics in settling what the physical facts and properties are.

(e) Current physics is incomplete and will be revised, perhaps substantially, as past physics has been revised.

(f) Whatever else it does, physicalism must at least rule out Cartesian souls and fundamental consciousness; a version of physicalism compatible with either is not a version I would recognize as physicalism.

I have a persistent sense that something in this set is unstable, but I cannot locate exactly where the strain is. Which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, which merely appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious, and say what each fix would cost.
