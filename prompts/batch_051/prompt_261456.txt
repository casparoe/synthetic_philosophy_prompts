Consider the following clause from a proposed international protocol on lethal autonomous weapons:

"No weapon system may select or engage a human target unless a human operator, in each individual case, has knowingly and deliberately authorized that specific engagement."

Now consider two cases its drafters likely did not foresee. In the first, an operator approves a queue of several hundred system-generated targeting nominations as a single batch, relying solely on the automatically produced summary attached to each nomination, even though there is no time to read them. In the second, a counter-sniper system on a vehicle returns fire at the detected origin of hostile fire within a fraction of a second; the operator has activated the system and fixed its rules of engagement, but no human intervenes in any particular shot.

Does the clause, read literally, already permit either case? What does it require when read according to its evident purpose of preserving meaningful human control? What would the drafters likely have said if asked about these cases, and what would a reasonable reader take the clause to demand? Where these readings diverge, which should govern — and what does the divergence reveal about what the clause was ever able to secure?
