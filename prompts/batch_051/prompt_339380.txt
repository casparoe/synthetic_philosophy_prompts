On here, every utilitarianism thread eventually bottoms out in someone saying the view ignores the seperateness of persons, so I tried to write that out as an actual argument and now the argument proves too much. (1) A loss to one person can only be made right by a benefit to that same person; if breaking your arm would give two strangers a big windfall, that doesn't make the broken arm okay. (2) That's exactly what distinguishes trade-offs across people from trade-offs within a single life, where the same subject pays the cost and gets the benefit. (3) So no policy may impose a cost on one person in order to produce a benefit for a different person. (4) But nearly every real policy does that: progressive taxation, pandemic restrictions, draft lotteries, triage. (5) It's just not true that nearly every real policy is wrong. So either (1) is false or the move from (1)-(2) to (3) is overshooting; I'd much rather keep (1) than (5), but I don't see how to block (3) without making (1) vacuous. And there's a second worry that might be fatal on its own: if (2) is what's doing the work, then my present self and my future self are also distinct subjects in the relevant sense, so saving for retirement would be no more rational or required than paying for a stranger's retirement. Which step would you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
