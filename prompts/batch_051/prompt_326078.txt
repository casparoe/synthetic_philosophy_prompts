For the closing session of my seminar on rationality, we have been studying money pumps, and I want to end with a poem that does real work rather than merely gesture at the theory. The synchronic case is straightforward: an agent has intransitive pairwise preferences, preferring A to B, B to C, but C to A, so a trader can charge a small fee to swap B for A, then C for B, then A for C, leaving the agent where they started but poorer, because every exchange was one the agent preferred at the time. What I want the poem to capture is the diachronic version, in which there need be no single inconsistent ranking at all. Each choice looks locally rational from the standpoint of the person making it; what shifts is the agent's perspective between anticipating an exchange and assessing it afterward, or between successive moments of evaluation, so that the circularity appears only across time.

Please write a villanelle from the point of view of an agent being led around such a cycle. The two refrains should be the two recurring verdicts the agent reaches at the successive legs of the cycle: one the prospective justification that makes the trade seem best before it is made, and the other the retrospective disappointment that sets up the next trade. Their alternation should do the structural work of moving the agent through the trades and back to the starting point, and their final convergence in the closing couplet should register that the only thing that has accumulated is the fee. Someone who knows the theory should be able to see exactly which leg of the cycle each tercet performs and why the form keeps reasserting itself; someone who has never heard of a money pump should simply find it a good poem.
