I've been keeping a list of my friend Nadia's rulings about who bears the burden of proof, because she's weirdly consistant about it and I'm trying to reverse-engineer her principle. So far: a guy who claimed Bigfoot is real had to produce the evidence, while the rest of us who just weren't convinced owed no argument. A wellness influencer she knows who claimed homeopathy cures infections had the burden, even though you can't strictly prove a negative. When she was on a jury, she said the state had to prove the defendant's guilt and the defense could get by just poking holes, because the accused starts out presumed innocent. Her uncle who insisted climate change is a hoax had to argue for it, and no one had to re-litigate mainstream science. And a classmate in her seminar who said we're all living in a simulation had the burden, even though nobody could refute him.

The new case came up last weekend. Alex has started telling people he thinks there is no God—not the "I just lack belief" thing, but the actual claim that theism is false, and he explicitly says he isn't trying to deconvert anyone, just stating his view when religion comes up. Sam is his mirror image: she states that God exists, also without trying to argue anyone around. And I'm the agnostic in the room; I don't assert either way, I just say I haven't settled it. If Nadia wants to stay consistent with her own record, what should she say about who owes an argument here—Alex, Sam, me, or nobody? Which earlier case is the closest analog, what principle would she be adopting with each answer if her precedents underdetermine it, and what further case would settle which rule she's actually running?
