write me a pantoum — the form where the second and fourth lines of each quatrain get reused as the first and third of the next — in the voice of someone trying to answer "why do you love me?" and failing in exactly the right way. the two available answers are the appraisal view (we love a person because of their qualities, which are valuable before the love) and the bestowal view (the qualities matter because the love makes them matter). the catch is that the form should do real philosophical work: a line that first shows up sounding like evidence for the appraisal view should, when it returns in the next stanza, read as something the lover's own love has bestowed. maybe five quatrains, and don't name the views or any philosophers in the poem itself. leave it genuinely open wich came first.
