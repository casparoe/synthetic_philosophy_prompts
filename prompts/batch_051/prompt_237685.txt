Longtime lurker on the rationality-adjacent forums here. The one-liner I keep bumping into is “technology is just a tool.” Last week it showed up in a thread about wether the design of an algorithmic feed can itself be radicalizing people, with the person basically saying the feed is inert and all the agency, blame, and politics live in the humans choosing to build, tune, and watch it. I have a feeling that sentence is smuggling several distinct claims at once: that artifacts don’t have intentions, that any given artifact can be turned to many ends, that artifacts don’t determine outcomes on their own, that the moral problem is always upstream in the user or designer, and maybe even that it’s a category mistake to regulate the artifact instead of the people. But I can’t tell which of those are tautologies, which are substantive and defensible, and which are just false. Lay out the precise theses hiding in “technology is just a tool,” flag which are platitudes and which are actually contentious, and say which one is really doing the work when someone uses it to settle a debate about whether a ranking algorithm has politics. For each reading, also tell me whether it would actually license the “so the artifact is off the hook” conclusion, or whether the slogan is earning rhetorical force the reading can’t cash.
