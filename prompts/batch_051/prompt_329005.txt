In a discussion about whether a just God could consign anyone to eternal conscious torment, a friend cut the argument short by declaring, "No finite sin can deserve infinite punishment." He said it as though it settled the matter, but I suspect that one sentence compresses several distinct arguments into one. Could you lay out the precise claims this one-liner might be making — for example, about what proportionality of punishment requires, whether the relevant magnitude is duration or intensity, whether a sin against God might be "infinite" in some non-temporal sense, whether hell is better understood as retribution imposed from outside or as a state freely persisted in, and whether the force of the objection changes if the alternatives are annihilation or universal salvation rather than eternal torment? For each reading, tell me whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and say which reading is actually doing the work when the sentence is used to rule out eternal conscious torment.
