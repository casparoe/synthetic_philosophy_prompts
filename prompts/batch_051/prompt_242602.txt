I am a condensed-matter physicist with a serious amateur interest in the Presocratics but no formal training in the history of philosophy. I would like you to restate the ancient atomist theory of matter — the Leucippus–Democritus picture on which the world consists of indivisible, solid, impenetrable atoms moving in an empty void, and on which all macroscopic qualities such as colour, heat, taste, and hardness are not in the atoms themselves but arise from their shapes, arrangements, and motions — in the vocabulary of my own discipline: quantum fields, the vacuum, exchange symmetry and the Pauli exclusion principle, Coulomb and exchange interactions, lattice structure, and the like. Proceed commitment by commitment: for solidity, impenetrability, indivisibility, the void, motion through the void, and the reduction of sensible qualities to atomic arrangement, tell me what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then provide the residue report: where the translation fails, say whether the failure exposes a limitation in the modern vocabulary, a place where the atomists were simply wrong or asking the wrong question, or a place where their concept is doing work my vocabulary cannot recover. End with a specific verdict on impenetrability: when I say that two macroscopic bodies cannot occupy the same region of space, am I preserving the atomist doctrine of solid, impenetrable matter, or has the modern explanation via electromagnetic repulsion and the exclusion principle dissolved the very notion of solidity the atomists were trying to explain? Please write the answer in British English.
