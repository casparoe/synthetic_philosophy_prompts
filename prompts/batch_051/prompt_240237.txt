Here's an asymmetry I keep coming back to. A doctor can put you on a drug for something it was never tested for, with side effects that are basically unknown for someone like you, and so long as it's presented as clinical judgment, you might never be told how thin the evidence is, and nobody treats what's happening as an experiment on you. But if a researcher at the same hospital wants one extra tube of your blood to study how your body handles that exact drug, suddenly there are consent forms, an ethics committee, and a list of risks longer than the risks of the drug. Both impose risk on you without you choosing the risk level; we just call one care and the other research. What's doing the moral work there? The clinician's aim of benefiting you, even when the evidence doesn't support that aim? The fact that you technically consented to treatment but not to research, even though the unproven nature of the treatment was never disclosed? And does the distinction survive a case where the standard-of-care treatment is shakier than the research procedure is dangerous? If you had to keep, scrap, or redraw the line, what would you do?
