I have no formal training in philosophy, but for the past few years I have been working out, on my own, something I call the casting-call theory of fictional names, and I would like an honest assessment of it. The central claim is that a fictional proper name such as Sherlock Holmes is not a label for an object the way London is, and it is not simply empty the way a nonsense syllable is. Instead, it denotes a weighted profile: a structured list of properties drawn from the canonical stories, each one carrying a centrality weight. Being a consulting detective and living at 221B Baker Street carry very high weight; playing the violin and having a certain drug habit carry moderate weight; the color of his dressing gown carries low weight. Given any candidate individual in the world projected by a text, one can measure how well that candidate fits the profile, with the weights deciding how much each match or mismatch matters. My view is then that a sentence is true in the fiction just in case the best-fitting candidate in that text's world makes the sentence true. This also explains, I think, why we tolerate loose adaptations: each retelling simply casts a different individual in the old role, and a retelling is unfaithful only where it departs from the high-weight properties.

The problem I keep getting stuck on is conflict among the canonical properties themselves. Watson's war wound is placed in his shoulder in one story and in his leg in another, and nothing in the canon says which detail should prevail. My original rule was to let higher-weight properties override lower-weight ones, but when two canonically fixed details collide with roughly equal weight, the casting call ends in a tie. I have also tried to block a different objection: that the best Holmes-fit in the actual world might be some real historical detective, which would make my account say that Holmes refers to that real person. I block it by restricting candidates to the worlds projected by texts, but I suspect that makes the phrase world projected by a text do all the real work while my weighted profile just rides along.

What I would like to know is whether this is really a version of a view that already exists under some other name, and if so, which one. Then give me the three or four strongest objections to the theory as I have stated it, including at least one I have not anticipated, and say whether any of them is fatal or whether it can be repaired without giving up the core idea.
