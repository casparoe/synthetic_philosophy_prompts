I chair the care review committee at a skilled nursing facility, and were split on a discharge case. Margaret is 79, ready to leave rehab after a fall, but she has early dementia and can't manage her meds, meals, or appointments reliably without help. Her only child, Dana, is 54 and has been basically estranged since she left home at 17. Margaret was never abusive — she provided the basics and nothing more — but she was cold, critical, and never really showed up for Dana emotionally, so its not a neat abuse case. Dana has made clear she won't take the caregiver role; she isn't hostile, she just says the two of them never had the kind of relationship that would make her the obvious person. Our social worker still wants the discharge plan to name Dana as primary caregiver and has been pushing the line that adult children owe their parents care, full stop. Another committee member keeps invoking Jane English's friendship model — roughly, that whatever grown children owe their parents comes out of the actual relationship they built, not from biology or some debt for being raised — and thinks Dana may owe very little here and that we should stop building the plan around her. We have to decide whether the plan should keep trying to place the burden on Dana or proceed as if there is no available family and send Margaret down the guardianship / long-term care route, which is slower and mostly on the public dime.

Could you draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Wednesday's meeting? It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should engage the strongest consideration on the side you don't recommend rather than just dismissing it. Also say how confident you are in the recommendation and single out the point you're least sure of.
