Two mates of mine, Dan and Priya, run a small betting syndicate — football, politics, the odd novelty market. Last week I watched them fall out over staking policy. Their model spits out a probability for each bet, they take the market price, and then they have to choose how much of the bankroll to put down. Dan wants them on full Kelly. I stayed out of it at the time, but I honestly couldn't sort out who was right, and I'd like you to adjudicate the argument move by move.

(1) Dan said: full Kelly maximises the expected log of the bankroll, and in the long run any other fixed fraction grows slower almost surely. If we're serious about compounding, we should bet the full Kelly fraction and stop second-guessing it.

(2) Priya replied: that only works if your probabilities are the true probabilities. Ours are estimates, and Kelly punishes estimation error brutally — if you bet as though you have an edge when you don't, or overbet a small edge, the long-run growth rate can go negative. Half Kelly is a hedge against model error.

(3) Dan came back: hedging by just halving everything is arbitrary. If you don't trust your probabilities, fix the probabilities — calibrate the model, shrink toward the market price, demand a bigger margin before you bet. Then bet the corrected full Kelly. Scaling a dodgy estimate by a half doesn't make it good; it just makes us poorer on every bet where the edge was real.

(4) Priya said: half Kelly isn't arbitrary, it's a different utility function. Full Kelly falls out of maximising log utility; half Kelly roughly matches relative risk aversion around two, and most of the syndicate are more risk-averse than log. The syndicate doesn't have a single risk tolerance, so full Kelly is really just imposing Dan's. And once you admit there's estimation error, the genuinely optimal fixed fraction is smaller than full Kelly anyway, so "full Kelly" isn't even the right target.

That's where they stalled. Please adjudicate the disagreement move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, and briefly justify each rating. Then say who's right about which specific point, and what, if anything, both of them are missing. And please seperate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this — don't present a contested view as if it were the consensus.
