I'm putting together a handout for an undergrad unit on the sorites paradox, and I want it to include a "which theory of vagueness do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label sounds nice to them. The leaves should be: epistemicism, supervaluationism, degree/many-valued views, contextualism, and a fifth leaf reserved for patterns that fit none of them — call it "the whole business is rigged" or something — if the data actually pushes that way. Quick glosses so we're on the same page: epistemicism holds there's a sharp cutoff but we can't know where it is; supervaluationism holds borderline statements are neither true nor false while classical tautologies still hold; degree views hold truth comes in degrees; contextualism holds the cutoff moves around with context and interests.

Do it in two layers. First lay out the logical space: pick five or six yes/no questions (or forced-choice mini-cases) that genuinely split these positions, then say for each combination of answers whether it's occupied by one of the leaves, incoherent, or just empty. Then turn the questions into the actual quiz items — aim for seven or eight — with natural answer options and a scoring key.

I've got four cases you have to include, though you can add a couple of your own:

1. A standard sorites line: a thousand paint chips running from clearly red to clearly orange, adjacent chips indistinguishable. Ask the reader what's going on when the redness stops. (yes this is load-bearing — dont skip it.)
2. A judge has to rule on whether someone is bald for an insurance payout, and the guy is dead-center borderline. Is the verdict making it true, reporting something already settled, or choosing one of several admissible lines?
3. Alex and Sam differ in height by a millimeter, Sam the taller one. Can a sane classification call Alex tall and Sam not tall, yes or no?
4. Whether it makes sense to call someone "borderline borderline" tall — i.e. whether the borderline region itself has a vague edge.

For the scoring key, map actual response patterns onto the leaves, and put a one-sentence "this is the part you're stuck with" note under each. Then tell me straight which pairs of leaves your questions can't tell apart, and what extra question about higher-order vagueness or penumbral connections would be needed to split them.
