In a reading group I've been in for years, the argument over whether culture is genuinely Darwinian has stalled in a way I want to understand rather than just relitigate. One camp, following Dawkins's original coinage, treats memes—tunes, words, rituals, techniques, stories—as replicators: units that get copied with enough fidelity to form lineages, vary, and are selected by attention, memory, media, and other social environments. Another camp, associated with Sperber's critique, says this gets transmission backwards: communication is not high-fidelity copying but reconstruction—each hearer or learner produces a new representation by drawing inferences from the previous one plus prior knowledge, which is why representations converge on stable attractor forms without anything gene-like doing the replicating. A third group, the dual-inheritance theorists, accepts that culture evolves through population-level variation and selective retention, but denies that the evolving units must be discrete or faithfully copied. What puzzles me is that the three sides largely accept the same broad descriptive facts—cultural variants spread, persist, change, and sometimes accumulate refinements—and yet no one moves. My hypothesis is that the real fault line is about what Darwinian selection requires: the meme camp treats particulate, high-fidelity copying as essential, while the others think that demand is an artifact of the gene analogy and that population-level selection can proceed without it. On this reading, the argument gets conducted as if it were an empirical dispute about how faithfully humans copy, when it may largely be a conceptual dispute about what counts as replication in the first place. Is that diagnosis right? If it is, tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is—empirical, conceptual, or two research programs answering different questions (micro-mechanisms of transmission versus population-level dynamics)—and what would count as progress given that diagnosis. If my hypothesis is wrong, give me the correct structure of the impasse and explain what, if anything, would move it.
