Three affluent industrial democracies have drawn the boundary between working time and private time in sharply different ways, and I want to know what view of work and leisure each arrangement already commits a society to.

In France the standard workweek has been set by statute at 35 hours since 2000, with overtime above that threshold carrying a premium. Senior and autonomous employees, however, can be moved onto annualized day packages that exempt them from the weekly limit, and since 2017 larger firms must negotiate a right to disconnect so that employees are not required to answer work email outside ordinary hours. The stated rationale is protection of health and private life.

In Japan the state officially recognizes karoshi, death from overwork, as a compensable occupational injury. The certification standard is, roughly, a hundred hours of overtime in the month before death or an average of eighty hours a month over the preceding two to six months, and both cardiovascular deaths and suicides are eligible. The government caps overtime and runs prevention campaigns, but the caps loosen during busy periods and unpaid service overtime persists. Overwork is treated as a serious hazard, yet one managed within an economy that continues to read extreme devotion to work as a virtue.

In the United States there is no federal entitlement to paid vacation and no maximum working hours for most adults. Covered hourly workers must be paid a premium for overtime after forty hours, but salaried exempt employees may be required to work any number of hours for no additional pay. Working time is treated largely as a matter of private contract.

Reconstruct the theory of work and leisure that each regime presupposes. Is leisure being treated as a right the worker retains against work, as a scarce resource the law must ration, as recovery time whose point is to restore the worker's productivity, or as a commodity the worker sells at whatever price the market sets? Distinguish what each regime strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with, and flag the places where a single regime seems to presuppose inconsistent things. Then take the Protestant work ethic, the idea that disciplined worldly labor is itself a calling or moral duty while idleness is a kind of moral failure, and say which of the three regimes it genuinely licenses. Finally, describe how any one of these legal regimes would have to change if it were rebuilt around the rival view that leisure has intrinsic value and work is at best a necessary cost.
