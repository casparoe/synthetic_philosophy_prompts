Im not any kind of philosopher, just a guy who's been on his neighborhood association board for going on ten years and keeps a running list of gut calls on building fights. Lately I tried to see if there's one rule underneath all of them and I'm starting to think there isnt, which bothers me. Can you find it, or tell me which of my calls I have to give up?

Here's the list.

1. A developer from two states over buys the little bungalow at the end of my street and wants to tear it down and put up a four-story walkup, eight apartments, no parking. The block is all single-family houses. The neighbors are furious about traffic, shadows, the "character of the neighborhood." My gut says let him build. The character stuff mostly sounds like people who just don't want renters parking near their tulips, and we are short on housing.

2. On the other side of the neighborhood there's an old rent-controlled building, tenants who have been there twenty and thirty years. A developer wants to buy it, empty it out, turn it into luxury condos. The tenants are fighting it. My gut says they should win, or at least the city should make the developer give them first crack at the new units at the same rent plus moving costs, and block it if he won't. Throwing a 78-year-old woman out of the only home she's got so somebody can have granite countertops is not okay.

3. There's a vacant lot near the train and somebody wants to put up a hundred percent affordable building. Homeowners come to the meeting talking about "changing the fabric" and property values and how people in affordable housing don't maintain their places. My gut says their objections shouldn't count for anything. That's just prejudice wearing a nicer name.

4. There's a privately owned empty lot two blocks over that people have used as a community garden for twenty years. The owner now wants to build five apartments on it. The neighbors are trying to get the city to buy it for a park, but the city says it has no money. My gut says I'm with the neighbors and I'd fight the apartments. Five homes is five homes, but paving over the one green spot for a mile feels like a real loss, not a "character of the neighborhood" loss.

5. People who live out in the suburb two towns over have been writing in about our zoning vote, saying they'd move here if we built more apartments so they should get a say. My gut says no. You don't live here, you don't get a vote, and "it would be nice to live there" is not a stake.

6. A woman who moved here three years ago, rents two blocks away, works at the hospital, is the loudest supporter of the building from case 1. A man who's owned his house for forty years says he should get more say than her because he's paid taxes here longer. My gut says equal. Longevity shouldn't buy extra votes, even if it makes me a little sad to say so.

Here's what I can't square. In case 1 I'm counting the eight families who'd move into the new building as a point for it, without knowing who they are, but in case 5 I'm saying outsiders get no say at all. And in case 1 I'm telling neighbors to eat the loss of their quiet street, but in case 4 I'm treating another group of neighbors' loss of a garden as almost sacred. I feel like the right rule is something like "you get a say based on what you'd actually lose," but then the people losing their afternoon sun and the people losing the garden both count, and I want only the garden to count. I don't know where to draw that line without it just being "losses I personally care about."

Then the one I keep going back and forth on: there's a proposal for a transitional house for men coming out of prison, and it would be on my own block, basically facing my kitchen window. My brain says I should support it for the same reason I support the affordable building. My stomach says I don't want it there. I honestly cant tell whether the rule that makes my other calls sensible would let me fight it or make me eat it. So: is there one rule that gets all six, what does it say about the transitional house, and which of my gut calls does it make me give up?
