Every intro-to-experimental-philosophy discussion of the Knobe effect runs through the chairman and the environment case, where a foreseen side effect gets called intentional when it is bad but not when it is good. I want to figure out whether the thing doing the work is genuinely moral badness, or just violation of a norm more broadly, and I need a pair of vignettes to run in a survey. Build me a pair of realistic vignettes where an agent has the same indifferent attitude toward a side effect, and the side effect either complies with or violates a norm, but neither outcome is morally wrong — think etiquette, a style rule, or a house rule. The two vignettes should be near-identical except for which side of the norm the side effect lands on. Then walk me through the mechanics: what a moral-valence account predicts, what a norm-violation account predicts, and which feature of your construction actually pries them apart. Last, if the asymmetry showed up in the etiquette case anyway, what is the strongest move a defender of the moral-valence account could make to keep the result consistent with their view?
