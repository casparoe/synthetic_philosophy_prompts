Started a new job a few months ago as a policy researcher for a backbench MP, and I keep getting handed private members bills that want to criminalise some consensual adult behaviour because the sponsor thinks it's immoral or disgusting. Last week it was a bill to criminalise paying for sex; the week before, one to make adultery a criminal offence again; next month there's one on banning all full-contact combat sports, boxing and MMA included. I did law at uni so I half-remember the Hart–Devlin debate from jurisprudence, but I've never actually had to use it, and I suspect I've been improvising badly.

I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the Hart side of that debate (the harm principle and the critique of legal moralism), that I can run on any of these bills in about ninety minutes and turn into a one-page note for my MP. She's socially conservative and has to vote and speak publicly on them, so the stakes are real though not usually rushed; I normally get a few days' warning and there's no one checking my work before it reaches her.

The procedure should be a checklist or an ordered set of questions: what to ask first, what counts as an adequate answer to each, where it stops with "do not support on moral grounds alone", and where it hands the decision to her (or to me) as a matter of values rather than facts. It also needs to handle indirect-harm arguments — the it-normalises-bad-behaviour move, the it-degrades-the-participants-even-if-they-consent move — because that's where I currently get lost.

Please commit: pick one procedure, make the hard choices, and give the reasons. I don't want three options or a balanced survey of the debate; I need something I can actually run. Where the theory genuinely gives out — say, what counts as a harm, or how remote a harm can be before we stop counting it — say so explicitly and mark it as a judgement call, but still set a default so I'm not left with a blank.

Then run it on these three: (1) a bill criminalising the purchase of sexual services, with an exemption for victims of trafficking; (2) a bill making adultery a criminal offence again; (3) a bill banning all full-contact combat sports on the ground that they're degrading. At least one worked example should produce an answer my MP won't like.

Write it in British English. And dont bother with references, case law, or citations; I can't check them anyway and I care about the reasoning, not the reading list.
