I've been reading some popular articles about the Anthropocene, the proposal that human activity has become a force powerful enough to define a new geological epoch, and I ended up in a long conversation with a friend who studies geology. He made the following argument against treating the Anthropocene as a scientific question at all. I'll number the steps as he did:

(1) If the Anthropocene is a genuine geological epoch, then there must be a fact of the matter about when it began.
(2) But the evidence does not force any single starting point on us: the rise of agriculture, the Columbian Exchange, the Industrial Revolution, and the mid-twentieth-century surge in population and industry are all reasonable candidates, and each reflects a different kind and timing of human impact.
(3) If the evidence does not force a single answer on us, then choosing one is not a matter of following the evidence but of deciding what matters to us.
(4) Therefore, choosing a start date for the Anthropocene is not a matter of following the evidence but of deciding what matters to us.
(5) A choice that is not a matter of following the evidence is not a scientific choice at all; it is a political or ideological choice dressed up in the language of science.
(6) Therefore, the question of when the Anthropocene began is not a scientific question but a political or ideological one.

He concluded that the scientists debating the starting date are really doing politics under a scientific disguise, and that the only honest position is to admit that we pick the date roughly the way we pick a commemorative holiday. I find the steps individually hard to reject, but the conclusion seems too strong, and I suspect the argument slides somewhere between what the evidence tells us and what we choose to care about. Is the argument valid? If not, please tell me exactly which step fails, how it would need to be rephrased to be acceptable, and whether the conclusion survives in any more modest form; if you think the argument is sound, explain why my unease is misplaced.
