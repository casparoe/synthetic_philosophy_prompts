I've come round to the view that physics has a bottom. By that I mean there is some final theory — a finite set of laws, equations, or principles — from which the behavior of everything else follows, with no unexplained constants left over, and that the theory is short enough for us to write down and genuinely explanatory. I don't mean we'll reach it soon or that we could ever test it at the energies it might require; I mean the world is actually like that. I'm trying to find out how exposed this view is. What would have to be true for it to be wrong? I can list the obvious skeptical stuff myself: every previous "bottom" turned out to be an approximation to something deeper, and the Standard Model has a bunch of free parameters nobody has yet derived from anything. But those feel like arguments from our track record, not from how the world could actually be. What I want is a picture of the live alternatives: that physics is an endless tower of effective theories, that there is a bottom but it's computationally irreducible so no short theory compressess it, that the final theory is a landscape which doesn't pick out our world, or that the bottom is a disunified list of constants nobody would call a theory. Which of these would actually count against me, and which are merely logical possibilities? What does my view predict that those alternatives don't, and vice versa? And separately: even if a final theory exists, is there any way we could ever know we'd found it rather than just run out of money and accelerator? Please rank the problems from "this should genuinely move me" to "this is just awkward for the view," and say whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that matters for a claim of this kind.
