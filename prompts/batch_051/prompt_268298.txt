Construct a thought experiment in which a single person comes out as self-deceived on a partition account but not on a deflationary account. Take the partition account to say that self-deception requires simultaneously holding two incompatible representations, one kept from view; take the deflationary account to say that it requires only a motivationally biased process, such as selective attention or biased hypothesis testing, that produces a false belief the person wholeheartedly holds. Constraints: the case must be mundane (a workplace, a family, or a friendship), the state must be generated by ordinary mechanisms rather than stipulated, and the incompatibility must be established by your argument about what explains the person's behavior, not assumed up front. Work through what each account says about the case and why, identify the single structural feature that makes the accounts diverge, and say what would happen if that feature were removed. Then give a deflationist's strongest objection to your construction and the consideration that would most likely change your mind about which account handles the case better. Answer in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) the partition account's verdict and reasoning; (3) the deflationary account's verdict and reasoning; (4) the feature doing the work and what happens if it is removed; (5) the strongest objection and what would change your mind.
