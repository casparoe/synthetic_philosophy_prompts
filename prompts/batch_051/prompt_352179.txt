Last night a friend and I went around and around about respect—whether what we owe each other is only a pattern of treatment or also an attitude, a way of seeing the other person. I was on the attitude side: you can be perfectly polite to someone while writing them off internally, and being written off matters even if it never changes a single action. She came back with an argument I cant get out of, and the worst part is it seems to prove way more than even she wanted. I want to know where it collapses.

Her argument, numbered as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) If I owe someone respect as an attitude—actually taking their concerns as reasons—then I have to be able to have that attitude; otherwise the demand would be noise.

(2) But I can't. Whether someone's complaint strikes me as worth taking seriously isn't up to me; it happens, the way a belief does. I can't decide to find an argument weighty any more than I can decide to believe it's raining.

(3) If I can't do something, I can't be obligated to do it.

(4) So I can't owe anyone respect as an attitude. At most I can owe respectful outward behavior.

But the same steps seem to keep going:

(5) I also can't decide to keep a promise out of fidelity, or to help out of genuine concern rather than vanity or embarrassment. Motives, like beliefs about what's weighty, arise or they don't.

(6) So by (3), I can't be obligated to keep promises out of fidelity or help out of concern—only to produce the matching outward acts.

(7) That can't be right. If you keep a promise only to look reliable to an audience, the way you kept it is itself a moral failure, not just a character flaw attached to a correctly done act.

So the argument seems to show no one ever owes anyone anything on the inside, period. That's wildly more than she wanted to establish. I'd most like to keep (1) and (7); I realize that means something else in the chain has to go, but I don't know which piece is actually doing the damage, or what I'd be paying to drop it. Where does the argument go wrong—or, if it doesn't, which premise is least costly to give up?
