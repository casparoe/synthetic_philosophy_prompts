Sidgwick argues in The Methods of Ethics that the bare fact that a future person's good lies further away in time cannot reduce its value from a universal point of view: the mere difference between now and later does not, by itself, justify preferring the nearer good. But if that is right, how can climate economists legitimately use a positive discount rate when weighing damages to people in 2100 against mitigation costs today? Shouldn't the discount rate simply be zero, with a life in 2100 counting morally exactly as much as a life today? I can understand why individuals might care less about temporally distant harms, but isn't the whole point of ethical analysis to correct for that sort of parochial bias?
