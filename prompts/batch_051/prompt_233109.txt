Here is an argument I have been turning over, and I cannot tell whether it is too quick. Imagine the careenium: a wide, shallow dish packed with thousands of tiny self-propelled simmballs, each one following only a local rule for adjusting its speed and direction in response to the simmballs around it. Seen from above, the dish settles into a large, slowly rotating circulation pattern. Now consider:

(1) The circulation pattern is wholly constituted by the simmballs and their local interactions; it is nothing over and above them.
(2) If a higher-level pattern is nothing over and above its constituents, then it cannot make any constituent move otherwise than the constituents' own local interactions already determine.
(3) So the circulation pattern never pushes a simmball around.
(4) Words, meanings, and other symbolic contents stand to their neural or electronic substrate as the circulation pattern stands to the simmballs.
(5) So words and meanings never push a neuron or a transistor around.

Is the argument valid? If not, say exactly which step fails, and if the failure is an ambiguity, say which reading of the ambiguous term makes the premise true and whether the argument still goes through on that reading. If the argument is valid, is the conclusion a serious deflationary result, or only a ban on a confused picture of an extra shove?
