I'm a journalist covering courts and regulation for a science-and-ideas section, and I keep slamming into the same move in the comments. Whenever I write about someone getting hit with a strict-liability penalty — say, a corner store sold recalled peanut butter without knowing it was recalled, or a trucker failed an inspection because of a cargo strap he had no reason to think was defective — someone will reply with some version of: ignorance of the law is no excuse, you cant just say you didn't read the regulations. But thats exactly the weird thing about strict liability: the accused can be completely reasonable, can have done everything a careful person would do, and the law still treats them as guilty because the usual mens rea requirement is dropped. So I want to know what that slogan is actually claiming when people deploy it in that context. Give me exactly five distinct precise theses the slogan could be expressing, from the trivially true to the genuinely controversial. For each, in a sentence or two, say whether the thesis is defensible and whether it actually supports fining the store or the trucker in my examples. No introduction, no conclusion — just the numbered list.
