I cover physics and data science, and lately I've been turning over a parallel I can't settle. In Bayesian statistics the preference for simpler models isn't a matter of taste — it falls out of the math: a flexible model spreads its credibility over too many possible outcomes, so it's penalized for what it could have predicted. Physicists also prefer constrained theories, but they talk about it as elegance: symmetry, unification, equations with a built-in inevitability. I keep wanting to say these are the same move in different clothes, and that 'elegance' is just Bayesian simplicity after physics grad school.

The rough edge: epicycles were exactly the kind of thing a Platonist would call beautiful (perfect circles), and Kepler's ellipses were seen as a bit ugly, yet the ellipse picture was the simpler, constrained theory. If elegance and Bayesian simplicity are the same thing, why did the genuinely simpler theory look less elegant? I can't tell if that's a counterexample or a confusion between elegance and familiarity.

Is the parallel deep or superficial? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, on where Bayesian simplicity and the physicists elegance are the same and where they come apart, and whether the epicycle/ellipse case breaks the analogy.
