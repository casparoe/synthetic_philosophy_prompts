I keep ending up in arguments about whether the Trojan War, King Arthur, or the Exodus "actually happened," and I've noticed nobody ever moves. I'm trying to figure out whether this is a normal evidence dispute that just happens to be underdetermined, or whether it's stuck because it's really several different disagreements wearing one coat. On the ground, the moves are familiar: one side says there's a destroyed Bronze Age city on the right site and Hittite names that sound like the right people, the other says a burned layer and a few names don't get you to a ten-year seige over a stolen queen. For Arthur there's no contemporary source at all, but a continuous tradition of retellings. For the Exodus, the text describes a massive movement of people, and the Egyptian record, as far as I know, has nothing that matches it. The sides also differ about burden of proof: the text-first side treats the narrative as a source that gets the benefit of the doubt, the other treats it as a later construction until independent evidence shows otherwise. My hypothesis is that "it happened" quietly shifts between four claims: a named person existed; the central events happened much as described; the story preserves a garbled memory of some smaller real episode or process; and the story is true to the community that told it, so "did it happen" is the wrong question. I'm not asking for a verdict on any of the three. I want a diagnosis of the impasse: which of those claims, if any, is a real historical disagreement that ordinary evidence could move, and which are just different questions being run together? Use whichever one of the three cases makes the diagnosis sharpest, say what each side actually says in that case, and tell me what, under your diagnosis, would actually count as progress.
