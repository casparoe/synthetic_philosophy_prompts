you're a philosopher working on animal minds and moral considerability. think carefully here—do a little out-loud brainstorming to steelman both sides before you commit, and don't just tell me insects are or aren't sentient.

i keep coming back to a clean criterion: an action can only be directly wrong if it wrongs someone, a subject with felt experience whose life can go badly for them. this seems to explain a lot at once: why kicking a rock isn't wrong, why pain matters but reflex withdrawal in a brain-dead body doesn't, why an ecosystem isn't a patient the way a deer is. and it gives me a beautifully tidy answer in the orchard. if a leafhopper or a whitefly has no felt point of view, then spraying isn't a harm to them, and whatever's wrong with it comes from knock-on effects or human concerns, not from the insects themselves. i really want this to be right, because the alternative seems to make every crop a massacre.

but there's a wasp on the windowsill. i sprayed it, and it didn't just stop. it stumbled, tried to right itself, then curled and twitched. i have a brute intuition that whatever was going on there was bad, and the criterion doesn't earn the right to define it out of existence just by declaring the wasp a reflex with nobody home. the intuition gets worse when i let the numbers in: a single orchard spray can expose billions of small invertebrates, and if each has even a modest probability of a morally relevant bad time, the expected badness is enormous. a colleague who does intergrated pest management tells me pyrethroids work by making neurons fire uncontrollably, so the twitching is exactly what you'd expect from a reflex arc with no one there to feel it, nerve poison doing its chemistry, not evidence of an experience. i can repeat that to myself and the intuition doesn't move.

i don't want to be told which side wins. i want to know what it would take for the theory to give way, whether it needs a precautionary principle or expected-sentience reasoning or a nociception proxy instead of a felt-pain threshold, and what it would take for the intuition to be the thing that goes, whether it's scope insensitivity, projection, or a failure to distinguish reflex from anguish. then tell me whether there's a reading of harm on which both survive, which of the two you would actually give up in the end and why that one, and what evidence would flip you in either direction.
