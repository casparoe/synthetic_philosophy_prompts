I teach early modern philosophy, and I keep turning over a parallel I cant decide about. In machine learning, an adversarial example is an input engineered by someone who knows a network's internals to be indistinguishable from a normal one, and the network misclassifies it with high confidence. That looks like a scrubbed-up evil demon: the deceiver doesn't get to rewrite reality, only to control the input channel, and it tailors the false experience to your cognitive wiring so the error is invisible from inside. Descartes's response was to red-team his beliefs against a few scenarios—dream, madness, demon—and keep only what survives all of them. Is the method of doubt just adversarial training on a tiny training set? If it is, the skeptic's old worry (why trust this shortlist—there might be a demon you didn't imagine) becomes the ML fact that robustness against the attacks you trained on is not robustness against the ones you didn't. But I suspect the analogy breaks where it matters: the demon can rewrite my whole stream of experience, while an adversarial example changes a single input; and a classifier has ground-truth labels to train against, while a believer has no outside check. I want you to take the parallel seriously: say whether it is deep or superficial, where the mapping holds and where it breaks, what would have to be true for an adversarial example to count as a genuinely skeptical possibility for a human inquirer, and name something the parallel predicts that turns out false.
