Last night a friend and I argued for several hours about whether the left–right distinction in politics is real. One of us defended the view that the distinction tracks a genuine, if messy, underlying structure in political life; the other defended the view that it is a historically contingent shorthand, held together by inherited labels, party systems, and identity rather than by any single axis that explains the clustering. We ended with the sense that we had been talking past one another, and we would like to salvage the disagreement through an adversarial collaboration. Please draft the joint statement that such a collaboration would produce, between these two named positions:

Structuralism about left and right: the distinction is anchored in a recurring political conflict, roughly over hierarchy versus equality, and that conflict gives the axis enough continuity across issue change, enough cross-national recurrence, and enough predictive power to count as a real feature of politics.

Historicist nominalism about left and right: the distinction is a contingent coalition-marker descended from the seating arrangements of the French Revolution, continually renegotiated, and lacking any stable underlying dimension; the correlations we observe are produced by institutions, party competition, and social identity, not by a pre-political axis.

The document should have four sections. First, a numbered list of substantive claims both camps can sign: about the labels' origin and persistence, their rhetorical and social-symbolic power, the cross-issue clustering found in contemporary democracies, and the shared rejection of any claim that the categories are eternal, biologically fixed, or internally homogeneous. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the structuralist's wording, the nominalist's wording, and a column marking whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal; each side's wording should be one its advocates would recognize as accurate rather than a caricature. Third, a section on cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. These should include whether left-right-like polarization would emerge in a political community with no inherited labels; whether hierarchy-versus-equality and openness-versus-closure dimensions recur in cultures without a Western left-right history; whether issue correlations would persist if the labels were stripped away; and whether a single axis can be traced through the historical replacement of issues, or only a chain of verbally linked but different axes. Fourth, for each crux, specify what both camps would count as probative evidence and record each side's prediction in advance. Throughout, separate disagreements about the facts from disagreements about what would license calling the distinction real, and if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say exactly which part and why.
