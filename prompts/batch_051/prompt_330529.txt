After thirty-odd years in physical chemistry I've started spending retirement on the metaphysics I used to roll my eyes at, and the statue/lump case has me stuck. In my reading group the standard line is that a clay statue and the lump of clay it's made from are two distinct objects even though they share all their matter, and the person running the session kept cutting me of with "constitution is not identity" whenever I said same atoms, same arrangement, same thing. I can tell that slogan is meant to do real work, but I can't pin down which precise claim it's making. It could be the boring verbal point that "constitutes" and "is identical with" don't mean the same thing; it could be the dramatic claim that two material objects can exactly coincide in space and time; or it could be something in between. Could you lay out the distinct readings of "constitution is not identity," flag which are merely verbal, which are substantive and likely true, and which are outright false, and then say which reading actually supports the two-objects verdict my reading group keeps landing on? And if the slogan only gets you the verbal point and not the two-objects conclusion, say that plainly.
