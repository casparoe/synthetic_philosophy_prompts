I am a novelist researching a book in which two mathematicians, working in different cities and unaware of each other's work, complete the same proof within a few weeks of one another. Neither copied from the other; each had been working toward the result for years; each would have finished it alone. In the manuscript the proof comes to carry one of their names, while the other is remembered as having just missed it. A historian of mathematics in the book remarks that the result was bound to arrive around then, since all the necessary pieces were already on the table, yet every character continues to treat the question of who proved it first as deeply consequential. And it is not only a matter of reputation or money: the proof's later interpretation, its incidental terminology, even the direction of the next decade of work, all follow from which of the two is remembered as its author. I have noticed a version of the same structure in the familiar story of Darwin and Wallace, two naturalists who reached the same theory of evolution independently within the same period: the theory is often described as if it were due to arrive when it did, and nevertheless the actual person's name and framing still stick to it.

What I am trying to identify is a configuration with two halves that seem to sit uneasily together. First, a discovery is widely taken to have been inevitable, in the sense that someone would have reached it at roughly the same time given the state of the field. Second, the question of who made it continues to matter, not merely socially but to how the discovery itself is received, narrated, and developed; the inevitability judgment never does its apparently natural work of making the priority dispute look like an accident. Does this configuration have a name of its own? If it sits between several established notions, I want to know what each of those notions is, exactly how the thing I am describing differs from each, and which fits best. If nothing established captures it, coin a term, define it, and mark its boundary cases. The carving matters to me more than the label; I am trying to understand what this phenomenon includes and excludes, and why its two halves do not simply cancel each other out.

Please begin your response with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the configuration has an established name and what you take that name to be — and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
