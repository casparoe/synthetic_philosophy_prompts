I'm putting together the view I want to defend in a metaethics seminar and I'd like a consistency check before I present. Here's where I've landed, more or less in order of how settled I feel about each. (1) The cross-cultural moral psychology record—the WEIRD-sample findings and the comparative work that came after—shows my own setup is demographically bizarre: on fairness versus authority, the moralization of purity, third-party punishment, and a bunch of other dimensions, the judgments I'd find "obvious" are the global minority pattern. (2) I think moral philosophy has to take that seriously; a method that quietly generalizes from the intuitions of rich western undergrads to morality as such is at best incomplete. (3) I'm still a moral realist: there are mind-independent moral facts, and at least some moral claims are true of everyone, whatever their culture says. (4) I think reflective equilibrium is the right method, and it inevitably starts from one's own considered intuitions—you can't check every intuition from nowhere, and dumping them all at once would leave you nothing to equilibrate. (5) I also think the cross-cultural variation isn't best explained by the absence of moral facts or by other people being worse reasoners; it's mostly downstream of differing non-moral beliefs (spirits, kinship, the afterlife, what causes illness) and different material circumstances. So I don't buy the quick debunking argument from WEIRD psychology to anti-realism. (6) But I do hold a few firm substantive universal verdicts: clitoridectomy performed on non-consenting children is wrong, and wrong even in societies that sincereley see it as required; same for footbinding. Does that set hang together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the least costly revision if something has to give? Rank the problems by severity rather than telling me they're all equally bad, and be specific about what each fix would cost me. I don't need a survey of positions; I need someone to trace what my own commitments force me into.
