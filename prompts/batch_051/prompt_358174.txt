My city's municipal code says: "The power to arrest, detain, or use physical force to enforce any law or ordinance is vested solely in the sworn officers of the city police department." Last year the council voted to defund several police divisions and stood up an unarmed civilian Crisis Response Team to take mental-health calls instead. A worker on that team recently used a standard two-person hold — not to arrest someone or hand them to police, just to stop a person in acute psychosis from walking into a busy road. The team carries no weapons and has no law-enforcement powers. Does the code section reach that worker? I'm asking less about what a court would actually do than about the interpretive question underneath: is that hold "physical force to enforce any law or ordinance"? Does the clause's point — the state's monopoly on legitimate force — apply anyway, even though the council itself deliberately routed crisis work away from sworn officers? It feels like the defund/reform debate in miniature. Walk me through the text, the purpose, and what the drafters would probably have said if asked, and say which should win here and why. And please be careful to separate what's fairly settled from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
