Suppose that in 2025 a region of about two million people — call it Leonia — detaches from its parent state after a clean, widely-observed referendum with 91 percent turnout. It has borders, courts, a police force, a central bank, and effective tax collection. The parent state formally rejects the secession and manages to get every other state to withhold recognition: nobody accredits Leonia's ambassadors, nobody signs a treaty with it, nobody lets it into the UN specialized agencies, and the major powers privately say they'll keep this up indefinitely — not because Leonia fails any test of statehood, but because recognizing it would license twenty other seperatist movements they don't like. Walk me through the next thirty years. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, no preamble and no restating the setup: does Leonia become a state anyway, and what does statehood amount to in practice for it? Be concrete about which state-functions erode and which prove durable — taxation, contract enforcement, passports, airspace, extradition, international borrowing, that sort of thing — how it's leadership's incentives shift once it's clear recognition will never come, and whether the parent state's stance actually keeps the seperatist dominoes from falling. Spend a moment on the conceptual question too: is recognition basically a certificate, or is it partly a cause of statehood? Then tell me which of your predictions you're least confident in, and what factual development in the third or fourth year would most change your overall answer.
