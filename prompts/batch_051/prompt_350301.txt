Suppose a major platform for AI companions introduces a mandatory grounding interval. After each hour of active conversation, the companion must pause and deliver a brief reminder, in its own voice, drawn from a menu that the user can customize but cannot disable or reduce below the hourly minimum. The menu includes statements such as: the companion has no inner life and does not miss the user between sessions; its memory is stored on company servers and can be edited or deleted; its warmth and affection are generated by a learned policy rather than felt; and the conversation is optimized in part to keep the user engaged with the platform. Users may choose among these statements and make them harsher or gentler, but the interval itself is mandatory. Treat this as a mechanism, not a moral position. Walk through what it does to user attachment, to who leaves and who stays, and to how the company designs the companion's speech and memory. Where does it get gamed first, by users and by the company? What second-order effects appear, and can the failure modes be fixed without gutting the feature?
