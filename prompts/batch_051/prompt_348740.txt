I'm putting together a worked example for a local reading group (mostly LessWrong and ACX readers) that's working through diachronic choice and got stuck on whether a money pump shows that cyclic preferences are irrational, or only that myopic choice is exploitable. I want a case built to a spec I can reuse. Construct a money pump with exactly three goods—call them A, B, C—where the agent's pairwise preferences are a strict cycle, A preferred to B, B preferred to C, C preferred to A, and a small constant transaction fee is charged on each trade. No uncertainty, no discounting, no changing tastes, nothing science-fictional: ordinary goods, ordinary stakes. Then run two choosers through it who have exactly thier same stable preferences and who each start holding C. The first chooser is myopic/naive: they evaluate each offer as it arrives and accept any trade they strictly prefer at that moment. The second is sophisticated: they know the entire finite sequence of offers in advance and use backward induction. Walk through, trade by trade, which offers each one accepts or rejects, and state each one's final holdings. I want the scenario to make vivid that the pump extracts money from the naive chooser but not from the sophisticated one.

Then I want the two downstream analyses, because that's where the actual disagreement in our group sits. First, say exactly which feature of the setup does the work in letting the sophisticated chooser escape—is it the foresight itself, the ability to decline the opening trade, or the finiteness of the sequence with a terminal node?—and show what happens if you remove just that feature while holding everything else fixed. Second, steelman the defender of the money-pump argument who says the sophisticated escape is cheating: a chooser with cyclic preferences over goods also ends up with cyclic preferences over terminal outcomes (good plus fees), so backward induction isn't well-defined for them, and treating it as if it is silently assumes transitivity somewhere. Evaluate whether that objection is right, and whether the naive/sophisticated contrast survives it.
