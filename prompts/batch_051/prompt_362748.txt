I co-run a small media-literacy account, and we've been arguing for months about how to handle propaganda when we debunk it: do we embed the actual manipulated clip or the fake screenshot, or just describe it and archive it? One team member always ends the argument with "sunlight is the best disinfectant," and I've realized I don't know what precise claim that sentence is doing in this context anymore. Sometimes he seems to mean showing people the lie now inoculates them against the real thing later; sometimes he means hiding it makes it look forbidden and more credible; sometimes he means our readers have a right to inspect the primary artifact; sometimes it's a grander claim about an open information environment always correcting itself. But we also know that repeating a false claim, even to debunk it, can make it stick better, and that once we post the clip we don't control where it travels.

I want to bring something sharper then vibes to the next meeting. Can you lay out the different precise theses "sunlight is the best disinfectant" could express in this debate, mark which are near-tautologies, which are substantive psychological or empirical claims about how people process misinformation, and which are probably false or overstated, and then say which readings would actually support reproducing the propaganda for critique and which would support describing it instead? A menu-style answer would be ideal, since I suspect different team members are endorsing different readings and we're mostly talking past each other.
