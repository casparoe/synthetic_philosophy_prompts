I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether virtual objects are real and when virtual harms are genuine harms, going by my gut on cases. Here are mine:

- When a guildmate cleaned out our guild bank in an MMO and then deleted his account, I thought he'd really stolen from us, even though the items couldn't be sold for real money and only mattered inside the game.
- When a friend deleted my years-old single-player Minecraft world as a joke, I was more upset than I'd have been about losing my bike; I'd put hundreds of hours into it. That felt like a real harm, and the builds felt real in whatever sense matters.
- When a stranger corpse-camped me in a PvP zone for an hour and cost me a bunch of in-game currency, I didn't think he'd wronged me. The game allows it, and I'd opted in.
- When someone scammed me out of a tradable Steam inventory skin worth about $200, I thought that was outright theft, and I'd say the item itself was my property, not just the money.
- When the company shut down an online game I'd played for years and deleted everything, I felt a real loss but not like I'd been wronged. The stuff is gone, but I don't think they stole from me.
- When someone in a social VR app pressed their avatar against mine and made grabbing motions, I felt violated and I'd say a real harm happened, even though my body was never touched.

I'm tempted by a principle that says virtual objects are real or property only when they're tied to real-world cash value or enforceable obligations, but it doesn't fit the Minecraft world or the guild items. Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: someone uses my stream to find and kill my hardcore permadeath character, costing me a hundred hours of progress, through mechanics the game allows? And which of my verdicts does the best principle force me to drop?
