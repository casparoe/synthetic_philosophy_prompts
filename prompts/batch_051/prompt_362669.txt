I cant stop seeing a parallel and I want someone to tell me if I'm onto something or just pattern-matching.

The Euthyphro dilemma: either an act is right because God commands it, or God commands it because the act is right. First horn makes rightness arbitrary — God could command anything and make it right. Second horn makes the command idle, just a barometer for a standard that does the real work. The standard theist escape is to distinguish God's nature from God's commands: the commands flow from a necessarily good nature, so nothing arbitrary, and the commands aren't idle because they're how the standard gets to us.

Now the other side, which I only half-remember from linguistics. There's an argument about whether a sentence is grammatical because native speakers of a language accept it, or whether they accept it because it's grammatical. First option: the communitys usage is what constitutes well-formedness; whatever gets entrenched in usage is by definition grammatical. Second option: there's a fact of the matter about the grammar — a speaker's internal grammar, or the language's — and speaker judgments are just noisy evidence for that fact. And the escape route looks suspiciously similar: linguists distinguish the speaker's internal grammar from the judgments speakers give when you ask them whether a sentence sounds okay, where the grammar does the grounding and the judgments are a performance-y window onto it.

So the mapping would be: God to the speech community, right to grammatical, God commands X to speakers accepting or using X, God's nature to internal grammar, and God's commands to acceptability judgments.

I can see the obvious surface disanalogy — one is a single perfect agent and a moral ought, while the other is a distributed mess and a descriptive is. But the structure, including the standard escape route, feels identical to me. So: is this a real structural identity or a loose analogy? If it's real, what would have to be true of both sides for results to transfer from one debate to the other, and what actually does transfer? Give me at least one thing the parallel would predict that you think turns out false when you look at either the linguistics or the theology. And skip the lazy "it's a false dilemma in both cases" exit.
