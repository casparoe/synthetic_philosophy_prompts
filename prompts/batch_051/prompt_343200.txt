I keep running into that old one-liner about whether God could make a stone so heavy he couldn't lift it, and its always used to shut down any talk of omnipotence being coherent. But it seems like the phrase is doing a lot of different things at once, and people rarely say exactly which claim they think it establishes. I want you to lay out the distinct precise claims the stone question could be making — for example, about whether there's a task no possible being could do, about whether omnipotence requires being able to do impossible things, about whether the ability has to survive after the stone is created, about what the weights and lifting conditions even are, about whether the problem is in the object or in the concept of unlimited power. For each reading: is it a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or actually false? Then tell me which reading has to be true if the one-liner is supposed to show omnipotence is self-contradictory, and which readings a defender of classical omnipotence — roughly, can do anything metaphysically possible — could accept without any real trouble. I'm writing something on divine attributes and want to move past the version of this that gets repeated as a gotcha, so I need a menu with verdicts, not just a single argument for one side.
