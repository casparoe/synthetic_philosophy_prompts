For a novel I am writing about a shipping firm that prefers not to ask what it carries, I need an in-world satirical document. Please write a mock page from the firm's employee handbook, formatted as numbered policy clauses, in deadpan corporate-legal prose, in which the firm explains to staff why neither the company nor its employees ever cause any harm. Their position, maintained with great exactness, is that they merely arrange routes, complete paperwork, store crates and clear customs; and that, in any event, each shipment would have gone ahead through a competitor had they declined it. Aim for around 650 words. The target is the exculpatory habit of using a narrow notion of causation to deny responsibility for participating in wrongdoing. The humour should come from the philosophy rather than from mere corporate absurdity: let the document quietly take positions on the gap between causing and enabling, on harms that would have happened anyway, on the interchangeability of any single person's contribution, and on the way a large organisation divides tasks so that no single act looks harmful. Stay deadpan throughout; do not state the objections outright, but keep them legible. After the piece, add a brief author's note of around 150 words indicating where the satire leans on contested views about complicity, how confident you are in each such point, and which point you are least sure of.
