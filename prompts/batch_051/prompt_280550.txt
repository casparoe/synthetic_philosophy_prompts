I've been going back and forth with my flatmate about whether "possible" and "probability greater than zero" are basically the same thing. It came up because she's been trying to get me to buy travel insurance and keeps saying that if something is possible I can't just write it off as having no chance, whereas I want to say some things are possible but so unlikely that treating them as zero is the sane move. I tried writing the disagreement out as a numbered argument to see where it goes wrong, but now I can't tell whether it does.

(1) If an outcome is genuinely possible, a rational person should assign it some positive probability, not zero.
(2) Consider an idealised dart thrown at a perfectly continuous circular board. Landing exactly on the board's geometrical centre is a possible outcome.
(3) So a rational person should assign positive probability to the dart landing exactly on the centre.
(4) But the centre is just one of infinitely many exact points on the board, and by symmetry each exact point is as likely as any other.
(5) If each of infinitely many points got a positive probability, the total would end up bigger than one; so each exact point must actually have probability zero.
(6) Therefore a rational person should assign zero probability to the dart landing exactly on the centre.

Steps (3) and (6) can't both be right, but each of them followed from things that sounded right to me. Is the argument valid? If not, what is the earliest step that fails, and exactly why? If it is valid, which of the plausible-sounding claims should I give up, and what should I conclude about how possibility and probablity relate? Please answer in British English.
