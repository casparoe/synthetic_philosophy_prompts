I have a policy, picked up in something close to its present form from years of reading rationalist blogs and forums, that I have never managed to state precisely: I never allow a way of forming beliefs to supply the evidence for its own reliability. Before I trust a source, I want some independent line of evidence that already vouches for it, however approximately. This feels like the most elementary lesson about circular justification. Consider a fuel gauge in an unfamiliar car. The gauge reads full. It seems I can then reason as follows: since the gauge reads full, the tank is full; since the tank is full while the gauge reads full, the gauge read correctly on this occasion; and by accumulating enough such occasions I obtain a track record that certifies the gauge. I regard this move as epistemically vicious. The track record that purported to certify the gauge was itself produced entirely by the gauge, so the whole exercise never makes contact with anything independent of the source it is supposed to check.

The problem is that this policy appears to have unacceptable consequences when applied to my own perceptual faculties. Standing in front of a wall that looks red, I want to say that I know the wall is red. I also accept that knowledge is closed under known entailment: if I know a proposition, and I know it entails another, then I can come to know the entailed proposition. But the wall's being red entails that it is not white with red light shining on it. By closure, then, an ordinary look lets me come to know that a certain kind of visual error is not occurring — which, as far as structure is concerned, is my visual faculty ruling out the very scenario in which it misleads me. A philosopher friend who enjoys reductio arguments tells me the policy must therefore be abandoned: either it forbids a pattern I cannot independently identify, or it leads straight to skepticism about perception. I find the fuel-gauge reasoning genuinely bad, and I find his conclusion genuinely bad as well.

I want to know whether this policy can be stated so that it condemns the fuel-gauge bootstrapping while still permitting ordinary perceptual knowledge together with closure under known entailment. If it can be, what is the best statement of it? If it cannot, what should replace it, and is the fuel-gauge inference actually acceptable after all? I am especially interested in whether the right diagnosis is to reject closure, to deny that the fuel-gauge inference and the red-wall inference are structurally the same, or to accept the apparent easy knowledge and explain why it is not a problem.
