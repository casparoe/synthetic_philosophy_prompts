I'm a retired chemist who got into political philosophy a few years ago, and my reading group keeps stalling on Rawls on religion. We can't agree whether the later proviso—religious reasons can come into public political discussion as long as proper political values are supplied in due course—is a big concession or no concession at all. One guy says it's just a translation requirement in disguise; another says once you let religious reasons in the door you've already conceded that there's no special duty of restraint at all. I want to settle it with a handout that sorts people by what they'd say about cases, not by which label they prefer.

So: write me a decision tree, about a page and a half when typed out, that takes someone from a first question to a named position about the proper role of religious reasons in public political justification. The leaves must include at least:

(a) Rawlsian wide view (the proviso as stated: religious reasons allowed in public political culture if, in due course, proper political reasons are also given);
(b) strict secular exclusion (religious reasons have no place in any genuinely political advocacy);
(c) translation view (religious speech is fine in the public sphere, but official decisions and official justifications must be rendered in secular terms);
(d) no-restraint view (citizens may advocate solely on the basis of religious reasons);
(e) officials-only restraint (citizens are free; officeholders and candidates have the duty).

Each branch node has to be a concrete diagnostic question a non-philosopher could answer without knowing the labels—about a town council meeting, a candidate's stump speech, a judge's opinion, a ballot initiative argued from scripture, that sort of thing. Build the tree from the logical space first: pick the questions that do the most work (e.g., does the duty attach to citizens or only to officeholders? must the religious reason be paired with a political reason on each occasion or only 'in due course'? does the political reason have to be the one the person actually acted on? does the restriction cover the background culture or only official forums?). Then locate the leaves in that space. If some leaf ends up empty, label it 'unnamed' and say in one sentence whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Finish by flagging any pair of leaves the tree can't actually seperate, and say what further question would split them if anything would.

I know this is a lot for a handout; the sorting is the point, not elegance. And please keep the whole answer in American English.
