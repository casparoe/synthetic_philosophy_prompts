In a debate about the ethics of using IVF with preimplantation genetic testing to select embryos, someone ended the argument by saying that parents should give their children the best possible start in life, as though that obviously settled the question in favor of selecting the embryo with the best projected health. I suspect this slogan is doing more work than it can bear. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims it might be making — what counts as the best start, best compared with which alternative, best for whom, and what force the should has — and say for each reading whether it is trivial, plausible, or false, and whether it actually supports embryo selection, or only weaker duties like ordinary parental care, or stronger conclusions like routine genetic enhancement? Present the readings as a menu rather than a single interpretation.
