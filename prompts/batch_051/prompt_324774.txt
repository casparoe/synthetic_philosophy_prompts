I've been noticing a kind of sentence that seems to do something different from stating, asking, or instructing, and I don't have a good word for it. If I say "that's beside the point" in an argument, I'm not exactly reporting a fact about the world—it's more like I've changed what counts as on-topic, and the other person usually just accepts it unless they explicitly push back. Same with "we've already been over this" or "grant me this one assumption and I'll drop everything else." These feel related to promises, bets, and declarations, where saying the words just does the thing, but they're also not those: they don't commit me to a future act or change any official status, they adjust the rules of the conversation itself—what's relevant, what's settled, whose turn it is, what has to be defended. I know "performative" is somewhere in the general vicinity, but it's too broad and I suspect it covers a lot of unrelated terrain. Is there an established name for exactly this, the class of moves that don't add content so much as they steer the discussion? And if the closest concepts only partialy fit, which fit best and where do they fall short? I'd rather have the edges and a couple of boundary cases than just a one-word label.
