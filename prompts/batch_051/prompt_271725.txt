I'm curating a small anthology of philosophical satire for a reading group of philosophers and finance-curious undergraduates, and the next piece is to be on Pascal's wager. Please write a deadpan parody of a private placement memorandum (the offering document a hedge fund circulates to prospective investors), issued by a fictional firm called Pascal Capital Partners, which sells shares in a product whose return just is the wager: an infinite payoff, payable after death, conditional on the existence of a god of the required kind, in exchange for a modest but non-zero subscription in the form of belief, devotion, and lifestyle compliance. The target of the satire should be the wager itself as a piece of decision-theoretic salesmanship — the practice of treating belief as a portfolio allocation to be optimized — rather than religious belief as such. The document should run about 700 words, should be laid out as numbered sections (for example, Summary of the Offering, Investment Thesis, Use of Proceeds, Risk Factors, Subscription and Redemption Terms), and should sustain a dry compliance register throughout without winking.

The comedy should come from getting the decision theory and the theology right rather than from cheap shots. In the Investment Thesis, reproduce the wager's dominance and expected-value logic faithfully: an infinite payoff means that any positive probability of the proposition overwhelms all finite earthly costs. The Risk Factors should then quietly bury that same logic under the standard difficulties, without ever naming them: rival issuers whose mutually exclusive products promise competing infinite payoffs; the point that, once infinite expected value is on the table, no finite subscription cost can actually be justified or bounded rather than any nearby cost; the obscurity of the probability assignment, including the investor who sees no basis for assigning any positive probability at all; the fact that genuine faith may not be deliverable on demand as a contractual obligation; the possibility that the relevant deity rewards honest unbelief rather than a calculated purchase; and the uneasy sense that the product has turned religion into a position sizing. Each of these should be presented as a lawyerly "risk factor" that is acknowledged and then deftly waived, so the irony stays legible to a reader who has never encountered the wager while a specialist recognizes that the piece understands what it is sending up.

After the piece and clearly separated from it, provide a brief structured summary as a single JSON object with exactly these fields: title (string, the offering's title), apparent_genre (string, the genre being parodied), satirized_commitments (array of strings, each a plain-language statement of one commitment of Pascal's wager that the piece exaggerates or examines), objections_landed (array of objects, each with objection (string, a one-sentence plain statement of a standard objection) and location (string, the section or clause of the piece that carries it)), target_verdict (string, one or two sentences on which part of the wager's reasoning the piece treats as defensible, if any, and which it treats as absurd), and word_count (number, the word count of the piece alone). Keep the JSON under 250 words and include every field.
