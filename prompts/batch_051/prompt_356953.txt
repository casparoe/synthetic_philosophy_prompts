Not sure if this is philosophy proper or just a thing that keeps happening in my reading group, but I keep ending up in arguments where the other person shifts from one question to a different one, and I can't tell whether the second question has a standard name. The first question is why there's anything it's like to be me — why the taste of coffee, say, isn't just a physical event happening in the dark. The second question is why we're so convinced that the first question is deep and unsolved: why the sheer felt weirdness of being a body that experiences things seems like evidence for a mystery rather than just a fact about human psychology. For instance, a friend of mine will shrug and say 'sure, but your conviction that this is mysterious is itself just another thing your brain does, and neuroscience should eventually explain why you keep having that conviction.' He never claims to have explained experience itself; hes pointing at a separate phenomenon.

So is there an established concept for that separate phenomenon: explaining how and why the intuition of an unbridgeable gap between physical processes and subjective feel arises and stays so compelling, as opposed to actually closing the gap? If I had to describe it I'd call it a second-order problem about our reaction to the first one. I get that this is different from the view that consciousness itself is an illusion, but I also get why it gets lumped in with that, and I want a way of carving it apart. Does it have a name of its own, or is it just ordinary psychology of belief? And if it doesn't have a name, what exactly would make it a distinct problem rather than a dodge?
