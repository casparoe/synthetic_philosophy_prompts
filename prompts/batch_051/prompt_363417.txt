After a year of argument, two political theorists in my department — call them the substantivist and the coalitionist — have agreed to produce an adversarial-collaboration document on whether the left–right distinction is real. The substantivist holds that left and right name a genuine, recurrent axis of political conflict with a substantive core: a stable opposition between equality-oriented and hierarchy-oriented (or change-oriented and order-oriented) political sensibilities, one that explains why parties, voters, and policy disputes line up along a single dimension even as the specific issues change. The coalitionist holds that left and right are path-dependent alliance labels whose content is supplied entirely by the historical coalitions that happen to bear them; on this view there is no shared substantive core across time and place, and asking what left and right really are reifies something that is only a useful shorthand. Please write the statement they could both sign.

The document should have the following parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: what any account of left and right must explain, what is plainly not universal, what the distinction actually does in ordinary political discourse, and the points about its revolutionary-era origin and later mutations that both accept. These claims should be substantive enough that signing them costs each side something. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the exact question; the substantivist's answer phrased so a substantivist would own it; the coalitionist's answer phrased so a coalitionist would own it; and a sentence on why this disagreement is not merely verbal. Third, a cruxes section identifying at least four sub-questions such that a settled answer would materially weaken at least one side, each with the two sides' predictions written down in advance and a concrete body of evidence or case both agree bears on it. Use material such as post-communist party systems, the American New Deal realignment and subsequent culture-war sorting, the recurrence of something like a left–right axis in countries with very different histories, or survey data in which self-reported left–right placement comes apart from policy preferences. Include at least one crux on which the evidence could plausibly favor the coalitionist and one on which it could plausibly favor the substantivist. Conclude with a brief note on which parts of the year-long dispute each side now regards as merely verbal, if any.
