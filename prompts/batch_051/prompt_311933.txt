I am a novelist, and the novel I am planning turns on a question about what recorded evidence can still do for us. In it, a small community is deciding a legal dispute in which a video is the only decisive piece of evidence, and at the same moment the community learns how good current video-synthesis technology has become. A character in the novel, a retired archivist, presses a view I am trying to think through rather than merely assert. She claims that the deepest loss is not that any particular recording is now unreliable, but that recordings have stopped functioning as what she calls an epistemic backstop: the shared practice of letting a recording settle a disagreement when memories and testimony conflict, not because the recording is infallible but because the community treats it as the thing one appeals to when other evidence gives out. On her view, once the possibility of undetectable fabrication is common knowledge, that function is destroyed. Any disputed recording, however genuine and however carefully authenticated, can be dismissed as a probable forgery by whoever finds it inconvenient, and no recording can again play the deciding role.

I do not want to write this simply as an ominous premise. I want to know whether the archivist's claim is testable, and if so in exactly what respects. Design the strongest concrete test of the sharpest testable version of her position: that common knowledge of deepfakes destroys the epistemic-backstop function of recordings. Specify the observation or measurement that would count, the result that would count for the claim, and the result that would count against it. Then say precisely what a positive result would and would not show. In particular, distinguish three things a test might track: whether recordings have become less reliable in fact, whether people have come to believe they are less reliable, and whether the community has stopped treating them as decisive. Which of these, if any, does the test reach, and what residue does no test reach? Be honest about whether some core of the archivist's position is conceptual rather than empirical, and whether that is a defect in the position or just a limit on what evidence can do for it.
