Analyze the posthumously published private letters of Mother Teresa, in which she reports decades of feeling the absence of God while continuing her public ministry, as a two-way case study in the problem of divine hiddenness. State the strongest version of the argument from divine hiddenness you can defend — roughly, that a perfectly loving God would make his existence sufficiently evident for a reciprocal relationship, and that the persistence of sincere, nonresistant nonbelief therefore counts against such a God — and then apply it to her case. Use the argument to say precisely what her experience does and does not show, and use her case to test the argument's scope and assumptions. In particular, address whether the dark night of the soul tradition treats her experience as spiritual maturation rather than evidence of God's absence, whether that treatment is ad hoc, and whether hiddenness felt by a committed believer is even the kind of evidence the standard argument was designed to handle; note where the argument is silent. Conclude with a judgment about whether the episode strengthens, weakens, or falls outside the argument from divine hiddenness. Answer in American English.
