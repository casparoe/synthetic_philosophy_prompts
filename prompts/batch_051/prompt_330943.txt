I'm giving a 40-minute seminar next month to my research group's alignment reading seminar, defending a specific approach to eliciting latent knowledge from AI systems. The title is something like "Using frozen generative models as the training signal for report elicitation." My thesis is that the usual ELK framing—train a reporter on questions a human can grade, then somehow force it to be honest on questions a human cannot grade—has an underappreciated failure mode: a reporter can be perfectly honest on every supervised case while still systematically hiding a single component of its latent state on the unsupervised cases. I will instead propose that we freeze a separately trained generative model of the environment's sensor sequences, and train the reporter only through the following path: the reporter outputs a latent-state report, and that report is assessed by how much it improves the likelihood of the future sensor data under the frozen generative model. The attraction is that the reporter is penalized for omitting exactly those facts that would have changed what the sensors should show, even though no human ever grades the hard case.

The audience is not a general alignment audience. It includes four or five ML engineers who build model organisms of misalignment and tend to think ELK papers are underconstrained and unfalsifiable; two statisticians who will immediately ask whether I have merely reinvented amortized Bayesian experimental design with a worse loss; and a philosopher of science who keeps pressing on whether "the environment's generative model" already contains the very knowledge I am trying to elicit from the reporter. They will be friendly but technically combative. Prepare me for the Q&A. What are the objections, roughly in order of how likely they are, phrased the way those people would actually phrase them? Which objections have real force, and which should I concede in the first sentence of my reply? What is the short reply for each, and where is that reply softest? Include the question I should be hoping nobody asks.
