I keep bumping into a pattern I don't have a word for, and its making me unsure about something I'd always taken for granted. Take two possible lives. The first is short but extremely good; the second is a bit longer but every individual moment is a touch less intense. If you add enough length and shave off only a little intensity, the longer life can look clearly better, not just different. Now keep stepping in that direction: a long chain of lives where each link is a little longer and a little less intense than the last, and at each step the swap seems like an improvement. After a few hundred steps you arrive at a life that is enormously long but packed with moments barely worth having, and that final life seems obviously worse than the short intense one you started from. So "better than" appears to have walked me in a circle even though every step felt right when I took it. I've seen the same shape in arguments about populations, where each step adds many people at a slightly lower average welfare and each addition seems good, but the last population in the chain looks much worse than the first, and in political discussions where a little freedom is traded for a little stability, again and again.

What I want to know is whether this configuration has an established name, and whether it's supposed to show that "better than" isn't transitive, or whether the argument has a standard flaw. Please don't just give me a label though. I want the carving: which cases genuinely threaten transitivity, and which are really vagueness about where to draw a line, or cases where the two options aren't comparable at all, since those feel like different phenomena to me and I doubt they can all be the relevant thing. If there's a standard concept, tell me how it's defined and where its edges are; if nothing fits quite right, say so and propose a term with a definition and boundary cases. And keep the settled part separate from the contested part: I can't tell from what I've read whether this is a closed problem, a live debate, or a fringe position, and I'd rather not have one camp's view handed to me as the consensus.
