I have been working through the portions of the Zhuangzi that treat death directly and indirectly, and I want to make sure I have exhausted the main families of interpretation rather than simply rearranging the ones I already know. Here are the readings I can articulate without external reference:

1. A natural-transformation reading: death is one phase in a continuous process of change, like the alternation of the seasons, and the proper response is to follow that transformation rather than resist it.

2. An epistemological reading: because we cannot know whether death is worse than life, fear of death and confident claims that death is an evil rest on an unjustified preference for the state we currently happen to occupy.

3. A therapeutic reading: the anecdotes about death are meant not to establish a metaphysical doctrine but to loosen the observer's attachment to a fixed personal identity and to the emotions that attend it.

4. A perspectival reading: from a sufficiently broad perspective, perhaps the perspective of the Dao, the distinction between life and death ceases to have the importance we ordinarily give it, so death is not a genuine loss.

5. A ritual-critique reading: the scenes of singing and drumming in the presence of a corpse are deliberate violations of mourning ritual, exposing the artificiality of social conventions rather than advancing a doctrine of post-mortem existence.

6. A skeptical-phenomenal reading: just as the butterfly passage destabilizes the waking/dream distinction, the death passages destabilize the living/dead distinction, leaving us without a firm basis for judging one state better than the other.

I want one additional family of interpretation, or one substantive argument for a particular interpretation, that is not on this list and not a restatement, special case, or combination of items on it. If you produce one, tell me honestly whether it is any good, and how it relates to the epistemological and perspectival readings in particular.
