I started a month ago trying to actually live out a position I read in Dharmakirti: that there are only two valid ways of knowing, perception and inference, and that things like testimony and memory aren't independent sources but get reduced to inference. So now, when someone tells me something, I don't count the fact that they said it as a separate kind of evidence. I take their words as a perception, then I infer the content from how reliable they've been and how plausible the claim is. Same with my own memory: I don't treat a memory as direct contact with the past; I treat it as a present mental event from which I infer what happened. In practice I mostly believe the same things, which makes me wonder if I've changed anything at all or just relabeled trust. My partner says it's the latter and that the whole policy only looks principled because I never push it: if each moment is gone and even my own mind is a fresh event every instant, than there's no stable me that's doing the inferring or holding the track record. So: is this policy actually defensible, is it more than a relabeling, and does the momentariness make it incoherent?
