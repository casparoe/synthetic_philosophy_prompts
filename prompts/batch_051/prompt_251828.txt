I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I've been noticing something at work that I'd like help characterizing. For the past couple of years I've been using AI pair-programming tools heavily, and I can now produce substantially more working code than I could before; the code passes its tests and gets through review. But my private grip on what the system does has been thinning. I can run the test suite without being able to say which behaviors the tests actually rule out. I can merge a change and yet be unable, a week later, to reconstruct why one design was chosen over the alternatives. I can fix a failing build by asking the assistant, but I couldn't explain the fix without asking again. I notice a similar thing with navigation: I reliably get where I'm going while my own map of the area gets worse, not better.

The pattern seems to be this: when an external system reliably supplies outputs and I can still verify those outputs against some check (tests pass, I arrive at the destination), the check can keep succeeding even as the understanding that would let me generate, explain, or extend the outputs stops being maintained. Performance and comprehension come apart. It is not simply loss of a skill, because the work still gets done and I still exercise a real kind of judgment in checking it; rather, the judgment becomes local and piecemeal while the broader sense of why things work decays. Is there an established name or a body of work for this specific gap between retained performance and eroded understanding, and how should it be distinguished from the older worry that machines make particular manual skills obsolete? If nothing fits, I would be glad to hear a proposed term with a definition, including what cases it covers and what cases it deliberately leaves out. A bare label is not what I'm after; I want to know precisely what the phenomenon is and is not, and where its edges are. Finally, it would help me to know how confident you are in the answer and which specific point you are least sure about.
