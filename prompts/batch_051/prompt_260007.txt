I've been trying to pin down what makes something a language after a friend insisted that sign languages aren't real languages, just gestures standing in for spoken words. I'm convinced he's wrong about American Sign Language, but I can't say exactly why without a definition, and every definition I try also has to handle the cases I keep going back and forth on.

Here are my cases and hunches. American Sign Language: definitely a language. A dog's growl: not a language. The honeybee waggle dance: I lean toward not, but it does convey distance and direction. A home sign system invented by a deaf child whose hearing family never learns to sign: I can't decide. A village sign language used by both deaf and hearing members of a community where deafness is common: I want to say yes, but then I can't explain what makes it different from the home sign case. Traffic lights: not a language. A programming language such as Python: it's called a language, but I'd probably say no. A parrot that can reliably produce words for objects: probably not a language, but I can't say what's missing. A two-year-old's halting speech: clearly language, even though it lacks most grammar.

Give me a working definition of "language" that gets these cases right. Run the definition through all of them, say where it conflicts with my hunches or gives no clear answer, and tell me which of your verdicts is least secure. Please write in American English.
