I help run a small historical reenactment society. Over the winter, some of us began a weekly game night in which we play a popular multiplayer first-person shooter set in the Second World War; players choose a side, respawn after dying, and score points for kills and captured objectives. One of the senior members has started arguing that these game nights are essentially what we do on the field during a reenactment, only in another medium. In both, he says, you take on the role of a soldier, make fast decisions under pressure, and try to grasp a battle from the inside rather than watch it from outside. From this he draws a strong conclusion: someone who calls the game a glorification of war would have to say the same about our reenactments, and since we already accept the reenactments as serious remembrance, we should accept the game nights on the same terms.

I do not think the analogy can carry that much weight, but I want it examined rather than dismissed. Please audit it for me. What corresponds to what between a battlefield reenactment and a historical war game, where does the mapping break down, and what does the reenactment side of the comparison bring with it that a game may lack? Which of those differences actually matter for the claim that the game is serious remembrance rather than glorification, and which are incidental? A side-by-side table of correspondences would help me. Then tell me whether the senior member's conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and offer a better analogy if one exists.
