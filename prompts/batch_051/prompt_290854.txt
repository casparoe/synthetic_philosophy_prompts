Here's where I've landed:

(1) In civil cases, proof by a preponderance of the evidence just means the probability that the defendant is liable is over .5, and that is necessary and sufficient for liability.

(2) But in the blue bus case — 80% of the buses on the route are run by Blue Bus Co., with no other evidence — I don't think the plaintiff should win. Same for the gatecrasher case: if 501 of 1,000 rodeo attendees never paid, you can't find a randomly selected attendee liable on that alone.

(3) I'm tempted to explain (2) by saying that legal verdicts claim knowledge of what this particular defendant did, and naked statistical evidence can't give knowledge of this defendent's conduct.

(4) Yet I also think I know my lottery ticket won't win before the draw, purely from the odds, and I know a drug works from clinical-trial frequencies alone.

(5) In ordinary life I act on bare statistics all the time and think that's rational — after a norovirus outbreak at a restaurant I just don't go, even though I can't point to anything wrong with my particular would-be meal.

(6) In criminal cases I don't think a naked 99% likelihood alone should support a conviction.

(7) But I'd also say proof beyond a reasonable doubt is just a very high probability threshold, and 99% surely clears any such threshold.
