I keep running into the phrase "defer to your future self" in online arguments about probability and rationality, and each time it is made to do a slightly different job. Most recently, someone used it to claim that if I expect that after reading a long report tomorrow I will have lowered my confidence in some empirical claim, then I should lower it today—otherwise I'm being incoherent. I have a feeling there is something right in the vicinity: if tomorrow's version of me will have read the report, shouldn't I trust where he is heading and move now? But when I try to pin the slogan down, I get several different ideas that don't obviously say the same thing.

The versions I can extract from people are roughly these:

(a) If I am certain that I will believe a claim later, then I should believe it now.
(b) My current degree of confidence in a claim should equal the degree of confidence I expect to have later, averaged over the possible values it might take.
(c) A future version of me who has strictly better information is an authority I should defer to now, roughly the way I'd defer to a reliable expert.

I'm told one of these is close to a named principle in philosophy, but I don't want the answer to depend on my knowing the label. What I want is the ideas pulled apart. Lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be making, from the trivially true to the clearly false. For each reading, say what it assumes about the later self—its rationality, information, memory, and continuity with me—and whether it survives cases where the future self will be drunk, exhausted, brainwashed, forgetful, or simply worse at reasoning. Most importantly, say for each reading whether it actually supports the move my acquaintance wanted to make: lowering my confidence now just because I expect to lower it after reading the report. I'd also like a guess at which reading is doing the real work when people use the slogan to end an argument about pre-updating, and why. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the menu as a whole and which single claim or verdict you are least sure of.
