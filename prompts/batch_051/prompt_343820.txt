I run the science grants program at a small private foundation, and every year we fund maybe eight or ten projects split across three communities that barely talk to each other: origin-of-life chemists, exoplanet biosignature people, and SETI-adjacent technosignature searches. We always get more strong proposals than we can fund, and our board has started complaining that our ranking is basically vibes-based—whoever gave the most exciting talk or whatever. I'd like to rebuild the ranking around the Great Filter argument instead: the idea that somewhere between prebiotic chemistry and a galaxy-spanning civilization there has to be a step that's very hard, and different kinds of evidence tell us different things about where that step is. Please turn that argument into a real procedure my team can run on each proposal, not a philosophy essay. My team has no philosophy background and can spend maybe a hour per proposal, so it needs to be a checklist or ordered set of questions: what to ask first, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, where we stop and say no, and which steps are secretly value judgments (like whether it would be worse to learn the filter is behind us or ahead of us) rather than scientific judgments. We also need some honest expected-value-of-information logic, since we are comparing evidence about different rungs of the ladder rather than just 'better' or 'worse' science.

Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical proposals: a wet-dry cycling experiment on whether RNA-like polymers form readily from simple precursors; a space telescope survey looking for oxygen/ozone in the atmospheres of rocky habitable-zone exoplanets; and an all-sky search for anomalous infrared waste heat from very advanced civilizations. Please make sure the worked examples include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer we will not like—say it ranks a proposal we are currently excited about below one we have been lukewarm on—and be explicit about what the procedure cannot decide for us no matter how carefully we run it.
