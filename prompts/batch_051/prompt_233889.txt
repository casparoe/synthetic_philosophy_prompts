I've been turning over an argument a friend made about the South African Truth and Reconciliation Commission, and I can't decide whether it's a reductio or just a bad analogy. Here's how he laid it out. (1) The TRC was justified in granting amnesty to people who committed gross human rights violations under apartheid, so long as they gave a full public account of what they did. (2) The best rationale for (1) is that giving the victims and the public the truth mattered more than retributive punishment, at least in that context. (3) But if truth matters more than punishment there, the same should go for ordinary cases: a murderer who gives a full and credible confession also gives the victim's family the truth about what happened. (4) By parity, we should grant amnesty to ordinary murderers who confess fully. (5) Obviously we shouldn't. (6) So either the TRC was wrong, or the rationale in (2) wasn't what was actually doing the work. (7) If something else was doing the work—say, the need to stabilize a fragile new democracy—then all the talk about truth and victims was really just a fig leaf for paying off violent people. I really want to hold on to (1). But the only way I can see to block (4) is to say transitional justice and ordinary justice run on different moral rules, and that feels like special pleadng. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
