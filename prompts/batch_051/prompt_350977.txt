I am a philosophy professor, and in my social epistemology seminar I rely on a distinction whose robustness I now want to test. Please do not supply readings or citations for this exercise; I will state the distinction myself, in compressed form, so that the task tests translation rather than retrieval.

The distinction is this. An epistemic bubble is a condition of impoverished input. A person in such a bubble may reason competently, but the information environment has been arranged — through personalization, sorting, or plain omission — so that relevant contrary sources and arguments simply never reach them. The defect is in the coverage of what is delivered, not in the person's processing of it. Consequently, a bubble can in principle be punctured by exposure: supply the missing voices, and the problem is at least partly repaired.

An echo chamber is different in kind. It is not the absence of dissent but the engineered neutralization of it. In a chamber, opposing voices do reach the person; what has been manufactured is the person's standing disposition to discount them before genuinely weighing them. The chamber works not by narrowing what is delivered but by installing a credence function of a certain shape — one that assigns low default trust to outsiders, prizes insider credentials, and treats listening to the other side as itself a sign of disloyalty. Adding missing voices can therefore deepen a chamber rather than puncture it, because the chamber has already trained its inhabitant to hear those voices as proof of the enemy's reach.

Your task is to restate this distinction, as faithfully as the target vocabulary permits, in the language of a competent machine-learning engineer whose daily work is recommender systems, ranking, candidate generation, trust and safety, content moderation, objective functions, and adversarial robustness. Assume your reader is fluent in that vocabulary and has no philosophical background. I want the translation itself to be exact: show what a bubble is in the engineer's terms, what a chamber is, and why a chamber is not merely an extreme bubble or a bubble plus stubbornness.

Then give me the residue report. Identify which parts of the distinction carried over cleanly, which carried over only under distortion, and which could not be expressed in the engineer's vocabulary at all. In particular, tell me what happens to the prescriptive content — the claim that a reasonable person ought not to be in either condition, and that the two conditions may call for different remedies and different judgments of responsibility. Does the target vocabulary contain anything corresponding to the difference between an account of harm to the user and an account of what the user is entitled to believe, or does its normative reach stop at the optimization of some objective?

Conclude with a verdict on the residue. Where the translation breaks down, is that a defect of the engineering vocabulary — a sign that it is missing something real, such as normativity or the aim of belief — or is it a sign that the philosophical distinction was carrying more rhetorical weight than descriptive content? Be honest about both possibilities, and do not smuggle either framework's assumptions into the other merely to force a mapping.
