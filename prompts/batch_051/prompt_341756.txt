Hey, I'm a novelist and I've been going back and forth on Descartes' animals-are-machines thing — that a dog or a parrot doesn't have a mind because it can't use language flexibly and everything it does can be explained by the arrangement of its organs, not by thought. I'm not sure I buy it, but I've been trying to turn it into explicit criteria, partly because it would help me think about whether I can honestly say my characters "make decisions." Here's what I came up with. Something has a mind only if: (1) it combines signs or words in new, relevant ways rather than just emitting fixed responses, (2) it handles novel situations by reasoning instead of running an built-in routine, and (3) its behavior isn't fully explained by some outside thing pulling the strings. A parrot saying "nice weather" when the sun comes out fails on (1) and (2); a dog fails on (1) and mostly (2); a typical human passes all three.

Now the case. The point-of-view character in my work-in-progress, Priya, in the book uses language creatively — some of her lines genuinely startled me, I did'nt plan them. She works through setbacks on the page in ways that are new to the situation, and from inside the story nothing is pulling her strings; if you stay inside the fiction she passes all three. But from outside she's just marks on paper or pixels, produced by me and then reconstructed by readers; she has no inner activity apart from the text, and the whole thing is scripted, even the bits that surprised me. Does Priya have a mind by my own criteria? Go criterion by criterion. If she passes, did I leave out a criterion — something like "has its own real internal source" — and is that fourth criterion actually the one Descartes needed all along? If she fails, say exactly where, and explain why a parrot is in a different boat.
