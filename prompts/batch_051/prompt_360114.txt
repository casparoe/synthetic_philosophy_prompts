I teach a fourteen-student upper-level undergraduate seminar in applied ethics, and we have one eighty-minute session set aside for David Benatar's claim that coming into existence is a net harm. In past years I have found that if I simply present the four-part asymmetry on the board, most students agree with each piece while refusing the conclusion, and we never get past the feeling that they have been baited by a trick of wording. I would like to try the opposite: an exercise in which students construct the asymmetry themselves, from their own judgments about made-up procreative cases, before I name it or attribute it to anyone.

Design a structured small-group activity, roughly sixty minutes with a twenty-minute debrief, in which participants play the role of a demographic review board that must decide whether to authorize the creation of a single future person with a specified life-profile. The heart of the exercise should be two accounting ledgers the students must fill in: one for the world in which the person exists, and one for the world in which the person is never created. The rules of the ledger should first have them record the experienced goods and bads in the world where the person exists, and then ask what, if anything, they should record in the other world. My hope is that they will discover they are willing to enter the absence of the bad things as something good in the never-exists world, but refuse to enter the absence of the good things as something bad in that same world. Please supply the exact wording of the ledger instructions, three or four life-profiles designed to make the pattern visible (including one life that is awful and one that is by ordinary measures good), the sequence of comparisons or votes that forces the discovery, a debrief that maps their ledger entries onto the four claims of the asymmetry, the likely places where students will resist the move or quietly cheat their own ledger, and an honest note on which feature of Benatar's argument the activity cannot by itself produce and that I will need to supply after the debrief.
