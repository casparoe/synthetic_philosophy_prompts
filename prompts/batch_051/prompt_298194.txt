A friend of mine keeps deploying the line "a corporation is a person" whenever I push back on his claim that a sufficiently advanced AI should be able to have legal rights. His move is basically this: corporations are already legal persons even though they have no inner life, so why is it such a big leap to make an AI a legal person too? It always ends the argument because I don't quite know how to respond, even though I feel like something is going wrong in the move. I'm trying to figure out what that sentence could actually mean, because it's obviously false if "person" means a flesh-and-blood human being, and yet courts and law textbooks do say something like it. I want a menu of the distinct claims the slogan could be expressing, from the boring/trivial to the radical, with a verdict on each: which are true, which are false, which genuinely support extending legal personhood to an AI, and which just sound like they do. On each reading, say exactly where the analogy between corporate personhood and AI personhood breaks down. I mostly want to be able to answer him next time he says "well corporations are persons and nobody riots in the streets about that."
