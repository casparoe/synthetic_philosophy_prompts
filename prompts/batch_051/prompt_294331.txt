I am preparing a presentation for my high school's science-and-society seminar, and I want a worked example that pulls apart two senses of "alignment" that my classmates keep using interchangeably. In AI safety, "intent alignment" is often defined narrowly: a system is aligned when it reliably does what its human principal actually intends, even if that intention is not a morally good one. "Value alignment" is the broader demand that a system bring about what is actually good for the people its actions affect. Please construct a single near-term scenario—say, a language-model assistant in customer service or an online tutoring product, not an AGI—that yields two paired cases. In the first case, the system should be intent-aligned but not value-aligned: it executes the operator's actual intention, yet leaves the affected person worse off. In the second case, the system should be value-aligned but not intent-aligned: it acts for the good of the affected person in a way that frustrates the operator's actual intention. Constraints: the operator in the first case must not be a cartoon villain; the harm should flow from an ordinary commercial incentive such as maximizing engagement, repeat purchases, or billable hours, not from deliberate malevolence; and in both cases the system must be functioning exactly as designed, with no malfunction, hallucination, or miscommunication between operator and system.

After presenting the scenario, walk through each verdict: state how you are applying the two notions, say precisely what the principal intended in each case, why the system satisfied or frustrated that intention, why the affected person was harmed or helped, and therefore which kind of alignment obtains in each case. Then identify the single structural feature of the construction that does the conceptual work—the feature that, if removed, would make the two cases collapse back together—and anticipate how a defender of the narrower view might object that your second case is not really value alignment but just intent alignment with a better-chosen principal.
