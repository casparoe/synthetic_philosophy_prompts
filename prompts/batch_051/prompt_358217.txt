Take the Enron collapse as a case study for the "does the market corrupt or civilize character?" question. I already know the mechanics of the fraud: mark-to-market accounting, special purpose entities, debt hidden off balance sheet, the rank-and-yank culture, and Arthur Andersen going down with it. What I want is the character-theoretic reading, not the accounting. Before the collapse, what would the "commerce civilizes" thesis have predicted about the character of a firm full of traders and dealmakers, and what would the corruption thesis have predicted? Then read the actual Enron story through both lenses: which market features (repeated dealing, reputation, competition, price transparency) should have disciplined people, and where did they fail or actually work; which features (commodification, performance pay, deregulated trading) eroded honesty, prudence, or restraint? Finally give me your honest verdict: does Enron count as evidence against the civilizing-commerce thesis, or is it an anomaly the market itself punished, and be disciplined about separating what was genuinely a market mechanism from ordinary greed or bad luck.
