I came to philosophy late, after a career in the natural sciences, and I have been surprised to find myself defending a version of transhumanism about radical self-transformation. My scientific training disposed me to see organisms as contingent homeostatic systems whose set points are products of selection rather than expressions of any normative order, and I have carried that disposition into philosophy. The position I hold is this: there is no non-arbitrary line between the transformations that medicine, training, and education have always pursued and the more radical transformations now being discussed — in mood, cognition, lifespan, body plan, and reproduction. What should settle any proposed transformation is only whether the person affected would, all things considered, be better off by their own lights, judged both before and after the change. The standard reply that something important would be lost — an unchosen nature, a human given, a form of life — appears to me to be status-quo bias in philosophical dress.

I do not want a list of the usual objections. I want a map of this view's exposure. Which observations, arguments, cases, or findings would count decisively against it, and which would be merely awkward? Which would a committed defender of the position concede as real evidence against it, as opposed to objections they would dismiss as misdescribed or question-begging? Are there conditions under which the before-and-after endorsement test cannot do the work I am assigning it — for example, because a transformation alters the very standards by which the person would assess it afterwards, or because the post-transformation endorsement is itself manufactured by the change? I have also been toying with a distinction between transformations that preserve a person's capacity to revise their values and transformations that alter that capacity itself; I suspect the second class is where the view faces its hardest test, and I want that suspicion examined. Finally, tell me what this view predicts that its rivals do not — where it makes contact with anything that could turn out otherwise — and whether, in the form I have stated it, it has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be a defect for a view of this kind.
