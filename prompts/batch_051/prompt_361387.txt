I am on the philosophy discussion group at my school, and we each have to prepare a short statement of our actual convictions on a single topic before the rest of the group can cross-examine us. I chose gratitude, partly because a recent conversation about whether it makes sense to be grateful to the universe for good luck revealed that I hold a cluster of strong opinions I have never tried to line up against one another. I would like a consistency check before I present it. Here are the commitments:

(a) Gratitude is owed only to someone who acted in order to benefit me. If someone benefits me by accident, or only as a side effect of pursuing their own aims, I may be glad, but gratitude would be misplaced.

(b) Gratitude is not merely a warm feeling. It carries a real requirement to do something: at least to acknowledge the benefit sincerely, and, where possible, to benefit the benefactor in return.

(c) I can owe gratitude to someone who is dead or otherwise permanently out of reach. The fact that I can never express it directly to them does not cancel the debt.

(d) I think I can be grateful to a great-grandparent I never met, or to a more distant ancestor, for advantages I now enjoy because of sacrifices they made, even though they could not have had me as an individual in mind when they made them.

(e) I do not think anyone owes gratitude to luck, chance, nature, the universe, or the past itself. If I say that I am grateful the train was late and I therefore missed an accident, I am speaking loosely: the real state is relief, not gratitude, because there is no one to be grateful to.

(f) Ingratitude can be a serious moral failing, but it would be wrong for a benefactor to demand gratitude, and wrong to treat a benefit as if it created a debt that the beneficiary must be forced to pay.

Could you go through this set and tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? I would like the genuine tensions ranked by severity rather than treated as equally damaging, and for each one I would like to know what would have to change and what the cheapest repair would be. Finally, tell me which single revision would relieve the most tension while staying as close as possible to what I actually believe. I am not sure where the weak point is, so please be specific about which commitments conflict rather than pointing at the topic in general.
