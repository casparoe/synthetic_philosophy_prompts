I've been trying to write down everything I actually believe about origin essentialism -- the claim that I couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg than I did -- and I've ended up with five commitments I'd like pressure-tested before my metaphysics seminar. Here is the package:

(a) I hold origin essentialism: if my parents had conceived a child even a few minutes later than they did, with a different sperm or egg, that child would have been a different person, not me.
(b) But I think I could have been switched at birth and raised by completely different people in another country -- different name, language, religion, formative experiences -- and still have been me. My actual upbringing isn't essential to me.
(c) For identity over time, I hold a psychological continuity theory: what makes me now the same person as someone at another time is the right sort of overlapping chains of memories, intentions, character, etc. No immaterial soul and no brute biological persistence is needed.
(d) I think an identical twin is a different person from me even though our DNA matches, which I take to show origin essentialism isn't a thesis about having a particular genome.
(e) And I think teletransportation would preserve me: scan me, destroy the original, build an exact psychological duplicate from new matter on Mars, and the duplicate is me, not someone who merely thinks they're me.

A friend has already told me that two of these are obvious incompatible but refused to say which, so now I'm suspicious of all of them. Please go through my commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Don't just tell me the whole set is coherent; if you think it is, that claim had better survive a real attempt to break it. At the very end, on its own line, write \answer{...} with your severity ranking of the genuine tensions as comma-separated pairs, e.g. \answer{a-c > b-d}. Leave out any pair that's only apparently a problem.
