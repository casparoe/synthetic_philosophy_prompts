I am a student in a philosophy course on the epistemology of emerging media, and I am trying to clarify the distinct ways in which deepfakes are supposed to compromise the role of audio and video recordings as a final epistemic backstop for claims about public events. So far I have identified four alleged mechanisms.

(1) Fabrication: a synthetic recording can depict an event that never occurred, so viewers who take the medium at face value acquire a false belief about what happened.

(2) The liar's dividend: because plausible fabrication is possible, a person caught on a genuine recording can dismiss it as synthetic, so audio and video stop being decisive evidence against someone willing to lie.

(3) Backstop erosion: recordings functioned as the last court of appeal when memory, testimony, and documents conflicted; once recordings can themselves be reasonably suspected, disputes that used to be settled by the tape are reopened without any obvious successor to settle them.

(4) Verification overload: fakes can be produced cheaply and circulated faster than they can be checked, so even where most items could in principle be verified, the social machinery for sorting genuine from fabricated material breaks down from volume.

I have the nagging sense that a further mechanism is missing from this list, one that is not a restatement, special case, or combination of (1) through (4). Give me that mechanism, framed as precisely as the others. Then tell me honestly whether it deserves to stand as a fifth item or should be folded into one of the four, and explain why.
