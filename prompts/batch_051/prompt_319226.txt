I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and there is a dialectical move I keep encountering that I cannot yet evaluate with any precision. In a recent comment-section debate about whether there is a fixed human nature—one commenter had argued on the basis of behavioral genetics and developmental psychology that some substantial innate structure is real—another commenter simply replied, "Existence precedes essence," as if that closed the matter. I know the phrase is an existentialist slogan, and I have seen critics dismiss it as either incoherent or trivially true, but I cannot tell which of several possible claims it is supposed to express. Could you lay out the readings as a menu? In particular: what could "existence" range over here (an individual human life, the bare fact that someone exists before any description of them, the species as historically prior to its self-understanding), what could "essence" mean (a definition, a purpose or function, a built-in psychological nature, the set of properties without which the thing would not be what it is), and what relation could "precedes" pick out (temporal priority, explanatory priority, normative priority)? For each precisification, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and then say which of them would actually support the use the slogan was being put to in that argument—the conclusion that no claim attributing an innate human nature can be true.
