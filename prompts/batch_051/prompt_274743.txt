I keep trying to formalize how I think value combines across intensity, duration, consciousness, and number of people, and my gut verdicts keep breaking whatever formula I write down. Maybe you can find the principle, or tell me which verdicts to drop. I'll rate intensity 0 to 10, consciousness 0 to 1 (with 1 being normal wakefulness), duration in hours, and I'll treat each person's welfare as a number.

Here are the cases I feel settled on.

1. For a single experience, duration and intensity trade off as a product. Two hours at 5/10 feels exactly as good to me as one hour at 10/10. Ten hours at 1/10 also feels about as good as one hour at 10/10. There's no obvious diminishing returns over time at these levels; I just seem to integrate intensity times duration.

2. Consciousness doesnt behave like the other factors. If there's a drug that gives me the exact neural state of a 10/10 pleasant experience but leaves me only half conscious — a warm fog where I'm barely tracking anything — I'd rate the whole hour around 2/10, not 5. And if the same neural state happens under full anesthesia, with no experience at all, I say it's worth zero. Low-but-nonzero consciousness seems to matter more than proportionally, and zero is a hard gate.

3. Across people, sometimes adding works. I'd rather spare two strangers a 5/10 hour of pain than one stranger a 9/10 hour. But I'd also rather spare one stranger a 10/10 hour of agony than any number of strangers a 1/10 hour of mild itch, no matter how many itches there are.

4. The pain side and the pleasure side aren't symmetric for me. On the positive side, I think enough 1/10 pleasant afternoons do eventually outweigh one 10/10 hour of joy. But on the negative side, as I said, no number of 1/10 itches outweighs one 10/10 agony. I realize that asymetry looks ad hoc, but I keep finding myself with it.

My working formula was just multiply within a person — consciousness times intensity times duration — and then add across people. That handles case 1 and the first half of case 3, but it overvalues the half-conscious state in case 2, predicts that some huge number of itches outweighs the agony, which I reject, and it makes no room for the pleasure/pain asymmetry in case 4.

Is there one well-motivated value function that gets all or most of these, even if it has a threshold or a nonlinear term? Which of my verdicts do I have to abandon to keep it simple? And what does your account predict for the cases I'm still unsure about: (a) a half-conscious person in 9/10 pain for a minute versus a fully conscious person in 3/10 pain for an hour; (b) one person living a 10/10 life for a year versus a million people living 1/10 lives for the same year with normal consciousness; (c) whether 0.01 consciousness is basically zero for value or still does a tiny but real amount of work?
