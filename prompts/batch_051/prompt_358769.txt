I've come around to something like Nozick's entitlement theory: a distribution is just if it arose from just acquisition and just transfer, plus whatever rectification is owed for past injustices in the chain. And the Wilt Chamberlain argument feels close to decisive against patterned principles—once you start from a distribution and let people voluntarily pay a quarter to watch Wilt play, you either accept the resulting inequality or you're committed to forbidding a huge range of consensual transactions, and the second looks obviously worse to me. So my question is: what would count against this view? I don't want the standard objections just restated; I want an honest map of where the position is exposed. Which possible cases, intuitions, or results about acquisition, transfer, or rectification would be decisive, and which are merely awkward? What should a committed defender admit counts against the view, and what can they fairly dismiss? And be straight with me: once you add the clause that we correct for all past injustices, does the theory still predict or forbid anything specific, or has it been qualified into vacuity—and if so, is that a problem for a theory like this or just the normal situation for a high-level principle?
