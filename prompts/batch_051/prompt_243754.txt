Question: Explain the distinction between vicious and benign infinite regresses. Then propose and critically evaluate a criterion for determining when a regress is vicious. Your answer must discuss at least two examples, one of which should be the regress of justification for a belief.

Student's answer:

An infinite regress is what you get when applying a rule or relation to an item produces another item of the same kind, and applying it again produces yet another, with no end. A regress is benign when the infinity does no harm. The standard example is the truth regress: if p is true, then the proposition that p is true is true, and then the proposition that that proposition is true is true, and so on. This regress is benign because each new step is generated automatically and nothing about the truth of p depends on finishing the series.

A vicious regress, by contrast, blocks the original claim from ever being established. The clearest case is the epistemic regress: if a belief is justified only when it is inferred from another justified belief, then justification either stops somewhere or runs on forever. Since nobody can actually complete an infinite chain of justifications, a belief resting on such a chain is never really justified.

So the criterion I would defend is this: a regress is vicious just in case establishing the original claim would require actually carrying out infinitely many steps, and benign just in case the steps are generated by a rule and the original claim does not require them to be carried out. The truth regress is benign because the further truths come for free, whereas the epistemic regress is vicious because each justification would have to be given in turn and the chain never bottoms out.

This is why the epistemic regress is standardly taken to establish foundationalism. If an infinite chain cannot be completed, and if a circular chain cannot justify because it assumes what it is meant to prove, then the only structure left is a chain that terminates in beliefs justified without inference from further beliefs. So the difference between vicious and benign regresses ultimately comes down to whether the regress asks a finite inquirer to do something impossible.
