Draft the joint statement that a committed Strawsonian and a committed responsibility-skeptic would issue after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the reactive attitudes. Use the following scaffolding, and do not cite or quote anyone: the task is to reason the positions out from their commitments.

The two camps.

The Strawsonian is a participant-stance compatibilist. They hold that the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, hurt feelings, indignation, forgiveness — are natural, non-optional ways of being involved with other people. On this view, a negative reactive attitude such as resentment is governed by an internal standard of appropriateness: it is fitting when, and because, the target showed ill will, disregard, or a failure of adequate good will toward the person, and when no excuse (accident, coercion, non-culpable ignorance) or exemption (serious mental disorder, developmental immaturity, incapacity for normal adult relationships) applies. Excuses show that, despite appearances, the quality of will in this particular performance was not bad; exemptions show that the target falls outside the ordinary demand altogether. Whether the target is the ultimate source of their own character, and whether determinism is true, is irrelevant to this internal standard.

The responsibility-skeptic is an ultimacy-based incompatibilist about desert. They hold that ordinary blame carries a desert-entailing claim: resentment says, in effect, that the target deserves to be resented for what they did. That claim is made true only if the target is ultimately responsible for the aspect of themselves from which the wrong flowed; a person cannot deserve blame for a self they did not author. Since every person's constitution and starting points are ultimately the product of causes they did not choose, the desert-entailing reading of the reactive attitudes is false across the board. The skeptic therefore holds that mature moral life should move as far as is feasible toward the objective stance — relating to wrongdoers mostly as things to be managed, treated, deterred, and repaired around, rather than as fitting targets of moral resentment — while retaining grief, concern, and protest that do not assert a debt of suffering.

Produce the document with the following structure.

Section 1: Numbered agreed claims. These should be non-vacuous and jointly signable: claims about the phenomenology and interpersonal role of the reactive attitudes, about how excuses differ from exemptions, about what the objective stance involves, and about what is not at stake. Both sides should be able to sign a claim saying that the dispute would not be settled by simply showing that determinism or indeterminism is true.

Section 2: A disagreements table. State each residual question in neutral language, then give the Strawsonian's and the responsibility-skeptic's own formulations of their answers, each phrased so that its own side would sign it, and note where a formulation quietly commits the side to more than the question requires.

Section 3: The cruxes, as a table. For each crux: name it; state the sub-question such that a settled answer would move at least one side; supply a concrete diagnostic case or argument that both sides accept bears on it; and record in advance what each side predicts about it. Cover at least the following three.

(a) The origin-and-history crux: does the fittingness of resentment track the quality of will displayed at the time of wrongdoing, or also the history by which the agent came to have that quality of will? Diagnostic case: two agents perform identical harmful acts with identical current ill will, and both now wholeheartedly endorse their attitudes; one acquired those attitudes through an ordinary developmental history, the other had them installed by a neuroscientific intervention three days earlier and now fully owns them. Which side predicts what about whether resentment is equally fitting in the two cases?

(b) The stability-of-the-objective-stance crux: can a wholehearted, durable adoption of the objective stance toward a loved one remain psychologically and morally coherent, or does it covertly draw on the participant stance it was meant to replace? Diagnostic case: a parent resolves on skeptical grounds to respond to a child's persistent cruelty entirely with clinical management and forward-looking correction, never allowing themselves felt hurt or disappointment. What does each side predict about the long-run quality of that relationship?

(c) The content-of-the-demand crux: is the demand internal to resentment a demand that the target could have met at the time of action (ordinary capacities, no excuse, no exemption), or a demand that the target be the ultimate author of their own constitution? Diagnostic case: a person with a congenital empathy deficit intellectually regrets the harm they cause but cannot viscerally care, and on a particular occasion chooses an action they know will humiliate another. How much, if at all, should resentment be tempered, according to each side?

Section 4: A short assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal — for instance, whether the two sides are using words such as desert or appropriate in systematically different senses — and which disagreements would survive after the terminology is aligned.

Write the document in a voice the two camps could share, making the residual disagreement exact rather than softened, and avoid the tone of a referee ruling for one side.
