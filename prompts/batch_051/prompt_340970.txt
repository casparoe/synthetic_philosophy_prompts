I've been trying to get clear on my own educational instincts by testing them against Locke's Some Thoughts Concerning Education. He keeps saying in there that children are rational creatures and should be reasoned with as such, but that the kind of reasoning has to be suited to their age and capacity. I can't tell whether my gut reactions actually line up with him or with something more modern.

Here are my verdicts on cases:

- When my 3-year-old reaches for the stove, I say "hot, no," and if they keep reaching, I move them. I don't think I owe them an explanation of burns. Fine.
- When my 6-year-old asks why they have to wear shoes in the yard, I give them the thorn-and-bee reason. And if they come back with "but I'll watch where I step," I sometimes let them go barefoot. Also fine.
- When my 10-year-old lies about having done their homework, I think they should get a consequence and a real explanation of why lying was wrong—but I don't think the abstract lecture about trust does anything. Fine, but something about my view here feels different from the stove case.
- When my 14-year-old wants to quit piano, I think after one serious conversation about what they might be giving up, they should get to make a bad decision and live with it. I don't like it, but I think overriding them would be wrong.
- But when my 5-year-old says they don't want to go to school, that's not a real vote. School isn't optional, and no amount of "but why" changes that. I feel no tension about this one.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? Also, is it actually Locke's principle, or have I slipped into something more like a modern children's-autonomy view? And what would the principle you settle on say about two cases I keep going back and forth on: a 12-year-old who refuses to wear a coat in freezing weather, and a 4-year-old who asks "why do people die?"—do I owe the 4-year-old a straight answer, and does the 12-year-old get to learn by being cold?

I know the last two might pull in different directions, so if the principle you propose can't keep all my original verdicts, tell me which ones I should drop and why. Also spell out which feature of each case is doing the work.
