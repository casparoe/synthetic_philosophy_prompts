I'm giving a seminar next month to the scientific advisory board of a large conservation NGO, defending a position I have spent the past year developing: that the board should endorse a staged pilot in which a threshold-dependent, locally confinable suppression gene drive is released against invasive house mice on one small, uninhabited Southern Ocean seabird island, as an alternative to the current protocol of repeated aerial baiting with anticoagulant rodenticides. By threshold-dependent I mean a construct that propagates only above a local population density and is expected to disappear once suppression drives the population below that threshold; by locally confinable I mean a design intended to limit spread beyond the target population. The protocol also includes a countermeasure strain held in reserve, engineered to spread through and dilute the original construct if something goes wrong, along with a binding commitment to two decades of post-release monitoring. My central argument is that the board is applying reversibility asymmetrically. They treat the poisoning status quo as the cautious option because no gene is released, but a poisoned non-target bird is not recovered, and mouse populations on such islands often rebound from survivors or reinvasion; on my view the real choice is between two interventions with different failure modes, not between intervention and non-intervention. The board is mostly field-based conservation biologists who have run successful eradications themselves and are rightly proud of that record, plus two population geneticists who model drive spread, and the chair is a former regulator with strongly precautionary instincts; the field people in particular tend to regard molecular tools as a laboratory indulgence that would erode public trust in the field methods they have spent careers perfecting.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet rather than a further defense of the position. I want the six to eight objections I am actually likely to get from this group, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is, phrased the way these specific people would phrase them, and attributed to the kind of board member who would raise it. For each objection, give me a two-minute reply and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Also tell me what to concede up front, which one reply has to land for the proposal to survive, and what the question I am hoping nobody asks is.
