I am a novelist, and the underlying logic of my next book has convinced me of a thesis that may be too pessimistic. The thesis, sometimes called the unilateralist's curse, is this: when several well-intentioned agents each possess the power to unilaterally initiate an irreversible action whose value depends on disputed empirical or moral judgments, adding more agents with heterogeneous views makes the group more likely, not less likely, to take a harmful action. Under the model, the chance that at least one actor will wrongly judge the action net-beneficial rises with the number of actors, so a decentralized group can be more dangerous than any of its members would be acting alone. The broader claim I have drawn from this is that epistemic diversity plus individual veto power can turn more information into more danger, and that consensus requirements or strong norms against unilateral action are generally safer.

I want to know what would count against this position. I am not asking for objections in the abstract, but for a structured map of its exposure. Specifically:

What would have to be true for the unilateralist's curse to be false, or at least much weaker than I have taken it to be? Which formal assumptions of the model are load-bearing, and what would the world have to look like for each to fail? Are there institutional or social mechanisms that reliably break the logic in practice, and have any real or fictional cases demonstrated that a decentralized group can be safer than its individual members? I would also like to know which of these escape routes a committed defender of the thesis would themselves concede as counting against it, and which they would dismiss as merely showing that the curse can be managed, not that it is false.

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the thesis as I have stated it risks being insulated into unfalsifiability. I want this because my plot turns on whether a decentralized network of scientists, each able to release a climate intervention on their own authority, is genuinely more dangerous than a single decision-maker or a consensus rule would be. I would rather have the novel's logic broken than write a thesis in disguise.
