I'm compiling a list of the basic mechanisms by which multipolar traps arise — situations where several actors each respond rationally to local incentives and the result is an outcome all of them would prefer to avoid. So far I have: (1) common-pool resource depletion, where an unowned or weakly governed resource is overused because each user captures the full benefit of use while the cost is shared; (2) arms-race dynamics, where each actor's investment is made to keep up with or stay ahead of rivals, so the investments cancel out but the costs remain; (3) coordination failure around a mutually preferred equilibrium, as in assurance games where everyone would switch if confident the others would, but no one moves first; (4) positional competition, where agents spend on goods whose value is purely relative, leaving the ranking unchanged while imposing real costs on all; (5) selection effects, where competition filters for traits or strategies that help an agent survive the contest but damage the collective outcome; and (6) short time horizons with deferred costs, where decision-makers capture benefits now while harms fall on future others or on their own later selves. I want a seventh mechanism that is not on this list and not a restatement, special case, or combination of any of these — one with a genuinely different underlying structure. Give me the mechanism, explain how it differs from whichever listed items it resembles, and tell me honestly whether it is a real addition to the list or just a relabeling of something already there.
