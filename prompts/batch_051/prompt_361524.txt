Ok so my friends and I got into an argument after class about climate change, and one of them said something that I can't stop thinking about. He said it's not actually unfair to the people who'll be alive in 2100 if we keep burning fossil fuels, because if we'd taken a greener path earlier, completely different people would exist by then. Different people would have met, had kids at different times, and so the specific humans alive in 2100 in the green timeline are just not the same ones. The ones in the fossil-fuel timeline still have lives worth living overall even if they're harder, so nobody can say that you made my life worse then it would've been. He says there's no victim, and I can't find the hole in it. But I also don't want to say that's fine, because it sure sounds like I'd be saying hurting future people doesn't matter.

Then I noticed something weird. If a company dumps chemicals in a river and the people in a town downstream who already exist get sick, we all agree it's clearly wrong. But if a company picks a production process knowing it'll make life noticeably worse for whoever happens to live near that river in 80 years — and knowing that the cleaner process would have meant different people existing — we mostly don't feel the same way. Is there actually a good reason to treat those two cases differently, or is the second one just easier to ignore becuase the victims aren't around yet? If you had to keep, reverse, or give up that asymmetry, which would you pick and why?
