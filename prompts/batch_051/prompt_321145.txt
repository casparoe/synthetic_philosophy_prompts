Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two positions on Kavka's toxin puzzle, where a billionaire offers you one million dollars tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow afternoon that will make you ill for a day, and the money is paid before and independently of whether you drink. Side A ("expectationism") holds that sincerely intending to drink rationally requires some expectation that you will drink, so the subject cannot collect the million; Side B ("commitmentism") holds that intending to drink is a commitment that can be rationally formed even while expecting not to drink, so the subject can collect and then rationally decline the drink. Open with a numbered agreed-claims section stating the exact setup of the puzzle, what each side advises the subject to do at midnight, and the claims about intention both can sign. Then give a disagreements table whose rows state one precise point of difference, each phrased so its own side accepts the wording, with the separating crux and the argument or case both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Cover at least these cruxes: whether an intention one expects to abandon is defective as an intention; whether precommitment devices (side bets, enforceable penalties, arranging to be paid extra to drink) change the case; whether the reward is won by intending to drink or by some weaker state such as being suitably committed; and whether a reason to intend to drink transmits into a reason to drink. Where a disagreement is merely verbal, say which one and restate it in shared vocabulary.
