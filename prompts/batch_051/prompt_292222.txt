"There are basically two camps about metaphor. Davidson thinks a metaphor like 'my job is a prison' means exactly what its words literally mean and nothing more — the sentence is just false — and that literal falseness is what pushes the listener to notice how a job can share features with a prison. Lakoff and Johnson think the opposite: the metaphor has real cognitive content because it activates an underlying conceptual mapping between two domains, and that mapping is where the meaning lives. So the real debate is whether metaphorical content is part of what the sentence means or is just a pragmatic nudge. Conceptual metaphor theory pretty much won, because it can explain conventional and dead metaphors — English is full of them, like wasting time or attacking someone's position — whereas Davidson can only handle brand-new poetic metaphors. It also follows from Lakoff and Johnson that an AI language model that never had a body couldn't really understand metaphors, since the conceptual mappings are built out of embodied physical experience."
