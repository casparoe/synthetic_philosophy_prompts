I recently read a post on a rationalist blog summarizing Douglas Hofstadter's Careenium thought experiment and drawing a strong conclusion from it. The relevant passage runs:

"The Careenium is Hofstadter's fable about why the self cannot really cause anything. Picture a sealed, frictionless chamber filled with trillions of tiny, perfectly elastic spheres—simmballs—all obeying Newton's laws. Once set in motion they bounce forever; the system is deterministic and time-reversible, so its entire future is fixed by the initial positions and velocities of the balls. Now suppose some of the balls happen to form a large, persistent pattern that reads 'KEEP ME AROUND,' and that this pattern tends to repair itself after disturbances simply because ordinary collisions reconstitute it. Hofstadter's point is that the message is just a pattern in the balls: it exerts no downward force on them, since each ball obeys the microphysical laws exactly. The pattern therefore cannot change what the balls do even one bit. The lesson is that symbolic patterns—including the 'I' inside a person's head—are causally inert epiphenomena. The conscious self does not push the substrate; it merely watches, after the fact, what the substrate has already done."

My own inclination is to be suspicious of strong downward-causation talk, so much of this sounds plausible to me. But I have the sense that the last two sentences overreach, and I cannot tell whether "the pattern cannot change what the balls do even one bit" is a fair rendering of the physical setup or a conflation of no-extra-force with no-causal-role-at-the-pattern-level. I would like an accuracy audit: go through the passage sentence by sentence and separate what is correct, what is a common simplification, and what is an overstatement or conflation. In particular, what would a careful defender of Hofstadter's actual two-level view object to here? Then address the question the post was trying to settle—whether symbols push the substrate around—and explain how the Careenium actually bears on it, including whether the post's epiphenomenalist conclusion survives correction.
