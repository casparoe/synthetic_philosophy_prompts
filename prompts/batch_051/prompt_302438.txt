You are an expert philosopher of mathematics specializing in constructive logic and the proof-theoretic interpretation of the quantifiers. Think carefully, and begin by briefly restating the criteria in your own words before you apply them.

Consider the following proposed test for whether a proof counts as constructive:

(C1) An existence claim of the form ∃nP(n) is established only by constructing a particular number n and proving that P(n) holds.
(C2) A disjunction A ∨ B is established only by proving one of the two disjuncts.
(C3) The proof nowhere appeals to the unrestricted validity of the law of excluded middle or double-negation elimination, though appeals to excluded middle over a decidable predicate with an explicit decision procedure are permitted.

Now consider the disputed principle known as Markov's principle, restricted to arithmetic. Let P be a decidable property of natural numbers: for every n, there is an effective procedure that decides whether P(n) holds. Markov's principle says that if the assumption that every natural number fails P leads to a contradiction, then there exists a natural number satisfying P. More formally: from ¬∀n ¬P(n), infer ∃n P(n), for decidable P.

Imagine an existence proof that works in exactly this way: it assumes that no witness exists, derives a contradiction from that assumption, and concludes that a witness must exist after all, where the predicate P in question is decidable.

Does this proof count as constructive according to the proposed test? Go through C1–C3 one by one. In evaluating C1, address this specifically: the proof does not display a particular witness in advance, but because P is decidable it licenses the algorithm "test n = 0, 1, 2, ... until P(n) holds," and the earlier contradiction guarantees that this search terminates. Is that algorithm a construction of a witness? In evaluating C3, address whether the move from "no witness leads to a contradiction" to "there is a witness" is really just excluded middle or double-negation elimination, or whether it is a distinct principle the test has failed to name. Finally, say whether the criteria themselves need to be revised or supplemented in order to handle Markov's principle, and if so, how.
