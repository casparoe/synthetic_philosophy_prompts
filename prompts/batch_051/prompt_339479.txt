I'm writing something on orphan works and abandoned software, and I keep using the word "abandoned" for a bunch of cases that probably aren't all one thing. I'd like a working definition of "abandoned" — applied to a created thing (a novel, a codebase, a photograph, a song) where the practical question is whether the owner's claim has lapsed enough that someone else can use, copy, or build on the thing without tracking her down. I've got a list of cases that are giving me trouble; please run whatever definition you propose through all of them, say where it gives the wrong answer or no answer, and then either fix the definition or defend the surprising verdict.

1. A novel from the late 1970s, still in copyright, whose author died decades ago and whose heirs can't be found after a genuinely diligent search.
2. A GitHub repo with no commits in six years and no LICENSE file; the maintainer doesn't respond to issues.
3. A developer who posts a notice saying the project is dead and she won't maintain it anymore, but leaves the MIT-licensed code up on GitHub.
4. A house whose owner hasn't visited in more than a decade and has stopped paying property tax.
5. A personal website that now 404s because the owner stopped paying for hosting; the source files are presumably still on her laptop.
6. A half-finished manuscript found in a drawer after the writer's death, with no instructions either way about what to do with it.
7. A domain name that lapsed and is now registered by someone else who is just parking it.
8. A repo whose README says it's in maintenance mode, critical bug fixes only.
9. A commercial font from a design studio that went defunct twenty years ago; nobody has shipped an update or answered a support email since.
10. A photo on a long-inactive Flickr account with an "all rights reserved" badge still on it.

I don't want a survey of possible criteria. Commit to one definition (or a two-part definition if you think the word is doing two jobs, but then tell me which part does the real normative work), run it through every case, and tell me which verdict you're least confident about and why.
