ok so ive been trying to get a version of negative utilitarianism to work without the usual blow-up-the-world conclusions, and the version i keep landing on is a lexical-threshold one. the idea: draw a line on the intensity scale, around torture-level or anything that makes life not worth living while it happens. above the line, value is non-archimedean: any amount of above-threshold suffering, however brief, lexically outweighs any amount of below-threshold suffering or positive welfare. below the line, pain and pleasure trade off normally, more or less. that gives me a clean decision rule (prevent all above-threshold suffering first, then optimize below the line) and it blocks the world-exploder for populations whose suffering is all below the line. i find the arguments for it honestly pretty compelling.

but then i hit the headache case. compare one person being tortured for ten minutes with a billion people each getting a a moderate tension headache for a week. i have a very firm, pre-reflective reaction that the billion headaches are worse. not just sadder: worse, in exactly the sense the theory says they can't be. so either the intuition is a scope-insensitivity artefact, or the non-archimedean move is doing too much work, or both. i dont want the standard bite-the-bullet-or-drop-the-theory answer.

give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion. point one: the strongest case for letting the theory overrule the intuition. point two: the strongest case for letting the intuition overrule the theory. point three: whether there is a reading of worse on which both survive, and how stable that reading is. point four: the cheapest concrete revision to the lexical-threshold view that keeps what i liked about it, and what that revision gives up. point five: which one yields on reflection, and what the move costs.
