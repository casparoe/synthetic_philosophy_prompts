I have been trying to think clearly about an idea I associate with Douglas Hofstadter: that a self is a pattern of perception, habit, and response, and that after someone dies, parts of that pattern can genuinely persist in the brains of the people who knew them well. A friend lost her mother last year and wants to insist on something stronger than the ordinary account: she says her mother is not merely remembered, but that her mother's self is literally distributed across her and her uncle. What would it take to test that stronger claim, even partially? Design the best concrete test you can, distinguish what a positive result would show from what it would not, and say which part of the claim no observation could settle even in principle.
