I facilitate a non-credit philosophy group for working adults at my local library, and I have been asked to write this term’s take-home assessment. I am not an academic, but I have been thinking on my own for some time about the logic of ‘what if everyone did that?’, mainly by comparing Kant with R. M. Hare and Peter Singer. I want the assessment to catch people who can only recite the standard Kantian explanation and cannot see where Hare and Singer genuinely change the question.

Please draft one 90-minute essay question for this group, anchored by the passage from Kant’s Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals where he discusses a lying promise. In my own words, the passage runs as follows: someone borrows money while knowing he cannot repay, and his maxim is, in effect, that whenever he believes he is short of money he will borrow and promise to repay even though he knows he will not. Kant says that if this maxim became a universal law of nature, the very practice of promising would collapse, because no one would trust a promise, and so the maxim could not even be conceived as a universal law. The question should not simply ask for a summary of that passage. It should make students confront whether R. M. Hare’s universal prescriptivism, which treats moral judgement as prescribing that everyone should act in the same way in relevantly similar circumstances, and Peter Singer’s argument that ethics requires taking a point of view from which one’s own interests count as no more important than the like interests of others, actually capture the force of Kant’s test. It should also ask whether either Hare or Singer takes over a hidden Kantian assumption that they have not earned, such as that a moral judgement must be universal in form rather than merely impartial in application.

Then give a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points, each worth four marks for a total of 20, plus a one-line note for each point on the characteristic mistake students make. The model answer and marking scheme should be written in British English.
