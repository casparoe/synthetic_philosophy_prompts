Write a dialogue between a total utilitarian and a person-affecting theorist about whether there's a moral reason to create more happy lives. Make it feel like an actual argument, not two people taking turns giving mini-lectures. The total utilitarian accepts the repugnant conclusion and will defend it: a huge population of lives that are just barely worth living is better than a much smaller population of very happy lives, because total welfare is what matters. The person-affecting theorist holds a procreation asymmetry: it's bad to create a child whose life will be miserable, but it isn't bad, at least not for the childs sake, to fail to create a child whose life would be happy. They should respond to each other's actual moves. The person-affecting theorist should press the total utilitarian on whether you can really add up barely-positive lives until they outweigh a smaller number of wonderful ones, and the total utilitarian should press the person-affecting theorist on whether the asymmetry survives once you picture the specific child who would be fine and loved if only the parents had them. Each character should concede at least one real cost or awkward implication of their own view, and neither should come off as a strawman.
