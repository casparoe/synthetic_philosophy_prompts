I work on the staff of a national bioethics council and have been asked to think through a proposed change to our procedure before it goes to a vote. The proposal is this. Whenever a council member, staff report, or invited witness invokes human dignity as a ground for recommending or rejecting a policy — a ban on markets for organs, a right to assisted dying, a moratorium on heritable genome editing, or anything similar — the speaker must at the same time state which more specific norm or interest the dignity claim is tracking: individual autonomy, bodily integrity, equal standing, freedom from degrading treatment, a duty not to commodity persons, or some other candidate to be named. If the speaker cannot supply such a specification on request, the claim is not weighed in the council's deliberation, and the record notes that it was set aside. The council is not asserting that dignity is empty; it means to run the rule as a multi-year discipline to see whether dignity ever does work that resists restatement in more specific terms, and whether forcing the restatement changes the balance of reasons in hard cases.

I would like an analysis of how this rule would actually behave, not whether it is a good idea in the abstract. What incentives does it create for council members, for advocacy organizations that submit testimony, and for courts and legislatures that later cite the council's opinions? How would sophisticated actors game it — for instance, by relabeling dignity arguments as autonomy or equality arguments — and would that relabeling be harmless paraphrase or would it silently change outcomes? Are there recognizable cases where dignity might be doing independent work — degrading treatment, treatment of the dead, the limits of valid consent, the symbolic dimension of punishment — that the rule would systematically mishandle, and could the rule be amended to capture them without becoming toothless? Is the council's premise coherent: can an institution run this experiment without already having resolved whether dignity is basic or derivative?

Finally, say what would most likely change your mind about the rule's likely effects, and where your reasoning is most fragile.
