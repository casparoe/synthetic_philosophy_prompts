For not every one who says a false thing lies, if he believes or opines that to be true which he says.

For from the sense of his own mind, not from the verity or falsity of the things themselves, is he to be judged to lie or not to lie.

Then he offered this gloss: "Augustine defines lying as saying something false with the intention to deceive. So if you state a falsehood you honestly believe, you haven't lied, but if you state a truth while trying to mislead someone, that's still a lie because of the intent. He then spends the book classifying lies into eight kinds, from malicious lies down to lies told in jest, and argues that all of them are sins, so a Christian is never permitted to lie even to save someone's life."
