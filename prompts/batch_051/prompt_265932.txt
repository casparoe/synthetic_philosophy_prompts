I was a forest ecologist for thirty-five years — early successional plots, bark beetle outbreaks, that sort of thing — and I've been retired for a while now, reading philosophy instead. Lately I've gotten interested in the debate about the net welfare of ecosystems, specifically the argument that habitat destruction reduces wild animal suffering. The argument, as I understand it, goes roughly: clearing a habitat, say a patch of mature forest, prevents an enormous number of future wild animal lives; since, on that view, most of those lives would have contained more suffering than happiness, the clearing reduces total suffering, so from a suffering-focused perspective it might be morally neutral or even good. I'll bracket the value theory — whether a short painful life is better than never having existed, and so on. The part that bothers me is the ecology. When you clear a mature forest and then just leave the land alone, you do not get a low-population system. You get early successional habitat, and within a few seasons it is densely occupied by r-selected species (the fast-breeding, high-mortality types), like rodents, grasshoppers, aphids, and eventually deer. The total number of individual animals often goes up, not down, compared with the mature forest, and those lives look every bit as full of starvation, predation and failed reproduction — the juveniles especially die in huge numbers. So I'm trying to understand wether this observation actually undercuts the argument, and if so which premise it bites on: the empirical premise that there are fewer future lives, or the welfare premise that the lives which do occur are mostly suffering? Could the argument be repaired into something weaker but still worth keeping — for instance that habitat destruction changes the composition of suffering in an ecosystem rather than the total amount of it — or does the r-selected boom kill the original claim outright? And am I being unfair by thinking of abandoned clearing when the argument may have been about permanent conversion to pavement or row crops?
