I've gradually come around to the view that we should engage in moral trade a lot more than we do. Not compromise exactly: I mean the deliberate swap where I do something you think is good and I think is mildly bad, in exchange for you doing something I think is very good and you think is mildly bad, so that by our own seperate lights we both think the world ends up better. The standard cases are stuff like the vegetarian buying meat for a dinner guest, or two people giving to each other's top charity when each privately thinks the other's charity is mediocre. I don't mean lying to yourself about what's right, and I don't mean excusing serious wrongdoing. But I've become convinced that a lot of the resistance is really a bundled-up sense of purity rather than a response to what matters. Tell me what actually counts against this. I want the defeaters: which cases, intuitions, and results in nearby areas should make me think moral trade is wrong, useless, or much narrower than I claimed. Which of these would a committed moral trader themselves concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as misplaced? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. And I'd like an honest answer to a meta-question: once the view is properly hedged, does it risk anything at all, and if it's unfalsifiable, does that itself defeat it?
