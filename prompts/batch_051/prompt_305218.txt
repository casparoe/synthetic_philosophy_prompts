Analyse the 1975 Australian constitutional crisis as a case study in the concept of a constitutional gap — the condition in which the formal rules run out and someone must nevertheless exercise authority. The salient facts: the Whitlam government retained a majority in the House of Representatives, but the opposition-controlled Senate repeatedly refused to pass supply, the appropriation bills without which a government cannot lawfully spend; on 11 November 1975 the Governor-General, Sir John Kerr, dismissed Whitlam and commissioned the Opposition Leader, Malcolm Fraser, as caretaker Prime Minister on the understanding that Fraser would secure supply and advise an immediate double-dissolution election, which Fraser then won decisively. Please give a disciplined mapping of the crisis through that concept — the relevant actors, which constraints on them were written rules and which were unwritten conventions, where exactly the rules fell silent, and what the concept implies about who ought to have governed in the interval. Then say what the concept explains about the episode and what it misses or misdescribes: was this a genuine gap or a collision between competing conventions, and did the resolution rest on legal norms or on something else, such as the Governor-General's judgement and the voters' retrospective endorsement? Finally, say what the episode shows about the concept — whether constitutional gaps are in fact always already filled by unwritten norms, or whether the gap can be closed only ex post, and what your answer implies for the broader question of who governs when the rules run out.
