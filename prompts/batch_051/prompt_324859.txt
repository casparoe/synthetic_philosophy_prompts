I'm cataloguing the main live solutions to the Frege-Geach problem for expressivism, and I'm after one that isn't on my list — not a rewording or special case or combination of things on it. The ones I already have: Blackburn's quasi-realist solution, where the logic of moral language comes from higher-order attitudes (disapproving of certain combinations of attitudes etc.); Gibbard's norm-expressivism, where judgments express acceptance of norms and embeddings are handled by evaluating them against complete, internally coherent norm-systems; Schroeder's being-for solution, where every attitude expressed by a moral sentence is treated as one general attitude of being for, so the embeddings are supposed to compose; and hybrid expressivism, where moral sentences express both a belief-like state and a desire-like state, with the belief-state doing the embedding work. I'm not counting the old prescriptivist line here. Give me a candidate that is not on this list and whose basic architecture is different from these four — no "hybrid of Gibbard and Schroeder" type moves — and then tell me honestly whether it actualy solves the Frege-Geach problem or just pushes it somewhere else.
