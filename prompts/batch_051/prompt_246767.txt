I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I would like an audit of an analogy that has become load-bearing in my team's discussions. A senior engineer has argued that a black-box optimizer's objective and search space can be recovered from its behavior the way an economist recovers a consumer's utility function from her observed purchases. If a shopper could have bought any bundle within her budget and chose the one she did, that choice reveals something about her ranking; across enough prices and budgets, the revealed choices allow the economist to reconstruct a stable utility function that rationalizes them. The same, he argues, is true of any optimizer. Put a system in an environment, record which actions it takes when others were available, and with enough observations you can reconstruct both the objective it is maximizing and the search space it is effectively exploring. He leans on this analogy to support a strong practical conclusion: external behavior is sufficient for inferring a system's objective and search space, and looking inside the system or knowing how it was trained is convenient but not necessary for the inference.

I have repeated this argument myself, but I have started to suspect that the shopper analogy imports features of consumers and markets—visible choice sets, stable preferences, choices that maximize a stable ranking—that the target case may not share, and that the conclusion may be resting on those imports rather than on anything about optimizers as such. I cannot tell which disanalogies are fatal and which are harmless. Could you lay out what maps onto what, where the analogy breaks, and which features of the source are quietly assumed in the target? Then say whether the strong conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, whether some weaker version of it survives, and which assumptions would have to be made explicit for any version to remain valid. If the consumer analogy cannot carry the weight, is there a better analogy for what we are actually trying to do, or does the inference need to be rebuilt on explicit assumptions rather than an analogy at all?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
