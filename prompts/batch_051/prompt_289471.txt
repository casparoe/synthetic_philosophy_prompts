I am a philosophy professor working on a short position piece about temperance and self-control, and I would like a consistency audit of the view I have drifted into before I circulate it. Here are the five commitments I find myself holding, stated as plainly as I can.

(a) Temperance (sophrosyne) is principally a matter of desiring well: the temperate person's appetites have been educated or habituated to accord with correct judgment, so that she characteristically acts without an inner fight.
(b) Self-control (enkrateia) is a distinct, genuine, and praiseworthy state, second to temperance: the self-controlled person judges rightly, still feels contrary appetite, and acts against it.
(c) Repression, by which I mean trying to keep an unwanted appetite out of awareness, to refuse to acknowledge it, or to mentally hold it down, is generally a poor strategy: it is plausibly self-defeating, tends toward ironic rebound, and tends to leave the desire intact or intensified.
(d) The sound practical guidance for self-regulation is therefore mostly indirect: restructure one's environment, precommit, build routines and habits, and train attention, so that the moment of struggle rarely arises at all.
(e) A person who succeeds through the indirect strategies in (d) while the contrary appetite persists is genuinely self-controlled in the strict sense introduced in (b), not merely prudent about her weaknesses.

Treat this as an audit rather than a lecture. You are a specialist in ancient Greek ethics and contemporary self-regulation research. Before drafting your answer, work through each pair of commitments and determine which pairs genuinely pull against one another, which only seem to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Do not include this preliminary work.

Then reply with exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Rank the tensions by severity, say explicitly where an apparent tension dissolves, and for each genuine tension state what would have to give.
