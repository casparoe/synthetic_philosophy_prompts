Freegans typically refuse to purchase animal products because doing so supports a system that treats animals as resources, yet many freegans will use animal products that would otherwise be wasted, including eggs from rescued hens. I am trying to understand whether the waste criterion yields a principled distinction rather than an ad hoc exception. If the objection to a commercial egg is that consuming it channels the hen's reproductive capacities into human purposes, does a rescued hen's egg not do the same thing merely because the hen is cared for rather than owned? What are the respective roles of exploitation and mere use in the freegan position, and what principle determines when using an animal byproduct is permissible? In particular, if the hen would not be harmed by the taking of an egg she has already laid, has the freegan quietly shifted from a non-exploitation standard to a welfare standard?
