I'm an engineer who reads philosophy in the evenings, so forgive me if I'm putting this badly or using the wrong words. I've noticed a pattern in my own values and in a couple of people I know, and I don't know wether it has an established name or whether philosophers have properly carved it up. I'd be grateful if you'd think through it properly rather than just giving me a label.

Here are two instances of the thing I mean.

A friend of mine is a composer working on a string quartet she will almost certainly never finish. She's perfectly aware of that, and she says she's fine with it; she doesn't work on it for money or for a premiere. But a while back I asked her this: if she found out that, one day after she died, every human being on earth would vanish, would she still bother with the quartet? She went quiet and said no — it would make the whole project feel hollow and pointless to her right now. And it wasn't about missing the premiere; she'd be dead either way. It was about the work itself somehow no longer being worth doing.

Same thing with a retired oncologist I know. He volunteers in a lab that's developing treatments which won't be ready for ten or fifteen years, well past his life expectancy. He's an atheist and not bothered about his own death at all. But he's told me that if he had convincing evidence that no one would be around after, say, his own death plus a month, it would retroactively drain the point out of what he is doing today — the experiments would feel like an elaborate private joke.

So the phenomenon seems to be this: the value or point of a great deal of present activity depends, not as a useful side-effect but in its very point, on the assumption that there will be human beings after us who can take it up, appreciate it, continue the practice, or simply carry on. Not any particular named individuals — just some indefinite future community. And this dependence is mostly invisible; it only becomes obvious when you imagine the future going dark.

Is there an established name for this specific configuration? I know a few nearby ideas — posthumous harm, legacy, symbolic immortality, generativity — but none of them quite captures the way the present activity's value, not just the agent's welfare or hopes, depends on an actual future audience. If there is a technical term, I want to know what it covers and what it rules out; and if the literature treats this mostly as one corner of something larger, I'd rather understand how to carve the phenomenon at the right joints. Please don't just hand me a word — tell me the boundaries, the nearest concepts and how this differs from each of them, and whether the dependence I've described is really one thing or several different things I've accidentally run together. If nothing established fits, say so and propose a term with a definition and a couple of boundary cases.

You're a professional philosopher working on death, value, and future generations. Please think carefully: start by brainstorming three or four candidate concepts or literatures, then say which fits best and why the others don't.
