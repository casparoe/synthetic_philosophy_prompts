I am writing a paper that needs a clean, purpose-built thought experiment about pre-AGI bargains: agreements made now, before anyone builds a general AI, that are supposed to govern who gets what after one party acquires capabilities that render ordinary legal and reputational enforcement irrelevant. I would like you to construct the case and then verify, step by step, that it satisfies the specifications below.

Let me fix three positions, defined just as I am using them. Promissory continuity: a free, informed agreement about the future division of gains from AGI remains morally binding even after enforcement and reputation cease to operate, because the ground of the obligation is what was promised, not what can be extracted. Enforcement realism: an agreement obligates only insofar as enforcement or reputation will actually constrain the stronger party after the relevant event; if one party will become unaccountable, then the "obligation" is morally empty no matter what was signed. Changed-circumstances discharge: obligations lapse when a fundamental background condition of the agreement fails, unless the parties expressly assumed the risk of that change, in which case the obligation is not frustrated and survives.

Build me a realistic case—a startup, a research lab, or a small treaty arrangement, not an allegory—in which changed-circumstances discharge sides with promissory continuity: both say the stronger party must still perform, while enforcement realism alone says the promise is void. The scenario itself should be no more than two paragraphs, and the agreement must be fair and rational at the time of signing: both parties fully informed, neither coerced, no deception, and no terms a reasonable observer at signing would call unfair. The power shift must be generated by the deal itself or by the project it funds, not by an unrelated stroke of luck, and the shift must be of the kind the agreement explicitly contemplates, so that a changed-circumstances theorist cannot simply call it frustrated. No magical enforcement: no assumption that the AGI will be aligned with the contract, no surviving reputation mechanism, and no escrow or asset the powerful party cannot expropriate. The case is for a paper whose aim is to separate the ground of obligation—the promise—from the machinery of enforceability, so it matters that the verdicts fall out of the three positions as I have defined them rather than being stipulated.

After the scenario, walk through exactly what each position says and why it gives that verdict. Then tell me which single structural feature of the case makes changed-circumstances discharge side with promissory continuity, and what happens to the verdict pattern if you remove that feature. Finish by explaining how an enforcement realist would attack the case, and tell me what would change your mind about your construction: which consideration, if it turned out to be true, would show that the case does not actually deliver the specified verdicts, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
