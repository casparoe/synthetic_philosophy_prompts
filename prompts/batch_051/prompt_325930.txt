I keep coming back to a line from Emily M. Bender and Alexander Koller’s paper Climbing towards NLU: On Meaning, Form, and Understanding in the Age of Data. They write that a system trained only on form has a priori no way to learn meaning. That seems to settle it: if these models see only text, then no matter how much they improve, they can’t be said to understand. So why do serious researchers still talk about the question whether LLMs understand as if it were open, and why do people keep designing tests for “understanding” rather than treating these systems as pure pattern-extraction machines? If the argument really is a priori, shouldn’t it rule out all future text-trained systems too, not just the ones we have now?

When you answer, please separate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don’t present one side’s position as the consensus if it isn’t.
