I've been sitting in on a metaphysics seminar on persistence through time, and one analogy has been doing a great deal of argumentative work, so I want it audited properly. The analogy, roughly, is that an object persists through time in much the same way that a road runs across space. A road is not wholly present at any one point along its length; rather, it has spatial parts—the stretch outside my window, the stretch over the bridge—and it is the whole road only in virtue of all those parts taken together. In the same way, the analogy says, a person is spread out across time: I have temporal parts (me-yesterday, me-today, me-tomorrow), and I am the whole person only in virtue of all of them taken together. The seminar instructor uses this to argue that anyone who freely accepts spatial parts should equally accept temporal parts, and therefore should accept perdurantism—the view that things persist by having temporal parts and are never wholly present at a single moment—over endurantism, the view that a thing is wholly present at each moment at which it exists.

Please act as a professional metaphysician and audit this analogy with care. I suspect it may be smuggling in the very conclusion it is meant to support. Give me, first, an explicit mapping between source and target: which element of the road corresponds to which element of the persisting object, including spatial part versus temporal part, spatial extension versus temporal duration, and the way the whole relates to its parts. Then identify the main disanalogies—for example, the privileged direction of time, the fact that spatial parts coexist while temporal parts do not, whether we perceive and count temporal parts the way we perceive and count spatial parts, and the extent to which the analogy already presupposes a four-dimensional or eternalist picture of time. For each disanalogy, say whether it is dialectically damaging to the argument from spatial analogy or merely a surface difference that does not matter. Finally, tell me whether the perdurantist conclusion survives once the disanalogies are set out, whether the analogy is carrying the whole argument by itself, and whether there is a better analogy a perdurantist should have used instead. Please think through the issues step by step rather than just reporting the standard labels.
