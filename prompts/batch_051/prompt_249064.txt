I serve on the Professional Conduct Committee of a mid-sized scholarly society in the behavioral sciences, and we must decide what, if anything, to do about a fellow of the society, Dr. M., whose public standing has collapsed over the past year. On a semi-public online forum and later on her own account, Dr. M. posted a long series of contemptuous remarks about an adjacent subfield: she called its standard methods pseudo-empirical, suggested that its leading journals publish work unlikely to survive replication, and named two junior researchers as examples of the field's low standards. The posts circulated widely. A podcast appearance and a book event were cancelled, her university issued a statement distancing itself from the remarks, and a petition with several thousand signatures asked our society to strip her of her fellowship. Within the society she has been excluded from our online forums, and several members now decline to collaborate with her. She has submitted a written apology and asked us to close the matter without formal action, on the ground that she has already been punished enough.

The committee is split. Some members want to issue a formal censure and suspend her conference privileges for the next two annual meetings, arguing that only an official finding can mark the society's rejection of her conduct and deter further public disparagement of colleagues. Others regard any further sanction as piling on, since she has already lost opportunities, collaborations, and standing far beyond anything the society itself would impose. There is also a second-order disagreement: if the informal penalties she has already suffered count as punishment, then allowing them to reduce our own penalty seems to let an unaccountable crowd set the sentence, but refusing to count them seems to double it. Our rules require sanctions to be proportionate, but they are silent on this question.

Please draft a memo for the committee that states a clear recommendation and the reasoning for it. The memo should treat the case as an instance of the larger problem of whether deplatforming, ostracism, and reputational damage should be understood as forms of punishment, and it should give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
