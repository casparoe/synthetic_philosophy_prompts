My friend runs a small blog where she tags historical claims either "pseudohistory" or "live question, maybe wrong." I've kept a list of her calls and now I want to predict a new one.

Here's the record. She says Atlantis taken as a real sunken continent with advanced technology is pseudohistory, especially the versions where geologists are ignoring the evidence. She says the ancient-aliens-built-the-pyramids stuff is pseudohistory—at least the version where the pyramids are far older than Egyptologists allow and the whole field is covering that up. She says Fomenko's New Chronology, the one where all of ancient history was stitched together by early modern chronologists, is pseudohistory. She also says the Phantom Time Hypothesis, where a few centuries of the early Middle Ages are a deliberate forgery, is pseudohistory. But she's refused to label the idea that Polynesians reached South America before Columbus as pseudohistory; she says it's a real but weak archaeological question. And she accepts the Norse settlement at L'Anse aux Meadows as plain history.

Now someone has asked her whether the Shakespeare authorship question counts as pseudohistory—the version where someone else really wrote the plays and the academic establishment has quietly supressed the fact. What do you think she'll say, and why? I'm trying to work out what principle actually drives her calls, because I can see at least two different principles that would each fit her record so far. If you can lay those principles out, say what each one predicts about the Shakespeare case, and tell me which further case would most cleanly force a choice between them, that's what I'm after.
