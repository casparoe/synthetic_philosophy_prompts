I cover science and ideas, and I'm working on a feature about whether structured argumentation could be organized, ranked, and scored as a spectator sport in the way that chess or esports are. Write a dialogue for that feature between two equally sophisticated interlocutors: an advocate who argues that formal competitive argument leagues would improve public reasoning, and a critic who argues that they would corrupt it. The advocate should develop the view that consistent judging, mandatory engagement with opposing views, and the incentive structure of repeated competition would reward preparation, responsiveness, and intellectual stamina, while giving audiences a clearer comparative picture of rival positions. The critic should develop the view that scored argumentation selects for judge-pleasing and audience capture rather than accuracy, that defending assigned positions weakens the link between argument and belief, and that gamifying dispute would strengthen motivated reasoning. Have the two speakers address each other's strongest points directly rather than talking past one another, and let the dialogue press on the question of whether excellence in adversarial argument can come apart from reliability in tracking the truth.
