I'm a PhD student working on Sellars's critique of the given, and I'd like you to run an argument for me. Please argue that Sellars doesn't actually eliminate the given—he relocates it into his own positive account of observational knowledge as a trained, reliable verbal disposition. The setup: Sellars wants to deny that a non-inferential sensory state can justify a belief, because justification is a normative standing that has to sit inside the space of reasons. But he also wants to preserve observational knowledge; I see a red patch and come to know that it is red, without inferring it from a red sense-datum. His story is that I've been trained into a reliable causal disposition to respond to red things by producing the relevant sentence, and that reliable causal sensitivity is what makes the report observational rather than inferential. The problem is that a reliable disposition, however well trained, is just a non-normative causal fact about me and my environment. If Sellars can let that purely causal fact put an empirical belief in touch with the world, it's not clear why the foundationalist can't say the same about a sensory given—i.e., a non-inferential awareness that is sufficent to justify. What principled difference does Sellars have between the two cases? Please make the strongest version of this objection, defend the conclusion that he has merely moved the given one level down, and then steelman the Sellarsian reply (probably an appeal to the normative status of the report, or to the fact that the disposition is the product of training within a practice) and explain why it doesn't fully work. I don't need citations or secondary literature—just run the argument carefully.
