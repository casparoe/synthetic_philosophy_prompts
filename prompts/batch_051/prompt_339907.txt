So this is bothering me. My university's conduct code has a clause: "A student shall not engage in behavior that creates a substantial risk of serious harm to another person." Last week a senior, done with finals, drove drunk across the pedestrian mall at 2am and hit no one. The only reason no one was there is that a fire alarm went off in the dorm overlooking the mall about ten minutes earlier, so the usual late-night foot traffic had cleared out. He is now charged under that clause. I care less about whether he actually gets punished than about whether the clause reaches him at all: what do the plain words, the likely purpose, and what the drafters would have said if asked each give you? And if the plain words do reach him, does that mean the university has quietly committed itself to rejecting resultant moral luck—treating two identical drunk drivers the same when only one happens to kill somebody—and is that defensible?
