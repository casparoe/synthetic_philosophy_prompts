I'm in a philosophy of psychiatry class and we've been stuck on the two-factor theory of delusions for a couple weeks now, and my friend laid out an argument that's been bugging me because it seems to prove way too much. The background is this: on the two-factor theory, a delusion like Capgras (the belief that your spouse has been replaced by an impostor) requires two things—an anomolous experience (the spouse looks subtly off, or the usual feeling of familiarity is missing) plus a defective reasoning or attribution step that makes the person accept the bizarre explanation for that experience.

My friend's argument goes like this. (1) On the two-factor story, a delusion is just an anomalous experience plus a biased or damaged inference. (2) Ordinary perceptual belief is also experience plus inference, and the inference is always biased by priors, context, mood, and so on. (3) So delusions are beliefs—they're generated in the same basic way as ordinary beliefs, just worse. (4) But a belief is the kind of state that guides action and gets revised when the evidence turns against it. (5) Delusions often don't do either: the Capgras patient says his wife is an impostor but keeps living with her and treating her as his wife, and he doesn't update when shown evidence that she's the same person. (6) So delusions are not beliefs. (7) Therefore delusions are both beliefs and not beliefs. (8) And the very move in (4) through (6) would also rule out a lot of ordinary irrational belief—my uncle's conspiracy theories, people's sports superstitions—since these are just as insensitive to counterevidence and often don't guide action much either.

I don't want to accept that delusions are both beliefs and not beliefs, and I also don't want to say my uncle's conspiracy theories aren't beliefs. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, how do the standard options—doxasticism about delusions, anti-doxasticism, and the two-factor theory itself—each handle this argument, and what bullet does each one have to bite?
