Write an essay on the following controversy. In his 1945 paper Studies in the Logic of Confirmation, Carl Hempel discusses why a white shoe’s confirming the hypothesis that all ravens are black strikes most people as absurd. His explanation is that the apparent absurdity is a psychological by-product of prior knowledge: because we already know the object is a shoe and not a raven, the observation that it is white contributes very little to our rational confidence in the raven hypothesis; the confirmation is genuine but negligible, not paradoxical. Take this passage as the target to interpret and test. Weigh the main diagnoses of the paradox: that Hempel is right and the white shoe is a real but minuscule confirming instance; that the equivalence condition should be rejected; that the Nicod criterion of instance confirmation should be rejected; or that the problem lies in how confirmation is measured. Do not end on a merely neutral survey. Reach a definite verdict about which element of the paradox should be revised or retained, and justify it. Also say how confident you are in your claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
