i've been reading about the absurdity doctrine for a civics essay and i came up with a distinction i want checked. i call it text-absurdity vs world-absurdity. text-absurdity is when the literal wording is broken on its own terms: the sentence says the opposite of what everyone agrees it was supposed to say, or it commands something self-defeating, like a typo that turns a fine into a reward. world-absurdity is when the text itself is fine, but applying it to a weird case gives an insane result, like a law against obstructing mail delivery being applied to a sheriff arresting a mail carrier, or a park rule against vehicles being applied to an ambulance. my view is courts should only ignore plain words for text-absurdity, because world-absurdity is really just the law having a bad consequence in one situation, and fixing that is the legislature's job, not the court's. is that line actually stable, or does it collapse once you poke at it? whats the strongest case against my rule, and is my distinction just a worse version of something that already exists?
