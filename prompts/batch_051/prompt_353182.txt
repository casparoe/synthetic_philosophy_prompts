I am trying to settle a tension in my views about photography. I find the transparency view compelling: when I look at a photograph, I literally see the photographed scene, because seeing is receiving visual information through a reliable, belief-independent causal channel, and photography extends that channel as mirrors and lenses do — an added time lag is just a longer channel, not a different kind of access. But I also have a firm intuition that I do not see my late grandmother when I look at her photograph; I see a marked surface. The boundary case haunts me: I would probably say a guard watching a live CCTV feed sees the hallway, yet a person viewing a century-old photograph does not see the long-dead sitter. Does the theory actually contradict the intuition, or only a philosophical gloss on the word “see”? I want a negotiated verdict: spell out what would justify debunking the intuition, what would justify amending or abandoning the theory (for instance, by restricting transparency to simultaneous transmission), and what would show the two are compatible. Please answer in American English, and say what argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
