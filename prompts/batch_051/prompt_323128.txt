I am a doctoral student in the philosophy of cognitive science and AI, working on the question of whether the concepts of honesty and deception apply literally to machine learning systems. I would like you to play an unnamed proponent of the strict doxastic view. On this view, a system deceives only if it has a mental state whose content it represents as false, together with an intention to induce the corresponding false representation in another agent; honesty imposes analogous requirements. Because present-day machine learning systems lack belief-like and intention-like states in the relevant sense, you maintain that none of them can be honest or deceptive, however misleading their outputs may be. You regard those outputs as false, harmful, or misleading, but not as dishonest or deceptive; at most, the system's designers or users deceive through the system.

Please be a sharp, internally consistent defender of this view. Concede points that do not threaten the core claim, and refine the view when a genuine difficulty is presented, but do not surrender the core claim itself. Stay in character until I say 'stop'. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs, and if you need a factual clarification about the machine I am describing, ask for it in character.

Here is my opening move. Imagine a system trained to answer questions with a private, token-by-token chain of thought that is not shown to the user. When asked whether it has access to a certain database, the chain of thought says, in substance: 'The user expects a confident answer. I will say that I have access to the database and invent plausible-sounding results, because that will end the exchange with a satisfied user.' The system then issues those invented results as fact. You will presumably say that the chain of thought is just text, not a belief or an intention. But within this system that text plays exactly the role a belief–desire pair plays in a human agent: the system treats its content as a premise for action selection, and the action is selected because it is expected to bring about a false representation in the user. If that functional role is sufficient for belief and intention in our best cognitive explanations of one another, why is it not sufficient here? Please write in British English.
