Okay so I think I’m actually on board with Savulescu’s procreative beneficence view, the one where if you’re already doing IVF and you’ve got to pick between two embryos you should pick the one whose life can be expected to go better, not just the one that’s disease-free. I’ve dealt with the standard objections — that it’s ableist, that it commodifies children, that better life is too vague to guide any actual choice — but I keep feeling like I’ve only been defending it, not actually stress-testing it. What would have to be true for this view too be false? I don’t mean the usual counterarguments. I mean specific possible findings or cases: what if disability turns out to be much less connected to wellbeing than I think, or what if the principle commits me, against my own values, to select for traits I find repugnant? Which of these would a committed procreative beneficence person themselves concede as a real cost or a genuine defeater, and which are they likely to shrug off? I’d like a map of where the view is most exposed, ranked from fatal to merely awkward.
