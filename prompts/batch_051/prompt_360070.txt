In my class on equality of opportunity and the family we've been on inheritance taxes for two weeks, and one of the proposals that came up is bugging me. Instead of an estate tax, every gift or inheritance would be taxed only in the hands of the recipient. Each person gets a lifetime allowance of $500,000 — below that you owe nothing, above it you pay ordinary income tax on the amount, plus a "lineage surcharge" that starts at 10% and goes up by 10 points for every earlier generation in your direct family line that already received above-allowance transfers at some point (parents, grandparents, and so on). The pitch is that a first-generation transfer pays very little, but money that keeps moving down a dynastic line gets progresively taxed until the family either earns it again or spreads it out so widely it doesn't matter. I'm supposed to treat it as a mechanism and work out how it actually behaves, not argue for or against the goal. So what would this do in practice? What does it push people to do with their money and their family trees? Which loopholes open up first — generation-skipping, splitting inheritances among lots of heirs to stay under each one's allowance, dressing transfers up as salaries, moving into assets that are hard to value? And what happens to family businesses, a shared house, or a parent who just wants to help a child without creating a "dynasty"? Can the biggest loophole be patched without the whole thing turning back into a regular estate tax?
