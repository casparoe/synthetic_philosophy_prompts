I am a PhD student in the philosophy of religion, but my working habits come from formal causal modelling: I think in structural equations, directed acyclic graphs, interventions, and asymmetric dependence. I would like you to take Plato's Euthyphro, specifically the passage around 10a to 11b in the standard pagination in which Socrates moves through carried things, led things, and loved things, and restate the argument as precisely as you can in the vocabulary of a structural causal model. In that passage, Socrates gets Euthyphro to accept that a thing is carried because someone carries it, not that someone carries it because it is carried; he then applies the same pattern to the gods' love, so that a thing is god-loved because the gods love it, not loved because it is god-loved. This move is what breaks Euthyphro's proposed definition of the pious as what all the gods love.

Now report systematically. Give me the variables, the graph, and the direction of the arrows you would need to capture the argument. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the argument map cleanly onto structural equations, which parts require fudging, and which parts cannot be expressed in this vocabulary at all. In particular, I want to know whether Socrates' because is a causal relation, a grounding relation, or definitional priority, and whether a directed graph can represent the view that holiness is constitutively prior to divine approval rather than caused by it. If the correct model makes the gods' love endogenous to their nature, and that nature is itself the standard, does the structural equation framework still survive, or does it force a false choice between two wrong arrow directions? Finally, say whether the residue is a defect of my framework or evidence that the original argument is less clear than it looked. Please write the answer in British English.
