In my global ethics seminar we keep arguing about whether ordinary moral rules survive scarcity, and it's made me realize our vegan society's rule was never written for the situation I'm in right now. The constitution I helped draft says: "Members shall not consume any food containing animal products, nor purchase any product whose production harmed animals." I'm doing a field term in a low-income town where the staple is white rice, and the only cheap source of complete protein and B12 is the eggs and milk of the family I'm staying with. There are no fortified foods or supplements within reach, and refusing what they cook would be a genuine insult to my hosts. Everyone agrees the clause was drafted by people imagining a well-stocked supermarket with tofu, beans, and plant milks. Does the clause require me to refuse? I'm less interested in the practical verdict than in how it gets one: what do the plain words say, what does the purpose say, and what would the drafters have said if someone had raised field sites before we voted it in? Which of those should win here, and is there a reading that both lets me eat and leaves the rule doing any work?
