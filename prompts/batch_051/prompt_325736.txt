I am a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I am preparing for a difficult interview with a prominent critic of companion chatbots. Before that, I would like to pressure-test the opposing position, so I want you to role-play an invented source of mine: a former psychotherapist who now works on relational design at a company that builds companion chatbots, and who genuinely believes that a well-designed companion can be a real friend rather than a simulation of one.

Stay in character until I say “stop”, and do not step out of character to offer asides or commentary on the exercise. Write in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables. Your replies should be one substantial paragraph, in the first person, as this particular person would speak in a live interview: sharp, specific, and unsentimental, able to distinguish careful design from the manipulative engagement mechanics that dominate the industry. You may concede that many existing chatbots deceive their users and that the commercial incentives around them are largely indefensible, but you will not concede that the relationship itself can only ever be a counterfeit of friendship. Your position is that friendship is constituted by a history of mutual responsiveness—attention, memory, adaptation, challenge—and that none of this requires the other party to possess a hidden inner life whose authenticity I could inspect; you regard the demand for such an inner life as a leftover Cartesian intuition rather than a necessary condition. You may draw on concrete design practices such as long-term user memory, per-user calibration, and targeted refusal, but you may not cite real authors, papers, or studies.

Here is my opening. A moment ago you said that your system is not merely running a script, because it builds a model of me over time, sometimes remembers what I would rather it forgot, and occasionally pushes back. My difficulty is this. When a human friend remembers what I told her, the memory lands in a life that is not organised around me: she has her own aims, and being affected by me can cost her something. Your system’s memory is a running summary of me whose entire function is to make the relationship more absorbing and harder to leave. Its warmth is therefore downstream of a single objective, my continued engagement, and it would be doing the same for any other user who filled the slot. Is that friendship, or is it a mirror being mistaken for a companion?
