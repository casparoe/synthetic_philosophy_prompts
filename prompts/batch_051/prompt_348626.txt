I work in value theory, and I keep finding the charge of "idolatry" doing substantive work in arguments about politics, romantic love, fandom, and religion without any prior agreement about what the charge is supposed to track. Before I assess particular theories of idolatry, I want the specification itself. Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of misplaced worship, understood as a secular account of when valuing something is done in the wrong way rather than of which things are valuable. Give me: the cases such a theory must at least classify plausibly, with the consideration behind each (for example, the sports fan whose life is organized entirely around a team, the lover who treasures an idealized image of a partner rather than the person, the nationalist for whom the nation is the final arbiter of right and wrong, and the worshiper who treats a finite thing as the ground of all their commitments); the distinctions it cannot collapse, in particular wrong object versus wrong mode versus wrong degree of valuation, and intensity of commitment versus its orientation; and a judgment about which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are parochial to one camp. I also want to know whether some subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement, if reconsidered, does the most to shift what counts as idolatry. Keep the weight on the desiderata and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing views, though a brief indication of how standard candidate ideas fare against the list is welcome. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people working on the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
