I'm a novelist and I've been using one of those editing tools on my current draft—the kind that claims it can tell you when a passage "means the same thing" as something in ten thousand other books. The documentation says it converts every sentence into a point in a high-dimensional vector space and then measures how close the points are, and close equals same meaning. So last week it flagged a sentence where my character walks out of her apartment, because it's apparently too near some passage in a novel where a woman is leaving her marriage. Close in the space, sure, but I was writing about a door and a hallway; the other guy was writing about a whole relationship ending. The tool doesn't care. It's telling me the two are "the same idea" and suggesting I cut mine. It also marks lines as cliches without ever saying what a cliche is in vector terms.

I want to get straight what that software is committed to, underneath the hood. What view of meaning or semantic similarity does treating texts as points in a space like that presuppose? Is it honestly just "meaning is use" in a new outfit, or is it something more specific—position and distance doing all the work, with reference, imagery, and authorial intention left out? And if I dont buy that view for my own writing, what would I have to change about how I use the tool—can I still trust its flags for some things, like repeated phrasing, but not for metaphor or theme? Be blunt. I'd rather have the philosophy than the marketing pitch.
