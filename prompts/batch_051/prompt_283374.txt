Struggling with something I'd love a second brain on. Hobbes thinks geometry is the one science we can actually have, because we construct the figures ourselves — we make the causes, so we can know the effects. He leans on this so hard that he denies the new algebraic methods are real geometry and spends years claiming he's squared the circle with compass and straightedge. So here's what I'm stuck on: what would Hobbes have made of non-Euclidean geometry, or even more, of Hilbert-style axiomatics where points and lines are just undefined terms that satisfy some postulates? On his own principles he'd maybe have to accept them, since he thought definitions are arbitrary and the rest just follows, but those geometries are exactly the ones where you can't draw the object in any straightforward way, which seems to undercut his whole we-know-it-because-we-make-it story. I keep flip-flopping on wether his constructivism has the resources to handle formalist geometry, or whether he'd just call it empty word-play and go back to his compass. Curious how you'd predict him.
