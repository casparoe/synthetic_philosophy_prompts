i have a rule for what I'll call a disease and it keeps getting me into arguments. when someone announces that some condition is a disease now, i ask whether there's a part or process in the body that's malfunctioning in a way I could describe without mentioning anyone's values — a valve that doesn't close, an enzyme that doesn't get produced, a tissue that's disorganized. if the only evidence is that the person hurts, or that people treat them badly, or that they're statistically unusual, I'll say that can be a serious problem but its not a disease in the strict sense; at most it's a mismatch between a person and an environment, or society being cruel. I know I'm staking out the naturalist side of the naturalism-vs-normativism debate about health, and I like that my rule stops 'disease' from becoming a medical-sounding term for 'condition I disapprove of.' but the more I use it, the more the crack shows. to say a valve 'doesn't close' counts as a malfunction i need an idea of what the valve is supposed to do, and natural selection doesn't hand out purposes. and when I pick which reference class to compare against — species-typical, age-typical, context-typical — I've already made decisions that look value-laden. am I actually being a naturalist, or just a normativist with extra steps? and if my rule as stated is indefensible, is there a patch that keeps what's good about it without the pretense, or should I admit that drawing the line around 'disease' is always partly a social act?
