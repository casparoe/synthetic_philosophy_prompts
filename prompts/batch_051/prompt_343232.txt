I've been reading back and forth on the self-sampling assumption versus the self-indication assumption and I'm struck by how stuck the whole debate feels. The positions are easy to state: SSA says reason as if you're randomly selected from the observers in some reference class; SIA says the fact you exist is itself evidence for hypotheses with more observers, since more observers means more possible people who could have been you. But then you get the presumptuous philosopher result, where SIA tells you to be overwhelmingly confident in the many-observer cosmology before you've looked at any data, and SSA people treat that as a reductio while SIA people just shrug and say it's a feature of self-locating evidence, not a bug. Both sides seem to agree on the formal Bayes; they only disagree about what gets to count as evidence and what the reference class should be. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it substantive, verbal, a proxy for the reference class problem, or just different bedrock intuitions about whether being born is like being sampled? And is there any realistic thing that would count as progress, or has this debate stalled because the question is ill-posed?
