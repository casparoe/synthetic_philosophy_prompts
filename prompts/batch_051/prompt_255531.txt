I'm sketching an essay and want comments on where the argument is weakest.

Thesis: philosophy makes progress mainly by dissolving what look like factual questions into choices between concepts, and that's real progress rather than a consolation prize. My analogy is from math: 'Is 0 a natural number?' looked like a question about how the world is, but it turned out to be a convention choice, and the real work is now tracking which theorems depend on which convention. I want to say the free will debate has progressed the same way: it's sorted out which sense of being able to do otherwise, or which notion of control, is load-bearing for moral responsibility, rather than discovering whether free will exists simpliciter. The risky step is that the old question actually stopped being debated in math, but it didnt in philosophy—people still take themselves to genuinely disagree. So maybe my analogy shows philosophy is worse at progress, not that it progresses the same way. I want to resist that by saying the lingering disagreement is itself about the costs of the different concepts, and cost disagreements are progress too. Is that move too cute? And does the zero-as-natural-number analogy carry the weight I'm putting on it, or does it break somewhere?
