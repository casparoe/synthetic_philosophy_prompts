I keep going in circles on animal suffering before the Fall. Darwin, in his 1860 letter to Asa Gray, says he can’t see how a beneficent God could have made ichneumon wasps to feed inside living caterpillars. I want a probability, not a survey. Specifically: my credence that extensive pre-human animal pain and death make the doctrine that creation was originally good internally untenable (not merely difficult or emotionally hard) is around 0.4; a friend of mine puts it at 0.75. Give me your own number, but decompose the question into the sub-questions that actually do the work—e.g. whether pre-human animal suffering really occurred on a large scale, whether a good creation requires the absence of such suffering, whether the usual theological moves (animal pain isn’t conscious, it’s collateral damage from human sin, etc.) are plausible, or whatever else you think belongs. For each sub-question give a rough probability and a one-line justification, then show how you combine them. Finally, say where the 0.4 person and the 0.75 person most likely disagree, and what evidence or argument would move your number the most.
