I've been in a reading group for ages now where two people keep going round and round about generics — bare plural statements like "ducks lay eggs" and "mosquitoes carry malaria". One camp (call them the prevalence camp) thinks these are basically statistical/dispositional claims: "Ks are F" is true if F is common enough among Ks, or if Ks are disposed to F under the right conditions, with context fixing the thresholds. The other camp (call them the kind-norm camp) thinks the truth-maker is what's normal or characteristic for the kind itself — a sort of stereotype or "job description" for Ks — which is why "sharks attack swimmers" is true even though almost no shark ever does, and why a property held by most Ks can still fail to make a true generic if it's arbitrary.

They've finally agreed to do an adverserial collaboration. Write up the document they should have produced. It should be something both of them would sign, word for word.

Include:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides accept. Cover at least: the truth-values of the stock examples (ducks lay eggs, mosquitoes carry malaria, and the claim that a property held by a majority can still give a bad generic, e.g. the paperback case); why a simple "more than half" account cannot be the whole story; how generics differ from explicitly quantified sentences ("all ducks lay eggs", "some mosquitoes carry malaria"); and how context or a comparison class shifts judgements. But don't pad it with platitudes — include the real concessions each side had to make after a year of arguing.

2. A table of exact disagreements. For each row: the question in neutral wording, each side's answer in wording that side would endorse, and an honest verdict on whether that disagreement is substantive or partly verbal.

3. The cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. For each crux, give a concrete test case or minimal pair of generics both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Include at least one stipulated case where more than 90 percent of Ks have F but the generic is intuitively false, and one where fewer than 1 percent have F but the generic is intuitively true; for each, say what observation or argument each side has precommitted to treat as a concession. The point is the residual disagreement should be as exact as possible, and the document should be more than a cease-fire: it should be something future readers could use to run the decisive tests.
