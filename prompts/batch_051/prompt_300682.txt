You are a philosopher of mind with a talent for turning subtle positions into crisp diagnostic flowcharts. I'm preparing a handout for an undergraduate philosophy of mind seminar, and I want you to produce a decision tree that leads a student from a classic color phi demonstration to a named view about the timing of conscious experience.

In the demonstration, a red dot flashes, then a green dot flashes a short distance away. Subjects report seeing one dot move across and change from red to green roughly midway between the two flashes. The puzzle is that this midpoint report appears to use information from the second flash before the second flash could have been consciously experienced.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. Each branch node should be a concrete question an undergraduate who has only observed the demonstration could answer without knowing any theory names. The splits I care about are: (i) whether there is a determinate fact about what the subject consciously experiences at each instant; (ii) whether the subject's report is direct evidence about an experience or only evidence about a later memory or reconstruction; (iii) whether a signal can be delayed and edited before it becomes conscious, and whether that is genuinely distinct from revising a memory after consciousness; (iv) whether the original-experience/later-memory distinction can do the work the puzzle asks of it. The leaves should include at least the Stalinesque view (pre-conscious delay and editing), the Orwellian view (brief experience followed by memory revision), the Multiple Drafts view (no single stream with a determinate timeline), and a deflationary 'the question is misframed' leaf. Flag any logically available leaf that nobody defends and any pair of leaves the tree cannot actually separate.

Before writing the tree, brainstorm the combinations of answers the splits make available and where each combination leads. Then present the final tree as a clean nested list or flowchart. Write in American English.
