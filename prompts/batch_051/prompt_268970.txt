Okay, so here's a way of carving things up that a lecturer threw at us. Say a musical work is an abstract, repeatable sound-structure that a composer creates, that can be instantiated in performances, and that recordings capture one instantiation of. Under that picture, a performance is an event that realizes the work, and a recording is a record of such an event (or of a studio construction of one). It's not meant to be airtight, just a starting criteria.

Now the borderline case. An artist releases something called *Seeded Stream no. 4*. It isn't a song or album in the usual sense: it's a small program. When you run it, it generates 22 minutes of piano music by following rules about harmony, register, tempo, and weighted random choices of gestures. The outputs are never identical; every run gives a different sequence of notes, but you can save the seed and replay that exact sequence. The artist wrote the program and the rules, but never wrote a score, never made a canonical recording, and never performed any output herself. The object on the release page has a title, an author, and a hash, and people refer to it as "a piece." So my question: is *Seeded Stream no. 4* a musical work under the criteria above? Go through the criteria honestly. Where it fails, say whehter that's a problem with the criteria or with the case, and whether we should loosen the criteria (for example, allow works to be rule-specifications rather than sound-structures).
