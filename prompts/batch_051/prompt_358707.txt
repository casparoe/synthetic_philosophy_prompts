Recently a colleague used it to reject a proposal to promote one of our user-satisfaction proxies to a formal optimization objective, on the grounds that optimizing the proxy would destroy its validity as an indicator of the outcome we actually care about. I am fairly sure the maxim cannot carry all that weight on every available interpretation, but I cannot yet articulate the alternatives precisely.

Lay out the distinct precise claims this maxim could be expressing, from the near-tautological to the strong. For each reading, state its content in a sentence or two; classify it as a logical truth, a statistical regularity, a causal or incentive claim, or something else; say what kind of evidence or counterexample would test it; and give a verdict on whether it is true, false, or merely contingent as stated. Then address the conclusion my colleague drew: for each reading, say whether it actually supports refusing to make this particular metric an optimization target. In doing so, separate readings about a metric's validity as an indicator from readings about how targeting changes the underlying statistical relationship by changing behavior, and separate both from readings that amount only to the observation that a target invites gaming. I want a menu I can take into our review, with the false and overreaching readings flagged so I can push back with precision.
