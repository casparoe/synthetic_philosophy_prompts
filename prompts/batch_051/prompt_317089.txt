I'm a PhD student in comparative cognition and my advisor's lab-meeting verdicts on animal cases have been driving me a bit nuts. She clearly runs some version of Morgan's canon — don't attribute a higher mental capacity when a lower one will do — but I can't figure out how she draws the line. Cases from the last year:

- Digger wasps that drag a paralyzed cricket to the burrow, park it at the entrance, inspect the nest, then repeat the whole parking routine if someone moves the cricket a few centimeters: she says no planning, it's a fixed action pattern.
- The classic chimpanzee crate-stacking and stick-joining stuff, where the solution comes in a sudden unpracticed move after a pause: she thinks that's genuine insight, not trial and error.
- Rats that learned a maze and then, when a familiar route was flooded, took a novel shortcut: she says yes, cognitive map.
- Scrub jays that re-cache food when a rival watched them, but only if they've stolen caches themselves: she says no mental-state attribution, just an evolved cache-protection rule keyed to whether another bird is watching.
- And the guilty-look dog when the owner finds the mess: no guilt, she says it just readsthe owner's scolding and reacts to that.

Next meeting we're looking at a Portia spider that, when the direct route to its prey is blocked, detours around a rock, loses sight of the prey for over a minute, climbs a branch that gives it a fresh view, then gets behind the prey and attacks. It does this on the first trial from a novel starting position. I can think of a simple heuristic — head for where you last saw the prey and hug the barrier until you clear it — that would generate a lot of the route, though you'd need a few patches.

What should I expect her to say about the spider: planned detour, or elaborate fixed route? I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the principle behind the earlier verdicts. Can you lay out the candidate rules that fit them, what each predicts for the spider case, which rule she's probably running and why, and what follow-up case would settle which one she's actually using?
