I keep seeing the handicap principal summarised as: a signal is honest when (1) it costs the signaller something, (2) it costs low-quality signallers more than high-quality ones, and (3) receivers respond to the signal because of those costs. Fine. Now take stotting in Thomson's gazelles: when a predator shows up, a gazelle springs straight up in the air with stiff legs, over and over, before it runs. That's obviously energetic and draws attention, so it looks like a textbook handicap signal. But I've also seen it argued that the honest information is carried by the height and coordination of the jump itself—a weak animal just can't do it—and that the jump's main job is to tell the predator it has been spotted and that an ambush is pointless. Under my three criteria, does stotting count as a handicap signal? Or does the case show that criterion (2) runs together paying a cost with being physically constrained, so the list needs fixing?
