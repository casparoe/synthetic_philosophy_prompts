(d) I think the standard autonomy objection to moral bioenhancement is mostly confused: if an intervention increases the capacity for autonomous choice, you can't object to it on autonomy grounds just because it's biomedical.
(e) I think there's no principled line between moral education and moral bioenhancement, and since liberal states already run schools that deliberately shape moral character, a state role in moral enhancement isnt the bright line people treat it as.
