I am a PhD student working at the intersection of philosophy of cognitive science and machine learning, and I have put together an argument for our working group that now strikes me as proving too much. I would like a diagnosis of where it goes wrong rather than a survey of positions.

The argument runs as follows.

(1) Natural selection is a blind, myopic maximizer: variation arises without regard to its likely effects, and what persists is only what leaves more descendants under the current regime. It has no lookahead, no representation of the fitness landscape it is traversing, and no capacity to tell a local peak from the global peak.

(2) Gradient descent, as applied to deep networks, is myopic in the same respects: each step moves in whatever direction lowers the loss from the current point, and the procedure has no lookahead, no representation of the loss surface beyond the current gradient, and no capacity to distinguish a shallow basin from the global minimum except by way of external aids such as restarts, noise, or architecture choices.

(3) If two search procedures are myopic in the same respects, then whatever defect licenses calling one a stupid optimizer licenses calling the other a stupid optimizer as well.

(4) Therefore, if natural selection is a stupid optimizer, gradient descent is a stupid optimizer.

(5) But that consequence is hard to accept. Gradient descent embedded in the modern deep-learning regime yields systems that solve novel problems—predicting protein structures, playing chess and Go above expert level, producing fluent text—results that seem beyond what a mere blind hill-climber should deliver.

(6) Thus either natural selection is not a stupid optimizer, or the apparent cleverness of these systems is not to the credit of the optimizer itself.

I would like to keep (1) and (5) if I can. If I must reject one of the numbered steps, I would rather reject (3) than the others, but I suspect that choice has hidden costs. Which step should be rejected, what makes it the weak one, and what does each available option commit me to?
