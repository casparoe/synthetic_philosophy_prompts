I'm writing a piece on why people still rank civilizations by the Kardashev scale. The line I keep running into is that a civilization's energy throughput is basically its metabolism: organisms that capture more energy can do more complex things, so the more watts a civilization commands, the more 'advanced' it must be. My friend says its like ranking people by how many calories they burn, and that the metabolism analogy is doing the actual argumentative work. I can feel that something's off but I cant pin down the disanalogies. Could you audit it? What maps onto what between an organism and a civilization? Where does the metabolism analogy hold, where does it quietly stop holding, and which features of being an organism does it import that a civilization just doesnt have? He also brought up a civilization that does atom-scale engineering as a counterexample — it would score low on the energy scale but looks more 'advanced' on any intuitive read, which makes me wonder whether the problem is with the energy metric or with treating 'advancement' as a single scalar at all. When the dust settles, does anything of a Kardashev-style ranking survive, and is there a better analogy for what civilizational 'advancement' tracks, if it tracks anything?
